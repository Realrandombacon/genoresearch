# EEIG2: N-terminal C2 Domain Scaffold Protein Linking Membrane Trafficking to Transcriptional Regulation in Immune Cells

## Description
EEIG2 (formerly FAM102B) encodes a 360 amino acid uncharacterized protein (UniProt: Q5T8I3) localized to chromosome 1p13.3, also known as SYM-3B. InterPro analysis identifies a defining N-terminal C2 domain (IPR019448/PF10358/PS51840, residues 1-149) characteristic of calcium-dependent membrane-binding modules, membership in the EEIG1/2-like family (IPR039931, residues 1-358), and PTHR21456 classification within the FAM102 family. AlphaFold structural prediction reveals high confidence (pLDDT 70.2), indicating a well-folded globular protein with stable tertiary structure, contrasting with intrinsically disordered scaffolds. HPA expression profiling demonstrates low tissue specificity with detection in many tissues, localized to nucleoli and cytosol, classified as predicted intracellular protein, with immune cell-enhanced expression in blood cells and cell type-enhanced single cell specificity. The nucleolar localization suggests potential roles in ribosome biogenesis or rRNA processing, while cytosolic distribution indicates parallel membrane-associated functions. STRING database returned no interaction partners, suggesting either limited characterization or transient/condition-specific interactions not captured in current datasets. ClinVar documents 16 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number variations spanning 1p21.3-13.2 to 1p36.33-q44 regions (deletions 95-119 Mb, gains 84-249 Mb coordinates), with one intronic variant (c.138-77G>T), indicating dosage sensitivity where altered gene copy number disrupts cellular function. Conservation includes orthologs in Mus musculus (Eeig2, 339 aa, Q8BQS4) and multiple avian species (369-393 aa). We hypothesize EEIG2 functions as a calcium-regulated peripheral membrane scaffold that bridges vesicular trafficking complexes to transcriptional machinery. The N-terminal C2 domain likely mediates calcium-dependent membrane association with endosomal or Golgi compartments, while the C-terminal region may recruit nucleolar proteins involved in ribosome assembly. Immune cell-enhanced expression suggests specialized roles in lymphocyte activation or cytokine secretion, potentially coordinating membrane remodeling with transcriptional reprogramming during immune responses. Dosage sensitivity implies precise stoichiometric requirements for C2-mediated membrane scaffolding, with haploinsufficiency or triplosensitivity causing vesicular trafficking defects or nucleolar stress.
