## Description
LOC129389608 is a 200bp cis-regulatory element (CRE) located on chromosome 6q21 (GRCh38: 109687012-109687212), functionally validated as a transcriptional silencer through STARR-seq massively parallel reporter assays (MPRAs) in K562 erythroleukemia cells. This element lacks canonical promoter, enhancer, or CTCF-bound insulator chromatin signatures but demonstrates robust repressive activity against the super core promoter SCP1. As a non-coding regulatory locus, it has no UniProt accession, protein domains, or STRING interaction partners. Human Protein Atlas could not resolve this locus to an Ensembl ID, consistent with its non-coding regulatory classification.\n\nClinVar analysis reveals 10 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variants disrupting this region—a strong deletion-biased burden (9 deletions:1 duplication ratio). Deletions span 6q16.3-23.2 regions (~10-68Mb) while the single duplication spans 6q13-24.1 (~68Mb). This striking deletion predominance (90% deletions) suggests strong haploinsufficiency where silencer loss causes derepression of target genes. These recurrent CNVs are associated with 6q deletion syndromes featuring developmental delay, intellectual disability, craniofacial dysmorphism, cardiac defects, and early-onset Parkinsonism. The 6q21 region is gene-dense, containing critical dosage-sensitive genes such as PARK2 (Parkinson Protein 2/parkin, E3 ubiquitin ligase), SIM1 (Single-Minded Homolog 1, hypothalamic development and obesity regulation), and MYCN proto-oncogene binding protein (MYCNBP).\n\nHYPOTHESIS: LOC129389608 functions as a critical distal silencer within the 6q21 topologically associating domain (TAD), regulating expression of dosage-sensitive neuronal and metabolic genes. Candidate targets include SIM1 (hypothalamic development, appetite regulation) or PARK2 (mitochondrial quality control, dopaminergic neuron survival). The silencer likely recruits co-repressor complexes (PRC2, NCoR/SMRT, HDACs) to deposit H3K27me3 marks at target promoters. The strong deletion-biased CNV burden (9:1 ratio) indicates haploinsufficiency predominance where silencer loss causes target gene derepression. The K562 validation suggests potential roles in hematopoietic lineage specification. Deletions may cause SIM1 derepression leading to hypothalamic dysfunction (obesity, developmental delay) or PARK2 overexpression causing mitochondrial stress and dopaminergic toxicity, contributing to 6q deletion syndrome with early-onset Parkinsonism features.
