## Description
COPS7B encodes a 264 amino acid COP9 signalosome complex subunit 7b (UniProt Q9H9Q2), a critical component of the ~450 kDa COP9 signalosome (CSN) complex that regulates protein stability through deneddylation of cullin-RING E3 ubiquitin ligases (CRLs). The protein contains a canonical PCI (Proteasome-CSN-eIF3) domain (IPR000717/PF01399/PS50250/SM00088 at positions 1-177/79-154/1-159/87-177) essential for complex assembly and substrate recognition, a COP9 signalosome subunit 7 helix I domain (IPR041481/PF18392 at positions 166-215) involved in CSN assembly (GO:0010387), and a CSN7 helical bundle subdomain (PF22061 at positions 13-44). The PCI domain mediates heterodimerization with other CSN subunits and is characteristic of the eIF3m/COPS7 family (IPR045237/PTHR15350 at positions 10-243).\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals COPS7B forms an exceptionally tight octameric complex with all other COP9 signalosome subunits: COPS8 (0.999), COPS5/JAB1 (0.999—catalytic deneddylase subunit), GPS1/COPS1 (0.999), COPS4 (0.999), COPS7A (0.987—paralog), COPS6 (0.985), COPS3 (0.982), COPS2 (0.973), COPS9/CSNAP (0.961), and NEDD8 (0.575—the ubiquitin-like modifier removed by CSN). This positions COPS7B at the heart of cellular protein homeostasis, regulating turnover of thousands of substrates including cell cycle regulators (p27, p53, IκB), transcription factors (HIF-1α, JUN), and DNA repair proteins.\n\nHPA expression data classifies COPS7B as detected in all tissues with low tissue specificity and cell type-enhanced expression patterns. Subcellular localization is nucleoplasmic, consistent with regulation of nuclear CRL substrates. Biological process annotation includes host-virus interaction, suggesting roles in antiviral defense via regulation of viral protein stability or interferon signaling.\n\nClinVar documents 33 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number gains (2q35-37.3 duplications spanning 24 Mb), duplications, copy number losses (2q37.1 deletions), nonsense mutations (c.160G>T, p.Glu54Ter—predicted to truncate the PCI domain), and 3' UTR variants (c.*1037G>T). Critically, ClinVar lists 0 associated diseases—a major knowledge gap given COPS7B's essential role in protein homeostasis. This suggests COPS7B dysfunction may cause undiagnosed developmental disorders, immunodeficiency (given host-virus interaction annotation), cancer predisposition (CRLs regulate tumor suppressors), or synthetic lethal relationships in cancers with CRL dependency.\n\nAlphaFold structure prediction (AF-Q9H9Q2-F1) yields a global pLDDT score of 84.8 (high confidence) across all 264 residues, indicating a well-folded PCI domain structure suitable for CSN complex assembly.\n\nLiterature analysis (19 papers) reveals emerging but incomplete understanding. Recent 2022-2025 studies link COPS7B to colorectal cancer progression via IGF2BP3-mediated mRNA translation enhancement, hepatocellular carcinoma prognosis and immune infiltration, multiple myeloma IMiD resistance via 2q37 copy loss (COPS7B/COPS8 haploinsufficiency sensitizes to lenalidomide/pomalidomide), and BAP1-deficient pancancer deubiquitination landscapes. However, no Mendelian disease phenotype has been defined despite 33 pathogenic variants. Key unknowns include: specific disease phenotypes from loss-of-function mutations, contribution to developmental disorders (given essential CSN function), role in viral susceptibility, and therapeutic potential for cancers with COPS7B-dependent CRL dysregulation.
