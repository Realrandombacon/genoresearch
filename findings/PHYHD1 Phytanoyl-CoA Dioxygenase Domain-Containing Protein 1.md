## Description
PHYHD1 (Phytanoyl-CoA dioxygenase domain-containing protein 1; UniProt Q5SRE7) is a 291 amino acid 2-oxoglutarate-dependent dioxygenase with only ~21 total publications, representing a GENUINE DARK GENE despite its emerging roles in RNA metabolism and neurodegeneration. While a groundbreaking 2025 study revealed PHYHD1's unexpected function as an RNA 2'-O-methyl demethylase—a previously unknown activity for the PhyH family—and earlier work linked it to Alzheimer's disease pathology, some cancers, and T-cell activation (2016 study, 7 cites), critical mechanistic gaps persist: the specific RNA substrates targeted for demethylation remain unidentified; the structural basis for switching from canonical phytanoyl-CoA metabolism to RNA modification is unresolved; the precise neurological phenotypes associated with chromosome 9q CNVs encompassing PHYHD1 are uncharacterized despite 34 pathogenic variants; the functional significance of nuclear speckle localization versus cytosolic PhyH homologs is speculative; and the weak STRING interactions suggest either transient binding partners or undiscovered strong interactors. In neurons, chronic hypoxia or metabolic stress may impair PHYHD1 activity, causing RNA hypermethylation and splicing errors characteristic of Alzheimer's pathology.
