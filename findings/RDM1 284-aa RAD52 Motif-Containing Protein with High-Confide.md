## Description
RDM1 (UniProt Q8NG50), or RAD52 motif-containing protein 1, encodes a compact 284-amino acid nuclear factor belonging to the RDM1 family (IPR040224/PTHR31164). The protein architecture features an N-terminal RNA recognition motif (RRM) domain (IPR000504/PF00076/PS50102, aa 15-98) conferring RNA binding capability, followed by a specialized double-stranded RNA binding motif (DSRM) domain (IPR057652/PF25517, aa 109-218) unique to this family, and a C-terminal region containing the canonical RAD52 motif implicated in DNA repair. AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (global pLDDT: 77.6), indicating a well-folded protein with distinct RRM and DSRM domains connected by flexible linkers, consistent with a multifunctional scaffold capable of simultaneous nucleic acid binding.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals RDM1's integration into DNA repair and signaling networks: RAD52 (0.898, direct homolog and repair partner), DRD1 (0.780, dopamine receptor D1—suggesting potential non-canonical signaling crosstalk), PI4KB (0.619, phosphatidylinositol 4-kinase), TP53 (0.515, tumor suppressor p53), AMZ1 (0.476), NKAIN4 (0.456), OR4D9 (0.455), STK31 (0.425), FASTKD3 (0.421, mitochondrial RNA metabolism), and CCDC57 (0.414). The strong RAD52 interaction confirms its role in homologous recombination, while the TP53 link supports cell cycle checkpoint regulation.\n\nWith only 61 papers, RDM1 qualifies as a genuine dark gene with emerging oncogenic mechanisms documented in 2025-2026. Recent studies reveal: (1) RDM1 plays an oncogenic role in clear cell renal cell carcinoma by modulating MCM2 expression, driving proliferation; (2) transferrin-modified liposomes enhance chemosensitivity in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) by suppressing RDM1-mediated DNA repair, identifying it as a therapeutic target for overcoming drug resistance; (3) loss of RDM1 enhances HCC progression via dysregulation of p53 and Ras/Raf/ERK pathways, indicating a context-dependent tumor suppressor function in specific genetic backgrounds; (4) RDM1 promotes breast cancer tumorigenesis and osteosarcoma progression via cell cycle regulation and MEK/ERK signaling activation. HPA data confirms testis-enriched expression (11.4 nTPM top tissue) with nucleolar and cytosolic localization, classified as an essential protein with DNA/RNA binding functions. ClinVar lists 7 pathogenic variants, primarily chromosome 17q11.2-q22 copy number losses/gains, confirming dosage sensitivity. We hypothesize RDM1 functions as a molecular switch at the intersection of DNA repair and transcriptional regulation: the RRM/DSRM domains bind nascent transcripts or DNA damage-induced RNA structures (R-loops), recruiting RAD52 to facilitate homologous recombination at stalled replication forks; simultaneously, RDM1 modulates MEK/ERK and p53 signaling outputs to determine cell fate (repair vs. apoptosis) following genotoxic stress—the dual oncogenic/tumor suppressor phenotypes suggest its activity is tightly coupled to cellular metabolic state and specific mutational landscapes, making it a critical node for precision oncology strategies targeting DNA repair dependencies.
