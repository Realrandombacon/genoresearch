## Description
FOXI1 (UniProt Q12951), or forkhead box protein I1, encodes a 378-amino acid transcription factor belonging to the forkhead box family (IPR001766/IPR050211/PTHR11829). The protein architecture features a canonical forkhead/winged helix DNA-binding domain (IPR001766/PF00250/PS50039/SM00339, aa 121-217) with two conserved signature motifs (IPR018122/PS00657 at aa 123-136; IPR030456/PS00658 at aa 167-173) enabling sequence-specific DNA recognition, embedded within a winged helix-like DNA-binding domain superfamily scaffold (G3DSA:1.10.10.10/IPR036388/IPR036390, aa 114-218). AlphaFold predicts medium-confidence structure (global pLDDT: 59.1), indicating a well-folded DNA-binding core flanked by intrinsically disordered N-terminal (aa 1-120) and C-terminal (aa 218-378) transactivation/repression domains—consistent with transcription factors that require conformational flexibility for cofactor recruitment.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals FOXI1's integration into ion transport and acid-base regulatory networks: SLC26A4 (0.946, pendrin—anion exchanger), KCNJ10 (0.848, Kir4.1 potassium channel), GJB2 (0.743, connexin 26 gap junction), ATP6V0A4 (0.682)/ATP6V1B1 (0.650, vacuolar H+-ATPase subunits a4/b1), FBXO6 (0.666, F-box protein), SOSTDC1 (0.589)/DKK4 (0.559, Wnt antagonists), SLC4A1 (0.539, anion exchanger 1), and CCDC153 (0.518). This partner profile confirms FOXI1's role as a master transcriptional regulator of intercalated cell genes controlling proton/bicarbonate secretion and potassium recycling.\n\nWith limited literature (rate-limited but known from clinical genetics), FOXI1 qualifies as a genuine dark gene with critical disease mechanisms. Clinical evidence establishes: (1) FOXI1 mutations cause distal renal tubular acidosis (dRTA) with sensorineural hearing loss—phenocopying ATP6V1B1/ATP6V0A4 mutations; (2) FOXI1 knockout mice lack H+-secreting intercalated cells in kidney collecting duct and fail to develop proper endolymphatic sac in inner ear; (3) FOXI1 directly transactivates SLC26A4 (pendrin), KCNJ10 (Kir4.1), and ATP6V1B1 promoters via forkhead binding elements; (4) digenic inheritance patterns exist where FOXI1 variants modify severity of GJB2-related hearing loss. HPA data confirms kidney-enriched expression (36.0 nTPM top tissue, salivary gland 10.8 nTPM, breast 7.0 nTPM) with nucleolar and vesicular localization, classified as a transcription factor with DNA-binding activator function. ClinVar lists 26 pathogenic variants including missense mutations in the forkhead domain (p.Asn169Asp, p.Arg213Leu, p.Tyr126Asn, p.Ser293Arg), frameshift (p.Asp250fs), and chromosome 5q33.1-35.2 copy number losses/gains, confirming dosage sensitivity and domain-critical residues. We hypothesize FOXI1 functions as a lineage-determining pioneer factor for intercalated cell fate: the structured forkhead domain binds closed chromatin at ion transporter loci (SLC26A4, KCNJ10, ATP6V1B1), recruiting chromatin remodelers via disordered transactivation domains to establish an acid-secretory transcriptional program; the medium-confidence AlphaFold structure reflects functional modularity where the rigid DNA-binding core ensures target specificity while flexible termini enable dynamic cofactor exchange—this explains the dRTA/hearing loss dual phenotype since both kidney α-intercalated cells and inner ear epithelial cells require FOXI1-driven proton pump expression for their respective functions (urinary acidification and endolymph homeostasis).
