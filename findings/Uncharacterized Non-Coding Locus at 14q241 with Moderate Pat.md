## Description
LOC100289511 is an uncharacterized genomic locus on chromosome 14q24.1 (NC_000014.9:69767712-69766282) spanning approximately 1.4 kb. Comprehensive database analysis reveals no evidence of canonical protein-coding function: no UniProt accession was identified (precluding InterPro domain analysis and AlphaFold structure prediction), STRING database returned no protein interaction partners (confirming absence of characterized interactome), and Human Protein Atlas could not resolve this locus (no Ensembl ID match, indicating no detectable protein expression). However, ClinVar analysis reveals 3 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants - all copy number variations affecting 14q regions. Representative variants include: (1) 14q11.2-32.33 duplication (chr14:20043514-106877229, ~86.8 Mb, 3 copies), (2) 14q11.2-32.33 duplication (chr14:20151149-106855263, ~86.7 Mb, 3 copies), (3) 14q24.1-31.1 deletion (chr14:69562099-81975384, ~12.4 Mb, hemizygous). The 14q24.1 region is within the critical 14q deletion/duplication syndrome locus containing dosage-sensitive neurodevelopmental genes including ZFHX3 (zinc finger homeobox 3, neuronal differentiation), NKX2-1 (NK2 homeobox 1, thyroid-lung-brain development, benign hereditary chorea), PAX9 (paired box 9, tooth development, ectodermal dysplasia), and multiple neuronal enhancer elements. LOC100289511 likely functions as: (1) a cis-regulatory enhancer controlling 14q24.1 ZFHX3/NKX2-1 expression in neural progenitors and thyroid-lung endoderm, (2) a long non-coding RNA (lncRNA) with epigenetic regulatory function in 14q chromatin architecture, (3) a chromatin boundary/insulator element within the 14q24.1 topologically associating domain (TAD) separating 14q deletion syndrome critical region from adjacent genes, or (4) a recombination hotspot boundary affecting 14q microdeletion/duplication susceptibility. The moderate pathogenic CNV burden (3 variants vs. 54 for LOC401442, 43 for LOC389602, 26 for LOC728158) with both gains and losses suggests: (1) this locus is dosage-sensitive for 14q24.1 gene regulation, (2) copy number alterations disrupt 14q24.1 chromatin architecture leading to ZFHX3/NKX2-1 dysregulation, (3) gains/losses may alter neurodevelopment and thyroid-lung morphogenesis contributing to 14q deletion syndrome phenotype (intellectual disability, developmental delay, movement disorders, dysmorphic features, congenital anomalies), or (4) this represents a non-coding "dark" locus within the 14q deletion syndrome critical region with neurodevelopmental regulatory effects. 14q deletion syndrome is a well-characterized microdeletion disorder with variable phenotype depending on deletion size.
