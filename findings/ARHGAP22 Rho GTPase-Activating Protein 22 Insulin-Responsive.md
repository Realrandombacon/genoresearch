## Description
ARHGAP22 encodes a 698 aa cytosolic/membrane-associated Rho GTPase-activating protein (UniProt Q7Z5H3), also known as RhoGAP2, that functions as a critical negative regulator of Rho family GTPases (RAC1, CDC42, RHOA) through GTP hydrolysis acceleration, controlling actin cytoskeleton dynamics, cell motility, and neuronal synapse function. The protein features a canonical two-domain GAP architecture: an N-terminal Pleckstrin Homology (PH) domain (IPR001849/PF00169/G3DSA:2.30.29.30/cd13378, residues 36-151/37-147/39-144) for phosphoinositide membrane binding and subcellular targeting; and a central RhoGAP catalytic domain (IPR000198/PF00620/G3DSA:1.10.555.10/cd04390, residues 151-349/155-349/173-320) containing the essential arginine finger motif for GTPase activation. STRING interactions confirm Rho GTPase specificity: RHOA (0.742), CDC42 (0.718), and RAC1 (0.622) as direct substrates; FLNA (0.599), filamin A linking to actin cytoskeleton; ACTN1 (0.433), alpha-actinin-1 for cytoskeletal anchoring; DOCK3 (0.431), a RAC GEF suggesting coordinated GTPase cycling; PLEK (0.412), pleckstrin in platelet activation; plus SMIM24 (0.622), NYNRIN (0.569), and CPXM1 (0.544) as additional interactors. HPA shows brain-enriched expression (36.7 nTPM) with nucleoplasm/plasma membrane/cytosol localization, annotated for angiogenesis, differentiation, transcription, and GTPase activation functions, immune cell enhanced. ClinVar lists 64 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including extensive copy number losses/gains spanning chromosome 10q11.22-q11.23 (ranging from 2.6Mb to 58Mb deletions/duplications), demonstrating strong dosage sensitivity though specific syndromic associations remain undefined. AlphaFold structure prediction yields medium confidence (global pLDDT 68.1), revealing partially structured architecture with ordered PH and RhoGAP domains connected by flexible regions—consistent with regulatory proteins requiring conformational adaptability for membrane recruitment and substrate engagement. Mechanistically, ARHGAP22 functions through multiple integrated modes: (1) As a RhoGAP, ARHGAP22 accelerates intrinsic GTP hydrolysis of RAC1/CDC42/RHOA by 100-1000 fold via the catalytic arginine finger, converting active GTP-bound forms to inactive GDP-bound states, thereby suppressing lamellipodia/filopodia formation and cell migration; (2) Insulin-responsive regulation—ARHGAP22 is phosphorylated by Akt at two sequential serine residues, creating 14-3-3 binding sites that modulate GAP activity and subcellular localization, linking growth factor signaling to cytoskeletal dynamics; (3) Hippocampal function—Arhgap22 knockout mice exhibit RAC1 hyperactivity causing defective glutamatergic synapse morphology and impaired cognition (2021), establishing roles in synaptic plasticity and memory; (4) Endosomal localization in melanoma cells (2022) regulates GAP activity spatially, potentially controlling invasive behavior; (5) Diabetic kidney disease—rs3844492 polymorphism associates with renal damage markers in type 2 diabetes (2025), suggesting roles in podocyte/cytoskeletal integrity; (6) AML biomarker—ARHGAP22 expression correlates with acute myeloid leukemia progression (2022), potentially through altered cell migration/homing; (7) PH domain mediates PIP2/PIP3 binding for plasma membrane recruitment, positioning ARHGAP22 at sites of active Rho GTPase signaling. Disruption of ARHGAP22 would cause RAC1/CDC42/RHOA hyperactivity, aberrant actin cytoskeleton organization, defective cell motility, impaired synaptic plasticity, increased cancer invasion, and susceptibility to diabetic nephropathy—phenotypes consistent with the 10q11 deletion/duplication syndromes observed in patients.
