# DEFB110: Epididymis-Specific Beta-Defensin Antimicrobial Peptide with Sperm Maturation Function

## Description
DEFB110 (Defensin Beta 110, also DEFB-10, DEFB-11, DEFB111) is a compact 67 amino acid secreted antimicrobial peptide (UniProt Q30KQ9) with a well-folded beta-defensin structure (AlphaFold pLDDT 80.6, high confidence). The protein contains a canonical beta-defensin domain (IPR025933/PF13841, residues 34-64) characteristic of the beta-defensin antimicrobial peptide family (PTHR20515), conferring innate immune function (GO:0045087) and extracellular localization (GO:0005576).\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals DEFB110 functions within the beta-defensin cluster network, showing high-confidence interactions with co-expressed defensins: DEFB113 (0.878), DEFB127 (0.834), DEFB115 (0.788), DEFB119 (0.767), DEFB128 (0.731), DEFB106B (0.626), DEFB107A (0.609), DEFB105A (0.558), DEFB129 (0.541), and DEFB114 (0.523). This co-expression pattern reflects the tandem gene cluster organization at chromosome 6p12.3.\n\nHPA expression shows exceptional epididymis enrichment (820.7 nTPM) with tissue-enriched specificity and detection in single tissue type, consistent with specialized reproductive function. The protein is classified as a predicted secreted protein with antibiotic, antimicrobial, and defensin molecular functions.\n\nClinVar documents 8 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large chromosome 6 copy number variations spanning 6p12.3-q27 regions. While specific DEFB110 point mutations are not yet catalogued, these CNVs suggest dosage sensitivity potentially affecting male fertility through impaired sperm maturation.\n\nConservation is evident from chimpanzee (Q30KL6, 67 aa), dog (Q30KU3, 62 aa), baboon (A0A096NJ15, 67 aa), and kangaroo rat (A0A1S3FEZ7, 62 aa) orthologs, indicating functional importance across mammals.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: DEFB110 acts as an epididymis-specific antimicrobial peptide that coats spermatozoa during epididymal transit, providing protection against ascending urogenital infections while potentially participating in sperm maturation and capacitation. The cationic beta-defensin fold enables membrane disruption of Gram-negative bacteria through electrostatic interactions with lipopolysaccharides. CNV-mediated copy number loss may cause male infertility through increased susceptibility to epididymal infection or impaired sperm surface modification, while gains may cause inflammatory conditions.
