## Description
ZNF133 (Gene ID: 7692, UniProt P52736, aliases ZNF150, pHZ-13, pHZ-66) is a 654 amino acid Krüppel-associated box (KRAB) domain-containing zinc finger protein located on chromosome 20p11.23 (NC_000020.11, positions 18288526-18316995, ~28kb genomic region). This gene encodes a canonical KRAB-zinc finger transcription factor architecture with well-defined molecular function but incompletely characterized biological targets and regulatory programs. InterPro domain analysis reveals N-terminal KRAB-A box domain (IPR001909/PF01352/cd07765, positions 1-72/1-41/1-40) functioning as transcriptional repression module that recruits co-repressor complexes, followed by multiple C2H2-type zinc finger DNA-binding domains (IPR013087/PF00096, positions 214-241/242-269/270-297; additional fingers at 307-363/363-419 per IPR036236) enabling sequence-specific DNA recognition. GO annotations confirm regulation of DNA-templated transcription (GO:0006355), RNA polymerase II cis-regulatory region sequence-specific DNA binding, DNA-binding transcription factor activity, and repressor function. STRING interaction network identifies 10 medium-confidence protein partners: MRPS2 (0.636, mitochondrial ribosomal protein S2), LGALS4 (0.567, galectin-4, carbohydrate-binding protein), MAP10 (0.531, microtubule-associated protein 10), POLR3F (0.526, RNA polymerase III subunit F), SLC24A3 (0.487, sodium/potassium/calcium exchanger 3), RBBP9 (0.486, retinoblastoma-binding protein 9), ZNF514 (0.485, another KRAB-ZFP suggesting co-regulation or heterodimerization), DAP3 (0.479, death-associated protein 3, mitochondrial ribosomal protein), DZANK1 (0.424, double zinc ribbon and ankyrin repeat-containing protein 1), and critically TRIM28 (0.408, also known as KAP1/TIF1β—the universal KRAB domain co-repressor that recruits SETDB1 histone methyltransferase and HP1 for heterochromatin formation). The TRIM28 interaction places ZNF133 within the canonical KRAB-ZFP epigenetic silencing machinery. Human Protein Atlas classifies ZNF133 (ENSG00000125846) as a predicted intracellular protein and transcription factor with nucleoplasm and vesicle subcellular localization, detected in all tissues with low tissue/cancer/immune cell specificity, and cell type-enhanced single cell specificity. Biological process annotations include transcription and transcription regulation; molecular function includes DNA-binding and repressor activity. AlphaFold structure prediction (AF-P52736-F1) yields high-confidence model (global pLDDT 73.5), indicating a well-folded stable architecture across all 654 residues—consistent with KRAB-ZFPs where zinc finger domains form ordered structures while linker regions provide flexibility for DNA scanning. ClinVar lists 24 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large chromosomal copy number variations spanning chromosome 20p regions (e.g., chr20:68351-23860313 triplication, chr20:87153-23635465 triplication, chr20:8571696-22088650 deletion, chr20:16400000-24400000 deletion); one splice region variant (c.121+10A>G) is also listed. No specific monogenic disease is attributed solely to ZNF133 point mutations, suggesting dosage sensitivity within larger chromosomal syndromes rather than haploinsufficiency. Conservation evidenced by bovine ortholog (A8WFL5, 634 aa) and chimpanzee isoforms (A0A6D2W8H1, 654 aa identical length), indicating evolutionary constraint across mammals. Functional hypothesis: ZNF133 functions as a sequence-specific transcriptional repressor that recognizes target DNA motifs via its array of C2H2 zinc fingers (likely 10+ fingers based on domain pattern) and recruits TRIM28/KAP1 co-repressor complex through its KRAB domain, leading to H3K9me3 deposition, HP1 recruitment, and heterochromatin formation at target loci. Ubiquitous expression (detected in all tissues) suggests housekeeping regulatory role, potentially silencing retrotransposons, imprinted genes, or developmentally regulated genes in a cell type-specific manner. Cell type-enhanced specificity indicates potential specialization in certain lineages. Disease mechanism: ZNF133 haploinsufficiency or overexpression may contribute to chromosome 20p deletion/duplication syndromes, though specific phenotype remains undefined. The 24 ClinVar CNVs suggest dosage sensitivity; triplication may cause aberrant silencing of target genes while deletion may cause derepression of normally silenced loci. TRIM28 interaction confirms ZNF133 operates within epigenetic silencing pathway; disruption may alter chromatin states at developmental genes or retrotransposon control elements. Priority experiments include ChIP-seq to identify DNA binding motifs and genomic targets, co-IP validation of TRIM28 interaction and associated heterochromatin factors (SETDB1, HP1), CRISPR knockout in multiple cell lines to assess transcriptional consequences and retrotransposon activation, investigation of ZNF133 role in development using knockout mice, assessment of ZNF133 expression patterns during differentiation, and structural studies leveraging high-confidence AlphaFold model (pLDDT 73.5) to identify DNA-binding surfaces.
