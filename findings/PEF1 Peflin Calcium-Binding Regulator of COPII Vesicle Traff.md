# PEF1: Peflin Calcium-Binding Regulator of COPII Vesicle Trafficking and Collagen Secretion with Unmapped Clinical Phenotypes

## Description
PEF1 (Penta-EF-hand domain containing 1), commonly known as Peflin, is a 284-amino acid calcium-binding protein (UniProt Q9UBV8) representing a genuine dark gene with only 90 publications (many focused on non-human orthologs in yeast, Candida, and bacteriophage) despite its emerging role as a critical regulator of COPII vesicle trafficking and collagen secretion. Structurally, PEF1 belongs to the Peflin/Sorcin calcium-binding family (IPR051426/PTHR46212, residues 7-281) containing multiple EF-hand calcium-binding motifs: canonical EF-hand domains (IPR002048/SM00054, residues 114-183 and 181-216) with defined calcium-binding sites (PS00018, residues 127-139 and 194-206), EF-hand domain pairs (IPR011992/PF13499, residues 114-279), and the broader EF-hand superfamily (G3DSA:1.10.238.10/SSF47473, residues 108-283). The penta-EF-hand architecture suggests calcium-dependent conformational switching that regulates membrane association and partner binding. AlphaFold predicts confident structure (pLDDT 73.3), consistent with a well-folded calcium-binding protein capable of reversible membrane translocation. Functionally, PEF1 localizes to nucleoplasm, vesicles, and cytosol with ubiquitous expression across all tissues (HPA: detected in all, low tissue specificity), suggesting housekeeping roles in intracellular transport. STRING interactions reveal exclusive, high-confidence connections to COPII vesicle trafficking machinery: SEC31A (0.980, outer COPII coat component), SEC13 (0.965, COPII coat nucleator), SEC23A (0.937, inner COPII coat GTPase activating protein), SEC24C/A/B/D (0.921-0.929, cargo adaptors), KLHL12 (0.929, Kelch-like substrate adaptor for CUL3 ubiquitin ligase regulating COPII), PDCD6 (0.918, Alix/penta-EF-hand paralog involved in ESCRT), and CUL3 (0.716, cullin scaffold for ubiquitination). This interaction network positions PEF1 as a calcium-sensitive regulator of ER-to-Golgi transport, particularly for bulky cargoes like procollagen requiring specialized COPII coats. Critically, ClinVar documents 9 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number gains/losses spanning 1p36-35 (multiple entries affecting megabase regions), yet ZERO diseases are formally associated—a significant gap given the essential trafficking function. Recent literature establishes emerging roles: fungal Pef1 orthologs function at sites of membrane perturbation to support polarized growth and membrane integrity in Candida albicans (2024); Schizosaccharomyces pombe Pef1 (Cdk5 ortholog) regulates lifespan and autophagy (2024); and human PEF1/Sorcin family members modulate ER stress responses and collagen secretion. The mechanistic hypothesis: PEF1 functions as a calcium-sensitive molecular switch that reversibly associates with ER exit sites to regulate COPII coat assembly and cargo selection. Upon calcium elevation, PEF1 undergoes conformational change exposing hydrophobic surfaces that promote membrane binding and interaction with SEC31A/SEC23A, potentially stabilizing enlarged COPII coats required for procollagen export. Through KLHL12-CUL3 interactions, PEF1 may undergo ubiquitin-dependent regulation coordinating trafficking with quality control. In disease contexts, PEF1 dysfunction could cause collagen secretion defects manifesting as connective tissue disorders (Ehlers-Danlos-like phenotypes), ER stress-related pathologies, or neurodevelopmental defects given the 1p36 deletion syndrome overlap. The absence of formal disease associations suggests either embryonic lethality of homozygous loss, functional redundancy with Sorcin (PDCD6), or that PEF1 variants act as modifiers of trafficking disorders. Future work should define calcium-binding affinities of individual EF-hands, identify PEF1-dependent cargoes beyond collagen, and screen patients with 1p36 deletion syndrome or connective tissue disorders for PEF1 mutations.
