## Description
GALNT8 (Polypeptide N-Acetylgalactosaminyltransferase 8, UniProt Q9NY28) is a 637 aa Golgi-resident glycosyltransferase representing a partially characterized dark gene with 21 literature references and significant clinical relevance. Structural analysis reveals canonical pp-GalNAc-T architecture: N-terminal cytoplasmic/stem region (aa 1-103), central catalytic glycosyltransferase domain (aa 184-485, IPR045885/cd02510) containing GT2 fold (IPR001173/PF00535, aa 184-327) for UDP-GalNAc binding and catalysis, and C-terminal ricin B-type lectin domain (aa 485-629, IPR000772/PF00652/SM00458) with beta-trefoil fold (cd23472) for substrate recognition and acceptor specificity. AlphaFold predicts high-confidence structure (pLDDT=86.4) consistent with well-folded two-domain glycosyltransferase containing nucleotide-diphospho-sugar transferase superfamily fold (IPR029044, aa 104-493). STRING interactions place GALNT8 within mucin-type O-glycosylation network with very high confidence: GCNT1 (0.928, core 2 beta-1,6-N-acetylglucosaminyltransferase), B3GNT6 (0.908, beta-1,3-N-acetylglucosaminyltransferase), B4GALNT1 (0.852, beta-1,4-N-acetylgalactosaminyltransferase), C1GALT1 (0.775, core 1 beta-1,3-galactosyltransferase), C1GALT1C1/COSMC (0.751, chaperone for C1GALT1), C1GALT1C1L (0.712, COSMC-like), ST6GALNAC1 (0.546, alpha-2,6-sialyltransferase), MUC2 (0.492, intestinal mucin), MUC1 (0.461, transmembrane mucin), and MUC4 (0.444, membrane-bound mucin) — indicating GALNT8 functions in coordinated mucin O-glycan biosynthesis pathway initiating GalNAc attachment to Ser/Thr residues. HPA expression confirms tissue-specific pattern: intestine-enriched (29.3 nTPM), testis-enriched (10.4 nTPM), group-enriched specificity, cancer-enhanced expression, Golgi apparatus and vesicle localization consistent with glycosyltransferase function. ClinVar identifies 60 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including multiple copy number gains (12p13.33-11.1, 12p13.33-q13.12) and losses (12p13.33-13.31, 12p13.33-13.32) affecting GALNT8 locus, though specific GALNT8-associated disease phenotypes remain undefined — variants occur in chromosome 12p region affecting multiple genes. Literature establishes GALNT8 function in: (1) breast cancer metastasis suppression — GALNT8 suppresses metastasis potential by regulating EGFR O-GalNAcylation (19 citations), (2) breast cancer proliferation control — GALNT8 and O-glycosylation of BMP receptor 1A suppress proliferation by upregulating ERα levels (12 citations), (3) colorectal cancer progression — lncRNA GAU1 induces GALNT8 overexpression potentiating CRC progression (7 citations), (4) Golgi stress response — GALNT8 transcriptionally regulated by mucin pathway of Golgi stress response. Functional hypothesis: GALNT8 acts as initiator O-GalNAc transferase attaching GalNAc to specific Ser/Thr residues on mucin and receptor substrates (EGFR, BMPR1A), with ricin B lectin domain conferring substrate specificity through carbohydrate-protein interactions. The intestine-enriched expression suggests primary role in intestinal mucin glycosylation maintaining epithelial barrier function, while cancer-enhanced expression indicates dysregulation in tumor progression. Disease mechanism: GALNT8 exhibits context-dependent oncogenic/tumor-suppressive roles — suppresses breast cancer metastasis via EGFR O-GalNAcylation reducing EGFR signaling, but promotes colorectal cancer when overexpressed via lncRNA GAU1. The 60 ClinVar CNVs suggest dosage sensitivity where copy number alterations disrupt O-glycosylation homeostasis affecting mucin barrier integrity and receptor signaling. Priority experiments: (a) identify GALNT8 substrate specificity via glycoproteomics, (b) characterize GALNT8-EGFR and GALNT8-BMPR1A interaction interfaces, (c) test GALNT8 role in intestinal barrier function via knockout models, (d) determine GALNT8-associated disease phenotypes in 12p CNV patients. GALNT8 exemplifies 'dark glycosyltransferase' where domain architecture and cancer associations are defined but substrate repertoire, tissue-specific functions, and disease mechanisms remain unexplored despite 60 ClinVar variants.
