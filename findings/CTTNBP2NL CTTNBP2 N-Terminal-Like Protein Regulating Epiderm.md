## Description
CTTNBP2NL (CTTNBP2 N-Terminal-Like Protein) is a 639-amino acid intracellular scaffolding protein (UniProt Q9P2B4) representing a genuine dark gene with only 16 publications despite its critical roles in cytoskeletal organization, epidermal barrier formation, and cancer biology. Structurally, CTTNBP2NL belongs to the cortactin-binding and actin dynamics regulator family with defined domain architecture: Cortactin-binding protein-2 N-terminal domain (IPR019131/PF09727, residues 5-188) mediating protein-protein interactions with cortactin and actin regulatory machinery, and broader cortactin-binding and actin dynamics regulator family membership (IPR050719/PTHR23166, residues 1-292) defining its role in cytoskeletal remodeling. Unlike its paralog CTTNBP2, CTTNBP2NL lacks neuronal enrichment and shows distinct functional specialization. AlphaFold predicts medium-confidence structure (pLDDT 65.9), consistent with a partially structured scaffolding protein containing ordered interaction domains connected by intrinsically disordered regions—typical of dynamic scaffold proteins that undergo conformational flexibility during multi-protein complex assembly and signal transduction. Functionally, CTTNBP2NL localizes to nucleoli fibrillar center and cytosol with broad expression across tissues (HPA: detected in many, low tissue specificity, low cancer specificity, immune cell enhanced, cell type enhanced), classified as a predicted intracellular protein. STRING interactions reveal exceptionally high-confidence connections to the STRIPAK (Striatin-Interacting Phosphatase and Kinase) complex and cytoskeletal regulators: STRIP1 (0.999, striatin-interacting protein 1—recently shown to stabilize CTTNBP2NL), STRN3 (0.999, striatin-3), STRN4 (0.998, striatin-4), MOB4 (0.998, MOB-like protein phocein—STRIPAK component), PDCD10 (0.998, programmed cell death 10/CCM3), STRN (0.997, striatin family), PPP2R1A (0.997, protein phosphatase 2A scaffold subunit A—core STRIPAK component), STK24 (0.997, serine/threonine kinase 24/MST3), STK25 (0.996, serine/threonine kinase 25), and STRIP2 (0.943, striatin-interacting protein 2). This interaction network positions CTTNBP2NL as an integral STRIPAK complex member regulating phosphorylation signaling, cytoskeletal dynamics, and cell polarity. Critically, ClinVar documents 12 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number losses spanning 1p13.3-13.1 (chr1:110066946-116672408), 1p21.3-13.2 (chr1:95046805-114714931), 1p21.2-12 (chr1:102021465-119737478), large deletions (chr1:?_112318699_115576848_?, chr1:?_111145905_114454813_?), copy number gains at 1p13.3-11.2 (chr1:111647582-121343783), 1p22.1-11.2 (chr1:93837992-121343783)x3, whole chromosome arm gains 1p36.33-q44 (chr1:849467-249224684), and intronic variants (c.331-165G>T), yet ZERO diseases are formally associated—a profound gap given the essential epidermal and cancer functions. Recent literature establishes emerging roles: STRIP1 stabilizes CTTNBP2NL to ensure proper barrier formation in developing mouse skin epidermis (2024)—demonstrating essential developmental function; CTTNBP2 (but not CTTNBP2NL) regulates dendritic spinogenesis and synaptic distribution of striatin-PP2A complex (65 citations, 2012)—establishing paralog-specific neuronal vs. non-neuronal functions; and TEAD factor 1 regulates CTTNBP2NL to control cell apoptosis in papillary thyroid carcinoma (2025)—linking to Hippo pathway and cancer biology. The mechanistic hypothesis: CTTNBP2NL functions as a STRIPAK complex scaffolding component that bridges striatin family proteins (STRN3/STRN4) to phosphatase (PPP2R1A/PP2A) and kinase (STK24/MST3, STK25) modules, regulating dephosphorylation of cytoskeletal targets controlling cell junctions, polarity, and migration. In epidermal development, CTTNBP2NL likely stabilizes adherens junctions and tight junctions through cortactin interactions, enabling proper barrier formation—CTTNBP2NL loss would cause skin permeability defects and potentially ichthyosis-like phenotypes. In thyroid cancer, CTTNBP2NL may function downstream of TEAD transcription factors (Hippo pathway effectors) regulating apoptosis resistance through cytoskeletal reorganization or PP2A-mediated dephosphorylation of survival signals. The PDCD10/CCM3 interaction suggests potential roles in vascular development and cerebral cavernous malformation pathways. The nucleolar localization indicates possible ribosome biogenesis or rRNA processing functions distinct from cytoplasmic cytoskeletal roles. The immune cell enhancement suggests functions in lymphocyte activation or migration. The absence of formal disease associations despite 12 pathogenic variants suggests CTTNBP2NL dysfunction contributes to skin barrier disorders (atopic dermatitis, ichthyosis), thyroid cancer susceptibility, vascular malformations, or immunodeficiency not yet mapped to Mendelian phenotypes. Future work should define CTTNBP2NL's specific STRIPAK complex assembly role, identify cytoskeletal substrates regulated through PP2A recruitment, investigate whether CTTNBP2NL mutations cause skin barrier defects or thyroid cancer predisposition, and determine functional divergence from neuronal CTTNBP2.
