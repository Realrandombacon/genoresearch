# CFAP61: Cilia- and Flagella-Associated Protein with FAD/NAD-Binding Domains Functioning in Axonemal Dynein Complex Assembly

## Description
CFAP61 (Cilia- and Flagella-Associated Protein 61, UniProt Q8NHU2) is a large 1237-amino acid structural protein functioning as an essential component of motile cilia and sperm flagella axonemes. The protein contains a conserved N-terminal domain (IPR032151/PF16092, residues 16-266) characteristic of the CFAP61 family (IPR038884/PTHR21178, residues 13-1235), multiple FAD/NAD(P)-binding domain folds (G3DSA:3.50.50.60/IPR036188/SSF51905, residues 665-998) that may mediate nucleotide-dependent conformational changes or redox sensing within the axoneme, and a C-terminal dimerization domain (IPR056299/PF23150, residues 1033-1151) enabling homodimer or heterodimer formation critical for complex assembly. AlphaFold predicts a highly confident well-folded structure (pLDDT=84.6) consistent with stable structural scaffolding roles within the ciliary apparatus. HPA reveals tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in skeletal muscle (26.0 nTPM), testis (14.5 nTPM), heart muscle (11.6 nTPM), and fallopian tube (11.4 nTPM) — tissues rich in motile cilia or flagella — with subcellular localization to nucleoplasm, cytosol, and critically the principal piece of sperm flagella where axonemal dynein arms generate motility. STRING interaction network reveals exceptional associations with established ciliary/axonemal proteins: MAATS1 (0.805, meiosis arrest spermatogenic 1, required for sperm flagella assembly), WDR66 (0.795, WD-repeat protein essential for ciliogenesis and sperm motility), CFAP58 (0.628, cilia-associated protein 58, component of nexin-dynein regulatory complex), THEGL (0.625, tectonic-like protein involved in ciliary transition zone), CFAP43 (0.590, cilia-associated protein 43, mutations cause primary ciliary dyskinesia), RSPH3 (0.589, radial spoke head protein 3, critical for ciliary beat regulation), and CFAP70 (0.544, cilia-associated protein 70). This interaction profile places CFAP61 within the outer dynein arm-docking complex (ODA-DC) that anchors dynein motor complexes to the axonemal microtubule doublets — a role supported by mouse ortholog studies where Cfap61/Odad2 knockout causes immotile sperm and ciliary dysfunction. ClinVar documents 36 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large chromosomal deletions (20p13-11.21) and intronic/splice-site mutations affecting CFAP61, indicating dosage sensitivity where haploinsufficiency or loss-of-function causes disease phenotypes consistent with primary ciliary dyskinesia and male infertility. The combination of FAD/NAD-binding structural domains, dimerization capacity, principal piece localization, dynein complex interaction partners, multi-tissue ciliary expression, and CNV-associated pathology supports a mechanistic model: CFAP61 functions as a structural scaffold within the outer dynein arm-docking complex, using its dimerization domain to stabilize ODA-DC assembly and its FAD/NAD-binding folds to regulate conformational states during ciliary beat cycles, with loss-of-function causing ciliary immotility through failed dynein arm anchoring to axonemal microtubules.
