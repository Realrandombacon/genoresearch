## Description
ITPRIP (Inositol 1,4,5-trisphosphate Receptor-Interacting Protein) encodes a 547-amino acid well-folded protein (AlphaFold pLDDT 78.9) that functions as a calcium signaling regulator through direct interaction with IP3 receptors. This LIGHT GRAY gene has 14 literature references, with recent 2024 work revealing its role in epilepsy through brain iron homeostasis regulation, 2021 research demonstrating glioma progression via MYL9-DAPK1 inhibition, and a seminal 2018 paper (64 citations) showing the long noncoding RNA ITPRIP-1 positively regulates innate immune response through MDA5 oligomerization and activation. InterPro analysis reveals multiple functional domains: MAB21L/Cyclic GMP-AMP synthase-like receptor family (IPR024810, residues 195-525), ITPRIP-like family signatures (IPR026250 at residues 295-309, 380-393, 399-415), Mab-21-like HhH/H2TH-like domain (IPR046906, PF20266, residues 425-479), and ITPRIP/mab-21 nucleotidyltransferase family membership (PTHR10656, residues 134-541), suggesting potential nucleotidyltransferase activity analogous to cGAS-STING pathway components. Human Protein Atlas shows tissue-enhanced expression with bone marrow enrichment (192.1 nTPM), immune cell enhancement, and vesicular subcellular localization, consistent with calcium signaling and immune function. STRING interactions reveal a comprehensive network: COQ3 (0.794 high confidence, coenzyme Q biosynthesis), RASSF3 (0.607), ITPR3 (0.602), ITPR1 (0.574), RASSF5 (0.531), LSMEM1 (0.499), CSRNP1 (0.490), RASSF1 (0.456), MDP1 (0.456), OSBPL2 (0.450), placing ITPRIP at the intersection of calcium signaling (ITPR1/3), Ras signaling (RASSF family), and lipid metabolism (OSBPL2). ClinVar documents 18 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all large copy number variations spanning 10q23.1-26.3 region including duplications and deletions, indicating dosage sensitivity though specific monogenic phenotypes remain undefined. Functional hypothesis: ITPRIP acts as a calcium signaling scaffold that bridges IP3 receptors to downstream effectors, potentially regulating calcium-dependent processes in immune cells and neurons through its MAB21 domain. Mechanistic proposal: The MAB21/H2TH domain structure suggests ITPRIP may bind nucleotides or DNA/RNA, analogous to cGAS; through interaction with ITPR1/ITPR3, ITPRIP could modulate calcium release from ER stores, affecting immune cell activation (MDA5 pathway), neuronal excitability (epilepsy), and tumor progression (glioma via MYL9-DAPK1); copy number alterations disrupt calcium homeostasis, contributing to 10q genomic disorder phenotypes including neurodevelopmental and immune dysfunction.
