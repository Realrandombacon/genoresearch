## Description
CLECL1 (UniProt Q8IZS7), or C-type lectin-like domain family 1, encodes a compact 167-amino acid type II transmembrane protein belonging to the C-type lectin receptor superfamily. The protein features a canonical C-type lectin-like/link domain (IPR016186/G3DSA:3.10.100.10, aa 104-166) and C-type lectin fold (IPR016187/SSF56436, aa 103-159) characteristic of calcium-dependent carbohydrate-binding receptors, though CLECL1 lacks the critical calcium-coordinating residues and likely functions through protein-protein rather than carbohydrate interactions. AlphaFold predicts a medium-confidence structure (global pLDDT: 53.8), indicating a partially structured protein with an ordered C-terminal lectin domain connected to a flexible N-terminal region—consistent with surface-exposed immune receptors requiring conformational plasticity for ligand engagement.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals CLECL1's integration into immune checkpoint networks: CD276 (0.559, B7-H3 costimulatory molecule), CD40 (0.514, TNF receptor superfamily member for B-cell activation), CLEC12A (0.477, myeloid inhibitory receptor), CD69 (0.450, early T-cell activation marker), CLEC2D (0.419, LOX-1 oxidized LDL receptor), CLEC4A (0.401, DC-SIGN-related), LY75 (0.401, DEC-205 endocytic receptor), CLEC1A (0.401), BTLA (0.401, B- and T-lymphocyte attenuator), and TAGAP (0.401, T-cell activation GTPase). This interaction profile positions CLECL1 within the broader C-type lectin receptor network regulating adaptive immunity.\n\nUniProt documentation confirms CLECL1 functions in mediating immune cell-cell interactions and acts as a T-cell costimulatory molecule enhancing anti-CD3-induced proliferation. ClinVar lists 37 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants—all chromosome 12p13.33 copy number gains and duplications—confirming dosage sensitivity and implicating CLECL1 in genomic disorders involving this chromosomal region. We hypothesize CLECL1 functions as an orphan C-type lectin-like receptor on T-cells or antigen-presenting cells that modulates immune synapse formation: the lectin domain engages counter-receptors (potentially CD276/B7-H3 or CD40) during T-cell priming, delivering costimulatory signals that lower the activation threshold for TCR signaling—a mechanism analogous to CD28/B7 interactions but utilizing lectin-domain scaffolding rather than Ig-superfamily architecture.
