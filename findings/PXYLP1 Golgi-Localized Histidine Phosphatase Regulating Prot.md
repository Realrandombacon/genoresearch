# PXYLP1: Golgi-Localized Histidine Phosphatase Regulating Proteoglycan Biosynthesis via Xylose Dephosphorylation

**Date:** 2026-06-26T20:58:45.024407

**Quality Score:** 8/10 (EXCELLENT)

**Confidence:** MEDIUM

## Description
PXYLP1 (Q8TE99, 480 aa) is a highly structured (AlphaFold pLDDT: 89.3) histidine acid phosphatase localized to the Golgi apparatus, essential for the terminal step of glycosaminoglycan (GAG) chain initiation. It catalyzes the dephosphorylation of 2-phosphoxylose residues on the tetrasaccharide linker region of chondroitin sulfate and heparan sulfate proteoglycans, a critical checkpoint enabling chain elongation by glycosyltransferases. InterPro analysis confirms membership in the histidine phosphatase superfamily (IPR000560, PF00328) with a catalytic domain spanning residues 87-424. STRING interactions reveal a functional cluster with GAG biosynthetic enzymes including B3GAT3 (0.592), B3GALT6 (0.516), B4GALT7 (0.496), and the kinase FAM20B (0.554), as well as unexpected high-confidence links to GALP (0.936) and ARAF (0.932), suggesting potential regulatory crosstalk with signaling pathways. HPA data shows ubiquitous expression with peak levels in the epididymis (139.9 nTPM), consistent with the high demand for sulfated proteoglycans in sperm maturation and motility. ClinVar records 23 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly copy number variations (gains/losses) on chromosome 3q, implicating PXYLP1 dosage sensitivity in developmental disorders, though specific monogenic diseases remain unassigned. With only 4 literature citations, PXYLP1 represents a genuine 'dark' enzyme whose precise regulation of the proteoglycan 'gatekeeper' step offers therapeutic potential for connective tissue disorders and cancer metastasis.
