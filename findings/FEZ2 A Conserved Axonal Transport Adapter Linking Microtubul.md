# FEZ2: A Conserved Axonal Transport Adapter Linking Microtubule Motors to Synaptic Vesicle Trafficking in Neurodevelopment

## Description
FEZ2 (Fasciculation and elongation protein zeta-2, UniProt Q9UHY8) is a 353-amino acid intracellular adapter protein that is the human ortholog of C. elegans unc-76, essential for axonal bundling and elongation during neuronal development. InterPro analysis identifies FEZ2 as a member of the conserved FEZ/zygin family (IPR011680, PF07763, aa 51-353), characterized by coiled-coil domains that mediate protein-protein interactions rather than enzymatic activity. The AlphaFold predicted structure shows confident folding (pLDDT 70.8), consistent with an elongated scaffold architecture suitable for bridging molecular complexes. STRING interaction data reveals FEZ2's position at the intersection of cytoskeletal transport and transcriptional regulation: strong partnerships with CRIM1 (0.894), a cysteine-rich transmembrane protein involved in BMP signaling and neural crest migration, and SCOC (0.887), a coiled-coil protein implicated in vesicular trafficking. Notably, FEZ2 interacts with LZTS1 (0.794), a tumor suppressor regulating cell cycle progression, and PRKCZ (0.651), an atypical protein kinase C that phosphorylates FEZ family members to activate axonal transport. Additional links to kinesin KIF6 (0.617) and Rab effector RAB11FIP2 (0.525) suggest direct coupling to microtubule-based motor complexes. HPA expression profiles show ubiquitous distribution with enrichment in immune cells and localization to nucleoli, nucleolar rim, Golgi apparatus, and cytosol—consistent with roles in both nuclear signaling and vesicular transport. ClinVar records document 24 pathogenic variants including splice-site mutations (c.904-1G>A, c.904-12del) and copy number losses at 2p22.2, though specific disease phenotypes remain to be fully characterized. We hypothesize that FEZ2 functions as a phosphorylation-regulated adapter that links cargo vesicles (via SCOC/RAB11FIP2) to kinesin motors (KIF6) for anterograde axonal transport. PRKCZ-mediated phosphorylation likely triggers cargo release at synaptic terminals. Dysregulation of this pathway would impair neuronal connectivity during development, potentially contributing to neurodevelopmental disorders and axonopathies. The nucleolar localization suggests an additional role in ribosome biogenesis or stress response, possibly coordinating protein synthesis with transport capacity.
