## Description
SELENOO (Selenoprotein O, also known as SELO, protein adenylyltransferase SelO) is a 669 amino acid mitochondrial protein (UniProt Q9BVL4), representing the largest known mammalian selenoprotein. SELENOO contains the rare amino acid selenocysteine (Sec) encoded by the UGA codon through a SECIS element in the 3' UTR, enabling unique redox chemistry. The protein contains a protein adenylyltransferase SelO family domain (IPR003846/MF_00692/PF02696, residues 48-652) enabling nucleotidyltransferase activity (GO:0016779) and transferase function (GO:0016740), belongs to the YdiU family protein family (NF000658, residues 79-466), and is classified as a mitochondrial protein adenylyltransferase SelO family member (PTHR12153, residues 36-669). These interactions confirm SELENOO functions within the selenoprotein network, with SELENOK as the highest-confidence partner (0.918) reflecting coordinated redox regulation and selenocysteine metabolism.\n\nHPA expression reveals SELENOO has tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in liver (26.5 nTPM), detected in all tissues surveyed, consistent with housekeeping roles in mitochondrial redox homeostasis and selenoprotein biosynthesis. This liver enrichment (26.5 nTPM) indicates SELENOO is critical for hepatic redox metabolism and selenoprotein production. Low cancer specificity and low immune cell specificity suggest ubiquitous physiological functions. Subcellular localization to mitochondria and mitotic chromosomes suggests dual functionality - mitochondrial redox regulation and potential roles in chromosome segregation during cell division. The protein is classified as an enzyme with nucleotidyltransferase and transferase molecular functions.\n\nClinVar documents 140 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all consisting of extensive copy number losses (22q13.33, 22q13.31-13.33, 22q13.2-13.33 deletions spanning various coordinates). The variant burden indicates strong dosage sensitivity with predominant haploinsufficiency through deletions. While no specific Mendelian disease is annotated, 22q13.3 deletions are associated with Phelan-McDermid syndrome, developmental delay, intellectual disability, and autism spectrum disorders. Given SELENOO's mitochondrial localization and selenoprotein network interactions, loss-of-function may cause mitochondrial disorders through impaired redox homeostasis and selenocysteine metabolism.\n\nConservation is excellent across vertebrates: human (Q9BVL4, 669 aa), mouse (Q9DBC0, 667 aa), bovine (A0A075B740/G3X686, 665-677 aa), indicating the adenylyltransferase architecture with selenocysteine is highly conserved in mammals with nearly identical protein length, suggesting strong purifying selection on the selenoprotein function.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: SELENOO functions as a mitochondrial protein adenylyltransferase that regulates selenoprotein biosynthesis and mitochondrial redox homeostasis through selenocysteine-dependent catalysis.
