## Description
LOC101928211 is an uncharacterized genomic locus on chromosome 7q31.33 (genomic coordinates: 124337379-124352095 on NC_000007.14, spanning approximately 14.7 kb on the reverse strand). This region lacks protein-coding evidence (no UniProt entry, no STRING interactions, no HPA protein expression data), indicating it is likely a non-coding regulatory element, long non-coding RNA, or structural genomic region rather than a protein-coding gene. ClinVar analysis identified 10 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants with predominantly deletion-associated pathogenicity: 7 copy number losses (deletions) and 3 copy number gains (duplications), indicating haploinsufficiency is the primary pathogenic mechanism with some triplosensitivity. The 7q31 chromosomal region is critically important for development and contains multiple dosage-sensitive genes including MET (hepatocyte growth factor receptor - kidney development, cancer susceptibility), BRAF (serine/threonine kinase - RAS/MAPK signaling), WNT2 (wingless signaling pathway - brain development), and FOXP2 (forkhead box P2 - speech and language development). The deletion-predominant pattern (70% losses vs 30% gains) contrasts with the more balanced mixed dosage sensitivity seen in adjacent LOC101928283 (5 deletions, 4 duplications), suggesting LOC101928211 may function as a haploinsufficiency-critical regulatory element. Loss of this element likely causes reduced expression of target genes in cis, contributing to 7q deletion syndrome phenotypes including developmental delay, intellectual disability, autism spectrum disorders, speech abnormalities (FOXP2 pathway), and renal anomalies (MET pathway). This 14.7 kb element may represent a critical enhancer, chromatin boundary, or lncRNA that precisely regulates MET or FOXP2 expression during embryonic development.
