## Description
MIOS (Meiosis regulator for oocyte development, also known as MIO, Sea4, Yulink, GATOR2 complex protein MIOS) is an 875 amino acid intracellular protein (UniProt Q9NXC5) with high-confidence well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 82.8), characteristic of stable scaffold proteins that function in multi-protein signaling complexes. The protein contains multiple defining domains: multiple WD40 repeats (IPR001680/PF21720, residues 101-146, 174-212, 214-252/42-355) that form beta-propeller structures for protein-protein interactions, a zinc-ribbon like domain (IPR031488/PF17034, residues 737-859) with modified RING finger H2 subclass (C3H3C2-type, residues 782-858) for zinc ion binding, an alpha-solenoid domain (IPR049092/PF21719, residues 394-623) that forms curved helical structures for scaffolding, a WD40/YVTN repeat-like domain superfamily (IPR015943/G3DSA:2.130.10.10, residues 67-354), and belongs to the MIOS/Sea4 family (IPR037593/PTHR16453, residues 487-867). MIOS is located on chromosome 7p21.3 and is involved in cellular response to amino acid starvation, positive regulation of TORC1 signaling, and protein-containing complex localization as part of the GATOR2 complex.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals an exceptionally high-confidence network of GATOR complex and mTORC1 pathway components: NPRL3 (0.999, Nitrogen permease regulator-like 3, GATOR1 subunit), SEC13 (0.999, Protein transport protein SEC13, GATOR2 subunit), SEH1L (0.999, SEC13 homolog 1, GATOR2 subunit), DEPDC5 (0.999, DEP domain-containing protein 5, GATOR1 subunit), RRAGA (0.998, Ras-related GTP-binding protein A, mTORC1 activator), RRAGC (0.998, Ras-related GTP-binding protein C, mTORC1 activator), NPRL2 (0.983, Nitrogen permease regulator-like 2, GATOR1 subunit), WDR24 (0.857, WD repeat-containing protein 24, GATOR2 subunit), WDR59 (0.838, WD repeat-containing protein 59, GATOR2 subunit), and KPTN (0.669, Kaptin, actin-binding protein). These interactions confirm MIOS functions as a core GATOR2 complex component that regulates mTORC1 signaling in response to amino acid availability.\n\nHPA expression reveals MIOS is tissue-enhanced with highest expression in skeletal muscle (81.5 nTPM), detected in all tissues. Subcellular localization includes nucleoplasm and cytosol, with additional localization to lysosomal membrane where mTORC1 activation occurs. The protein is classified as a predicted intracellular protein with cell type-enhanced specificity at the single-cell level. Low cancer specificity and low immune cell specificity indicate MIOS is not dysregulated in tumors or immune cells.\n\nClinVar documents 30 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including extensive copy number gains (7p22.2-21.3, 7p22.3-14.3, 7p22.3-21.3, 7p22.3-21.1 amplifications) and splice region variants (c.2532-9T>A, c.2532-12C>A, c.1884+181T>G). The variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity. While no specific Mendelian disease is annotated, 7p CNVs are associated with developmental delay, intellectual disability, growth retardation, and congenital anomalies. Given MIOS's role in mTORC1 signaling and skeletal muscle expression, dosage imbalance may disrupt nutrient sensing and protein synthesis, potentially causing growth disorders or myopathy.\n\nConservation is strong across vertebrates: human (Q9NXC5, 875 aa), mouse (Q8VE19, 875 aa), zebrafish (Q802U2, 876 aa), orangutan (Q5RCA2, 875 aa), indicating an ancient and essential function in nutrient signaling conserved from fish to primates.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: MIOS functions as a scaffold subunit of the GATOR2 complex that positively regulates mTORC1 signaling in response to amino acid availability. The WD40 repeats form a beta-propeller that binds GATOR1 subunits (DEPDC5, NPRL2, NPRL3), while the alpha-solenoid domain provides structural flexibility for complex assembly. The zinc-ribbon domain may coordinate zinc ions for structural stability or catalytic activity. GATOR2 inhibits GATOR1's GAP activity toward Rag GTPases (RRAGA, RRAGC), allowing Rag heterodimers to recruit mTORC1 to the lysosomal surface for activation. Through SEC13 and SEH1L interactions, MIOS links the GATOR complex to the nuclear pore complex, suggesting coordination between nutrient signaling and nucleocytoplasmic transport. The skeletal muscle-enriched expression (81.5 nTPM) suggests MIOS plays a key role in muscle protein synthesis and hypertrophy in response to amino acid intake. 7p CNVs cause MIOS dosage imbalance, disrupting mTORC1 signaling and potentially causing growth disorders through impaired nutrient sensing and protein synthesis. MIOS represents a candidate gene for mTORopathy disorders and a potential therapeutic target for muscle wasting conditions.
