## Description
CASTOR2 (Cytosolic Arginine Sensor for mTORC1 Subunit 2) is a 329 amino acid cytosolic protein (UniProt A6NHX0) with partial characterization (17 papers) that functions as an arginine-responsive regulator of mTORC1 signaling, controlling cell growth, muscle metabolism, and cancer progression. The protein contains two ACT (Aspartate Kinase, Chorismate Mutase, TyrA) domains (IPR027795/PF13840, residues 73-140 and 261-322) that form the arginine-binding pocket, a CASTOR family N-terminal domain (IPR040778/PF18700, residues 9-69), and an ACT-like domain (IPR049479/PF21389, residues 177-259), placing it in the cytosolic arginine sensor for mTORC1 family (IPR051719/PTHR31131, residues 1-325) with VC0802-like structural fold (G3DSA:3.30.2130.10, residues 1-148/179-329). STRING interactions reveal high-confidence partnerships with GATOR2 complex components WDR24 (0.898), WDR59 (0.895), MIOS (0.847), nucleoporin SEH1L (0.765), paralog CASTOR1 (0.742), and GATOR1 complex regulators KPTN (0.711), SZT2 (0.696), DEPDC5 (0.694), placing CASTOR2 in the mTORC1 amino acid sensing machinery. The landmark 2016 Cell study (711 citations) established CASTOR proteins as arginine sensors, while 2026 research demonstrates CASTOR1 and CASTOR2 respond to different arginine concentration thresholds to fine-tune mTORC1 activity. HPA data shows skeletal muscle-enriched expression (61.7 nTPM, tissue enhanced), cancer-enhanced pattern, and ubiquitous tissue distribution, consistent with mTORC1's role in muscle protein synthesis and tumor growth. ClinVar contains 29 pathogenic variants including copy number losses/gains at 7q11.23 (Williams syndrome critical region), though no specific diseases are directly assigned—suggesting potential metabolic, neuromuscular, or neurodevelopmental phenotypes. AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 88.6) indicates high confidence with well-folded ACT domains forming the arginine-binding pocket. Functional hypothesis: CASTOR2 acts as a cytosolic arginine sensor that binds arginine through its dual ACT domains, undergoing conformational change that disrupts CASTOR2-GATOR2 interaction, relieving GATOR2-mediated inhibition of GATOR1, which then inhibits Rag GTPases to suppress mTORC1 activation under low arginine conditions, with CASTOR1/CASTOR2 heterodimerization enabling differential arginine sensitivity thresholds for graded mTORC1 responses, skeletal muscle enrichment supporting mTORC1-dependent protein synthesis and muscle hypertrophy regulation, cancer-enhanced expression driving tumor growth through constitutive mTORC1 activation, and 7q11.23 copy number variants potentially contributing to Williams syndrome metabolic phenotypes through mTORC1 pathway dysregulation, making CASTOR2 a therapeutic target for muscle wasting disorders, cancer, and metabolic diseases.
