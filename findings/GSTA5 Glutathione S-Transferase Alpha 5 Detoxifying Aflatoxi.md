## Description
GSTA5 encodes Glutathione S-transferase alpha 5 (222 aa, Q7RTV2), a cytosolic phase II detoxification enzyme functioning in glutathione conjugation of electrophilic compounds including aflatoxin B1, alkylating agents, and toxic aldehydes as part of the GST alpha class family. InterPro analysis reveals canonical GST architecture: GST N-terminal domain (cd03077/IPR004045, positions 4-82/3-83) containing glutathione binding site; C-terminal alpha helical domain (cd03208/IPR004046, positions 86-220/100-192) forming substrate binding pocket; Glutathione S-transferase alpha class family (IPR003080, positions 15-29/82-98/134-148) defining catalytic specificity; Thioredoxin-like superfamily (IPR036249, positions 4-80) providing structural scaffold; Glutathione transferase family (IPR040079, positions 5-203) spanning most of protein. Functional hypothesis: GSTA5 functions as phase II detoxification enzyme catalyzing glutathione conjugation of electrophilic carcinogens and toxins through conserved GST active site; N-terminal domain (positions 4-82) binds glutathione while C-terminal domain (positions 86-220) binds hydrophobic substrates enabling nucleophilic attack on electrophiles. Mechanistic proposal: GSTA5 forms part of coordinated phase I/II detoxification system with CYP450 enzymes (CYP1A1/3A4/2E1/2C9/2A13/2A6 interactions 0.911-0.928) performing sequential metabolism - CYPs oxidize substrates creating reactive intermediates that GSTA5 conjugates with glutathione for excretion; EPHX1 interaction (0.933) links GSTA5 to epoxide detoxification pathway critical for aflatoxin B1 and polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbon metabolism; GSTA2 interaction (0.958) suggests functional redundancy or heterodimer formation within alpha class GSTs; GSS interaction (0.918) connects GSTA5 to glutathione biosynthesis ensuring substrate availability; GSR interaction (0.450) links to glutathione recycling maintaining redox balance; 1996 antioxidant-responsive element study demonstrates GSTA5 inducible by oxidative stress via Nrf2/ARE pathway enabling adaptive chemoprotection; 2002 alkylating agent resistance study shows GSTA5 overexpression protects cells from DNA-damaging chemotherapy agents suggesting role in cancer chemoresistance; 2018 aflatoxin B1 review positions GSTA5 in hepatocarcinogen detoxification explaining liver cancer susceptibility variants; 9 ClinVar CNVs at 6p GST cluster locus indicate GSTA5 dosage affects detoxification capacity - deletions may increase toxin sensitivity while amplifications may confer chemoresistance; very high AlphaFold confidence (97.2) reflects conserved GST fold enabling structure-based drug design; restricted tissue expression suggests specialized detoxification roles in specific cell types; cytosol localization enables access to both cytoplasmic and nuclear substrates; GSTA5 represents important but understudied detoxification enzyme (70 papers vs hundreds for GSTA1/GSTM1) with roles in aflatoxin B1 metabolism, alkylating agent resistance, toxic aldehyde clearance, and liver disease susceptibility through glutathione conjugation chemistry.
