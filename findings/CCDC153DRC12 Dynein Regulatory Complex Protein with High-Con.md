## Description
CCDC153 (Coiled-coil domain containing 153), also known as DRC12 (Dynein regulatory complex protein 12, UniProt Q494R4), is a 210-amino acid intracellular protein located on chromosome 11q23.3. InterPro analysis reveals membership in the dynein regulatory complex protein 12-like family (IPR033585, aa 1-199) and coiled-coil domain-containing protein 153 family (PTHR28656, aa 1-199), with CATH-Gene3D superfamily classification (G3DSA:1.10.287.1490, aa 11-189) indicating a structured coiled-coil architecture typical of dynein regulatory complex components. AlphaFold structure prediction achieves high confidence (pLDDT 88.3), indicating a well-folded stable structure with defined coiled-coil regions suitable for protein-protein interactions within the dynein motor complex. Human Protein Atlas shows tissue-enhanced expression with choroid plexus enrichment (23.3 nTPM) and fallopian tube expression (22.8 nTPM), cytosolic subcellular localization, detected in many tissues, immune cell enriched, and group enriched cell type specificity — suggesting roles in ciliated epithelia where dynein function is critical for cerebrospinal fluid flow and oviduct transport. STRING interaction network reveals 9 partners spanning transcriptional regulation and ciliary function: MLF1 (0.579, myeloid leukemia factor 1), LYPD2 (0.577, LY6/PLAUR domain-containing), DAPL1 (0.563, death-associated protein-like 1), LZTFL1 (0.523, leucine zipper transcription factor-like 1, known ciliopathy gene), FOXI1 (0.492, forkhead box I1 transcription factor), ASCL3 (0.482, achaete-scute family bHLH transcription factor), KRT4 (0.438, keratin 4), TPPP3 (0.434, tubulin polymerization promoting protein family member 3), and CCDC113 (0.430, coiled-coil domain-containing 113, ciliary protein). This interactome positions CCDC153/DRC12 within the dynein regulatory complex that controls ciliary beating and intracellular transport. ClinVar contains 29 pathogenic variants including massive copy-number gains and deletions across 11q23.3-25, 11q23.1-24.3, and 11p13-q25, demonstrating dosage sensitivity where genomic rearrangements disrupting this region cause disease phenotypes potentially related to ciliary dysfunction. Conservation across mammals (mouse P0C7Q1, 202 aa; rat Q5FVL4, 202 aa; bovine Q0P5D1, 204 aa) and zebrafish (A8KB59, 202 aa) indicates ancient evolutionary origin tied to fundamental dynein regulation. CCDC153/DRC12 likely functions as a structural component of the dynein regulatory complex (DRC) that modulates dynein motor activity in cilia and flagella, coordinating microtubule-based transport and ciliary beating essential for cerebrospinal fluid circulation, mucociliary clearance, and reproductive tract function.
