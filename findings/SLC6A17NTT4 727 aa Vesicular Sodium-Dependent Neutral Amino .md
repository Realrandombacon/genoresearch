## Description
SLC6A17 (Q9H1V8, 727 aa), also known as NTT4 (Na+-dependent Transporter 4), is a specialized member of the SLC6 neurotransmitter transporter family functioning as a vesicular transporter for neutral amino acids including proline, glycine, leucine, and alanine. InterPro identifies a canonical LeuT-fold architecture characteristic of the sodium:solute symporter family: a core sodium:neurotransmitter symporter domain (IPR000175, PF00209, PS50267, aa 47-646) forming 12 transmembrane helices with a central substrate-binding site, flanked by extensive N- and C-terminal cytoplasmic domains unique to this subfamily. Specific signatures include the neutral amino acid SLC6 transporter motifs (IPR002438, aa 195-219, 357-367) and the broader sodium:neurotransmitter symporter superfamily (IPR037272, cd11521, aa 60-648). AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (pLDDT 81.0, residues 1-727), indicating a stable, well-folded protein with the characteristic inverted repeat topology of SLC6 transporters, but with unusually long intracellular loops likely involved in vesicle targeting or regulatory interactions. HPA shows group-enriched expression with highest levels in brain (53.7 nTPM) and pituitary gland (23.8 nTPM); localized to mitochondria (though functional consensus places it on synaptic vesicles); classified as a disease-related gene, potential drug target, and transporter involved in amino acid/neurotransmitter symport; associated with intellectual disability. STRING reveals 10 interactions linking it to neuronal development and transport: UBL4B (0.926, ubiquitin-like protein), ALX3 (0.841, aristaless-like homeobox 3, a transcription factor), TEX15 (0.762, testis-expressed protein), UBL4A (0.547), FAM135B (0.512), SLC7A5 (0.481, large neutral amino acid transporter LAT1), SLC29A3 (0.462, equilibrative nucleoside transporter), SLC1A4 (0.457, alanine/serine/cysteine transporter), RTN4RL1 (0.455, reticulon receptor), and SLC32A1 (0.450, vesicular inhibitory amino acid transporter VIAAT). The strong link to SLC32A1 and SLC7A5 suggests coordinated handling of inhibitory neurotransmitter precursors. ClinVar contains 20 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number losses (chr1:110066946-116672408, chr1:109483388-112293512, chr1:95046805-114714931) and deletions affecting the 1p13 region, suggesting dosage sensitivity; although no specific monogenic disease is explicitly named beyond 'intellectual disability', the variant burden and brain expression implicate it in neurodevelopmental disorders (MRT48 locus). Functional hypothesis: SLC6A17 functions as a vesicular transporter that loads synaptic vesicles with neutral amino acids (proline, glycine, leucine, alanine) using the proton gradient, distinct from plasma membrane reuptake transporters. Unlike classic neurotransmitter transporters (e.g., GAT, DAT), NTT4 may regulate the quantal size of amino acid release or provide substrates for local protein synthesis at synapses. The long cytoplasmic tails likely interact with vesicle trafficking machinery (UBL4B) and transcriptional regulators (ALX3) to couple vesicle filling with developmental programs. Disruption via CNVs leads to haploinsufficiency, impairing synaptic transmission plasticity and causing intellectual disability and potentially autism spectrum features due to altered excitatory/inhibitory balance. Conservation across vertebrates (mouse 727 aa, rat 727 aa, zebrafish ~730 aa) confirms essential neurological role.
