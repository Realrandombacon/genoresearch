## Description
ZNF366 encodes a 744-amino acid C2H2 zinc finger transcription factor containing multiple tandem C2H2-type zinc finger DNA-binding domains (PF00096, residues 254-275, 281-303, 309-331, 505-527) and additional zinc finger regions (PF13912, IPR013087, IPR036236). This domain architecture classifies ZNF366 as a sequence-specific DNA-binding protein within the KRAB-ZNF superfamily, functioning as a transcriptional repressor. HPA data shows cancer-enhanced expression with nucleoplasmic subcellular localization, consistent with chromatin-associated transcriptional regulation. The gene is detected in many tissues with immune cell enrichment, suggesting roles in both housekeeping and immune-specific gene regulation. STRING interactions identify 10 medium-confidence partners including NRIP1 (0.587), a nuclear receptor co-regulator that bridges transcription factors with chromatin modifiers; CTBP1 (0.442), a transcriptional corepressor known to interact with zinc finger proteins; and ESR1 (0.487), the estrogen receptor, suggesting potential hormone-responsive regulatory functions. Additional partners include DCLK3 (0.545), TMEM171 (0.536), PSMA3 (0.526), and other zinc finger proteins (ZNF654, 0.455), indicating operation within multi-protein repression complexes. ClinVar documents 6 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all copy number losses or gains on chromosome 5q encompassing the ZNF366 locus, demonstrating dosage sensitivity and potential disease mechanisms through gene amplification or deletion. The combination of C2H2 zinc finger architecture, nuclear receptor co-regulator interactions (NRIP1, ESR1), and cancer-enhanced expression supports a hypothesis that ZNF366 functions as a ligand-modulated transcriptional repressor, potentially regulating hormone-responsive genes in reproductive tissues or oncogenic pathways in cancer. Disruption through copy number variation may cause disease via altered chromatin remodeling or dysregulated hormone signaling.
