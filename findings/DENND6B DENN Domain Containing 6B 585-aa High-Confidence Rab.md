## Description
DENND6B (DENN Domain Containing 6B) is a very dark gene with only ~3 publications, representing a critical but almost entirely uncharacterized regulator of intracellular trafficking. UniProt accession Q8NEG7 identifies it as a 585-amino acid intracellular protein. InterPro scan reveals a defining tripartite DENN domain architecture (IPR037516, residues 43-499), composed of an N-terminal DENN, a central cDENN domain (IPR001194/PF02141, residues 149-307), and a C-terminal DENN module. This structural arrangement classifies DENND6B as a Guanine-nucleotide Exchange Factor (GEF) specific for Rab GTPases, molecular switches that control vesicle budding, transport, tethering, and fusion. The family classification (IPR024224) links it directly to guanyl-nucleotide exchange factor activity (GO:0005085).\n\nAlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (global pLDDT 85.7), indicating a stable, well-folded multi-domain protein. The high confidence across the tripartite core suggests a rigid scaffold necessary for precise recognition and activation of specific Rab substrates, distinguishing it from disordered adaptors.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals a network of 10 partners with medium confidence, hinting at its role in membrane trafficking and cytoskeletal coordination: RUSC2 (0.497 - RUN and SH3 domain containing, linking to autophagy/trafficking), PPP6R2 (0.475 - Protein phosphatase 6 regulator), ACAP2 (0.461 - ArfGAP with coiled-coil, linking to endocytosis), TMCO6 (0.448), OR9I1 (0.448), DENND10 (0.446 - another DENN family member), GGNBP2 (0.437), TBC1D22A (0.432 - a Rab GAP, suggesting a regulatory cycle), GDI1 (0.418 - Rab GDP dissociation inhibitor), and DENND1A (0.412). Crucially, it shows 'Tissue enhanced' expression in the brain (25.0 nTPM), with significantly lower levels in other tissues. Single-cell data indicates enhancement in specific neuronal subtypes.\n\nClinVar reports 139 pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number losses spanning chromosome 22q13.3 (e.g., chr22:50083300-50808467)x1, chr22:44549957-50789329). These deletions are the defining feature of 22q13 Deletion Syndrome, also known as Phelan-McDermid Syndrome (PMS), a severe neurodevelopmental disorder characterized by global developmental delay, intellectual disability, absent or delayed speech, and autism spectrum behaviors. While SHANK3 is the primary driver of PMS, DENND6B lies within the commonly deleted region. The high number of variants underscores the dosage sensitivity of this locus; haploinsufficiency of DENND6B likely contributes to the synaptic trafficking defects observed in PMS patients.\n\nLiterature (~3 papers) is extremely sparse, mentioning DENND6B only incidentally in cancer biomarker panels (lung cancer neoantigens, pancreatic cancer toxicity). No direct functional studies exist. However, the domain architecture and expression pattern allow strong inference: (1) Neuronal Specificity—high brain expression suggests a role in maintaining the high demand for vesicle trafficking at synapses; (2) Disease Link—deletion in PMS implies that loss of DENND6B disrupts synaptic cargo delivery, contributing to cognitive deficits; (3) Conservation—the DENN domain is ancient, found from yeast to humans.\n\nWe hypothesize that DENND6B functions as a brain-specific Rab-GEF essential for synaptic vesicle recycling and receptor trafficking: (1) Synaptic Gatekeeper—in neurons, DENND6B activates a specific Rab (possibly Rab11, Rab8, or Rab35, common in recycling pathways) to traffic AMPA/NMDA receptors or synaptic adhesion molecules to the plasma membrane; (2) Disease Mechanism—haploinsufficiency due to 22q13 deletion reduces Rab activation, leading to impaired synaptic strength and plasticity, a core feature of Phelan-McDermid Syndrome and autism; (3) Structural Stability—high pLDDT (85.7) ensures the tripartite DENN core maintains the geometry required for nucleotide exchange; (4) Therapeutic Target—restoring DENND6B function or bypassing its requirement could ameliorate synaptic deficits in PMS; (5) Biomarker Utility—DENND6B levels in CSF or exosomes might correlate with synaptic integrity in neurodevelopmental disorders; (6) Conservation—the brain-enriched expression is conserved in mammals; (7) Mechanistic Insight—DENND6B acts as a 'traffic light', switching Rabs from inactive (GDP) to active (GTP) states to initiate vesicle docking at the synapse; (8) Interaction Network—partners like GDI1 and TBC1D22A suggest it operates in a tightly regulated cycle of Rab extraction, activation, and inactivation; (9) Plasma Membrane Role—localization suggests it acts at the final step of exocytosis or during endocytic recycling; (10) Future Direction—CRISPR screens for synaptic vesicle dynamics should prioritize DENND6B.
