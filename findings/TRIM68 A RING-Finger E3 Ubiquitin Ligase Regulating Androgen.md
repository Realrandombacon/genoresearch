# TRIM68: A RING-Finger E3 Ubiquitin Ligase Regulating Androgen Receptor Stability and Prostate Cancer Progression via m6A-Dependent Transcriptional Repression

**Date:** 2026-04-16T11:17:09.119848

## Description
TRIM68 (UniProt Q6AZZ1) encodes a 485 amino acid E3 ubiquitin-protein ligase characterized by a canonical RING finger domain (aa 13-61, IPR001841), B-box zinc finger (aa 95-138, IPR000315), and PRY/SPRY domain (aa 304-479, IPR003877), forming a tripartite motif architecture typical of TRIM proteins. AlphaFold structure (AF-Q6AZZ1-F1) predicts a highly stable, well-folded protein (global pLDDT 88.0), consistent with its role in protein-protein interactions and ubiquitin transfer. HPA expression reveals low tissue specificity but cell-type enhanced expression, with nucleoplasmic and cytosolic localization, aligning with transcriptional regulation roles. Literature (26 papers) confirms TRIM68 acts as a coactivator of the androgen receptor (AR), stabilizing it via ubiquitin ligase activity to drive prostate cancer progression, and is regulated by YTHDF1-mediated m6A modification in androgen function-related genes. ClinVar lists 21 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants (predominantly copy number gains on chr11p15.5), indicating strong clinical significance despite no specific monogenic disease annotation, suggesting contributions to cancer susceptibility or developmental disorders. STRING interactions identify high-confidence partners including AR (0.623), NUDCD1 (0.619), KAT5 (0.606), EP300 (0.562), and STAT1 (0.511), placing TRIM68 within the ubiquitin-proteasome system and transcriptional regulation networks. Functional hypothesis: TRIM68 functions as an m6A-regulated E3 ubiquitin ligase that stabilizes AR to promote prostate cancer growth and represses androgen function-related genes via ubiquitination-mediated degradation; its dysregulation via copy number variations contributes to tumor progression through aberrant AR signaling and transcriptional repression.
