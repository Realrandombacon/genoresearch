## Description
ABTB2 (Ankyrin Repeat and BTB Domain Containing 2, alias ABTB2A, BTBD22, CCA3) is a 1025 amino acid intracellular scaffold protein (UniProt Q8N961) with a high-confidence folded structure (AlphaFold pLDDT 76.4) containing N-terminal histone-fold domain, central ankyrin repeats, and C-terminal BTB/POZ-BACK domains, functioning as a CUL3 E3 ubiquitin ligase adaptor that regulates protein homeostasis, cellular response to toxic substances, and ciliary function through substrate recruitment and ubiquitin-dependent proteasomal degradation.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals ABTB2 contains multi-domain scaffold architecture: an N-terminal histone-fold domain (IPR009072/G3DSA:1.10.20.10, residues 104-300) mediating protein heterodimerization (GO:0046982), central ankyrin repeats (IPR002110/G3DSA:1.25.40.20, residues 521-553/566-635/649-678) for protein-protein interactions, and C-terminal BTB/POZ domain (IPR000210/cd18350/G3DSA:3.30.710.10, residues 838-947) for CUL3 binding. The BACK domain (cd18526, residues 947-1025) extends the BTB module for substrate recognition. The protein belongs to the SKP1/BTB/POZ superfamily (IPR011333). GO annotations include protein binding (GO:0005515) and protein heterodimerization activity (GO:0046982). The high-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 76.4) confirms a stable multi-domain architecture optimized for simultaneous binding of CUL3 and substrate proteins.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places ABTB2 within the ubiquitin ligase-ciliary network: strongest interactions with DEPDC7 (0.536, DEP domain-containing protein 7, mTORC1 regulator), CUL3 (0.429, cullin-3, E3 ubiquitin ligase scaffold), IFT46 (0.440, intraflagellar transport protein 46, ciliary assembly), TTC30B (0.417, tetratricopeptide repeat protein 30B, ciliary), LRRC42 (0.412, leucine-rich repeat-containing protein 42, ciliary/centrosomal), CKAP5 (0.410, cytoskeleton-associated protein 5, microtubule plus-end tracking), and TCP11L1 (0.406, t-complex protein 11-like 1). The CUL3 partnership (0.429) confirms ABTB2 functions as a BTB-CUL3 E3 ubiquitin ligase adaptor. DEPDC7 interaction (0.536) links ABTB2 to mTORC1 signaling regulation. IFT46-TTC30B-LRRC42 partnerships indicate ciliary/centrosomal localization and function in intraflagellar transport.\n\nHPA expression shows tissue-enhanced specificity with brain enrichment (21.6 nTPM), consistent with neuronal requirements for protein homeostasis and ciliary function. Detected in many tissues indicates broad housekeeping function. Cancer-enhanced specificity suggests dysregulation in tumors through disrupted ubiquitination. Subcellular localization in nucleoplasm and vesicles confirms nuclear-cytoplasmic distribution with membrane association. Classification as metabolic protein supports roles in protein turnover.\n\nClinVar documents 20 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large chromosome 11p copy number losses and gains (11p14.1-13, 11p14.3-12, 11p15.1-12, 11p13-12, 11p13-q25, 11p15.3-13, 11p14.3-13, 11p15.5-q25) and one splice acceptor variant (c.1398-1G>A). The high CNV burden indicates extreme dosage sensitivity, where haploinsufficiency causes neurodevelopmental disorders through disrupted protein homeostasis, aberrant mTORC1 signaling, and ciliary dysfunction.\n\nConservation is strong across mammals: human (Q8N961, 1025 aa), mouse (Q7TQI7, 1024 aa), rat (O08764, 1009 aa), indicating evolutionary conservation of BTB-adaptor functions.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: ABTB2 functions as a BTB-CUL3 E3 ubiquitin ligase adaptor that recruits specific substrates for ubiquitin-dependent degradation. The BTB/POZ domain (residues 838-947) binds CUL3, assembling the E3 ligase complex. The BACK domain (residues 947-1025) and ankyrin repeats (residues 521-678) recognize substrate proteins through protein-protein interactions. The histone-fold domain (residues 104-300) mediates heterodimerization, potentially with ABTB3 or related proteins. DEPDC7 partnership (0.536) suggests ABTB2 regulates mTORC1 signaling through DEPDC7 ubiquitination. IFT46-TTC30B-LRRC42 interactions (0.440/0.417/0.412) position ABTB2 at cilia/centrosomes, regulating ciliary protein turnover during intraflagellar transport. CKAP5 interaction (0.410) links ABTB2 to microtubule dynamics. The stable structure (pLDDT 76.4) enables simultaneous CUL3 and substrate binding. Brain enrichment (21.6 nTPM) reflects neuronal dependence on precise protein homeostasis. 11p CNVs cause ABTB2 haploinsufficiency, leading to substrate accumulation, disrupted mTORC1 signaling, ciliary defects, and neurodevelopmental pathology. ABTB2 represents a critical BTB-CUL3 adaptor linking ubiquitination to ciliary function and neuronal proteostasis.
