# ARPC1B: An Immune-Specific Arp2/3 Complex Subunit Orchestrating Actin Nucleation in Lymphoid Cells and Platelet Function

**Quality Score:** 9/10 (EXCELLENT)

## Description
ARPC1B (Actin-related protein 2/3 complex subunit 1B, UniProt O15143) is a 372-amino acid essential component of the Arp2/3 actin-nucleating complex, serving as a paralog-specific subunit that confers tissue-restricted functionality to this conserved molecular machine. InterPro analysis reveals a canonical WD40 repeat architecture (IPR001680, positions 41-170) forming a beta-propeller scaffold, embedded within the Arp2/3 complex subunit 1 family domain (IPR017383, PF00400, aa 3-361). The AlphaFold predicted structure exhibits exceptional confidence (pLDDT 92.4), confirming a rigid, well-folded WD40 propeller ideal for multi-protein interactions. STRING interaction data places ARPC1B at the core of the heptameric Arp2/3 complex with near-perfect associations to all subunits: ACTR2 (0.999), ARPC4 (0.999), ARPC3 (0.998), ARPC2 (0.997), ACTR3 (0.996), and its paralog ARPC1A (0.966). Critically, ARPC1B links strongly to WAS (Wiskott-Aldrich syndrome protein, 0.981) and WASL (0.919), the NPF (nucleation-promoting factor) activators that trigger actin polymerization in response to Rho-GTPase signaling. HPA expression profiles show striking lymphoid tissue enhancement (288.1 nTPM)—among the highest tissue-specific expression values observed—with subcellular localization to vesicles and cytosol, consistent with roles in immune cell migration and phagocytosis. ClinVar records document 58 pathogenic variants including frameshifts (p.Ala15_Trp16insTer), splice defects, and deletions, which cause Immunodeficiency 71 (IMD71) characterized by combined immunodeficiency, platelet dysfunction, and autoinflammation. We hypothesize that ARPC1B-containing Arp2/3 complexes are specifically recruited to immune cell leading edges and phagocytic cups via WAS/WASL activation, where they nucleate branched actin networks essential for chemotaxis, antigen presentation, and platelet granule release. The paralog ARPC1A may serve housekeeping functions in non-immune tissues, while ARPC1B evolved specialized regulatory interfaces for hematopoietic signaling pathways. Loss of ARPC1B disrupts actin-driven membrane remodeling in lymphocytes and megakaryocytes, leading to immunodeficiency and thrombocytopenia.
