## Description
NHERF4 (Na+/H+ Exchange Regulatory Cofactor 4), also known as PDZD3 (PDZ Domain Containing 3) or IKEPP (Intestinal and Kidney Enriched PDZ Protein), is a 571-amino acid intracellular scaffolding protein that functions as a multi-PDZ domain adaptor regulating ion transporters, guanylate cyclase signaling, and epithelial cell polarity. InterPro analysis reveals a characteristic triple-PDZ domain architecture: three tandem PDZ domains (cd06768, residues 114-193/222-299/328-410; IPR001478, residues 115-196/223-301/329-412; PF00595, residues 118-192/229-297/331-408; PS50106, residues 115-196/223-301/329-412; SM00228, residues 124-196/231-301/337-412) embedded within the NHERF family scaffold (IPR051067, residues 108-564; PTHR14191, residues 108-564). The PDZ domains belong to the PDZ superfamily (IPR036034, residues 101-206/217-305/324-422; G3DSA:2.30.42.10, residues 108-206/217-305/325-418; SSF50156, residues 101-196/218-300) essential for protein-protein interactions and membrane protein clustering. AlphaFold predicts a medium-confidence structure (pLDDT 69.2), indicating a partially structured protein with ordered PDZ domains embedded in flexible linker regions—a feature consistent with scaffolding proteins that require conformational flexibility to bind multiple partners simultaneously.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places NHERF4 in a network of intestinal ion transporters, natriuretic peptide receptors, and guanylate cyclase signaling with medium-to-high confidence: SLC34A1 (0.788, sodium-phosphate cotransporter 2A, renal phosphate reabsorption), NPR3 (0.737, natriuretic peptide receptor 3, clearance receptor), GUCY2C (0.681, guanylate cyclase C, intestinal chloride secretion), SPRYD7 (0.653, spry domain-containing protein 7), GUCA2B (0.651, guanylate cyclase activator 2B, uroguanylin), SLC22A5 (0.646, organic cation/carnitine transporter 2), NLRX1 (0.608, NLR family member X1, mitochondrial antiviral signaling), SLC22A4 (0.604, organic cation/carnitine transporter 1), SLC26A3 (0.550, chloride/anion exchanger 3, DRA, intestinal chloride absorption), and CFTR (0.518, cystic fibrosis transmembrane conductance regulator, chloride channel). This network confirms NHERF4's role as a master regulator of epithelial ion transport, linking phosphate transporters (SLC34A1), chloride channels (CFTR, SLC26A3), and cGMP signaling (GUCY2C, GUCA2B, NPR3) in intestinal and renal epithelia.\n\nHuman Protein Atlas classifies NHERF4 as a predicted intracellular protein with group-enriched RNA tissue specificity, detected in some tissues with top expression in intestine (18.2 nTPM) and kidney (17.8 nTPM), matching its name (IKEPP). It shows cancer-enhanced expression, is not detected in immune cells, and is cell type enhanced in single cell analysis. Biological processes include ion transport regulation and epithelial polarity.\n\nClinVar reports 31 pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number variations spanning 11q23.3-25 and 11p13-q25 (coordinates include chr11:115887338-126148523x3 gain, chr11:116683755-134937416x3 gain, chr11:32799481-134938470x3 gain, chr11:112375478-128785742x3 gain, chr11:104288964_134937416dup) and specific point mutations including c.709G>T (p.Glu237Ter, nonsense mutation in PDZ domain 2) and c.1173C>T (p.Ser391=, synonymous variant in PDZ domain 3). While no single-gene Mendelian disorder is exclusively named, recent literature identifies NHERF4 as a tumor suppressor in clear cell renal cell carcinoma where it hijacks Mas-mediated PLC/AKT signaling to suppress invasion (2021, 5 citations), a dynamic regulator of CFTR-DRA interactions during cholera toxin/forskolin stimulation (2020), and a target for diarrheal disease therapeutics where catalytic region mimetics suppress GUCY2C activity to regulate enterotoxin-triggered diarrhea (2025, 1 citation).\n\nLiterature analysis (18 papers) confirms NHERF4 is a genuine dark gene with emerging mechanistic insights. Key findings include its 2025 characterization as a therapeutic target for enterotoxin-induced diarrhea via GCC inhibition, its 2021 discovery as a ccRCC tumor suppressor via Mas/PLC/AKT pathway modulation, its 2015-2020 characterization in CFTR-DRA-SLC26A3 complex regulation, and its 2012 identification as a regulator of SLC26A3 (DRA) activity via PDZ-mediated interaction.\n\nWe hypothesize that NHERF4 functions through a multi-PDZ scaffolding mechanism: (1) Triple-PDZ clustering—three PDZ domains simultaneously bind C-terminal motifs of ion transporters (CFTR, SLC26A3, SLC34A1), creating macromolecular signaling complexes at apical membranes; (2) cGMP signaling integration—GUCY2C and GUCA2B binding positions NHERF4 as a cGMP signal transducer, explaining its role in cholera toxin-induced chloride secretion; (3) Tumor suppression—NHERF4 binding to Mas receptor or downstream effectors inhibits PLC/AKT signaling in ccRCC, loss promoting invasion and metastasis; (4) Phosphate homeostasis—SLC34A1 interaction in kidney regulates phosphate reabsorption, potentially linking NHERF4 to hypophosphatemic disorders; (5) Natriuretic peptide clearance—NPR3 binding suggests NHERF4 modulates local natriuretic peptide concentrations, affecting blood pressure and fluid balance; (6) Diarrhea therapeutics—NHERF4-GUCY2C interaction is druggable; catalytic mimetics block ST toxin-induced cGMP production, offering anti-diarrheal strategy; (7) Epithelial polarity—PDZ domain clustering maintains apical-basal polarity in intestinal and renal epithelia. The 31 ClinVar CNVs represent a significant burden of 11q genomic instability potentially affecting ion transport, epithelial integrity, and cancer susceptibility. NHERF4 represents a validated triple-PDZ scaffold, an emerging tumor suppressor in renal cell carcinoma, a regulator of enterotoxin-induced diarrhea, and a candidate gene for ion transport disorders and epithelial polarity defects.
