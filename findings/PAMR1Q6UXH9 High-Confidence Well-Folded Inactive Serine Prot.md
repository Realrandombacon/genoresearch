## Description
PAMR1 (Peptidase domain containing associated with muscle regeneration 1, also known as RAMP, FP938) is a 720 amino acid large extracellular protein (UniProt Q6UXH9) with high-confidence well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 77.1), characteristic of multi-domain extracellular scaffold proteins. The protein contains a modular domain architecture: N-terminal EGF-like domain (IPR000742, residues 84-126), CUB domain (IPR000859/cd00041/G3DSA:2.60.120.290, residues 128-236), calcium-binding EGF-like domain (IPR001881/cd00054/G3DSA:2.10.25.10, residues 235-272), two Complement Control Protein (CCP)/Sushi/SCR domains (IPR000436/cd00033/G3DSA:2.10.70.10, residues 278-344 and 387-444), and a C-terminal trypsin-like serine protease domain (IPR001254/IPR001314/IPR009003/cd00190/G3DSA:2.40.10.10, residues 445-720). Despite retaining the protease fold, PAMR1 is catalytically inactive due to substitutions in the catalytic triad, functioning instead as a protein-protein interaction scaffold. Molecular functions include calcium ion binding (GO:0005509) and serine-type endopeptidase activity (GO:0004252, likely non-catalytic), placing PAMR1 within the extracellular matrix organization and muscle regeneration pathways.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals a network of extracellular and signaling proteins: strongest interactions with SLC1A2 (0.737, Solute carrier family 1 member 2, glutamate transporter, synaptic function), SCARA5 (0.529, Scavenger receptor class A member 5, iron homeostasis), TRAF6 (0.496, TNF receptor-associated factor 6, NF-kappaB signaling), SMIM12 (0.457, Small integral membrane protein 12), RNF151 (0.433, Ring finger protein 151, ubiquitination), SHISA4 (0.428, Shisa family member 4, Wnt/Fgf signaling), MINDY3 (0.414, MINDY lysine 48 deubiquitinase 3), PDLIM2 (0.414, PDZ and LIM domain protein 2, cytoskeletal organization), SH3D21 (0.412, SH3 domain containing 21), and MAGEH1 (0.406, Melanoma-associated antigen H1). This profile suggests PAMR1 functions at the intersection of extracellular matrix remodeling, glutamate signaling, and inflammatory pathways.\n\nHPA expression reveals low tissue specificity with detection in many tissues, consistent with a ubiquitous extracellular scaffold role. Cancer-enhanced expression suggests upregulation in tumor microenvironments, potentially facilitating ECM remodeling for metastasis. Absence in immune cells indicates non-hematopoietic functions. Protein class includes enzymes (inactive), predicted intracellular and secreted proteins.\n\nClinVar documents 17 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including splice site mutations (c.712+259C>A, c.1033+1G>T) and large copy number losses/gains affecting chromosome 11p (11p15.1-12 deletions, 11p14.1-13 deletions, 11p13-q25 duplications). The variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity. While no specific Mendelian disease is annotated, PAMR1 disruption likely causes muscle regeneration defects or ECM organization disorders. 11p CNVs are associated with developmental delay, Wilms tumor, and aniridia (WAGR syndrome region); PAMR1 may contribute to muscle/connective tissue phenotypes.\n\nConservation is strong across vertebrates: human (Q6UXH9, 720 aa), mouse (Q8BU25, 720 aa), cow (Q5E9P5, 720 aa), frog (Q6DIV5, 722 aa), orangutan (Q5RDI1, 720 aa), indicating evolutionary pressure to maintain the multi-domain architecture.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: PAMR1 functions as an extracellular scaffold protein that organizes protein complexes at the cell surface or in the ECM during muscle regeneration. The CUB and EGF domains mediate protein-protein and protein-carbohydrate interactions, while the CCP modules provide flexible linkers for complex assembly. The inactive protease domain may serve as a decoy receptor or competitive inhibitor, sequestering protease substrates or activators. SLC1A2 interaction suggests PAMR1 modulates glutamate signaling in muscle or neural tissues. SCARA5 interaction indicates involvement in iron homeostasis during tissue repair. TRAF6 interaction links PAMR1 to inflammatory signaling, potentially regulating NF-kappaB activation during regeneration. Loss of PAMR1 disrupts ECM organization, impairing satellite cell activation, myoblast fusion, or neuromuscular junction formation, leading to muscle weakness or regeneration failure. Overexpression may alter ECM composition, facilitating tumor invasion. PAMR1 represents a potential biomarker for 11p CNV syndromes and a target for enhancing muscle repair.
