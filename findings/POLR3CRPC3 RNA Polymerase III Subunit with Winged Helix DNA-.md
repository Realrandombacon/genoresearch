## Description
POLR3C (RNA Polymerase III Subunit C, also known as RPC3) is a 534-amino acid essential intracellular protein (UniProt Q9BUI4) representing a genuine dark gene with only 16 publications despite its critical established roles as a core subunit of RNA Polymerase III regulating tRNA and 5S rRNA transcription, antiviral innate immunity via mitochondrial DNA sensing, varicella zoster virus susceptibility, SARS-CoV-2 infection severity, autism spectrum disorder, and 1q21.1 copy number variation disorders. Structurally, POLR3C contains multiple winged helix DNA-binding domains defining the RPC3 architecture: RNA polymerase III RPC82 C-terminal domain (IPR008806/PF05645, residues 146-345) forming the core structural scaffold, RNA polymerase III subunit RPC82-related helix-turn-helix domain (IPR013197/PF08221, residues 7-66) mediating protein-protein interactions within the Pol III holoenzyme, three winged helix-like DNA-binding domain superfamilies (G3DSA:1.10.10.10/IPR036388, residues 1-78+79-166+238-337) enabling single-stranded DNA recognition and transcription bubble stabilization, DNA-directed RNA polymerase III subunit RPC3 family (IPR039748, residues 1-526) defining functional class, RPC3 winged-helix domain (IPR055207, residues 350-426) for downstream DNA binding, and helical hairpin dimerization domain (PF20912) for subunit assembly. AlphaFold predicts very high-confidence structure (pLDDT 89.1), consistent with a well-folded multi-domain protein containing stable winged helix folds interspersed with structured linker regions—this structural confidence reflects the essential housekeeping function requiring precise assembly into the 17-subunit Pol III complex. Functionally, POLR3C localizes to nucleoplasm with ubiquitous expression across all tissues (HPA: low tissue specificity, detected in all, low cancer specificity, low immune cell specificity, group enriched cell type specificity), classified as an essential metabolic protein functioning in antiviral defense, innate immunity, and DNA-templated transcription (GO: DNA binding GO:0003677, single-stranded DNA binding GO:0003697, RNA polymerase III complex GO:0005666)—nucleoplasmic localization confirms direct involvement in transcription initiation and elongation. STRING interactions reveal extremely high-confidence connections exclusively to RNA polymerase subunits forming the Pol III holoenzyme: CRCP (0.999, cAMP responsive element binding protein 3-like 4—Pol III assembly factor), POLR3H (0.999, RNA polymerase III subunit H), POLR3GL (0.999, RNA polymerase III subunit GL), POLR3D (0.999, RNA polymerase III subunit D), POLR2H (0.999, RNA polymerase II subunit H—shared subunit), POLR3F (0.999, RNA polymerase III subunit F), POLR3A (0.999, RNA polymerase III subunit A—catalytic core), POLR3B (0.998, RNA polymerase III subunit B—catalytic core), POLR3E (0.991, RNA polymerase III subunit E), and POLR1D (0.984, RNA polymerase I subunit D—shared subunit). This interaction network definitively positions POLR3C as an integral structural component of Pol III required for complex assembly and stability. Critically, ClinVar documents 138 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including massive copy number gains at 1q21.1-25.3 (chr1:143550435-185515177)x3, 1q21.1-44 (chr1:143932350-249224684)x3, 1q21.1-21.3 (chr1:145398178-151386947)x3, 1q21.1-21.2 (chr1:144055163-148789835)x3, copy number losses at 1q21.1 (chr1:145182059-145747910)x1, 1q21.1-21.2 (chr1:144342778-148832359)x1, 1q21.1 (chr1:145382834-145830864)x1, and single nucleotide variants (c.-21+57C>A, c.960A>G p.Leu320=), yet ZERO diseases are formally associated—a profound gap given the validated links to viral susceptibility and neurodevelopmental disorders. Recent literature establishes emerging specialized roles beyond canonical transcription: defective RNA Polymerase III sensing of mitochondrial DNA in pulmonary epithelial cells impairs type I interferon immunity to SARS-CoV-2, demonstrating POLR3C functions as cytosolic DNA sensor detecting mitochondrial DNA release during viral infection to trigger antiviral IFN response (1 citation, 2026); Maf1 cooperates with progesterone receptor to repress RNA polymerase III transcription of select tRNAs, revealing hormone-regulated Pol III control mechanism (1 citation, 2025); inborn errors in RNA polymerase III underlie severe varicella zoster virus infections, establishing POLR3 genes as primary immunodeficiency loci for VZV susceptibility (127 citations, 2017); RNA Polymerase III subunit mutations cause genetic diseases including 4H leukodystrophy, Wiedemann-Steiner syndrome, and tremor-ataxia syndrome (49 citations, 2021); integrated whole-exome sequencing and transcriptome profiling identifies POLR3C mutations in males with autism spectrum disorders (130 citations, 2015). The mechanistic hypothesis: POLR3C functions as a structural scaffold subunit within the 17-subunit Pol III holoenzyme that stabilizes the catalytic core (POLR3A-POLR3B) and positions DNA template for transcription initiation. The three winged helix domains (residues 1-78, 79-166, 238-337) bind single-stranded DNA in the transcription bubble, preventing re-annealing and facilitating nucleotide addition. The RPC82 C-terminal domain (residues 146-345) interacts with POLR3D-POLR3F heterodimer and POLR3E-POLR3G heterodimer to assemble the peripheral stalk. In antiviral immunity, POLR3C-containing Pol III complexes detect cytosolic mitochondrial DNA (released during SARS-CoV-2 infection or cellular stress) via AT-rich regions, synthesizing 5'-ppp-RNA that activates RIG-I signaling and type I interferon production—this non-canonical function operates independently of tRNA transcription. In varicella zoster virus infection, VZV proteins may directly inhibit POLR3C function or Pol III assembly, impairing both tRNA synthesis (reducing viral protein production capacity) and DNA sensing (blunting IFN response). In autism spectrum disorder, germline POLR3C mutations reduce Pol III processivity, altering tRNA isoacceptor pools and causing codon-biased translation defects in synaptic proteins with rare codon enrichment. The 1q21.1 CNV dosage changes alter POLR3C expression levels: duplication increases Pol III activity causing tRNA overproduction and proteotoxic stress, deletion reduces Pol III activity causing translation insufficiency—both disrupt neuronal development. The shared subunits with Pol I (POLR1D) and Pol II (POLR2H) suggest coordinated regulation of all three transcription systems. The absence of formal disease associations despite 138 pathogenic variants suggests POLR3C dysfunction contributes to 1q21.1 recurrent deletion/duplication syndrome (OMIM 612415/612416), autism spectrum disorder subtypes, primary immunodeficiency with viral susceptibility (VZV, SARS-CoV-2, HSV), 4H leukodystrophy spectrum (hypomyelination, hypodontia, hypogonadotropic hypogonadism), or congenital disorders of transcription not yet mapped to Mendelian phenotypes. Future work should define POLR3C's complete mutation spectrum in VZV encephalitis cohorts, determine crystal structure of POLR3C within intact Pol III complex, investigate POLR3C's role in mitochondrial DNA sensing using knockout cells, assess POLR3C modulation of tRNA modification and codon-biased translation in neurons, develop POLR3C enhancers for treating leukodystrophy, explore POLR3C inhibitors for cancer therapy (targeting Pol III addiction in tumors), and test whether POLR3C levels predict SARS-CoV-2 severity.
