## Description
INSL6 (Insulin-like 6, also known as RIF1 or Relaxin-like factor 1) is a 213 amino acid secreted hormone localized to the extracellular region (UniProt Q9Y581) with medium-confidence partially disordered architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 54.8). The protein features the canonical insulin/relaxin-like fold comprising an A-chain and B-chain connected by disulfide bridges, characteristic of the insulin superfamily. Key domains include the Insulin-like domain (IPR016179/PF00049/SM00078, residues 30-192) forming a ~163 aa core module (~76%) that mediates hormone activity (GO:0005179). The structure belongs to the Insulin-like superfamily (IPR036438/G3DSA:1.10.100.10) and the Mammalian Reproductive Hormone Insulin-like family (IPR051042/PTHR12004). Conserved sites include the Insulin family signature (IPR022353/PS00262, residues 178-192) critical for receptor binding. The low AlphaFold confidence likely reflects the flexibility of the inter-chain linker or the dynamic nature of the unprocessed pro-hormone, which undergoes proteolytic cleavage to generate the mature heterodimer. Molecular functions include hormone activity and regulation of spermatogenesis, placing INSL6 within the reproductive endocrine system.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places INSL6 within a specialized reproductive hormone network: strongest interactions with RLN3 (0.777, Relaxin-3, a neuropeptide involved in stress response and feeding) and TCEAL9 (0.768, Transcription elongation factor A-like 9, a potential regulator). High confidence links include paralogs INSL5 (0.743) and INSL3 (0.704), indicating functional redundancy or co-regulation in Leydig cell function. Connections to relaxin family receptors RXFP3 (0.667), RXFP4 (0.639), RXFP1 (0.624), and RXFP2 (0.582) suggest INSL6 may signal through one or more of these G-protein coupled receptors, although its specific receptor remains debated (potential candidate RXFP2 or an orphan). Interaction with RMND5A (0.627) hints at roles in germ cell development. INSL4 (0.574) is another placental/testis-specific relaxin-like hormone.\n\nHPA expression reveals a group-enriched pattern with exceptional specificity to the testis (14.5 nTPM) and moderate expression in the retina (6.1 nTPM), detected in some tissues. The 'cancer enriched' status suggests upregulation in certain tumors, possibly reflecting re-activation of developmental programs. Subcellular location as a predicted secreted protein aligns with its hormonal function. Expression is restricted to interstitial cells (Leydig cells) surrounding seminiferous tubules, supporting a paracrine role in sperm development.\n\nClinVar documents a striking 206 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including both single nucleotide variants (e.g., c.302G>T p.Trp101Leu) and massive copy number gains and losses affecting the 9p24 region: CNV gains (9p24.3-q21.13, 9p24.3-22.2 duplications) and CNV losses (9p24.3-22.2 deletions). The extremely high variant burden indicates strong dosage sensitivity and potential involvement in 9p deletion/duplication syndrome phenotypes (sexual development disorders, intellectual disability, craniofacial anomalies). Haploinsufficiency would likely impair Leydig cell signaling, causing spermatogenic failure, reduced sperm motility, or male infertility. Duplications may cause hormonal imbalance, potentially leading to precocious puberty or testicular dysgenesis.\n\nConservation is strong across mammals: human (Q9Y581, 213 aa), mouse (Q9QY05, 191 aa), rat (Q9WV41, 188 aa), cow (Q32L79, 205 aa), indicating evolutionary pressure to maintain the insulin-like fold.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: INSL6 functions as a paracrine hormone produced by Leydig cells that regulates spermatogenesis and sperm maturation. Upon secretion, the mature heterodimer binds to a specific relaxin-family receptor (likely RXFP2 or a yet-unidentified orphan GPCR) on nearby germ cells or Sertoli cells. This activates cAMP or MAPK signaling pathways, promoting germ cell survival, differentiation, or blood-testis barrier integrity. The interaction with INSL3 suggests complementary roles: INSL3 regulates testicular descent, while INSL6 fine-tunes post-natal spermatogenesis. The retinal expression hints at a potential neuroprotective or developmental role in the eye, possibly via RXFP3/4. Dysregulation via 9p CNVs disrupts this delicate hormonal balance: loss causes spermatogenic arrest and infertility, while gain causes aberrant signaling and developmental defects, contributing to 9p syndrome phenotypes.
