# TMEM260: ER-Localized O-Mannosyl-Transferase Regulating Protein Maturation in Epithelial Tissues with Lymphoma Association

## Description
TMEM260 (Gene ID: 54916, UniProt Q9NX78, aliases C14orf101/SHDRA) encodes a 707 amino acid O-mannosyl-transferase localized to the endoplasmic reticulum membrane. The protein contains three structured domains: an N-terminal TMEM260 domain (IPR021280/PF11028, aa 52-211), a central Glycosyltransferase 117 catalytic domain (IPR052724/PTHR16214, aa 17-697), and a C-terminal TMEM260-associated domain 2 (IPR060492/PF28495, aa 421-479). AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 92.4) indicates exceptional folding confidence across all 707 residues, consistent with a stable enzymatic scaffold. HPA shows tissue-enhanced expression with peak levels in cervix (37.3 nTPM), detected ubiquitously across tissues with low cancer specificity. STRING interactions reveal connections to cytoskeletal myosins (MYH3 0.615, MYH4 0.613, MYH1 0.495), Golgi apparatus protein GOLGA7 (0.509), DNA demethylase ALKBH8 (0.507), chromatin regulator FAM111A (0.503), mitochondrial ATP synthase TMEM70 (0.490), myosin kinase MYLK2 (0.485), and ciliopathy protein TCTN1 (0.478). ClinVar contains 56 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including nonsense mutations (p.Glu454Ter, p.Trp139Ter), frameshifts (p.Leu272fs), and large deletions, though no specific Mendelian disease is assigned; the gene is implicated in T-cell non-Hodgkin lymphoma and stomach cancer. Mouse ortholog Tmem260 (Q8BMD6, 703 aa) shows strong conservation. Functional hypothesis: TMEM260 catalyzes O-mannosylation of secretory and membrane proteins in the ER, specifically targeting IPT/TIG domain-containing substrates. This post-translational modification regulates protein folding, stability, and trafficking. Interaction with myosins suggests coordination between glycosylation and cytoskeletal dynamics, potentially affecting cell motility and division. GOLGA7 interaction indicates Golgi-ER trafficking coordination. ALKBH8 and FAM111A connections implicate epigenetic regulation through glycosylation of chromatin modifiers. The TCTN1 link suggests ciliary protein glycosylation, relevant to ciliopathy pathways. Disease-associated variants likely disrupt catalytic activity or substrate recognition, causing aberrant glycosylation of oncogenic or tumor suppressor proteins. Elevated cervical expression may reflect specialized glycosylation requirements in reproductive epithelium. Lymphoma and stomach cancer associations suggest TMEM260 dysfunction promotes oncogenesis through dysregulated cell surface receptor glycosylation or impaired protein quality control in the ER stress response.
