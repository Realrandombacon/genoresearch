## Description
CRISP2 (UniProt P16562) encodes a compact 243-amino acid cysteine-rich secretory protein containing a canonical CAP domain (IPR014044/PF00188, aa 35-172/41-169) within the CAP superfamily (IPR035940/G3DSA:3.40.33.10). Additional domains include a C-terminal CRISP domain (IPR013871/PF08562, aa 189-243/201-241) and ShKT toxin-like domain (IPR003582, aa 205-238), characteristic of ion channel-modulating proteins. AlphaFold predicts an exceptionally well-folded structure (pLDDT: 93.1), among the highest confidence predictions and indicative of stable extracellular function.\n\nSTRING interactions reveal CRISP2's role in sperm biology: connections to glycolysis enzyme GART (0.831), immunomodulator IL4I1 (0.657), paralogue CRISP3 (0.625), phospholipase PLB1 (0.619), germ cell nuclear binding protein GGNBP2 (0.579), A-kinase anchoring protein AKAP4 (0.574), and voltage-gated calcium channel CATSPER1 (0.494). HPA confirms extreme testis specificity (482.3 nTPM), classifying it as a predicted secreted protein.\n\nWith 135 papers (many reflecting acronym confusion with CRISP2 imaging studies/collateral scores), CRISP2 is a transitional dark gene—extensively characterized in rodent/boar models but with emerging human functional understanding. Recent 2025 studies provide novel insights into human CRISP2 localization in reproductive tissues and sperm, with computational analyses linking CRISP2 SNPs to asthenospermia infertility. ClinVar contains 9 pathogenic variants, predominantly 6p12 copy number alterations. This positions CRISP2 as a critical epididymal secreted protein regulating sperm motility through calcium channel (CATSPER1) modulation and glycolytic pathway integration, with untapped relevance in male infertility diagnostics.
