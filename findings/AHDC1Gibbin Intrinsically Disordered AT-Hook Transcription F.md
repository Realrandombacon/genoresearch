## Description
AHDC1/Q5TGY3 (1603 aa) is a predominantly intrinsically disordered (AlphaFold pLDDT: 38.6) nuclear transcription factor functioning as a chromatin-associated regulator of gene expression through AT-hook DNA binding motifs and heterochromatin protein interactions. InterPro analysis reveals a unique architecture dominated by a C-terminal domain of unknown function DUF4683 (IPR032757/PF15735, residues 584-641) of uncharacterized molecular activity within the Gibbin transcription factor family (IPR039225/PTHR15617), with the low-confidence AlphaFold structure suggesting extensive intrinsically disordered regions typical of chromatin regulators engaging in dynamic protein-protein interactions rather than stable globular folding. HPA data confirms ubiquitous nucleoplasmic expression across all tissues with roles in transcription regulation, differentiation, and development, consistent with fundamental chromatin regulatory functions, and disease involvement in intellectual disability. STRING interactions define a chromatin-focused network linking AHDC1 to heterochromatin proteins CBX1 (0.625) and CBX5/HP1α (0.431), transcriptional repressor SCAI (0.623), E3 ubiquitin ligase HUWE1 (0.405), DNA polymerase subunit POLD2 (0.401), and uncharacterized proteins SCPEP1 (0.420) and CDRT1 (0.402), positioning it within heterochromatin maintenance and transcriptional repression complexes. Literature establishes AHDC1 as the causative gene for Xia-Gibbs syndrome, a rare neurodevelopmental disorder characterized by intellectual disability, developmental delay, sleep apnea, hypotonia, and distinctive facial features. ClinVar documents 317 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants predominantly truncating mutations including frameshifts (p.Pro459fs, p.Val704fs, p.Val900fs, p.Ala1432fs, p.Leu337fs, p.Ser890fs) and nonsense mutations (p.Arg151Ter, p.Arg108Ter, p.Gln648Ter) distributed throughout the coding sequence, confirming haploinsufficiency as the disease mechanism through de novo loss-of-function events. The convergence of intrinsic disorder, CBX/heterochromatin interactions, AT-hook DNA binding capacity, and neurodevelopmental disease phenotypes suggests AHDC1 functions as a dynamic chromatin scaffold coordinating heterochromatin-euchromatin boundaries during neural development, with the DUF4683 domain potentially mediating specific protein recruitment or post-translational modification recognition. Therapeutic implications include understanding chromatin dysregulation in intellectual disability and potential targeting of downstream epigenetic pathways.
