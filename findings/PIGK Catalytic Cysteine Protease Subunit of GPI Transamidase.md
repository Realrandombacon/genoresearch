# PIGK: Catalytic Cysteine Protease Subunit of GPI Transamidase Complex Attaching Glycosylphosphatidylinositol Anchors to Surface Proteins, Legumain-Family Peptidase C13 Member with 39 Pathogenic Variants Causing GPI Biosynthesis Defect 22 (GPIBD22) with Cerebellar Atrophy and Seizures, and Lipophagy Regulator in Colorectal Cancer via ABHD5 Interaction

## Description
PIGK (Phosphatidylinositol Glycan Anchor Biosynthesis Class K, also known as GPI8, NEDHCAS, UniProt Q92643) is a 395-amino acid catalytic cysteine protease functioning as the enzymatic core subunit of the GPI transamidase complex that cleaves C-terminal signal peptides from precursor proteins and attaches glycosylphosphatidylinositol (GPI) anchors to enable cell surface localization of over 150 human proteins including complement regulators, adhesion molecules, and enzymes; biallelic loss-of-function mutations cause GPI biosynthesis defect 22 (GPIBD22), a severe neurodevelopmental disorder characterized by developmental delay, hypotonia, cerebellar atrophy, epilepsy, and intellectual disability; recent studies implicate PIGK in regulating lipophagy in colorectal cancer through interaction with ABHD5 and as an oncogenic factor with prognostic relevance in head and neck squamous cell carcinoma. The protein architecture features defining Peptidase C13 legumain family domain (IPR001096/PF01650/G3DSA:3.40.50.1460 aa 15-277/45-277/41-325) containing the catalytic cysteine-histidine-asparagine triad characteristic of asparaginyl endopeptidases adapted for transamidation rather than hydrolysis, GPI-anchor transamidase family membership (IPR028361/PIRSF019663/PIRSF500138/PTHR48067 aa 1-395/15-233/1-395/13-314) confirming role in GPI attachment (GO:0003923, GO:0016255), HEMOGLOBNASE signature motifs (PR00776 aa 46-70, 71-100, 120-135) potentially involved in substrate recognition or heme binding, and GO term annotations for peptidase activity (GO:0008233), proteolysis (GO:0006508), GPI-anchor transamidase complex membership (GO:0042765), and transferase molecular function. The AlphaFold predicted structure shows high confidence (pLDDT 85.1), indicating a well-folded stable protein with defined legumain-like catalytic core—consistent with enzyme requiring rigid active site geometry for precise peptide bond cleavage and amide bond formation during GPI anchor attachment while maintaining conformational flexibility for signal peptide recognition and complex assembly with regulatory subunits.
