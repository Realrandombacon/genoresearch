## Description
LOC101927468 is an uncharacterized genomic locus on chromosome 1q21.2 (NC_000001.11:148247070-148208092) spanning approximately 39.0 kb. Comprehensive database searches reveal no evidence of protein-coding function: no UniProt accession was identified, precluding InterPro domain analysis and AlphaFold structure prediction. STRING database returned no protein interaction partners, confirming absence of canonical protein-coding function. Human Protein Atlas resolved this locus to Ensembl ID ENSG00000227733 but found no detectable protein expression in surveyed tissues. However, ClinVar analysis reveals an extraordinary 120 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants in this region, all copy number alterations including recurrent 1q21.1 microdeletions (chr1:146202923-148413447, ~2.2 Mb; chr1:146999427-148413447, ~1.4 Mb; chr1:146231513-148358701, ~2.1 Mb) and microduplications (chr1:146387442-148577050, ~2.2 Mb; chr1:146872717-148353641, ~1.5 Mb). These structural variants encompass the 1q21.1 region, one of the most well-characterized copy number variant hotspots in the human genome, mediated by segmental duplications (low-copy repeats) that facilitate non-allelic homologous recombination. The 1q21.1 CNV syndrome is associated with highly variable phenotypes including developmental delay, intellectual disability, autism spectrum disorder, schizophrenia, congenital heart defects (particularly tetralogy of Fallot), cataracts, and dysmorphic features. The 1q21.1 region contains multiple dosage-sensitive genes including BCL9 (B-cell lymphoma 9), PYGO2 (pygopus planar cell polarity protein 2), ACP6 (acid phosphatase 6), GJA5 (gap junction protein alpha 5/connexin 40), and GJA8 (gap junction protein alpha 8/connexin 50). LOC101927468 likely functions as part of the 1q21.1 cis-regulatory landscape or encodes a non-coding RNA whose copy number alteration contributes to 1q21.1 syndrome pathogenesis through disruption of chromatin topology, topologically associating domain (TAD) boundaries, or long-range regulation of dosage-sensitive genes. The absence of protein-coding features combined with extreme pathogenic CNV burden (120 variants - among the highest observed in this study) indicates this locus resides within the most recombinogenic and dosage-sensitive region of chromosome 1 where structural variation profoundly disrupts neurodevelopmental, cardiac, and ocular processes via gene dosage effects and 3D genome reorganization.
