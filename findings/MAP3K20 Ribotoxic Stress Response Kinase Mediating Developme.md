## Description
MAP3K20 encodes an 800 amino acid mitogen-activated protein kinase kinase kinase 20 (UniProt Q9NYL2), also known as MLTK (Mixed Lineage Triple Kinase) or ZAK (Sterile Alpha Motif Kinase). With 31 publications, this gene has emerging disease associations but incompletely characterized mechanisms.\n\nSTRUCTURAL FEATURES: AlphaFold predicts a partially structured protein (pLDDT 68.6) across all 800 residues, consistent with ordered catalytic domains connected by flexible regulatory regions. Subcellular localization is cytosolic.\n\nCLINICAL SIGNIFICANCE: ClinVar reports 41 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including nonsense mutations (p.Trp197Ter, p.Trp534Ter, p.Ser531Ter), frameshift deletions (p.Ser184fs), splice site variants (c.1476+2T>A), microsatellite variants, and large copy number losses (2q31.1-32.2, 2q24.2-31.1). Recent literature defines MAP3K20 haploinsufficiency syndrome causing deafness, ectodermal dysplasia, craniosynostosis, ectrodactyly, and skeletal anomaly spectrum (DECS syndrome), plus centronuclear myopathy-6.\n\nFUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: Recent literature (31 papers) reveals emerging roles: (1) Ribotoxic stress response (RSR) pathway activation driving M1 macrophage polarization in ulcerative colitis—MAP3K20 inhibition downregulates inflammatory responses. (2) Hotspot variants cause DECS syndrome via disrupted developmental signaling. (3) Centronuclear myopathy-6 with fiber-type disproportion. Mechanistically, MAP3K20 functions as a stress-activated MAP3K phosphorylating MAP2K4/7 to activate JNK and MAP2K3/6 to activate p38 pathways. The SAM domain mediates oligomerization and recruitment to ribosomal complexes during ribotoxic stress (damaged rRNA from toxins/viruses). In development, MAP3K20 regulates ectodermal differentiation and limb patterning via p38 signaling. Loss-of-function causes DECS syndrome through impaired stress adaptation during embryogenesis; gain-of-function or hyperactivation drives inflammatory macrophage polarization in colitis.
