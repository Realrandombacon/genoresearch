# PYURF: Mitochondrial Trm112-Like Methyltransferase Chaperone Co-Expressed with GPI Anchor Biosynthesis Protein PIGY

**Date:** 2026-03-27T13:15:55.836966

**Quality Score:** 3.47/10  (E=5.85, D=1.1) [WEAK]

## Description
PYURF (PIGY upstream open reading frame) encodes a small 114 amino acid mitochondrial protein (UniProt Q96I23) that is well-conserved across vertebrates including mouse (Q9D1C3, 112 aa) and rat (Q5U1Z8, 112 aa). InterPro analysis reveals a Trm112-like domain (IPR005651, aa 50-103; PF03966, aa 51-90) characteristic of methyltransferase chaperones that stabilize S-adenosylmethionine-dependent methyltransferases. AlphaFold structure prediction shows high confidence (pLDDT 76.8), indicating a well-folded stable structure despite small size. HPA classifies PYURF as an essential metabolic protein with ubiquitous expression across all tissues and nucleoplasmic localization. STRING interaction network reveals remarkably strong associations with GPI anchor biosynthesis pathway proteins: PIGY (score 0.912), PIGP (0.881), PIGH (0.839), PIGC (0.839), PIGA (0.828), and PIGQ (0.806), plus moderate connection to NDUFAF5 (0.578), a mitochondrial complex I assembly factor. This dual interaction pattern reflects PYURF's unique genomic organization: it shares a bicistronic transcript with PIGY but encodes an unrelated protein. ClinVar documents 27 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly copy number losses and gains spanning 4q21.21-22.3, indicating dosage sensitivity. Functional hypothesis: PYURF acts as a mitochondrial methyltransferase chaperone that stabilizes and activates methyltransferases involved in both GPI anchor biosynthesis (through physical or functional coupling with PIGY complex) and mitochondrial respiratory chain assembly (via NDUFAF5 interaction). The Trm112-like fold suggests it may bind and protect catalytic subunits from degradation, similar to yeast Trm112 which activates multiple tRNA methyltransferases. Disease mechanism likely involves disrupted protein methylation affecting both cell surface GPI-anchored protein expression and mitochondrial energy metabolism.
