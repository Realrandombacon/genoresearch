## Description
CCDC65 (Coiled-coil domain containing 65, also known as DRC2, CFAP250, FAP250, NYD-SP28) is a 484 amino acid ciliary protein (UniProt Q8IXS2) with high-confidence well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 82.9), characteristic of structural components of the axoneme. The protein contains a conserved N-terminal domain (IPR039505, residues 25-124) shared with DRC1/2 family members and belongs to the Dynein Regulatory Complex protein family (IPR039750/PTHR21625, residues 4-387). A sperm tail-specific domain (PF14772, residues 25-124) is present, explicitly linking CCDC65 to flagellar function. Molecular functions include axonemal dynein complex assembly (GO:0070286) and structural constituent of axonemal dynein complex (GO:0005858), placing CCDC65 within the ciliary motility and primary ciliary dyskinesia (PCD) pathways.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals a network exclusively composed of ciliary and dynein regulatory complex components: strongest interactions with DNAI1 (0.906, Dynein intermediate chain 1, outer dynein arm), CCDC40 (0.904, Coiled-coil domain containing 40, DRC subunit), CCDC39 (0.892, Coiled-coil domain containing 39, DRC subunit), DNAI2 (0.852, Dynein intermediate chain 2), DRC7 (0.843, Dynein regulatory complex subunit 7), CCDC114 (0.826, Coiled-coil domain containing 114, outer dynein arm docking complex), CCDC103 (0.821, Coiled-coil domain containing 103, DRC assembly), DRC1 (0.818, Dynein regulatory complex subunit 1), GAS8 (0.601, Dynein intermediate chain 8), and RSPH4A (0.585, Radial spoke head component 4A). This profile confirms CCDC65/DRC2 is a core component of the Nexin-Dynein Regulatory Complex (N-DRC), which acts as a mechanical linker between adjacent outer doublet microtubules and regulates dynein arm activity to coordinate ciliary bending.\n\nHPA expression reveals tissue enhancement in testis (34.8 nTPM), fallopian tube (25.0 nTPM), and choroid plexus (18.3 nTPM), with detection in many tissues. Subcellular localization is vesicular, at the primary cilium transition zone, centriolar satellites, and within the mid and principal pieces of the sperm tail, consistent with its role in ciliogenesis and flagellar assembly. The protein is classified as disease-related (Ciliopathy, Kartagener syndrome, Primary ciliary dyskinesia) and predicted intracellular.\n\nClinVar documents 5 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including a truncating nonsense mutation (c.876T>G, p.Tyr292Ter), missense variants (c.474G>C, p.Lys158Asn), and large copy number gains affecting chromosome 12q11-13.12. The presence of loss-of-function mutations confirms haploinsufficiency or recessive mechanisms. Clinical phenotypes include primary ciliary dyskinesia (PCD), characterized by chronic respiratory infections, situs inversus (Kartagener syndrome), and male infertility due to immotile sperm.\n\nConservation is strong across ciliated eukaryotes: human (Q8IXS2, 484 aa), mouse (Q8VHI7, 493 aa), zebrafish (Q6DHI2, 492 aa), and Chlamydomonas (A8JB22, 573 aa), indicating an ancient and essential function in ciliary motility.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: CCDC65/DRC2 functions as a structural scaffold within the Nexin-Dynein Regulatory Complex, stabilizing the connection between adjacent outer doublet microtubules in the ciliary axoneme. The N-terminal domain mediates interactions with CCDC39, CCDC40, and DRC1, forming the core of the N-DRC. This complex acts as a mechanical bridge and regulatory hub, transmitting forces during ciliary beating and modulating dynein arm activity to ensure coordinated, asymmetric waveforms. Loss of CCDC65 disrupts N-DRC assembly, leading to disorganized dynein arms, impaired microtubule sliding, and defective ciliary motility. This causes mucus accumulation in airways, impaired left-right patterning during embryogenesis (situs inversus), and sperm immotility. CCDC65 represents a validated cause of primary ciliary dyskinesia and a critical structural determinant of ciliary function.
