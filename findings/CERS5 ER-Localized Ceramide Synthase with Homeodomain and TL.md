## Description
CERS5 (Ceramide synthase 5, also known as LASS5 or Longevity assurance homolog 5, Q8N5B7) encodes a 392 aa multi-pass transmembrane enzyme functioning as the primary C16-palmitoyl-CoA-specific ceramide synthase that catalyzes N-acylation of sphinganine to generate C16-ceramide, a critical sphingolipid species regulating apoptosis, membrane microdomain organization, and metabolic signaling. Structural architecture reveals a unique domain organization among ceramide synthases: an N-terminal homeodomain (IPR001356/PF00046/PS50071/G3DSA:1.10.10.60/SSF46689, residues 89-136/90-134/92-136/84-136/83-134) adopting a helix-turn-helix DNA-binding fold potentially involved in protein-protein interactions or substrate recognition rather than DNA binding, followed by a TRAM/LAG1/CLN8 homology (TLC) domain (IPR006634/PF03798/PS50922/SM00724, residues 139-340/140-333/139-340/139-340) forming the catalytic core containing the conserved active site for acyl-CoA binding and ceramide synthesis, belonging to the sphingosine N-acyltransferase Lag1/Lac1-like family (IPR016439/PIRSF005225/PTHR12560, residues 8-377/8-377/16-359) with GO terms for sphingosine N-acyltransferase activity (GO:0050291) and ceramide biosynthetic process (GO:0046513); AlphaFold prediction (pLDDT 84.8, high confidence) confirms a well-folded transmembrane enzyme with structured TLC catalytic domain embedded in ER membrane topology consistent with stable enzyme requiring precise active site geometry for acyltransferase catalysis. STRING interactions define the complete sphingolipid metabolism pathway: KDSR (0.967, highest confidence) as 3-ketodihydrosphingosine reductase producing sphinganine substrate for CERS5, glucosylceramide synthase UGCG (0.964) converting ceramide to glucosylceramide for glycosphingolipid synthesis, acid sphingomyelinase SMPD1 (0.964) hydrolyzing sphingomyelin to generate ceramide, acid ceramidases ASAH1 (0.963) and ASAH2 (0.961) degrading ceramide to sphingosine providing feedback regulation, sphingolipid desaturases DEGS1 (0.948) and DEGS2 (0.904) introducing double bonds into dihydroceramide, sphingosine kinase SPHK1 (0.675) phosphorylating sphingosine to sphingosine-1-phosphate, sphingomyelin synthase SMPD2 (0.519) producing sphingomyelin from ceramide, and ceramide kinase CERK (0.453) phosphorylating ceramide to ceramide-1-phosphate. HPA localizes CERS5 to nuclear membrane, endoplasmic reticulum, and primary cilium with ubiquitous expression detected in all tissues, low tissue and cancer specificity indicating housekeeping metabolic function, low immune cell specificity, and cell type enhanced single cell specificity. ClinVar documents 6 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including splice donor variant c.434+10C>T potentially disrupting intron 4 splicing, and chromosome 12 copy number alterations: whole arm gains at 12p13.33-q24.33 (chr12:173787-133777902x3, chr12:1-133851895x3, chr12:121271-133196807x3) duplicating the locus, and loss at 12q13.12-13.13 (chr12:50122359-53248460x1); these CNVs are associated with developmental disorders and cancer susceptibility. Literature (105 papers) establishes CERS5 as the major C16-ceramide producer in mammalian cells where it serves as pan-cancer biomarker across multiple tumor types correlating with poor prognosis, promotes liver fibrosis development in NAFLD through hepatocyte lipotoxicity and stellate cell activation, modulates insulin sensitivity in skeletal muscle where CerS5 silencing improves glucose uptake unlike CerS1, is differentially modulated by fenretinide (synthetic retinoid) reducing C16-ceramide levels in cancer therapy, regulates epicardial adipose tissue inflammation in coronary artery disease, and functions in apoptosis signaling through mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilization; ER/nuclear membrane localization positions CERS5 at the interface of lipid synthesis and nuclear signaling where C16-ceramide may regulate transcription factors or chromatin modifiers; homeodomain presence suggests potential protein-protein interaction platform recruiting regulatory partners; high-confidence STRING network with KDSR/DEGS1/UGCG indicates CERS5 operates within a metabolon channeling intermediates efficiently; conservation from mouse (Q9D6K9, 414 aa) confirms evolutionary preservation of C16-specific acyltransferase function.
