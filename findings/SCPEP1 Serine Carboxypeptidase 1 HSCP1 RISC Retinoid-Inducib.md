## Description
SCPEP1 encodes a 452 aa serine carboxypeptidase enzyme (UniProt Q9HB40), also known as HSCP1 (Human Serine Carboxypeptidase 1) or RISC (Retinoid-Inducible Serine Carboxypeptidase), that functions as a critical regulator of vasoactive peptide metabolism and cardiovascular homeostasis through C-terminal amino acid cleavage from peptide substrates. The protein features a canonical serine carboxypeptidase architecture: an alpha/beta hydrolase fold core (IPR029058/G3DSA:3.40.50.1820/SSF53474, residues 25-452) forming the catalytic scaffold; a peptidase S10 family domain (IPR001563/PF00450/PTHR11802, residues 35-449) defining substrate specificity for C-terminal residue removal; and a conserved serine active site motif (IPR018202/PS00131, residues 163-170) containing the catalytic triad essential for peptide bond hydrolysis. STRING interactions reveal SCPEP1 operates within peptide metabolism and signaling networks: PTTG1IP (0.508), pituitary tumor-transforming 1 interacting protein potentially linking to cell cycle regulation; CD2AP (0.497), CD2-associated protein involved in cytoskeletal organization and podocyte function; IMPA1 (0.487), inositol monophosphatase 1 in phosphoinositide signaling; RASL10B (0.486), RAS-like family member; PRCP (0.429), prolylcarboxypeptidase representing functional paralog with overlapping substrate specificity; ABHD10 (0.426), alpha/beta hydrolase domain-containing protein; SMTNL2 (0.424), smoothelin-like protein 2 in vascular smooth muscle; AHDC1 (0.420), AT-hook DNA-binding protein; and C17orf67 (0.413), chromosome 17 neighbor suggesting genomic context interactions. HPA confirms tissue-enhanced expression with remarkable parathyroid gland enrichment (283.0 nTPM), detected in all tissues, Golgi apparatus subcellular localization consistent with secretory pathway processing, classified as enzyme/carboxypeptidase/hydrolase/protease with both intracellular and secreted forms, immune cell enhanced in blood. ClinVar lists 16 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including splice site mutations (c.995-33A>G, c.316-179G>T) and large copy number losses (17q12-22 spanning 41-41Mb, 17q22 spanning 55-57Mb, 54-55Mb, 54-55Mb) plus gains (17p13.3-q25.3 spanning 0.0005-81Mb x2, 17q21.31-25.3 spanning 43-81Mb), demonstrating dosage sensitivity though specific monogenic diseases remain undefined. AlphaFold structure prediction yields very high confidence (global pLDDT 90.1), revealing well-folded alpha/beta hydrolase structure with properly positioned catalytic triad characteristic of active serine carboxypeptidases. Mechanistically, SCPEP1 operates through integrated modes: (1) Vasoactive peptide inactivation—cleaves C-terminal residues from endothelin-1 (ET-1) and angiotensin II terminating their vasoconstrictive signals, complementary to cathepsin A function demonstrated in 2014 double-knockout studies; (2) Blood pressure regulation—gene ontology predicts involvement in negative blood pressure regulation and blood vessel diameter maintenance through peptide hormone processing; (3) Retinoic acid metabolism—retinoid-inducible expression links vitamin A signaling to cardiovascular peptide homeostasis; (4) Mitochondrial bioenergetics—2025 study demonstrates SCPEP1 inhibition improves mitochondrial function post-myocardial infarction suggesting unexpected role in cardiac energy metabolism potentially via peptide substrate modulation; (5) Pulmonary fibrosis—2025 study identifies SCPEP1+ basal cells in idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis remodeling oxidative stress signaling networks; (6) Ocular/skin homeostasis—2017 mouse studies show SCPEP1/Cathepsin A double deficiency causes hyperproliferative vesicular corneal dystrophy and hypertrophic skin thickenings via ET-1 accumulation affecting epithelial cell growth; (7) Extracellular exosome localization—secreted form may process circulating peptides or function in intercellular communication. Loss-of-function causes vasoactive peptide accumulation (ET-1, angiotensin II), sustained vasoconstriction, elevated blood pressure, disrupted retinoic acid-responsive cardiovascular adaptation, impaired mitochondrial bioenergetics in cardiac stress, and epithelial hyperproliferation in cornea/skin.
