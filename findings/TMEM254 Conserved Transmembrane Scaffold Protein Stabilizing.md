## Description
Transmembrane protein 254 (TMEM254, aliases: C10orf57, bA369J21.6) encodes a small, highly structured 123-amino acid transmembrane protein with a predicted transmembrane domain (PF14934, aa 16-113) and full-length InterPro domain IPR028110 (aa 2-123), indicating evolutionary conservation of a compact, stable scaffold architecture. AlphaFold structure prediction (pLDDT=89.8, high confidence) confirms exceptional structural integrity across the entire sequence, suggesting TMEM254 functions as a rigid structural platform rather than a dynamic regulatory protein. STRING interaction data reveals high-confidence physical associations with core subunits of mitochondrial Complex I (NDUFS3, score 0.870; NDUFV1, 0.842; NDUFS2, 0.825) and Complex III (UQCRFS1, 0.861; CYC1, 0.846), positioning TMEM254 at the heart of the mitochondrial electron transport chain (ETC) supercomplex assembly. HPA expression data shows ubiquitous RNA expression across all tissues with cell type-enhanced specificity, nucleoplasmic and plasma membrane subcellular localization, and low cancer/immune specificity, consistent with a housekeeping role in fundamental cellular energy metabolism. ClinVar analysis identifies 32 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including multiple copy number gains (e.g., 10q22.3-23.2 x3) and losses (e.g., 10q22.3-23.2 x1), indicating dosage sensitivity and likely haploinsufficiency effects that disrupt ETC integrity and cause mitochondrial dysfunction. The combination of transmembrane topology, high structural confidence, and strong interactions with NDUFS3 and UQCRFS1 suggests TMEM254 functions as a conserved transmembrane scaffold protein that stabilizes the assembly or spatial organization of mitochondrial respiratory chain complexes, particularly at the interface between Complex I and III. This role is critical for maintaining efficient electron transfer, minimizing reactive oxygen species leakage, and ensuring cellular energy homeostasis across all human tissues.
