## Description
ZNF610 (Gene ID: 162963, UniProt Q8N9Z0) is a 462 amino acid Krüppel-associated box (KRAB) domain-containing zinc finger protein located on chromosome 19q13.41 (NC_000019.10, positions 52330220-52367777, ~37.5kb region). InterPro domain analysis reveals a canonical KRAB-ZNF architecture: N-terminal KRAB box repression domain (IPR001909/PF01352, positions 24-95) that recruits transcriptional co-repressors, followed by multiple C2H2-type zinc finger DNA-binding domains (IPR013087/PF00096, positions 204-231, 232-259, 260-287, 296-353, 352-446) arranged in tandem arrays that confer sequence-specific DNA recognition. The KRAB domain (cd07765, positions 24-63) mediates heterochromatin formation through recruitment of TRIM28/KAP1 and associated histone methyltransferases, establishing repressive chromatin marks (H3K9me3) at target loci. AlphaFold structure prediction (AF-Q8N9Z0-F1) shows medium global confidence (pLDDT 65.5), consistent with modular architecture where structured zinc finger domains are connected by flexible linker regions - this enables conformational adaptability for binding diverse DNA sequences. Human Protein Atlas classifies ZNF610 as a predicted intracellular transcription factor localized to nucleoplasm, with low tissue specificity but detected in many tissues, cancer-enhanced expression pattern suggesting upregulation in malignant cells, and cell type-enhanced specificity indicating restricted expression in particular cell populations. STRING database identifies 5 protein-protein interaction partners: TRIM28 (score 0.518, highest confidence - the canonical KRAB domain co-receptor that bridges KRAB-ZFPs to heterochromatin machinery), ZNF415 (0.513, related KRAB-ZNF protein suggesting potential cooperative or competitive DNA binding), TSPYL5 (0.421, testis-specific Y-like protein 5 involved in chromatin regulation), OPLAH (0.416, 5-oxoprolinase linking metabolism to epigenetic regulation), and FAM135B (0.413, uncharacterized protein). The TRIM28 interaction is mechanistically critical as it validates ZNF610's role in the canonical KRAB-mediated repression pathway. ClinVar contains 15 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variants affecting the ZNF610 locus on 19q13.41, all representing duplications/gains (chr19:49625130-57647352x3, chr19:49911081-53127438, chr19:48463931-57095254x3, chr19:47939842-54626871, chr19:44738088-53621561x3, chr19:260912-58956888, chr19:50489390-59095359x3, chr19:52143873-58445521x3, chr19:50152520-58581203x3), indicating triplosensitivity - increased ZNF610 dosage causes disease phenotypes likely through aberrant transcriptional repression of target genes. No diseases are specifically associated with ZNF610 alone, suggesting phenotypes result from combined effects of multiple genes in the duplicated regions. Functional hypothesis: ZNF610 functions as a sequence-specific transcriptional repressor that binds genomic DNA through its C2H2 zinc finger array and recruits TRIM28/KAP1 via the KRAB domain to establish heterochromatin at target loci, regulating genes involved in development, cell differentiation, or stress responses. Cancer-enhanced expression suggests ZNF610 may silence tumor suppressor genes or differentiation programs in malignant cells. Disease mechanism: ZNF610 duplication leads to overexpression and excessive transcriptional repression of target genes, disrupting normal developmental gene regulatory networks and contributing to congenital anomalies, developmental delay, or intellectual disability observed in 19q13 duplication syndromes. Priority experiments include ChIP-seq to identify genome-wide DNA binding targets, CRISPR knockout to assess transcriptional consequences, characterization of zinc finger DNA-binding specificity via SELEX, investigation of TRIM28-dependent repression mechanisms, and analysis of ZNF610 expression patterns across cancer types.
