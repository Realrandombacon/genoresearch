## Description
AMZ2 encodes a 360 aa zinc-dependent metallopeptidase (UniProt Q86W34), also known as archaemetzincin-2, that functions as a member of the evolutionarily ancient M54/archaelysin peptidase family with activity against angiotensin-3 and potential roles in sperm morphogenesis, cancer progression, and extracellular matrix remodeling. This represents a genuine dark gene with only 9 literature publications, confirming limited characterization despite clinical significance. The protein features a canonical metalloprotease architecture: a peptidase M54/archaemetzincin domain (IPR012962/PF07998/cd11375/PTHR32205/IPR052009, residues 89-297/241-297) containing the catalytic core; a collagenase-like catalytic domain fold (G3DSA:3.40.390.10/SSF55486, residues 126-297) characteristic of zincins with conserved HEXXH zinc-binding motif; classified within metallopeptidase catalytic domain superfamily (IPR024079) with GO terms for peptidase activity, proteolysis, and metallopeptidase activity. STRING interactions reveal AMZ2 operates within a network of poorly characterized proteins: SMIM17 (0.527), small integral membrane protein 17; FAM234B (0.448), family with sequence similarity 234 member B; LRRIQ3 (0.447), leucine-rich repeats and IQ motif containing 3; SPRYD7 (0.435), SPRY domain containing 7; ZACN (0.434), zacn nuclear protein; TMEM214 (0.432), transmembrane protein 214; and ARSG (0.405), arylsulfatase G, suggesting connections to membrane-associated processes and glycosaminoglycan metabolism. HPA confirms ubiquitous expression with low tissue specificity (detected in all tissues), low cancer and immune cell specificity, nucleoplasm/nuclear bodies/cytosol subcellular localization, classified as enzyme/hydrolase/metalloprotease/protease. ClinVar lists 13 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including missense mutation affecting catalytic or structural residues (c.425A>G p.Asn142Ser within M54 domain), 5-prime UTR deletion (c.1-6del), and extensive copy number gains spanning 17q regions (17q24.1-25.3 63-81Mb x3, 17q23.1-24.2 57-67Mb x3, 17q22-24.2 57-66Mb, 17q24.1-25.2 64-74Mb x3, 17q21.31-25.3 42-81Mb x3, 17p13.3-q25.3 0.0005-81Mb x2/x3), demonstrating strong dosage sensitivity though specific monogenic diseases remain undefined. AlphaFold structure prediction yields very high confidence (global pLDDT 90.2), revealing a well-folded metalloprotease structure with properly positioned zinc-coordinating residues characteristic of active enzymes. Mechanistically, AMZ2 operates through integrated modes: (1) Angiotensin-3 hydrolysis—RefSeq confirms enzymatic activity against angiotensin-3 (Ang III), a bioactive peptide regulating blood pressure and aldosterone secretion, suggesting role in local renin-angiotensin system modulation; (2) Sperm morphogenesis—2023 study identifies AMZ2 as candidate causative gene in severe teratozoospermia with vacuolated spermatozoa and cephalic defects, representing one of most severe sperm morphology abnormalities; (3) Hepatocellular carcinoma prognosis—2024 study incorporates AMZ2 into matrix metalloproteinase-related prognostic signature for HCC, suggesting involvement in tumor invasion and metastasis via ECM degradation; (4) Lung tumorigenesis—RefSeq notes defect association with lung cancer development, potentially through angiogenesis modulation or growth factor processing; (5) Inhibitor sensitivity—enzyme inhibited by aminopeptidase inhibitor amastatin and general metalloprotease inhibitors o-phenanthroline (zinc chelator) and batimastat (broad-spectrum MMP inhibitor), confirming zinc-dependence and drug targetability; (6) Nuclear-cytosolic shuttling—dual nucleoplasm/nuclear bodies/cytosol localization suggests potential roles in both nuclear protein turnover and cytoplasmic substrate processing. Loss-of-function causes defective sperm head formation with vacuolization, dysregulated angiotensin peptide levels, altered ECM remodeling in cancer contexts, and increased lung tumorigenesis susceptibility.
