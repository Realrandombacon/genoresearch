# HROB: OB-Fold Single-Stranded DNA Binding Scaffold Recruiting MCM8-MCM9 Helicase Complex to DNA Double-Strand Breaks for Homologous Recombination Repair

## Description
HROB (homologous recombination factor with OB-fold, alias C17orf53, MCM8IP, ODG11) encodes a 647-amino acid nucleoplasmic protein belonging to the HROB family (IPR028045/PTHR14523, residues 14-629) that functions as a single-stranded DNA (ssDNA) binding scaffold recruiting the MCM8-MCM9 helicase complex to DNA double-strand breaks (DSBs) for homologous recombination (HR) repair. The protein contains a C-terminal OB-fold domain (IPR058570/PF15072, residues 493-576) characteristic of oligonucleotide/oligosaccharide binding folds that recognize ssDNA structures at resected DSB ends. AlphaFold predicts medium confidence structure (pLDDT=54.8), indicating a partially folded protein with structured OB-fold core and flexible N-terminal regions typical of dynamic DNA repair scaffolds. HPA classifies HROB as a predicted intracellular protein with tissue-enhanced expression (testis 10.7 nTPM, bone marrow 8.8 nTPM), nucleoplasm/cytosol localization, and DNA damage/repair/recombination/synthesis annotations, consistent with roles in proliferating cells undergoing replication stress or meiotic recombination. Gene ontology annotations include recombinational repair (GO:0000725), positioning HROB within HR pathway for error-free DSB repair. STRING interactions reveal strong coupling to MCM helicase machinery: MCM8 (0.753, DNA helicase MCM8, HR-specific replicative helicase), MCM8-2 (0.658, MCM8 isoform), MCM9 (0.613, DNA helicase MCM9, MCM8 partner), ZBTB40 (0.530, zinc finger BTB domain protein, chromatin regulator), TMEM89 (0.507, transmembrane protein), STARD3NL (0.447, START domain protein), FAM199X (0.447, family with sequence similarity), FAM210A (0.445, family with sequence similarity), DCDC1 (0.433, doublecortin domain protein), and FAM71E1 (0.431, family with sequence similarity). The MCM8-MCM9 heterohexamer represents the canonical HR helicase that unwinds DNA at DSBs for strand invasion, with HROB serving as a recruitment factor. ClinVar documents 15 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including nonsense mutations (p.Arg451Ter, p.Arg240Ter), frameshift deletions (p.Glu141fs), splice site variants (c.54+1G>A, c.1309-1G>C, c.1771-234G>T), and 17q12-22 copy number losses/gains, indicating dosage sensitivity that may contribute to genomic instability disorders, premature ovarian insufficiency, or cancer predisposition. Conservation is evident across mammals: mouse (Q32P12, 615 aa), rat (Q6GX86, 607 aa). Mechanistically, HROB functions as an OB-fold ssDNA binding protein that recognizes 3′ ssDNA overhangs generated by DSB resection (via MRN-CtIP-EXO1/DNA2). The OB-fold domain binds ssDNA with sequence-independent affinity, anchoring HROB to resected DSBs. HROB then recruits the MCM8-MCM9 helicase complex through direct protein-protein interactions, loading the helicase onto ssDNA for 3′→5′ translocation and D-loop formation during strand invasion. This positions HROB as a critical HR initiation factor analogous to BRCA2-RAD51 loading but specific to the MCM8-MCM9 pathway. Loss of HROB impairs MCM8-MCM9 recruitment, causing defective D-loop formation, reduced HR efficiency, increased error-prone NHEJ usage, and genomic instability. Testis/bone marrow expression reflects high HR demand in meiotic cells and hematopoietic stem cells. HROB represents a paradigm of OB-fold-mediated helicase recruitment in genome maintenance.
