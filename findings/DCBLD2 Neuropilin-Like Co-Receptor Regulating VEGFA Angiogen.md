## Description
DCBLD2 encodes a 775-amino acid discoidin, CUB and LCCL domain-containing protein 2 (UniProt Q96PD2), a type I transmembrane protein with structural similarity to neuropilins that functions as a co-receptor for receptor tyrosine kinases (RTKs). The protein contains a modular domain architecture: N-terminal CUB domain (IPR000859, PF00431, positions 72-187) for protein-protein interactions, LCCL domain (IPR004043, PF03815, positions 191-285) mediating ligand binding, and F5/8 type C domain (IPR000421, PF00754, positions 291-449) for membrane association, classifying it within the neuropilin/multicopper oxidase/coagulation factor superfamily (IPR050633, positions 43-754). AlphaFold predicts a medium-confidence structure (pLDDT 67.7, residues 1-775) with partially ordered domains and flexible regions consistent with co-receptor conformational dynamics. STRING interaction analysis reveals DCBLD2 associates with semaphorin SEMA4B (0.906, high confidence), transcription factor E2F7 (0.557), mitotic regulator ASPM (0.536), insulin receptor INSR (0.442), EGFR (0.432), and adaptor CRKL (0.454), supporting roles in RTK signaling and semaphorin pathways. HPA data shows ubiquitous expression across all tissues with Golgi apparatus, plasma membrane, and cytosolic localization, consistent with trafficking co-receptor function. ClinVar contains 11 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number gains/losses at 3q11-21 and splice variants (c.1671-11T>C), though specific Mendelian disorders remain undefined. With 105 publications, DCBLD2 is moderately characterized: recent studies identify DCBLD2 potentiating VEGFA-driven angiogenesis in retinal detachment with choroidal detachment (2025), DCBLD2 regulating Hedgehog signaling to drive pancreatic cancer progression (2025), EGFR phosphorylation of DCBLD2 recruiting TRAF6 to stimulate AKT-promoted tumorigenesis (2025), lncRNA MIR100HG/miR-5590-3p/DCBLD2 axis in lung cancer metastasis (2024, 6 cit), and DCBLD2 deletion causing hyperglycemia and vascular remodeling by inhibiting insulin receptor recycling in endothelial cells (2024, 3 cit). The functional hypothesis: DCBLD2 acts as a context-dependent RTK co-receptor that modulates ligand binding and receptor trafficking for VEGFR (angiogenesis), EGFR (tumorigenesis via TRAF6-AKT), and INSR (glucose homeostasis), with tissue-specific effects explaining vascular, metabolic, and oncogenic phenotypes; disease variants may disrupt extracellular domain interactions or intracellular trafficking signals, altering RTK signaling amplitude and duration.
