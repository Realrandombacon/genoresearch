## Description
CES4A (Q5XG92) encodes a 561 amino acid type-B carboxylesterase belonging to the serine hydrolase superfamily that functions as an intracellular enzyme involved in lipid metabolism, xenobiotic detoxification, and tumor suppression. InterPro analysis reveals the canonical carboxylesterase architecture built on an alpha/beta hydrolase fold (IPR029058/G3DSA:3.40.50.1820, positions 22-560) with a large esterase_lipase domain (cd00312, positions 29-537; IPR002018/PF00135 carboxylesterase type B, positions 26-546) containing the catalytic machinery. The active site (IPR019826, positions 208-223) features the conserved serine nucleophile characteristic of type-B carboxylesterases (PS00122), while conserved signature motifs (IPR019819/PS00941, positions 114-124) define substrate specificity. AlphaFold predicts an exceptionally well-folded structure (pLDDT 92.8, very high confidence) consistent with stable enzymatic activity. STRING interactions identify only two medium-confidence partners: TMEM265 (0.477), a transmembrane protein of unknown function, and AADAC (0.441), another arylacetamide deacetylase/carboxylesterase family member suggesting functional redundancy or co-regulation in xenobiotic metabolism. HPA shows tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in fallopian tube (16.9 nTPM), cytosolic localization, and cancer-enhanced expression patterns consistent with roles in tissue-specific lipid handling and tumor metabolism. ClinVar contains 28 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including multiple copy number gains and losses spanning chromosome 16q12.2-q24.3 regions, though specific monogenic disease associations remain undefined. Literature (only 6 papers) demonstrates CES4A inhibits malignant biological behavior in nasopharyngeal carcinoma through PI3K/AKT pathway suppression, serves as a biomarker in glioma subtype classification, and belongs to the five mammalian carboxylesterase gene families involved in drug and lipid metabolism. The mechanistic hypothesis: CES4A functions as a cytosolic serine hydrolase that hydrolyzes ester bonds in endogenous lipids (potentially cholesterol esters, triglycerides, or signaling lipids) and exogenous xenobiotics (drugs, environmental toxins) using its catalytic serine within the alpha/beta hydrolase scaffold; in cancer contexts, CES4A acts as a tumor suppressor by metabolizing oncogenic lipid signaling molecules that would otherwise activate PI3K/AKT survival pathways, explaining why CES4A loss promotes nasopharyngeal carcinoma progression; tissue-specific expression in fallopian tube suggests specialized roles in reproductive tract lipid homeostasis; copy number alterations on 16q disrupt CES4A dosage, potentially contributing to metabolic disorders or cancer susceptibility through impaired detoxification capacity and dysregulated lipid signaling; functional overlap with AADAC may provide compensatory mechanisms but also creates vulnerability when both enzymes are compromised.
