## Description
ACAD10 (Acyl-CoA dehydrogenase family member 10) is an emerging metabolic enzyme with only 49 publications, recently characterized (2024-2025) as enabling mammalian 4-hydroxy acid lipid catabolism and encoding orphan enzymes in ether lipid biosynthetic/salvage pathways. This large 1059-amino acid mitochondrial protein (UniProt Q6JQN1) contains multiple functional domains: N-terminal lipase phosphatase domain (cd02603, positions 43-242, potentially inactive), Acyl-CoA dehydrogenase N-terminal domain (cd05154, positions 286-536), aminoglycoside phosphotransferase domain (IPR002575, positions 288-502), and multiple ACAD catalytic domains (G3DSA:1.10.540.10, G3DSA:1.20.140.10, G3DSA:2.40.110.10). AlphaFold predicts high-confidence structure (pLDDT 86.9), indicating stable multi-domain architecture.\n\nSTRING interactions place ACAD10 within fatty acid oxidation network: MRI1 (0.842, methylthioribulose-1-phosphate dehydratase), HSDL2 (0.750, hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase-like), HADH (0.715, hydroxyacyl-CoA dehydrogenase), CRYL1 (0.677, crystallin lambda-1), ECI2 (0.677, enoyl-CoA delta isomerase), plus HACL1 (0.473, 2-hydroxyacyl-CoA lyase) and ALDH2 (0.412, aldehyde dehydrogenase). This interaction profile confirms roles in specialized fatty acid metabolism beyond canonical β-oxidation.\n\nHPA shows ubiquitous expression across all tissues with low tissue specificity and cell type-enhanced expression. ClinVar contains 7 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number gains and losses on 12q23-24, though specific diseases remain uncharacterized. Conservation across mouse (1069 aa) indicates functional constraint.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: ACAD10 is a specialized mitochondrial dehydrogenase that processes 4-hydroxy fatty acids and ether lipid intermediates that are incompatible with standard β-oxidation machinery. The N-terminal phosphatase domain (potentially inactive) may have evolved to regulate substrate access or coordinate with the C-terminal ACAD catalytic core. Recent work shows ACAD10 enables entry of 4-hydroxy fatty acids into β-oxidation, suggesting it acts as a gatekeeper for oxidized lipid metabolites generated during oxidative stress or peroxisomal metabolism. Pathogenic CNVs may disrupt specialized lipid catabolism, potentially contributing to metabolic disorders with neurological or hepatic manifestations given the brain expression and mitochondrial localization.
