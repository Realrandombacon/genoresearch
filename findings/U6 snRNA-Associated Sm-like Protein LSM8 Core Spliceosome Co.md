# U6 snRNA-Associated Sm-like Protein LSM8: Core Spliceosome Component with Emerging Cancer Fusion Relevance

**Date:** 2026-06-28T13:27:06.182805

**Quality Score:** 9/10 (EXCELLENT)

**Confidence:** MEDIUM

## Description
LSM8 (LSM8 Homolog, U6 Small Nuclear RNA Associated) is a 96-amino acid essential nuclear protein functioning as a critical component of the U6 snRNP and U4/U6 x U5 tri-snRNP complexes required for pre-mRNA splicing fidelity. InterPro analysis confirms a canonical Sm-fold domain architecture (IPR001163/Sm-domain eukaryotic-archaeal, positions 3-72; IPR034103/Sm-like-protein-Lsm8, 3-92; IPR044642/U6-snRNA-associated-Sm-like-Lsm1/8, 1-94; PF01423/LSM-domain, 5-71) forming the characteristic toroidal ring structure that binds U6 snRNA through conserved RNA-binding surfaces (GO:0003723/RNA-binding, GO:0000398/mRNA-splicing-via-spliceosome). STRING interactions reveal an exceptionally tight network (all scores 0.998-0.999) with core spliceosome machinery: SNRPD1, LSM7, LSM6, LSM3 (other Lsm ring components), PRPF3, PRPF6 (splicing factors), SART1 (recycling factor), SNRNP200 (helicase), SNRPF, and SNRPD3 (Sm core proteins), confirming its role as an integral member of the constitutive splicing apparatus rather than a regulatory factor. HPA data shows ubiquitous expression across all tissues with low specificity, nucleoplasmic and vesicular localization, classification as an essential protein and plasma protein, consistent with housekeeping function in mRNA processing. ClinVar contains 22 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains and losses spanning 7q22.1-36.3 regions, though specific monogenic disease phenotypes remain under characterization (significance marked '?' for most); notably, a novel LSM8-MET fusion was identified in 2021 as a tertiary resistance mechanism to selpercatinib/capmatinib in RET-positive NSCLC with secondary MET amplification, suggesting oncogenic potential when dysregulated. AlphaFold structure (AF-O95777-F1) predicts very high confidence folding (pLDDT 95.4) consistent with the highly conserved Sm-fold β-barrel structure. With only 38 papers, LSM8 represents a 'gray' gene: while its molecular function in spliceosome assembly is well-established from yeast studies (2001-2012), human-specific roles remain understudied; recent work (2026) demonstrates conserved Lsm8-exosome modules maintaining RNA splicing fidelity for stress adaptation in fungi, and the MET fusion discovery suggests potential oncogenic mechanisms via aberrant tyrosine kinase activation. The mechanistic hypothesis posits that LSM8 functions as a stoichiometric scaffold enabling U6 snRNA stability and catalytic center formation within the spliceosome, with haploinsufficiency potentially causing splicing errors in rapidly dividing cells (cancer) or developmental defects, while fusion events may drive oncogenic signaling through MET pathway activation.
