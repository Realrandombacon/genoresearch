## Description
SLC12A9 (Q9BXP2, 914 aa), also known as CCC6 or CIP1, is a member of the electroneutral cation-chloride cotransporter (CCC) family within the SLC12A solute carrier group. InterPro identifies a canonical architecture for this family: an N-terminal amino acid permease/SLC12A domain (IPR004841, PF00324, aa 42-535) forming the core transport module, followed by a specific SLC12A C-terminal domain (IPR018491, PF03522, aa 547-634) involved in regulatory interactions, all embedded within the larger SLC12A transporter family scaffold (IPR004842, PTHR11827, aa 33-803). The structure belongs to the amino acid/polyamine transporter I superfamily (G3DSA:1.20.1740.10, aa 33-497), consistent with the LeuT-fold adopted by many neurotransmitter and ion transporters. AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (pLDDT 81.2, residues 1-914), indicating a stable, well-folded transmembrane protein with extensive cytoplasmic N- and C-terminal tails typical of CCC regulators. HPA shows low tissue specificity with expression detected in many tissues; classified as a predicted membrane protein and transporter involved in general transport processes. STRING reveals 10 interactions linking it to ion transport and signaling networks: SLC12A2 (0.679, Na-K-2Cl cotransporter NKCC1), ANKRD13D (0.546, ankyrin repeat protein), TRIP6 (0.497, thyroid receptor-interacting protein 6), CLSTN2 (0.488, calsyntenin-2), PASK (0.488, PAS kinase), MAP3K7CL (0.483), MAP3K11 (0.483, MLK3), MNT (0.481), C10orf62 (0.449), and LRCH4 (0.440). The strong link to SLC12A2 suggests functional redundancy or heteromeric complex formation in chloride handling. ClinVar contains 33 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including splice site mutations (c.1344-3C>A, c.866-22T>G, c.182-3C>T) and frameshifts/deletions primarily mapped to the neighboring EPHB4 gene in large CNVs, but also specific intronic variants within SLC12A9, suggesting potential dosage sensitivity or disruption of splicing, though no specific monogenic disease is yet explicitly assigned (potential links to renal salt wasting or neurological defects). Functional hypothesis: SLC12A9 functions as an electroneutral potassium-chloride (K-Cl) symporter responsible for cell volume regulation and transepithelial chloride reabsorption, particularly in the kidney and nervous system. Unlike other CCC members with defined roles (e.g., NKCC1 in secretion, KCC2 in neuronal inhibition), SLC12A9's ubiquitous expression and interaction with SLC12A2 suggest it acts as a fine-tuner of basal Cl- flux or a backup system for volume recovery after osmotic stress. The long cytoplasmic tails likely serve as platforms for phosphorylation-dependent regulation by kinases like WNK/SPAK or interacting partners like TRIP6, modulating transport activity in response to cellular signals. Disruption via splice variants or CNVs could impair renal salt handling (leading to hypotension or electrolyte imbalance) or neuronal excitability (contributing to epilepsy or neurodevelopmental disorders). Conservation across vertebrates (mouse 914 aa, zebrafish 899 aa) confirms essential physiological role.
