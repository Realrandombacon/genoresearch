## Description
PODN encodes a 613 amino acid secreted proteoglycan (UniProt Q7Z5L7), also known as Podocan, that functions as a small leucine-rich proteoglycan (SLRP) family member regulating extracellular matrix organization, collagen fibrillogenesis, and TGF-β signaling pathway modulation. With 61 publications, PODN is moderately characterized—its SLRP classification was established but emerging roles in cancer prognosis, immune infiltration, and epigenetic regulation through m6A methylation reveal previously unappreciated functions in tumor biology.\n\nSTRUCTURAL FEATURES: AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (pLDDT 86.6) across all 613 residues, indicating a well-folded stable proteoglycan characteristic of leucine-rich repeat proteins. InterPro analysis reveals extensive LRR domain architecture optimized for protein-protein interactions: Leucine-rich repeat N-terminal domain (IPR000372, residues 67-100) capping the LRR solenoid; Canonical leucine-rich repeats (IPR001611/IPR032675/PF13855/PS51450, residues 238-310/380-452/451-513/289-310/431-452/523-544) forming the curved solenoid structure with GO annotation for protein binding (GO:0005515); Leucine-rich repeat typical subtype (IPR003591, residues 119-144/145-168/191-215); BspA-type LRR region (IPR026906/PF13306, residues 106-218) with 6 copies; Small leucine-rich proteoglycans family (IPR50333, residues 212-468). The high pLDDT confirms an extended curved solenoid architecture typical of SLRPs, with concave surface optimized for binding collagen fibrils and growth factors.\n\nINTERACTION NETWORK: STRING-DB identifies 10 interaction partners with medium confidence (0.430-0.563), linking PODN to extracellular matrix organization and cell adhesion. While no specific monogenic disease is yet exclusively attributed to PODN point mutations, the chromosomal location overlaps with 1p deletion syndromes, and recent literature links PODN to cancer prognosis and treatment response.\n\nFUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: PODN functions through coordinated mechanisms: (1) Collagen fibrillogenesis—the LRR solenoid (residues 106-544) binds collagen fibrils (COL1A2 0.525, COL3A1 0.518) regulating fibril diameter and organization analogous to decorin/biglycan. (2) Integrin signaling—interaction with ITGA11 (0.563) mediates cell-ECM adhesion and mechanotransduction. (3) TGF-β modulation—recent literature (2022, 6 citations) reveals hypermethylated PODN represses osteosarcoma progression by inactivating TGF-β/Smad2/3 pathway, suggesting PODN normally promotes TGF-β signaling. (4) Cancer biomarker—PODN is prognostic biomarker in osteosarcoma (2021, 19 citations) correlated with immune infiltrates; m6A methylation of PODN regulated by METTL3 synergizes with sorafenib in papillary thyroid cancer (2025, 0 citations). (5) Bladder mechanotransduction—miR-3180-5p targeting PODN promotes bladder smooth muscle cell proliferation under hydrodynamic pressure (2016, 21 citations). Mechanistically, PODN LRR domains may sequester TGF-β or present it to receptors, while epigenetic silencing (hypermethylation, m6A) reduces PODN expression altering ECM-tumor crosstalk. Loss-of-function would impair collagen organization and reduce TGF-β signaling; gain-of-function drives fibrosis and cancer progression through enhanced ECM remodeling.
