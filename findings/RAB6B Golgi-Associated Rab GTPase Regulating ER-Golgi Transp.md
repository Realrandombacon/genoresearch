## Description
RAB6B (Ras-Related Protein Rab-6B, UniProt Q9NRW1) is a 208 amino acid Golgi apparatus and basal body-localized small GTPase with moderate characterization (58 papers) that functions as a key regulator of ER-Golgi transport, vesicle trafficking, and cytokine release through GTP-dependent membrane targeting. The protein contains a Rab6 GTPase family domain (cd01861, residues 14-174), small GTPase domain (IPR001806/IPR005225, residues 9-208/13-169) with GTPase activity and GTP binding function (GO:0003924/GO:0005525), Ras family domain (PF00071/SM00173, residues 15-173/11-177), and belongs to the P-loop containing NTP hydrolase superfamily (G3DSA:3.40.50.300/IPR027417, residues 9-200), placing it in the Rab GTPase family involved in vesicle trafficking and membrane fusion. STRING interactions reveal high-confidence partnerships with Golgi trafficking machinery: GCC2 (0.883, GRIP domain-containing protein 2), GDI2 (0.874, GDP dissociation inhibitor 2), RAB6A (0.869, paralog), RIC1 (0.867, RAB6A-interacting protein 1), GDI1 (0.855, GDP dissociation inhibitor 1), TMF1 (0.839, TATA element modulatory factor 1), GDAP1 (0.779, ganglioside-induced differentiation-associated protein 1), VPS39 (0.765, vacuolar protein sorting 39), COG6 (0.737, conserved oligomeric Golgi complex subunit 6), and ARL1 (0.534, ADP-ribosylation factor-like 1), placing RAB6B in the Golgi vesicle tethering and trafficking network. HPA data shows brain enrichment (131.0 nTPM, tissue enriched), immune cell enrichment, Golgi apparatus and basal body localization, ER-Golgi transport and protein transport biological processes, and low cancer specificity. ClinVar contains 15 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number gains at 3q22.1-29/3q13.31-26.31/3p26.3-q29/3q11.1-24, copy number losses at 3q22.1-25.1/3q22.1-22.3/3q22.1-24, and single nucleotide variants (c.71-72C>G), though no specific diseases are directly assigned—suggesting potential neurodevelopmental, immunologic, or trafficking disorder phenotypes. AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 85.0) indicates high confidence with well-folded Rab GTPase core typical of small GTPases. Functional hypothesis: RAB6B acts as a GTP-dependent molecular switch localized to the Golgi apparatus where it cycles between active GTP-bound and inactive GDP-bound states to regulate vesicle budding, tethering, and fusion through its switch I/II regions, where recent 2021-2023 research demonstrates: (1) structural basis of ELKS/RAB6B interaction reveals vesicle capturing enhanced by liquid-liquid phase separation (LLPS) at Golgi membranes (2023, 11 citations)—RAB6B recruits ELKS proteins to form condensates that capture secretory vesicles, (2) miR-6216 regulates neural stem cell proliferation by targeting RAB6B (2023, 3 citations), linking RAB6B to neurogenesis, (3) RAB6B is a potential prognostic marker in hepatocellular carcinoma correlated with tumor immune microenvironment remodeling (2022, 5 citations), (4) RAB6B localizes to Golgi in murine macrophages and promotes TNF release in response to mycobacterial infection (2021, 8 citations), establishing role in innate immunity, (5) STRING partners (RAB6A paralog, GDI1/2 regulators, GCC2/RIC1 effectors, COG6 tethering complex) confirm conserved Golgi trafficking function, with brain enrichment (131.0 nTPM) suggesting neuronal vesicle trafficking roles and 15 ClinVar variants at 3q22.1 potentially contributing to neurodevelopmental disorders or immunodeficiency through Golgi trafficking defects, making RAB6B a potential therapeutic target for mycobacterial infections, HCC, and neurodevelopmental disorders through Rab GTPase pathway modulation.
