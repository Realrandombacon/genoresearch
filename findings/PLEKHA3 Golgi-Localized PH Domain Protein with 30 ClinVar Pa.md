## Description
PLEKHA3 encodes a 300 amino acid pleckstrin homology domain-containing family A member 3 (UniProt Q9HB20), a Golgi apparatus-localized protein that functions in phosphoinositide-dependent membrane trafficking and lipid transport. The protein contains a canonical pleckstrin homology (PH) domain (IPR001849/PF00169/PS50003/SM00233 at positions 1-95/1-91/1-93/1-95) embedded within a larger PH domain superfamily scaffold (G3DSA:2.30.29.30 at positions 1-100; IPR011993 at positions 1-100; SSF50729 at positions 1-91). The PH domain is characteristic of the Boi1/Boi2-like protein family (IPR045188/PTHR22902 at positions 1-297), which in yeast regulates polarized growth through phosphoinositide binding and Rho GTPase activation.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals PLEKHA3 forms a functional network centered on Golgi-to-plasma membrane lipid trafficking: ARF1 (score 0.998—ADP-ribosylation factor 1, master regulator of Golgi vesicle budding), CEL (0.902—carboxyl ester lipase), GLTP (0.902—glycolipid transfer protein), CDKN2A (0.701—cell cycle regulator p16INK4a), PI4KB (0.620—phosphatidylinositol 4-kinase beta, generates PI4P at Golgi), OSBP (0.611—oxysterol-binding protein, cholesterol/PI4P exchange at Golgi-ER contact sites), SNF8 (0.601—ESCRT-II component), PLCD1 (0.572—phospholipase C delta 1), PLEK2 (0.400), and PLEK (0.400). This interaction profile strongly suggests PLEKHA3 functions as a PH domain adaptor that recruits ARF1 effectors to PI4P-enriched Golgi membranes, coordinating vesicle budding and lipid transfer for secretory pathway trafficking.\n\nHPA expression data classifies PLEKHA3 as detected in all tissues with low tissue specificity and cell type-enhanced expression patterns. Subcellular localization is definitively Golgi apparatus, consistent with its predicted role in Golgi membrane trafficking. Protein class is predicted intracellular proteins.\n\nClinVar documents 30 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number losses (2q31.1-32.2 deletions spanning 18 Mb; 2q31.1-31.3 deletions), copy number gains (2p25.3-q37.3 whole chromosome gains; 2q31.1-35 gains; 2q31.2-37.3 duplications), complex rearrangements (NC_000002.11:g.(?_178257518)_(179914668_?)del), duplications (NC_000002.11:g.(?_179296824)_(179502166_?)dup), and missense variants (NM_019091.4:c.515A>G, p.Glu172Gly—within the PH domain). Critically, ClinVar lists 0 associated diseases—a major knowledge gap given the substantial variant burden and essential Golgi trafficking function. This suggests PLEKHA3 dysfunction may cause undiagnosed developmental disorders affecting secretory pathways (neurodevelopmental defects, skeletal dysplasia from impaired collagen secretion), metabolic disorders (lipid trafficking defects), or cancer predisposition (CDKN2A interaction suggests cell cycle regulation).\n\nAlphaFold structure prediction (AF-Q9HB20-F1) yields a global pLDDT score of 71.5 (high confidence) across all 300 residues, indicating a well-folded PH domain structure with stable phosphoinositide-binding surfaces suitable for Golgi membrane recruitment.\n\nLiterature analysis (7 papers) confirms genuine dark gene status with minimal characterization. Related studies mention PLEKHA3 in cardiac conduction disorder GWAS, copy number variants in short stature, and as part of the Plekha protein family (Plekha7 being the best-studied member). No direct functional studies exist. Key unknowns include: specific disease phenotypes from loss-of-function mutations, precise phosphoinositide specificity of the PH domain, role in ARF1-dependent vesicle budding mechanisms, contribution to Golgi-ER contact site formation via OSBP interaction, and therapeutic potential for trafficking disorders.
