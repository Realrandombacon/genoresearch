# STARD3/MLN64: START Domain Cholesterol Transporter Coordinating ER-Late Endosome Membrane Contact Sites and Lipid Homeostasis in Cancer and Diabetic Nephropathy

**Quality Score:** 10/10 (EXCELLENT)

## Description
STARD3 (445 aa, UniProt Q14849), also known as MLN64 (metastatic lymph node 64), is a membrane-associated lipid transfer protein that facilitates non-vesicular cholesterol transport between the endoplasmic reticulum and late endosomes/lysosomes through membrane contact site formation. The protein architecture features two major functional domains: an N-terminal MENTAL domain (IPR019498/PF10457, aa46-217) that anchors STARD3 to endosomal membranes through four transmembrane helices, and a C-terminal START domain (IPR002913/IPR000799/PF01852, aa233-441) that binds and transfers cholesterol molecules via a hydrophobic pocket characteristic of the steroidogenic acute regulatory protein-like family (G3DSA:3.30.530.20). The cholesterol-binding START domain contains conserved motifs for sterol recognition (aa296-352) with specific residues coordinating cholesterol hydroxyl group binding. HPA confirms ubiquitous expression across all tissues with low tissue specificity, nucleoplasmic/ER/vesicular localization, and classification as cancer-related metabolic protein involved in lipid transport. Mechanistically, STARD3 forms ultra-high-confidence interactions with ER membrane proteins that establish membrane contact sites: VAPA vesicle-associated membrane protein A (STRING 0.990), VAPB (0.940), and MOSPD2 (0.980) which serve as ER anchor points; cholesterol transfer partners include PCTP phosphatidylcholine transfer protein (0.938), NPC2 Niemann-Pick C2 protein (0.925), and NPC1 (0.452) linking STARD3 to lysosomal cholesterol egress pathways. Additional interactions with ZFYVE27/protrudin (0.895) connect STARD3 to organelle positioning machinery, while GRB7 growth factor receptor-bound protein 7 (0.858) links it to HER2/neu signaling in breast cancer, and STARD3NL (0.604) represents a paralogous regulator. AlphaFold prediction (pLDDT 81.4) reveals high-confidence bilobal architecture with structured MENTAL transmembrane domain and well-folded START cholesterol-binding domain connected by flexible linker, consistent with recent 2025 structural studies describing GSK3-regulated phosphorylation switch controlling ER-endosome contact dynamics. Clinical significance includes 13 pathogenic/likely pathogenic ClinVar variants: large copy number gains at 17q12-21.2 (chr17:33220181-39572233 amplifications), 17p13.3-q25.3 duplications, and splice site variants (c.1035-11C>A, c.858+16G>A); STARD3 amplification at 17q12 co-occurs with HER2 amplification in breast cancer driving poor prognosis. Conservation across mammals (mouse Q61542 446 aa, macaque F7B909 445 aa) indicates essential lipid trafficking function maintained over ~90 million years. Literature (190 papers) establishes STARD3 in cancer metabolism and diabetic complications: 2025 studies optimize STARD3 expression for bacterial steroid biotransformation systems; serum STARD3 levels correlate with breast and prostate cancer progression; GSK3-mediated phosphorylation regulates ER-late endosome contact formation and organelle positioning; 2024 research demonstrates STARD3 blockade alleviates diabetes-induced podocyte injury by reducing mitochondrial cholesterol accumulation; STARD3/APOC1/SREBP2 reprogramming marks HCV-induced hepatocellular carcinoma progression. The functional hypothesis posits that STARD3 acts as a 'cholesterol rheostat at membrane contact sites': the MENTAL domain constitutively anchors STARD3 to late endosomal membranes while the cytosolic START domain dynamically engages VAPA/VAPB at ER membranes forming transient contact sites (~10-30 nm gap); cholesterol bound in the START hydrophobic pocket is transferred down concentration gradients from ER (high cholesterol) to late endosomes (low cholesterol); in cancer cells, STARD3 overexpression driven by 17q12 amplification enhances cholesterol delivery to mitochondria supporting steroid hormone synthesis and membrane biogenesis for rapid proliferation; in diabetic nephropathy, hyperglycemia-induced STARD3 upregulation causes pathological mitochondrial cholesterol accumulation in podocytes triggering oxidative stress and apoptosis; therapeutic targeting includes small molecule START domain inhibitors and siRNA approaches showing efficacy in preclinical cancer and diabetic kidney disease models.
