# CDH15/M-Cadherin: Muscle Satellite Cell Adhesion Receptor Regulating Myogenic Differentiation via Catenin Complexes and WNT Signaling in Intellectual Disability and Muscle Regeneration

## Description
CDH15 (Cadherin-15, M-Cadherin, P55291) encodes an 814 aa type I transmembrane cell adhesion protein functioning as a muscle satellite cell-specific cadherin that regulates myogenic differentiation, muscle regeneration, and neurodevelopment through homophilic cell-cell adhesion and catenin-mediated signaling. Structural architecture reveals five extracellular cadherin repeat domains (IPR002126/IPR000233/G3DSA:2.60.40.60, residues 51-152/152-271/272-375/364-485/477-582/52-148/157-256/271-371) containing calcium-binding motifs for rigid rod formation and homophilic binding specificity, plus intracellular catenin-binding domain (IPR027397/G3DSA:4.10.900.10, residues 691-783) anchoring the actin cytoskeleton; AlphaFold prediction (pLDDT 78.4) confirms well-folded structure consistent with stable membrane adhesion complex. STRING interactions define adherens junction and WNT signaling networks: beta-catenin CTNNB1 (0.957), p120-catenin CTNND1 (0.892), alpha-catenin CTNNA1 (0.875), neural cadherins CDH7 (0.835)/CDH2 (0.819)/CDH6 (0.704)/CDH10 (0.672)/CDH20 (0.585)/CDH19 (0.575), and myogenic regulator MYF5 (0.402). HPA localizes CDH15 to Golgi apparatus and plasma membrane with group-enriched expression in brain (79.7 nTPM) and skeletal muscle (54.1 nTPM), classified as disease-related membrane protein involved in cell adhesion with intellectual disability involvement. ClinVar documents 94 pathogenic variants including 16q23.1-24.3 CNVs, deletions (p.Tyr739del, p.Gln699fs), missense (p.Pro708Ser, p.Gly694Ala), and splice site mutations (c.1993-5C>G). Literature (73 papers) demonstrates critical roles: anti-CDH15 monoclonal antibody Ca15Mab-1 development for muscle satellite cell detection (6x citations, 2025), novel CDH15 mutation in Iranian boy with borderline intelligence without dysmorphism showing reduced penetrance (2025), CDH13/CDH15 gene role in repeat revascularization after PCI (2x citations, 2019), CDH15 and KIRREL3 alterations in mild to severe intellectual disability (126x citations, 2008). Mechanistic hypothesis: CDH15 mediates homophilic adhesion between muscle satellite cells and myoblasts during regeneration; extracellular cadherin repeats bind calcium ions forming rigid rods that enable trans-homophilic dimerization across adjacent cell membranes; intracellular domain recruits catenin complex (CTNNB1/CTNND1/CTNNA1) linking to actin cytoskeleton for mechanical stability; CTNNB1 interaction couples CDH15 to WNT signaling pathway regulating MYF5 expression and myogenic differentiation; in brain, CDH15 mediates neuronal cell adhesion during development, with loss causing intellectual disability through disrupted synaptic connectivity; cancer-enhanced expression suggests role in tumor cell migration and metastasis.
