## Description
QRFPR encodes a 431 aa class A rhodopsin-like G protein-coupled receptor (UniProt Q96P65), also known as GPR103 or AQ27, that functions as the cognate receptor for the orexigenic neuropeptide QRFP (pyroglutamylated RF-amide peptide, also called 26RFa or 43RFa). The protein features a canonical seven-transmembrane helix architecture characteristic of rhodopsin-family GPCRs: an N-terminal extracellular domain (residues 1-46) containing N-glycosylation sites essential for ligand binding and receptor activation; seven transmembrane helices (IPR000276/IPR017452/PF00001/cd15205/G3DSA:1.20.1070.10, residues 47-340/62-332/41-377) forming the ligand-binding pocket and G protein coupling interface; Neuropeptide Y receptor family signature motifs (IPR000611/PR01012, residues 72-84/102-114/125-140) indicating evolutionary relationship to NPY/Y2 receptors; and a C-terminal intracellular domain (residues 341-431) containing phosphorylation sites for GRK-mediated desensitization and β-arrestin recruitment. ClinVar lists 22 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number gains (4q21.21-31.21 spanning 79-146Mb, 4q24-35.2 spanning 101-191Mb) and losses (4q26-28.2 spanning 117-130Mb), demonstrating dosage sensitivity though specific disease associations remain undefined—potentially contributing to metabolic syndrome, obesity, or neurodevelopmental phenotypes. AlphaFold structure prediction yields high confidence (global pLDDT 78.8), revealing well-folded 7TM bundle consistent with active GPCR conformation. Mechanistically, QRFPR functions through multiple integrated modes: (1) Ligand binding—QRFP-26 and QRFP-43 peptides bind the transmembrane pocket with nanomolar affinity, triggering conformational changes; (2) Gq/11 coupling—activates phospholipase Cβ via GNAQ, generating IP3-mediated Ca2+ release and DAG-mediated PKC activation; (3) Lipogenesis regulation—stimulates adipocyte differentiation and lipid accumulation through PPARγ and C/EBPα transcriptional activation; (4) Food intake stimulation—hypothalamic QRFPR activation increases appetite and reduces energy expenditure; (5) PI3K-AKT-mTOR modulation—genetic variants alter downstream signaling affecting cell growth and metabolism (2021 study); (6) N-glycosylation dependence—proper glycosylation at N-terminal asparagine residues is essential for surface expression and ligand binding (2021 study); (7) Reproductive axis integration—KISS1 interaction suggests cross-talk with gonadotropin regulation. Disruption of QRFPR would cause altered feeding behavior, impaired lipogenesis, potential metabolic dysfunction, and possible reproductive abnormalities—phenotypes observed in Qrfpr knockout mice showing reduced food intake and resistance to diet-induced obesity. With 50 literature papers focusing on ligand identification and metabolic roles but incomplete disease mechanism elucidation, QRFPR represents a partially characterized neuropeptide receptor.
