## Description
MTRF1L (Mitochondrial Translation Release Factor 1-Like) is a 380 aa mitochondrial protein representing a genuine dark gene with only 13 literature references despite essential roles in mitochondrial translation termination and oxidative phosphorylation. Multi-omics data reveals MTRF1L as a highly conserved bacterial-derived release factor adapted for mitochondrial protein synthesis. InterPro analysis confirms a bipartite domain architecture characteristic of class I release factors: an N-terminal PCRF domain (IPR005139/PF03462, aa 64-221) mediating ribosome binding and codon recognition, followed by a C-terminal RF-1 domain (IPR000352/PF00472, aa 229-338) containing the conserved GGQ motif (aa 245-261, PS00745) essential for peptidyl-tRNA hydrolysis. The protein belongs to the Prokaryotic/Mitochondrial Release Factor superfamily (IPR045853/IPR050057/PTHR43804, aa 63-378), reflecting its evolutionary origin from bacterial RF1. AlphaFold predicts high-confidence structure (pLDDT 83.8), indicating a well-folded protein with stable domain organization suitable for precise ribosome interactions. HPA shows ubiquitous expression across all tissues (detected in all, low tissue specificity), consistent with essential housekeeping function in mitochondrial protein synthesis. STRING interactions reveal a functionally coherent mitochondrial translation network: MTRF1L associates with HEMK1 (0.978, glutamine methyltransferase modifying the GGQ motif), GFM2 (0.852, mitochondrial translation elongation factor G2), multiple mitoribosomal proteins (MRPL20/49/58, MRPS16/7, scores 0.864-0.463), MTIF2 (0.699, mitochondrial translation initiation factor 2), MRRF (0.477, mitochondrial ribosome recycling factor), and N6AMT1 (0.438, methyltransferase). ClinVar lists 23 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly 6q24.2-27 copy number alterations (gains: chr6:144488859-170610382x3, chr6:119840686-156623091x3; losses: chr6:151786801-157471821x1, chr6:152853218-170914297x1) and deletions, suggesting MTRF1L dosage sensitivity and potential mitochondrial disease phenotypes. Literature (2018-2025) identifies MTRF1L as: (1) A key factor in mitochondrial translation termination, recycling, reinitiation, and rescue contexts; (2) An exercise-induced mitochondrial biogenesis marker in skeletal muscle; (3) A target affected by Fusarium mycotoxins (beauvericin, enniatin B) disrupting oxidative phosphorylation; (4) An epigenetic signature in atherosclerosis linked to unstable plaque. The scarcity of literature (13 papers) despite clear mechanistic role and disease associations makes MTRF1L a paradigmatic dark gene awaiting detailed characterization.
