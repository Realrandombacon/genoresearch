## Description
HAUS8 (HAUS Augmin-Like Complex Subunit 8, also known as Dgt6 or HICE1) is a 410 aa intracellular protein functioning as an essential structural component of the octameric augmin complex, which drives branching microtubule nucleation within the mitotic spindle. With only 16 literature references, HAUS8 represents a 'partially characterized' dark gene where its specific contribution to complex stability and non-mitotic functions is still emerging. InterPro analysis identifies a single defining QWRF motif-containing domain (PTHR31807, aa 29-331), a conserved sequence signature unique to augmin subunits that likely mediates intra-complex binding rather than possessing independent enzymatic activity. AlphaFold predicts a medium-confidence structure (pLDDT 68.5), suggesting a mix of structured regions involved in protein-protein interactions and flexible linkers typical of scaffold proteins within large complexes. HPA confirms ubiquitous expression with low tissue specificity, consistent with a fundamental housekeeping role in cell division across all proliferating tissues. STRING interactions reveal an extremely tight functional cluster: HAUS8 binds almost exclusively to other augmin subunits (HAUS1, HAUS2, HAUS6, HAUS7, HAUS5 all with scores of 0.999), forming the core scaffold, while the complex collectively interacts with the γ-TuRC recruiter NEDD1 (0.831), the kinetochore binder NDC80 (0.838), and tubulin gamma-1 (TUBG1, 0.451). This network confirms HAUS8's role in amplifying spindle microtubules by recruiting γ-tubulin ring complexes to existing spindle fibers. ClinVar lists 11 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including missense mutations (e.g., p.Gln41Arg) and large copy number gains/losses on chromosome 19p, implicating HAUS8 dosage sensitivity in developmental disorders, likely through mechanisms of chromosomal instability (aneuploidy). Recent literature (2018-2025) highlights two distinct roles: (1) Mitotic Fidelity: As part of augmin, HAUS8 is critical for generating the dense microtubule array needed for accurate chromosome segregation; loss leads to spindle defects and aneuploidy, a hallmark of cancer and microcephaly. (2) Antiviral Immunity: A 2018 study uniquely identifies HAUS8 as a positive regulator of RLR-VISA (MAVS) signaling, where it stabilizes the mitochondrial antiviral signaling protein to enhance type I interferon production against RNA viruses, suggesting a moonlighting function outside of mitosis.
