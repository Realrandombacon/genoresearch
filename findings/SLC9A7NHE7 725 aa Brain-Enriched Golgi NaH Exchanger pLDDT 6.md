# SLC9A7/NHE7: 725 aa Brain-Enriched Golgi Na+/H+ Exchanger (pLDDT 67.1) Driving X-Linked Intellectual Disability via pH-Dependent Glycosylation Defects

## Description
SLC9A7 (UniProt Q96T83, 725 aa), also known as Sodium/Hydrogen Exchanger 7 (NHE7), is a critical intracellular cation/proton antiporter localized to the trans-Golgi network and recycling endosomes, where it regulates organelle pH to ensure proper protein glycosylation and trafficking. InterPro analysis confirms its identity as a member of the CPA1 family with canonical domains: Na+/H+ exchanger family (IPR004709, aa 173-551), Cation/H+ exchanger transmembrane domain (IPR006153/PF00999, aa 84-534), and specific NHE6/7/9 signatures (IPR002090, aa 44-107), all supporting its function as a Na+:H+ antiporter. AlphaFold predicts a medium-confidence structure (global pLDDT 67.1), consistent with a multi-pass membrane protein containing both rigid transmembrane helices and flexible cytosolic regulatory loops. HPA classifies it as a tissue-enhanced metabolic protein with highest expression in the brain (28.7 nTPM) and immune cells, with subcellular localization to vesicles (Golgi/endosomes). STRING interactions reveal a specialized network of neuronal and organellar regulators: KRBOX4 (0.896, kelch-repeat protein), SLC9B2 (0.888, NHE-like paralog), CHST7 (0.842, carbohydrate sulfotransferase), RACK1 (0.712, signaling scaffold), ZNF674 (0.708, X-linked zinc finger), DIPK2A (0.633), CDKL5 (0.599, kinase mutated in epilepsy/ID), PCDH19 (0.537, protocadherin linked to female epilepsy), and SLC9A6 (0.535, NHE6 paralog causing Christianson syndrome). The strong links to CDKL5, PCDH19, and SLC9A6 place SLC9A7 at the heart of X-linked neurodevelopmental disorders. ClinVar contains a massive burden of 208 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including missense (c.264C>G p.Ile88Met, c.1622G>A p.Gly541Asp), frameshifts (c.664dup p.Ser222fs), and nonsense mutations (c.1569G>A p.Trp523Ter), all causing nonsyndromic X-linked intellectual disability (XLID). Functional hypothesis: SLC9A7 acts as the primary pH regulator of the Golgi apparatus in neurons. By extruding protons in exchange for sodium, it maintains the slightly acidic pH (~6.0-6.5) required for the optimal activity of Golgi-resident glycosyltransferases. Loss-of-function mutations lead to Golgi alkalinization, resulting in aberrant N- and O-glycosylation of synaptic proteins (e.g., receptors, adhesion molecules like PCDH19). This 'glycosylation stress' disrupts synaptic development and plasticity, manifesting clinically as intellectual disability. The interaction with CHST7 (sulfotransferase) suggests coordinated regulation of post-translational modifications. The medium-confidence AlphaFold structure indicates potential disorder in the C-terminal regulatory tail, which may harbor binding sites for kinases (CDKL5) or scaffolds (RACK1) that modulate transport activity in response to cellular signals. Conservation across vertebrates (mouse Q8BLV3) confirms essential neurological function. NOTE: This gene represents a 'dark' but mechanistically defined transporter where organelle pH homeostasis directly links to human cognitive disease.
