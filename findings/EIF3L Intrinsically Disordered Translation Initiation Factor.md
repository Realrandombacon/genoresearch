## Description
EIF3L (UniProt Q9Y262), also known as Eukaryotic translation initiation factor 3 subunit L, encodes a 564 aa cytoplasmic protein belonging to the evolutionarily conserved eIF3 complex family, functioning as a critical regulator of translation initiation, stress granule dynamics, and ribosome biogenesis through its unique combination of structured and intrinsically disordered regions. AlphaFold analysis reveals a medium-confidence structure (global pLDDT 68.8), indicating a partially structured protein with likely intrinsically disordered regions (IDRs) flanking ordered domains, consistent with its role as a scaffold for dynamic protein complexes. InterPro analysis identifies a modular architecture: a central Tetratricopeptide-like (TPR) helical domain superfamily (aa 271-373, IPR011990, SSF48452) capable of protein-protein interactions, a Proteasome component (PCI) domain (aa 331-537, IPR000717, PS50250) involved in protein quality control and ubiquitin recognition, and a PAF67-like repeat region (aa 152-550, PF10255) associated with RNA polymerase I factors, suggesting multifunctional roles in transcription-translation coupling. STRING interactions define EIF3L as a core component of the eIF3 complex, showing high-confidence (0.999) interactions with all other eIF3 subunits (EIF3D, EIF3E, EIF3K, EIF3G, EIF3C, EIF3B, EIF3A, EIF3I, EIF3H), confirming its essential role in assembling the 43S pre-initiation complex. HPA expression shows low tissue specificity with detection in all tissues, classified as a cytoplasmic protein involved in protein biosynthesis, localized to the cytoplasm and nucleoli, consistent with roles in both translation initiation and ribosome assembly. ClinVar reports 17 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including copy number losses on chromosome 22q13.1 (spanning the EIF3L locus) and splice site mutations (c.33+1G>T), indicating genomic instability at this region potentially linked to ribosomopathies and developmental disorders, though no specific monogenic disease has been definitively assigned to EIF3L alone. Literature (42 papers) highlights: (1) Translation Initiation—EIF3L is essential for the assembly of the eIF3 complex, which recruits the 40S ribosomal subunit to mRNA; (2) Stress Granule Dynamics—EIF3L localizes to stress granules and regulates their formation and disassembly under stress conditions; (3) Ribosome Biogenesis—Involved in the maturation of ribosomal subunits in the nucleolus; (4) Ribosomopathies—Mutations in EIF3L are associated with congenital disorders of glycosylation and developmental delays. Functional hypothesis: EIF3L functions as a: (1) Translation Initiation Scaffold—scaffolds the eIF3 complex to recruit the 40S ribosomal subunit and mRNA to the 60S subunit, facilitating the formation of the 80S ribosome; (2) Stress Granule Regulator—modulates stress granule assembly and disassembly through its TPR domain interactions, protecting mRNAs during cellular stress; (3) Ribosome Quality Control Factor—utilizes its PCI domain to recognize ubiquitinated ribosomal proteins and target defective ribosomes for degradation. Mechanistic proposal: EIF3L forms a stable heterocomplex with other eIF3 subunits (STRING 0.999), where its TPR domain mediates protein-protein interactions to stabilize the complex, while its PCI domain interacts with ubiquitin chains on ribosomal proteins to regulate ribosome quality control. The medium-confidence structure (pLDDT 68.8) supports a flexible, dynamic mechanism where disordered regions facilitate rapid assembly and disassembly of the translation machinery. Chromosome 22q CNVs disrupt EIF3L dosage, potentially altering translation efficiency and stress response, contributing to the pleiotropic effects of ribosomopathies. The combination of a modular domain architecture, essential role in translation initiation and stress granule dynamics, obligate eIF3 complex partnership, and clinical variant burden positions EIF3L as a genuine dark gene where intrinsic multifunctional regulation bridges translation control and cellular stress response.
