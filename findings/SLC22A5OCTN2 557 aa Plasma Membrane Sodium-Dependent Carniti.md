## Description
SLC22A5 (O76082, 557 aa), also known as OCTN2 (Organic Cation/Carnitine Transporter 2), is a high-affinity sodium-dependent carnitine transporter belonging to the SLC22 organic cation transporter family within the Major Facilitator Superfamily (MFS). InterPro identifies a canonical MFS architecture: SLC22 members 4/5 domain (cd17376, IPR045915, aa 106-510), organic cation transport protein family (IPR004749, aa 12-520), MFS general substrate transporter domains (G3DSA:1.20.1250.20, IPR020846, IPR036259, aa 88-524), and sugar transporter-like signatures (IPR005828, IPR005829, aa 144-517), forming a 12-transmembrane helix bundle with cytoplasmic N- and C-terminal tails. AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (pLDDT 86.5, residues 1-557), indicating a stable, well-folded transporter with ordered substrate-binding cavity. HPA shows tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in skeletal muscle (33.7 nTPM); localized to mitochondria and plasma membrane; classified as a disease-related gene, metabolic protein, and potential drug target involved in sodium symport and ion transport. STRING reveals 10 interactions forming a carnitine/organic cation transport network: SLC25A20 (0.672, carnitine-acylcarnitine translocase), SLC15A2 (0.669, peptide transporter PEPT2), P4HA2 (0.667, prolyl 4-hydroxylase), PDZD3 (0.646, PDZ domain protein), SLC47A1 (0.584, multidrug/toxin extrusion protein MATE1), SLC47A2 (0.560, MATE2), SLCO4C1 (0.540, organic anion transporter), NOD2 (0.496, Crohn's disease susceptibility protein), PDZK1 (0.493, scaffold for apical transporters), and SLC15A1 (0.438, PEPT1). The strong link to SLC25A20 confirms coordinated carnitine shuttling between cytoplasm and mitochondria. ClinVar contains 440 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including missense (c.847T>A/p.Trp283Arg), nonsense (c.619C>T/p.Gln207Ter, c.1437C>G/p.Tyr479Ter), frameshifts (c.464del/p.Leu155fs, c.426_436delinsG/p.Pro143fs, c.328dup/p.Gln110fs, c.861_864dup/p.Arg289fs), splice site (c.1267+1G>T), and large deletions, causing primary carnitine deficiency (CDSP, carnitine deficiency systemic primary). Functional hypothesis: SLC22A5/OCTN2 functions as the primary high-affinity carnitine uptake transporter (Km ~5 μM) in a 2:1 Na+:carnitine stoichiometry, responsible for maintaining systemic carnitine homeostasis by mediating intestinal absorption, renal reabsorption, and tissue uptake (especially heart and skeletal muscle). The sodium gradient drives concentrative carnitine accumulation against steep concentration gradients, enabling fatty acid β-oxidation in mitochondria. Interaction with PDZK1 suggests apical membrane targeting in kidney proximal tubule and intestinal epithelium, while linkage to SLC25A20 indicates coupling with mitochondrial carnitine-acylcarnitine exchange for complete fatty acid oxidation cycle. Disruption via loss-of-function mutations causes primary carnitine deficiency characterized by hypoketotic hypoglycemia, cardiomyopathy, skeletal myopathy, and sudden infant death due to inability to oxidize long-chain fatty acids during fasting. Conservation across vertebrates (mouse 557 aa, rat 557 aa) confirms essential metabolic role. NOTE: Despite clinical recognition, regulatory mechanisms and tissue-specific isoform functions remain areas of active investigation.
