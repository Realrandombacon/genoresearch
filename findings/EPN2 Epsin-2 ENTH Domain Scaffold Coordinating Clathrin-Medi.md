# EPN2: Epsin-2 ENTH Domain Scaffold Coordinating Clathrin-Mediated Endocytosis Through Ubiquitinated Cargo Recognition and Membrane Curvature Generation

## Description
EPN2 (Epsin-2, UniProt O95208) is a 641-amino acid clathrin-associated endocytic adaptor protein that functions as a molecular bridge between ubiquitinated membrane cargo and the clathrin coat machinery during receptor internalization. EPN2 belongs to the epsin family characterized by a modular domain architecture: an N-terminal ENTH (Epsin N-Terminal Homology) domain (IPR013809, PF01417, SM00273, aa 12-144) that binds phosphatidylinositol-4,5-bisphosphate (PI(4,5)P2) and induces membrane curvature through amphipathic helix insertion; two ubiquitin-interacting motifs (UIMs, IPR003903, PS50330, SM00726) at positions 275-294 and 300-319 that recognize K63-linked polyubiquitin chains on activated receptors (EGFR, Notch); and an unstructured C-terminal region (aa 145-641) containing clathrin-binding motifs and AP-2 interaction sites. The AlphaFold predicted structure shows medium confidence (pLDDT 59.1), consistent with a partially intrinsically disordered protein where the structured ENTH domain anchors to membranes while flexible linker regions enable conformational sampling for multi-protein complex assembly. STRING interaction network places EPN2 at the core of the clathrin-mediated endocytosis machinery with ultra-high confidence associations to: EPS15 (0.999), another endocytic adaptor recognizing ubiquitinated cargo; ITSN1 (0.992) and ITSN2 (0.979), intersectin scaffolds coordinating actin polymerization with vesicle formation; SNAP91 (0.990), clathrin-associated sorting protein; CLTC (0.988) and CLTCL1 (0.984), clathrin heavy chains forming the vesicle coat; EPS15L1 (0.973), epsin-related protein; PICALM (0.947), phosphatidylinositol-binding clathrin assembly protein mutated in Alzheimer's disease; EPN3 (0.944), epsin-3 paralog; and HIP1R (0.550), huntingtin-interacting protein regulating actin dynamics. HPA expression profiles show ubiquitous detection across tissues with low tissue specificity, vesicular subcellular localization consistent with endocytic function, classification under endocytosis biological processes, and blood cell group-enriched expression suggesting roles in immune receptor trafficking. ClinVar records document 103 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants predominantly consisting of copy number losses (chr17p11.2 deletions spanning 16.8-20.5 Mb) and gains, though specific EPN2-only point mutations remain uncharacterized; the 17p11.2 region is associated with Smith-Magenis syndrome (deletion) and Potocki-Lupski syndrome (duplication), but whether EPN2 haploinsufficiency contributes to these neurodevelopmental phenotypes remains unknown. Literature analysis reveals only 24 papers, indicating EPN2 is understudied relative to its paralog EPN1; recent work identifies EPN2 in cancer metastasis (NSCLC brain metastasis via miR-19b-3p regulation by tumor EV-derived XIST lncRNA), esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (CircSPARC-HNRNPC-EPN2 splicing axis activating Wnt/β-catenin), and Parkinson's disease (altered peripheral blood expression). We hypothesize that EPN2 functions as a selective gatekeeper for ubiquitinated cargo internalization through a multi-step mechanism: (1) PI(4,5)P2 binding via the ENTH domain recruits EPN2 to plasma membrane microdomains enriched in activated receptors; (2) amphipathic helix H0 insertion into the lipid bilayer generates positive membrane curvature priming vesicle budding; (3) dual UIMs engage polyubiquitin chains on cargo receptors with avidity-enhanced binding ensuring selective internalization of signaling-competent receptors; (4) disordered C-terminal region samples conformational space to simultaneously engage clathrin triskelia (via CLTC), AP-2 adaptor complex, and accessory proteins (EPS15, ITSN1); (5) coordination with actin polymerization machinery (ITSN1/2, HIP1R) provides mechanical force for vesicle scission; (6) tissue-specific splice variants may modulate UIM affinity or clathrin-binding capacity explaining differential cargo selectivity across cell types. The medium-confidence AlphaFold structure suggests functional disorder enabling dynamic interactions, while the high ClinVar variant count in the 17p11.2 region implies EPN2 dosage sensitivity potentially contributing to neurodevelopmental disorders when deleted. Cancer associations indicate EPN2 dysregulation may alter growth factor receptor turnover (EGFR, Notch) promoting oncogenic signaling persistence. Compared to extensively studied EPN1, EPN2 represents an underexplored paralog with potentially distinct cargo preferences and regulatory mechanisms worthy of targeted investigation.
