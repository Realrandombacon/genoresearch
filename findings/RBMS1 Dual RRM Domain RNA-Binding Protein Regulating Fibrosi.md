# RBMS1: Dual RRM Domain RNA-Binding Protein Regulating Fibrosis via Sumo2 mRNA Stabilization and Cancer Metastasis through m6A-YTHDF1-S100P Axis and circNFIB Biogenesis

**Confidence:** MEDIUM

## Description
RBMS1 (RNA Binding Motif Single Stranded Interacting Protein 1, also known as MSSP-1/2/3, HCC-4, YC1, UniProt P29558) is a 406-amino acid cytosolic RNA-binding protein featuring two tandem RNA recognition motif (RRM) domains that function as a critical post-transcriptional regulator in fibrosis, cancer metastasis, and circular RNA biogenesis. The protein architecture contains two conserved RRM domains (IPR000504, PF00076, cd12470 RRM1 aa 55-140, cd12473 RRM2 aa 141-225) belonging to the nucleotide-binding alpha-beta plait domain superfamily (IPR012677, IPR035979), with the first RRM specifically classified as MSSP-1 RRM1 (IPR034404). RBMS1 also shares homology with paraneoplastic encephalomyelitis antigen family (IPR002343), suggesting potential autoimmune relevance. The AlphaFold predicted structure shows medium confidence (pLDDT 64.1), indicating a partially structured protein with ordered RRM domains embedded within flexible linker regions enabling conformational adaptation for diverse RNA target recognition. STRING interaction network places RBMS1 within RNA processing and oncogenic signaling with high-confidence associations to: CR2 (0.737), complement receptor 2 linking to immune regulation; MYC (0.736), MYC proto-oncogene suggesting transcriptional coordination; PTBP3 (0.661), polypyrimidine tract binding protein 3 another RNA-binding regulator; FCER2 (0.639), low affinity IgE receptor; C3 (0.598), complement component 3; MRRF (0.591), mitochondrial ribosome recycling factor; ITGB6 (0.561), integrin beta-6 involved in TGFβ activation; MRPL53 (0.556), mitochondrial ribosomal protein; CD46 (0.550), complement regulatory protein; and RBMS2 (0.506), paralogous RNA-binding protein. HPA expression profiles demonstrate low tissue specificity with ubiquitous detection across all tissues, cytosolic localization consistent with RNA-binding function, classification as predicted intracellular protein involved in DNA replication with DNA-binding and RNA-binding molecular functions, and low cancer specificity at protein level despite strong functional roles in tumors. ClinVar records document 17 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants consisting primarily of large copy number losses on chromosome 2q24.1-24.3 (spanning 156-169Mb regions) and gains on 2p25.3-q37.3, suggesting haploinsufficiency may contribute to developmental disorders though no specific monogenic syndrome has been definitively assigned. Literature analysis (98 papers) reveals multi-context functionality: RBMS1 deficiency improves myocardial fibrosis and heart failure by unknown mechanisms potentially involving reduced ECM gene expression; promotes pulmonary fibrosis by directly binding and stabilizing Sumo2 mRNA facilitating Smad4 SUMOylation and fibroblast activation in TGFβ signaling; coordinates with m6A reader YTHDF1 to stimulate S100P translation driving non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) metastasis; activates HSPA8 axis promoting head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC) progression; mediates circular RNA circNFIB biogenesis enabling perineural invasion in pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma via L1CAM/MAPK pathway activation. We hypothesize that RBMS1 functions through a context-dependent RNA-binding mechanism: (1) In fibrosis, RBMS1 binds Sumo2 mRNA 3'UTR via tandem RRMs preventing degradation, increasing SUMO2 protein levels which enhance Smad4 SUMOylation amplifying TGFβ profibrotic signaling; (2) In cancer metastasis, RBMS1 cooperates with YTHDF1 recognizing m6A-modified S100P transcripts, stabilizing them for enhanced translation promoting cell migration and invasion; (3) In circRNA biogenesis, RBMS1 binds flanking intronic sequences of NFIB pre-mRNA facilitating back-splicing to generate circNFIB which sponges miRNAs or encodes peptides activating L1CAM/MAPK signaling; (4) The medium-confidence structure suggests flexible inter-RRM linkers enabling recognition of diverse RNA secondary structures; (5) MYC interaction suggests RBMS1 may be transcriptionally induced by MYC creating feed-forward loops in cancer; (6) PTBP3 partnership indicates coordinated splicing regulation; (7) ClinVar CNVs may cause disease through RBMS1 haploinsufficiency disrupting RNA homeostasis in development. Therapeutic targeting of RBMS1-RNA interfaces could treat fibrotic diseases or block metastasis by destabilizing profibrotic/pro-metastatic transcripts.
