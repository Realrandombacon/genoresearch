# TM7SF2: A Delta14-Sterol Reductase Catalyzing the Penultimate Step of Cholesterol Biosynthesis within the ER Membrane Metabolon

**Date:** 2026-06-26T17:01:34.757916

**Quality Score:** 10/10 (EXCELLENT)

**Confidence:** HIGH

## Description
TM7SF2 (Transmembrane 7 Superfamily Member 2, UniProt O76062), also known as DHCR14 or C14SR, is a 418-amino acid integral membrane enzyme that catalyzes the reduction of the C14-C15 double bond in sterol intermediates (converting obtusifoliol/lanosterol derivatives to their saturated counterparts), a critical and rate-limiting step in the post-squalene cholesterol biosynthetic pathway. InterPro analysis confirms its identity as a member of the ERG4/ERG24 sterol reductase family (IPR001171, PF01222, PTHR21257), featuring two highly conserved catalytic signatures (PS01017, aa 167-182; PS01018, aa 382-405) containing the essential NAD(P)H-binding motifs and proton donor residues required for hydride transfer. The AlphaFold predicted structure exhibits exceptional confidence (pLDDT 93.8), revealing a compact, multi-pass transmembrane fold optimized for embedding within the endoplasmic reticulum (ER) lipid bilayer while positioning its active site for substrate access. STRING interaction data places TM7SF2 at the heart of the cholesterol synthesis 'metabolon' with very high confidence associations to downstream/upstream pathway enzymes: EBP (emopamil binding protein, 0.991), which acts immediately after TM7SF2; DHCR24 (0.976) and SC5D (0.933), other late-stage reductases; LBR (lamin B receptor, 0.906), a dual-function sterol reductase/nuclear anchor; HSD17B7 (0.854); MSMO1 (0.740); CYP51A1 (0.714); SQLE (squalene epoxidase, 0.697); and ERG28 (0.580), a scaffold protein that organizes these enzymes into a functional complex. HPA expression profiles show ubiquitous detection with enhancement in the adrenal gland (243.0 nTPM)—the primary site of steroid hormone synthesis—and localization to the ER and vesicles, consistent with its metabolic role. ClinVar records document 10 pathogenic variants including splice site mutations (c.973+4A>G, c.973+2T>G) and large copy number gains/losses at 11q13.1, implicating TM7SF2 deficiency in congenital disorders of cholesterol synthesis (potentially resembling desmosterolosis or lathosterolosis phenotypes). We hypothesize that TM7SF2 functions as an essential gatekeeper in the Bloch pathway: by reducing the C14 double bond, it enables subsequent demethylation and isomerization steps required to generate mature cholesterol; its physical association with EBP, ERG28, and other reductases suggests it operates within a multi-enzyme complex that channels hydrophobic intermediates directly between active sites, preventing diffusion and enhancing catalytic efficiency. Loss of TM7SF2 would cause accumulation of toxic C14-unsaturated sterols, disrupting membrane fluidity, hedgehog signaling, and steroidogenesis, leading to severe developmental defects.
