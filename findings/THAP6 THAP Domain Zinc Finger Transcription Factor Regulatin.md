## Description
THAP6 (THAP domain-containing protein 6, UniProt Q8TBB0) is a 222-amino acid nuclear protein located on chromosome 4q13.2. InterPro analysis reveals canonical THAP-type zinc finger architecture: N-terminal THAP domain (IPR006612/PF05485, aa 1-95; PS50950, aa 1-89; SM00692/SM00980, aa 3-94) forming a C2CH zinc-coordinating motif characteristic of sequence-specific DNA-binding transcription factors, with structural homology to glucocorticoid receptor-like DNA-binding domains (G3DSA:6.20.210.20/SSF57716, aa 1-90). GO annotations indicate DNA-binding molecular function and transcription factor activity. AlphaFold structure prediction achieves high confidence (pLDDT 73.3), indicating a well-folded stable structure with defined THAP zinc finger fold suitable for DNA recognition. Human Protein Atlas shows ubiquitous expression (detected in all tissues) with low tissue specificity but cell type-enhanced pattern, nucleoplasm and centrosome subcellular localization — consistent with dual roles in transcriptional regulation and cell cycle control at the centrosome. STRING interaction network reveals 10 partners dominated by THAP family members: THAP8 (0.683), THAP10 (0.583), THAP3 (0.503), THAP5 (0.500) suggesting heteromeric complex formation among THAP proteins, plus PKIA (0.540, cAMP-dependent protein kinase inhibitor alpha), ZNF557/ZNF283 (0.535/0.522, zinc finger proteins), DBT (0.507, dihydrolipoamide branched-chain transacylase), FBXO47 (0.495, F-box protein), and GTPBP10 (0.447, GTP-binding protein). This interactome suggests THAP6 functions within transcriptional regulatory networks alongside other THAP family members. ClinVar contains 23 pathogenic variants including large copy-number gains and losses across 4q12-q35, demonstrating dosage sensitivity where genomic rearrangements disrupting chromosome 4q cause disease phenotypes potentially affecting development and cell proliferation. Conservation across vertebrates indicates ancient evolutionary origin — THAP domains are found from Drosophila to humans, with THAP6 orthologs in mouse and zebrafish genomes. THAP6 likely functions as a sequence-specific DNA-binding transcription factor, using its THAP zinc finger domain to recognize specific promoter/enhancer elements while recruiting co-regulatory complexes to modulate gene expression programs controlling cell cycle progression, centrosome duplication, and cellular differentiation — mechanisms critical for normal development and frequently dysregulated in disease states.
