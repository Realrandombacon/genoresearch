## Description
ZRANB3 (Zinc Finger RANBP2-Type Containing 3, UniProt Q5FWF4), also known as AH2, is a 1079-amino acid multifunctional enzyme that functions as a DNA-dependent ATPase and structure-specific endonuclease critical for replication fork remodeling and DNA damage repair. InterPro analysis reveals a modular SNF2 family architecture with an N-terminal SNF2 domain (IPR000330, cd18010, G3DSA:3.40.50.10810, aa 41-303) containing the ATP-binding motor; a C-terminal helicase domain (IPR001650, cd18793, G3DSA:3.40.50.300, aa 325-481) for DNA translocation; a RanBP2-type zinc finger (IPR001876, G3DSA:2.30.30.380, aa 621-650) for protein-protein interactions; and a C-terminal HNH endonuclease domain (IPR002711, IPR003615, cd00085, G3DSA:1.10.30.50, aa 1011-1050) that cleaves DNA substrates. The AlphaFold predicted structure shows confident folding (pLDDT 72.4), consistent with a stable multi-domain enzyme optimized for chromatin engagement. STRING interaction data places ZRANB3 within the DNA repair and replication stress response network with high confidence associations to: PCNA (0.954), the sliding clamp that recruits ZRANB3 to stalled forks; FANCM (0.835), another fork remodeler in Fanconi anemia pathway; WRN (0.790), RecQ helicase mutated in Werner syndrome; RAD51 (0.780), recombinase for homologous recombination; FBH1 (0.774), F-box helicase regulating RAD51; DNA2 (0.738), nuclease-helicase for Okazaki fragment processing; MUS81 (0.733), structure-specific endonuclease for fork cleavage; PRIMPOL (0.726), primase-polymerase for repriming; RADX (0.725), RAD51 regulator; and HLTF (0.717), paralogous fork remodeler. HPA expression profiles show detection in some tissues with nucleoplasmic localization, cell type enhanced expression, and classification as cancer-related with roles in DNA damage and repair. ClinVar records document 15 pathogenic variants including splice site mutations (c.-7-19G>T, c.162-11T>A), small deletions (c.162-8_162-5del), and large copy number gains/losses spanning 2q21.2-q23.2 (ranging from 5-15 Mb), implicating ZRANB3 haploinsufficiency or overexpression in developmental disorders and cancer predisposition. We hypothesize that ZRANB3 functions as a replication fork reversal engine: upon replication stress (UV, chemotherapeutics), PCNA ubiquitination recruits ZRANB3 to stalled forks via the RanBP2 zinc finger; the SNF2 ATPase domain translocates along DNA, using ATP hydrolysis to drive fork regression and form a four-way 'chicken foot' structure; this reversed fork provides a template for error-free homologous recombination repair via RAD51-mediated strand invasion; the HNH endonuclease can cleave the regressed arm if restart fails, enabling fork collapse and alternative repair pathways; coordination with FANCM/HLTF ensures appropriate pathway choice; loss of ZRANB3 causes fork instability, increased mutagenesis, and cancer susceptibility, while overexpression may contribute to chemotherapy resistance by enhancing repair capacity.
