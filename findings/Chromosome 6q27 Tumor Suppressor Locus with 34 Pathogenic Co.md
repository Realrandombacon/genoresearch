## Description
LOC154449 is an uncharacterized genetic locus on chromosome 6q27 (NC_000006.12, positions 170262568-170254333, ~8.2 kb region) with substantial clinical significance despite lacking protein-coding features. No UniProt accession exists, preventing InterPro domain analysis and AlphaFold structure prediction. No mRNA transcript was identified in NCBI nucleotide databases, and Human Protein Atlas found no expression data (ENSG00000230960). STRING-DB detected no protein-protein interactions. However, ClinVar contains 34 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all representing large-scale copy number variations spanning the 6q25.2-27 region (variants include 4796079, 4755357, 4755344, 2579276, 155552, 155515, 154527, 153525, 152206, 151758). These CNVs include both deletions (copy number loss, hemizygous) and duplications (copy number gain, trisomy) ranging from focal alterations (~3 Mb) to whole-arm changes (17 Mb). The 6q27 chromosomal location is clinically notable as this terminal region is associated with 6q27 deletion syndrome, characterized by developmental delay, intellectual disability, autism spectrum disorder, hypotonia, and dysmorphic features. The region contains multiple dosage-sensitive tumor suppressor and developmental genes including PARK2 (parkin, Parkinson disease, E3 ubiquitin ligase), PRDM1 (B-lymphocyte-induced maturation protein 1, B-cell lymphoma), AHI1 (Abelson helper integration site 1, Joubert syndrome), and MYB (MYB proto-oncogene, hematopoiesis). Functional hypothesis: LOC154449 likely represents a cis-regulatory element or chromatin boundary controlling expression of neighboring tumor suppressor genes in the 6q27 critical region. The 34 pathogenic CNVs indicate extreme dosage sensitivity where copy number alterations disrupt normal transcriptional regulation of neurodevelopmental and tumor suppressor genes. Disease mechanism likely involves haploinsufficiency of PARK2, PRDM1, or AHI1 through enhancer disruption, silencer deletion, or chromatin topology alteration leading to neurodevelopmental phenotypes and cancer predisposition. The absence of protein-coding features combined with strong ClinVar evidence and neurodevelopmental/oncological disease association suggests this locus functions as a tumor suppressor regulatory hub controlling cell cycle arrest, apoptosis, and neuronal development through long-range gene regulation.
