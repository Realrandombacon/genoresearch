## Description
ANAPC16 (Anaphase-promoting complex subunit 16, also known as APC16, C10orf104, CENP-27, or MSAG) is a compact 110 amino acid intracellular protein localized to the cytosol and kinetochore (UniProt Q96DE5) with high-confidence well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 71.3). The protein defines the ANAPC16 family (IPR029641/PTHR31564, residues 1-110) and contains a conserved APC16 domain (PF17256, residues 27-106, ~80 aa, ~73% of protein). This small but structured protein is an essential stoichiometric component of the anaphase-promoting complex/cyclosome (APC/C), a multi-subunit E3 ubiquitin ligase governing cell cycle progression. Molecular functions include protein polyubiquitination (GO:0016567), anaphase-promoting complex activity (GO:0005680), cell cycle regulation, cell division, and mitosis, placing ANAPC16 at the heart of chromosome segregation and cell proliferation.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals an exceptionally tight network within the APC/C holoenzyme: near-perfect interactions with ANAPC5 (0.999, Anaphase-promoting complex subunit 5, the catalytic RING-H2 subunit), CDC23 (0.999, Cell division cycle 23, a tetratricopeptide repeat scaffold), ANAPC15 (0.999, another small essential subunit), CDC16 (0.998, Cell division cycle 16, TPR protein), CDC26 (0.998, Anaphase-promoting complex subunit 9), ANAPC13 (0.998, smallest APC subunit), CDC27 (0.996, Anaphase-promoting complex subunit 3), ANAPC1 (0.994, largest scaffolding subunit), ANAPC7 (0.994, subunit 7), and CDC20 (0.986, Cell division cycle 20, the mitotic activator). This interaction profile confirms ANAPC16 is an integral, non-peripheral component of the APC/C complex, likely positioned at the core interface between catalytic and regulatory modules.\n\nHPA expression reveals low tissue specificity with detection in all tissues, consistent with an essential housekeeping role in cell division. The ubiquitous expression pattern reflects the universal requirement for APC/C activity in proliferating cells. Cytosolic localization aligns with APC/C assembly in the cytoplasm before nuclear entry during mitosis. Low cancer specificity suggests ANAPC16 is not oncogenically dysregulated but remains essential for tumor proliferation.\n\nClinVar documents 14 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including a missense mutation (p.Arg32Gln) within the conserved APC16 domain and large copy number gains/losses affecting chromosome 10q: CNV gains (10q11.21-24.2, 10p14-q26.3 duplications) and CNV losses (10p13-q26.3, 10q21.3-22.3 deletions). The variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity. Haploinsufficiency would impair APC/C assembly or activity, leading to defective ubiquitination of securin and cyclin B, failure of anaphase onset, mitotic arrest, and genomic instability. This could manifest as developmental disorders, microcephaly, or cancer predisposition. The p.Arg32Gln mutation likely disrupts protein-protein interfaces critical for APC/C integration.\n\nConservation is strong across vertebrates: human (Q96DE5, 110 aa), mouse (Q9CPV2, 110 aa), cow (Q58DR0, 110 aa), Xenopus (Q6GQ63, 112 aa), orangutan (Q5REH8, 110 aa), indicating evolutionary pressure to maintain this small but essential subunit.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: ANAPC16 functions as a structural linchpin within the APC/C holoenzyme, stabilizing interactions between the catalytic module (ANAPC5/RING-H2) and the scaffolding TPR subunits (CDC23, CDC16, CDC27). Despite its small size, ANAPC16's well-folded structure (pLDDT 71.3) suggests it forms a compact globular domain that docks into a specific pocket within the complex. The conserved Arg32 residue may form salt bridges or hydrogen bonds critical for complex integrity. ANAPC16 likely co-assembles with ANAPC15 and ANAPC13 as a triplet of small subunits that fine-tune APC/C conformation and substrate recognition. During mitosis, ANAPC16 ensures proper CDC20 binding and activation, enabling timely ubiquitination of securin (PDS1) and cyclin B1 to trigger anaphase. Loss of ANAPC16 destabilizes the APC/C, causing mitotic checkpoint override, chromosome missegregation, and aneuploidy. The ubiquitous expression reflects its non-redundant role in all dividing cells. 10q CNVs disrupt ANAPC16 dosage, potentially contributing to 10q deletion syndrome phenotypes through cell cycle dysregulation.
