## Description
WDR75 (UniProt Q8IWA0, 830 aa) is a critical nucleolar scaffold protein essential for ribosome biogenesis, specifically the assembly of the Small Subunit (SSU) processome. InterPro analysis reveals a unique architecture comprising two large beta-propeller domains: a first propeller (PF23869, aa 23-312) and a second propeller (PF23769, aa 340-663), both built from WD40 repeats (IPR001680) within the YVTN repeat-like superfamily (IPR015943). These structures create extensive protein-protein interaction surfaces. AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (global pLDDT 80.4), confirming the stability of these dual propellers which likely act as a central hub. HPA classifies WDR75 as an essential protein with ubiquitous expression across all tissues and cell types, consistent with its fundamental role in translation machinery production. STRING interactions define WDR75 as the core of the SSU processome, showing extremely high confidence links (scores >0.997) to key ribosomal biogenesis factors: NOP58 (0.999, box C/D snoRNP component), NOL6 (0.999, rRNA binding), WDR3 (0.999, pre-rRNA processing), UTP4 (0.999), RRP9 (0.998), UTP20 (0.998), UTP6 (0.998), UTP15 (0.998), UTP18 (0.997), and MPHOSPH10 (0.997). This network places WDR75 physically at the intersection of early rRNA cleavage and snoRNP recruitment. ClinVar lists 32 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number losses (deletions) and gains encompassing the 2q32-33 locus, indicating that haploinsufficiency or dosage imbalance of WDR75 causes severe developmental defects (though specific syndromic names are often grouped with larger chromosomal anomalies). Functional hypothesis: WDR75 acts as the structural 'keystone' of the SSU processome. Its dual beta-propeller topology allows it to simultaneously bind multiple distinct modules of the 90S pre-ribosome: one propeller likely anchors the t-UTP subcomplex (via UTP4/UTP15/UTP20), while the other recruits snoRNPs (via NOP58/NOL6) required for 18S rRNA modification. Loss of WDR75 destabilizes the entire 90S particle, halting 18S rRNA production and triggering p53-mediated nucleolar stress responses, explaining the severe developmental phenotypes associated with its deletion. The high conservation (mouse Q3U821, zebrafish Q7ZVR1) and essentiality underscore its non-redundant role in the earliest steps of ribosome assembly.
