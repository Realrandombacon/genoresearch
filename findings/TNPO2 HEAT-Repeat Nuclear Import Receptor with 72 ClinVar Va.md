## Description
TNPO2 (Transportin-2; UniProt O14787) is an 897 amino acid karyopherin-β family nuclear import receptor with only 16 publications, classifying it as a genuine dark gene despite critical roles in nucleocytoplasmic transport, neurodevelopment, and cancer progression.\n\nSTRUCTURAL ARCHITECTURE: InterPro analysis reveals a canonical importin-β architecture composed of tandem HEAT repeats (IPR000357/PF02985/PF13513) forming an elongated superhelical structure. The N-terminal region contains an importin-β N-terminal domain (IPR001494/PF03810, residues 31-99) essential for RanGTP binding and cargo release regulation. The protein belongs to the importin beta family (IPR040122/PTHR10527, residues 9-872) with armadillo-like helical folds (IPR011989/IPR016024, residues 1-887) throughout. Additional TPR-like repeats (IPR058584/PF25574, residues 663-763) contribute to the flexible scaffold. AlphaFold predicts exceptional structural confidence (global pLDDT 91.2), indicating a highly stable, well-folded import receptor optimized for conformational changes during cargo binding and release.\n\nINTERACTION NETWORK: STRING-DB identifies high-confidence interactions with nuclear transport machinery: NXF1 (0.924, mRNA export factor), NUP153 (0.852, nuclear pore protein), NUP62 (0.811, nucleoporin), RAN (0.807, small GTPase regulating transport directionality), IPO9 (0.712, importin-β paralog), ZC3H14 (0.702, zinc finger RNA-binding protein), plus medium-confidence partners NUP98 (0.452), IPO7 (0.445), NUTF2 (0.433, nuclear transport factor 2), and HNRNPA1 (0.404, heterogeneous nuclear ribonucleoprotein). This network positions TNPO2 at the intersection of protein import, mRNA export, and nuclear pore complex function.\n\nEXPRESSION & LOCALIZATION: HPA shows ubiquitous expression detected in all tissues with low tissue specificity, consistent with fundamental nuclear transport requirements in all cells. Subcellular localization is restricted to nucleoplasm and nucleoli, reflecting its role in importing cargo to nuclear compartments. Classified as a transporter involved in protein transport, with disease associations including intellectual disability.\n\nDISEASE LANDSCAPE: ClinVar contains 72 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including missense mutations affecting critical residues (p.Gln28Lys, p.Gly872Arg, p.Ser64Phe, p.Phe81Val, p.Asp226Glu, p.Gly689Arg, p.Pro227Ser). Recent 2021-2025 literature establishes TNPO2 variants as causative for human developmental delays, neurologic deficits, and dysmorphic features. A 2025 case report describes an 87-year-old female with intellectual disability, severe speech impairment, and behavioral issues carrying a rare missense variant. Additionally, TNPO2 operates downstream of DYNC1I1 to promote gastric cancer cell proliferation and inhibit apoptosis, and mediates nucleocytoplasmic shuttling of estrogen receptor-α.\n\nCONSERVATION: Orthologs confirmed in mouse (Q99LG2, 887 aa), indicating strong evolutionary constraint on the HEAT-repeat architecture.\n\nFUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: TNPO2 functions as a RanGTP-regulated nuclear import receptor that recognizes specific nuclear localization signals (NLS) on cargo proteins, particularly those containing M9-type or RGG-box motifs found in RNA-binding proteins like HNRNPA1 and ZC3H14. The HEAT-repeat superhelix undergoes conformational changes upon RanGTP binding in the nucleus, triggering cargo release. In neurodevelopment, TNPO2 likely imports transcription factors and RNA-processing proteins essential for neuronal differentiation and synaptic function—explaining why variants cause intellectual disability and speech impairment. In cancer, TNPO2 may facilitate nuclear import of oncogenic factors (e.g., estrogen receptor-α, proliferation regulators) downstream of dynein signaling. The 72 ClinVar variants warrant investigation for genotype-phenotype correlations in neurodevelopmental disorders.
