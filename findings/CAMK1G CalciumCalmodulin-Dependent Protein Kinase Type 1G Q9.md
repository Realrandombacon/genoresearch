## Description
CAMK1G (Calcium/calmodulin-dependent protein kinase type 1G; UniProt Q96NX5, 476 aa) is a partially characterized calcium sensor-effector with only ~29 publications, representing a GENUINE DARK GENE despite critical roles in neuronal plasticity and cancer metastasis. While recent 2025 studies linked CAMK1G expression to DNA methylation subtypes dictating metastatic heterogeneity in osteosarcoma [2025, 2 cites], and 2023 research demonstrated its upregulation in mouse hippocampus under chronic social defeat stress [2023, 2 cites], critical mechanistic gaps persist: (1) The specific phosphorylation substrates beyond canonical CREB1/ATF1 remain uncharacterized despite high-confidence STRING interactions with CALM3(0.943), NOS3(0.907), and CREB1(0.754); (2) The functional distinction between CAMK1G and other CaMKI isoforms (alpha, beta, delta) in primary cilium vs. nucleoplasmic signaling compartments requires definition; (3) HPA data reveals tissue-enhanced expression in brain (nTPM: 48.1) and ovary (nTPM: 25.7) with specific localization to primary cilium and basal body [HPA], suggesting a unique role in ciliary calcium transduction not yet mapped; (4) The presence of 19 ClinVar pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants on chr1q21.1-q32.3 (mostly copy number gains/losses) with undefined clinical phenotypes suggests potential neurodevelopmental or ciliopathy disorders not yet cataloged [ClinVar]; (5) AlphaFold structure shows medium confidence pLDDT: 66.1, indicating a structured catalytic domain (residues 19-303, InterPro cd14166) flanked by disordered regulatory regions typical of CaMK family members, which may facilitate dynamic calmodulin binding and substrate recognition; (6) The autoinhibitory domain mechanism and its relief by Ca2+/Calmodulin binding in the context of ciliary signaling remains unresolved. This gene represents a critical but under-explored node in calcium-dependent transcriptional regulation and ciliary function, fitting the 'dark' criteria for deep functional characterization.
