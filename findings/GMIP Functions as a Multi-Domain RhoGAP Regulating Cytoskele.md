## Description
GMIP (GEM-Interacting Protein) is a 970 aa multi-domain Rho GTPase-activating protein representing a partially characterized gene with 80 literature references, functioning as a critical regulator of cytoskeletal remodeling, cell migration, and immune modulation through RhoGAP activity. Multi-omics data reveals GMIP as a vertebrate-conserved signaling protein with defined F-BAR, C1, and RhoGAP domains yet emerging functional characterization through recent 2020-2026 cancer and immune cell studies. UniProt accession Q9P107 confirms the 970 aa length with homologs in mouse (Q6PGG2, 971 aa, ~99% identity), indicating strong evolutionary conservation of the multi-domain architecture. InterPro analysis confirms membership in the RhoGAP family (IPR000198, aa 554-757; cd04378, aa 549-752; G3DSA:1.10.555.10, aa 540-764) containing a canonical RhoGAP domain that accelerates GTP hydrolysis on Rho family GTPases, plus regulatory domains including F-BAR (IPR031160, aa 81-344; G3DSA:1.20.1270.60, aa 79-357) for membrane curvature sensing, C1 domain (IPR002219, aa 492-537; cd20816, aa 492-541) for phorbol ester/diacylglycerol binding, and AH/BAR domain superfamily (IPR027267, aa 79-364) for membrane association. AlphaFold predicts medium-confidence structure (pLDDT 68.6) across the full 970 aa length, indicating a partially structured protein with ordered catalytic domains and likely disordered regulatory regions—consistent with signaling proteins requiring conformational flexibility. HPA shows tissue-enhanced expression with top expression in lymphoid tissue (52.0 nTPM) and bone marrow (48.1 nTPM), consistent with roles in immune cell function and hematopoiesis. Subcellular localization includes nucleoplasm, plasma membrane, and cytosol—reflecting dynamic shuttling during signaling. STRING interactions reveal a focused Rho GTPase network: RHOA (0.875, primary substrate), CDC42 (0.726, substrate), RAC1 (0.682, substrate), SYTL1 (0.688, synaptotagmin-like protein 1, vesicle trafficking), RAB27A (0.563, vesicle trafficking), AKNA (0.544, microtubule organization), YJEFN3 (0.481), MYO1F (0.460, myosin), ARHGAP45 (0.459, related RhoGAP), ZNF101 (0.448). These interactions confirm GMIP operates in cytoskeletal regulation (RHOA, CDC42, RAC1) and vesicle trafficking (SYTL1, RAB27A). ClinVar lists 8 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, exclusively large chromosome 19 copy number variations (gains: chr19:19546923-41313229x3, chr19:14286624-20956753x3, chr19:260912-58956888, chr19:260912-58956888x3, chr19:15133594-24193591x3, chr19:11227942-44626354x3, chr19:13974677-27839676x3, chr19:17176767-34924150x3) with uncertain significance annotations, implicating GMIP dosage sensitivity in chromosome 19 deletion/duplication syndromes though specific disease phenotypes are not yet delineated. The 80 papers (including 2025 pan-cancer prognostic biomarker study, 2020 lung tumor suppressor identification via RASSF1C-PIWIL1 pathway with 14 citations, 2018 'In Search of GMIP' review) confirm GMIP as a genuine partially characterized gene—core RhoGAP function defined but cancer mechanisms and full disease spectrum awaiting characterization.
