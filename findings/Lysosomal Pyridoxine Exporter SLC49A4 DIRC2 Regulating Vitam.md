# Lysosomal Pyridoxine Exporter SLC49A4 (DIRC2) Regulating Vitamin B6 Homeostasis and HIF-Dependent Hypoxia Adaptation

## Description
SLC49A4 (Disrupted in Renal Carcinoma 2, DIRC2) encodes a 478 aa Major Facilitator Superfamily (MFS) transporter localized to lysosomes (UniProt Q96SL1). InterPro analysis confirms its classification as a choline/ethanolamine transporter (IPR049680) and SLC49A4-like family member (IPR049604), characterized by a canonical MFS transmembrane domain (aa 59-425) and general substrate transporter superfamily architecture (G3DSA:1.20.1250.20; aa 48-465). AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (pLDDT 84.1), indicating a stable, well-folded dimeric transporter capable of proton-coupled substrate translocation. HPA expression data reveals ubiquitous tissue distribution with low specificity, vesicular localization, and absence in immune cells, consistent with its role in systemic vitamin B6 (pyridoxine) storage and release. STRING interactions highlight a critical link to hypoxia signaling: high-confidence associations with EPAS1 (0.781), VHL (0.761), EGLN1/3 (0.658), and HIF1A (0.626) suggest SLC49A4 modulates pyridoxal-5'-phosphate (PLP) availability, a cofactor for prolyl hydroxylases that degrade HIF-1α under normoxia. ClinVar identifies 19 pathogenic variants (splice sites, deletions, CNVs), confirming disease causality despite the gene's 'dark' literature status (only 5 papers). Recent literature (2022-2025) identifies SLC49A4 as an H+-driven lysosomal exporter of pyridoxine and cationic amphiphilic drugs, linking it to renal carcinoma progression and drug resistance. Mechanistically, SLC49A4 deficiency likely impairs intracellular PLP pools, leading to reduced prolyl hydroxylation, stabilized HIF-1α, and enhanced glycolytic metabolism in hypoxic tumors. This positions SLC49A4 as a metabolic node connecting lysosomal vitamin transport, hypoxia adaptation, and cancer progression.
