## Description
TSEN34 (tRNA splicing endonuclease subunit 34, also known as SEN34, SEN34L, LENG5, or PCH2C) is a 310 amino acid intracellular nuclear protein localized to the nucleus and cytoplasm (UniProt Q9BSV6) with high-confidence well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 83.6). The protein serves as a catalytic core component of the eukaryotic tRNA splicing endonuclease complex. Key domains include the tRNA-splicing endonuclease catalytic domain (IPR006676/IPR006677/PF01974, residues 218-306) forming an ~89 aa module (~29%) that contains the active site residues for phosphodiester bond cleavage. This domain belongs to the tRNA endonuclease-like superfamily (IPR011856/IPR036167/G3DSA:3.40.1350.10). An N-terminal domain (IPR059049, residues 4-70) likely mediates complex assembly. Molecular functions include tRNA-intron lyase activity (GO:0000213) and tRNA splicing via endonucleolytic cleavage and ligation (GO:0006388), placing TSEN34 at the heart of tRNA maturation.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places TSEN34 within the essential tRNA processing and mRNA 3'-end formation machinery: strongest interaction with CLP1 (0.969, Polyribonucleotide 5'-hydroxyl-kinase, a kinase essential for tRNA exon ligation), confirming its role in the second step of splicing. High confidence links include CSTF2 (0.807, Cleavage stimulation factor subunit 2) and CPSF1/CPSF4 (0.805/0.801, Cleavage and polyadenylation specificity factors), reflecting the known physical association between the tRNA splicing endonuclease and the pre-mRNA 3'-end processing machinery. Connections to SEPSECS (0.703, Selenocysteine synthase) suggest a link to selenoprotein synthesis, which requires specific tRNA Sec maturation. Interactions with other complex subunits TSEN2 (0.685), TSEN54 (0.614), and TSEN15 (0.481) confirm the heterotetrameric structure (Sen2-Sen34-Sen54-Sen15). RTCB (0.446, RNA 3'-terminal phosphate cyclase) is the ligase that joins tRNA exons after cleavage.\n\nHPA expression data was not retrieved for the specific ENSG ID, but literature confirms ubiquitous expression across all tissues, consistent with the fundamental requirement for tRNA splicing in all dividing and non-dividing cells. The gene is essential for viability.\n\nClinVar documents 32 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including specific missense mutations (e.g., c.905C>T p.Thr302Ile, c.115G>A p.Gly39Arg, c.451A>G p.Ser151Gly) and large copy number gains on 19q13. These missense mutations are causative for Pontocerebellar Hypoplasia type 2C (PCH2C), a severe neurodevelopmental disorder characterized by microcephaly, cerebellar hypoplasia, epilepsy, and early death. The mechanism involves impaired tRNA splicing leading to accumulation of unspliced pre-tRNAs, activation of the unfolded protein response (UPR), and subsequent apoptosis of developing neurons (particularly in the cerebellum and cortex). CNVs may cause dosage imbalance affecting complex stoichiometry.\n\nConservation is extremely strong across eukaryotes: human (Q9BSV6, 310 aa), mouse (Q8BMZ5, 316 aa), yeast homologs, indicating ancient evolutionary origin and critical function.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: TSEN34 functions as the catalytic engine of the tRNA splicing endonuclease holoenzyme. Upon recognition of the bulge-helix-bulge (BHB) motif in pre-tRNA introns by the structural subunits (TSEN54/TSEN2), TSEN34 positions its catalytic histidine/aspartate residues to perform two transesterification reactions, excising the intron and generating 2',3'-cyclic phosphate and 5'-OH termini. The complex then recruits CLP1 to phosphorylate the 5'-OH, followed by RTCB-mediated ligation. The interaction with CPSF/CSTF suggests a coordinated regulation of tRNA and mRNA processing, possibly coupling tRNA availability to transcriptional output. In PCH2C, mutant TSEN34 retains partial activity sufficient for housekeeping tRNAs but fails to efficiently splice specific tRNA isoacceptors (e.g., tRNA-Tyr, tRNA-His) under high demand in developing neurons, triggering stress responses and cell death.
