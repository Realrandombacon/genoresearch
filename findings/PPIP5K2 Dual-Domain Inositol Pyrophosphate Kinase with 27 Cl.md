## Description
PPIP5K2 (Diphosphoinositol Pentakisphosphate Kinase 2; UniProt O43314) is a massive 1243 amino acid bifunctional enzyme with only 53 publications, classifying it as a genuine dark gene despite critical roles in inositol pyrophosphate signaling, corneal health, and hearing.\n\nSTRUCTURAL ARCHITECTURE: InterPro analysis reveals a unique bipartite domain organization: an N-terminal VIP1-like domain (IPR040557/PF18086, residues 44-133) of unknown specific function, followed by a large C-terminal Histidine Phosphatase superfamily clade-2 domain (IPR000560/PF00328, residues 379-894) containing the catalytic active site (IPR033379, residues 380-394). This architecture places PPIP5K2 in the VIP1 family (IPR037446), capable of both synthesizing and hydrolyzing diphosphoinositol polyphosphates (IP7/IP8). AlphaFold predicts moderate-to-high confidence (global pLDDT 70.1), consistent with a structured catalytic core connected by flexible linkers typical of large signaling enzymes.\n\nINTERACTION NETWORK: STRING-DB identifies ultra-high confidence interactions with the inositol pyrophosphate metabolic network: IP6K1 (0.992), IPPK (0.949), IP6K2 (0.931), IP6K3 (0.913), and its paralog PPIP5K1 (0.491). Notably, it interacts with the VIP/ADCYAP1 signaling axis (VIP 0.732, ADCYAP1 0.789, ADCYAP1R1 0.613, VIPR2 0.433), suggesting crosstalk between inositol pyrophosphate levels and neuropeptide signaling. NHLRC3 (0.581, laforin-interacting protein) hints at potential roles in glycogen metabolism or aggregation diseases.\n\nEXPRESSION & LOCALIZATION: HPA shows ubiquitous expression detected in all tissues with low specificity, consistent with fundamental metabolic roles. Subcellular localization is restricted to vesicles, aligning with its function in membrane-associated signaling pools of inositol pyrophosphates. Classified as a metabolic enzyme, kinase, and transferase with involvement in hearing and non-syndromic deafness.\n\nDISEASE LANDSCAPE: ClinVar contains 27 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including large copy number losses/gains at 5q14-23 and truncating mutations (e.g., p.Arg229Ter). While no single monogenic disease is definitively assigned yet, HPA links it to deafness, and recent literature establishes PPIP5K2 variants as contributors to familial keratoconus (corneal thinning). Recent 2024-2025 studies demonstrate its role in promoting non-small cell lung cancer proliferation via AKT signaling, facilitating DNA homologous recombination repair in colorectal carcinoma, and regulating corneal energy metabolism.\n\nCONSERVATION: Orthologs confirmed in mouse (Q6ZQB6, 1129 aa) and Xenopus (Q5XHF8, 1131 aa), indicating strong evolutionary constraint on the dual-domain architecture.\n\nFUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: PPIP5K2 likely functions as a homeostatic regulator of cellular inositol pyrophosphate levels (IP7/IP8), acting as both a kinase and phosphatase to fine-tune these high-energy signaling molecules. By modulating IP7/IP8 pools in vesicular compartments, it controls downstream processes including DNA repair (via homologous recombination machinery), insulin/AKT signaling, and neuropeptide receptor trafficking (VIP/ADCYAP1). The corneal and auditory phenotypes suggest specialized roles in maintaining extracellular matrix integrity or ion homeostasis in these sensory tissues. The 27 ClinVar variants warrant investigation for haploinsufficiency effects on keratoconus susceptibility and deafness.
