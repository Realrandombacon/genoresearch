## Description
PDGFD encodes a 370 aa secreted growth factor (UniProt Q9GZP0), also known as SCDGF-B or IEGF, that functions as the fourth discovered member of the platelet-derived growth factor family, uniquely forming only homodimers while requiring proteolytic cleavage of its N-terminal CUB domain to activate the PDGF core for receptor binding and mesenchymal cell proliferation. This well-studied gene (256 publications) has emerging clinical significance for dermatofibrosarcoma protuberans (DFSP) through novel fusion events, pancreatic cancer perineural invasion, acute myeloid leukemia (AML) diagnosis, and craniofacial bone regeneration. The protein features bipartite domain architecture: N-terminal CUB domain (IPR000859/PF00431/cd00041/G3DSA:2.60.120.290/SSF49899, residues 52-170/53-174/55-168/59-167) mediating latency control and proteolytic activation; C-terminal PDGF/VEGF domain (IPR000072/PF00341/cd00135/G3DSA:2.10.90.10, residues 259-365/270-365/272-362) containing the cystine-knot cytokine fold (IPR029034, residues 201-368) with eight conserved cysteines forming disulfide bonds; classified within PDGF/VEGF growth factor family (PTHR11633, residues 42-367) with GO terms for growth factor activity and membrane association. STRING interactions reveal PDGFD operates within high-confidence growth factor signaling networks: PDGFC (0.985), platelet-derived growth factor C paralog; PDGFB (0.936)/PDGFA (0.929), other PDGF family members; EGF (0.935)/EGFR (0.932), epidermal growth factor and receptor; IGF1 (0.931), insulin-like growth factor 1; TGFA (0.919), transforming growth factor alpha; INS (0.918), insulin; PDGFRB (0.865), platelet-derived growth factor receptor beta (primary receptor); PDGFRA (0.764), platelet-derived growth factor receptor alpha. HPA confirms tissue-enhanced expression with adrenal gland enrichment (100.6 nTPM), detected in many tissues, cancer-enhanced expression, group enriched in blood cells, Golgi apparatus and vesicles subcellular localization consistent with secretory pathway, classified as developmental protein/mitogen/proto-oncogene involved in RAS pathway. ClinVar lists 22 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants dominated by large-scale copy number losses across 11q22.3 region (11q21-23.2 96.9-113Mb x1, 11q22.1-22.3 101.5-108.5Mb x1, 11q14.1-22.3 81.5-104.7Mb x1, 11q22.1-22.3 98.8-104.6Mb x1, 11q22.1-22.3 101.4-109.3Mb x1, 11q22.3-23.2 103.3-114.3Mb x1), copy number gain (11p13-q25 32.8-134.9Mb x3), and deletions (11q14.1-22.3, single allele deletion/duplication), demonstrating strong dosage sensitivity though specific monogenic diseases remain undefined beyond cancer susceptibility. AlphaFold structure prediction yields confident structure (global pLDDT 73.2), revealing a well-folded growth factor with properly positioned CUB and PDGF domains connected by flexible linker. Mechanistically, PDGFD operates through integrated modes: (1) Latency control—CUB domain maintains PDGFD in latent form requiring proteolytic cleavage by tissue plasminogen activator (tPA) or urokinase-type plasminogen activator (uPA) to release active PDGF core; (2) Receptor specificity—unlike other PDGFs, PDGFD exclusively activates PDGFRβ homodimers, not PDGFRα, providing signaling specificity; (3) DFSP oncogenesis—2025 studies (7+5 citations) identify EMILIN1::PDGFD and other PDGFD fusions as distinct molecular subtype of dermatofibrosarcoma protuberans with better survival than COL1A1::PDGFB fusion cases; (4) Pancreatic cancer perineural invasion—2025 study (2 citations) demonstrates Pdgfd-Pdgfrb axis orchestrates tumor-nerve crosstalk driving pancreatic cancer progression; (5) AML biomarker—2025 bioinformatics study (2 citations) validates PDGFD as promising diagnostic biomarker for acute myeloid leukemia; (6) Bone regeneration—2025 study (3 citations) shows EGR1 promotes craniofacial bone regeneration via activation of ALPL+PDGFD+ periosteal stem cells; (7) RAS pathway activation—PDGFRβ dimerization triggers downstream RAS/MAPK, PI3K/AKT, and PLCγ pathways driving proliferation and survival. Loss-of-function causes defective mesenchymal cell proliferation, impaired wound healing, altered vascular development, while overexpression/fusion drives oncogenesis through constitutive PDGFRβ activation.
