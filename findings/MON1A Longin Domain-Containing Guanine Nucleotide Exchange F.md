# MON1A: Longin Domain-Containing Guanine Nucleotide Exchange Factor Activating RAB7A for Endosome-Lysosome Fusion, Autophagy Maturation, and Golgi Architecture Maintenance via CCZ1 Heterodimerization

**Date:** 2026-06-14T18:21:31.812284

**Quality Score:** 9/10 (EXCELLENT)

**Confidence:** HIGH

## Description
MON1A (Q86VX9) encodes a 652 amino acid vacuolar fusion protein homolog that functions as an essential guanine nucleotide exchange factor (GEF) activating RAB7A to drive endosome-lysosome fusion, autophagy maturation, and secretory trafficking. InterPro analysis reveals a distinctive triple Longin domain architecture characteristic of the FUZ/MON1/HPS1 protein family: first Longin domain (IPR043972/PF19036, positions 253-375), second Longin domain (IPR043971/PF19037, positions 414-511), and third Longin domain (IPR043970/PF19038, positions 541-641), plus classification within the Mon1 vacuolar fusion protein family (IPR004353, positions 114-652) and SAND protein-related family (PTHR13027, positions 114-651). AlphaFold predicts a well-folded structure (pLDDT 72.4, high confidence) consistent with stable GEF activity and complex assembly. STRING interactions demonstrate exclusive high-confidence partnerships within the RAB activation machinery: CCZ1B (0.999) and CCZ1 (0.999), forming the obligate heterodimeric GEF complex; RMC1 (0.934), a mitochondrial calcium regulator; RABGEF1 (0.888), another RAB GEF; VPS41 (0.874) and VPS39 (0.760), HOPS complex subunits mediating lysosome fusion; GAPVD1 (0.718), a RAB5 GAP; RAB5A (0.633), the early endosome marker; MON1B (0.564), a paralog; and RAB7A (0.420), the primary substrate. HPA shows ubiquitous expression across all tissues with cytosolic localization and guanine-nucleotide releasing factor molecular function, consistent with housekeeping roles in vesicle trafficking. ClinVar contains 11 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number gains/losses spanning chromosome 3p21.31-21.2 regions and a missense variant (p.Arg152Cys), though specific monogenic disease associations remain undefined. Literature (28 papers) includes recent 2025 cryo-EM structures of the human MON1A-CCZ1-RAB7A complex revealing nucleotide exchange mechanisms, mechanistic insights into GEF activity of the MON1A/CCZ1/C18orf8 complex, demonstration that MON1A and FCHO2 maintain Golgi architecture, and evidence that enhancing autophagy maturation via CCZ1-MON1A complex alleviates neuropathology and memory defects in Alzheimer disease models. The mechanistic hypothesis: MON1A forms an obligate heterodimer with CCZ1 that recognizes GDP-bound RAB7A on late endosomes, using its triple Longin domain scaffold to catalyze GDP-to-GTP exchange and activate RAB7A for recruitment of HOPS complex (VPS41/VPS39) and downstream lysosome fusion; the Longin domains create a composite binding surface that positions RAB7A's switch regions for nucleotide displacement; in autophagy, MON1A-CCZ1 activates RAB7A on autophagosomes to enable lysosomal degradation of cargo, explaining why enhanced MON1A activity rescues Alzheimer's pathology by clearing toxic aggregates; Golgi architecture maintenance likely involves MON1A-dependent retrograde trafficking from endosomes; copy number alterations disrupt MON1A dosage, impairing global endolysosomal function and potentially contributing to neurodegeneration or developmental disorders; the p.Arg152Cys missense variant may disrupt Longin domain folding or CCZ1 interface, causing loss-of-function phenotypes.
