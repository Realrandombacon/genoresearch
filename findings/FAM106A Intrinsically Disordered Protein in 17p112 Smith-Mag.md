## Description
FAM106A (Gene ID: 80039, UniProt Q4KMX7) is a 169 amino acid protein located on chromosome 17p11.2 (NC_000017.11, positions 18524565-18526845, ~2.3kb region). This protein belongs to the FAM106 family with orthologs identified in chimpanzee (A0A2J8IJD0, 155 aa), orangutan (A0A2J8QZ33, 169 aa), and bear (FAM106B, A0A8M1GLW9, 134 aa), indicating evolutionary conservation across mammals. AlphaFold structure prediction (AF-Q4KMX7-F1) reveals an intrinsically disordered protein architecture with global pLDDT score of 29.7, well below the disorder threshold of 50. This extremely low confidence score suggests FAM106A lacks stable tertiary structure and likely functions as an intrinsically disordered protein (IDP), which typically mediate protein-protein interactions through flexible binding interfaces rather than enzymatic catalysis. Human Protein Atlas has no expression data for this gene (ENSG00000273018), suggesting either very low expression levels, tissue-restricted expression outside surveyed tissues, or lack of validated antibodies. STRING database returns no protein-protein interactions, consistent with IDPs that may have transient, context-dependent interactions difficult to capture in high-throughput assays. However, ClinVar contains 54 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants - all copy number variations spanning chromosome 17p11.2-p12 regions including deletions (chr17:16832948-20527478)x1, (chr17:15850859-20649235)x1 and duplications (chr17:16426633-20998588)x3. These CNVs range from ~3-5Mb spans covering the critical 17p11.2 region. The 17p11.2 chromosomal location places FAM106A within the well-characterized Smith-Magenis syndrome (SMS) deletion region and Potocki-Lupski syndrome (PTLS) duplication region, both caused by dosage alterations of this genomic segment. The critical region contains RAI1 (retinoic acid induced 1), the primary driver of SMS/PTLS phenotypes, along with multiple neighboring genes. Functional hypothesis: FAM106A functions as an intrinsically disordered regulatory protein that likely serves as a molecular scaffold or hub for protein-protein interactions in transcriptional regulation or signal transduction complexes. IDPs are enriched in regulatory roles, particularly in chromatin remodeling, transcription co-activation, and stress response pathways. The 17p11.2 location and extensive ClinVar evidence suggest FAM106A contributes additively to Smith-Magenis/Potocki-Lupski syndrome phenotypes when deleted or duplicated alongside RAI1 and other 17p11.2 genes. Disease mechanism likely involves haploinsufficiency where FAM106A deletion reduces dosage of a regulatory scaffold protein, disrupting protein interaction networks critical for neurodevelopment. Alternatively, FAM106A duplication may cause toxic gain-of-function through aberrant protein interactions. The intrinsically disordered nature suggests potential roles in phase-separated biomolecular condensates, increasingly recognized as important in gene regulation. Priority experiments include identification of binding partners via mass spectrometry of affinity-purified complexes, characterization of expression patterns across developmental stages and tissues (particularly brain), investigation of post-translational modifications common in IDPs (phosphorylation, acetylation), and CRISPR-based functional assays in neuronal models to determine contribution to SMS/PTLS phenotypes.
