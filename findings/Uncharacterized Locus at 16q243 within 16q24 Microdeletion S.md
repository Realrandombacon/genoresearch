## Description
LOC101927793 is an uncharacterized genomic locus on chromosome 16q24.3 (NC_000016.10:88883514-88887135) spanning approximately 3.6 kb. Comprehensive database searches reveal no evidence of protein-coding function: no UniProt accession was identified, precluding InterPro domain analysis and AlphaFold structure prediction. STRING database returned no protein interaction partners, confirming absence of canonical protein-coding function. Human Protein Atlas resolved this locus to Ensembl ID ENSG00000259881 but found no detectable protein expression in surveyed tissues. However, ClinVar analysis reveals 29 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants in this region, including copy number alterations (deletions: chr16:86950106-89335814, ~2.4 Mb; duplications: chr16:52899183-90088654, ~37.2 Mb whole arm; chr16:80067315-90057871, ~10.0 Mb; chr16:64389378-90081985, ~25.7 Mb; chr16:76336203-90088654, ~13.8 Mb; chr16:80717291-90096662, ~9.4 Mb) and notably a CBFA2T3 (core-binding factor subunit alpha-2 partner) splice variant (NM_005187.6:c.712-5C>A). These structural variants encompass the 16q24.3 region and are associated with 16q24 microdeletion syndrome phenotypes including developmental delay, intellectual disability, autism spectrum disorder, and craniofacial dysmorphism. The 16q24.3 region contains ANKRD11 (ankyrin repeat domain 11), whose haploinsufficiency causes KBG syndrome, and CBFA2T3 (ETO2), a transcriptional corepressor involved in hematopoietic differentiation and fused to RUNX1 in t(8;21) acute myeloid leukemia. LOC101927793 likely functions as part of the 16q24.3 cis-regulatory landscape or encodes a non-coding RNA whose copy number alteration contributes to 16q24 syndrome pathogenesis through disruption of chromatin architecture or long-range regulation of ANKRD11. The presence of CBFA2T3 splice variants suggests this locus may overlap with or regulate CBFA2T3 splicing elements. The absence of protein-coding features combined with high pathogenic variant burden (29 variants) indicates this locus resides within a critical dosage-sensitive genomic region where structural variation disrupts neurodevelopmental processes via epigenetic dysregulation.
