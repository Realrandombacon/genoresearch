# STEAP4/TIARP: Golgi-Localized Metalloreductase Coordinating Iron-Copper Homeostasis in Adipose Tissue Thermogenesis, Diabetic Retinopathy, and Cuproptosis Regulation

**Date:** 2026-06-26T15:58:49.748765

**Quality Score:** 9/10 (EXCELLENT)

**Confidence:** MEDIUM

## Description
STEAP4 (459 aa, UniProt Q687X5), also known as TIARP (TNF-alpha induced adipose related protein), STAMP2 (six-transmembrane epithelial antigen of prostate 2), or SchLAH, is a NADPH-dependent metalloreductase residing in the Golgi apparatus that functions as a critical regulator of intracellular iron and copper homeostasis linking metabolic control to oxidative stress responses in adipose tissue, retina, and cancer. The protein architecture features three major functional domains: an N-terminal NAD(P)-binding Rossmann-like domain (G3DSA:3.40.50.720/IPR036291, aa19-201) providing cofactor binding for electron transfer reactions; a central Pyrroline-5-carboxylate reductase catalytic N-terminal domain (IPR028939/PF03807, aa21-107) contributing to oxidoreductase activity; and a C-terminal Ferric reductase transmembrane component domain (IPR013130/PF01794, aa249-394) forming the membrane-spanning region with six transmembrane helices characteristic of STEAP family proteins. The full STEAP family metalloreductase signature spans aa9-458 (IPR051267/PTHR14239). HPA confirms tissue-enhanced expression with adipose tissue enrichment (106.6 nTPM), ubiquitous detection across many tissues, cancer-enhanced expression pattern, immune cell enrichment in blood cells, dual nucleoplasmic and plasma membrane localization, and classification as an enzyme/transporter involved in electron transport, ion transport, iron transport, and oxidoreductase molecular function. Mechanistically, STEAP4 forms medium-confidence interactions with metabolic and secretory pathway proteins: ACPP prostatic acid phosphatase (STRING 0.653) links to phosphate metabolism; PYCR1 pyrroline-5-carboxylate reductase 1 (0.648) connects to proline biosynthesis and mitochondrial function; KLK2 and KLK3 kallikreins (0.612, 0.552) associate with prostate-specific antigen processing; KLKB1 kallikrein B1 (0.602) links to kinin-kallikrein system; FUS RNA-binding protein (0.596) connects to RNA metabolism; ZNF154 zinc finger protein (0.574) represents transcriptional regulation; SLC25A37 mitochondrial sideroflexin-1 (0.527) bridges to mitochondrial iron transport; EEA1 early endosome antigen 1 (0.424) links to endosomal trafficking; TGM4 transglutaminase 4 (0.424) associates with extracellular matrix modification. AlphaFold prediction (pLDDT 90.9) reveals very high-confidence structure with well-defined Rossmann-fold NADPH-binding domain connected to six transmembrane helices forming the ferric reductase core, consistent with crystallographic studies of STEAP family members showing cytoplasmic oxidoreductase domain coupled to transmembrane electron transport channel. Clinical significance includes 12 pathogenic/likely pathogenic ClinVar variants: large copy number losses spanning 7q11.23-21.3 (chr7:74666254-97791584 deletions), 7q21.11-21.3 losses (chr7:84002634-95228883), copy number gains at 7q21.11-21.3/7q21.12-22.1/7p22.3-q36.3 (chr7:77821356-93340137, 87477185-100333327, 43361-159119707 amplifications), chromosomal inversion (chr7:20954043_21001537_114528369_114556605), and TMEM106B-BRAF fusion events; STEAP4 polymorphisms associate with obesity, type 2 diabetes, and metabolic syndrome risk. Conservation across mammals (mouse Q923B6 470 aa, rat Q4V8K1 470 aa, bovine E1BJW2 470 aa) indicates essential metalloreductase function maintained over ~90 million years. Literature (242 papers) establishes STEAP4 in metabolic disease, cancer, and copper toxicity: 2025 studies demonstrate lithium exposure causes trophoblast cuproptosis through FOXO1/STEAP4 axis upregulation in unexplained miscarriage pathogenesis; adipocyte-specific Steap4 deficiency reduces thermogenesis and energy expenditure in mice linking STEAP4 to brown fat function; diabetes-mediated STEAP4 enhancement increases retinal oxidative stress driving diabetic retinopathy development; STEAP4 target NQO1 mediates colon tumorigenesis through redox control mechanisms. The functional hypothesis posits that STEAP4 acts as a 'Golgi redox gatekeeper for metal homeostasis': the cytoplasmic Rossmann domain binds NADPH transferring electrons through the six-transmembrane helix bundle to reduce Fe3+ to Fe2+ and Cu2+ to Cu1+ at the Golgi lumen or cell surface; reduced iron facilitates transferrin receptor-mediated uptake and mitochondrial sideroflexin transport via SLC25A37 coupling; reduced copper loads cuproenzymes in the secretory pathway while excess copper triggers cuproptosis through lipoylated protein aggregation; in adipose tissue, STEAP4 regulates thermogenic capacity through iron-dependent mitochondrial respiration control; in diabetic retina, hyperglycemia-induced STEAP4 overexpression generates excessive ROS causing microvascular damage; in cancer, STEAP4-NQO1 axis maintains redox balance supporting tumor growth; in pregnancy, lithium accumulation upregulates FOXO1-STEAP4 pathway causing trophoblast copper overload and cell death; therapeutic strategies include STEAP4 inhibitors for diabetic retinopathy, STEAP4 activators for obesity/metabolic syndrome, and STEAP4 modulation for preventing lithium-associated miscarriage.
