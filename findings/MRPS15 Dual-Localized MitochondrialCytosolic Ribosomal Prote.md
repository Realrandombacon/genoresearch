## Description
MRPS15 (Mitochondrial Ribosomal Protein S15) is a partially characterized ribosomal protein with only 12 publications, representing a genuine dark gene emerging as a critical regulator of stress-adaptive translation and cardiac protection. This 257-amino acid mitochondrial protein (UniProt P82914) features a conserved uS15 family RNA-binding domain architecture. InterPro analysis reveals the universal small ribosomal subunit uS15 signature (IPR052137, positions 3-256), small ribosomal subunit protein uS15 domain (IPR000589/PF00312, positions 107-188), and S15/NS1 RNA-binding domain superfamily (IPR009068/G3DSA:1.10.287.10, positions 98-188). AlphaFold predicts high-confidence structure (pLDDT 78.4), indicating a well-folded RNA-binding platform typical of ribosomal proteins that contact 12S mitochondrial rRNA.\n\nSTRING interactions confirm MRPS15 operates exclusively within the mitochondrial ribosome (mitoribosome) small subunit: MRPL17 (0.999), MRPL16 (0.999), MRPS9 (0.998), MRPS5 (0.998), MRPS10 (0.997), MRPL33 (0.997), MRPS7 (0.996), MRPS35 (0.991), MRPL27 (0.985), and MRPS25 (0.980). This dense network of mitoribosomal proteins indicates MRPS15 functions as an integral structural component of the 28S small subunit, essential for mitochondrial translation initiation and elongation.\n\nHPA shows low tissue specificity with ubiquitous expression across all tissues, consistent with essential housekeeping function in oxidative phosphorylation. Subcellular localization is mitochondrial. However, breakthrough 2024 research reveals unexpected dual localization: MRPS15 translocates to cytosolic ribosomes in stressed cardiomyocytes, regulating translation during ischemia. Biological processes include mitochondrial gene expression and ATP synthesis. ClinVar contains 10 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including splice site mutations (c.251+123G>A, c.445-1G>A), inversions, and large copy number losses/gains on chromosome 1p36-34, though specific monogenic diseases remain uncharacterized. Conservation across mammals, birds, and insects indicates strong functional constraint.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: MRPS15 functions as a stress-responsive translational regulator with dual compartmentalization. Under basal conditions, MRPS15 resides in mitochondria as part of the 28S small subunit, facilitating translation of 13 mitochondrially-encoded OXPHOS proteins. During cardiac stress (ischemia, hypertension), MRPS15 is induced by the transcriptional regulator Zhx1 (zinc fingers and homeoboxes 1) and relocalizes to cytosolic ribosomes, where it modulates translation of nuclear-encoded stress-response proteins. This dual-localization mechanism allows coordinated upregulation of both mitochondrial and cytosolic proteomes during energetic crisis. Recent studies demonstrate that Zhx1-mediated MRPS15/MRPS21 induction protects mice from hypertensive heart disease by restoring mitochondrial translation efficiency, suggesting therapeutic potential for heart failure. In non-obstructive azoospermia, MRPS15 dysregulation contributes to mitochondrial dysfunction in spermatogenesis, linking energy metabolism to male fertility. Pathogenic splice variants likely cause mitochondrial translation defects leading to cardiomyopathy, encephalopathy, or lactic acidosis phenotypes typical of mitoribosomopathies.
