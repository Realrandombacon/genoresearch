# Sorting Nexin SNX20: A PX Domain-Dependent Regulator of Immune Cell Trafficking and Glioma Progression via PD-L1 Modulation

## Description
SNX20 (Sorting Nexin 20) is a 316-amino acid intracellular protein belonging to the sorting nexin family, characterized by a single N-terminal Phox Homology (PX) domain (IPR001683, positions 72-191; PF00787, 106-186; CD07300, 76-189) that mediates specific binding to phosphatidylinositol-3-phosphate (PI3P) on endosomal membranes, directing subcellular localization and cargo sorting. InterPro analysis confirms its classification within the SNX20/SNX21 subfamily (IPR039937, 45-316), with a predicted C-terminal region likely involved in protein-protein interactions or dimerization. STRING interactions reveal a network centered on immune regulation and cell adhesion: HECA (0.853, high confidence, endothelial leukocyte adhesion), FUT7 (0.699, fucosyltransferase for selectin ligands), ARHGAP30 (0.640, RhoGAP regulating cytoskeleton), SELP (0.600, P-selectin), SASH3 (0.591, signaling adaptor in immunity), CYLD (0.579, deubiquitinase in NF-κB signaling), and SNX16 (0.540, related sorting nexin), suggesting a role in modulating leukocyte trafficking, selectin-mediated rolling, and inflammatory signaling cascades. HPA data shows tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in lymphoid tissue (30.9 nTPM) and bone marrow (14.7 nTPM), cell type-enhanced specificity, low cancer specificity at the RNA level, and nucleoplasmic localization, classified as a predicted intracellular protein involved in protein transport. ClinVar contains 21 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains and losses spanning 16q11.2-24.3 regions, though specific monogenic disease phenotypes remain under characterization (significance marked '?' for most); the genomic location overlaps with regions implicated in developmental disorders and hematologic malignancies. AlphaFold structure (AF-Q7Z614-F1) predicts high confidence folding (pLDDT 83.0) with a well-defined PX domain fold. With only 14 papers, SNX20 represents a genuine 'dark' gene transitioning to gray: recent studies (2021-2022) identify it as a critical prognostic biomarker in low-grade glioma (LGG) and lung adenocarcinoma, where DNA methylation-regulated overexpression correlates with poor survival, IDH wild-type status, and increased immune cell infiltration; notably, SNX20 levels predict response to PD-1 inhibitors in lung cancer, potentially by regulating PD-L1 trafficking or stability via endosomal sorting. The mechanistic hypothesis posits that SNX20 functions as a PI3P-dependent endosomal sorter that controls the surface expression of key immune checkpoints (PD-L1) and adhesion molecules (selectin ligands) in both tumor cells and immune infiltrates, thereby fine-tuning the tumor microenvironment and therapeutic response, making it a promising target for combination immunotherapy.
