## Description
LOC129389356 is a 200bp cis-regulatory element (CRE) located on chromosome 5q22.3 (GRCh38: 125012082-125012282), functionally validated as a transcriptional silencer through STARR-seq massively parallel reporter assays (MPRAs) in K562 erythroleukemia cells. This locus lacks canonical promoter, enhancer, or CTCF-bound insulator chromatin signatures but demonstrates robust repressive activity against the super core promoter SCP1. As a non-coding regulatory sequence, it has no UniProt accession, protein domains, STRING interaction partners, or Human Protein Atlas expression data (query failed to resolve Ensembl ID).\n\nClinVar analysis reveals a high burden of 9 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variants disrupting this region. Notably, the variant spectrum is exclusively composed of large-scale deletions (copy number losses), ranging from focal events (~6Mb, 5q23.1-23.2) to massive arm-level losses spanning nearly the entire q-arm (e.g., 5q21.3-33.2, ~49.5Mb; 5q14.3-31.1, ~40Mb). The complete absence of duplications (0:9 deletion bias) is a striking feature, suggesting that haploinsufficiency of this silencer (or the genes it regulates) is the primary driver of pathology, while triplication may be tolerated or phenotypically silent. These CNVs are strongly associated with 5q- syndrome (myelodysplastic syndrome with isolated del(5q)) and broader 5q deletion syndromes, characterized by macrocytic anemia, thrombocytosis, developmental delay, and intellectual disability. The 5q22-23 region contains critical dosage-sensitive genes such as RPS14 (ribosomal protein, key to 5q- syndrome anemia phenotype) and potentially regulators of myeloid differentiation.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: LOC129389356 functions as a critical distal silencer within the 5q22-23 topologically associating domain (TAD), specifically acting as a brake on proto-oncogenes or lineage-inappropriate factors in hematopoietic stem cells. Given its validation in K562 cells, it likely represses targets that, if unleashed, drive myeloid dysplasia or block erythroid maturation. The mechanism involves recruitment of co-repressor complexes (e.g., PRC2, CtBP) to deposit repressive histone marks (H3K27me3). The exclusive deletion burden implies that loss of this silencer leads to aberrant overexpression of its target(s), contributing directly to the pathogenesis of 5q- syndrome (potentially synergizing with RPS14 haploinsufficiency) or causing neurodevelopmental defects via dysregulation of nearby neural genes. The lack of duplication phenotypes suggests that increased silencer dosage does not critically disrupt homeostasis, or that the target genes are already maximally repressed in the dup state, making the deletion the sole pathogenic mechanism.
