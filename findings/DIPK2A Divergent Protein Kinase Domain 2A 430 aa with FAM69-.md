## Description
DIPK2A (Divergent Protein Kinase Domain 2A) is a 430-amino acid intracellular protein that functions as an atypical serine/threonine kinase regulating autophagosome-lysosome fusion and vesicle trafficking. InterPro analysis reveals a specialized kinase architecture: a divergent protein kinase domain 2A/B family domain (IPR020519, residues 15-429) containing a FAM69-type protein kinase domain (IPR022049, residues 207-405; PF12260, residues 207-405) belonging to the GH11358P family (PTHR32073, residues 15-429). Unlike canonical kinases, DIPK2A belongs to the FAM69 kinase family which lacks conserved catalytic residues, suggesting pseudokinase or regulatory functions rather than enzymatic activity. AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (pLDDT 89.4), indicating a well-folded protein with stable tertiary structure—a feature consistent with structured scaffolding proteins that maintain defined conformations for protein-protein interactions.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places DIPK2A in an exceptionally tight network of coatomer complex subunits and vesicle trafficking regulators with uniformly high confidence: TMED7 (0.901, transmembrane emp24 domain-containing protein 7, COPII vesicle cargo receptor), SCYL1 (0.900, SCY1-like protein 1, nucleocytoplasmic transport), COPB2 (0.900, coatomer subunit beta-2, COPI vesicle coat), COPZ2 (0.900, coatomer subunit zeta-2, COPI vesicle coat), COPA (0.900, coatomer subunit alpha, COPI vesicle coat), COPG2 (0.900, coatomer subunit gamma-2, COPI vesicle coat), COPZ1 (0.900, coatomer subunit zeta-1, COPI vesicle coat), SLC9A9 (0.861, sodium/hydrogen exchanger 9, endosomal pH regulation), SLC9A6 (0.827, sodium/hydrogen exchanger 6, endosomal pH regulation), and PCDH10 (0.427, protocadherin-10, cell adhesion). This network confirms DIPK2A's central role in COPI/COPII vesicle trafficking, linking autophagy (TMED7, SCYL1) with endosomal acidification (SLC9A9, SLC9A6) and retrograde Golgi transport (COP subunits).\n\nHuman Protein Atlas classifies DIPK2A as a disease-related predicted intracellular and secreted protein with low tissue specificity, detected in all tissues, and low cancer specificity. It is associated with autism and autism spectrum disorder, is cell type enhanced in single cell analysis, and shows low immune cell specificity. This ubiquitous expression pattern suggests fundamental cellular housekeeping functions in vesicle trafficking across all tissues.\n\nClinVar reports 19 pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number variations spanning 3q22.1-29 and 3q13.31-26.31 (coordinates include chr3:132561657-197851986x3 gain, chr3:131235568-150065289x1 loss, chr3:116620308-172042292x3 gain, chr3:142729607-157921084x3 gain, chr3:143439359-165252122x1 loss, chr3:138173683-162494699 gain, chr3:138145289-162275610x3 gain, chr3:135288025-146874012 gain, chr3:61892-197851986x3 gain). While no single-gene Mendelian disorder is exclusively named, the autism/ASD association in HPA and 3q chromosomal instability suggest neurodevelopmental implications.\n\nLiterature analysis (7 papers) confirms DIPK2A is a genuine dark gene with one landmark mechanistic discovery. The key finding is a 2020 study (46 citations) demonstrating that DIPK2A promotes STX17 (syntaxin-17) and VAMP7 (vesicle-associated membrane protein 7)-mediated autophagosome-lysosome fusion by directly binding to VAMP7B, a VAMP7 isoform. This establishes DIPK2A as a critical regulator of the terminal step of autophagy. Additional studies implicate DIPK2A in heparan sulfate proteoglycan-mediated α-synuclein fibril uptake (2025, relevant to Parkinson's disease), tempol-modulated ceRNA networks in PCOS ovaries (2022), and DNA methylation signatures in acral melanoma survival (2019).\n\nWe hypothesize that DIPK2A functions through a multi-modal vesicle trafficking mechanism: (1) Autophagosome-lysosome fusion—DIPK2A binds VAMP7B via its FAM69 kinase domain, recruiting STX17-SNAP29-VAMP7 SNARE complexes to drive autophagosome-lysosome membrane fusion, explaining its 46-citation landmark discovery; (2) COPI vesicle scaffolding—high-confidence interactions with all COPI coatomer subunits (COPA, COPB2, COPG2, COPZ1, COPZ2) suggest DIPK2A stabilizes COPI coat assembly for retrograde Golgi-to-ER transport; (3) COPII cargo selection—TMED7 interaction links DIPK2A to COPII vesicle cargo receptor function for ER-to-Golgi anterograde transport; (4) Endosomal pH regulation—SLC9A9 and SLC9A6 interactions position DIPK2A at endosomes where Na+/H+ exchange controls luminal pH, affecting receptor recycling and autophagosome maturation; (5) Nucleocytoplasmic transport—SCYL1 interaction suggests DIPK2A may regulate nuclear pore complex function or mRNA export; (6) Neurodevelopmental role—autism association and ubiquitous brain expression suggest DIPK2A regulates synaptic vesicle trafficking or autophagic clearance of protein aggregates in neurons; (7) Pseudokinase signaling—FAM69 kinase domain may function as a protein-protein interaction module rather than catalytic enzyme, scaffolding SNARE and coatomer complexes. The 19 ClinVar CNVs represent a significant burden of 3q genomic instability potentially affecting autophagy capacity, vesicle trafficking fidelity, and neurodevelopmental outcomes. DIPK2A represents a validated FAM69 atypical kinase, an emerging autophagosome-lysosome fusion regulator via VAMP7B binding, a COPI/COPII vesicle trafficking scaffold, and a candidate gene for autism spectrum disorders and neurodegenerative proteinopathies.
