## Description
NOSTRIN (Nitric Oxide Synthase Traffic INducer, 506 aa, UniProt Q8IVI9) is an F-BAR domain-containing scaffold protein that regulates endothelial nitric oxide synthase (eNOS/NOS3) trafficking and membrane dynamics. Structural analysis reveals four key domains: (1) N-terminal F-BAR domain (IPR031160, aa 1-260) for membrane curvature sensing and tubulation; (2) FCH domain (IPR001060, aa 14-88) for membrane association; (3) central HR1 rho-binding domain (IPR011072, aa 292-372) for Rho GTPase interaction; (4) C-terminal SH3 domain (IPR001452, aa 438-497) for protein-protein interactions. AlphaFold structure shows high confidence (pLDDT 86.6), indicating stable folded architecture. STRING interactions identify NOS3 as primary partner (score 0.828), with secondary connections to WAS (0.579), NOSIP (0.548), WASL (0.514), CAV1 (0.500), and endocytic machinery (DNM2, TRIP10). ClinVar contains 14 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number variations at 2q24-31, suggesting clinical relevance. Literature (95 papers) indicates emerging roles in: (1) endothelial dysfunction - serum NOSTRIN predicts AKI outcomes and vascular impairment in MASLD; (2) eNOS trafficking - induces NOS3 internalization from plasma membrane reducing NO production; (3) benign prostatic hyperplasia - inhibits epithelial proliferation via oxidative stress modulation; (4) caveolae organization - interacts with caveolin-1 for membrane microdomain regulation. Functional hypothesis: NOSTRIN acts as a negative regulator of endothelial NO bioavailability by scaffolding NOS3 into endocytic vesicles via F-BAR-mediated membrane curvature and SH3-dependent protein recruitment, linking Rho GTPase signaling to cardiovascular homeostasis. Mechanistic proposal: F-BAR domain senses/bends plasma membrane while HR1 domain recruits active Rho GTPases, and SH3 domain binds NOS3/NOSIP complex, triggering clathrin-independent endocytosis that reduces surface eNOS and impairs vasodilation—positioning NOSTRIN as therapeutic target for endothelial dysfunction in kidney disease, metabolic syndrome, and hypertension.
