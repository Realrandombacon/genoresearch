## Description
FAM99B (family with sequence similarity 99 member B) represents an extremely understudied component of the human dark genome located at chromosome 11p15.5, a critically important imprinted genomic region. No UniProt accession exists for FAM99B, indicating the protein product has not been experimentally characterized or may represent a non-coding RNA or pseudogene despite gene symbol classification. STRING interaction network returns no protein partners, and HPA expression data is unavailable (ENSG00000291438 not in HPA database), reflecting complete absence of functional characterization in current proteomic resources. Despite this lack of molecular data, ClinVar contains 12 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all consisting of copy number gains (triplications) and losses affecting chromosome 11p15.5 region (chr11:198510-3400939, chr11:196855-2116185, chr11:196855-5321874, chr11:1132899-3213923, chr11:61793-10727969, chr11:1537379-3360769, chr11:446754-18904742, chr11:218365-3377077, chr11:758848-1998025, chr11:196966-3377077). The 11p15.5 region is one of the most clinically significant imprinted domains in the human genome, containing two imprinting centers (IC1 and IC2) that regulate expression of growth-related genes including IGF2 (insulin-like growth factor 2), CDKN1C (cyclin-dependent kinase inhibitor 1C), H19, and KCNQ1OT1. Alterations in this region cause Beckwith-Wiedemann syndrome (overgrowth, macroglossia, abdominal wall defects, embryonal tumors) through paternal allele duplications/maternal allele deletions, and Silver-Russell syndrome (growth restriction, feeding difficulties, triangular face) through opposite mechanisms. While no specific disease is currently attributed solely to FAM99B mutations, its location within this dosage-sensitive imprinted region suggests it may function as a regulatory element, non-coding RNA, or imprinted gene contributing to growth control pathways. The predominance of copy number gains (10 of 12 variants) over losses (2 of 12) in ClinVar suggests potential triplosensitivity. The mechanistic model positions FAM99B as a potential regulatory component of the 11p15.5 imprinting center, possibly functioning as a long non-coding RNA, enhancer element, or small peptide modulating expression of neighboring growth-regulatory genes (IGF2, CDKN1C, H19). Complete absence from protein databases suggests either: (1) FAM99B is a non-coding RNA misannotated as protein-coding, (2) expressed only in specialized cell types not profiled (embryonic tissues, placenta), or (3) rapidly degraded protein evading detection. Future characterization requires: determination of transcriptional status (coding vs non-coding), expression profiling in embryonic/placental tissues, investigation of imprinting status (parent-of-origin expression), and assessment of contribution to 11p15.5 syndrome phenotypes through knockout models.
