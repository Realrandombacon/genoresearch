## Description
ATP5MG (ATP Synthase F0 Complex Subunit g, UniProt O75964) is a 103 amino acid integral membrane protein functioning as a critical structural component of the mitochondrial ATP synthase (Complex V) F0 proton channel. With only 10 literature references (genuine dark gene status), ATP5MG represents a conserved but under-characterized subunit essential for enzyme dimerization, cristae curvature, and efficient oxidative phosphorylation. The protein contains a highly conserved mitochondrial ATP synthase g subunit domain (IPR006808, aa 4-103; PF04718, aa 11-102) classified within the metazoan-specific gamma subunit family (IPR016702, PTHR12386), featuring a predicted single transmembrane helix that anchors it within the inner mitochondrial membrane alongside other F0 subunits. AlphaFold predicts a very high-confidence structure (pLDDT 90.9) across all 103 residues, revealing a stable transmembrane helix flanked by short matrix and intermembrane space loops, consistent with its role as a rigid structural scaffold rather than a dynamic enzyme. STRING interactions reveal an extremely tight functional cluster (all scores 0.997-0.999) with core ATP synthase subunits: ATP5F1D (subunit delta), ATP5F1E (subunit epsilon), ATP5F1B (beta catalytic subunit), ATP5PO (subunit OSCP), ATP5ME (subunit e), ATP5F1C (gamma stalk), ATP5PB (subunit b), ATP5PD (subunit d), ATP5F1A (alpha catalytic subunit), and ATP5MF (subunit f). This interaction network confirms ATP5MG's integration into the F0-F1 holoenzyme, specifically associating with the dimerization interface formed by subunits e and g. HPA classifies ATP5MG as a metabolic transporter detected in all tissues with low specificity, localized to mitochondria and the sperm principal piece, reflecting the universal demand for ATP production and the specific energy requirements of sperm motility. ClinVar documents 28 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains (11q23.3-24.2, 11q23.3-25, 11p13-q25) and deletions/duplications encompassing the 11q23 locus, though specific monogenic diseases directly attributed to ATP5MG point mutations remain undefined, suggesting potential contribution to mitochondrial encephalomyopathies, lactic acidosis, or metabolic syndrome via dosage effects or haploinsufficiency. Functional hypothesis: ATP5MG functions cooperatively with subunit e (ATP5ME) to induce the sharp bending of the inner mitochondrial membrane required for cristae formation and ATP synthase dimerization. While the catalytic F1 head synthesizes ATP, the F0 motor rotates via proton flow; subunits e and g are non-catalytic but essential for organizing F0 complexes into rows along cristae ridges, maximizing proton motive force efficiency. Loss of ATP5MG likely disrupts dimer stability, leading to swollen cristae, reduced ATP output, and increased ROS leakage. The 2022 literature linking ATP5MG to metabolic syndrome-associated cardiovascular disease suggests that subtle variations in ATP5MG expression or function may impair cardiac energy metabolism, predisposing individuals to heart failure under metabolic stress. Disease mechanism: The 28 ClinVar CNVs at 11q23 (a region associated with Jacobsen syndrome and various cancers) suggest that gross genomic instability could disrupt ATP5MG dosage. Haploinsufficiency may cause partial Complex V deficiency, manifesting as exercise intolerance, cardiomyopathy, or infertility (given sperm localization). Overexpression or duplication might alter cristae density, potentially affecting apoptosis sensitivity (as cristae remodeling controls cytochrome c release). Therapeutic implication: ATP5MG represents a potential target for enhancing mitochondrial efficiency in heart failure or metabolic syndrome. Small molecules stabilizing the e-g dimer interface could preserve cristae architecture during ischemic stress. Conversely, disrupting this interface might sensitize cancer cells to apoptosis by inducing mitochondrial fragmentation.
