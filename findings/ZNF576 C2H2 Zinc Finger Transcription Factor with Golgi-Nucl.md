## Description
ZNF576 is a 170 amino acid C2H2-type zinc finger transcription factor located on chromosome 19q13.31 (UniProt Q9H609). InterPro analysis reveals three tandem C2H2 zinc finger DNA-binding domains (IPR013087) at positions 34-57, 71-93, and 112-139, plus additional zinc finger motifs extending to position 170 (PF00096, PF13912), characteristic of sequence-specific DNA-binding transcriptional regulators. The classic beta-beta-alpha zinc finger fold (G3DSA:3.30.160.60, SSF57667) enables recognition of specific DNA sequences through alpha-helix insertion into the major groove. AlphaFold structure prediction shows confident folding (pLDDT 74.5), consistent with stable zinc-coordinated domains. HPA classifies ZNF576 as a transcription factor with ubiquitous expression across all tissues, low tissue specificity, and dual subcellular localization in both nucleoplasm and Golgi apparatus—suggesting potential regulatory roles beyond canonical nuclear transcription, possibly involving Golgi-associated signaling or protein trafficking coordination. STRING interaction network reveals connections to adaptor protein complex subunit AP4B1 (score 0.713), suggesting links to vesicular trafficking, plus interactions with LIM domain proteins PDLIM7 (0.582), AJUBA (0.564), and LIMD1 (0.508) that may mediate cytoskeletal organization or mechanotransduction pathways. Additional partners include WDR83 (0.530), ALKBH4 (0.477), and fellow zinc finger protein ZBTB10 (0.410). ClinVar documents 6 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains and losses spanning 19p13.3-q13.43, indicating dosage sensitivity where altered ZNF576 expression contributes to disease phenotypes. Conservation evidence includes mouse and rat orthologs. Functional hypothesis: ZNF576 acts as a sequence-specific transcriptional regulator that coordinates gene expression programs linking nuclear transcription with Golgi-mediated protein processing/trafficking, potentially regulating genes involved in cell adhesion, cytoskeletal organization, or secretory pathway function through its LIM domain protein interactions. Disease mechanism likely involves transcriptional dysregulation from copy number variation affecting developmental or homeostatic gene networks.
