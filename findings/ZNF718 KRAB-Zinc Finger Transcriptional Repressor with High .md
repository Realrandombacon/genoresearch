# ZNF718: KRAB-Zinc Finger Transcriptional Repressor with High CNV Burden and TRIM28 Interaction

**Date:** 2026-03-23T10:02:56.088361

**Quality Score:** 2.75/10  (E=4.4, D=1.1) [WEAK]

## Description
ZNF718 (Gene ID: 255403) encodes a 478-amino acid Krüppel-associated box (KRAB) zinc finger protein located on chromosome 4p16.3. UniProt accession Q3SXZ3 represents the canonical isoform. InterPro domain analysis reveals a canonical KRAB repressor domain (PF01352, IPR001909, residues 4-75) coupled with three C2H2-type zinc finger motifs (PF00096, IPR013087, residues 171-249) that confer sequence-specific DNA binding. This domain architecture places ZNF718 within the largest family of mammalian transcriptional repressors. AlphaFold structure prediction (AF-Q3SXZ3-F1) yields high confidence (pLDDT 70.9), indicating a well-folded protein with structured zinc finger clusters. STRING interaction network identifies three medium-confidence partners: TRIM28 (score 0.572), FAM181A (0.479), and HOXA4 (0.444). TRIM28 (KAP1) is the canonical co-receptor for KRAB zinc finger proteins, recruiting heterochromatin machinery via HP1 and SETDB1 histone methyltransferase. HPA expression data shows low tissue specificity with detection across many tissues, nuclear localization, and cell type-enhanced expression patterns. ClinVar documents 139 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number losses and gains spanning 4p16.3-15.33 (e.g., chr4:68346-23792768 deletions), though no specific monogenic disease is yet attributed solely to ZNF718. Conservation is evident across vertebrates (Pan troglodytes, Nomascus leucogenys, Polypterus senegalus homologs identified). Functional hypothesis: ZNF718 functions as a sequence-specific transcriptional silencer that recruits the TRIM28/SETDB1 heterochromatin complex to target genomic loci, potentially regulating developmental genes or retrotransposon elements. The high CNV burden suggests dosage sensitivity—haploinsufficiency may disrupt epigenetic silencing programs, while triplosensitivity could cause ectopic repression. The 4p16.3 location overlaps with Wolf-Hirschhorn syndrome critical region, raising possibility of contributory role in this deletion syndrome phenotype.
