Functional hypothesis: LOC101927967 likely represents a critical cis-regulatory enhancer or chromatin organizer controlling HK2 expression in metabolically active tissues (muscle, adipose, cancer cells). The 2 pathogenic CNV duplications indicate strong dosage sensitivity where increased copy number causes HK2 overexpression leading to enhanced glycolytic flux and metabolic reprogramming. Disease mechanism involves altered HK2 transcriptional regulation affecting glucose metabolism, mitochondrial function, and apoptotic threshold. HK2 overexpression promotes Warburg effect (aerobic glycolysis) in cancer cells, confers resistance to apoptosis through mitochondrial outer membrane binding, and enhances glucose uptake in insulin-responsive tissues. The 2p12 duplication syndrome may present with metabolic abnormalities (hypoglycemia, hyperinsulinism), developmental features, or cancer predisposition. The presence of HERV-mediated recombination regions nearby suggests this locus may be prone to recurrent rearrangements through retrotransposon-mediated recombination mechanisms. The absence of protein-coding features combined with ClinVar evidence (2 CNV duplications) and HK2 metabolic gene neighborhood suggests this locus functions as a long-range enhancer regulating HK2 transcription through chromatin looping, enhancer RNA production, or metabolic response element clustering critical for glucose homeostasis and cancer metabolism.
