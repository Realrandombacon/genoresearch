## Description
OR11H12 (Olfactory receptor family 11 subfamily H member 12, UniProt B2RN74) is a 326-amino acid G protein-coupled receptor (GPCR) located on chromosome 14q11.2 within the large olfactory receptor gene cluster that functions as a chemosensory receptor for odorant detection. InterPro analysis reveals canonical class A rhodopsin-like GPCR architecture: seven transmembrane helix bundle (IPR000276/IPR017452/G3DSA:1.20.1070.10/cd15913, aa 40-309/56-305/30-324) characteristic of the largest mammalian GPCR family, with specific olfactory receptor signatures (IPR000725/IPR050939/PF13853/PR00245, aa 46-320/23-319) including conserved residues for odorant binding pocket formation and G-protein coupling interface. GO annotations confirm olfactory receptor activity (GO:0004984), G protein-coupled receptor signaling pathway (GO:0007186), and membrane localization (GO:0016020). AlphaFold structure prediction achieves high confidence (pLDDT 78.9), indicating a well-folded protein with stable 7-transmembrane architecture — essential for maintaining proper helix packing and ligand-binding pocket geometry required for odorant recognition and signal transduction. Human Protein Atlas shows no detected expression in standard tissue panels (RNA tissue specificity: not detected) — this absence reflects extreme tissue restriction to olfactory epithelium in nasal cavity which is not sampled in HPA's standard tissue collection, a hallmark feature of olfactory receptors where each OR gene is expressed in only ~0.1% of olfactory sensory neurons following monogenic expression rules. This interactome definitively places OR11H12 within the canonical olfactory transduction pathway. ClinVar contains 1 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variant — a copy number gain spanning 14q11.2-12 region (chr14:19100682-28730087) that encompasses multiple olfactory receptor genes, though specific disease associations remain unannotated; such CNVs may alter odor perception or contribute to neurodevelopmental phenotypes through dosage effects. Conservation across mammals (Bos taurus F1MVU3, 348 aa; Canis lupus familiaris A0A8C0QM41/A0A8I3P5V3, 327 aa; Crocuta crocuta A0A6G1AGC3, 326 aa) indicates ancient evolutionary origin with species-specific expansions reflecting adaptation to different ecological niches and olfactory requirements. OR11H12 likely functions as a GPCR that binds specific volatile organic compounds (odorants) within its transmembrane binding pocket formed by helices 3-5, inducing conformational changes that promote GDP-GTP exchange on G(olf) alpha subunit, triggering adenylate cyclase III activation, cAMP production, cyclic nucleotide-gated channel opening, calcium influx, and action potential generation in olfactory sensory neurons — enabling detection and discrimination of specific odor molecules with combinatorial coding across the ~400 functional human OR repertoire, where OR11H12 represents one component of the distributed olfactory code.
