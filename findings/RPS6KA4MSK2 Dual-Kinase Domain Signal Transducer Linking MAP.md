# RPS6KA4/MSK2: Dual-Kinase Domain Signal Transducer Linking MAPK/p38 Pathways to Chromatin Remodeling and Inflammatory Gene Expression in Cancer Progression

**Date:** 2026-06-26T15:39:14.058404

**Quality Score:** 9/10 (EXCELLENT)

**Confidence:** MEDIUM

## Description
RPS6KA4 (772 aa, UniProt O75676), also known as MSK2 (mitogen- and stress-activated protein kinase 2), is a dual-specificity serine/threonine kinase that serves as a critical downstream effector of MAPK and p38 signaling pathways to regulate chromatin structure and inflammatory gene transcription. The protein architecture features two non-identical kinase catalytic domains: an N-terminal catalytic domain (cd05614, aa32-364) belonging to the AGC kinase family (IPR000961, aa302-371) and a C-terminal kinase domain (IPR000719, aa411-674), connected by a regulatory linker region containing autoinhibitory sequences characteristic of the RSK/MSK family (IPR016239, aa4-771). Canonical active site motifs are present at aa157-169 and aa526-538 (IPR008271). HPA confirms tissue-enhanced expression with brain enrichment (54.3 nTPM), nucleoplasmic and cytosolic localization, and explicit roles in inflammatory response and stress response pathways. Mechanistically, RPS6KA4 forms a high-confidence signaling complex with its paralog RPS6KA5/MSK1 (STRING 0.904), enabling functional redundancy in many contexts. The kinase directly phosphorylates transcription factors CREB1 (0.891), ATF4 (0.900), and ATF2 (0.708) at serine residues within their transactivation domains, promoting recruitment of CBP/p300 coactivators to immediate-early gene promoters. Upstream activation occurs through direct phosphorylation by MAPK14/p38α (0.459), MAPK11 (0.514), MAPK13 (0.413), and MAPK12 (0.407), positioning MSK2 as a convergence point for stress-activated kinase cascades. AlphaFold prediction (pLDDT 71.2) reveals a confident bilobal kinase architecture with both catalytic domains adopting canonical protein kinase folds connected by an extended linker that likely undergoes conformational changes during activation loop phosphorylation. Clinical significance is demonstrated by 7 pathogenic/likely pathogenic ClinVar variants including large copy number gains at 11q12.1-13.5 and deletions spanning 11p15.5-q25, though no specific monogenic disease has been definitively established. Conservation across mammals (mouse Q9Z2B9 773 aa, rat D3ZSB7 773 aa) indicates essential function maintained over ~90 million years. Recent 2013-2023 literature establishes RPS6KA4 as a cancer prognostic marker: overexpression predicts poor survival in hepatocellular carcinoma through MAPK pathway hyperactivation; genetic variants in RPS6KA4 predict HBV-related HCC survival outcomes; promoter methylation of RPS6KA4 is altered in Wilms' tumor alongside MIR1237 and AURKC; α-lipoic acid blocks NF-κB-mediated RPS6KA4 induction to prevent p53 degradation in colon cancer cells. The functional hypothesis posits that RPS6KA4 acts as a 'stress-responsive chromatin modifier': upon cellular stress (UV, cytokines, growth factors), p38/MAPK cascades phosphorylate the MSK2 activation loop, triggering intramolecular rearrangement that relieves autoinhibition and enables C-terminal kinase domain to phosphorylate histone H3 at Ser10/Ser28 while the N-terminal domain phosphorylates CREB1/ATF transcription factors; this coordinated dual-substrate targeting opens condensed chromatin while simultaneously recruiting transcriptional machinery to inflammatory genes (IL6, TNF, COX2); in cancer contexts, constitutive MAPK signaling causes chronic MSK2 activation, maintaining pro-inflammatory gene expression programs that drive tumor progression, immune evasion, and therapy resistance; brain-enriched expression suggests specialized roles in neuronal activity-dependent gene regulation potentially relevant to neurodegenerative diseases.
