## Description
ENTPD8 (Q5MY95) encodes a 495 amino acid ectonucleoside triphosphate diphosphohydrolase (NTPDase-8), a membrane-associated enzyme that hydrolyzes extracellular nucleoside triphosphates and diphosphates to monophosphates. Despite its clear enzymatic classification within the CD39/NTPDase family, ENTPD8 remains partially characterized with only 28 publications.\n\nSTRUCTURAL FEATURES: InterPro analysis reveals canonical NTPDase architecture with a nucleotide-binding domain (cd24113, residues 19-449) and GDA1/CD39 family signature (IPR000407, PF01150, PS01238). The conserved apyrase motif confirms hydrolase activity (GO:0016787). AlphaFold prediction shows exceptional structural confidence (pLDDT 94.4) across all 495 residues, indicating a highly stable, well-folded enzyme suitable for catalytic function.\n\nINTERACTION NETWORK: STRING-DB reveals ENTPD8 operates within the extracellular nucleotide metabolism pathway through high-confidence interactions with other ectonucleotidases: ENPP1 (0.951), ENPP3 (0.947), ENTPD6 (0.942), ENTPD4 (0.912), and ENTPD5 (0.433). Additional partners include ADK (0.936, adenosine kinase), CTPS1 (0.930), and CTPS2 (0.932), linking extracellular nucleotide hydrolysis to intracellular nucleotide synthesis. This network suggests ENTPD8 functions as part of a coordinated system regulating purinergic signaling.\n\nEXPRESSION & DISEASE: HPA shows intestine-enriched expression (26.0 nTPM top tissue, liver 15.0 nTPM), consistent with roles in gut barrier function and nutrient sensing. Tissue-enhanced pattern with group-enriched single cell specificity. ClinVar contains 109 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including deletions, copy number losses/gains, and missense mutations (e.g., c.1131C>G p.Ile377Met), yet no specific disease phenotype is defined—representing a major knowledge gap. Recent literature (2025) links ENTPD8 overexpression to enhanced anti-PD-L1 therapy response in hepatocellular carcinoma via miR-214-5p inhibition, and ENTPD8 knockout mice show hepatic fibrosis susceptibility.\n\nFUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: ENTPD8 likely regulates intestinal purinergic signaling by hydrolyzing extracellular ATP/ADP/UTP/UDP, controlling P2 receptor activation on enterocytes and immune cells. This would influence gut inflammation, barrier integrity, and microbiome interactions. The high variant burden without defined phenotypes suggests either compensatory mechanisms by other NTPDases, subtle metabolic phenotypes requiring specific challenges, or unidentified gastrointestinal/metabolic disorders. The cancer immunotherapy connection suggests ENTPD8 modulates tumor microenvironment nucleotide levels affecting immune cell function.
