# LRRC10B: Brain-Enriched Leucine-Rich Repeat Protein with High-Confidence Structure and Extracellular Matrix Interaction Network

## Description
LRRC10B (Gene ID: 390205, UniProt A6NIK2) is a 292 amino acid leucine-rich repeat (LRR) domain-containing protein located on chromosome 11q12.2 (NC_000011.10, positions 61508748-61511017, ~2.3kb genomic region). This gene encodes a well-folded protein with clear structural features but incompletely characterized biological function. InterPro domain analysis reveals extensive LRR architecture: multiple leucine-rich repeats (IPR001611/PF13855, positions 44-102; IPR003591 typical subtype, positions 43-65/66-87/89-111; SM00369, positions 43-65/66-87/89-111) forming a curved solenoid structure characteristic of protein-protein interaction scaffolds. The protein belongs to the ribonuclease inhibitor-like superfamily (G3DSA:3.80.10.10, positions 4-126/127-223), suggesting potential capacity for binding diverse protein partners through its concave LRR surface. GO annotations confirm protein binding capability (GO:0005515). STRING interaction network identifies 10 medium-confidence partners revealing two distinct functional clusters: (1) Small leucine-rich proteoglycans (SLRPs) including LUM (lumican, 0.514), KERA (keratocan, 0.514), DCN (decorin, 0.508), and OMD (osteomodulin, 0.509)—all extracellular matrix components involved in collagen fibrillogenesis and tissue organization; (2) Adhesion G-protein coupled receptors including ADGRL1/2/3 (latrophilins, all 0.514)—cell surface receptors mediating cell-cell adhesion and synaptic organization. Additional partners include LRRC26 (0.514, auxiliary subunit of BK potassium channels), LRRTM1 (0.505, leucine-rich repeat transmembrane neuronal protein 1 involved in synapse organization), and PODNL1 (0.504, podocan-like protein 1). Human Protein Atlas classifies LRRC10B (ENSG00000204950) as a predicted intracellular protein with surprising nucleoplasmic and nucleolar subcellular localization, brain-enriched expression (59.2 nTPM top tissue), pituitary gland expression (31.9 nTPM), and fallopian tube expression (27.0 nTPM). RNA shows tissue-enhanced specificity, cancer-enhanced pattern, and is not detected in immune cells. AlphaFold structure prediction (AF-A6NIK2-F1) yields high-confidence model (global pLDDT 83.5), indicating a well-folded stable 3D structure across all 292 residues—consistent with LRR proteins that form rigid curved architectures. ClinVar lists 11 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains and deletions spanning chromosome 11 regions (e.g., chr11:51183549-61422182x3, chr11:32799481-134938470x3, chr11:59923608-76272324x3); no specific monogenic disease is attributed solely to LRRC10B point mutations, suggesting dosage sensitivity within larger chromosomal syndromes. Conservation evidenced by mouse (Q6UJB9 ortholog family), Roborovski hamster (A0AAU9ZMF9, 292 aa), prairie vole (A0ABM0LNI6, 292 aa), and zebrafish relatives with identical protein lengths indicating strong evolutionary constraint. Functional hypothesis: LRRC10B functions as a protein-protein interaction scaffold utilizing its LRR domain surface to bind partner proteins. The STRING network suggests dual potential roles: (1) Extracellular matrix organization through SLRP interactions (LUM, KERA, DCN, OMD), potentially regulating collagen assembly or growth factor sequestration; (2) Neuronal development and synaptic organization through ADGRL latrophilin and LRRTM1 interactions, consistent with brain-enriched expression. The nucleoplasmic/nucleolar HPA localization contradicts extracellular predictions, suggesting LRRC10B may shuttle between compartments or function in nuclear processes such as ribosome biogenesis (nucleolar role) or transcriptional regulation. Cancer-enhanced expression indicates potential oncogenic function through dysregulated protein interactions. Disease mechanism: LRRC10B haploinsufficiency or overexpression may contribute to chromosome 11q12 deletion/duplication syndromes, though specific phenotype remains undefined. The 11 ClinVar CNVs suggest dosage sensitivity; brain-enriched expression predicts potential neurodevelopmental contributions when disrupted. SLRP interactions suggest possible connective tissue abnormalities; ADGRL/LRRTM1 interactions suggest synaptic dysfunction. Priority experiments include determination of actual subcellular localization via immunofluorescence (resolving nuclear vs. extracellular discrepancy), co-IP/MS validation of STRING-predicted interactions, identification of LRR binding partners through structural studies, investigation of LRRC10B role in neuronal development using brain organoids, assessment of LRRC10B expression in brain tumors versus normal tissue, and generation of Lrrc10b knockout mice to assess neurodevelopmental and connective tissue phenotypes.
