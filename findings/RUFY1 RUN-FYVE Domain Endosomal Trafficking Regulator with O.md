## Description
RUFY1 encodes a 708 aa endosomal trafficking regulator (UniProt Q96T51) containing modular domains that coordinate membrane tethering and Rab GTPase interactions. InterPro analysis reveals a bipartite domain architecture: N-terminal RUN domain (IPR004012, cd17694, aa 112-271) for protein-protein interactions and membrane association, and C-terminal FYVE zinc finger (IPR000306, IPR017455, aa 634-704) that binds phosphatidylinositol-3-phosphate (PI3P) for endosomal membrane targeting, plus a RING-type zinc finger (IPR001841, aa 648-696). AlphaFold predicts a confident structure (pLDDT 75.3) with distinct domain separation. STRING interactions reveal a functional network centered on endosomal trafficking: RAB4A (0.989), RAB14 (0.849), and RAB5A (0.644) confirm its role as a dual Rab effector coordinating early endosome dynamics; BMX (0.876, tyrosine kinase) and EPHA3 (0.840, receptor tyrosine kinase) link to signaling pathways; RUFY2 (0.808) is a paralog; PODXL (0.765) is a membrane glycoprotein; RUBCNL (0.590) connects to autophagy. ClinVar reports 17 pathogenic variants, predominantly 5q35.2-35.3 copy number alterations. Literature (34 papers) reveals emerging disease mechanisms: (1) RUFY1-RET rearrangement mediates lorlatinib resistance in ROS1-rearranged NSCLC (2025), (2) RUFY1-PDGFRB fusion drives myeloid/lymphoid neoplasms with eosinophilia (2021), (3) mediates CI-M6PR retrieval from endosomes to trans-Golgi network via Arl8b binding for lysosomal cargo sorting (2022), (4) functional cross-talk between Rab14 and Rab4 through RUFY1 dual effector activity (2010). Mechanistically, RUFY1 serves as a PI3P-dependent endosomal tether that coordinates sequential Rab GTPase activity (Rab5→Rab4→Rab14) for cargo sorting, with oncogenic potential when fused to tyrosine kinases through chromosomal rearrangements. The 5q35.3 location suggests potential contribution to Sotos syndrome phenotypes through trafficking dysregulation.
