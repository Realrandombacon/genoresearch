## Description
C1orf162 (Gene ID: 128346, UniProt Q8NEQ5) is a 155 amino acid transmembrane protein located on chromosome 1p13.2 (NC_000001.11, positions 111473983-111478511, ~4.5kb region). This protein belongs to the transmembrane protein C1orf162 family (IPR037763, residues 9-155; PF27946, residues 18-154; PTHR37997, residues 9-155), a conserved protein family with orthologs in mouse (Q3U7U4, 132 aa) and macaque. AlphaFold structure prediction (AF-Q8NEQ5-F1) shows medium confidence folding with global pLDDT score of 64.9, suggesting a partially structured protein that may contain both ordered transmembrane domains and disordered regions typical of signaling adaptors. Human Protein Atlas classifies C1orf162 as a predicted membrane protein with tissue-enhanced RNA expression detected in all tissues, with striking enrichment in lymphoid tissue (151.3 nTPM) and bone marrow (108.7 nTPM). Subcellular localization is reported as nucleoplasm and Golgi apparatus, suggesting potential roles in intracellular trafficking or nuclear signaling. Notable interactors include LILRB2 (MHC class I receptor involved in immune tolerance), TYROBP/FCER1G (ITAM-bearing signaling adaptors), and MS4A family proteins (B-cell and myeloid cell regulators), strongly suggesting C1orf162 functions in leukocyte signaling pathways. ClinVar contains 14 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all copy number variations spanning chromosome 1p13 regions including deletions (chr1:110066946-116672408)x1 and duplications (chr1:111647582-121343783)x3, indicating dosage sensitivity of this genomic region. The 1p13 chromosomal location places C1orf162 within the leukocyte receptor complex (LRC) region containing multiple immune regulatory genes. Functional hypothesis: C1orf162 functions as an immune regulatory transmembrane protein that modulates leukocyte activation thresholds through interaction with ITAM-bearing signaling adaptors (TYROBP, FCER1G) and inhibitory receptors (LILRB2). The Golgi/nucleoplasm localization suggests it may regulate receptor trafficking or transcriptional programs in response to immune stimuli. High expression in lymphoid tissue (151.3 nTPM) and bone marrow (108.7 nTPM) combined with immune-focused interaction network indicates specific roles in hematopoietic cell development and function. Disease mechanism likely involves haploinsufficiency or triplosensitivity where altered C1orf162 dosage disrupts immune cell signaling balance, potentially contributing to immunodeficiency, autoimmunity, or hematologic malignancies. The 1p13.2 region is associated with developmental disorders and immune dysregulation when deleted or duplicated. Priority experiments include validation of STRING-predicted interactions via co-immunoprecipitation in immune cell lines, subcellular fractionation to confirm Golgi vs nuclear localization, CRISPR knockout phenotyping in primary immune cells, and investigation of C1orf162 expression changes during immune activation.
