## Description
RUFY2 encodes a 606 aa cytoplasmic/nucleoplasmic adapter protein (UniProt Q8WXA3), also known as RABIP4R or ZFYVE13, that functions as a dual-domain endosomal trafficking regulator coordinating Rab GTPase signaling with phosphoinositide membrane recruitment through its RUN and FYVE domains. The protein features a bipartite domain architecture: an N-terminal RUN domain (IPR004012/IPR037213/IPR047332/cd17695/G3DSA:1.20.58.900, residues 2-179/10-165/37-169) for binding active GTP-bound Rab GTPases as an effector/trapping protein; and a C-terminal FYVE zinc finger domain (IPR000306/IPR011011/IPR013083/IPR017455/cd15759/G3DSA:3.30.40.10, residues 525-605/532-599/534-604/536-602/540-598) for specific binding to phosphatidylinositol 3-phosphate (PI3P) on early endosome membranes. STRING interactions reveal RUFY2 positioned at the intersection of receptor tyrosine kinase signaling and Rab-mediated trafficking: EPHA3 (0.884), an ephrin type-A receptor tyrosine kinase critical for axon guidance and neuronal development; RUFY3 (0.684) and RUFY4 (0.683), paralogous RUN-FYVE proteins suggesting functional redundancy or complex formation; BMX (0.648), a cytoplasmic tyrosine kinase linking to growth factor signaling; PBLD (0.588), phenazine biosynthesis-like domain protein; FRMD4A (0.586), FERM domain protein associated with neuronal migration; CLIP1 (0.566), cytoplasmic linker protein connecting endosomes to microtubules; RUFY1 (0.493), another family member; MYO5C (0.466), myosin-Vc motor protein for actin-based vesicle transport; and RAB33A (0.416), a Golgi-associated Rab GTPase confirming Rab binding specificity. HPA shows brain-enhanced expression (31.3 nTPM) with nucleoplasm localization, tissue-enhanced pattern detected in all tissues—consistent with housekeeping endosomal trafficking roles with neuronal specialization. ClinVar lists 18 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number losses/gains spanning chromosome 10q11.21-q26.3 (ranging from 2.3Mb to 58Mb), intronic splice variants (c.1205+729G>A), and deletions, demonstrating dosage sensitivity though specific disease associations remain undefined—potentially contributing to neurodevelopmental disorders given brain expression and EPHA3 interaction. AlphaFold structure prediction yields high confidence (global pLDDT 81.9), revealing well-folded RUN and FYVE domains connected by an extended linker region—consistent with effector proteins requiring spatial separation for simultaneous Rab and membrane engagement. Mechanistically, RUFY2 functions through multiple integrated modes: (1) As a Rab effector, the RUN domain selectively traps active GTP-bound Rab35 (based on 2011 genome-wide RUN domain study showing Rab35 specificity for RUFY family), potentially also Rab33A per STRING interaction, regulating endosomal cargo sorting and recycling; (2) FYVE domain mediates PI3P-dependent recruitment to early endosome membranes, positioning RUFY2 at sites of active Rab signaling; (3) EPHA3 interaction suggests RUFY2 links ephrin receptor endocytosis to Rab-mediated trafficking—critical for growth cone collapse and axon guidance during neuronal development; (4) MYO5C and CLIP1 interactions indicate coordination of endosomal transport along both actin (myosin) and microtubule (CLIP) cytoskeletons; (5) FRMD4A partnership suggests roles in neuronal migration pathways; (6) RUFY1/3/4 interactions imply potential hetero-oligomerization or functional compensation within the RUFY family. Disruption of RUFY2 would cause defective endosomal trafficking, impaired EPHA3 receptor recycling, aberrant axon guidance, and potential neurodevelopmental defects—phenotypes consistent with the 10q deletion/duplication syndromes observed in patients. With only 13 literature papers, RUFY2 remains significantly undercharacterized despite clear mechanistic predictions from domain architecture.
