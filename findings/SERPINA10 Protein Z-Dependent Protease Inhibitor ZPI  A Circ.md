# SERPINA10: Protein Z-Dependent Protease Inhibitor (ZPI) — A Circulating Serpin Family Anticoagulant Regulating Factor Xa and Factor XIa Inhibition Through Protein Z Cofactor Enhancement in Thrombosis and Bleeding Disorders

## Description
SERPINA10 (Serpin Family A Member 10, also known as Protein Z-Dependent Protease Inhibitor or ZPI) is a 444-amino acid circulating serine protease inhibitor characterized by a canonical serpin domain architecture (IPR000215/IPR023796/IPR033835/IPR036186/PF00079/PTHR11461, positions 74-442 for serpin family, 79-441 for serpin domain core, 67-442 for ZPI-specific serpin domain) containing the characteristic serpin superfamily fold with three domains (G3DSA:2.30.39.10 alpha-1-antitrypsin domain 1 positions 242-339/393-441; G3DSA:3.30.497.10 antithrombin subunit I domain 2 positions 82-241/340-392; IPR042178/42185 serpin superfamily domains 1-2 positions 82-441), classified under serine-type endopeptidase inhibitor activity (GO:0004867), extracellular region localization (GO:0005576), and blood coagulation regulation (GO:0007596). STRING interactions reveal an exceptionally tight functional network exclusively with coagulation cascade components: PROZ (0.996, Protein Z - highest confidence partner indicating obligate cofactor relationship), F10 (0.816, coagulation factor X), FGA (0.777, fibrinogen alpha chain), PLG (0.734, plasminogen), CTRB2 (0.668, chymotrypsinogen B2), FGG (0.665, fibrinogen gamma chain), FAM20A (0.654), CTRB1 (0.634, chymotrypsinogen B1), FAM20C (0.632), and PLAT (0.529, tissue plasminogen activator), indicating SERPINA10 functions as an integral anticoagulant regulator. HPA data is unavailable for this gene (not in HPA database), though literature indicates liver synthesis and plasma circulation as a vitamin K-dependent pathway component. ClinVar contains 24 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including single nucleotide variants (c.-51+320A>G promoter variant, c.1143+2T>A splice site variant) and large copy number losses/gains spanning 14q31.1-32.2, 14q31.3-32.33, 14q23.1-32.33, and 14q13.3-32.33 regions affecting chromosome 14; specific monogenic disease phenotypes include deep vein thrombosis susceptibility and bleeding disorders though variant significance often marked '?' requiring further characterization. AlphaFold structure (AF-Q9UK55-F1) predicts high confidence folding (pLDDT 86.8) consistent with a well-structured serpin containing the characteristic three-domain antithrombin-like fold with reactive center loop (RCL) for protease trapping. With 41 papers, SERPINA10 represents a 'gray' gene with established biochemical mechanism but emerging clinical associations (2013-2026): Protein Z dramatically enhances ZPI-mediated inhibition of factor Xa (1000-fold enhancement); ZPI also directly inhibits factor XIa independent of Protein Z; SNP rs2232710 investigated for deep vein thrombosis association (negative result in 2016 study); recent work explores ZPI variants in congenital bleeding disorders via multigenic NGS approaches; integrative analysis links SERPINA10 genetic risk factors to acute myeloid leukemia; and acute phase response regulation of ZPI levels during inflammation. The mechanistic hypothesis posits that SERPINA10 functions as a dual-mechanism anticoagulant that: (1) forms a quaternary complex with Protein Z cofactor, calcium ions, and phospholipid membranes to achieve maximal inhibitory efficiency against factor Xa in the prothrombinase complex; (2) directly inhibits factor XIa in the intrinsic pathway independent of Protein Z providing redundant anticoagulant control; (3) utilizes its reactive center loop as bait for target proteases undergoing conformational change that traps the protease in a covalent acyl-enzyme intermediate; (4) undergoes acute phase regulation linking coagulation control to inflammatory states; chromosome 14q31-32 CNVs may contribute to thrombophilia or bleeding diathesis through dosage effects, while the PROZ interaction (0.996 score) represents one of the tightest cofactor dependencies in the coagulation cascade explaining why combined Protein Z/ZPI deficiency produces severe thrombotic phenotypes.
