## Description
ACTL8 (Actin-Like Protein 8) is a 366-amino acid intracellular cytoskeletal protein (UniProt Q9H568) representing a genuine dark gene with only 30 publications despite its emerging roles as a cancer/testis antigen driving tumor progression across multiple malignancies through metabolic reprogramming and cytoskeletal remodeling. Structurally, ACTL8 belongs to the actin superfamily with canonical actin fold architecture: Actin family domain (IPR004000/PF00022/SM00268, residues 3-366/4-363) containing the characteristic four-subdomain actin structure, ATPase nucleotide binding domain (IPR043129, residues 4-148/141-363) with conserved nucleotide-binding pocket, actin-like ATPase domain (SSF53067, residues 4-148/141-363) defining the actin/hexokinase/HSP70 ATPase superfamily, and G3DSA homologous superfamilies (G3DSA:3.30.420.40 at residues 6-132/337-363/133-174/270-336; G3DSA:3.90.640.10 at residues 175-269 corresponding to actin domain 4). AlphaFold predicts high-confidence structure (pLDDT 88.6), consistent with a well-folded actin-like protein maintaining the conserved ATP-binding cleft and subdomain organization despite sequence divergence from conventional actins. Functionally, ACTL8 localizes to cytosol with highly restricted tissue expression (HPA: tissue enriched, detected in single tissue, testis-specific at 6.8 nTPM, cell type enriched), classified as a predicted intracellular protein—this testis-restricted pattern is the hallmark signature of cancer/testis (CT) antigens that are normally silenced in somatic tissues but reactivated in malignancies. STRING interactions reveal connections to dynactin complex and actin-related proteins: DCTN2 (0.748, dynactin subunit 2/p50), ACTR10 (0.740, actin-related protein 10), DCTN4 (0.698), DCTN6 (0.684), DCTN5 (0.673), XAGE3 (0.668, GAGED2a cancer/testis antigen), ACTR1B (0.662), ACTR1A (0.662), DCTN3 (0.607), and LOC100509620 (0.578). The dynactin interaction network positions ACTL8 within dynein-mediated intracellular transport and microtubule dynamics, while the XAGE3 connection links it to the cancer/testis antigen family. Critically, ClinVar documents 17 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number losses at 1p36.13 (chr1:16950757-19234873)x1, 1p36.32-36.12 (chr1:4436802-22782007)x2, 1p36.21-36.12 (chr1:16194137-20561434)x1, 1p36.31-36.13 (chr1:6758933-19287770)x1, 1p36.13 (chr1:16773001-20221073)x1, 1p36.13-36.12 (chr1:16785250-23491592)x1, 1p36.21-36.12 (chr1:16041431-21295864)x1, deletions (NC_000001.10:g.4481271_20530242del), and copy number gains at 1p36.13-36.12 (chr1:17291707-23016395)x4, 1p36.33-35.1 (chr1:99125-34026935)x3, yet ZERO diseases are formally associated—a profound gap given the cancer driver functions. Recent literature establishes emerging oncogenic roles: ACTL8 promotes gastric cancer progression through PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway activation (6 citations, 2024); momordin Ic suppresses breast cancer growth by targeting ACTL8-dependent glutamine metabolism and PI3K/AKT/mTOR-MYC axis (2026); insulin resistance-related ten-gene signature identifies ACTL8 as prognostic and immunological biomarker in endometrial cancer (2026); upregulated ACTL8 contributes to invasion, metastasis, and poor prognosis in colorectal cancer (18 citations, 2019); and ACTL8 expression profile with CTCFL, OIP5, and XAGE3 in glioma shows prognostic significance (6 citations, 2020). The mechanistic hypothesis: ACTL8 functions as a divergent actin paralog that retains ATPase activity but has evolved specialized roles in cancer/testis antigen programs. In normal physiology, ACTL8 likely participates in spermatogenesis-specific cytoskeletal remodeling (testis enrichment at 6.8 nTPM), potentially regulating meiotic spindle dynamics, acrosome formation, or sperm flagellar assembly through dynactin-mediated transport. In cancer, ACTL8 reactivation drives tumor progression through multiple mechanisms: (1) PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway activation promoting cell proliferation and survival; (2) glutamine metabolism reprogramming supporting anabolic growth and MYC stabilization; (3) dynactin complex modulation altering intracellular trafficking of growth factor receptors; (4) cytoskeletal remodeling enhancing invasion and metastasis. The XAGE3 interaction suggests coordinated CT antigen expression programs. The 1p36 chromosomal location is a frequent deletion hotspot in neuroblastoma and other cancers—ACTL8 haploinsufficiency may contribute to 1p36 deletion syndrome phenotypes (developmental delay, intellectual disability) not yet mapped. Future work should define ACTL8's specific cytoskeletal functions versus conventional actin, identify ACTL8-specific binding partners in spermatogenesis versus cancer, investigate whether germline ACTL8 mutations cause male infertility or neurodevelopmental disorders, determine if ACTL8 is a viable immunotherapy target (CT antigen vaccine candidate), and clarify mechanisms linking ACTL8 to glutamine metabolism and mTOR signaling.
