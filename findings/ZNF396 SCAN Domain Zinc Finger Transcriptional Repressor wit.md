# ZNF396: SCAN Domain Zinc Finger Transcriptional Repressor with Oligomerization-Dependent DNA Recognition

**Date:** 2026-03-20T19:33:30.947787

**Quality Score:** 2.05/10  (E=4.1, D=0.0) [WEAK]

## Description
ZNF396 encodes a 335-amino acid SCAN domain-containing zinc finger protein functioning as a sequence-specific transcriptional repressor. The protein architecture features an N-terminal SCAN domain (PF02023, IPR003309, residues 48-135) mediating homo- and heterodimerization with related SCAN family members, enabling combinatorial DNA-binding specificity. Three C-terminal C2H2 zinc finger clusters (PF00096, IPR013087, residues 252-329) provide GC-rich DNA recognition. Human Protein Atlas classifies ZNF396 as a transcription factor/repressor with low tissue specificity, detected across many tissues, immune cell-enhanced expression, and dual nuclear/cytoplasmic localization. STRING interactions reveal a dense network of SCAN-zinc finger partners: ZNF446 (0.832), ZSCAN18 (0.831), ZBED9 (0.829), ZKSCAN4 (0.806), ZKSCAN3 (0.794), PGBD1 (0.783), ZSCAN20 (0.778), and SCAND1 (0.746), suggesting formation of higher-order repression complexes. ClinVar documents 43 pathogenic variants, predominantly chromosome 18q12.2 copy number alterations (multiple CNV gains and losses spanning 18q11.1-18q23), indicating extreme dosage sensitivity where gene copy number changes disrupt transcriptional regulatory networks. The SCAN domain oligomerization interface combined with C2H2 DNA-binding fingers supports a mechanism where ZNF396 heterodimerizes with paralogs to recognize composite DNA elements, recruiting corepressors to silence target genes involved in cell differentiation and immune function. Pathogenic CNVs likely cause haploinsufficiency or dominant-negative effects through altered stoichiometry of repression complexes.
