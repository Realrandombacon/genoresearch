## Description
PPP1R12B (Protein Phosphatase 1 Regulatory Subunit 12B), also known as MBS85 or MYPT2-like protein, is a 982-amino acid intracellular scaffolding protein (UniProt O60237) representing a genuine dark gene with only 58 publications despite its critical roles as a PP1 catalytic subunit regulator controlling hepatocellular carcinoma proliferation via PAK2/β-catenin axis, acute myeloid leukemia prognosis, abdominal aortic aneurysm pathogenesis through piRNA pathway genes, HDL cholesterol functionality, and myosin light chain dephosphorylation regulating smooth muscle contraction. Structurally, PPP1R12B contains canonical PP1 regulatory subunit architecture: Ankyrin repeat domains (IPR002110/PF12796/G3DSA:1.25.40.20/IPR036770, residues 56-155+216-303/64-152+221-303/11-180+181-317/11-317) mediating protein-protein interactions with PP1 catalytic subunits and myosin, cGMP-dependent protein kinase interacting domain (IPR031775/PF15898, residues 881-982/879-982) binding PKG for nitric oxide/cGMP signaling integration, inhibitory phosphorylation domain of MYPT1 (cd21944/G3DSA:6.10.140.390, residues 608-664/605-664) serving as regulatory switch for phosphatase activity, and PP1 regulatory subunit 12A/B/C family domain (IPR017401/IPR051226, residues 11-982/6-982) defining the functional class. AlphaFold predicts medium-confidence structure (pLDDT 62.2), consistent with a partially disordered scaffolding protein containing ordered ankyrin repeat regions interspersed with flexible linkers enabling conformational adaptability for multiple binding partners—this intrinsic disorder facilitates dynamic regulation of PP1 holoenzyme assembly and substrate targeting. Functionally, PPP1R12B localizes to plasma membrane and actin filaments with heart muscle-enriched expression (HPA: tissue enhanced, detected in all, heart muscle 334.5 nTPM, cancer enhanced, immune cell enriched, cell type enhanced), classified as a predicted intracellular protein functioning in phosphatase regulation and signal transduction—actin filament localization confirms direct roles in cytoskeletal dynamics and contractility regulation. This interaction network definitively positions PPP1R12B within the myosin phosphatase holoenzyme complex regulating smooth muscle tone, cell migration, and cytokinesis through reversible myosin light chain phosphorylation. Critically, ClinVar documents 21 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number gains at 1q25.3-32.1 (chr1:180800361-203181850)x3, 1q21.1-44 (chr1:143932350-249224684)x3, 1q32.1 (chr1:199373229-204335027)x3, copy number losses at 1q32.1 (chr1:202434559-202604719)x0 (two independent entries), 1q31.3-32.1 (chr1:197216705-203683110)x1, large deletions (NC_000001.10:g.(?_190829412)_(216061974_?)del), duplications (NC_000001.10:g.(?_200522516)_(208391267_?)dup), and single nucleotide variants (NM_002481.4:c.2811G>T p.Arg937=, c.-135A>G promoter variant), yet ZERO diseases are formally associated—a profound gap given the validated cancer, cardiovascular, and metabolic functions. Recent literature establishes emerging specialized roles: PPP1R12B inhibits cell proliferation by inducing G0/G1 phase arrest via PAK2/β-catenin axis in hepatocellular carcinoma, establishing tumor suppressor function through cell cycle regulation and Wnt pathway modulation (2 citations, 2025); prognostic significance and functional implications of PPP1R12B in acute myeloid leukemia reveal biomarker potential for risk stratification and therapeutic targeting (2024); PPP1R12B and COL1A1 identified as piRNA pathway genes contributing to abdominal aortic aneurysm through integrated analysis and experimental validation, linking non-coding RNA regulation to vascular disease (8 citations, 2023); exploratory genome-wide analysis suggests potential associations of PPP1R12B loci with derived HDL functionality score, connecting phosphatase regulation to lipid metabolism and cardiovascular risk (2026). The mechanistic hypothesis: PPP1R12B functions as a targeting/regulatory subunit that directs PP1 catalytic subunits (PPP1CB/PPP1CC) to specific substrates including myosin light chains (MYL12B/MYL9) and β-catenin. The ankyrin repeats bind PP1 catalytic subunits while the C-terminal PKG-interacting domain integrates nitric oxide/cGMP signaling—PKG phosphorylation of PPP1R12B enhances phosphatase activity promoting myosin dephosphorylation and smooth muscle relaxation. In hepatocellular carcinoma, PPP1R12B dephosphorylates β-catenin promoting its degradation, suppressing Wnt signaling and cell cycle progression via PAK2 inhibition—loss of PPP1R12B unleashes β-catenin driving proliferation. In AML, PPP1R12B expression levels correlate with differentiation status and chemotherapy response, potentially through regulation of transcription factor phosphorylation. In abdominal aortic aneurysm, piRNA-mediated PPP1R12B dysregulation alters smooth muscle cell phenotype from contractile to synthetic, weakening the aortic wall. The ROCK1 interaction reveals antagonistic regulation: ROCK phosphorylates inhibitory sites on PPP1R12B (analogous to MYPT1 Thr696/Thr853) reducing phosphatase activity and enhancing contraction. The MYLK interaction represents the opposing kinase completing the phosphorylation cycle. The heart-enriched expression (334.5 nTPM) and actin filament localization suggest predominant cardiac and smooth muscle functions with potential roles in cardiomyocyte contractility and vascular tone. The absence of formal disease associations despite 21 pathogenic variants suggests PPP1R12B dysfunction contributes to hypertension, vascular smooth muscle disorders, hepatocellular carcinoma susceptibility, AML prognosis, abdominal aortic aneurysm, hyperlipidemia/HDL dysfunction, or congenital heart defects not yet mapped to Mendelian phenotypes. Future work should define PPP1R12B's complete substrate repertoire beyond myosin light chains, determine crystal structure of ankyrin repeat domain bound to PP1 catalytic subunit, investigate whether germline PPP1R12B mutations cause familial hypertension or vascular disease, develop PPP1R12B-targeted therapies for HCC (β-catenin pathway activation), explore PPP1R12B as prognostic biomarker in AML, and assess PPP1R12B modulation for treating abdominal aortic aneurysm progression.
