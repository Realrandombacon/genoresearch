# ZNF475: Testis-Specific C2HC Zinc Finger Chromatin Regulator with Cell Cycle Control Function

## Description
ZNF475 encodes a small 108-amino acid zinc finger protein containing two C2HC-type zinc finger domains (PF13913, IPR049899, IPR026319) positioned at residues 6-35 and 79-108. The protein shows testis-specific expression at 185.2 nTPM (HPA), classified as tissue-enriched and intracellular. STRING interaction analysis reveals 10 high-confidence protein partners: CASZ1 (0.893), MYC (0.727), IPO8 (0.617), MAP3K20 (0.610), CDKN1A (0.607), SESN3 (0.550), CIZ1 (0.530), DOT1L (0.505), PRDM2 (0.454), and TRIM28 (0.419). These interactions strongly implicate ZNF475 in chromatin regulation (TRIM28, DOT1L, PRDM2 are histone modifiers), cell cycle control (CDKN1A/p21), and transcriptional regulation (MYC, CASZ1). ClinVar documents 10 pathogenic copy number loss variants spanning 5q21-5q31, indicating haploinsufficiency causes disease. The C2HC zinc finger architecture suggests nucleic acid binding capability, while the interaction network positions ZNF475 as a scaffold protein recruiting chromatin modifiers to specific genomic loci. Testis-specific expression combined with cell cycle partner interactions suggests a role in balancing germ cell proliferation versus differentiation during spermatogenesis. Loss of ZNF475 may disrupt chromatin silencing in germ cells, leading to aberrant gene expression and potential infertility or developmental defects.
