# HAAO: A Misnamed Kynurenine Dioxygenase with High Clinical Variants

**Date:** 2026-05-07T23:29:18.337421

**Quality Score:** 9/10 (EXCELLENT)

**Confidence:** MEDIUM-HIGH

## Description
HAAO (Hydroxysteroid 12-alpha-hydroxylase) is a 'dark gene' characterized by a severe nomenclature error. Despite its name implying a role in steroid metabolism, UniProt and InterPro data confirm it is 3-hydroxyanthranilate 3,4-dioxygenase, a cytosolic enzyme in the kynurenine pathway. It catalyzes the conversion of 3-hydroxyanthranilate to quinolinic acid (QUIN), a precursor for NAD+ biosynthesis. AlphaFold predicts a very high confidence (95.8 pLDDT) stable cupin-fold structure with nonheme ferrous iron binding. ClinVar reveals 29 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants (e.g., p.Phe128fs, p.Leu84Pro) with zero associated named diseases, suggesting dysfunction leads to neurotoxic metabolite accumulation or metabolic defects without a cataloged syndrome. HPA expression shows high liver abundance (276.6 TPM) and cancer enhancement, yet the gene remains clinically 'dark' due to the lack of a specific disease label despite clear mechanistic pathology.
