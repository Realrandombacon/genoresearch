## Description
BPY2 (UniProt: O14599, aliases BPY2A/VCY2/VCY2A) encodes a 106 amino acid testis-specific basic protein representing a dark gene with 79 publications in Semantic Scholar (many unrelated chemistry papers about bipyridine complexes). Located in the AZF (azoospermia factor) region of Yq11.223, BPY2 is one of three nearly identical copies on chromosome Y, with two copies in a palindromic region. The gene is expressed specifically in testis (HPA: 1.1 nTPM, tissue enriched, detected in single tissue) and not detected in cancer or immune cells. AlphaFold structural prediction yields very low confidence (global pLDDT: 41.5), indicating BPY2 is an intrinsically disordered protein (IDP) that likely functions through protein-protein interactions rather than enzymatic activity - consistent with its basic charge and small size. STRING interaction analysis identifies 10 high-confidence partners, all Y-chromosome genes involved in spermatogenesis: CDY2A (chromodomain protein Y-linked 2A, score 0.948), DAZ1 (deleted in azoospermia 1, 0.937), PRYP3 (PTPRZ1-related Y-linked pseudogene 3, 0.930), CDY2B (0.926), DAZ2 (0.897), CDY1 (chromodomain protein Y-linked 1, 0.810), PRY (PTPRZ1-related Y-linked, 0.777), PRY2 (0.768), VCY1B (vacuolar protein sorting 13 homolog C Y-linked 1B, 0.717), and VCY (0.717). This interaction network places BPY2 within the Y-chromosome spermatogenesis machinery alongside DAZ family RNA-binding proteins and CDY chromatin modifiers. ClinVar contains 41 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all copy number losses (deletions) and gains (duplications) spanning Yq11.221-q12, consistent with AZF microdeletion syndromes causing male infertility, though no specific disease names are assigned in ClinVar. Literature from 2000 suggests BPY2, CDY1, and DAZ are not essential for sustained fertility, but 2007 research links BPY2 Alu sequence variants to Sertoli cell-only phenotype in infertile men. The encoded protein interacts with UBE3A (ubiquitin protein ligase E3A), suggesting a role in ubiquitin-mediated protein degradation during germ cell development. Based on intrinsically disordered structure (pLDDT 41.5), testis-specific expression, Y-chromosome AZF localization, interaction with DAZ/CDY spermatogenesis factors, UBE3A binding, and infertility-associated copy number variants, BPY2 functions as a disordered scaffold protein that coordinates protein-protein interactions during spermatogenesis, potentially recruiting UBE3A ubiquitin ligase to target proteins for degradation in developing germ cells. The palindromic arrangement suggests gene conversion maintains functional copies, while deletions cause AZF syndrome and male infertility. BPY2 may regulate the stability of spermatogenesis factors through ubiquitin-proteasome pathways, with its disordered structure enabling flexible binding to multiple partners in the Y-chromosome protein network.
