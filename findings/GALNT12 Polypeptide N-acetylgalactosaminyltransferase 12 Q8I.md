## Description
GALNT12 (Polypeptide N-acetylgalactosaminyltransferase 12; UniProt Q8IXK2) is a 581 amino acid essential Golgi-resident glycosyltransferase with 55 publications, representing a PARTIALLY DARK gene encoding a critical enzyme that initiates mucin-type O-glycosylation by transferring N-acetylgalactosamine (GalNAc) to serine/threonine residues of target proteins. Despite its established role in cancer progression (glioblastoma, fibrosarcoma, prostate, esophageal squamous cell carcinoma) and recent mechanistic insights into BMP pathway activation via BMPR1A glycosylation, GALNT12 remains incompletely characterized regarding its precise substrate specificity beyond known mucins, the structural basis of its lectin-mediated substrate selection, and the full spectrum of developmental or metabolic diseases linked to its loss-of-function beyond cancer signatures. The protein functions as a molecular editor of the cell surface glycoproteome, directly influencing cell adhesion, signaling receptor activity, and metastatic potential.\n\nSTRUCTURAL ARCHITECTURE: InterPro analysis reveals GALNT12 contains the canonical pp-GalNAc-T architecture essential for O-glycan initiation: (1) N-terminal region (residues 1-67) — likely cytoplasmic tail and transmembrane anchor directing Golgi localization; (2) Catalytic Domain (cd02510/IPR001173/IPR045885/PF00535, residues 139-433/~139-322, ~294 aa) — Glycosyltransferase family 2 fold (G3DSA:3.90.550.10) containing the donor (UDP-GalNAc) and acceptor binding sites; this domain belongs to the nucleotide-diphospho-sugar transferase superfamily (IPR029044); (3) Ricin B-type Lectin Domain (cd23471/IPR000772/IPR035992/PF00652, residues 443-579/~445-577, ~136 aa) — beta-trefoil fold (G3DSA:2.80.10.50) critical for recognizing specific peptide motifs and existing O-glycans on substrates, enabling sequential glycosylation; (4) Linker region connecting catalytic and lectin domains, potentially flexible to allow conformational sampling.
