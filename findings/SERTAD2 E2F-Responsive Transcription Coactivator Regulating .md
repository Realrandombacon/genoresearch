# SERTAD2: E2F-Responsive Transcription Coactivator Regulating Cell Cycle Progression, Adipogenesis, and DNA Damage Tolerance via Rad18/REV1 Axis

## Description
SERTAD2 (SERTA Domain Containing 2, also known as TRIP-Br2 or Sei-2, UniProt Q14140) is a 314-amino acid intrinsically disordered transcriptional coactivator that functions as a critical node in cell cycle regulation, metabolic differentiation, and DNA damage tolerance. The protein architecture is dominated by a conserved SERTA domain (IPR009263, PF06031, PS51053, aa 33-80) which mediates protein-protein interactions and defines the E2F-responsive SERTA domain-containing protein family (IPR052262, PTHR16277). The AlphaFold predicted structure shows medium confidence (pLDDT 59.8), indicating a partially structured protein with extensive intrinsically disordered regions flanking the core SERTA motif, a feature typical of transcriptional coactivators that require conformational flexibility to engage multiple partners. STRING interaction network places SERTAD2 within the cell cycle and transcription regulation machinery with medium-confidence associations to: SERTAD3 (0.644), SERTAD1 (0.582), and SERTAD4 (0.549), paralogous family members with redundant or cooperative functions; XPO1 (0.613), exportin-1 mediating nuclear-cytoplasmic transport; E2F1 (0.560), E2F transcription factor 1 driving G1/S transition; PPP2R2A (0.554), PPP2R2C (0.493), PPP2R1B (0.478), and PPP2CB (0.477), regulatory and catalytic subunits of Protein Phosphatase 2A (PP2A) suggesting regulation by dephosphorylation; and CDK4 (0.434), cyclin-dependent kinase 4 essential for Rb phosphorylation. HPA expression profiles demonstrate tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in bone marrow (80.9 nTPM), ubiquitous detection across all tissues, nucleoplasmic and cytosolic localization consistent with shuttling coactivator function, and classification under transcription and transcription regulation biological processes. ClinVar records document 13 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants consisting primarily of large copy number gains and losses on chromosome 2p (spanning 2p25.3-q37.3 regions), though no specific monogenic disease has been definitively assigned yet, suggesting haploinsufficiency or dosage sensitivity may contribute to developmental disorders or cancer susceptibility. Literature analysis (21 papers) reveals emerging functional roles: SERTAD2 acts downstream of REV1 (translesion synthesis polymerase) to promote lung tumorigenesis via the Rad18/SERTAD2 axis, linking DNA damage tolerance to oncogenic proliferation; serves as a target of miR-128-3p regulating 3T3-L1 adipogenesis and lipolysis by modulating Pparg expression; functions as a long noncoding RNA host gene (SERTAD2-3) where the lncRNA inhibits osteosarcoma proliferation and migration by sponging miR-29c; participates in a novel regulatory axis with mmu_circ_0012122/mmu-miR-1843-5p during rabies virus infection affecting neuronal survival; and is transcriptionally activated by E2F1 to drive cell cycle progression. We hypothesize that SERTAD2 functions through a multi-context mechanism: (1) In the cell cycle, E2F1-induced SERTAD2 recruits CDK4/Cyclin D complexes to E2F target promoters while simultaneously recruiting PP2A phosphatases to dephosphorylate specific substrates, creating a balanced phosphorylation state optimal for G1/S transition; (2) In adipogenesis, SERTAD2 coactivates Pparg transcription, and its downregulation by miR-128-3p blocks fat cell differentiation; (3) In DNA damage response, REV1-mediated ubiquitination recruits Rad18 which stabilizes SERTAD2, allowing it to promote translesion synthesis and tolerance of replication stress in cancer cells; (4) The intrinsically disordered nature enables rapid turnover and dynamic exchange at promoters, allowing fine-tuned responses to mitogenic and stress signals; (5) Paralogous interactions with SERTAD1/3/4 suggest formation of heteromeric complexes expanding regulatory repertoire; (6) Nuclear-cytoplasmic shuttling via XPO1 may regulate availability for transcriptional activation versus cytoplasmic signaling roles. Therapeutic targeting of the Rad18/SERTAD2 axis could sensitize lung tumors to DNA-damaging chemotherapy, while modulation of SERTAD2 levels might influence metabolic disorders or bone marrow malignancies.
