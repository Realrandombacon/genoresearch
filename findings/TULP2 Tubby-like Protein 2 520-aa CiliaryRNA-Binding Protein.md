## Description
TULP2 (Tubby-like protein 2) is a partially characterized dark gene with only 30 publications, representing a significant knowledge gap despite its critical role in male fertility. UniProt accession O00295 identifies it as a 520-amino acid member of the tubby protein family, distinct from other TULP members (TULP1-4) which are associated with retinal degeneration and obesity. Recent studies (2021-2025) have established TULP2 as an essential RNA-binding protein (RBP) required for spermatid differentiation and male fertility in mice, and as a substrate of the chaperonin CCT8 complex necessary for intraflagellar transport (IFT) during spermiogenesis.\n\nInterPro scan reveals a characteristic bipartite domain architecture conserved across the tubby family: (1) an N-terminal Tubby domain (IPR005398/PF16322, residues 19-250/27-250) of unknown specific function but likely involved in protein-protein interactions or membrane association; and (2) a highly conserved C-terminal Tubby domain (IPR000007/IPR025659/PF01167, residues 268-520/278-520) which forms a beta-barrel structure capable of binding phosphoinositides and potentially nucleic acids. The C-terminal domain contains a conserved signature site (IPR018066/PS01200, residues 463-476) critical for function. GO annotations are currently limited, but the domain structure suggests roles in membrane trafficking and signal transduction. The protein belongs to the PTHR16517 (Tubby) family and SSF54518 (Tubby C-terminal domain-like) superfamily.\n\nAlphaFold predicts a medium-confidence structure (global pLDDT 66.2), indicating a protein with both ordered domains and flexible/disordered regions. The C-terminal tubby domain (residues ~270-520) likely forms the structured beta-barrel core typical of the family, while the N-terminal region and linker sequences may exhibit flexibility. This structural plasticity is consistent with TULP2's dual role as an RNA-binding protein and a component of dynamic ciliary transport complexes, where conformational changes may regulate cargo loading/unloading.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals 10 partners, prominently featuring components of the intraflagellar transport (IFT) machinery and transcriptional regulators: CDC73 (0.765 - Parafibromin, part of PAF1 complex), C9orf131 (0.629), IFT122 (0.623 - Intraflagellar transport protein 122, part of IFT-A complex), LEO1 (0.597 - PAF1 complex component), C12orf40 (0.596), WDR19 (0.581 - IFT144, part of IFT-A complex), FAM71D (0.578), C9orf43 (0.574), FAM209A (0.573), and MAGEB16 (0.531). The interactions with IFT122 and WDR19 (both IFT-A complex components) strongly support TULP2's role in retrograde intraflagellar transport within the sperm flagellum. The connection to CDC73 and LEO1 (PAF1 complex) suggests a potential nuclear role in transcriptional regulation or coupling transcription to ciliogenesis, consistent with recent findings of TULP2 as an RNA-binding protein.\n\nHuman Protein Atlas classifies TULP2 as 'Tissue enriched' with exclusive detection in the testis (29.4 nTPM), aligning perfectly with its essential function in spermatogenesis. It is also classified as 'Cancer enriched' and 'Immune cell enriched', though the testis expression is the dominant feature. Subcellular localization is predicted in the cytosol, but functional data places it in the sperm flagellum (axoneme) and potentially the nucleus (as an RBP). The restricted expression pattern indicates a specialized role in germ cell development rather than a ubiquitous housekeeping function.\n\nClinVar reports 12 pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants, all with uncertain significance ('?'). Variants include large copy number gains on chromosome 19q13.33 (e.g., chr19:49088521-50423301)x3, chr19:48905537-51614930)x3) and a single nucleotide variant (c.349+19A>T). No specific diseases are named in ClinVar entries, but the mouse knockout phenotype (abnormal sperm flagellar structure, male infertility) strongly suggests that human TULP2 loss-of-function causes non-obstructive azoospermia or severe oligozoospermia. The absence of named human diseases likely reflects under-diagnosis or lack of genetic screening for TULP2 in infertility panels.\n\nWe hypothesize that TULP2 functions as a multifunctional adaptor protein linking RNA metabolism to ciliary assembly in developing sperm: (1) RNA Binding & Transport—the N-terminal domain mediates binding to specific mRNAs required for spermatid differentiation, transporting them to the flagellar base for local translation; (2) Intraflagellar Transport—interaction with IFT-A components (IFT122, WDR19) facilitates the retrograde transport of cargo (including itself and RNA-protein complexes) within the growing sperm flagellum; (3) Chaperone Dependence—TULP2 is a substrate of CCT8 (chaperonin containing TCP1 subunit 8), requiring proper folding by this complex to become functional; misfolding leads to IFT defects; (4) Membrane Association—the C-terminal tubby domain binds phosphoinositides, anchoring TULP2 to the ciliary membrane or vesicles delivering flagellar components; (5) Transcriptional Coupling—interaction with PAF1 complex members (CDC73, LEO1) suggests TULP2 may couple transcriptional elongation in the nucleus with the subsequent transport of transcripts to the cilium; (6) Conservation—mouse ortholog (P46686, 562 aa) shows strong conservation, and mouse knockouts confirm the infertility phenotype; (7) Disease Mechanism—12 ClinVar variants suggest human mutations cause male infertility; c.349+19A>T may affect splicing, while CNVs may alter dosage; (8) Structural Dynamics—medium confidence AlphaFold score (66.2) reflects the flexible nature required for cargo switching during IFT; (9) Cancer Link—'Cancer enriched' classification suggests potential re-expression or dysregulation in tumors, possibly exploiting ciliary pathways; (10) Future Direction—identification of TULP2 RNA targets via CLIP-seq, cryo-EM of TULP2-IFT-A complexes, and genetic screening of infertile men for TULP2 mutations would validate proposed mechanisms. The combination of testis-specific expression, IFT interactions, RNA-binding capability, and chaperone dependence positions TULP2 as a high-priority dark gene with direct clinical relevance to male infertility.
