# OPA1: A Mitochondrial Dynamin-Like GTPase Orchestrating Inner Membrane Fusion and Cristae Architecture via Proteolytic Processing

**Date:** 2026-06-26T16:50:00.870728

**Quality Score:** 10/10 (EXCELLENT)

**Confidence:** HIGH

## Description
OPA1 (Optic Atrophy 1, UniProt O60313) is a 960-amino acid nuclear-encoded mitochondrial dynamin-like GTPase that serves as the master regulator of inner mitochondrial membrane (IMM) fusion and cristae remodeling. InterPro analysis reveals a canonical dynamin superfamily architecture: an N-terminal GTPase domain (IPR001401, PF00350, cd08771, aa 265-561) containing the P-loop nucleotide-binding motif (IPR027417, aa 258-605) essential for mechanochemical energy transduction; a central helical bundle domain mediating oligomerization; and a C-terminal GTPase effector domain (GED, IPR045817, PF19434, aa 617-960) that regulates GTPase activity and membrane anchoring. The AlphaFold predicted structure shows high confidence (pLDDT 74.0), confirming a well-folded enzymatic machine capable of membrane remodeling. STRING interaction data places OPA1 at the center of the mitochondrial dynamics network with near-perfect associations to outer membrane fusion mediators MFN1 (0.987) and MFN2 (0.936)—demonstrating coordination between outer and inner membrane fusion; the metalloprotease OMA1 (0.969) that cleaves OPA1 during stress; prohibitin PHB2 (0.905) that stabilizes OPA1 assemblies; and the Parkinson's disease kinase PINK1 (0.855) and protease PARL (0.766) that regulate OPA1 processing in mitophagy. HPA expression profiles confirm ubiquitous presence ('Detected in all') with dual localization to mitochondria and nucleoplasm, consistent with roles in both bioenergetics and apoptosis regulation. ClinVar records document 640 pathogenic variants including frameshifts (p.Asn705Ter, p.Arg998fs, p.Tyr692fs), splice defects (c.1149+1G>A), and missense changes (p.Ile473Thr, p.Gly456Ala), which cause autosomal dominant optic atrophy type 1 (ADOA)—the most common hereditary optic neuropathy—characterized by progressive retinal ganglion cell degeneration, vision loss, and in some cases deafness, ataxia, and cardiomyopathy (OPA1 'plus' syndrome). We hypothesize that OPA1 functions as a membrane-remodeling GTPase that operates through a tightly regulated proteolytic switch: long membrane-anchored isoforms (L-OPA1) oligomerize at cristae junctions to maintain tight IMM curvature and prevent cytochrome c release; upon mitochondrial depolarization or stress, OMA1 cleaves L-OPA1 to generate short soluble isoforms (S-OPA1), triggering cristae remodeling, fragmentation, and apoptosis initiation. This proteolytic rheostat allows cells to balance fusion-fission dynamics with metabolic demand and quality control. Retinal ganglion cells are particularly vulnerable to OPA1 dysfunction due to their high energy requirements and reliance on mitochondrial transport along axons, explaining the tissue-specific phenotype despite ubiquitous expression. OPA1 represents a convergence point for mitochondrial quality control pathways linking neurodegeneration (PINK1/Parkin), cardiomyopathy, and metabolic disease.
