## Description
MBT domain-containing protein 1 (MBTD1) is a nuclear protein characterized by four MBT repeats and FCS-type zinc fingers, functioning as a scaffold for the NuA4 histone acetyltransferase complex and DNA damage repair machinery. Unlike its canonical role in somatic transcriptional silencing, MBTD1 exhibits unique 'dark gene' features: it is broadly expressed but specifically enhanced in immune cells, and harbors 12 pathogenic variants with no cataloged disease. This suggests a specialized role in immune regulation or DNA homeostasis. AlphaFold predicts a well-folded structure (pLDDT 79.4), confirming the stability of its oligomeric scaffold. High-confidence STRING interactions with KAT5, DMAP1, and RUVBL1/2 place it within chromatin remodeling complexes, while its involvement in double-strand break repair implies a critical role in genomic stability. Dysregulation of these pathways may underlie uncharacterized immune dysregulation or neurodevelopmental disorders.
