# SPOUT1: 28S rRNA Methyltransferase Linking Ribosomal Function to Neurodevelopment via Mitotic Spindle Organization

## Description
SPOUT1 (Q5T280) encodes a 376 amino acid S-adenosyl-L-methionine-dependent RNA methyltransferase that specifically catalyzes N3-uridine methylation on 28S rRNA, a critical modification for ribosomal function. InterPro analysis reveals the canonical SPOUT superfamily methyltransferase domain (cd18086, positions 74-364) with an OB-fold nucleic acid-binding region (IPR012340, positions 178-261) and alpha/beta knot methyltransferase architecture (IPR029028). AlphaFold predicts a well-folded structure (pLDDT 88.4) consistent with stable enzymatic activity. STRING interactions identify high-confidence partners including TBC1D13 (0.935), KYAT1 (0.929), IMP4 (0.892), and ENDOG (0.886), suggesting roles in ribosome biogenesis and RNA processing complexes. HPA shows ubiquitous expression across all tissues with enhanced expression in specific cell types, classified as an essential protein involved in cell cycle, mitosis, and rRNA processing. ClinVar contains 46 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants associated with autosomal-recessive developmental and epileptic encephalopathy (DEE) and the neurodevelopmental disorder SpADMiSS. Recent literature (2024-2025) demonstrates SPOUT1/CENP-32 links mitotic spindle organization with centrosome attachment, and depletion causes centrosome detachment from the spindle. The mechanistic hypothesis: SPOUT1 methylates 28S rRNA to ensure proper ribosome assembly and function, while simultaneously localizing to mitotic chromosomes where it stabilizes centrosome-spindle connections; loss-of-function variants disrupt both ribosomal biogenesis and mitotic fidelity, leading to neurodevelopmental defects through combined proteostasis and cell division failures in developing neural tissue.
