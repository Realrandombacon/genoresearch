# ZNF408: PR-SET Domain Transcriptional Regulator with Retinal Disease Association

**Date:** 2026-03-23T04:45:45.637262

**Quality Score:** 2.55/10  (E=4.8, D=0.3) [WEAK]

## Description
ZNF408 encodes a 720 amino acid zinc finger protein containing an N-terminal PR-SET domain (residues 53-176, PF21549, IPR001214, IPR044412) and ten C2H2-type zinc finger motifs (PF00096, IPR013087) clustered in the C-terminal region (residues 353-629). The PR-SET domain belongs to the SET domain superfamily (G3DSA:2.170.270.10, IPR046341) which typically catalyzes histone lysine methylation, suggesting chromatin modification activity. The tandem zinc fingers provide sequence-specific DNA binding capability. HPA data shows ubiquitous expression across all tissues with low tissue specificity, localized to centriolar satellites and cytosol. ClinVar documents 46 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including nonsense mutations (p.Glu92Ter, p.Gln202Ter), missense changes in zinc finger regions (p.Cys265Phe, p.Cys316Trp, p.Asp440Asn, p.Gln507His, p.Arg407Trp), and frameshifts (p.Glu666fs). These variants cause familial exudative vitreoretinopathy (FEVR/EVR6) and retinitis pigmentosa (RP72), indicating critical role in retinal development and maintenance. The concentration of pathogenic variants in zinc finger domains suggests disruption of DNA binding specificity rather than catalytic SET domain function drives retinal pathology. ZNF408 likely functions as a sequence-specific transcriptional repressor/activator that methylates histone marks at retinal development gene promoters, with disease arising from loss of target gene regulation in photoreceptor progenitors.
