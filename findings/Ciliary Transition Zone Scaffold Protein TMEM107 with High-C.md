# Ciliary Transition Zone Scaffold Protein TMEM107 with High-Confidence Ciliopathy Interaction Network

## Description
TMEM107 encodes a 140 amino acid transmembrane protein (UniProt Q6UX40) located at chromosome 17p13.1 that functions as a core component of the primary cilia transition zone (TZ), the diffusion barrier separating ciliary and plasma membrane compartments. InterPro analysis identifies TMEM107 as a member of the transmembrane protein 107 family (IPR029248, residues 1-140) with a conserved transmembrane domain (PF14995, residues 7-128). AlphaFold predicts an exceptionally well-folded structure with global pLDDT of 94.2 (very high confidence), indicating stable tertiary structure consistent with its role as a structural scaffold protein. STRING interactions reveal a remarkably cohesive network of 10 high-confidence partners all involved in ciliogenesis and transition zone assembly: TMEM216 (score 0.966), TMEM231 (0.963), B9D1 (0.953), B9D2 (0.936), CC2D2A (0.929), TMEM17 (0.924), CEP290 (0.916), TCTN1 (0.914), TCTN2 (0.912), and TMEM67 (0.896). These proteins collectively form the MKS (Meckel syndrome) and NPHP (nephronophthisis) protein complexes that establish the ciliary gate. HPA data shows ubiquitous expression with highest levels in choroid plexus (53.5 nTPM), consistent with cilia-rich tissues, and membrane/cytoplasmic localization. ClinVar contains 88 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including nonsense mutations (p.Ser73Ter), deletions, and copy number variations spanning 17p13.1. Mutations cause multiple ciliopathy phenotypes including Meckel syndrome type 13 (MKS13), Joubert syndrome type 29 (JBTS29), and oral-facial-digital syndrome. Functionally, TMEM107 acts as a transmembrane scaffold that anchors the Y-shaped linkers of the transition zone, regulating ciliary protein composition by controlling bidirectional trafficking through the ciliary gate. Loss of TMEM107 disrupts sonic hedgehog signaling and causes defective ciliogenesis with reduced cilia numbers and altered cilia length.
