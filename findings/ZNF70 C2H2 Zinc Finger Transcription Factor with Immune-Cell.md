## Description
ZNF70 (zinc finger protein 70) encodes a 446 amino acid C2H2-type zinc finger transcription factor (UniProt Q9UC06) with low tissue specificity and immune cell-enhanced expression (HPA: detected in many tissues, cytosol/nuclear localization). The protein contains three canonical C2H2 zinc finger DNA-binding motifs (InterPro IPR013087, PF00096 at positions 140-162, 168-190, 196-218) that confer sequence-specific DNA recognition, plus additional zinc finger double domains (PF13465 at 238-263, 294-319) characteristic of the Krüppel C2H2-type zinc-finger family (PTHR24393). ClinVar documents 65 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly copy number gains and losses spanning 22q11.21-11.23 (DiGeorge/velocardiofacial syndrome region), indicating extreme dosage sensitivity. The chromosomal location within the 22q11.23 deletion syndrome critical region suggests ZNF70 haploinsufficiency contributes to neurodevelopmental and immunological phenotypes. As a C2H2 zinc finger protein, ZNF70 likely recognizes GC-rich promoter/enhancer motifs and recruits chromatin remodeling complexes to regulate target gene transcription. The immune cell-enhanced expression pattern implicates ZNF70 in lymphocyte differentiation or activation pathways. Mechanistically, ZNF70 functions as a sequence-specific DNA-binding scaffold that tethers transcriptional co-regulators to genomic loci, maintaining transcriptional homeostasis in hematopoietic lineages. The high burden of pathogenic CNVs at 22q11.23 suggests ZNF70 dosage is critical for normal development, with both haploinsufficiency and triplosensitivity causing disease.
