## Description
SERINC1 (Serine Incorporator 1) is a 453-amino acid multi-pass transmembrane protein functioning in serine-derived lipid synthesis and phospholipid biosynthesis, with emerging roles in intracellular vesicle trafficking. InterPro analysis confirms membership in the Serinc family with domain IPR005016 (Serine incorporator/TMS membrane protein, positions 16-452), PF03348 (Serine incorporator domain, 16-450), and PTHR10383 family classification, indicating an integral membrane transporter architecture. STRING interactions reveal connections to the AP-4 vesicle trafficking machinery: AP4E1 (0.521), AP4B1 (0.500), ATG9A (0.484), RUSC1 (0.594), RUSC2 (0.599), plus VAPA (0.583) for ER-membrane contact sites, CNOT6L (0.674) for mRNA regulation, PJA2 (0.659) for ubiquitination, and SLC1A5 (0.538) for amino acid transport—suggesting coordination between lipid metabolism and autophagy-related vesicle trafficking. HPA data shows ubiquitous expression across all tissues with low tissue specificity, cytosolic localization, and classification as a plasma protein involved in lipid/phospholipid biosynthesis and metabolism pathways. ClinVar contains 21 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number losses and gains on chromosome 6q21-23.2, though specific monogenic disease phenotypes remain under characterization; the variant burden suggests clinical relevance potentially in neurodevelopmental or metabolic disorders. AlphaFold structure (AF-Q9NRX5-F1) predicts high confidence (pLDDT 77.5) with multiple transmembrane helices consistent with transporter function. With only 23 papers, SERINC1 represents a genuine 'dark' gene: while its paralog SERINC5 is well-known as an HIV-1 restriction factor, SERINC1's specific physiological substrates, regulatory mechanisms, and contribution to human disease remain largely unexplored despite clear involvement in lipid metabolism and vesicle trafficking pathways.
