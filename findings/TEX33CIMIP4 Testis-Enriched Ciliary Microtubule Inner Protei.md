## Description
TEX33 (Testis Expressed 33, also CIMIP4, Ciliary Microtubule Inner Protein 4, C22orf33, EAN57) is a 280 amino acid intracellular protein (UniProt O43247) with a partially structured conformation (AlphaFold pLDDT 59.8, medium confidence), localized to the sperm flagellum where it functions as a structural component of the ciliary microtubule inner apparatus essential for sperm motility.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals TEX33 defines the Ciliary Microtubule Inner Protein 4 family (IPR029234, residues 1-280) and the Testis-Expressed Sequence 33 Protein family (PF15400/PTHR31702, residues 140-276/1-280). The medium-confidence AlphaFold structure suggests a mix of ordered domains and flexible/disordered regions, characteristic of proteins that undergo conformational changes during axonemal assembly or interact with multiple binding partners through intrinsically disordered segments.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals TEX33 functions within a testis-specific protein network: strongest interactions with MORN5 (0.519, membrane occupation and recognition nexus repeat-containing protein 5, lipid binding), SCP2D1 (0.518, SCP2 domain-containing protein 1, sterol carrier), C20orf173 (0.513, chromosome 20 open reading frame 173), CCER1 (0.507, coiled-coil glutamate-rich protein 1), CXorf65 (0.507, chromosome X open reading frame 65), FAM71E1 (0.507, family with sequence similarity 71 member E1), CCDC187 (0.506, coiled-coil domain-containing protein 187), TMCO2 (0.505, transmembrane and coiled-coil domain-containing protein 2), FAM71F1 (0.434), and TMCO5A (0.414). These partners are predominantly testis-enriched coiled-coil proteins, suggesting TEX33 assembles into a multi-protein complex within the sperm flagellar axoneme or accessory structures.\n\nHPA expression shows testis-enriched specificity (79.6 nTPM) with detection in a single tissue and group-enriched single cell specificity, consistent with a specialized spermatogenic function. The protein is classified as predicted intracellular.\n\nClinVar documents 0 pathogenic variants, indicating no established disease associations, though this may reflect incomplete phenotyping of male infertility cases rather than true benign status.\n\nConservation is evident from mouse ortholog (Q9D9J2, 266 aa), indicating ancient evolutionary conservation of flagellar assembly mechanisms.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: TEX33 acts as a structural component of the sperm flagellar axoneme, specifically integrating into the microtubule inner protein (MIP) complex that stabilizes doublet microtubules and regulates dynein motor activity. The MORN5 interaction suggests lipid-binding capacity, potentially anchoring TEX33 to flagellar membranes or lipid-rich accessory structures. SCP2D1 partnership indicates sterol metabolism integration, possibly for membrane fluidity regulation during sperm capacitation. Coiled-coil partners (CCER1, CCDC187, TMCO2, TMCO5A) suggest TEX33 forms elongated structural scaffolds within the axonemal lumen or connects outer doublets to the central pair apparatus. FAM71 family interactions link TEX33 to other testis-specific proteins implicated in male infertility. The medium-confidence structure (pLDDT 59.8) suggests conformational flexibility enabling adaptation to different axonemal positions or assembly stages. During spermiogenesis, TEX33 incorporates into the growing flagellum, stabilizing microtubule doublets against shear forces during sperm propulsion. Loss-of-function would cause asthenozoospermia (reduced sperm motility) through axonemal instability or dynein dysregulation. The absence of ClinVar variants suggests either recessive inheritance, phenotypic heterogeneity, or underdiagnosis of TEX33-related male infertility.
