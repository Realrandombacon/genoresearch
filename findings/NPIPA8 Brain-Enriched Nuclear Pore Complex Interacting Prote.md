## Description
NPIPA8 (Nuclear pore complex-interacting protein family member A8, UniProt P0DM63) is a 369-amino acid intracellular protein located on chromosome 16p13.11 within a genomic region prone to copy-number variations. InterPro analysis reveals membership in the nuclear pore complex interacting protein family (IPR009443/PTHR15438, aa 9-364) with an N-terminal NPIP domain (IPR054697/PF06409, aa 22-243), defining its role in nucleocytoplasmic transport machinery. AlphaFold structure prediction achieves medium confidence (pLDDT 51.1), indicating a partially structured protein with both ordered domains and intrinsically disordered regions — characteristic of nuclear pore-associated proteins that require flexibility for dynamic interactions with transport receptors and nucleoporins. Human Protein Atlas shows tissue-enhanced expression with strong brain enrichment (96.1 nTPM), detected in many tissues, nucleoplasmic subcellular localization, and cell type enhanced specificity — suggesting critical roles in neuronal nuclear transport and potentially neurodevelopment. STRING interaction network reveals 6 partners spanning nuclear organization and cytoskeletal dynamics: PNMA6A (0.566, paraneoplastic antigen family member), ECT2L (0.507, epithelial cell transforming protein-like, RhoGEF involved in cytokinesis), CD300LD (0.464, immune receptor), ALG1L2 (0.423, dolichyl-phosphate mannosyltransferase), and DNAH10 (0.415, dynein heavy chain involved in microtubule-based transport). This interactome positions NPIPA8 at the interface of nuclear pore function, cytoskeletal organization, and intracellular trafficking. ClinVar contains 11 pathogenic variants including massive copy-number gains across 16p13.11-12.1, 16p13.11-12.3, 16p13.3-11.2 and losses/duplications, demonstrating extreme dosage sensitivity where genomic rearrangements disrupting this 16p13 hotspot cause neurodevelopmental disorders. Conservation in primates (chimpanzee isoforms ranging 128-389 aa) indicates evolutionary origin tied to nuclear transport regulation. NPIPA8 likely functions as a flexible adaptor protein at the nuclear pore complex, facilitating nucleocytoplasmic transport of specific cargoes through transient interactions with nucleoporins and transport receptors, with particular importance in neuronal cells where high nuclear transport demand supports synaptic plasticity and gene expression programs.
