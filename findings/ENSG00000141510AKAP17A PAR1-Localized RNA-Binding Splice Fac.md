## Description

Recent literature (2024) identifies AKAP17A as a regulator of PRMT5 inhibitor sensitivity in MTAP-deleted cancers, and earlier work (2019) links AKAP17A transcript levels to aging phenotypes in human peripheral blood as a splicing factor. Based on domain architecture (RRM RNA-binding domain), interaction partners (spliceosomal SRRM2, PAR1 network), medium-confidence AlphaFold structure with disordered regions characteristic of splicing factors, and emerging cancer/aging literature, AKAP17A functions as an RNA-binding splice factor that regulates alternative splicing decisions through PKA-dependent phosphorylation mechanisms. The RRM domain likely recognizes specific cis-elements in pre-mRNA substrates, while intrinsically disordered regions enable dynamic interactions with spliceosomal components. PKA anchoring may localize the splicing machinery to specific subcellular compartments or couple splicing regulation to cAMP signaling pathways. The PAR1 location suggests dosage-sensitive functions affecting both sexes equally, potentially explaining pathogenic copy number variants. AKAP17A may coordinate splicing programs during cellular stress responses, cancer progression, and aging through regulation of splice site selection in target transcripts.
