# GIMAP5: AIG1-Type GTPase Regulating T-Cell Survival via Ceramide Homeostasis and GSK3A Sequestration

## Description
GIMAP5 is a 307 aa anti-apoptotic GTPase belonging to the immuno-associated nucleotide (IAN) family, containing a canonical AIG1-type G domain (IPR006703/PF04548, aa25-230) within the P-loop NTPase superfamily (IPR027417). AlphaFold predicts a highly confident structure (pLDDT 89.4), indicating a stable globular fold typical of regulatory GTPases. HPA shows strong lymphoid tissue enrichment (99.5 nTPM) with cancer-enhanced expression, consistent with its role in immune cell homeostasis. STRING reveals high-confidence interactions with paralogs GIMAP4 (0.898), GIMAP6 (0.829), GIMAP1 (0.710), and the GIMAP1-5 cluster (0.802), plus key apoptosis regulators BCL2 (0.733) and BCL2L1/Bcl-xL (0.613), and mitochondrial protein CISD2 (0.642). This places GIMAP5 in a specialized survival complex preventing lymphocyte death. Mechanistically, GIMAP5 functions via two distinct pathways: (1) it inhibits GSK3A by sequestering it in cytoplasmic vesicles, preventing pro-apoptotic signaling; and (2) recent 2024 studies identify it as part of the MFSD1-GLMP-GIMAP5 complex essential for lysosomal ceramide export—deficiency leads to toxic ceramide accumulation, driving immune senescence and cell death. ClinVar contains 77 pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number losses/gains (deletions/duplications) spanning 7q34-q36.3 that disrupt the entire GIMAP cluster, associated with severe combined immunodeficiency (SCID), T-cell lymphopenia, and autoimmune disorders like systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE). The 2025 literature highlights its role in pancreatic islet infiltration in diabetes-prone rats and novel mutations causing porto-sinusoidal vascular disorder. With only 104 publications, GIMAP5 remains an incompletely characterized checkpoint linking lysosomal lipid metabolism to adaptive immunity.
