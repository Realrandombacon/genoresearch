# METTL15: High-Confidence Mitochondrial 12S rRNA N4-Cytidine Methyltransferase Essential for Mitoribosome Assembly and Function via MRPS Protein Network and SAM-Dependent Catalytic Mechanism

**Date:** 2026-04-06T15:52:18.118482

**Quality Score:** 3.6/10  (E=6.1, D=1.1) [MODERATE]

## Description
METTL15 (Methyltransferase 15, alias METT5D1) is a 407 amino acid mitochondrial methyltransferase (UniProt A6NJ78) with a high-confidence folded structure (AlphaFold pLDDT 80.7) containing MraW-family S-adenosylmethionine (SAM)-dependent methyltransferase domains, functioning as the dedicated N4-cytidine methyltransferase for mitochondrial 12S rRNA that catalyzes site-specific rRNA base methylation essential for mitoribosome small subunit assembly, mitochondrial translation, and oxidative phosphorylation through interactions with mitochondrial ribosomal proteins and assembly factors.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals METTL15 contains canonical MraW-family methyltransferase architecture: a ribosomal RNA small subunit methyltransferase H domain (IPR002903/MF_01007, residues 38-407) with MraW methylase family signature (PF01795, residues 71-405). The protein belongs to the SAM-dependent methyltransferase superfamily (IPR029063/SSF53335, residues 72-407) with VP39-like catalytic fold (G3DSA:3.40.50.150, residues 72-179/295-353/375-404) and insert domain (G3DSA:1.10.150.170/SSF81799/ITR023397, residues 180-294) for substrate recognition. GO annotations include methyltransferase activity (GO:0008168). The high-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 80.7) confirms a stable bilobal architecture with SAM-binding pocket and rRNA recognition surface optimized for site-specific cytidine methylation.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places METTL15 at the center of the mitochondrial ribosome assembly network: strongest interactions with DAP3 (0.930, death-associated protein 3, mitochondrial ribosomal small subunit component), MRPS6 (0.885, mitochondrial ribosomal protein S6), MRPS7 (0.883), MRPS2 (0.882), MRPS12 (0.881), MRPS11 (0.878), MRPS9 (0.837), MRPS5 (0.817), RBFA (0.815, ribosome-binding factor A, ribosome assembly factor), and MRPS10 (0.813). This dense network of high-confidence interactions (all >0.81) confirms METTL15 as an integral component of the 28S mitoribosomal small subunit. DAP3 partnership (0.930) links METTL15 to the ribosomal platform. MRPS protein interactions indicate direct association with the maturing small subunit. RBFA interaction (0.815) positions METTL15 in the ribosome assembly pathway.\n\nHPA expression shows ubiquitous housekeeping pattern with low tissue specificity and detection in all tissues, consistent with essential mitochondrial translation function across all cell types. Classification as essential protein confirms critical cellular role. Subcellular localization in mitochondrial matrix matches 12S rRNA substrate location. Molecular function annotations confirm methyltransferase and transferase activities.\n\nClinVar documents 22 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large chromosome 11p copy number losses and gains (11p14.3-13, 11p14.3-12, 11p15.1-12, 11p14.1-13, 11p15.3-13, 11p15.5-13) and specific variants (c.681G>T p.Gly227=, c.270+216A>G). The high CNV burden indicates extreme dosage sensitivity, where haploinsufficiency causes mitochondrial disease through disrupted 12S rRNA methylation, impaired mitoribosome assembly, and defective oxidative phosphorylation.\n\nConservation is strong across mammals: human (A6NJ78, 407 aa), mouse (Q9DCL4, 406 aa), bovine (A0JN95, 407 aa), orangutan (Q5R5T5, 407 aa), indicating evolutionary conservation of mitochondrial rRNA methylation functions.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: METTL15 functions as the dedicated N4-cytidine methyltransferase for mitochondrial 12S rRNA, catalyzing site-specific methylation essential for small subunit function. The MraW catalytic domain (residues 71-405) binds SAM cofactor in the VP39-like fold (G3DSA:3.40.50.150), positioning the methyl donor for nucleophilic attack on cytidine N4. The insert domain (residues 180-294) recognizes specific 12S rRNA sequences, ensuring site specificity. DAP3 partnership (0.930) anchors METTL15 to the ribosomal platform during assembly. MRPS protein interactions (MRPS6 0.885, MRPS7 0.883, MRPS2 0.882) indicate METTL15 modifies 12S rRNA co-transcriptionally or during early assembly. RBFA interaction (0.815) suggests coordination with ribosome maturation factors. The stable structure (pLDDT 80.7) enables precise catalytic geometry. Ubiquitous expression reflects universal requirement for mitochondrial translation. 11p CNVs cause METTL15 haploinsufficiency, leading to hypomethylated 12S rRNA, defective small subunit assembly, impaired mitochondrial protein synthesis, and respiratory chain deficiency. METTL15 represents a critical epitranscriptomic regulator linking rRNA methylation to mitochondrial gene expression and cellular energy production.
