## Description
ANAPC4 encodes an 808 aa nucleoplasmic scaffold protein (UniProt Q9UJX5) that functions as an essential structural core subunit of the anaphase-promoting complex/cyclosome (APC/C), a multi-subunit RING-type E3 ubiquitin ligase that drives metaphase-anaphase transition by ubiquitinating securin and mitotic cyclins for proteasomal degradation. This partially characterized gene (24 publications) has emerging clinical significance for papillary thyroid cancer, cattle puberty timing, and cell cycle disorders. The protein features extensive WD40 repeat architecture enabling protein-protein interactions: N-terminal WD40 domain (IPR024977/IPR036322/G3DSA:2.130.10.10, residues 27-117/20-220/12-127) forming beta-propeller scaffold; long central domain (IPR024790, residues 233-431) unique to APC4; C-terminal half WD40 domain (IPR056358, residues 575-745) completing the multi-propeller structure; classified within metazoan APC4 family (IPR017169/IPR024789, residues 6-804/2-766) and WD40/YVTN repeat-like superfamily (IPR015943, residues 12-127) with GO terms for regulation of mitotic metaphase/anaphase transition, APC/C-dependent catabolic process, and protein binding. STRING interactions reveal ANAPC4 operates within extremely high-confidence APC/C and cell cycle networks: NEK2 (0.999), NIMA-related kinase 2 regulating centrosome separation; CDC16 (0.989), cell division cycle 16 (APC3) core APC/C subunit; ANAPC2 (0.985)/ANAPC1 (0.971), catalytic RING subunit and largest scaffold; BUB1B (0.964), budding uninhibited by benzimidazles 1 beta (BubR1) spindle checkpoint kinase; ANAPC5 (0.950), APC5 core subunit; CCNB1 (0.797), cyclin B1 (primary substrate); ANAPC11 (0.670), small catalytic subunit; MAD2L1 (0.669), mitotic arrest deficient 2-like 1 checkpoint protein; FBXO5 (0.578), F-box protein 5/Emi1 APC/C inhibitor. HPA confirms low tissue specificity (detected in all tissues), low cancer specificity, low immune cell specificity, group enriched cell type specificity, classified as essential intracellular protein involved in cell cycle, cell division, mitosis, and Ubl conjugation pathway. ClinVar lists 34 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including intronic variants near exon boundaries (c.1624-121A>G, c.130-271G>T, c.-10-13G>T affecting regulatory regions), large-scale copy number gains across 4p16.3-11 region (4p16.3-11 0.07-49.1Mb x3, 4p16.3-q12 0.09-57.1Mb x3, 4p16.3-13 2.9-43.0Mb x3, 4p16.3-11 0.00-49.1Mb x3, 4p16.3-15.1 0.07-34.5Mb x3), copy number loss (4p15.33-14 11.4-38.1Mb), demonstrating strong dosage sensitivity though specific monogenic diseases remain undefined beyond cancer susceptibility and developmental phenotypes. AlphaFold structure prediction yields high confidence (global pLDDT 80.9), revealing a well-folded multi-WD40 propeller architecture suitable for scaffolding multiple APC/C subunits and coactivators. Mechanistically, ANAPC4 operates through integrated modes: (1) APC/C scaffold—WD40 domains form rigid platform organizing catalytic core (ANAPC2-RBX1) and substrate recognition modules; (2) Coactivator binding—WD40 propellers bind CDC20 and CDH1 activators positioning substrates for ubiquitination; (3) Substrate recruitment—interacts with CCNB1 (cyclin B1) and securin enabling their K11-linked polyubiquitination; (4) Spindle checkpoint integration—BUB1B/MAD2L1 interactions link APC4 to checkpoint control preventing premature anaphase; (5) Thyroid cancer regulation—2023 study (5 citations) demonstrates TRMT13 RNA methylase inhibits papillary thyroid cancer growth by targeting ANAPC4, suggesting tumor suppressor role; (6) Puberty timing—2026 genome-wide scan identifies ANAPC4 as candidate modulator of Chinese Holstein heifer puberty timing, linking cell cycle to developmental timing; (7) Centrosome regulation—NEK2 interaction coordinates APC/C activity with centrosome separation; (8) Emi1 inhibition—FBXO5 binding enables cell cycle checkpoint control. Loss-of-function causes defective cyclin B1 degradation, metaphase arrest, aneuploidy, genomic instability, while overexpression may drive uncontrolled cell cycle progression and tumorigenesis.
