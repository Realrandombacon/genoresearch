## Description
MLF2 (Myeloid Leukemia Factor 2, Q15773) encodes a 248 aa nucleoplasmic protein functioning as a phase separation modulator at the nuclear envelope that coordinates p53 stability through USP7 deubiquitinase interaction and participates in nuclear pore complex assembly via Torsin ATPase-dependent mechanisms. Structural architecture reveals Myeloid leukemia factor domain (IPR019376/PF10248/PTHR13105, residues 25-244/25-199/82-244) forming the core functional region; AlphaFold prediction (pLDDT 65.1) indicates medium confidence partially structured protein with ordered domains interspersed with intrinsically disordered regions consistent with phase separation behavior and dynamic protein-protein interaction capacity. STRING interactions define chaperone-mediated protein quality control and nuclear envelope networks: HSP40 co-chaperone DNAJB6 (0.899) preventing protein aggregation, ARF GTPase activating protein ARFGAP2 (0.750) regulating vesicular trafficking, secretagogin calcium-binding protein SCGN (0.726), rootletin filament protein CROCC (0.705) for centrosome cohesion, HSP40 co-chaperones DNAJB1 (0.694)/DNAJB4 (0.691), E3 ubiquitin ligase STUB1/CHIP (0.677) linking chaperones to ubiquitin-proteasome system, calcium-dependent exocytosis protein DOC2A (0.649), and BAG family co-chaperones BAG3 (0.628)/BAG5 (0.610) regulating HSP70 activity. HPA localizes MLF2 to nucleoplasm/plasma membrane/cytosol as intracellular protein with ubiquitous expression detected in all tissues and cell type-enhanced specificity. ClinVar documents 48 pathogenic variants including extensive 12p13.33-11.1 copy number gains (chr12:64621-34650483, chr12:82453-49847230, chr12:173787-34835837 duplications/triplications) and single allele duplications. Literature (44 papers) demonstrates emerging disease mechanisms: MLF2 negatively regulates p53 tumor suppressor by promoting USP7-mediated deubiquitination driving colorectal carcinogenesis (12x citations, 2023), Torsin ATPase deficiency causes nuclear pore biogenesis defects with MLF2 sequestration in nuclear envelope blebs providing mechanistic insight into DYT1 dystonia etiology (64x citations, 2020), MLF2 modulates phase-separated nuclear envelope condensates provoking dual proteotoxicity in dystonia pathogenesis (1x citation, 2021). Mechanistic hypothesis: MLF2 contains intrinsically disordered regions enabling liquid-liquid phase separation at nuclear envelope; under normal conditions, MLF2 transiently associates with nuclear pore complexes facilitating proper assembly through Torsin AAA+ ATPase activity; MLF2 binds USP7 deubiquitinase stabilizing p53 negative regulators promoting cell proliferation in cancer; in DYT1 dystonia, mutant TorsinA fails to properly assemble nuclear pores causing MLF2 accumulation in nuclear envelope blebs where it forms pathological condensates; chaperone network (DNAJB6/DNAJB1/BAG3/STUB1) attempts to refold or degrade aggregated MLF2 but becomes overwhelmed leading to proteotoxicity; 12p CNVs cause dosage effects altering nuclear envelope homeostasis; partial disorder (pLDDT 65.1) enables conformational flexibility required for phase separation dynamics.
