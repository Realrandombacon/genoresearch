## Description
TNS2 (Tensin-2, UniProt Q63HR2) is a 1409 aa cytoskeletal protein belonging to the Tensin family (IPR006020/IPR013625/IPR014020) that functions as a focal adhesion scaffold linking integrin-mediated cell-matrix adhesion to intracellular signaling and cytoskeletal organization. The molecular architecture consists of an N-terminal C1 domain (IPR002219/cd20887, positions 30-83/31-80) for phorbol ester/diacylglycerol binding and membrane association, a protein tyrosine phosphatase-like domain (cd14562/G3DSA:3.90.190.10, positions 132-290/130-294) for potential catalytic activity, a C2 domain (IPR014020/G3DSA:2.60.40.1110, positions 295-415/297-425) for calcium-dependent phospholipid binding, a central SH2 domain (IPR000980/cd09927/G3DSA:3.30.505.10, positions 1136-1249/1136-1245/1138-1247) for phosphotyrosine recognition, and a C-terminal PTB domain (IPR006020/IPR013625/cd01213/G3DSA:2.30.29.30, positions 1261-1409/1271-1409/1273-1405/1275-1409) for integrin cytoplasmic tail binding. Literature (45 papers) establishes TNS2 roles in: (1) adult-onset nephrotic syndrome due to homozygous TNS2 truncating variant broadening mutational spectrum (2025), (2) automated scoring of glomerular injury in TNS2-deficient nephropathy (2024), (3) TNS1 and TNS4 play potential role in pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma development but not TNS2 and TNS3 (2026), (4) TNS2 gene polymorphism association with productivity traits in Rainbow Trout (2025). HPA confirms low tissue specificity with detection in many tissues, predicted intracellular protein classification, hydrolase and protein phosphatase molecular functions, and cell type enhanced expression. ClinVar contains 32 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including missense mutations (c.2579C>T p.Pro860Leu, c.1455G>C p.Gln485His, c.1645G>A p.Gly549Arg, c.2539G>A p.Glu847Lys, c.2273C>G p.Pro758Arg, c.2495C>T p.Ser832Phe, c.490G>A p.Val164Met, c.4058G>A p.Arg1353Gln) and splice variants (c.3667+5G>A, c.1095+46G>A), causing autosomal recessive nephrotic syndrome with focal segmental glomerulosclerosis. AlphaFold shows medium-confidence structure (pLDDT 58.3) indicating partially structured protein with ordered domains (PTB, SH2, C2) and intrinsically disordered linker regions typical of scaffold proteins. Conservation: mouse Tns2 (Q8CGB6) is 1400 aa - highly conserved across mammals. Functional hypothesis: TNS2 functions as a multi-domain scaffold at focal adhesions that integrates integrin signaling, cytoskeletal dynamics, and phosphotyrosine signaling to maintain podocyte foot process architecture and glomerular filtration barrier integrity. The mechanistic model proposes: (1) C-terminal PTB domain binds beta-integrin cytoplasmic tails anchoring TNS2 to focal adhesions, (2) SH2 domain recruits phosphotyrosine-containing signaling proteins (SRC, AXL) for adhesion-dependent signaling cascades, (3) C1 domain mediates membrane association through diacylglycerol binding, (4) C2 domain provides calcium-dependent phospholipid binding for membrane recruitment, (5) Phosphatase-like domain may regulate focal adhesion turnover through dephosphorylation of adhesion components, (6) TNS2-TLN1/TLN2-VCL-PXN network stabilizes integrin-cytoskeleton linkage in podocytes, (7) TNS2-AXL-SRC signaling regulates podocyte survival and stress response. In nephrotic syndrome, homozygous loss-of-function variants disrupt focal adhesion stability in podocytes, causing foot process effacement, proteinuria, and progressive glomerulosclerosis. The rare disease phenotype (only 5 documented cases) suggests TNS2 is essential but functionally redundant with other tensin family members (TNS1, TNS3, TNS4) in most tissues. TNS2 represents an understudied focal adhesion scaffold linking cell-matrix adhesion to kidney disease.
