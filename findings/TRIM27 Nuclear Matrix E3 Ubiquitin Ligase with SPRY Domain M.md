# TRIM27: Nuclear Matrix E3 Ubiquitin Ligase with SPRY Domain Mediating PTEN Degradation and Germ Cell Differentiation

## Description
TRIM27 (also known as RFP or RNF76) encodes a 513 amino acid nuclear matrix-localized E3 ubiquitin-protein ligase belonging to the tripartite motif (TRIM) family (UniProt: P14373). The protein architecture features a canonical TRIM motif comprising a N-terminal RING domain (aa 16-57, IPR001841) conferring E3 ligase activity, followed by two B-box zinc fingers (B-box type 2 at aa 91-132, IPR000315) and a coiled-coil region facilitating oligomerization. The C-terminal region contains a PRY/SPRY domain (aa 315-491, IPR001870, IPR003877) which serves as a protein-protein interaction interface for substrate recognition.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals high-confidence associations with MAGEL2 (0.979), PTEN (0.963), CHD4 (0.903), UBE2D1 (0.831), and IKBKE (0.812). The robust interaction with PTEN suggests TRIM27 functions as a negative regulator of the PI3K/AKT pathway by promoting PTEN ubiquitination and degradation. Interaction with CHD4 (chromatin remodeling complex) and MAGEL2 (imprinting control) supports its role in transcriptional repression and male germ cell differentiation. The enzyme likely operates via K48-linked polyubiquitination to target substrates for proteasomal degradation.\n\nClinVar documents 7 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including copy number gains on 6p25.3-q27 and specific deletions, indicating clinical relevance though specific monogenic disease phenotypes remain under characterization. The gene is expressed in tissues associated with reproductive function and immune regulation. Mechanistically, TRIM27 acts as a scaffold E3 ligase where the SPRY domain recruits specific substrates like PTEN to the catalytic RING domain, modulating cell proliferation and differentiation signals.
