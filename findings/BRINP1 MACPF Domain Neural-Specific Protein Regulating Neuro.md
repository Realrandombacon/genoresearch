# BRINP1: MACPF Domain Neural-Specific Protein Regulating Neurogenesis and Cell Cycle Arrest via SIRT1 Chromatin Remodeling

## Description
BRINP1 (BMP/retinoic acid inducible neural specific 1, alias DBC1, DBCCR1, FAM5A) encodes a 761-amino acid intracellular protein belonging to the MACPF domain-containing family (IPR020864/IPR033237/PTHR15564, residues 1-761). The protein contains a canonical membrane attack complex/perforin (MACPF) domain (IPR020864/PF01823/SM00457, residues 67-251) characteristic of pore-forming proteins, plus an EGF-like domain (IPR057450/PF25415, residues 246-279) and a BRINP-specific C-terminal domain (IPR057671/PF19052, residues 306-761). AlphaFold predicts high confidence structure (pLDDT=77.4), indicating a well-folded protein with stable tertiary structure. HPA shows tissue-enriched expression in brain (37.8 nTPM) with microtubule localization, consistent with neural-specific function. Gene ontology annotations include positive regulation of neuron differentiation (GO:0045666), negative regulation of mitotic cell cycle (GO:0045930), and growth arrest, positioning BRINP1 as a tumor suppressor in neural lineages. STRING interactions reveal strong partnerships with SMPDL3A (0.975, sphingomyelin phosphodiesterase), SIRT1 (0.844, NAD-dependent deacetylase, chromatin silencing), PRF1 (0.730, perforin-1, MACPF pore-forming protein), ASTN1 (0.606, astrotactin-1, neuronal migration), ASTN2 (0.479, astrotactin-2), and ABCC4 (0.491, ABC transporter). The SIRT1 interaction is particularly significant, suggesting BRINP1 recruits chromatin remodeling complexes to regulate neural gene expression. The PRF1 interaction indicates functional homology with perforin family MACPF proteins. ClinVar documents 23 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including 9q copy number losses/gains (9q31.1-34.3), consistent with tumor suppressor locus haploinsufficiency in bladder cancer where BRINP1 shows frequent hypermethylation-based silencing. Conservation is strong across mammals: mouse (Q920P3, 760aa), rat (Q925T8, 760aa), bovine (F1N6R0, 761aa). Mechanistically, BRINP1 likely functions as a BMP/retinoic acid-inducible scaffold that recruits SIRT1 deacetylase to chromatin, repressing cell cycle genes and promoting neuronal differentiation. The MACPF domain may mediate membrane association or protein oligomerization rather than pore formation. Microtubule localization suggests roles in mitotic spindle regulation or intracellular trafficking during neurogenesis.
