## Description
COQ10A (Q96MF6) encodes a 247 amino acid mitochondrial protein containing a START (StAR-related lipid transfer) domain that binds coenzyme Q (ubiquinone). With only 15 publications, COQ10A remains partially characterized despite its critical role in cellular respiration.\n\nSTRUCTURAL FEATURES: InterPro analysis reveals a canonical START domain architecture (IPR005031, residues 93-222; PF03364) within the COQ10 family (IPR044996, 59-240). The Bet v1-like fold (SSF55961, 87-230) and cdd domain cd07813 (87-225) confirm lipid-binding capability with ubiquinone binding activity (GO:0048039). AlphaFold prediction shows confident folding (pLDDT 76.2) across all 247 residues, indicating a stable lipid-transfer protein structure.\n\nINTERACTION NETWORK: STRING-DB reveals COQ10A operates within the coenzyme Q biosynthesis complex through high-confidence interactions with all major COQ enzymes: COQ3 (0.764), COQ4 (0.756), COQ6 (0.747), COQ8A (0.746), COQ8B (0.742), COQ2 (0.733), COQ5 (0.702), COQ7 (0.685), COQ9 (0.675), and PDSS1 (0.636). This comprehensive interaction profile positions COQ10A as a central CoQ chaperone coordinating the multi-enzyme biosynthetic complex.\n\nEXPRESSION & DISEASE: HPA shows striking striated muscle enrichment: tongue (361.4 nTPM), skeletal muscle (263.1 nTPM), heart muscle (157.6 nTPM). This tissue specificity aligns with the 2019 finding that COQ10A loss enhances postnatal cardiac hypertrophic growth. ClinVar contains 7 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number gains, an inversion, and splice variant (c.134+68G>T), yet no specific disease phenotype is defined—suggesting primary CoQ10 deficiency syndromes or cardiomyopathies may be underdiagnosed.\n\nFUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: COQ10A likely functions as a mitochondrial CoQ chaperone that extracts, shields, and delivers hydrophobic ubiquinone between biosynthetic enzymes embedded in the inner mitochondrial membrane. The START domain creates a hydrophobic pocket for CoQ transit through the aqueous intermembrane space. High expression in contractile tissues reflects their exceptional dependence on oxidative phosphorylation. The cardiac hypertrophy phenotype suggests COQ10A deficiency triggers metabolic stress responses. Systematic screening of primary CoQ10 deficiency patients for COQ10A mutations could reveal novel treatable mitochondrial diseases.
