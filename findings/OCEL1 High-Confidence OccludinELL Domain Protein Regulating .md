## Description
OCEL1 (Occludin/ELL Domain Containing 1, aliases FWP009, S863-9) is a 264 amino acid intracellular protein (UniProt Q9H607) with high-confidence folded structure (AlphaFold pLDDT 73.5) containing a canonical occludin/ELL homology domain (residues 147-257), functioning as a dual-function protein that regulates bicellular tight junction assembly at the plasma membrane and RNA Polymerase II transcriptional elongation through ELL family protein interactions.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals OCEL1 contains a classic occludin/ELL architecture: an occludin homology domain (IPR010844/PF07303/PS51980, residues 147-257/153-254/147-257) belonging to the ELL/occludin family (IPR031176/PTHR23288, residues 26-255) with occludin/ELL-like structural fold (SSF144292/G3DSA:6.10.140.340, residues 149-255/157-256). This domain architecture links OCEL1 to both tight junction proteins (occludin) and transcriptional elongation factors (ELL), suggesting dual functionality. The high-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 73.5) confirms a stable OCEL fold optimized for protein-protein interactions.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places OCEL1 within tight junction and transcription networks: strongest interaction with ABHD8 (0.477, alpha/beta hydrolase domain-containing protein 8, lipid metabolism), followed by TMEM205 (0.470, transmembrane protein 205), TLCD1 (0.436, TLC domain-containing protein 1, lipid binding), CLDND1 (0.429, claudin domain-containing protein 1, tight junction), POLR2C (0.423, RNA Polymerase II subunit C, transcription), DYDC2 (0.415, DPY-30 domain-containing protein 2), and SMIM29 (0.401, small integral membrane protein 29). The CLDND1 partnership (0.429) links OCEL1 to tight junction assembly through claudin family interactions. POLR2C interaction (0.423) connects OCEL1 to the transcriptional elongation machinery, consistent with ELL family function. ABHD8-TLCD1 partnerships (0.477/0.436) suggest lipid metabolism roles at membrane junctions.\n\nHPA expression shows tissue-enhanced specificity with liver enrichment (75.7 nTPM), detected in all tissues. Subcellular localization in Golgi apparatus indicates trafficking through secretory pathway en route to plasma membrane tight junctions. The ubiquitous expression pattern suggests OCEL1 functions in both epithelial barrier formation and basal transcription across cell types.\n\nClinVar documents 10 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including one missense variant (c.244C>T, p.Pro82Ser) and multiple chromosome 19p copy number variations (19p13.12-12, 19p13.3-q13.43, 19p13.12-13.11 gain/loss). The CNV burden indicates dosage sensitivity, where haploinsufficiency may cause developmental disorders through disrupted tight junction assembly or transcriptional dysregulation.\n\nConservation is strong across mammals: human (Q9H607, 264 aa), mouse (Q8VCR9, 219 aa), indicating evolutionary conservation of OCEL domain functions.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: OCEL1 functions as a bifunctional protein linking membrane junction assembly to transcriptional regulation. The OCEL domain (residues 147-257) mediates protein-protein interactions with both tight junction components and transcription machinery. CLDND1 interaction (0.429) positions OCEL1 at bicellular tight junctions, where it stabilizes claudin-based barrier formation. POLR2C partnership (0.423) recruits OCEL1 to RNA Polymerase II, where it may function as an elongation factor analogous to ELL proteins, enhancing transcriptional processivity. Golgi localization suggests OCEL1 traffics to the plasma membrane via the secretory pathway, potentially carrying transcriptional regulators. ABHD8-TLCD1 interactions (0.477/0.436) link OCEL1 to lipid metabolism at membrane junctions, possibly regulating lipid composition for tight junction integrity. Liver enrichment (75.7 nTPM) indicates specialized roles in hepatocyte barrier function and metabolic gene transcription. 19p CNVs cause OCEL1 haploinsufficiency, leading to defective tight junction assembly, impaired transcriptional elongation, and developmental pathology. OCEL1 represents a unique dual-function protein linking epithelial barrier formation to transcriptional control.
