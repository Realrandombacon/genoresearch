## Description
IPP (Intracisternal A Particle-Promoted Polypeptide, also known as KLHL27) is a 584-amino acid kelch family protein (UniProt Q9Y573) that represents a genuine dark gene with minimal gene-specific literature despite clear structural domains and disease-associated variants. Structurally, IPP contains a canonical BTB/POZ domain (IPR000210/G3DSA:3.30.710.10, residues 7-135) for protein-protein interactions and CUL3 binding, a BACK domain (IPR011705/cd18466, residues 139-241) characteristic of BTB-Kelch proteins, and six Kelch repeats (IPR006652) forming a beta-propeller structure (G3DSA:2.120.10.80/IPR015915, residues 285-584) that mediates substrate recognition. AlphaFold predicts very high-confidence structure (pLDDT 90.1), indicating a stable, well-folded architecture typical of functional kelch adaptors. Functionally, IPP localizes intracellularly with ubiquitous expression across all tissues (HPA: detected in all, low tissue specificity) and is classified as an actin-binding protein. STRING interactions confirm kelch family function through associations with MINPP1 (0.881, phytase), KLHL28 (0.710), CUL3 (0.613, cullin-3 E3 ligase scaffold), and other KLHL paralogs (KLHL26: 0.523, KLHL20: 0.521), placing it within the CUL3-RBX1 E3 ubiquitin ligase complex system. Critically, ClinVar documents 6 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants (large copy number gains/losses spanning 1p34-36, inversions) yet ZERO formally associated Mendelian diseases—a significant gap. The mechanistic hypothesis: IPP functions as a substrate adaptor protein that bridges CUL3 E3 ubiquitin ligase complexes with specific cytoskeletal or cell cycle substrates via its Kelch beta-propeller, while the BTB domain mediates CUL3 binding. This positions IPP to regulate protein turnover of actin-binding partners, potentially influencing cytoskeletal dynamics, cell division, or intracellular trafficking. The MINPP1 interaction suggests possible roles in inositol phosphate metabolism regulation. The absence of formal disease nomenclature despite pathogenic variants implies phenotypic complexity, embryonic lethality of null alleles, functional redundancy with other KLHL family members (~50 human kelch genes), or requirement for specific environmental triggers. Future work should identify IPP-specific ubiquitination substrates and investigate whether variants contribute to neurodevelopmental disorders given chromosomal 1p deletions/duplications.
