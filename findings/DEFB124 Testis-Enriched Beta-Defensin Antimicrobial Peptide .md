## Description
DEFB124 (UniProt Q8NES8) encodes a 71 aa secreted beta-defensin antimicrobial peptide, functioning as part of the innate immune defense system with specialized roles in reproductive tract immunity and potential cancer associations. InterPro analysis reveals a canonical beta-defensin architecture: a compact beta-defensin domain (aa 26-55, IPR025933, PF13841) forming the characteristic three-stranded antiparallel beta-sheet stabilized by three conserved disulfide bonds, classified within the Beta-defensin family (PTHR47897, aa 1-70) and the Antimicrobial Peptide superfamily (G3DSA:3.10.360.10, aa 24-62). AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (global pLDDT 78.2), consistent with a well-folded, stable defensin fold resistant to proteolytic degradation—a hallmark of secreted antimicrobial peptides. STRING interactions define DEFB124 as a node within the beta-defensin cluster network, interacting exclusively with other defensin family members: DEFB125 (0.899), DEFB135 (0.896), DEFB128 (0.896), DEFB127 (0.843), DEFB134 (0.820), DEFB119 (0.793), DEFB123 (0.770), DEFB116 (0.765), DEFB129 (0.679), and DEFB114 (0.648), indicating co-expression and potential functional redundancy or cooperative antimicrobial activity within the chromosome 20q11 defensin gene cluster. HPA expression shows highly restricted tissue distribution with testis enrichment (1.0 nTPM), classified as tissue-enhanced and cancer-enriched, with predicted secretory localization consistent with antimicrobial peptide function in the reproductive tract microenvironment; notably absent from immune cells, suggesting epithelial-specific production. ClinVar reports 11 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains on chromosome 20q11.21-11.23 (spanning the defensin cluster region), indicating genomic instability at this locus potentially linked to cancer susceptibility, though no specific monogenic disease has been definitively assigned. Literature (11 papers) highlights: (1) Anti-Staphylococcal Activity—yak DEFB124 ortholog alleviates intestinal injury caused by Staphylococcus aureus infection via direct bacterial killing and microbiome modulation; (2) UV-Induced Expression—human DEFB124 is upregulated by UV exposure, potentially mediating phototherapy benefits in atopic dermatitis through antimicrobial and immunomodulatory effects; (3) Cancer Association—chromosome 20q11 amplifications encompassing the defensin cluster are recurrent in multiple cancers, suggesting DEFB124 dosage alterations may contribute to tumor microenvironment modulation. Functional hypothesis: DEFB124 functions as a: (1) Reproductive Tract Guardian—secreted by testicular epithelial cells to maintain sterile conditions and prevent ascending infections that could compromise spermatogenesis; (2) Skin Immune Modulator—UV-inducible expression in keratinocytes provides photoprotection against bacterial colonization, explaining therapeutic benefits in atopic dermatitis; (3) Cancer Microenvironment Regulator—copy number gains may alter defensin cluster expression, modulating tumor-associated inflammation and bacterial interactions that influence cancer progression. Mechanistic proposal: DEFB124 is secreted as a cationic amphipathic peptide that binds negatively charged bacterial membranes (particularly Gram-positive S. aureus), inserting via the disulfide-stabilized beta-sheet core to form pores that disrupt membrane potential and cause bacterial lysis. In the testis, constitutive low-level expression maintains immune privilege while preventing infection. Under UV stress, keratinocyte upregulation provides rapid antimicrobial defense, reducing bacterial superinfection in compromised skin barriers. Chromosome 20q11 amplifications in cancer may dysregulate the entire defensin cluster, altering the tumor microbiome and inflammatory milieu to favor tumor growth. The combination of a stable high-confidence defensin fold, exclusive beta-defensin interaction network, testis-restricted expression, cancer-associated copy number variations, and demonstrated anti-staphylococcal activity positions DEFB124 as a genuine dark gene requiring mechanistic dissection of its specific contributions to reproductive immunity, skin host defense, and cancer biology.
