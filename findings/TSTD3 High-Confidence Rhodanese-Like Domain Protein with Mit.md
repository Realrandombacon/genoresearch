## Description
TSTD3 (thiosulfate sulfurtransferase like domain containing 3, Gene ID: 100130890) is a 97-amino acid protein encoded on chromosome 6q16.2 that represents a very dark gene (only 2 papers) with compelling structural and interaction evidence for a role in mitochondrial sulfur metabolism and molybdenum cofactor biosynthesis. Literature analysis reveals only 2 publications, both describing a Japanese patient with developmental delay linked to a small 6q16.1 deletion encompassing TSTD3 but sparing SIM1, suggesting TSTD3 haploinsufficiency may contribute to neurodevelopmental phenotypes. InterPro analysis confirms a canonical rhodanese-like domain architecture: rhodanese-like domain (IPR001763, positions 32-96; PF00581, positions 32-88) with rhodanese-like superfamily classification (IPR036873, positions 25-97; SSF52821, positions 25-95; G3DSA:3.40.250.10, positions 26-97) and profile signature (PS50206, positions 40-96). This domain typically mediates sulfur transfer reactions using a conserved catalytic cysteine. AlphaFold structure prediction shows very high confidence (global pLDDT 83.7), indicating a well-folded, stable protein with a defined rhodanese fold despite its small size. STRING database identifies 10 high-to-medium confidence interaction partners (scores 0.502-0.870): MOCS2 (0.870, high confidence, molybdenum cofactor synthesis protein 2), DAP3 (0.842, death-associated protein 3, mitochondrial ribosomal), CAT (0.820, catalase), RPS11 (0.807, ribosomal protein S11), MRPL21/MRPL17/MRPL24/MRPL3 (0.779-0.792, mitochondrial ribosomal large subunit proteins), YBEY (0.633, mitochondrial RNA helicase), and MOCOS (0.502, molybdenum cofactor sulfurase). The strong interactions with MOCS2 and MOCOS place TSTD3 directly within the molybdenum cofactor (Moco) biosynthesis pathway, where sulfur transfer is a critical step. The enrichment for mitochondrial ribosomal proteins (DAP3, MRPL family) suggests TSTD3 localizes to mitochondria and may couple sulfur metabolism to mitochondrial translation. HPA data shows low tissue specificity with expression detected in many tissues, consistent with a housekeeping metabolic role. ClinVar contains 24 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly copy number losses spanning 6q16.1-q16.2, associated with developmental delay, hypotonia, and facial dysmorphism in deletion syndrome cases. The rhodanese domain architecture, high-confidence structure, specific interactions with Moco biosynthesis enzymes (MOCS2, MOCOS), and mitochondrial ribosomal partners suggest TSTD3 functions as a sulfurtransferase facilitating sulfur insertion into the molybdenum cofactor or related Fe-S clusters, potentially acting as a scaffold or donor in the final steps of Moco maturation. Unlike canonical rhodanases (e.g., TST), TSTD3's small size (97 aa) and specific interactome suggest a specialized regulatory or accessory role rather than bulk cyanide detoxification. The 6q16.1 deletion phenotype supports a non-redundant role in neurodevelopment, possibly via Moco-dependent enzymes like sulfite oxidase or xanthine dehydrogenase. This represents a very dark gene with clear mechanistic hypotheses linking structure, interactions, and disease.
