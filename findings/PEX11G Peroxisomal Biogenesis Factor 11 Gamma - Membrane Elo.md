## Description
PEX11G (Peroxisomal Biogenesis Factor 11 Gamma, also known as PEX11C, UniProt Q96HA9) is a 241 amino acid peroxisomal membrane protein functioning as a critical regulator of peroxisome proliferation, membrane elongation, and fatty acid oxidation. With only 3 literature references (genuine very dark gene status), PEX11G represents the least characterized member of the PEX11 family (alongside PEX11A/PEX11B), yet plays essential roles in metabolic homeostasis. The protein contains a conserved Peroxisomal biogenesis factor 11 domain (IPR008733, PF05648, aa 6-229) and a metazoan-specific PEX11C region (IPR026510, aa 1-237). AlphaFold predicts an exceptionally well-folded structure (pLDDT 91.8, very high confidence) across all 241 residues, indicating a stable transmembrane or membrane-associated architecture distinct from the more disordered regions often seen in membrane regulators. STRING interactions reveal exclusive high-confidence partnerships with peroxisome biogenesis machinery: PEX11B (0.997), PEX11A (0.997), PEX13 (0.837), PEX19 (0.816), PEX10 (0.752), PEX12 (0.744), PEX16 (0.728), PEX14 (0.727), PEX6 (0.708), and PEX7 (0.705), confirming integration into the peroxisomal matrix protein import and division complexes. HPA shows tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in testis (27.7 nTPM) and liver (25.2 nTPM), consistent with roles in steroidogenesis and beta-oxidation, classified as a predicted membrane protein with low cancer specificity. ClinVar documents 7 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number gains (19p13.3-q13.43) and deletions, though specific monogenic diseases directly attributed to PEX11G point mutations remain uncharacterized, suggesting potential redundancy with PEX11A/B or contribution to complex metabolic disorders. Key literature findings: (1) 2024 Drosophila study showed dietary cysteine/methionine induce CG33474 (PEX11 homolog) to promote peroxisome elevation and fat loss, providing functional conservation evidence, (2) 2024 twin study linked PEX11G DNA methylation to BMI-blood pressure association, implicating epigenetic regulation in obesity-hypertension axis, (3) 2016 grass carp study described genomic organization, indicating deep evolutionary conservation. Functional hypothesis: PEX11G functions as a membrane curvature-inducing scaffold that recruits dynamin-related proteins (e.g., DLP1/DRP1 via adaptors) to initiate peroxisome fission. Unlike PEX11A (constitutive) and PEX11B (inducible by fatty acids), PEX11G may specialize in maintaining basal peroxisome numbers in specific tissues (testis, liver) or responding to specific lipid signals (sulfur-containing amino acids). The high structural confidence (pLDDT 91.8) suggests a rigid helical bundle or amphipathic helix arrangement that inserts into the membrane to drive tubulation. Through interactions with PEX19 (chaperone/import receptor) and PEX13/14 (docking complex), PEX11G likely coordinates membrane remodeling with matrix protein import efficiency. Disease mechanism: Dysregulation of PEX11G (via CNVs or epigenetic silencing) may contribute to metabolic syndrome features (obesity, hypertension) by altering peroxisomal beta-oxidation capacity, leading to accumulation of very long-chain fatty acids (VLCFAs) and reactive oxygen species (ROS). The 2024 methylation data suggests PEX11G expression is environmentally plastic, linking diet to peroxisome density. The 7 ClinVar CNVs suggest potential developmental defects if dosage is severely altered, possibly overlapping with Zellweger spectrum disorders though no specific PEX11G-null phenotype is yet defined. Therapeutic implication: Targeting PEX11G-mediated peroxisome proliferation could offer a novel strategy for treating obesity and metabolic dysfunction by enhancing fatty acid catabolism.
