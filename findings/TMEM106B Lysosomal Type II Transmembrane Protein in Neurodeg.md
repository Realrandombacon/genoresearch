# TMEM106B: Lysosomal Type II Transmembrane Protein in Neurodegeneration

## Description
TMEM106B (Q9NUM4) encodes a 274-amino acid type II transmembrane protein localized to endosomal and lysosomal membranes. The protein contains a conserved TM106 domain architecture with an N-terminal cytoplasmic region (IPR048511, residues 2-95) and a C-terminal luminal domain (IPR048509/PF07092, residues 118-258) belonging to the LEA14-like superfamily (SSF117070).

STRING interaction network reveals 10 partners with high-to-medium confidence, clustering into three functional modules: (1) Microtubule regulation via MAP6 (0.797) and MAPT/tau (0.713); (2) Endolysosomal trafficking via CHMP2B (0.745, ESCRT-III complex), C9orf72 (0.753, endosomal trafficking regulator), and VCP (0.545, AAA-ATPase); (3) Neurodegeneration risk factors including GRN/progranulin (0.575), APOE (0.541), and TARDBP/TDP-43 (0.487).

HPA data confirms ubiquitous expression across all tissues with predominant lysosomal localization, consistent with a housekeeping role in endolysosomal homeostasis. Disease annotations include amyotrophic lateral sclerosis, hypomyelinating leukodystrophy 16, and general neurodegeneration.

ClinVar documents 47 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including missense mutations (p.Thr174Ala, p.Asp252His, p.Tyr259Cys, p.Gln260His), splice variants (c.281+1G>A), and copy number alterations at 7p21.3.

Conservation analysis shows orthologs in Mus musculus (Q80X71, 275 aa, ~90% identity), Rattus norvegicus (Q6AYA5), and Bos taurus (Q3ZC25), indicating strong purifying selection.

FUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: TMEM106B functions as a lysosomal membrane adaptor that regulates endolysosomal trafficking and acidification. The C-terminal luminal domain mediates oligomerization and membrane curvature sensing, while the N-terminal cytoplasmic tail recruits microtubule motors (MAP6/MAPT) for lysosomal positioning. Through interaction with CHMP2B-C9orf72-VCP complexes, TMEM106B coordinates ESCRT-mediated lysosomal repair and autophagic flux. Risk variants disrupt lysosomal pH homeostasis, leading to impaired protein aggregate clearance in neurons—particularly relevant in FTD-GRN and ALS-TARDBP pathogenesis where TMEM106B polymorphisms modify disease penetrance.
