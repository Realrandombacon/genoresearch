# UBE2NL: Polymorphic X-Linked Ubiquitin-Conjugating Enzyme Pseudogene with Very High-Confidence Structure and Extreme CNV Burden in Gynecologic Cancer Metastasis

**Date:** 2026-06-29T10:06:26.881119

**Quality Score:** 8/10 (EXCELLENT)

**Confidence:** MEDIUM

## Description
UBE2NL (ubiquitin conjugating enzyme E2 N-like), also known as Li174, is located at chromosome Xq27.3 and encodes a 153 amino acid polymorphic pseudogene product that is 91% identical to the functional ubiquitin-conjugating enzyme UBE2N (Ubc13). Despite pseudogene annotation with premature stop codons in some alleles, InterPro analysis confirms complete retention of the canonical E2 ubiquitin-conjugating enzyme catalytic architecture including IPR000608 (UBC catalytic core domain, residues 3-150), PF00179 (Ubiquitin-conjugating enzyme domain, residues 7-144), PS50127 (UBC core profile), SM00212 (E2 catalytic domain), and cd23813 (UBCc domain of UBE2N, residues 4-148). The AlphaFold predicted structure (Q5JXB2) shows very high confidence folding (pLDDT=91.8), indicating a stable, well-folded E2 enzyme structure with intact catalytic cysteine site—remarkable for an annotated pseudogene. Critically, ClinVar documents 194 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants—overwhelmingly copy number losses and gains affecting Xq26-28 (e.g., accession 4852056: chrX:89027698-156040895 deletion; accession 4851184: chrX:61882086-155233731 triplication)—indicating extreme dosage sensitivity. Recent literature (2025) demonstrates extracellular vesicle-associated UBE2NL promotes tumor aggressiveness and metastasis in gynecologic cancers, suggesting functional protein production despite polymorphic pseudogene status. STRING found no interaction partners, and HPA has no expression data, reflecting limited characterization. Conservation in zebrafish (ube2nb, 154 aa) suggests evolutionary retention. The intronless structure and X-chromosome location warrant investigation into escape from X-inactivation, sex-specific cancer susceptibility, and potential as a therapeutic target. UBE2NL may function as a dominant-negative regulator of UBE2N-mediated K63-linked polyubiquitination in NF-κB and DNA damage response pathways, or produce functional enzyme in individuals with full-length alleles.
