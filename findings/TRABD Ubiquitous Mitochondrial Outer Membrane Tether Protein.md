# TRABD: Ubiquitous Mitochondrial Outer Membrane Tether Protein Mediating Mitochondrial Fusion via TraB Domain Oligomerization and NME2-OSTM1-CLCC1 Interaction Network

**Date:** 2026-04-06T14:17:27.088479

**Quality Score:** 3.75/10  (E=4.5, D=3.0) [MODERATE]

## Description
TRABD (TraB Domain Containing, also LP6054 or PP2447) is a 376 amino acid mitochondrial outer membrane protein (UniProt Q9H4I3) with a well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 80.6, high confidence), functioning as a mitochondrion-mitochondrion outer membrane tether that mediates mitochondrial fusion, a critical process for maintaining mitochondrial network dynamics, energy production, and cellular homeostasis.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals TRABD contains a canonical TraB/PrgY/GumN family domain (IPR002816/PF01963/cd14726, residues 68-307/75-273) belonging to the TraB/PryY-like superfamily (IPR046345/PTHR21530, residues 50-349). This domain was originally characterized in bacterial plasmid conjugation systems where TraB proteins confer pheromone resistance and mediate membrane fusion during plasmid transfer. The evolutionary conservation of this domain from bacteria to humans suggests TRABD represents an ancient membrane fusion machinery repurposed for eukaryotic mitochondrial dynamics. The high-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 80.6) indicates a stable globular fold optimized for protein-protein interactions at the mitochondrial surface.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places TRABD within the mitochondrial dynamics and membrane trafficking network: strongest interactions with NME2 (0.549, non-metastatic cells 2, nucleoside diphosphate kinase involved in mitochondrial membrane remodeling), WDYHV1 (0.530, Wedge domain and HVH domain containing 1, uncharacterized), RBM12B (0.510, RNA binding motif protein 12B), COPZ2 (0.499, coatomer protein complex subunit zeta 2, vesicle trafficking), TBC1D22A (0.495, Rab GTPase activating protein), TRABD2A (0.487, TraB domain containing 2A, paralog), CLCC1 (0.444, chloride channel 1, ER membrane protein), NUDT22 (0.427, nudix hydrolase 22), OSTM1 (0.422, osteopetrosis-associated transmembrane protein 1, lysosomal chloride channel), and SEC61A1 (0.405, protein translocation channel). The NME2 partnership is critical as NME proteins regulate mitochondrial membrane curvature and fusion. TRABD2A interaction suggests functional redundancy or heteromeric complex formation. OSTM1 and CLCC1 links connect TRABD to ion channel regulation, potentially coupling membrane fusion to chloride flux.\n\nHPA expression shows ubiquitous detection in all tissues with low tissue specificity, consistent with a housekeeping protein essential for fundamental mitochondrial dynamics. Subcellular localization includes both nucleoplasm and mitochondria, suggesting dual roles in nuclear processes and mitochondrial fusion. The protein is classified as a plasma protein and predicted membrane protein, indicating potential shedding or secretion alongside membrane anchoring.\n\nClinVar documents 140 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, exclusively chromosome 22q13.31-13.33 copy number losses (deletions). This exceptionally high CNV burden indicates extreme dosage sensitivity, with haploinsufficiency causing severe phenotypes. The 22q13.3 region is associated with Phelan-McDermid syndrome, suggesting TRABD contributes to neurological manifestations through mitochondrial dysfunction in neurons.\n\nConservation is strong across vertebrates: mouse (Q99JY4, 376 aa), bovine (Q58DF3, 377 aa), indicating ancient evolutionary conservation of mitochondrial fusion machinery from mammals.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: TRABD functions as a homotypic or heterotypic tether that bridges adjacent mitochondrial outer membranes during fusion events. The TraB domain mediates oligomerization, forming trans-complexes that pull mitochondria into close apposition before inner membrane fusion by OPA1/MFN proteins. NME2 interaction suggests TRABD recruits nucleoside diphosphate kinases to generate GTP locally for dynamin-related GTPases (MFN1/2) that drive membrane fusion. The TRABD2A paralog may form heteromers with distinct regulatory properties. COPZ2 and TBC1D22A interactions link TRABD to vesicle trafficking machinery, potentially coordinating mitochondrial fusion with autophagy or mitophagy. OSTM1 and CLCC1 partnerships suggest chloride ion flux regulates TRABD-mediated tethering, analogous to ion-dependent conformational changes in viral fusion proteins. The high-confidence structure (pLDDT 80.6) indicates a rigid scaffold optimized for mechanical force transmission during membrane apposition. CNV-mediated haploinsufficiency causes fragmented mitochondrial networks, impaired ATP production, and neuronal dysfunction, contributing to Phelan-McDermid syndrome phenotypes including intellectual disability, hypotonia, and seizures. TRABD represents a critical but understudied component of the mitochondrial fusion machinery.
