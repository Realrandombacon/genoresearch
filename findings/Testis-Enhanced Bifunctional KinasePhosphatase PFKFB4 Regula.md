## Description
PFKFB4 encodes 6-phosphofructo-2-kinase/fructose-2,6-bisphosphatase 4 (PFKFB4/iPFK2), a 469 aa bifunctional enzyme (UniProt Q16877) belonging to the PFK-2/FBPase-2 family that functions as a critical metabolic regulator controlling glycolytic flux through synthesis and degradation of fructose-2,6-bisphosphate (F2,6BP), the most potent allosteric activator of phosphofructokinase-1 (PFK-1) and rate-limiting metabolite determining glycolytic versus gluconeogenic fate in response to cellular energy demands and hypoxic stress. InterPro reveals characteristic bifunctional architecture with two catalytic domains in tandem: N-terminal 6-phosphofructo-2-kinase domain (IPR013079/G3DSA:3.40.50.300/IPR027417, residues 35-249/38-250) containing P-loop nucleotide triphosphate hydrolase fold for ATP binding and phosphate transfer to fructose-6-phosphate generating F2,6BP, C-terminal histidine phosphatase domain (cd07067/G3DSA:3.40.50.1240/IPR013078/IPR029033, residues 252-437/248-468) containing phosphoglycerate mutase-like fold with conserved active site histidine residue (IPR001345, residues 254-263) that becomes phosphorylated during catalytic cycle enabling F2,6BP dephosphorylation back to fructose-6-phosphate, Fructose-2,6-bisphosphatase family domain (IPR003094, residues 32-451) with GO annotations for catalytic activity (GO:0003824), ATP binding (GO:0005524), fructose 2,6-bisphosphate metabolic process (GO:0006003), and 6-phosphofructo-2-kinase activity (GO:0003873) defining dual enzymatic functions. Functional mechanism: PFKFB4 operates through bifunctional enzymatic cycle: (1) Kinase mode - N-terminal domain binds ATP and fructose-6-phosphate transferring gamma-phosphate to fructose-6-phosphate C2 position generating F2,6BP which allosterically activates PFK-1 (PFKM/PFKP/PFKL 0.979/0.969/0.966) accelerating glycolytic flux; (2) Phosphatase mode - C-terminal histidine phosphatase domain dephosphorylates F2,6BP back to fructose-6-phosphate through phospho-histidine intermediate reducing PFK-1 activation and slowing glycolysis; (3) Reciprocal regulation - Kinase/phosphatase activities are reciprocally regulated by phosphorylation, metabolite levels, and protein interactions ensuring appropriate F2,6BP concentrations for metabolic state; (4) Hypoxia response - PFKFB4 is hypoxia-inducible via HIF-1α binding to promoter increasing F2,6BP production and glycolytic ATP generation when oxidative phosphorylation is limited; (5) Cancer metabolism - Elevated PFKFB4 in cancer cells supports Warburg effect (aerobic glycolysis) providing biosynthetic precursors and maintaining redox balance; (6) Nucleolar function - Nucleolar localization suggests potential role in coordinating ribosome biogenesis with metabolic state or nuclear NAD+/NADH balance. Disease mechanism: PFKFB4 CNVs likely cause disease through: (1) Haploinsufficiency reducing F2,6BP synthesis capacity impairing glycolytic flux especially under hypoxic stress affecting high-energy tissues; (2) Impaired hypoxic adaptation causing tissue damage in ischemic conditions (stroke, myocardial infarction); (3) Dosage effects from duplications potentially causing aberrant glycolytic activation or metabolic imbalance; (4) 3p syndromes typically include developmental delay, growth abnormalities, intellectual disability, and organ defects consistent with disrupted energy metabolism affecting multiple organ systems; (5) Potential cancer relevance given PFKFB4 role in tumor metabolism though HPA shows low cancer specificity suggesting context-dependent oncogenic role requiring additional hits; (6) Testis-enriched expression (23.8 nTPM) suggests potential male reproductive phenotypes in deficiency states given high glycolytic demands of spermatogenesis.
