## Description
KU-MEL-3 (Gene ID: 497048) encodes a small 58-amino acid uncharacterized protein located on chromosome 6p25.1. UniProt accession Q5G014 confirms this as a reviewed entry. AlphaFold structure prediction (AF-Q5G014-F1) yields a medium confidence pLDDT score of 63.7, suggesting the protein is partially structured with potential intrinsically disordered regions—common for small regulatory peptides. InterPro domain scan failed to identify conserved motifs, and STRING database found no protein interaction partners, indicating KU-MEL-3 remains functionally orphaned. HPA expression data is unavailable. Critically, ClinVar documents 44 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number losses and gains spanning 6p25.3-22.3 (e.g., chr6:156974-21955964 duplications, chr6:154749-5886174 deletions). These structural variants suggest KU-MEL-3 resides in a dosage-sensitive genomic region, though it remains unclear whether KU-MEL-3 itself drives pathology or is co-altered with neighboring genes. The 6p25.1 locus is associated with developmental disorders when deleted/duplicated. Functional hypothesis: KU-MEL-3 may encode a micropeptide involved in chromatin regulation or transcriptional modulation, given its small size and disordered character. Alternatively, it could serve as a cis-regulatory element precursor or compete with neighboring gene expression. The high CNV burden without point mutations suggests haploinsufficiency or triplosensitivity mechanisms rather than dominant-negative effects.
