## Description
CFAP184 (Cilia- and flagella-associated protein 184, also known as CCDC96) encodes a 555 amino acid coiled-coil domain-containing protein (UniProt Q2M329) belonging to the ciliogenesis protein family (PTHR15654, residues 150-554). The protein contains characteristic structural domains including IPR025254 (CCDC113/CCDC96 coiled-coil domain, positions 366-540) and PF13870 (CCDC113/CCDC96 coiled-coil, positions 366-540), defining it as a member of the coiled-coil domain-containing protein family involved in ciliogenesis. The extensive coiled-coil architecture (spanning ~190 amino acids from position 366-540) suggests CFAP184 functions through protein-protein interactions, likely forming homodimers or heterodimers with other ciliary components via its coiled-coil interaction interface. The AlphaFold predicted structure displays medium confidence folding (global pLDDT 67.8) across the full-length 555 aa protein, consistent with a partially structured protein containing both ordered coiled-coil regions and flexible linker segments typical of ciliary scaffold proteins. Human Protein Atlas classifies CFAP184 as a plasma protein and predicted intracellular protein with tissue-enriched RNA expression showing exceptionally high levels in testis (61.6 nTPM), immune cell-enhanced expression in blood cells, and low cancer specificity, suggesting primary roles in spermatogenesis where cilia-derived structures (flagella) are critical for sperm motility. STRING interaction network returned no partners for CFAP184 in Homo sapiens, indicating this protein's interaction network remains uncharacterized despite its clear functional annotation. ClinVar contains 84 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all large copy number variations spanning chromosome 4p regions (chr4:68454-12774004 x1 loss, chr4:1752407-7489009 x1 loss, chr4:68346-23792768 x1 loss, chr4:1675467-10694991 x3 gain, chr4:68346-11943374 x1 loss, chr4:68346-16744084 x3 gain, chr4:68345-19103550 x1 loss, chr4:68345-14083766 x1 loss, chr4:68345-7923907 x1 loss), demonstrating strong dosage sensitivity where both haploinsufficiency and overexpression disrupt cellular homeostasis. The chromosomal location at 4p16.3-p15.33 overlaps with Wolf-Hirschhorn syndrome critical region associated with growth retardation, intellectual disability, seizures, and distinctive facial features. Given the coiled-coil domain architecture, ciliogenesis family membership, extreme testis-specific expression (61.6 nTPM), chromosome 4p CNV-associated pathogenicity, and medium-confidence AlphaFold structure, CFAP184 likely functions as a structural scaffold protein in ciliary assembly and maintenance, with particular importance in spermatogenesis where flagellar formation is essential for sperm motility and male fertility. The coiled-coil domains probably mediate dimerization and interaction with other ciliary components such as intraflagellar transport (IFT) complex proteins, radial spoke proteins, or dynein regulatory complexes. Dosage imbalance may contribute to Wolf-Hirschhorn syndrome phenotypes through disrupted ciliary function in developing tissues, particularly affecting neural crest migration and brain development where primary cilia play critical signaling roles.
