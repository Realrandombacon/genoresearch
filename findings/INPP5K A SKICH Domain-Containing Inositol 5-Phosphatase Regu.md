# INPP5K: A SKICH Domain-Containing Inositol 5-Phosphatase Regulating Phosphoinositide Signaling in Muscle Development and Neuronal Function

**Date:** 2026-06-26T17:32:10.353173

**Quality Score:** 10/10 (EXCELLENT)

**Confidence:** HIGH

## Description
INPP5K (Inositol Polyphosphate-5-Phosphatase K, UniProt Q9BT40), also known as SKIP (Skeletal muscle and Kidney-enriched Inositol Phosphatase), is a 448-amino acid cytosolic phosphatase that hydrolyzes the 5-position phosphate from phosphatidylinositol (3,4,5)-trisphosphate [PI(3,4,5)P3] and related inositol phosphates, thereby antagonizing PI3K-AKT signaling and regulating actin cytoskeleton dynamics. INPP5K belongs to the type II inositol 5-phosphatase family characterized by an N-terminal catalytic domain and a C-terminal SKICH regulatory domain. InterPro analysis reveals a bipartite domain architecture: an N-terminal catalytic inositol polyphosphate phosphatase domain (IPR000300, PF22669, SM00128, aa 12-326) belonging to the endonuclease/exonuclease/phosphatase superfamily (IPR036691, G3DSA:3.60.10.10, SSF56219, aa 1-336) with the conserved catalytic motifs required for Mg2+-dependent phosphomonoester hydrolysis; a C-terminal SKICH domain (IPR041611, PF17751, G3DSA:2.60.40.2840, aa 332-431) named after SKIP and CHIC proteins that mediates protein-protein interactions and subcellular targeting; the protein belongs to the inositol 5-phosphatase family (IPR046985, PTHR11200, aa 1-384). The AlphaFold predicted structure shows high confidence folding (pLDDT 88.4), consistent with a well-ordered two-domain architecture where the catalytic domain forms a compact α/β fold typical of metallophosphatases and the SKICH domain adopts a distinct immunoglobulin-like β-sandwich structure connected by a flexible linker. STRING interaction data places INPP5K within the inositol phosphate metabolism network with very high confidence associations to: ITPKB (0.925), inositol-trisphosphate 3-kinase B generating IP4 from IP3; IPMK (0.921), inositol phosphate multikinase with pleiotropic signaling roles; INPP5A (0.916), type I inositol 5-phosphatase sharing substrate specificity; INPP1 (0.915), inositol polyphosphate 1-phosphatase; ITPK1 (0.911), inositol-tetrakisphosphate 1-kinase; INPP5J (0.744), another type II 5-phosphatase; ITPKA (0.712), inositol-trisphosphate 3-kinase A; PLCE1 (0.682), phospholipase C epsilon generating IP3; ITPKC (0.665), inositol-trisphosphate 3-kinase C; and MINPP1 (0.441), multiple inositol polyphosphate phosphatase. HPA expression profiles confirm tissue-enhanced expression detected in all tissues with peak expression in choroid plexus (137.9 nTPM), group-enriched patterns in specific cell types, and low cancer/immune cell specificity; classified as a hydrolase involved in lipid metabolism with disease associations including cataract, congenital muscular dystrophy, and intellectual disability. ClinVar records document 133 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number losses spanning 17p13.3-13.2 (up to 5.7 Mb deletions), copy number gains (2-4 copies of 17p13.3 regions), splice site mutations (c.262-1G>T affecting exon 3 acceptor, c.777-134G>A, c.1291-7C>A), and synonymous variants (c.1101G>A/p.Lys367=), though specific disease phenotypes are not fully annotated in ClinVar; literature reveals biallelic mutations cause autosomal recessive congenital muscular dystrophy with cataracts and intellectual disability (MDCCAID syndrome). We hypothesize that INPP5K functions as a spatial regulator of PI3K signaling during myogenesis and neurodevelopment: upon growth factor stimulation, PI3K generates PI(3,4,5)P3 at the plasma membrane recruiting AKT and downstream effectors; INPP5K is recruited to specific membrane microdomains via its SKICH domain interacting with adaptor proteins; the catalytic domain hydrolyzes PI(3,4,5)P3 to PI(3,4)P2, terminating AKT activation and preventing excessive proliferation; during muscle differentiation, INPP5K downregulation permits sustained PI3K-AKT signaling required for myoblast fusion; in neurons, INPP5K regulates growth cone dynamics by controlling local PIP3 pools that guide actin polymerization; the SKICH domain may mediate homodimerization or heterodimerization with other 5-phosphatases (INPP5A, INPP5J) creating combinatorial regulation; loss of INPP5K causes constitutive PI3K-AKT hyperactivation disrupting muscle fiber formation (congenital muscular dystrophy), lens development (cataract), and neuronal migration/synaptogenesis (intellectual disability); the high-confidence AlphaFold structure suggests the SKICH domain undergoes conformational changes upon binding partners, relieving autoinhibition of the catalytic site; coordination with ITPK kinases creates a push-pull system balancing PIP3 synthesis versus degradation.
