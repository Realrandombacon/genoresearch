## Description
DKK3 (UniProt Q9UBP4), also known as REIC (Reduced Expression in Immortalized Cells) or CRRL, encodes a 350 aa secreted glycoprotein functioning as a context-dependent modulator of Wnt signaling and independent tumor suppressor inducing apoptosis through endoplasmic reticulum stress. InterPro analysis reveals a bipartite cysteine-rich architecture characteristic of the dickkopf family: an N-terminal cysteine-rich domain (Cys-1, IPR047301/cd23014, residues 41-196) containing the conserved Dickkopf N-terminal motif (IPR006796/PF04706, residues 146-196) with multiple disulfide bonds stabilizing the fold; and a C-terminal cysteine-rich domain (Cys-2, IPR047300/cd23274, residues 199-285) belonging to the lipase subunit A superfamily (G3DSA:2.10.80.10, residues 196-287) though lacking catalytic activity, instead mediating protein-protein interactions; all embedded within the Dickkopf-related protein 1-4 family (IPR039863/PTHR12113, residues 7-286). AlphaFold predicts high confidence structure (pLDDT 73.6) indicating a well-folded secreted protein where the two cysteine-rich domains are connected by a flexible linker enabling conformational adaptability for binding multiple partners. STRING interactions define a comprehensive Wnt signaling network: LRP5 (0.947, low-density lipoprotein receptor-related protein 5, canonical Wnt co-receptor), WNT2B (0.858, Wnt ligand), KREMEN1 (0.787, kringle containing transmembrane protein 1, forms ternary complex with DKK-LRP5/6), DKK2 (0.777), DKK1 (0.776), FZD8 (0.753, frizzled receptor), SFRP2 (0.730, secreted frizzled-related protein 2), WNT6 (0.675), WIF1 (0.498, Wnt inhibitory factor 1), and SFRP1 (0.400). HPA classifies DKK3 as tissue-enhanced with exceptional expression in heart muscle (1240.0 nTPM, highest), choroid plexus (371.6 nTPM), cancer-enhanced expression, localized to nuclear bodies/vesicles/cytosol though primarily secreted, reflecting dual intracellular and extracellular functions. ClinVar lists 15 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number gains on chromosome 11p15 (11p15.5-14.2x3, 11p15.3-13x3, 11p15.5-13x3, 11p15.5-15.3, 11p15.5-15.1x3, 11p15.5-q25) and coding mutations (c.952C>T p.Arg318Cys in Cys-2 domain, c.1006dup p.Glu336fs frameshift) though no single-gene disorder is definitively assigned suggesting contribution to 11p15 imprinting disorders or cardiac developmental defects given heart-enriched expression. Functional hypothesis: DKK3 operates through dual mechanisms depending on cellular context: (1) Wnt modulation - Unlike DKK1 which strongly inhibits Wnt/β-catenin signaling via LRP5/6-KREMEN ternary complex formation, DKK3 exhibits weaker/context-dependent Wnt antagonism potentially fine-tuning rather than blocking signaling; (2) Tumor suppression via ER stress - REIC/DKK3 overexpression induces apoptosis through endoplasmic reticulum stress pathway involving Bax activation, JNK signaling, and caspase cascade independent of Wnt; this mechanism underlies cancer gene therapy approaches using adenoviral REIC delivery; (3) TGF-β synergy - DKK3 cooperates with TGF-β signaling enhancing pro-apoptotic responses in cancer cells; (4) Cardiovascular development - Exceptional heart muscle expression (1240 nTPM) suggests critical role in cardiac development/function potentially regulating Wnt-dependent cardiomyocyte differentiation or stress responses; (5) Disease mechanism - CNV gains cause overexpression potentially disrupting Wnt homeostasis contributing to developmental defects; loss-of-function mutations impair tumor suppression enabling cancer progression; 11p15.3 location places it in imprinted region associated with Beckwith-Wiedemann/Silver-Russell syndromes; (6) Conservation - Mouse Dkk3 (Q9QUN9, 349 aa), chicken DKK3 (Q90839, 350 aa) indicate preserved function across vertebrates. Dark aspects requiring investigation include: precise structural basis for differential Wnt modulation vs DKK1, complete receptor repertoire beyond LRP5/KREMEN1, mechanism of ER stress induction, contribution to specific cardiac diseases (cardiomyopathy? arrhythmia?), role in choroid plexus function (CSF production? blood-CSF barrier?), therapeutic efficacy of REIC gene therapy in clinical trials, genotype-phenotype correlations in 11p15 syndromes, post-translational modifications controlling secretion/activity, and crosstalk with other Wnt antagonists (SFRPs, WIF1).
