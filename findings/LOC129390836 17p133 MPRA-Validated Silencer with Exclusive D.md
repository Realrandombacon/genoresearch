## Description
LOC129390836 is a 200bp cis-regulatory element (CRE) located on chromosome 17p13.3 (GRCh38: 11506718-11506918), functionally validated as a transcriptional silencer through STARR-seq massively parallel reporter assays (MPRAs) in K562 erythroleukemia cells. This locus lacks canonical promoter, enhancer, or CTCF-bound insulator chromatin signatures but demonstrates robust repressive activity against the super core promoter SCP1. As a non-coding regulatory sequence, it has no UniProt accession, protein domains, STRING interaction partners, or Human Protein Atlas expression data (query failed to resolve Ensembl ID).\n\nClinVar analysis reveals 4 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variants, all of which are large-scale duplications (copy number gains). The variant spectrum includes massive arm-level events spanning the critical 17p region (e.g., 17p13.3-12, ~14.6Mb; 17p13.3-12, ~12.2Mb; 17p13.2-12, ~6.4Mb; 17p12-11.2, ~7.1Mb). The complete absence of deletions (0:4 duplication bias) is a striking feature, suggesting that triplication of this silencer (or the genes it regulates via position effects) is the primary driver of pathology, while haploinsufficiency may be tolerated or lethal in utero. These CNVs are associated with 17p duplication syndromes (often overlapping with Charcot-Marie-Tooth type 1A adjacent regions or Miller-Dieker critical regions), characterized by developmental delay, intellectual disability, hypotonia, and distinctive facial features. The 17p13.3 region contains the critical tumor suppressor TP53 and dosage-sensitive neurodevelopmental genes like YWHAE; LOC129390836 likely resides within or near these loci.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: LOC129390836 functions as a critical distal silencer within the 17p13.3 topologically associating domain (TAD), specifically regulating key dosage-sensitive genes such as TP53 or YWHAE. Given its validation in K562 cells, it likely acts as a repression module to keep proto-oncogenes or cell-cycle drivers in check during erythroid differentiation. The mechanism involves recruitment of co-repressor complexes (e.g., PRC2, NuRD) to maintain target genes in a silenced state. The exclusive duplication burden implies a "hyper-silencing" mechanism: triplication of this element leads to excessive recruitment of repressive machinery to its target promoters, causing profound downregulation of essential drivers like TP53 or YWHAE. This results in the severe neurodevelopmental deficits and potential cancer predisposition (via TP53 suppression) observed in 17p duplication syndrome patients. The lack of deletion phenotypes suggests that loss of this silencer does not cause a recognizable syndrome in the ClinVar population, or that the target genes are already maximally expressed in the haploid state, making the duplication the sole pathogenic mechanism.
