## Description
MYO18B (UniProt Q8IUG5) encodes a massive 2567 aa unconventional class XVIII myosin that functions as a specialized cytoskeletal motor protein with emerging roles in lysosomal exocytosis, focal adhesion dynamics, and striated muscle sarcomere organization. InterPro analysis reveals a complex domain architecture characteristic of class XVIII myosins: a canonical myosin head motor domain (aa 565-1334, IPR001609, IPR036064, cd01386) containing ATP-binding P-loop NTP hydrolase motifs (IPR027417) and kinesin motor domain superfamily folds (IPR036961, G3DSA:3.40.850.10), enabling actin-based motility and force generation; multiple CATH-Gene3D homologous superfamilies confirm structural conservation with myosin VI head domains (G3DSA:1.20.120.720) and myosin subunit A folds (G3DSA:4.10.270.10). AlphaFold predicts a medium-confidence structure (pLDDT 59.8), consistent with a large scaffolding protein containing both ordered motor domains and intrinsically disordered regions suitable for protein-protein interactions and mechanotransduction. STRING interactions define MYO18B as a central node in the unconventional myosin network with remarkably high confidence scores: MYO1F (0.916) and MYO1C (0.913), class I myosins involved in membrane-cytoskeleton linkage; MYO6 (0.814), a minus-end directed myosin critical for endocytosis and autophagy; MYO9A (0.795), another unconventional myosin with RhoGAP activity; myosin light chains MYL6 (0.811), MYL6B (0.811), MYLPF (0.742), and MYL1 (0.401), essential for motor domain regulation; plus GRK3 (0.823), a G-protein coupled receptor kinase, and SEZ6L (0.816), a seizure-related protein. HPA expression shows striking tissue enhancement in striated muscle: skeletal muscle (251.6 nTPM), heart muscle (114.3 nTPM), and tongue (51.9 nTPM), with cancer-enhanced expression pattern and dual nucleoplasm/centrosome subcellular localization, suggesting roles in muscle contraction, cell division, and cytoskeletal organization. ClinVar reports 203 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including nonsense mutations (p.Arg1962Ter, p.Gln332Ter, p.Lys1480Ter), frameshift deletions (p.Ala852fs, p.Cys416fs, p.Leu2387fs, p.Ala2286fs), and splice variants (c.4435-1G>A, c.3885+1G>A), though no single monogenic disease is yet definitively assigned, suggesting MYO18B contributes to heterogeneous neuromuscular phenotypes. Literature (89 papers) reveals emerging functional characterization: (1) Lysosomal exocytosis—MYO18B promotes lysosomal secretion by facilitating focal adhesion maturation in cancer cells, linking paucimannose glycan production to invasive behavior; (2) Sarcomeric specialization—class 18 myosins (MYO18A/B) function as specialist sarcomeric proteins in striated muscle, diverging from ancestral roles; (3) Congenital myopathy—biallelic pathogenic variants cause early-onset muscle weakness with nemaline rod features; (4) Sensory polyneuropathy—novel pathogenic variants associate with generalized sensory nerve dysfunction; (5) Hepatocellular carcinoma—MYO18B transcription regulated by C19MC miRNA-520G, IFN-γ, CEBPB, p53, and bFGF network. Functional hypothesis: MYO18B functions through: (1) Actin-Based Transport—unconventional myosin motor activity transports lysosomes and vesicles to focal adhesions, enabling exocytosis and matrix remodeling; (2) Sarcomere Assembly—centrosome/nucleoplasm localization suggests roles in organizing contractile apparatus during myogenesis; (3) Mechanotransduction—large disordered regions enable scaffolding of adhesion complexes, transmitting mechanical signals; (4) Disease Mechanism—loss-of-function variants disrupt lysosomal trafficking and sarcomere integrity, causing congenital myopathy and neuropathy; (5) Cancer Progression—upregulated expression promotes invasive phenotypes via enhanced focal adhesion turnover. The combination of conserved myosin motor domains, striated muscle-specific expression, extensive pathogenic variant burden, and modest literature (confirming genuine dark gene status despite functional importance) positions MYO18B as a high-priority target for neuromuscular disease therapeutics and cancer metastasis inhibition.
