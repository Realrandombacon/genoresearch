## Description
LIPN encodes a 398 aa lipase member N (UniProt Q5VXI9), functioning as a secreted alpha/beta hydrolase lipase that regulates epidermal lipid barrier formation, ceramide metabolism, and skin homeostasis through hydrolysis of ester bonds in lipid substrates. The protein contains a canonical alpha/beta hydrolase fold-1 domain (IPR000073/PF00561, aa79-379) with hydrolase activity acting on ester bonds (GO:0016788), belongs to the eukaryotic lipase family (IPR025483/PIRSF000862/PTHR11005, aa1-397/aa32-395), and is classified within the alpha/beta hydrolase superfamily (G3DSA:3.40.50.1820/SSF53474/IPR029058, aa23-396/aa23-394). AlphaFold prediction shows very high confidence (pLDDT 91.9), indicating an exceptionally well-folded stable lipase structure with canonical catalytic triad geometry. HPA shows tissue-enriched expression with highest levels in skin (25.2 nTPM), classified as a predicted secreted protein involved in lipid degradation/metabolism, with disease involvement in Ichthyosis. STRING interactions reveal LIPN forms epidermal lipid metabolism networks: PCSK5 (0.892, proprotein convertase subtilisin/kexin type 5), SP6 (0.880, transcription factor Sp6), CYP4F22 (0.872, cytochrome P450 family 4 subfamily F member 22), ACRV1 (0.805, acrosomal vesicle protein 1), PNPLA1 (0.795, patatin-like phospholipase domain containing 1), MARVELD1 (0.795, MARVEL domain containing 1), ABCA12 (0.788, ATP binding cassette subfamily A member 12), MTRNR2L5 (0.786, MT-RNR2 like 5), SP9 (0.554, transcription factor Sp9), LRP5 (0.444, LDL receptor related protein 5). ClinVar contains 32 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including 10q23.31 copy number loss, 10q23.1-25.1/10q11.21-24.2/10p14-q26.3 copy number gains, deletions (NC_000010.10:g.90533969_90911342del), duplications, and missense variant c.731C>T (p.Thr244Ile). Literature (215 papers) includes substantial research on mycobacterial LipN homologs, but human LIPN specifically functions in epidermal lipid barrier formation. LIPN functions as a secreted epidermal lipase—its alpha/beta hydrolase catalytic domain hydrolyzes ester bonds in lipid substrates including ceramide precursors and eicosanoids critical for stratum corneum barrier function. The ultra-high confidence CYP4F22 interaction (0.872) is particularly significant: CYP4F22 is a cytochrome P450 enzyme that omega-hydroxylates very-long-chain fatty acids in ceramides, and CYP4F22 mutations cause autosomal recessive congenital ichthyosis. This places LIPN in the same pathogenic pathway for ichthyosis. The ABCA12 interaction (0.788) links LIPN to ATP-dependent lipid transporter function—ABCA12 mutations cause harlequin ichthyosis, the most severe form of congenital ichthyosis. The PNPLA1 interaction (0.795) connects LIPN to patatin-like phospholipase that also regulates epidermal lipid barrier, with PNPLA1 mutations causing ichthyosis. The PCSK5 interaction (0.892) suggests LIPN may be proteolytically processed by proprotein convertase for activation. SP6/SP9 interactions (0.880/0.554) link LIPN to transcriptional regulation by Sp-family zinc finger transcription factors controlling epidermal differentiation. The skin-enriched expression (25.2 nTPM) and secreted classification enable LIPN to function in extracellular lipid processing at the epidermal surface. Ichthyosis pathogenesis reflects LIPN loss disrupting ceramide/eicosanoid metabolism, compromising skin barrier integrity leading to hyperkeratosis and scaling. Copy number variants on 10q may cause LIPN dosage effects contributing to skin disorders or developmental abnormalities. The very high confidence structure (pLDDT 91.9) reflects a stable catalytic lipase fold with conserved catalytic triad (Ser-His-Asp/Glu) typical of alpha/beta hydrolases.
