## Description
EPN1 (Q9Y6I3) encodes a 576 amino acid clathrin adaptor protein that functions as a critical scaffold orchestrating clathrin-mediated endocytosis (CME), ubiquitin-dependent cargo sorting, and growth factor receptor signaling regulation. InterPro analysis reveals a modular architecture combining structured and disordered regions: an N-terminal Epsin N-Terminal Homology (ENTH) domain (IPR003903/IPR013809/PF01417/SM00273, positions 12-144) that binds phosphatidylinositol-4,5-bisphosphate (PI(4,5)P2) at the plasma membrane and induces membrane curvature through amphipathic helix insertion; three ubiquitin-interacting motifs (UIMs) (IPR003903/SM00726/PS50330, positions 183-202, 208-227, 233-252) enabling recognition of ubiquitinated cargo proteins for endocytic sorting; classified within the epsin family (PTHR12276, positions 3-540) and ENTH/VHS superfamily (IPR008942/G3DSA:1.25.40.90/SSF48464, positions 1-144). AlphaFold predicts a partially structured architecture (pLDDT 63.8, medium confidence) consistent with epsin's known biology—the structured ENTH domain provides stable membrane anchoring while extensive intrinsically disordered regions (IDRs) between UIM motifs enable conformational flexibility required for multi-protein complex assembly and phase separation at endocytic sites. STRING interactions demonstrate exclusive partnerships with endocytic machinery: EPS15 (0.939), the epidermal growth factor receptor pathway substrate 15 forming early endocytic complexes; CLTC (0.929) and CLTCL1 (0.921), clathrin heavy chains forming the vesicle coat; SNAP91 (0.908), synaptojanin-1 binding protein regulating vesicle uncoating; ITSN1 (0.825), intersectin-1 scaffold protein coordinating CME initiation; EPS15L1 (0.814), epsin-related protein; AP2A1 (0.756), adaptor protein complex 2 subunit; REPS2 (0.547), RALBP1-associated Eps domain protein; HIP1R (0.448), huntingtin-interacting protein 1 related; and EGFR (0.437), the epidermal growth factor receptor substrate. HPA shows ubiquitous expression across all tissues with low specificity, nucleoplasm/vesicles/plasma membrane/cytosol localization consistent with shuttling between membrane and cytosol during endocytic cycles, classified under endocytosis biological process. ClinVar contains 21 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number gains spanning chromosome 19q13.33-13.43 regions (e.g., chr19:49625130-57647352 duplications, chr19:50489390-59095359 gains) and losses (chr19:55247893-56503347 deletions), plus splice site variants (c.1178-54G>A, c.1066+220G>T), though specific monogenic disease associations remain undefined. Literature (43 papers) demonstrates KRT17 stabilizes EPN1 via inhibiting SMURF1-mediated ubiquitination to modulate Wnt/β-catenin signaling output and stem-like traits in ovarian cancer; EPN1 functions in alternative promoter-driven transcriptomic reprogramming in triple-negative breast cancer. The mechanistic hypothesis: EPN1 functions as a multi-valent scaffold coupling PI(4,5)P2-rich plasma membrane domains to clathrin coat assembly—the ENTH domain anchors epsin to membranes while inducing curvature through hydrophobic insertion; three UIMs simultaneously bind ubiquitinated cargo (e.g., activated EGFR) ensuring selective internalization of signaling receptors; disordered regions between structured domains enable conformational plasticity allowing epsin to bridge multiple interaction partners (EPS15, CLTC, AP2A1, ITSN1) into functional endocytic hubs; REPS2 and HIP1R interactions suggest coordination with RAB GTPase cycling and actin polymerization during vesicle scission; copy number gains on 19q may drive oncogenic programs through elevated receptor endocytosis altering growth factor signaling dynamics, while losses may impair synaptic vesicle recycling in neurons; medium-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 63.8) reflects biological reality—structured ENTH domain (high pLDDT) connected by flexible linkers (low pLDDT) to UIM motifs enabling dynamic cargo capture; KRT17-SMURF1-EPN1 axis in ovarian cancer suggests post-translational regulation of epsin stability controls Wnt/β-catenin pathway output influencing cancer stem cell maintenance.
