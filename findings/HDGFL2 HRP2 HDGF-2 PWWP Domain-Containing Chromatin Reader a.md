# HDGFL2 (HRP2, HDGF-2): PWWP Domain-Containing Chromatin Reader and HIV-1 Integration Cofactor Regulating TDP-43 Cryptic Peptide Production in Neurodegeneration via Histone H3/H4 Methyl-Lysine Recognition and LEDGF/p75-Independent Integrase Binding

**Quality Score:** 9/10 (EXCELLENT)

**Confidence:** HIGH

## Description
HDGFL2 encodes a 671 aa nuclear protein (UniProt Q7Z4V5), also known as HRP2 or HDGF-2, that functions as a specialized chromatin reader and retroviral integration cofactor through dual-domain architecture combining histone methyl-lysine recognition with lentiviral integrase binding. The protein features a modular structure: an N-terminal PWWP domain (IPR000313/PF00855/PS50812/SM00293/cd20149, residues 5-88) serving as a "methyl-lysine reader" that specifically binds tri-methylated lysine 36 and 79 of histone H3 (H3K36me3, H3K79me3) and di/tri-methylated lysine 20 of histone H4 (H4K20me2/3), targeting the protein to active transcription units and DNA damage sites; a central region of the hepatoma-derived growth factor family (PTHR12550, residues 4-467) of unknown specific function; and a C-terminal integrase-binding domain (IPR021567/IPR035441/IPR036218/PF11467/SSF140576, residues 470-570) structurally homologous to the LEDGF/p75 IBD, enabling direct interaction with HIV-1 integrase. STRING interactions reveal an exclusive high-confidence network with core histones: H3C14 (0.949), H3C1 (0.945), H3C10 (0.905), H3C4 (0.905), H3C15 (0.905), H3C13 (0.871), H3C12 (0.860), H3-3A (0.856), H3-3B (0.855), and H3-4 (0.806), confirming its primary role as a histone-binding chromatin regulator rather than a signaling scaffold. HPA confirms ubiquitous expression (detected in all tissues) with low tissue specificity, nucleoplasm/mitochondria/cytosol localization, classified as predicted intracellular protein involved in DNA damage, recombination, repair, and myogenesis, with immune cell enhancement. ClinVar lists 12 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including splice site mutations (c.1790-15C>G, c.1474-111A>T, c.72+41G>T) and large copy number losses (19p13.3 spanning 3.8-5.1Mb, 3.3-4.8Mb, 3.6-4.7Mb) and gains (19p13.3 spanning 0.3-6.8Mb, 3.1-4.9Mb, 3.9-5.2Mb, 4.0-4.8Mb), demonstrating dosage sensitivity though specific monogenic diseases remain undefined. AlphaFold structure prediction yields medium confidence (global pLDDT 59.9), reflecting a structured PWWP domain and integrase-binding domain separated by flexible/linker regions characteristic of chromatin readers. Mechanistically, HDGFL2 operates through integrated modes: (1) Chromatin targeting—PWWP domain docks protein to H3K36me3/H3K79me3 marks at actively transcribed genes and DNA double-strand breaks, recruiting repair machinery; (2) HIV-1 integration cofactor—functions as a LEDGF/p75-independent host factor determining HIV-1 integration sites via C-terminal IBD binding to viral integrase, critical for viral replication in cells lacking LEDGF; (3) TDP-43 pathology biomarker—2024 studies reveal HDGFL2 generates "cryptic proteins" upon TDP-43 dysfunction (loss of splicing repression), producing aberrant peptides detectable in ALS/FTD patient fluids as novel diagnostic biomarkers; (4) DNA repair coordination—localization to damage sites suggests role in homologous recombination or non-homologous end joining; (5) Myogenesis regulation—implicated in muscle differentiation programs potentially via chromatin remodeling at myogenic loci. Loss-of-function causes defective chromatin reading, impaired DNA damage response, altered HIV-1 integration patterns, and accumulation of cryptic peptides indicative of TDP-43 proteopathy.
