## Description
RNF144A (P50876) encodes a 292 amino acid RING-between-RING (RBR) type E3 ubiquitin-protein ligase that functions as a critical regulator of protein ubiquitination, stress granule assembly, and autophagy pathways. InterPro analysis reveals the canonical RBR E3 ligase architecture including a modified RING-type zinc finger (IPR001841, positions 20-66; cd16777, positions 18-72), dual IBR domains (IPR002867, positions 91-156 and 168-232; cd20366 BRcat domain at 99-163; cd20352 Rcat domain at 178-247), and the TRIAD supradomain (IPR044066, positions 16-236) characteristic of RBR family ubiquitin ligases. AlphaFold predicts a well-folded structure (pLDDT 81.2) consistent with stable catalytic activity and protein-protein interactions. STRING interactions identify high-confidence partners within the ubiquitination machinery: UBE2L3 (0.919), the cognate E2 conjugating enzyme that charges RNF144A with ubiquitin; UBC/UBB/UBA52 (0.543-0.684), ubiquitin sources; and EIPR1 (0.582), linking to endosomal trafficking. HPA shows tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in brain (54.5 nTPM), localized to vesicles, consistent with roles in neuronal protein homeostasis and stress responses. ClinVar contains 25 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly copy number gains on chromosome 2p25, though specific disease associations remain undefined. Literature (69 papers) demonstrates RNF144A regulates stress granule assembly via the VRK2-G3BP1 axis, inhibits autophagy by targeting BECN1 for degradation during bacterial infection, and mediates TSHR ubiquitination in pneumonia models. The mechanistic hypothesis: RNF144A functions as a stress-responsive E3 ligase that recognizes specific substrates (G3BP1, BECN1, TSHR) through its RBR catalytic domain, recruiting UBE2L3-charged ubiquitin to tag targets for proteasomal degradation or altered trafficking; under cellular stress, RNF144A modulates stress granule dynamics to balance cell survival versus death decisions, while in infection contexts it restricts autophagy to prevent pathogen exploitation of cellular degradation pathways; copy number gains may cause haploinsufficiency or dominant-negative effects disrupting protein quality control in neuronal tissues.
