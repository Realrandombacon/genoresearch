# PLCL2: Inactive Phospholipase C-Like Scaffold Regulating GABA Signaling and Cardiovascular Disease Risk

## Description
PLCL2 encodes a 1127 aa inactive phospholipase C-like protein (UniProt Q9UPR0) that functions as a molecular scaffold rather than an enzymatically active phospholipase. InterPro analysis reveals a multi-domain architecture characteristic of PLC-related proteins: N-terminal pleckstrin homology domain (cd13364, aa 143-251) for membrane targeting, EF-hand calcium-binding motifs (cd16223, aa 271-414), catalytically inactive PLC catalytic domain (cd08597, aa 425-721), and C-terminal C2 domain (IPR000008, aa 734-863) for calcium-dependent membrane association. AlphaFold predicts a well-folded structure (pLDDT 80.6), consistent with stable scaffold function. STRING interactions reveal associations with protein phosphatase 1 catalytic subunits PPP1CA (0.651) and PPP1CC (0.645), suggesting roles in dephosphorylation signaling cascades, plus INPP5A (0.408, inositol phosphatase), MTHFS (0.504), and DENND1B (0.403, Rab GEF). HPA shows tissue-enhanced expression with skeletal muscle enrichment (25.8 nTPM) and cancer-enhanced patterns. ClinVar reports 18 pathogenic variants, predominantly chromosome 3p copy number gains/deletions. Literature (39 papers) reveals emerging disease associations: (1) GWAS-identified susceptibility locus for myocardial infarction (rs4618210) and ischemic stroke in metabolic syndrome patients, (2) regulator of uterine contractility via Oxtr-Plcl2-Trpc3 pathway controlled by progesterone receptor isoform B, (3) dysregulation in Rosai-Dorfman disease through GSK3β/β-catenin signaling, (4) GABA receptor binding activity linking to neurotransmission. Mechanistically, PLCL2 likely serves as a calcium-sensitive scaffold that bridges membrane receptors (GABA, oxytocin) to downstream effectors (TRPC3 channels, protein phosphatases), modulating phosphoinositide signaling without catalytic activity. The cardiovascular and uterine phenotypes suggest tissue-specific roles in calcium signaling and smooth muscle regulation.
