## Description
PTGIR encodes prostacyclin receptor (IP/PRIPR), a 386 aa G-protein coupled receptor family member (UniProt P43119) belonging to the rhodopsin-like GPCR superfamily that serves as primary receptor for prostacyclin (PGI2). InterPro reveals canonical class A GPCR architecture: rhodopsin 7-helix transmembrane domain (G3DSA:1.20.1070.10, residues 6-324), GPCR rhodopsin-like family (IPR000276/PF00001, residues 30-291) mediating ligand binding and G-protein coupling, prostacyclin receptor-specific family (IPR000370/PR00856, residues 4-24/40-55/76-91) conferring PGI2 selectivity over other prostanoids, prostanoid receptor family (IPR008365, residues 20-318), and GPCR family 1 signature (PS00237, residues 105-121) essential for receptor activation. AlphaFold predicts high confidence structure (pLDDT 76.2) consistent with well-folded 7-transmembrane helix bundle typical of class A GPCRs with structured extracellular ligand-binding pocket and intracellular G-protein coupling interface. STRING reveals functional clustering with prostacyclin pathway components and G-protein signaling: GNAS (0.991, Gαs subunit mediating cAMP production), PTGIS (0.832, prostacyclin synthase producing PGI2 ligand), TBXA2R (0.825, thromboxane receptor with opposing function), PTGS1/COX1 (0.753) and PTGS2/COX2 (0.672, cyclooxygenases producing prostaglandin precursors), PDZK1 (0.622, scaffolding protein regulating receptor trafficking), SRC (0.620, tyrosine kinase downstream effector), plus other G-protein subunits GNG8/GNGT2 and leukotriene receptor CYSLTR2. HPA shows tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in blood vessel (72.9 nTPM) and lung (55.0 nTPM), group-enriched in blood cells, cell type-enriched specificity, classified as FDA-approved drug target/G-protein coupled receptor/membrane protein. ClinVar lists 11 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants consisting entirely of copy number variations including gains at 19q13.32-13.33/19q13.31-13.42/19q11-13.33/19p13.3-q13.43 and losses at 19q13.32-13.33/19q13.2-13.32 though specific monogenic disease phenotypes remain undefined suggesting haploinsufficiency may contribute to vascular dysfunction syndromes. Functional mechanism: PTGIR operates through canonical Gs-coupled signaling cascade where PGI2 binding to extracellular N-terminal domain and transmembrane pocket induces conformational change transmitted to intracellular loops, activating Gαs which stimulates adenylyl cyclase increasing cAMP, activating PKA which phosphorylates targets causing: (1) vascular smooth muscle relaxation via myosin light chain kinase inhibition, (2) platelet aggregation inhibition via VASP phosphorylation preventing integrin activation, (3) endothelial barrier enhancement via VE-cadherin stabilization. Recent literature reveals expanded disease relevance beyond canonical vasodilation: (1) CD8+ T cell exhaustion in cancer via NRF2-dependent PTGIR upregulation driving exhausted phenotype reversible by PTGIR antagonism (2025/10 citations), (2) intestinal fibrosis in Crohn's disease through fibroblast-specific YAP/TAZ inhibition where restored PGI2-PTGIR signaling reduces fibrosis (2025/2 citations)
