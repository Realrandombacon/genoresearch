# Secreted Chaperone FGFBP3 Mobilizing FGFs for Metabolic Regulation in Brain and Diabetes

## Description
FGFBP3 (Fibroblast Growth Factor Binding Protein 3) is a 258-amino acid secreted protein functioning as a specific chaperone that mobilizes locally-acting paracrine fibroblast growth factors (FGFs) from the extracellular matrix (ECM) and heparan sulfate proteoglycan storage pools, thereby potentiating FGF receptor signaling. InterPro analysis confirms a dedicated FGF-binding domain architecture: IPR010510 (FGF binding 1, positions 12-258), PF06473 (FGF binding protein 1 family, 14-258), and PTHR15258, with a predicted N-terminal signal peptide (PS51257, 1-21) directing secretion. STRING interactions reveal a focused network centered on FGF ligands and receptors: FGFR2 (0.609), FGF10 (0.584), FGF1 (0.572), FGF7 (0.543), FGF2 (0.539), FGF3 (0.534), and FGF22 (0.427), alongside uncharacterized partners ARHGEF33, HDHD3, and MDFIC2, suggesting a role in modulating specific FGF signaling axes rather than broad sequestration. HPA data shows tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in brain (10.2 nTPM), cancer-enhanced RNA patterns, immune cell enhancement in blood, and classification as a predicted secreted protein with growth factor binding and heparin-binding molecular functions. ClinVar contains 15 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains and losses spanning 10q23-26 regions, though specific monogenic disease phenotypes remain under characterization (significance marked '?' for most); the genomic location overlaps with regions implicated in metabolic and neurodevelopmental disorders. AlphaFold structure (AF-Q8TAT2-F1) predicts medium confidence folding (pLDDT 68.1) consistent with a partially structured secreted chaperone containing both ordered binding interfaces and flexible linkers typical of ECM-interacting proteins. With only 24 papers, FGFBP3 represents a 'gray' gene transitioning from dark: key studies (2018) demonstrate its critical role in modulating fat and glucose metabolism in mouse models, with commercialization efforts for treating diabetes and obesity; recent proteomic analyses (2025) link FGFBP3 levels to Type 2 Diabetes etiology and hyperthyroidism complications. The mechanistic hypothesis posits that FGFBP3 acts as a rate-limiting 'releaser' of bioactive FGFs (particularly FGF1, FGF2, FGF10) in the brain and peripheral tissues, fine-tuning metabolic homeostasis and neuronal development by controlling the spatial availability of these potent morphogens, making it a promising therapeutic target for metabolic syndrome and potentially neurodegenerative conditions linked to FGF signaling deficits.
