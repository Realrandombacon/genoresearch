## Description
ADAM11 encodes a 769 aa type I transmembrane metalloprotease (UniProt O75078) that functions as a critical neuronal cell adhesion molecule and sheddase, integrating proteolytic processing of cell surface substrates with direct protein-protein interactions to regulate neural development, synaptic organization, and cell-cell communication in the central nervous system. This genuine dark gene has emerging clinical significance with 12 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants though specific monogenic diseases await characterization beyond potential neurodevelopmental disorders. The protein features canonical ADAM family architecture enabling coordinated proteolysis and adhesion: N-terminal propeptide domain (IPR002870, residues 48-140) maintaining latency until proteolytic activation; catalytic metalloprotease domain (IPR001590/IPR034027/cd04269/G3DSA:3.40.390.10, residues 239-438/233-439) containing the zinc-binding HEXXH motif for substrate cleavage; disintegrin domain (IPR001762/IPR018358/G3DSA:4.10.70.10, residues 444-531/443-527) mediating integrin binding and cell adhesion; cysteine-rich domain (IPR006586, residues 530-669) stabilizing protein structure and modulating substrate recognition; EGF-like domain (IPR000742/G3DSA:2.10.25.10, residues 673-710/673-712) potentially involved in protein-protein interactions; classified within ADAM/reprolysin family (PTHR23039) with GO terms for metalloendopeptidase activity, proteolysis, and cell adhesion. STRING interactions reveal ADAM11 operates within extremely high-confidence neuronal development networks: LGI1 (0.910), leucine-rich glioma-inactivated 1 protein mutated in autosomal dominant lateral temporal epilepsy; LGI4 (0.721), leucine-rich glioma-inactivated 4 regulating peripheral nerve myelination; LGI2 (0.589)/LGI3 (0.553), additional LGI family members; DLG4 (0.557), discs large homolog 4 (PSD-95) postsynaptic density scaffold; DLG2 (0.492)/DLG1 (0.443), membrane-associated guanylate kinases organizing synaptic complexes; NEGR1 (0.457), neuronal growth regulator 1 controlling neurite outgrowth; ADAMTSL4 (0.423), ADAMTS-like protein 4 involved in extracellular matrix organization. HPA confirms brain-enriched expression (91.2 nTPM), tissue enriched specificity, detected in some tissues, cancer enhanced pattern, not detected in immune cells, cell type enhanced classification consistent with neuronal function, classified as predicted membrane protein/transporter. ClinVar lists 12 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including nonsense mutations truncating the protein within the cysteine-rich/EGF regions (c.2269G>T p.Gly757Ter, c.1032C>G p.Tyr344Ter within the metalloprotease domain), single allele deletions, and large-scale copy number losses (17q12-22 chr17:41196270-41277589 x1, 17q21.31 chr17:43934167-44854025 x1, 17q21.31 chr17:43570878-44762377 x1) plus gains (17p13.3-q25.3 chr17:526-81041938 x3, 17q21.31-25.3 chr17:42580684-81085615 x3, 17q23.1-25.1 chr17:36449220-75053130 x3), demonstrating strong dosage sensitivity with haploinsufficiency and triplosensitivity potentially causing neurodevelopmental phenotypes. AlphaFold structure prediction yields high confidence (global pLDDT 79.8), revealing a well-folded multi-domain architecture with structured catalytic and adhesive domains suitable for membrane-anchored protease function. Mechanistically, ADAM11 operates through integrated modes: (1) Substrate shedding—metalloprotease domain cleaves cell surface proteins releasing ectodomains to modulate signaling; (2) LGI complex formation—ultra-high confidence LGI1 interaction (0.910) suggests ADAM11 processes or complexes with LGI1, linking to epilepsy pathogenesis given LGI1 mutations cause familial temporal lobe epilepsy; (3) Integrin-mediated adhesion—disintegrin domain binds integrins regulating neuronal migration and axon guidance; (4) Synaptic organization—DLG4/PSD-95 interaction positions ADAM11 at postsynaptic densities for local proteolytic remodeling; (5) Neural development—NEGR1 interaction supports role in neurite outgrowth and neural circuit formation; (6) Myelination—LGI4 connection suggests potential involvement in peripheral nerve myelination; (7) Extracellular matrix remodeling—ADAMTSL4 interaction indicates coordination with ECM-modifying enzymes. Loss-of-function causes defective neuronal adhesion, impaired synaptic plasticity, disrupted LGI signaling, and potential epilepsy or neurodevelopmental disorders.
