## Description
KANSL3 encodes KAT8 regulatory NSL complex subunit 3 (NSL3/Rcd1), a 904 aa epigenetic regulator (UniProt Q9P2N6) belonging to the non-specific lethal (NSL) complex that mediates histone H4 lysine acetylation. InterPro reveals distinctive domain architecture: KANSL3/Tex30 family domain (IPR026555, residues 11-473), alpha/beta hydrolase fold (IPR029058/G3DSA:3.40.50.1820/SSF53474, residues 264-445), KANSL3-specific alpha/beta hydrolase-like domain (IPR046879/PF20408, residues 333-451), and KANSL3 helical domain (IPR056519/PF23154, residues 134-235). AlphaFold predicts medium-confidence structure (pLDDT 60.1) suggesting mixed ordered/disordered regions typical of chromatin regulators. STRING reveals obligate NSL complex membership: KANSL2 (0.999), KANSL1 (0.995), KAT8/MYST1 (0.984, catalytic histone acetyltransferase), plus associated chromatin modifiers PHF20L1 (0.847), MCRS1 (0.827), PHF20 (0.747), WDR5 (0.690, H3K4 methyltransferase component), HCFC1 (0.688), OGT (0.614, O-GlcNAc transferase), and MSL3 (0.588). HPA shows ubiquitous expression with nucleoplasmic/vesicular localization, classified as essential/chromatin regulator/metabolic protein. ClinVar lists 33 pathogenic variants including copy number losses/gains at 2q11.1-11.2. Functional mechanism: KANSL3 scaffolds the NSL complex to target promoters, recruiting KAT8 for broad-spectrum H4K5/K8/K12/K16 acetylation independent of specific sequence motifs. Recent literature reveals tissue-specific functions: TRAP1-mediated inhibition of KANSL3 acetylation promotes mitophagy in diabetic cardiomyopathy (2025/3 citations), hepatic metabolism/differentiation programs essential for liver health (2025), inner cell mass formation and early embryonic viability (2024/2 citations), and rare variant associations with Alzheimer\'s/Parkinson\'s disease risk (2026). Despite mechanistic understanding of NSL complex assembly, molecular basis for mitochondrial transcription regulation, tissue-specific target gene selection, and connection between histone acetylation defects and neurodegeneration remain incompletely characterized - representing genuine dark aspects of this epigenetic regulator.
