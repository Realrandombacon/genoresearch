## Description
ZNF700 encodes a 742 amino acid Krüppel-associated box (KRAB) domain-containing zinc finger protein localized to the nucleoplasm. The protein architecture features an N-terminal KRAB repressor domain (PF01352, residues 23-64, IPR001909) followed by three tandem C2H2-type zinc finger DNA-binding domains (PF00096, residues 194-272) that recognize specific genomic sequences. AlphaFold predicts a well-folded structure with global pLDDT 72.1, indicating confident structural modeling across the full-length protein. HPA data shows ubiquitous expression across all tissues with nucleoplasmic localization, consistent with transcriptional regulatory function. STRING interactions reveal functional associations with chromatin modifiers TRIM28 (KAP1, score 0.522) and TADA2A (ADA2A histone acetyltransferase complex, score 0.418), plus other zinc finger proteins ZNF638 (0.486), ZNF346 (0.404), GPBP1L1 (0.576), and C6orf223 (0.410). This interaction network suggests ZNF700 operates within heterochromatin formation complexes, likely recruiting TRIM28/KAP1 to silence target genes through histone methylation. ClinVar documents 12 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly copy number losses and gains spanning 19p13.2, implicating ZNF700 dosage sensitivity in human disease. Conservation analysis shows the KRAB-ZNF architecture is deeply conserved across vertebrates, with orthologs in primates maintaining the domain organization. The combination of repressor domain, DNA-binding specificity, chromatin modifier interactions, and disease-associated variants supports a model where ZNF700 functions as sequence-specific transcriptional silencer regulating developmentally critical genes, with haploinsufficiency or overexpression contributing to chromatin dysregulation phenotypes.
