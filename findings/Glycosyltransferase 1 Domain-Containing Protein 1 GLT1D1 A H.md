## Description
GLT1D1 encodes a 346 aa intracellular enzyme (UniProt Q96MS3) characterized by a canonical Glycosyl Transferase Group 1 domain (IPR001296, PF00534, residues 159-318) and a broader phosphatidyl-myo-inositol mannosyltransferase-like fold (cd03801, residues 41-338). The protein belongs to the GT-B fold superfamily (SSF53756), typical of enzymes that transfer sugar moieties from activated donor molecules to specific acceptors. Unlike many dark genes with disordered regions, GLT1D1 exhibits an exceptionally high-confidence AlphaFold structure (global pLDDT 95.7), indicating a stable, well-folded catalytic core suitable for precise substrate binding. STRING interaction analysis reveals moderate-confidence associations (scores 0.40-0.47) with TMEM132D/C, PRORP, SLC15A4, and LRMDA, suggesting potential roles in membrane-associated glycosylation or lysosomal trafficking, though no high-confidence hub partners were identified. HPA data shows tissue-enhanced expression peaking in bone marrow (26.5 nTPM), liver (25.6 nTPM), and choroid plexus (20.0 nTPM), with specific enrichment in immune cells, hinting at a role in immune cell surface glycan modulation or glycolipid synthesis. ClinVar lists 20 pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains on chromosome 12q24, implicating dosage sensitivity in disease etiology, potentially affecting neurodevelopment or immune function given the choroid plexus and bone marrow expression. Mechanistically, GLT1D1 likely functions as a mannosyl- or glucosyl-transferase modifying cytosolic or organellar substrates, possibly influencing protein stability, signaling complex assembly, or cell-cell recognition in hematopoietic lineages.
