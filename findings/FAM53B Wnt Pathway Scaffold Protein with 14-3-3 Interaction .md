## Description
FAM53B encodes a 422 amino acid nuclear/cytosolic protein functioning as a positive regulator of canonical Wnt signaling. The protein contains a conserved FAM53 family domain (IPR029356, PF15242, residues 1-302) present across vertebrates. AlphaFold prediction (pLDDT 52.4) indicates a partially disordered structure typical of scaffold/adaptor proteins. STRING analysis reveals a protein interaction network centered on 14-3-3 family members: SFN (0.612), YWHAE (0.595), YWHAZ (0.556), suggesting FAM53B acts as a phospho-dependent scaffolding hub. Additional partners include SNW1 (splicing coactivator, 0.510), ZXDC (transcription factor, 0.483), and chromatin regulators CNIH3 (0.550). HPA shows ubiquitous expression across all tissues with low specificity, consistent with housekeeping Wnt pathway function. ClinVar documents 56 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly copy number losses/gains at 10q26.13, implicating FAM53B dosage in disease. Conservation is strong: mouse ortholog (Q8BGR5) shares 422 aa length, zebrafish (F1QN48) has 379 aa, indicating ~90% sequence conservation. The mechanistic hypothesis: FAM53B recruits 14-3-3 dimers to Wnt signaling complexes via phosphoserine motifs, stabilizing β-catenin/TCF transcriptional machinery in the nucleus. Disordered regions enable conformational flexibility for multi-protein assembly. CNV pathogenicity suggests precise FAM53B stoichiometry is critical for Wnt-dependent development and tissue homeostasis.
