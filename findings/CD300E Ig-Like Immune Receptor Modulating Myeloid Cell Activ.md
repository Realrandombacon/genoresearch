## Description
CD300E (CMRF35-like molecule 2, also known as LMIR3) is a 205 amino acid type I transmembrane immune receptor belonging to the CD300 family of myeloid cell surface molecules. InterPro analysis reveals a canonical immunoglobulin V-set domain architecture (IPR003599, IPR007110, IPR013106, PF07686, residues 20-106; G3DSA:2.60.40.10, residues 13-141; SSF48726, residues 18-133) characteristic of pathogen recognition receptors, with specific classification within the CD300 family immune receptors (IPR050671, PTHR11860, residues 1-187). AlphaFold structure (AF-Q496F6-F1) predicts high confidence (pLDDT 77.8) across the entire 205 residue sequence, indicating a stable Ig-like fold typical of cell surface recognition molecules. HPA data classifies CD300E as a tissue-enhanced receptor with group-enriched expression in bone marrow (45.3 nTPM) and lymphoid tissue (35.3 nTPM), with blood cell specificity confirming its restriction to myeloid lineage cells. STRING interactions reveal tight functional coupling to the CD300 receptor family: CD300LB (0.768), CD300LF (0.690), CD300A (0.629), CD300LD (0.610), and CD300C (0.446), forming a coordinated network of activating and inhibitory signals. Additional connections to FCER1G (0.554), the Fc receptor gamma chain; TYROBP (DAP12, 0.490), the signaling adaptor; and TREM1/TREML4 (0.436-0.484), related triggering receptors, place CD300E within the activating receptor complex that transduces signals through ITAM motifs. ClinVar lists 12 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains on chromosome 17q24-25, though no specific monogenic disease is yet defined. Literature (72 papers) identifies CD300E as a critical regulator of macrophage activation, facilitating liver regeneration after splenectomy in cirrhotic patients, driving immunosuppressive tumor microenvironments in colorectal cancer through macrophage reprogramming, and modulating obesity-induced inflammation via monocyte-T cell interplay. As an understudied immune checkpoint molecule, CD300E represents a potential therapeutic target for manipulating myeloid cell function in cancer, metabolic disease, and regenerative medicine.
