## Description
LAMTOR2 (Late Endosomal/Lysosomal Adaptor, MAPK and MTOR Activator 2; UniProt Q9Y2Q5) is a 125 amino acid essential scaffold protein of the Ragulator complex with 63 publications, representing a moderately characterized component of nutrient-sensing mTORC1 signaling with emerging roles in cancer metabolism, macrophage immunity, and xenophagy.\n\nSTRUCTURAL ARCHITECTURE: InterPro analysis reveals compact Roadblock/LC7 domain architecture optimized for protein-protein interactions within the Ragulator-mTORC1 supercomplex. The entire protein comprises a single Roadblock/LAMTOR2 domain (IPR004942/PF03259/SM00960, residues 7-95/8-95) belonging to the dynein light chain 2a cytoplasmic fold (G3DSA:3.30.450.30, residues 1-125). This domain belongs to the Ragulator complex protein LAMTOR2-like family (IPR037587/PTHR13323, residues 1-125) with GO annotations for guanyl-nucleotide exchange factor activity (GO:0005085), molecular adaptor activity (GO:0060090), and positive regulation of TOR signaling (GO:0032008). AlphaFold predicts very high confidence structure (global pLDDT 91.4), indicating an exceptionally well-folded compact scaffold—consistent with its role as a stable structural component of the pentameric Ragulator complex that must maintain precise geometry for Rag GTPase binding and mTORC1 recruitment.\n\nINTERACTION NETWORK: STRING-DB identifies exclusively ultra-high-confidence interactions with Ragulator complex components and mTORC1 regulators: RRAGB (0.999, Ras-related GTP-binding protein B, RagB), LAMTOR3 (0.999, Ragulator complex protein LAMTOR3/p18), SLC38A9 (0.999, solute carrier family 38 member 9, lysosomal amino acid sensor), RRAGC (0.999, Ras-related GTP-binding protein C, RagC), RRAGA (0.999, Ras-related GTP-binding protein A, RagA), LAMTOR1 (0.963, Ragulator complex protein LAMTOR1/MP1, membrane anchor), LAMTOR4 (0.953, Ragulator complex protein LAMTOR4/p14), LAMTOR5 (0.952, Ragulator complex protein LAMTOR5/p18), MTOR (0.852, mechanistic target of rapamycin kinase), and RPTOR (0.778, regulatory associated protein of MTOR, mTORC1 scaffold). This interaction profile establishes LAMTOR2 as a core Ragulator subunit that bridges Rag GTPases (RRAGA/B/C/D) to the lysosomal membrane via LAMTOR1, enabling amino acid-dependent mTORC1 activation through RPTOR binding.\n\nEXPRESSION & LOCALIZATION: HPA shows ubiquitous expression detected in all tissues with low tissue and cancer specificity, consistent with fundamental requirements for nutrient sensing and metabolic regulation across all cell types. Subcellular localization includes nucleoplasm and vesicles (late endosomes/lysosomes where Ragulator anchors). Protein class includes disease-related genes, essential proteins, and predicted intracellular proteins. Single cell type specificity is cell type-enhanced.\n\nDISEASE LANDSCAPE: ClinVar contains 18 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number gains at 1q21.1-25.3, 1q21.1-44, 1q21.3-22, 1q22-23.2, 1q21.1-23.1, 1q21.1-23.1, 1q12-23.1, and 1q130980840-248900000, plus copy number losses at 1q21.3-23.1 and deletions encompassing LAMTOR2 at 1q21. No monogenic disease is yet definitively assigned, but 1q CNVs cause developmental anomalies and intellectual disability. Recent 2020-2025 literature establishes prominent roles: VSIG2 promotes pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma malignant progression by enhancing LAMTOR2-mediated mTOR activation (2023, 13 citations); microbiota-derived short-chain fatty acids promote LAMTOR2-mediated immune responses in macrophages against Klebsiella pneumoniae (2020, 68 citations); LAMTOR2/LAMTOR1 complex required for TAX1BP1-mediated xenophagy against Group A Streptococcus (2019, 27 citations); BCLAF1 regulates osteoarthritic cartilage degradation through interaction with LAMTOR2 (2025, 2 citations).\n\nCONSERVATION: Orthologs confirmed in mouse (Q9JHS3, 125 aa) and Drosophila melanogaster (Q9V8I2, 125 aa), indicating strong evolutionary constraint on the Roadblock domain architecture across metazoans for lysosomal nutrient sensing.\n\nFUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: LAMTOR2 functions as an essential structural scaffold within the pentameric Ragulator complex (LAMTOR1-5) that anchors Rag GTPases to the lysosomal surface and catalyzes their nucleotide loading for mTORC1 activation. In amino acid-replete conditions, LAMTOR2 cooperates with LAMTOR3 to form a heterodimer that binds RRAGA-RRAGB or RRAGC-RRAGD heterodimers, positioning them for guanine nucleotide exchange. The LAMTOR1 subunit anchors the entire complex to the lysosomal membrane via palmitoylation, while SLC38A9 senses intralysosomal arginine and transmits this signal to Ragulator. When Rag GTPases are loaded with GTP (RRAGA/B) and GDP (RRAGC/D), they recruit mTORC1 (via RPTOR) to the lysosomal surface where Rheb-GTP activates the kinase. This triggers anabolic processes (protein synthesis, lipid biosynthesis, nucleotide synthesis) while inhibiting catabolism (autophagy). In cancer, LAMTOR2 is hijacked to sustain mTORC1 signaling: VSIG2 overexpression in pancreatic cancer enhances LAMTOR2-mediated mTOR activation, driving proliferation and metastasis. In macrophages, LAMTOR2 mediates SCFA-induced immune responses against bacterial pathogens, linking nutrient sensing to innate immunity. The LAMTOR2-LAMTOR1-TAX1BP1 axis enables xenophagy, selectively targeting intracellular bacteria for autophagic degradation. Therapeutic targeting of LAMTOR2-Rag GTPase interfaces could disrupt oncogenic mTORC1 signaling while sparing basal metabolic functions.
