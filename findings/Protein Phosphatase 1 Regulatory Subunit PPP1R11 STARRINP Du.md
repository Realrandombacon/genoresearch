## Description
PPP1R11 (Protein Phosphatase 1 Regulatory Subunit 11), also known as STARRINP or Inhibitor-3 (I-3), is a 126-amino acid nuclear protein with dual functionality as both a type 1 protein phosphatase inhibitor and an E3 ubiquitin-protein ligase. InterPro analysis reveals a canonical protein phosphatase inhibitor domain (IPR011107, positions 10-125; PF07491, 30-80; PTHR20835, 10-125) conferring protein serine/threonine phosphatase inhibitor activity (GO:0004865), enabling regulation of PP1 catalytic subunits through direct binding and inhibition. STRING interactions demonstrate tight functional coupling with PP1 holoenzyme components: PPP1CB (0.955, high confidence catalytic subunit beta), PPP1R2 (0.767, inhibitor-2), PPP1CC/PPP1CA (0.515/0.444, other catalytic subunits), alongside ubiquitin-related partners UBXN2B (0.805), UBXN2A (0.719), TRIM26 (0.716, E3 ligase), VCP (0.517, valosin-containing protein), and surprisingly HLA-A (0.722, MHC class I antigen), suggesting unexpected connections to antigen presentation and immune surveillance. HPA database lacks expression data for this gene, indicating potential underrepresentation in standard proteomic atlases. ClinVar contains 7 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number gains spanning 6p25.3-q27 regions and deletions, though specific monogenic disease phenotypes remain under characterization (significance marked '?' for most); the chromosomal location overlaps with regions implicated in developmental disorders and cancer susceptibility. AlphaFold structure (AF-O60927-F1) predicts medium confidence folding (pLDDT 65.9) consistent with a partially structured small protein containing both ordered inhibitor domains and flexible regions typical of regulatory proteins. With 36 papers, PPP1R11 represents a 'gray' gene transitioning from dark: recent breakthrough studies (2024-2026) identify critical roles in cancer therapy resistance—glycolysis-regulated exosomal LINC01214 inhibits CD8+ T cell function and induces anti-PD1 resistance in melanoma via the miR-4492/PPP1R11 axis, while POU2F1 promotes chemoresistance in colorectal cancer by attenuating MDR2 degradation mediated by PPP1R11 lactylation; earlier work established roles in male fertility (bull/yak breeding, Sertoli cell proliferation/apoptosis via miR-34b-5p targeting). The mechanistic hypothesis posits that PPP1R11 functions as a phosphorylation-dependent molecular switch: under basal conditions it inhibits PP1 to maintain specific substrates in phosphorylated states regulating cell cycle and apoptosis, while under stress (hypoxia, glycolysis) it undergoes post-translational modifications (lactylation) enabling E3 ligase activity that targets key proteins (MDR2, immune regulators) for degradation, thereby coordinating metabolic adaptation with therapeutic resistance and immune evasion.
