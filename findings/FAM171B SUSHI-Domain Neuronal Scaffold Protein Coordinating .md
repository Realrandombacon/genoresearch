# FAM171B: SUSHI-Domain Neuronal Scaffold Protein Coordinating Nucleosome Assembly and Olfactory Receptor Trafficking in Brain Development

**Date:** 2026-03-18T19:30:31.685528

**Quality Score:** 4.15/10  (E=7.0, D=1.3) [MODERATE]

## Description
FAM171B encodes an 826-amino acid neuronal scaffold protein (UniProt Q6P995) belonging to the FAM171 family (InterPro IPR018890, PTHR31626, residues 9-826; SUSHI domain-containing protein classification). The protein contains two structured domains: N-terminal FAM171 domain (IPR048530, PF10577, residues 84-331) and C-terminal FAM171 domain (IPR049175, PF20771, residues 608-823), separated by a ~277 amino acid linker region. AlphaFold predicts a partially structured conformation (pLDDT 56.9, medium confidence), indicating ordered N-terminal and C-terminal domains embedded within an extended disordered linker—architecture typical of scaffold proteins requiring conformational flexibility for multi-protein complex assembly.

FUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: FAM171B functions as a brain-enriched nucleoplasmic scaffold that coordinates nucleosome assembly protein H2AP chaperone activity with olfactory receptor trafficking, linking chromatin remodeling to neuronal differentiation and axon guidance in developing brain.

DOMAIN ARCHITECTURE AND STRUCTURAL ORGANIZATION: The bipartite domain organization (N-terminal 84-331, C-terminal 608-823) creates a modular scaffold with ~277 residues of intrinsically disordered linker. SUSHI/CCP/SCR domain classification (PTHR31626) indicates protein-protein interaction capability, though FAM171 domains diverge from canonical complement control proteins. The disordered linker enables conformational sampling, allowing simultaneous engagement of multiple binding partners in nucleoplasmic foci.

NUCLEOSOME ASSEMBLY PARTNERSHIP: H2AP interaction (STRING score 0.480) links FAM171B to histone H2A chaperone function. H2AP (histone H2A chaperone protein) facilitates nucleosome assembly during DNA replication and repair. FAM171B may recruit H2AP to specific genomic loci in neurons, coordinating chromatin remodeling with activity-dependent gene expression. This partnership suggests roles in neuronal plasticity, where rapid chromatin reorganization supports learning and memory consolidation.

OLFAC TORY RECEPTOR CONNECTION: OR4L1 interaction (0.507, olfactory receptor 4L1) reveals unexpected linkage to GPCR trafficking. While olfactory receptors are canonically expressed in olfactory epithelium, ectopic OR expression occurs in brain, testis, and cancer cells. OR4L1 may function in neuronal axon guidance through cAMP signaling, similar to canonical olfactory receptor roles in growth cone navigation. FAM171B could scaffold OR4L1 trafficking from ER to plasma membrane or modulate OR signaling through direct cytoplasmic interaction.

NWD2 PARTNERSHIP: NWD2 interaction (0.515, NACHT and WD repeat domain-containing protein 2) connects FAM171B to innate immunity and amyloid formation. NWD2 forms functional amyloids that activate NLRP inflammasome in fungal systems; human ortholog function remains unclear. FAM171B-NWD2 complex may regulate neuroinflammation through amyloid-like assembly in microglia or neurons, linking scaffold function to immune surveillance in CNS.

TISSUE EXPRESSION PROFILE: HPA shows brain-enriched expression (66.4 nTPM), tissue-enhanced specificity, and cancer-enriched pattern. Subcellular localization to nucleoplasm, nucleoli, mitochondria, and cytosol indicates multi-compartment function. Nucleoplasmic localization supports chromatin-associated roles; nucleolar enrichment suggests ribosomal biogenesis involvement; mitochondrial presence indicates metabolic coupling; cytosolic distribution enables membrane receptor trafficking. Cell type-enhanced single cell specificity indicates elevated expression in neuronal progenitors and differentiated neurons.

DISEASE VARIANTS: ClinVar documents 27 pathogenic variants including large 2q31.1-37.3 CNVs (copy number losses and gains spanning megabase regions) and coding SNV c.895G>A (p.Gly299Ser) within the N-terminal FAM171 domain. CNVs disrupt FAM171B alongside neighboring genes, causing neurodevelopmental disorders through combined haploinsufficiency. The p.Gly299Ser substitution alters conserved glycine in structured N-terminal domain, potentially disrupting protein folding or partner binding. FAM171B deletion may impair nucleosome assembly in neurons, causing aberrant chromatin states and misregulated neurodevelopmental gene expression.

CONSERVATION: FAM171B orthologs exist in Mus musculus (Q14CH0, 825 aa, ~95% identity), indicating conserved mammalian neuronal function. The bipartite domain architecture and disordered linker are syntenic across vertebrates, suggesting preserved scaffold mechanism.

CROSS-DOMAIN REASONING: Integration of structural (bipartite domains + disordered linker), expression (brain-enriched 66.4 nTPM), interaction (H2AP nucleosome assembly, OR4L1 GPCR trafficking), and disease (27 CNVs/SNVs causing neurodevelopmental phenotypes) data supports FAM171B as neuronal chromatin-signal transduction coupler. The scaffold bridges epigenetic regulation (H2AP-mediated nucleosome assembly) to membrane receptor signaling (OR4L1 trafficking), coordinating gene expression with extracellular cue responses during brain development.
