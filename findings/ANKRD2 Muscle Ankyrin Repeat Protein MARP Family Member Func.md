# ANKRD2: Muscle Ankyrin Repeat Protein (MARP) Family Member Functioning as Mechanosensitive Scaffold in Sarcomeric Stress Response

## Description
ANKRD2 (Ankyrin Repeat Domain 2, UniProt Q9GZV1, also known as ARPP) is a 360-amino acid protein belonging to the muscle ankyrin repeat protein (MARP) family, which functions as a mechanosensitive transcriptional co-regulator in striated muscle. The protein contains multiple ankyrin repeat domains (IPR002110, residues 148-311; IPR036770 superfamily, residues 146-326) organized in tandem arrays that mediate protein-protein interactions critical for sarcomeric assembly and stress signaling. AlphaFold predicts a confident structure (pLDDT=76.8) with well-ordered ankyrin repeats forming a solenoid architecture typical of this fold. HPA RNA-seq reveals extreme muscle-specific expression with skeletal muscle at 1141.9 nTPM, tongue at 769.1 nTPM, and heart muscle at 495.5 nTPM — among the highest tissue-enriched expression levels observed, consistent with its role as a core sarcomeric component. STRING interaction network identifies high-confidence associations with titin/TTN (0.969), telethonin/TCAP (0.941), myopalladin/MYPN (0.748), Y-box binding protein 1/YBX1 (0.745), muscle LIM protein/CSRP3 (0.735), Z-band alternatively spliced PDZ-containing protein/LDB3 (0.721), myozenin-2/MYOZ2 (0.708), beta-myosin heavy chain/MYH7 (0.671), embryonic myosin/MYH3 (0.656), and tumor suppressor p53/TP53 (0.575). These interactions place ANKRD2 within the I-band region of the sarcomere where it bridges structural elements (titin, telethonin) with signaling molecules (YBX1, p53). ClinVar documents 19 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number gains and losses across 10q23-q26, indicating dosage sensitivity where both deletion and duplication cause disease, potentially dilated cardiomyopathy or limb-girdle muscular dystrophy. The combination of ankyrin repeat-mediated protein binding, extreme muscle specificity, titin/telethonin interactions, and nuclear translocation capability (via YBX1/p53 binding) supports a dual-function model: ANKRD2 acts as a mechanical sensor at the sarcomeric I-band that transduces stretch signals to the nucleus, modulating transcription of hypertrophic and stress-response genes through interactions with transcription factors and chromatin regulators.
