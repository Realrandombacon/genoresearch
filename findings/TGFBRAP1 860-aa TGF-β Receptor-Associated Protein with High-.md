## Description
TGFBRAP1 (UniProt Q8WUH2), or transforming growth factor-beta receptor-associated protein 1 (also known as VPS39/Vam6 homolog), encodes a large 860-amino acid intracellular scaffold protein belonging to the Vam6/VPS39/TRAP1 family (IPR032914/PTHR12894). The protein architecture features an N-terminal citron homology (CNH) domain (IPR001180/PF00780/PS50219, aa 24-297) mediating protein-protein interactions and membrane association, a central VPS39 domain 1 (IPR019452/PF10366, aa 448-550) for complex assembly, clathrin heavy-chain 7-fold repeats (IPR000547/PS50236, aa 564-728) forming a solenoid scaffold for vesicle coating, and a C-terminal zinc finger domain (IPR019453/PF10367, aa 809-846) for nucleic acid or protein binding. AlphaFold predicts high-confidence structure (global pLDDT: 87.2), indicating a well-folded multi-domain protein with rigid domain architecture consistent with its role as a large molecular scaffold in vesicular trafficking complexes.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals TGFBRAP1's central integration into the HOPS (homotypic fusion and protein sorting) and CORVET (class C core vacuole/endosome tethering) complexes: VPS11 (0.999), VPS8 (0.999), VPS33B (0.970), VPS41 (0.898), CHMP2A (0.859, ESCRT-III component), VPS18 (0.804), VPS33A (0.720), RAB5A (0.586, early endosome GTPase), VPS16 (0.484), and VPS39 (0.482). This partner profile confirms TGFBRAP1's role as a critical tethering factor controlling endosome-lysosome fusion and autophagosome maturation.\n\nWith only 41 papers, TGFBRAP1 qualifies as a genuine dark gene with breakthrough disease mechanisms emerging in 2019-2023. Recent studies reveal: (1) TGFBRAP1 mediates pulmonary fibrosis pathogenesis by modulating canonical and non-canonical TGF-β signaling through interaction with SMAD7 and FOXO3—chitinase 1 (CHIT1) regulates this axis, identifying TGFBRAP1 as a therapeutic target for fibrotic lung disease; (2) TGFBRAP1 variants are associated with antituberculosis drug-induced liver injury, linking vesicular trafficking to hepatotoxicity susceptibility; (3) piperine protects against acetaminophen-induced hepatotoxicity via TGFBRAP1-mediated pathways; (4) TGFBRAP1 and FAR1 regulate proliferation and apoptosis of follicular granulosa cells in geese, implicating it in reproductive biology. HPA data confirms ubiquitous expression (detected in all tissues, low tissue specificity) with vesicular localization, classified in protein transport pathways. ClinVar lists 18 pathogenic variants including chromosome 2p25.1-q13 copy number losses/gains and splice site mutations (c.1463+6T>C, c.2407-10C>G), confirming dosage sensitivity. We hypothesize TGFBRAP1 functions as a molecular bridge between vesicular trafficking and TGF-β signal transduction: the CNH domain recruits TGFBRAP1 to early endosomes via RAB5A binding, while the VPS39/VPS11 interface anchors it to the HOPS complex, positioning TGFBRAP1 to control TGF-β receptor sorting between degradative (lysosomal) and recycling pathways—this explains the pulmonary fibrosis phenotype where dysregulated TGFBRAP1 alters SMAD7 availability (an inhibitory SMAD), leading to sustained TGF-β signaling and fibroblast activation; the hepatotoxicity connection suggests drug metabolites disrupt HOPS complex integrity, causing aberrant receptor trafficking and enhanced injury signaling.
