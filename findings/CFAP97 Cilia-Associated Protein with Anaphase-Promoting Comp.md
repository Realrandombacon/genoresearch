## Description
CFAP97 (Gene ID: 56996, UniProt Q9P2B7) is a 532 amino acid cilia- and flagella-associated protein located on chromosome 4q35.1 (NC_000004.12). This gene encodes a conserved axonemal protein with dual localization to primary cilium and nucleoli fibrillar center, suggesting roles in both ciliary structure/function and nucleolar organization. InterPro domain analysis reveals CFAP97-specific architecture without canonical enzymatic domains: Hemingway/CFA97 family signature (IPR029488/PF13879 at positions 346-447, ~101 residues), Cilia- and flagella-associated protein 97 family (IPR038791/PTHR23035 at positions 99-528, ~429 residues spanning most of the protein). The absence of structured catalytic domains combined with medium-confidence AlphaFold prediction suggests CFAP97 functions as a structural scaffold or adapter protein. STRING interaction network identifies 10 medium-confidence partners revealing unexpected functional connections: ANAPC5 (0.649, anaphase-promoting complex subunit 5/cyclosome subunit 5, E3 ubiquitin ligase component), ANAPC4 (0.646), ANAPC10 (0.645), ANAPC16 (0.639), ANAPC1 (0.619), ANAPC7 (0.605), CDC16 (0.604, anaphase-promoting complex subunit 3), CFAP97D2 (0.592, cilia- and flagella-associated protein 97 domain-containing protein 2, paralog), ZNHIT6 (0.538, zinc finger HIT-type containing protein 6), and C4orf47 (0.524, chromosome 4 open reading frame 47). The striking enrichment for ANAPC/cyclosome subunits (7 of 10 top partners) suggests CFAP97 links ciliary biology to cell cycle regulation through the anaphase-promoting complex—a critical E3 ubiquitin ligase controlling mitotic progression via cyclin B and securin degradation. Human Protein Atlas classifies CFAP97 (ENSG00000164323) as a predicted intracellular protein with remarkable dual subcellular localization: nucleoli fibrillar center AND primary cilium AND cytosol—indicating shuttling between nuclear/nucleolar and ciliary compartments. RNA expression shows low tissue specificity with detection in ALL tissues (ubiquitous housekeeping expression), low cancer specificity, low immune cell specificity, but cell type-enhanced single cell specificity—suggesting fundamental cellular function with specialized cell type modulation. AlphaFold structure prediction (AF-Q9P2B7-F1) yields MEDIUM-confidence model (global pLDDT 57.6), indicating a partially structured protein with mixed ordered and disordered regions across all 532 residues—the Hemingway/CFA97 domain (346-447) likely forms structured core while flanking regions show disorder consistent with scaffold/adapter function requiring conformational flexibility. Medium-low confidence reflects challenging prediction of proteins with extensive flexible linkers typical of ciliary scaffold proteins. ClinVar lists 112 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large chromosomal copy number variations spanning chromosome 4q regions (chr4:157628420-189863176x1 deletion, chr4:177853624-190036305x1 deletion, chr4:161355371-190957473x3 gain, chr4:184213971-190957473x1 deletion, chr4:179475329-190957473x1 deletion, chr4:123399154-190957473x3 gain, chr4:161589441-190957473x1 deletion, chr4:175496275-186495932x1 deletion); two intronic SNVs (NM_020827.3:c.1055-14G>T, NM_020827.3:c.1321-16A>G) of uncertain significance; no specific monogenic disease is attributed solely to CFAP97, suggesting dosage sensitivity within larger 4q chromosomal syndromes affecting development and cognition. Conservation is evidenced by orthologs across metazoans: Mus musculus Cfap97d1 (Q9DAN9, 164 aa paralog), Rattus norvegicus Cfap97 (Q66H34, 548 aa), Bos taurus CFAP97 (Q2KJH5, 527 aa), Drosophila melanogaster hemingway/hmw (Q9VE87, 604 aa)—the Drosophila name 'hemingway' reflects genetic studies linking this protein to neuronal development and axoneme function. Functional hypothesis: CFAP97 functions as a bifunctional scaffold protein coordinating ciliary assembly/maintenance and cell cycle progression through anaphase-promoting complex regulation. Dual localization to primary cilium and nucleoli fibrillar center suggests compartmentalized functions: (1) In cilia, CFAP97 serves as axonemal scaffold organizing microtubule doublets and dynein arms for motility or sensory signaling; (2) In nucleoli, CFAP97 participates in ribosome biogenesis or rRNA processing within fibrillar centers where RNA polymerase I transcription occurs. ANAPC interactions suggest third function: CFAP97 may regulate APC/C activity during cell cycle transitions, potentially linking ciliary resorption (required for mitotic entry) to APC/C-mediated cyclin degradation. CFAP97D2 paralog interaction indicates functional redundancy or cooperative assembly into ciliary complexes. Disease mechanism: CFAP97 haploinsufficiency or overexpression from chromosome 4q35 CNVs may contribute to 4q deletion/duplication syndrome phenotypes including intellectual disability, growth abnormalities, craniofacial defects, and ciliopathy features. The 112 ClinVar variants suggest strong dosage sensitivity; 4q35 deletions cause facioscapulohumeral muscular dystrophy (FSHD)-adjacent phenotypes. CFAP97 may contribute through disrupted ciliary signaling (affecting developmental pathways like Hedgehog), aberrant cell cycle control (causing proliferation defects), or impaired nucleolar function (disrupting ribosome biogenesis). Priority experiments include immunofluorescence colocalization with ciliary markers (acetylated tubulin, ARL13B) and nucleolar markers (fibrillarin), co-IP validation of ANAPC subunit interactions across cell cycle phases, investigation of CFAP97 role in ciliogenesis using serum starvation assays, assessment of CFAP97 involvement in APC/C activity via ubiquitination assays, CRISPR knockout to assess ciliary and cell cycle phenotypes, structural characterization leveraging AlphaFold model (pLDDT 57.6) to map interaction interfaces, and analysis of CFAP97 expression in 4q syndrome patient cells and ciliopathy tissues.
