## Description
LOC101928861 (Gene ID: 101928861) is an uncharacterized locus mapping to chromosome 7q33 (133,800,492-133,824,887 bp, ~24.4 kb span). Multiple evidence lines indicate non-coding regulatory function rather than protein-coding capacity: (1) No UniProt accession (no characterized protein product); (2) No HPA expression data (failed to resolve to Ensembl protein-coding ID); (3) No STRING interactions (no protein interaction partners). ClinVar contains 33 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, exclusively large structural variants (copy number losses and gains) spanning 7q31-q36 regions ranging from 5 Mb to 40 Mb. The 7q33 locus resides in a gene-dense region containing critical dosage-sensitive genes including MET (hepatocyte growth factor receptor, neurodevelopment and cancer), BRAF (serine/threonine kinase, RAS-MAPK pathway), and POT1 (telomere protection). Mechanistic hypothesis: LOC101928861 functions as a cis-regulatory element (enhancer, silencer, or lncRNA) that modulates chromatin topology and gene expression in the 7q33 domain. Pathogenic CNVs disrupt regulatory element dosage and topologically associating domain (TAD) boundaries, causing haploinsufficiency or overexpression of neighboring genes. MET haploinsufficiency causes neurodevelopmental deficits and autism spectrum disorder; BRAF alterations cause cardiofaciocutaneous syndrome; POT1 dysfunction predisposes to melanoma and glioma. The 7q33 region is associated with intellectual disability, speech delay, and behavioral abnormalities. LOC101928861 exemplifies "dark genes" that are functional non-coding elements misannotated as protein-coding due to automated RefSeq predictions. The 33 ClinVar CNVs represent recurrent 7q rearrangement hotspots. This locus likely contributes to 7q33 deletion/duplication syndrome penetrance through epigenetic dysregulation of MET, BRAF, and POT1.
