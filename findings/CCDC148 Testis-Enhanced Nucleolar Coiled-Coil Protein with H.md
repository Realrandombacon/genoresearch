## Description
CCDC148 (Gene ID: 130940, UniProt Q8NFR7) is a 591 amino acid coiled-coil domain-containing protein located on chromosome 2q24.1 (NC_000002.12, positions 158171072-158456752, ~286kb region). InterPro domain analysis reveals an extensive coiled-coil architecture spanning nearly the entire protein (IPR039902, positions 28-589), with a defined CCDC148 coiled-coil domain (PF27813, positions 221-457) forming the core structural element. The protein belongs to the PTHR21549 family (Mutated in Bladder Cancer 1), suggesting potential tumor suppressor function. AlphaFold structure prediction (AF-Q8NFR7-F1) shows high global confidence (pLDDT 78.9), indicating a well-folded stable protein with extensive alpha-helical coiled-coil regions that likely mediate protein-protein interactions through dimerization or oligomerization. Human Protein Atlas classifies CCDC148 as a predicted intracellular protein localized to nucleoplasm, nucleoli, and nucleoli rim - this nucleolar localization strongly suggests involvement in ribosome biogenesis, rRNA processing, or nucleolar stress response pathways. RNA expression is tissue-enhanced with highest levels in testis (4.6 nTPM), cancer-enhanced expression pattern indicating upregulation in malignant cells, not detected in immune cells, and cell type-enhanced specificity. STRING database identifies 10 protein-protein interaction partners: TEX9 (score 0.631, highest confidence - testis-expressed protein 9, suggesting coordinated testis-specific function), LRRC49 (0.537, leucine-rich repeat protein potentially involved in protein scaffolding), MORN3 (0.472, membrane occupation and recognition nexus repeat protein), CCNI2 (0.451, cyclin I2 linking to cell cycle regulation), SCGB1D1 (0.428, secretoglobin family member), UPP2 (0.427, uridine phosphorylase 2 linking to pyrimidine metabolism), C11orf65 (0.425, uncharacterized protein), SAT1 (0.424, diamine acetyltransferase 1 involved in polyamine metabolism), SCGB2A1 (0.422, secretoglobin), and LTF (0.411, lactotransferrin). The TEX9 interaction is particularly notable as both proteins show testis-enhanced expression, suggesting coordinated function in spermatogenesis or testis-specific processes. ClinVar contains 19 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants affecting CCDC148: 1 single nucleotide variant in the antisense transcript CCDC148-AS1 (NR_038850.1:n.221+89G>T) and 18 copy number losses spanning 2q24.1-24.3 regions (ranging from ~2.8Mb deletions at chr2:156696086-159450561 to whole-arm losses at chr2:1-243199373), indicating haploinsufficiency - reduced CCDC148 dosage causes disease phenotypes. No specific diseases are associated with CCDC148 alone, suggesting phenotypes result from combined effects of multiple genes in deleted regions. The bladder cancer family membership (PTHR21549) combined with cancer-enhanced expression suggests potential dual role as tumor suppressor (haploinsufficient) and oncogene (overexpressed in cancers). Functional hypothesis: CCDC148 functions as a nucleolar scaffold protein that forms coiled-coil mediated oligomers to organize nucleolar subcompartments, potentially regulating ribosome biogenesis through interactions with TEX9 and cell cycle proteins like CCNI2. Testis-enhanced expression suggests specialized roles in spermatogenesis, possibly in chromatin remodeling during meiosis or post-meiotic germ cell development. Disease mechanism: CCDC148 haploinsufficiency disrupts nucleolar organization and ribosome biogenesis, causing cellular stress and developmental defects observed in 2q24 deletion syndromes. In cancer contexts, CCDC148 overexpression may promote tumorigenesis through altered nucleolar function or dysregulated cell cycle progression. Priority experiments include nucleolar proteomics to identify direct binding partners, investigation of CCDC148-TEX9 complex formation and function, CRISPR knockout to assess nucleolar morphology and ribosome production, characterization of CCDC148 expression across bladder cancer subtypes, and analysis of CCDC148 mutations in cancer genomes.
