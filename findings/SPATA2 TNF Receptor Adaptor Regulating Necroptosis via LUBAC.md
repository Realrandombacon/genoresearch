# SPATA2: TNF Receptor Adaptor Regulating Necroptosis via LUBAC Complex

## Description
SPATA2 (spermatogenesis-associated protein 2) is a 520-amino acid cytoplasmic and nucleoplasmic adaptor protein (UniProt Q9UM82) that functions as a critical regulator of TNF-mediated necroptotic signaling. The protein contains a PUB-like domain (IPR048839/PF21388, residues 15-207) and a B-box type 1 zinc finger (residues 460-503), structural features characteristic of ubiquitin-binding adaptors. SPATA2 forms a high-confidence protein interaction network (STRING scores 0.85-0.997) with TNF receptor signaling components including TNFRSF1A (0.997), the deubiquitinase CYLD (0.985), TNF ligand (0.941), and LUBAC complex subunits SHARPIN (0.936), RNF31/HOIP (0.861), and RBCK1/HOIL-1 (0.747). This interaction profile positions SPATA2 as a scaffold that recruits CYLD deubiquitinase to the TNF receptor complex, enabling removal of M1-linked ubiquitin chains and switching signaling from NF-κB survival to RIPK1/RIPK3/MLKL-mediated necroptosis. HPA data shows ubiquitous expression across all tissues with nucleoplasm and fibrillar center localization, consistent with its role in signal transduction. ClinVar documents 9 pathogenic copy number variants (deletions and duplications spanning 20q13.13), indicating dosage sensitivity and disease relevance. Mouse ortholog (Q8K004, 515 aa) shows ~99% sequence conservation, confirming essential function. The mechanistic hypothesis: SPATA2 acts as a molecular switch in TNF signaling by bridging TNFR1 to CYLD and LUBAC, controlling the ubiquitination status of RIPK1 to determine cell fate between survival (NF-κB activation) and programmed necrosis (necroptosis execution).
