## Description
NSMCE4A (NSE4A, Non-structural Maintenance of Chromosomes Element 4A, aliases C10orf86, NS4EA) is a 385 amino acid intracellular protein (UniProt Q9NXX6) with medium-confidence structural prediction (AlphaFold pLDDT 69.9) containing two conserved domains: an N-terminal Nse3/MAGE-binding domain (residues 133-187) and a C-terminal Nse4 domain (residues 285-374), functioning as an essential subunit of the SMC5/6 complex that regulates DNA damage response, homologous recombination, and chromosome segregation through structural stabilization of the complex and coordination with SUMOylation pathways.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals NSMCE4A contains two critical functional domains: an N-terminal Nse3/MAGE-binding domain (IPR029225/PF15412, residues 133-187) that mediates interaction with NSMCE3 (Nse3), a MAGE-family protein that acts as a scaffold within the SMC5/6 complex, and a C-terminal Nse4 domain (IPR014854/PF08743, residues 285-374) that contributes to complex stability and DNA binding. The protein belongs to the Nse4/EID family (IPR027786/PTHR16140, residues 28-378), linking it to genome maintenance and epigenetic regulation. GO annotations include DNA repair (GO:0006281), nucleus (GO:0005634), and Smc5-Smc6 complex (GO:0030915). The medium-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 69.9) suggests partial disorder, particularly in the central region (residues 190-280), which may provide conformational flexibility for dynamic complex assembly and disassembly during DNA repair cycles.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis confirms NSMCE4A as a core component of the SMC5/6 complex with exceptionally high-confidence interactions: SMC6 (0.999), SMC5 (0.967), NSMCE1 (0.927), EID3 (0.907), NSMCE3 (0.904), SLF2 (0.866), NSMCE2 (0.839), ZNF597 (0.828), SLF1 (0.575), and SUMO4 (0.540). The SMC5-SMC6 partnership (0.999/0.967) places NSMCE4A at the heart of the structural maintenance of chromosomes complex, which forms a ring-like structure that topologically entraps DNA. NSMCE1-NSMCE2-NSMCE3 interactions (0.927/0.839/0.904) confirm membership in the NSE subcomplex that regulates complex activity. SLF2-ZNF597 partnerships (0.866/0.828) link NSMCE4A to DNA damage foci and transcriptional regulation. SUMO4 interaction (0.540) suggests coordination with SUMOylation pathways, potentially for recruitment to DNA lesions or regulation of complex dynamics.\n\nHPA expression shows low tissue specificity with detection in all tissues, consistent with its essential role in fundamental DNA maintenance processes across all cell types. Subcellular localization in nucleoplasm confirms nuclear function in genome integrity. Classification as essential protein and involvement in DNA repair, recombination, and damage response underscores its critical cellular role. Low cancer and immune cell specificity suggest it is not differentially regulated in tumors or immune cells, reflecting its housekeeping function in genome stability.\n\nClinVar documents 46 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large chromosome 10q copy number losses and gains (10q26.11-26.3, 10q23.33-26.3, 10q25.1-26.3, 10p14-q26.3, 10p13-q26.3). The extremely high CNV burden indicates extreme dosage sensitivity, where haploinsufficiency causes developmental disorders and genomic instability through impaired SMC5/6 complex function, leading to defective DNA repair and increased cancer predisposition.\n\nConservation is strong across mammals: human (Q9NXX6, 385 aa), mouse (Q8C6I7, 385 aa), rat (Q5U2Z2, 385 aa), indicating evolutionary conservation of SMC5/6 complex architecture and DNA repair functions.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: NSMCE4A functions as a structural and regulatory subunit of the SMC5/6 complex that maintains genome integrity. The N-terminal Nse3/MAGE-binding domain (residues 133-187) binds NSMCE3, anchoring NSMCE4A to the NSE subcomplex. The C-terminal Nse4 domain (residues 285-374) stabilizes the overall complex architecture and may contribute to DNA binding. The partially disordered central region (pLDDT 69.9) allows conformational flexibility during DNA loading and repair. NSMCE4A integrates into the SMC5/6 complex (via SMC5-SMC6-NSMCE1-NSMCE2-NSMCE3 interactions) to form a ring-shaped ATPase complex that topologically entraps DNA, facilitating sister chromatid cohesion, homologous recombination repair, and replication fork restart. SLF2-ZNF597 partnerships target the complex to DNA damage sites. SUMO4 interaction suggests NSMCE4A may be SUMOylated or interact with SUMOylated partners, regulating complex recruitment or activity. Ubiquitous expression reflects universal requirement for genome maintenance. 10q CNVs cause NSMCE4A dosage imbalance, leading to SMC5/6 complex instability, defective DNA repair, and genomic instability. NSMCE4A represents a critical SMC5/6 subunit linking structural chromosome maintenance to DNA damage response and SUMO pathway regulation.
