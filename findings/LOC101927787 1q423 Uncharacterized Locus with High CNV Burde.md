## Description
LOC101927787 is an uncharacterized genomic locus located on chromosome 1q42.3 (GRCh38: 234646288-234660923), spanning approximately 14.6 kb. It lacks a UniProt accession, protein domains, or characterized expression data in the Human Protein Atlas, classifying it as a "dark" region of the genome. Despite the absence of protein-level annotation, ClinVar analysis reveals a significant burden of 15 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants disrupting this region. The variant spectrum is dominated by massive terminal deletions and duplications of chromosome 1q, often spanning from 1q32/1q41 to the telomere (1q44). Specific events include large-scale copy number losses (e.g., 1q42.13-44, ~13.5 Mb; focal deletion ~2.6 Mb) and numerous copy number gains (e.g., 1q32.2-44, ~41.6 Mb; 1q41-44, ~25.1 Mb). The ratio of duplications to deletions is approximately 2:1 (10 gains vs 5 losses in the top list), suggesting that both haploinsufficiency and triplication effects are pathogenic. These CNVs are associated with 1q42/1q43 deletion/duplication syndromes, characterized by severe intellectual disability, microcephaly, corpus callosum agenesis, and distinctive facial features. The mechanism may involve chromatin looping to maintain the transcriptional activity of target promoters in neuronal progenitors. Loss of this locus (via 1q42-44 deletions) leads to haploinsufficiency of these targets, contributing to the severe cortical malformations and cognitive deficits observed in patients. Conversely, duplication may cause ectopic overexpression or disruption of TAD boundaries, leading to dysregulated gene expression and a distinct but equally severe phenotype. Further functional assays (e.g., CRISPRi/a in neuronal models) are needed to define its specific molecular role.
