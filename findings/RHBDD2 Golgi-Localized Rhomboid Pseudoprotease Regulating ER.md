# RHBDD2: Golgi-Localized Rhomboid Pseudoprotease Regulating ERAD and Chemoresistance in Brain-Enriched Tissues

**Date:** 2026-05-01T20:19:06.200963

**Quality Score:** 4/10 (MODERATE)

**Confidence:** LOW-MEDIUM

## Description
RHBDD2 (Rhomboid domain containing 2, also known as RHBDL7 or NPD007) encodes a 364 amino acid membrane protein (UniProt Q6NTF9) belonging to the rhomboid superfamily of intramembrane serine proteases. InterPro analysis reveals a canonical rhomboid domain architecture: a rhomboid-like superfamily fold (G3DSA:1.20.1540.10, residues 15-204; IPR035952) containing the peptidase S54 rhomboid domain (IPR022764, PF01694, residues 58-202) with predicted serine-type endopeptidase activity. However, RHBDD2 is classified as a pseudoprotease—the catalytic serine residue is not conserved, suggesting it functions as a substrate-binding scaffold rather than an active protease.\n\nAlphaFold prediction shows medium global confidence (pLDDT 67.8) across the 364 aa length, consistent with a transmembrane protein containing structured rhomboid domains embedded in lipid bilayers with flexible terminal regions.\n\nSTRING interactions reveal RHBDD2 operates within the ER-associated degradation (ERAD) and rhomboid protease network: RHBDF1 (0.666) and RHBDD1 (0.634) are close paralogs; DERL1 (0.566), DERL2 (0.636), and DERL3 (0.573) are derlin proteins that form ERAD retrotranslocation channels; RHBDL1 (0.596) and RHBDL2 (0.566) are active rhomboid proteases; UBAC2 (0.573) and AUP1 (0.555) are ERAD ubiquitination factors; OS9 (0.552) is an ER lectin that recognizes misfolded glycoproteins for degradation. This interaction profile strongly positions RHBDD2 in protein quality control and ERAD substrate selection.\n\nHPA expression shows RHBDD2 localizes to the Golgi apparatus with tissue-enhanced expression pattern, detected in all tissues but enriched in choroid plexus (356.5 nTPM) and brain (348.9 nTPM). The Golgi localization is consistent with rhomboid protease function in regulated intramembrane proteolysis and protein trafficking. Blood cell expression is immune cell-enhanced, suggesting potential immunomodulatory roles.\n\nClinVar reports 26 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly 7q11.23 copy number losses and gains. While no specific monogenic disease is definitively assigned, the 7q11.23 region overlaps with Williams-Beuren syndrome and other neurodevelopmental disorders. The high brain/choroid plexus expression combined with CNV sensitivity suggests RHBDD2 dosage affects CNS development or function.\n\nLiterature (36 papers) reveals emerging cancer biology roles: RHBDD2 is upregulated in response to cisplatin-induced DNA damage in esophageal cancer (2025), enhances cisplatin chemosensitivity when suppressed by miR-4739 in cervical cancer (2024), promotes chemoresistance and invasion in rectal cancer via UPR and focal adhesion gene modulation (2020), and interacts with the tumor suppressor WWOX during proliferation and differentiation in breast cancer (2021). RHBDD2 also contributes to immune infiltration in colorectal adenocarcinoma.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: RHBDD2 functions as a rhomboid pseudoprotease scaffold that regulates ERAD substrate selection and protein trafficking at the Golgi-ER interface. Unlike active rhomboid proteases that cleave transmembrane substrates, RHBDD2 likely binds specific client proteins through its rhomboid domain and recruits them to the ERAD machinery (DERL1/2/3, UBAC2, AUP1, OS9) for ubiquitination and proteasomal degradation. In cancer, RHBDD2 overexpression may enhance survival by promoting degradation of pro-apoptotic factors or by modulating the unfolded protein response (UPR) to confer chemoresistance. The WWOX interaction suggests RHBDD2 may regulate tumor suppressor stability. Brain-enriched expression and 7q11.23 CNV sensitivity implicate RHBDD2 in neurodevelopmental processes, potentially through ERAD-mediated regulation of neuronal surface receptors or ion channels. The choroid plexus enrichment suggests a role in blood-CSF barrier protein homeostasis.
