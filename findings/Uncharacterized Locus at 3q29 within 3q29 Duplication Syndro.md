## Description
LOC105374297 is an uncharacterized genomic locus on chromosome 3q29 (NC_000003.12:195652158-195646634) spanning approximately 5.5 kb. Comprehensive database searches reveal no evidence of protein-coding function: no UniProt accession was identified, precluding InterPro domain analysis and AlphaFold structure prediction. STRING database returned no protein interaction partners, confirming absence of canonical protein-coding function. Human Protein Atlas could not resolve this locus to an Ensembl ID, indicating no detectable protein expression in surveyed tissues. However, ClinVar analysis reveals 18 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants in this region, all copy number gains (trisomy duplications) encompassing the 3q29 region: chr3:166137209-198125115 (~32 Mb), chr3:193704605-198125115 (~4.4 Mb focal 3q29 dup), chr3:156118441-198125115 (~42 Mb), chr3:156321878-198113452 (~41.8 Mb), chr3:152100512-198118383 (~46 Mb), chr3:176168525-198118383 (~22 Mb), chr3:194296197-198110198 (~3.8 Mb), chr3:176439911-198118383 (~21.7 Mb), chr3:192752937-198118383 (~5.4 Mb), chr3:168167568-198110178 (~30 Mb). These structural variants are associated with 3q29 duplication syndrome featuring developmental delay, intellectual disability, autism spectrum disorder, craniofacial dysmorphism, cardiac defects, and skeletal anomalies. The 3q29 region contains several dosage-sensitive genes including PAK2 (p21-activated kinase 2, critical for neuronal development and synaptic plasticity), FBXO45 (F-box protein 45, involved in ubiquitin-mediated protein degradation), and DL1 (delta-like 1 homolog, Notch signaling pathway). LOC105374297 likely functions as part of the 3q29 cis-regulatory landscape or encodes a non-coding RNA whose copy number gain contributes to 3q29 duplication syndrome pathogenesis through disruption of chromatin topology, topologically associating domain (TAD) boundaries, or long-range dysregulation of dosage-sensitive neurodevelopmental genes. The absence of protein-coding features combined with pathogenic CNV burden (18 variants) indicates this locus resides within a critical dosage-sensitive genomic region where trisomy disrupts neurodevelopmental processes via gene dosage effects and 3D genome reorganization.
