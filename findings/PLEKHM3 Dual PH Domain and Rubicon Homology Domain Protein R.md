## Description
PLEKHM3 (Pleckstrin Homology Domain-Containing Family M Member 3, UniProt Q6ZWE6) is a 761-amino acid intracellular protein functioning as a membrane-associated regulator of vesicle trafficking and autophagy through its dual PH domain architecture and C-terminal Rubicon Homology Domain, with emerging roles in cancer progression via circular RNA-mediated mechanisms. Functional hypothesis: PLEKHM3 serves as a dual-function membrane trafficking and autophagy regulator where the N-terminal tandem PH domains (aa 212-458) bind specific phosphoinositides (potentially PI3P, PI4P, or PI(3,5)P2) to recruit PLEKHM3 to endosomal, lysosomal, or autophagosomal membranes, while the C-terminal Rubicon Homology Domain (aa 529-732) mediates protein-protein interactions with autophagy machinery analogous to Rubicon's role in inhibiting autophagosome-lysosome fusion; this positions PLEKHM3 as a potential negative regulator of autophagic flux or a coordinator of vesicle trafficking decisions between endocytic and autophagic pathways. Mechanistic proposal: The first PH domain (aa 212-310) and second PH domain (aa 361-458) recognize distinct phosphoinositide species on endosomal or autophagosomal membranes, providing spatial specificity for PLEKHM3 recruitment; the Rubicon Homology Domain (aa 529-732) interacts with Beclin 1-VPS34 complex or other autophagy regulators to modulate autophagosome maturation, potentially inhibiting fusion with lysosomes similar to Rubicon's established function; AKT1 interaction (STRING 0.438) suggests PLEKHM3 may be phosphorylated by AKT to regulate its membrane binding or autophagy function, linking growth factor signaling to trafficking decisions; GDAP1 interaction (STRING 0.512) indicates potential roles in mitochondrial-ER contact sites or mitochondrial fission coordination with autophagy (mitophagy); curcumin's anti-cancer effect through circ-PLEKHM3/miR-320a/SMG1 axis suggests therapeutic targeting potential; vesicular and cytosolic localization (HPA) confirms membrane-associated trafficking function; PLEKHM3 represents an emerging autophagy/trafficking regulator with dual protein-coding and circular RNA functions in cancer and potential neurological disease.
