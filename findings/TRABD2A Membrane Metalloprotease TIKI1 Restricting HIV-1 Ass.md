# TRABD2A: Membrane Metalloprotease TIKI1 Restricting HIV-1 Assembly via Gag Degradation and Negatively Regulating Wnt Signaling

## Description
TRABD2A encodes a 505 amino acid metalloprotease TIKI1 (UniProt Q86V40), a membrane-associated protease with dual roles in antiviral defense and developmental signaling. With only 14 publications, TRABD2A remains partially characterized despite critical functions in HIV restriction and Wnt pathway regulation.\n\nSTRUCTURAL FEATURES: AlphaFold predicts medium confidence structure (pLDDT 68.9) across all 505 residues, consistent with a membrane protease containing ordered catalytic domains with flexible regulatory regions. InterPro analysis reveals a conserved metalloprotease architecture: Tiki homology domain (cd14789, residues 44-350) responsible for antagonizing Wnt function via cleavage of amino-terminal residues, TraB/PrgY/GumN N-terminal domain (IPR002816, PF01963, residues 43-351) characteristic of bacterial conjugation proteins repurposed for eukaryotic signaling, and Protease TIKI1/2-like family domain (IPR040230, PTHR31120, residues 10-498) spanning nearly the entire protein. GO annotations confirm endopeptidase activity (GO:0004175) and negative regulation of Wnt signaling pathway (GO:0030178).\n\nINTERACTION NETWORK: STRING-DB identifies 8 interaction partners with medium confidence (0.40-0.51), exclusively comprising Wnt pathway regulators. Wnt antagonists: NOTUM (0.449)—carboxylesterase that depalmitoleates Wnts; SOST (0.442)—sclerostin inhibiting Wnt/LRP binding; WIF1 (0.407)—Wnt inhibitory factor; DKK4 (0.405)—Dickkopf inhibitor; APCDD1 (0.413)—adenomatous polyposis coli down-regulated. Developmental regulators: TPBG (0.507)—trophoblast glycoprotein; TRABD (0.487)—paralogous TraB domain protein; NDP (0.433)—Norrie disease protein. This network confirms TRABD2A's exclusive role in Wnt pathway modulation.\n\nEXPRESSION & LOCALIZATION: HPA confirms group-enriched expression with highest levels in intestine (40.2 nTPM), ovary (38.2 nTPM), and lymphoid tissue (10.6 nTPM). Detected in some tissues with cell type-enhanced specificity. Immune cell enhanced pattern aligns with HIV restriction function. Classified as predicted membrane protein consistent with plasma membrane localization required for HIV Gag cleavage.\n\nCLINICAL SIGNIFICANCE: ClinVar reports 12 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number losses (2p25.1-q13, 2p12-11.2, 2p13.1-11.2) and gains (2p25.3-q37.3) encompassing the TRABD2A locus, plus single allele deletions/duplications. While no specific monogenic disease is yet annotated, the variant burden and Wnt pathway role suggest potential contributions to developmental disorders or cancer.\n\nFUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: TRABD2A functions as a dual-purpose membrane metalloprotease: (1) Antiviral restriction—recent literature (2019, 23 citations) demonstrates TRABD2A degrades HIV-1 Gag polyprotein at the plasma membrane, preventing virion assembly and release in resting CD4+ T cells and monocyte-derived dendritic cells. This represents a novel intrinsic immunity mechanism distinct from APOBEC3 or tetherin. (2) Wnt signaling inhibition—the Tiki homology domain (residues 44-350) cleaves N-terminal residues of Wnt ligands, promoting their aggregation and inactivation (supported by GO:0030178 and NOTUM/SOST/WIF1 interactions). (3) Tissue homeostasis—intestine and ovary expression suggests roles in epithelial renewal and folliculogenesis where Wnt signaling is critical. (4) Osteogenic differentiation—recent literature (2026) shows TRABD2A promotes periodontal ligament stem cell osteogenesis via TNF-α/IL-1β modulation. Mechanistically, the membrane localization positions TRABD2A to intercept both viral Gag assembly and secreted Wnt ligands. Loss-of-function would increase HIV susceptibility and potentially drive Wnt-dependent cancers; gain-of-function could impair tissue regeneration.
