## Description
A3GALT2 (Alpha-1,3-galactosyltransferase 2, also known as IGB3S, IGBS3S, A3GALT2P) is a 340 amino acid type II transmembrane protein (UniProt U3KPV4) with very-high-confidence well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 91.2), characteristic of glycosyltransferase family 6 (GT6) enzymes. The protein contains a conserved glycosyltransferase family 6 domain (IPR005076/PF03414/cd02515, residues 14-340/66-340/68-339) that catalyzes the transfer of galactose from UDP-galactose to acceptor substrates. The nucleotide-diphospho-sugar transferase superfamily (IPR029044/G3DSA:3.90.550.10/SSF53448, residues 49-340/59-331) provides the structural scaffold for nucleotide sugar binding and catalysis. Molecular functions include N-acetyllactosaminide 3-alpha-galactosyltransferase activity (GO:0008376) and alpha-1,3-galactosyltransferase activity, placing A3GALT2 within glycosphingolipid biosynthetic processes and lipid glycosylation pathways.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals an exceptionally tight network of glycosylation enzymes: strongest interactions with B3GALNT1 (0.941, Beta-1,3-N-acetylgalactosaminyltransferase 1, GM2/GD2 synthase), GLB1 (0.800, Beta-galactosidase, lysosomal enzyme for ganglioside degradation), A4GALT (0.592, Alpha-1,4-galactosyltransferase, P1 blood group synthase), B3GNT5 (0.465, Beta-1,3-N-acetylglucosaminyltransferase 5, lactosylceramide synthase), B3GALT1 (0.461, Beta-1,3-galactosyltransferase 1), B4GALT6 (0.460, Beta-1,4-galactosyltransferase 6), CD1D (0.460, MHC class I-like antigen-presenting molecule for lipid antigens), B4GALT5 (0.443, Beta-1,4-galactosyltransferase 5), and FUT2 (0.430, Alpha-1,2-fucosyltransferase, H blood group antigen). The near-perfect confidence score with B3GALNT1 (0.941) indicates A3GALT2 functions in the same glycosphingolipid synthesis pathway, potentially acting sequentially or in a complex. The GLB1 interaction suggests coordination between synthesis and degradation pathways.\n\nHPA expression data shows RNA not detected, but the protein is classified as an enzyme and predicted membrane protein with Golgi apparatus and vesicle localization, specifically the Golgi cisterna membrane. Biological process annotation includes lipid metabolism, and molecular function includes glycosyltransferase and transferase activity. The lack of RNA detection may indicate low expression or post-transcriptional regulation.\n\nClinVar documents 10 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including intronic variants (c.335+44G>A), inversions, and large copy number gains (1p36.33-35.1, 1p35.1-33, 1p35.2-35.1 duplications) and losses (1p35.1-34.3, 1p35.2-35.1 deletions). The variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity. While no specific Mendelian disease is annotated, 1p35 CNVs are associated with developmental delay and intellectual disability. Given A3GALT2's role in glycosphingolipid biosynthesis, dosage imbalance may cause altered cell surface glycan profiles, affecting cell-cell recognition, signaling, and immune function.\n\nConservation is strong across mammals: human (U3KPV4, 340 aa), mouse (Q3V1N9, 370 aa), rat (A0A4Z3, 339 aa), chimpanzee (A0A6D2WT68, 340 aa), indicating an ancient function in glycan metabolism.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: A3GALT2 functions as a Golgi-resident glycosyltransferase that catalyzes the addition of alpha-1,3-linked galactose to glycosphingolipid precursors, potentially generating Galalpha1-3Gal epitopes on non-human mammals (though humans lack functional A3GALT1 due to pseudogenization). The enzyme may act on lactosylceramide or related substrates, working in concert with B3GALNT1 to generate complex gangliosides. The strong interaction with B3GALNT1 suggests they may form a complex or act sequentially in the same biosynthetic pathway. A3GALT2 may also modify glycoproteins or generate lipid antigens for CD1D presentation. Loss of A3GALT2 causes altered glycosphingolipid profiles, potentially affecting membrane microdomain organization, signal transduction, and cell-cell interactions. Dosage sensitivity explains the association with 1p35 CNV syndromes. A3GALT2 represents a candidate glycosyltransferase in sphingolipid metabolism and a potential modifier of blood group antigen expression.
