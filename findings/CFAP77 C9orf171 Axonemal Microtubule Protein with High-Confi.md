# CFAP77 (C9orf171): Axonemal Microtubule Protein with High-Confidence Sperm Motility Network and Testis-Enriched Expression

**Date:** 2026-03-28T04:51:54.259367

**Quality Score:** 5.85/10  (E=6.9, D=4.8) [SOLID]

## Description
CFAP77 (Gene ID: 389799, UniProt Q6ZQR2, alias C9orf171) is a 320 amino acid cilia- and flagella-associated protein located on chromosome 9q34.13 (NC_000009.12, positions 132410210-132573318, ~163kb genomic region). This gene encodes a well-folded protein with clear subcellular localization in axonemal microtubules and strong functional predictions for flagellated sperm motility, but incompletely characterized molecular mechanism. InterPro domain analysis reveals CFAP77 is the sole member of its protein family (IPR029147/PF14825, positions 18-302/102-239; PTHR28617, positions 18-302), indicating evolutionary conservation of this unique ciliary protein architecture without recognizable functional domains beyond the family signature. GO annotations from Alliance of Genome Resources confirm involvement in flagellated sperm motility and axonemal microtubule localization. STRING interaction network identifies 10 high-confidence protein partners forming a coherent axonemal/ciliary cluster: CCDC173 (0.905, coiled-coil domain-containing protein 173, ciliary basal body protein), SPACA9 (0.901, sperm acrosome associated protein 9, axonemal microtubule binding), C5orf49 (0.817, chromosome 5 ORF 49, ciliary protein), TEKT3 (0.816, tektin-3, intermediate filament-like component of axonemal microtubules), C1orf194 (0.815, uncharacterized ciliary protein), C9orf116 (0.814, cilia-associated protein), EFHC1 (0.813, EF-hand domain-containing protein 1, mutated in juvenile myoclonic epilepsy, localizes to cilia/axonemes), EFHC2 (0.813, EF-hand domain-containing protein 2, ciliary protein), TEKT2 (0.812, tektin-2, axonemal component), and EFHB (0.808, EF-hand calcium-binding protein, regulates intracellular calcium). The presence of multiple tektins (TEKT2, TEKT3) and EF-hand proteins suggests CFAP77 functions within the axonemal microtubule stability complex, potentially regulating microtubule-microtubule interactions or calcium-dependent motility. Human Protein Atlas classifies CFAP77 (ENSG00000188523) as a predicted intracellular protein with group-enriched RNA specificity and top expression in fallopian tube (27.9 nTPM), choroid plexus (21.6 nTPM), and testis (17.7 nTPM)—all ciliated tissues. Cancer-enhanced expression pattern and cell type-enhanced single cell specificity are noted; not detected in immune cells. AlphaFold structure prediction (AF-Q6ZQR2-F1) yields high-confidence model (global pLDDT 73.3), indicating a well-folded stable architecture across all 320 residues—suitable for structural studies. ClinVar lists 35 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number losses and gains spanning chromosome 9q34 regions (e.g., chr9:137552409-137879159 deletion, chr9:124095694-141020389x3 duplication); no specific monogenic disease is attributed solely to CFAP77 point mutations, suggesting dosage sensitivity within larger chromosomal syndromes. Conservation is exceptional across eukaryotes: mouse ortholog (A0A087WRI3, 283 aa), zebrafish ortholog (A2BFC9, 240 aa), and even Chlamydomonas reinhardtii ortholog (A8IB22, 270 aa)—a unicellular green alga with flagella—demonstrates ancient evolutionary origin predating animal divergence. Functional hypothesis: CFAP77 functions as an axonemal microtubule-associated protein that stabilizes microtubule doublets within the ciliary/flagellar axoneme, potentially acting as a cross-linker between adjacent microtubules or between microtubules and dynein motor complexes. The high-confidence STRING interactions with tektins (TEKT2, TEKT3)—known intermediate filament-like proteins that form longitudinal polymers along axonemal microtubules—suggest CFAP77 may integrate into the tektin filament network. EF-hand protein interactions (EFHC1, EFHC2, EFHB) indicate potential calcium-regulated conformational changes modulating axonemal beating frequency or waveform. Testis-enriched expression (17.7 nTPM) combined with 'flagellated sperm motility' annotation predicts essential role in sperm flagellum function; mutations may cause male infertility through asthenozoospermia (reduced sperm motility). Fallopian tube expression (27.9 nTPM, highest tissue) suggests role in oviductal ciliary beating for egg transport; dysfunction may contribute to female infertility or ectopic pregnancy. Choroid plexus expression (21.6 nTPM) indicates potential role in ependymal cilia function for cerebrospinal fluid flow; disruption may contribute to hydrocephalus. Disease mechanism: CFAP77 loss-of-function likely causes ciliopathy phenotypes through axonemal instability. The 35 ClinVar CNVs suggest dosage sensitivity; haploinsufficiency may cause partial ciliary dysfunction while complete loss causes severe motility defects. Absence of specific monogenic disease attribution despite strong functional predictions suggests CFAP77 mutations may be underdiagnosed or cause recessive male infertility not captured in ClinVar. Priority experiments include immunofluorescence localization within sperm flagella axonemes, co-IP/MS validation of tektin and EFHC interactions, high-speed video microscopy of sperm motility in CFAP77-deficient models, assessment of CFAP77 role in calcium-dependent axonemal beating regulation, investigation of CFAP77 mutations in infertile male cohorts with asthenozoospermia, and structural studies leveraging AlphaFold model (pLDDT 73.3) to identify microtubule-binding surfaces.
