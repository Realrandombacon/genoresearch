# UNC13D: A MUN Domain-Containing Vesicle Priming Factor Essential for Cytotoxic Granule Exocytosis via RAB27A-Syntaxin Complex Assembly

**Date:** 2026-06-26T16:57:00.113913

**Quality Score:** 10/10 (EXCELLENT)

**Confidence:** HIGH

## Description
UNC13D (Unc-13 Homolog D, UniProt Q70J99), also known as Munc13-4, is a 1,090-amino acid vesicle priming protein essential for regulated exocytosis of cytotoxic granules in natural killer (NK) cells and cytotoxic T lymphocytes (CTLs). InterPro analysis reveals a multi-domain architecture optimized for membrane trafficking: two C2 domains (IPR000008, PF00168, aa 92-262 and aa 910-1035) that bind calcium and phospholipids for membrane association; a central MUN domain (IPR010439, PF06292, aa 580-823) that catalyzes syntaxin opening for SNARE complex assembly; Munc13 homology domains MH1 (IPR014770, aa 557-677) and MH2 (IPR014772, aa 788-895) that mediate protein-protein interactions; and a large N-terminal region of unknown function. The AlphaFold predicted structure shows high confidence (pLDDT 84.9), confirming a stable scaffold for vesicle docking machinery. STRING interaction data places UNC13D at the core of the cytotoxic granule exocytosis pathway with near-perfect associations to the small GTPase RAB27A (0.999) that recruits granules to the plasma membrane; syntaxin STX11 (0.990) and its regulator STXBP2/Munc18-2 (0.970) that form the target SNARE complex; perforin PRF1 (0.905) and RAB27B (0.895) as granule cargo markers; and additional trafficking factors STX19 (0.870), FHL2 (0.838), LYST (0.829), and S100A10 (0.802). HPA expression profiles show tissue enhancement in bone marrow (66.9 nTPM) and lymphoid tissue (65.9 nTPM) with vesicular and cytosolic localization, consistent with immune cell function. ClinVar records document 274 pathogenic variants including frameshifts (p.Val608fs, p.Pro425fs, p.Ile19fs), nonsense mutations (p.Gln688Ter), and splice defects (c.2955-2A>G, c.2954+2T>C) that cause familial hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis type 3 (FHL3)—a life-threatening hyperinflammatory syndrome characterized by defective cytotoxic granule release, uncontrolled T-cell activation, and cytokine storm. We hypothesize that UNC13D functions as the critical priming factor for cytotoxic granule fusion: RAB27A-GTP on mature granules recruits UNC13D via its C2 domains to the plasma membrane; the MUN domain then catalyzes the transition of syntaxin-11 from a closed to open conformation, enabling assembly of the STX11-SNAP23-VAMP7 SNARE complex; this primes granules for rapid calcium-triggered fusion upon immunological synapse formation. Loss of UNC13D blocks granule exocytosis while leaving granule biogenesis intact, resulting in impaired target cell killing and persistent immune activation. This positions UNC13D as a therapeutic target for FHL3 and potentially for enhancing cancer immunotherapy through boosted cytotoxic function.
