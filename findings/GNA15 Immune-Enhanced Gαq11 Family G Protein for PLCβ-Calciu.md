## Description
GNA15 encodes a 374 aa guanine nucleotide-binding protein subunit alpha-15 (UniProt P30679), also known as Gα15/Gα16, functioning as a Gq/11 family G protein alpha subunit that couples GPCRs to phospholipase C-beta (PLCβ) activation and calcium mobilization in immune cells and cancer. The protein contains a canonical G-protein alpha subunit structure (IPR001019/PF00503, aa10-374) with Gαq group-specific motifs (IPR000654, aa12-272), P-loop NTPase domain for GTP binding/hydrolysis (IPR027417/G3DSA:3.40.50.300, aa40-370), and helical insertion domain (IPR011025/G3DSA:1.10.400.10, aa70-189) characteristic of Gα subunits. AlphaFold prediction shows very high confidence (pLDDT 90.6), indicating a well-folded Ras-like GTPase domain with regulatory helical bundle. HPA shows immune cell-enhanced expression with top expression in esophagus (99.4 nTPM) and bone marrow (71.2 nTPM), cancer-enhanced specificity consistent with oncogenic roles. STRING interactions reveal GNA15 couples to PLCβ isoforms: PLCB3 (0.948), PLCB1 (0.947), PLCB4 (0.940), PLCB2 (0.520), plus GPCRs RXFP4 (0.927, relaxin receptor), BDKRB2 (0.921, bradykinin receptor), S1PR4 (0.892, sphingosine-1-phosphate receptor), kinase GRK2 (0.878, GPCR desensitization), paralog GNA14 (0.832), and GNB5 (0.553, beta subunit). ClinVar contains 14 pathogenic variants including 19p13.3 copy number gains/losses and splice site mutations, though GNA15-specific disease phenotypes remain undefined. Literature (60 papers) demonstrates GNA15 induces drug resistance in B-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia (B-ALL) through AMPK pathway activation and fatty acid oxidation (FAO), predicts poor outcomes in ovarian cancer via M2 macrophage infiltration, serves as prognostic biomarker in clear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC), mediates pediatric ALL through CD312/GNA15 non-classical GPCR signaling, and regulates platelet activation, mast cell degranulation, and neutrophil chemotaxis. GNA15 functions as a Gq/11 family GTPase—upon GPCR activation, exchanges GDP for GTP, dissociates from Gβγ dimer, and activates PLCβ to generate IP3/DAG second messengers triggering calcium release and PKC activation. Unlike Gαi/o (inhibitory) and Gαs (stimulatory), Gα15/16 uniquely activates PLCβ independently of receptor specificity, making it a promiscuous signaling hub in hematopoietic cells. Cancer-associated overexpression drives metabolic reprogramming (AMPK-FAO axis) and immunosuppressive microenvironment (M2 TAM recruitment), explaining poor prognosis in multiple malignancies.
