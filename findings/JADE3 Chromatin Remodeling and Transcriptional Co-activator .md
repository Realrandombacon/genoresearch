# JADE3: Chromatin Remodeling and Transcriptional Co-activator Regulating Cancer Stemness, Viral Immunity, and Epigenetic Plasticity via PHD-Finger Mediated Histone Acetylation

## Description
JADE3 (UniProt Q92613), also known as Jade family PHD finger 3, encodes a 823 aa nuclear transcriptional co-activator belonging to the JADE/JMJD6 family, functioning as a critical regulator of epigenetic plasticity, cancer stem cell maintenance, and antiviral immunity through its unique combination of PHD-finger and RING-finger domains. InterPro analysis reveals a modular architecture: an N-terminal 'Enhancer of Polycomb-like' domain (aa 57-177, IPR019542, PF10513) involved in chromatin interaction; two distinct PHD-finger domains (aa 202-251, IPR001965; aa 255-365, IPR011011) capable of reading histone H3K4me3 marks and recruiting chromatin modifiers; and a C-terminal RING-finger domain (aa 190-250, IPR034732) with E3 ubiquitin ligase activity, linking it to protein degradation cycles. AlphaFold predicts a medium-confidence structure (global pLDDT 60.3), indicating a partially structured protein with likely intrinsically disordered regions (IDRs) in the linker segments, consistent with a 'fuzzy' complex assembler that requires flexible regions for multivalent chromatin binding. STRING interactions define JADE3 as a hub in the histone modification and ubiquitin pathways: KAT7 (0.960, histone acetyltransferase), MEAF6 (0.959, transcriptional co-activator), ING4 (0.922, tumor suppressor), ING5 (0.901, tumor suppressor), BRPF3 (0.595, bromodomain protein), MAGEA11 (0.585, melanoma antigen), JADE2 (0.546, homolog), RGN (0.546, RING finger protein), JADE1 (0.482, homolog), and KAT6A (0.449, HAT), suggesting roles in bridging histone acetylation, ubiquitination, and transcriptional activation. HPA expression shows low tissue specificity with detection in all tissues, classified as a predicted intracellular protein localized to nucleoplasm and nucleoli, with immune cell enhancement consistent with roles in innate immune signaling; also classified as cancer-related. ClinVar reports 60 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number losses and gains on chromosome Xp22.33-11.23 (spanning the JADE3 locus), indicating genomic instability at this region potentially linked to X-linked disorders and cancer susceptibility, though no specific monogenic disease has been definitively assigned to JADE3 alone. Literature (12 papers) highlights: (1) Cancer Stem Cell Driver—JADE3 promotes colon cancer stem cell-like properties and tumorigenicity by inducing histone acetylation and maintaining stemness; (2) Antiviral Activator—Identified as a novel activator of NF-kB–dependent IFITM3 expression in response to viral infection, linking it to innate immunity; (3) Epigenetic Plasticity—Regulates transcriptional plasticity in response to environmental cues, potentially facilitating adaptation in cancer cells. Functional hypothesis: JADE3 functions as a: (1) Histone Acetyltransferase Co-activator—Recruits KAT7/KAT6A to acetylate histones at target promoters, enhancing transcription of stemness and immune genes; (2) Chromatin Reader and Writer—Uses PHD-fingers to bind H3K4me3-marked nucleosomes and recruits RING-finger mediated ubiquitination to remodel chromatin accessibility; (3) Viral Immunity Modulator—Activates IFITM3 via NF-kB signaling, protecting cells from viral entry. Mechanistic proposal: As a partially structured IDP, JADE3 utilizes its PHD-fingers to bind specific histone marks on nucleosomes, while its RING-finger recruits E3 ligases (e.g., CUL3) to ubiquitinate co-repressors or histones, releasing transcriptional repression. The N-terminal EP domain anchors it to Polycomb-like complexes. In cancer, JADE3 overexpression (via CNV gain) drives histone hyperacetylation and stemness maintenance. In infection, it acts as a sensor/activator for antiviral responses. The medium-confidence structure supports a dynamic, flexible mechanism rather than a rigid catalytic core. The strong STRING interactions with KAT7/MEAF6/ING4 confirm its role in the histone-modifying complex. Chromosome Xp CNVs disrupt JADE3 dosage, contributing to cancer predisposition and potential immune dysregulation. The combination of a flexible modular architecture, essential role in histone acetylation and ubiquitination, strong complex interactions, cancer association, and clinical variant burden positions JADE3 as a genuine dark gene where intrinsic epigenetic regulation is critical for transcriptional plasticity and cellular adaptation.
