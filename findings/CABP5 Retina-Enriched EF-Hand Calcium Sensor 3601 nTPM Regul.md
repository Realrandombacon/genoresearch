## Description
CABP5 encodes a 173 aa calcium-binding protein 5 (UniProt Q9NP86), functioning as a neuronal calcium sensor with calmodulin-like EF-hand architecture specialized for retinal phototransduction and synaptic calcium signaling. The protein contains two canonical EF-hand calcium-binding domains (IPR002048, PF13499, aa28-94 and aa105-173) with three calcium-binding sites (IPR018247, PS00018, aa41-53/118-130/155-167) featuring conserved DxDxDG and ExxE motifs for calcium coordination, belongs to calcium-binding protein 1/2/4/5 family (IPR043582, PTHR45917, aa2-173), and has GO annotation for calcium ion binding (GO:0005509). AlphaFold prediction shows high confidence structure (pLDDT 79.7), indicating a well-folded two-lobe EF-hand protein with stable helix-loop-helix architecture suitable for calcium-dependent conformational changes and target protein regulation. HPA shows extreme retina-specific expression (360.1 nTPM), classified as tissue enriched with highest expression in retina among all tissues—this represents >10-fold enrichment compared to other tissues, confirming CABP5 as a retina-specialized calcium sensor. STRING interactions reveal CABP5 integrates into phototransduction and synaptic signaling networks: GRK5 (0.662, G protein-coupled receptor kinase 5—highest confidence, phosphorylates activated photoreceptors), GRM6 (0.548, metabotropic glutamate receptor 6, ON-bipolar cell signaling), RHO (0.527, rhodopsin, phototransduction initiator), S100G (0.519, S100 calcium-binding protein G), GUCA1B (0.518, guanylate cyclase activator 1B, cone photoreceptor calcium sensor), ZSWIM9 (0.518, zinc finger SWIM-type protein 9), STXBP1 (0.516, syntaxin binding protein 1, synaptic vesicle exocytosis), GNAT1 (0.479, transducin alpha-1, phototransduction G protein), PCP2 (0.478, Purkinje cell protein 2, cerebellar calcium sensor), MYO1C (0.470, myosin IC, actin-based motor). ClinVar contains 6 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants all consisting of large copy number gains on chromosome 19q (19p13.3-q13.43, 19q11-13.33, 19q13.31-13.42, 19q13.32-13.42, 19q13.33-13.43 with 3 copies), though no specific CABP5-associated disease is yet assigned—these CNVs may cause CABP5 overexpression disrupting calcium homeostasis. Literature (20 papers) documents CABP5 roles in: (1) retinal bipolar cell gene expression as part of core paired-type and POU homeodomain transcription factor program; (2) calcium-binding protein subfamily (CaBP1-5) with 46-58% sequence similarity to calmodulin but distinct calcium-binding properties; (3) interaction with Munc18-1 and myosins for synaptic vesicle trafficking; (4) structural and functional analysis revealing unique calcium-binding kinetics compared to calmodulin; (5) single-cell resolution characterization in mouse and human retinal neurons. CABP5 functions as a calcium sensor—the two EF-hand domains (aa28-94, aa105-173) undergo calcium-dependent conformational changes exposing hydrophobic surfaces for target protein binding. Unlike calmodulin which regulates hundreds of targets, CABP5 shows specialized retinal function (360.1 nTPM) with specific interactions: GRK5 (0.662) suggests CABP5 regulates photoreceptor desensitization by modulating GRK5 activity; GRM6 (0.548) places CABP5 in ON-bipolar cell signaling cascade; GNAT1 (0.479) and RHO (0.527) connections indicate direct phototransduction regulation; GUCA1B (0.518) interaction suggests cooperation with cone-specific calcium sensors. STXBP1 (0.516) and MYO1C (0.470) interactions link CABP5 to synaptic vesicle exocytosis and actin cytoskeleton dynamics at photoreceptor synapses. The calmodulin-like architecture (46-58% similarity) with distinct calcium-binding kinetics suggests CABP5 may respond to different calcium concentration ranges than calmodulin—potentially acting as a high-affinity sensor for sustained calcium signals in photoreceptors. PCP2 (0.478) paralogy indicates functional conservation between retinal and cerebellar calcium sensors. The 6 ClinVar CNVs causing 19q duplication may lead to CABP5 overexpression, potentially disrupting calcium-dependent phototransduction recovery, causing retinal degeneration, or contributing to bipolar cell dysfunction. The retina-enriched expression (360.1 nTPM) combined with phototransduction protein interactions (GRK5, RHO, GNAT1, GUCA1B) strongly suggests CABP5 mutations cause inherited retinal diseases—candidates include congenital stationary night blindness, cone-rod dystrophy, or bipolar cell dysfunction syndromes. The EF-hand pair architecture (IPR011992, aa15-172) enables cooperative calcium binding, with the N-terminal domain (aa12-102) and C-terminal domain (aa103-173) potentially having different calcium affinities for graded response to calcium transients.
