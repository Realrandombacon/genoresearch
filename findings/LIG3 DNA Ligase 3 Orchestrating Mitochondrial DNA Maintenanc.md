## Description
LIG3 (DNA Ligase 3) is a 1009 amino acid essential ATP-dependent DNA ligase that functions as the sole ligase in mitochondria and a critical component of nuclear base excision repair (BER) and single-strand break repair (SSBR) pathways, serving as a master regulator of mitochondrial genome integrity, nuclear DNA repair fidelity, and recently identified extrachromosomal DNA (ecDNA) biogenesis. InterPro analysis identifies characteristic multi-domain architecture including N-terminal PARP-type zinc finger domain (G3DSA:3.30.1740.10, residues 91-186) for DNA break sensing and PAR binding, central ATP-dependent DNA ligase catalytic core (IPR000977, residues 319-830) with adenylation domain (cd07902, residues 468-680) performing DNA ligase activity (GO:0003910) and ATP binding (GO:0005524), OB-fold DNA-binding domain (cd07967, G3DSA:2.40.50.140, residues 686-824) for substrate recognition, C-terminal BRCT domain (IPR001357, cd18431, G3DSA:3.40.50.10190, residues 934-1007) mediating critical protein-protein interactions with XRCC1, and structural homology to DNA ligase/mRNA capping enzyme superfamily (G3DSA:3.30.470.30). AlphaFold structure (AF-P49916-F1) predicts high confidence (pLDDT 75.4) across the 1009 residue sequence, indicating a stable multi-domain enzyme with distinct catalytic and interaction modules suitable for coordinating repair complexes. HPA data classifies LIG3 as an essential disease-related enzyme involved in cell cycle, DNA damage, recombination, and repair, with nucleoplasmic localization reflecting nuclear repair roles, low tissue specificity (detected in all tissues), and disease involvement in primary mitochondrial disease and mitochondrial DNA depletion syndrome 20 (MTDPS20). STRING interactions reveal exceptionally tight coupling to the BER/SSBR machinery: XRCC1 (X-ray repair cross-complementing protein 1, 0.985) as the obligate scaffolding partner stabilizing LIG3; APTX (Aprataxin, 0.975) for strand break end processing; PARP1 (Poly [ADP-ribose] polymerase 1, 0.917) for break detection and recruitment via PARylation; APLF (Aprataxin and PNKP-like factor, 0.868) as a scaffold; POLB (DNA polymerase beta, 0.810) for gap filling; TDP1 (Tyrosyl-DNA phosphodiesterase 1, 0.802) for topoisomerase adduct removal; PNKP (Polynucleotide kinase 3'-phosphatase, 0.749) for end processing; XRCC6 (Ku70, 0.714) for alternative end-joining; PARP2 (0.547) as a backup sensor; and APEX1 (Apurinic/apyrimidinic endonuclease 1, 0.402) for AP site cleavage. ClinVar lists 44 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number losses on 17q11.2-12, missense mutations (c.2011C>T p.Arg671Trp), frameshift duplications (c.1684dup p.Leu562fs), insertions, splice site mutations (c.2331+1G>T, c.1209-2A>G), and deletions (c.535del p.Gln179fs), causing MTDPS20 characterized by progressive external ophthalmoplegia, mitochondrial myopathy, and immunodeficiency. Literature (274 papers) establishes LIG3 as a multifunctional genome guardian: extrachromosomal DNA biogenesis is dependent on DNA looping and religation by YY1-LIG3-PARylation complex linking LIG3 to cancer amplicon formation; computational investigation unveils pathogenic LIG3 non-synonymous mutations as therapeutic targets in acute myeloid leukemia; JMJD1B-mediated FEN1 demethylation allows timely switching of Okazaki fragment maturation enzymes to avoid mutagenic flap ligation by PARP1-LIG3 revealing replication stress control; immunodeficiency represents a new phenotypic insight into LIG3 mutations expanding MTDPS20 clinical spectrum; and LIG3 functions as the essential mitochondrial ligase maintaining mtDNA copy number and integrity. As a well-studied essential DNA repair enzyme with defined monogenic disease, LIG3 represents a critical node connecting mitochondrial genome maintenance to nuclear DNA repair fidelity and cancer genome evolution through XRCC1-dependent complex assembly and PARP1-mediated recruitment to DNA breaks.
