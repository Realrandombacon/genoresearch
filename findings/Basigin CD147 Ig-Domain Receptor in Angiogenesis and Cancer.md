## Description
Basigin (CD147) is a cell surface receptor characterized by multiple Immunoglobulin (Ig) domains (PF13927, IPR003598), forming a stable 3D structure with high AlphaFold confidence (pLDDT 85.2). Functionally, it operates within lipid rafts, interacting with key partners including SLC16A1 (MCT1), Caveolin-1 (CAV1), and CD44, facilitating cell adhesion, transport, and angiogenesis. High expression in heart muscle (1787.2 nTPM) and its role in host-virus interaction highlight its physiological importance. Clinically, Basigin is significant with 27 pathogenic variants, primarily copy number gains/losses at 19p13.3, and is frequently overexpressed in various cancers (breast, lung, prostate), where it promotes metastasis and drug resistance. While well-studied, its specific mechanistic role in integrating transport and adhesion signals remains a key area of research.
