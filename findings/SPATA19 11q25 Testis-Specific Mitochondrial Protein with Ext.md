## Description
SPATA19 (Spermatogenesis Associated 19, UniProt Q7Z5L4, Gene ID: 219938) is a 167 amino acid mitochondrial protein encoded on chromosome 11q25 (GRCh38: 133835929-133845512). InterPro analysis identifies a family-specific domain spanning the C-terminal two-thirds of the protein (IPR028219, PF15212, residues 64-167), classified under the PTHR36468 family. This domain architecture is unique to spermatogenesis-associated proteins, suggesting a specialized function restricted to male germ cells.\n\nSTRING interaction network reveals 10 partners (scores 0.433-0.651), forming a coherent functional cluster centered on sperm structure and mitochondrial organization: OAZ3 (0.651, Ornithine Decarboxylase Antizyme 3, polyamine regulation in spermatids), ODF4 (0.589, Outer Dense Fiber 4, sperm tail cytoskeleton), SPATA3 (0.562, Spermatogenesis Associated 3), TNP2 (0.561, Transition Protein 2, chromatin condensation), SUN5 (0.560, Sad1 and UNC84 domain containing 5, nuclear envelope-sperm head linkage), VDAC3 (0.545, Voltage Dependent Anion Channel 3, mitochondrial outer membrane, sperm motility), ODF3 (0.543, Outer Dense Fiber 3), SPEM1 (0.507, Spermiogenesis Specific 1), SPATA18 (0.486, another mitochondrial spermatogenesis factor), and ODF1 (0.433). The strong enrichment for Outer Dense Fiber (ODF) proteins and mitochondrial components (VDAC3, SPATA18) places SPATA19 squarely within the machinery governing sperm flagellar assembly and energy metabolism.\n\nHuman Protein Atlas shows extreme testis specificity, classified as "tissue enriched" and "detected in single" tissue, with very high expression levels (125.4 nTPM) in the testis and no detectable expression in other tissues, cancers, or immune cells. Subcellular location is confirmed as the mitochondrion. Biological process annotations include "Differentiation" and "Spermatogenesis." This expression profile is among the most restricted observed, indicating an essential, non-redundant role in male fertility.\n\nClinVar analysis reveals an extreme burden of 89 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, overwhelmingly large-scale copy number losses (deletions) and gains (duplications) spanning the 11q24-25 terminal region. Variants include massive deletions (11q24.1-25, ~11Mb; 11q25, ~3Mb) and duplications (11q23.3-25, ~18Mb). The high variant count reflects the instability of the 11q telomere (Jacobsen syndrome locus). While no specific monogenic disease is attributed solely to SPATA19 point mutations in ClinVar, its location within the critical region for 11q deletion syndrome suggests that haploinsufficiency contributes to the phenotype. Given its function, loss of SPATA19 likely manifests specifically as asthenozoospermia (reduced sperm motility) or teratozoospermia (abnormal sperm morphology) in male patients, a feature often overlooked in broader syndromic diagnoses.\n\nAlphaFold structure prediction (AF-Q7Z5L4-F1) covers residues 1-167 with a global pLDDT score of 69.7 (medium confidence, bordering on confident). The model predicts a compact, largely helical structure for the C-terminal domain (residues 64-167), consistent with a globular mitochondrial matrix or inner membrane protein. The N-terminal region (1-63) appears more flexible, potentially serving as a mitochondrial targeting sequence (MTS) that is cleaved upon import.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: SPATA19 functions as a critical mitochondrial protein required for the organization of sperm mitochondria into the midpiece helix and/or the regulation of oxidative phosphorylation during sperm motility. The interaction with VDAC3 (a key mitochondrial porin) and ODF proteins suggests a dual role: anchoring mitochondria to the outer dense fibers of the axoneme and regulating metabolite flux for ATP production. The extreme testis expression (125.4 nTPM) and specific interaction network imply that SPATA19 is indispensable for the high-energy demands of flagellar beating. Loss of SPATA19 (via 11q deletions) likely causes severe defects in sperm motility due to disrupted mitochondrial sheath formation or bioenergetic failure, contributing to male infertility in Jacobsen syndrome patients. The medium-confidence AlphaFold structure supports a stable globular domain capable of specific protein-protein interactions within the mitochondrial matrix.
