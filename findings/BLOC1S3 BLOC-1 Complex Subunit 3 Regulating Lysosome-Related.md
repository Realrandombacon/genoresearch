## Description
BLOC1S3 (Biogenesis of Lysosome-Related Organelles Complex 1 Subunit 3, also known as HPS8 or reduced pigmentation protein, UniProt Q6QNY0) is a 202 aa intracellular protein belonging to the BLOC-1 (Biogenesis of Lysosome-Related Organelles Complex 1) that functions in trafficking and biogenesis of lysosome-related organelles (LROs) including melanosomes, platelet dense granules, and lamellar bodies. InterPro analysis reveals canonical BLOC-1 subunit architecture: BLOC-1 complex subunit 3 family domain (IPR017245, positions 12-202; PF15753, positions 16-187; PIRSF037630, positions 12-202; PTHR31974, positions 12-190) with no additional catalytic domains, consistent with a scaffolding/adaptor protein role in vesicular trafficking complexes. AlphaFold structure shows high confidence (pLDDT 71.8), indicating a well-folded protein with stable structure for BLOC-1 complex assembly. HPA data confirms ubiquitous expression (detected in all tissues, low tissue specificity), Golgi apparatus localization, classified as predicted intracellular protein with disease involvement in albinism and Hermansky-Pudlak syndrome. STRING interactions reveal exclusive clustering with BLOC-1 complex subunits and Hermansky-Pudlak syndrome proteins: BLOC1S6 (0.999, pallidin), BLOC1S1 (0.999, dysbindin), DTNBP1 (0.996, dysbindin-1), SNAPIN (0.996, SNAP-associated protein), BLOC1S5 (0.995, Muted), BLOC1S2 (0.994, Cappuccino), BLOC1S4 (0.977, Sandy), HPS6 (0.882, HPS6 protein), HPS3 (0.815, HPS3 protein), HPS5 (0.807, HPS5 protein). ClinVar contains 27 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including frameshift (NM_212550.5:c.87dup, p.Ser30fs), nonsense (c.101C>A, p.Ser34Ter), missense (c.86G>T/p.Arg29Leu, c.335G>T/p.Arg112Leu, c.17G>A/p.Arg6His, c.449A>G/p.Gln150Arg), and inversion (c.100_101inv) mutations affecting BLOC1S3 function. Literature (28 papers) establishes BLOC1S3 roles in: (1) Hermansky-Pudlak syndrome type 8 (HPS8) pathogenesis with oculocutaneous albinism and platelet dense granule deficiency (2006, 147 citations), (2) novel BLOC1S3 mutations in mild HPS8 phenotypes (2020, 11 citations; 2012, 35 citations), (3) zinc oxide nanoparticle (ZNPs) reduction of epidermal mechanical strain resistance via mTORC1-TFEB-BLOC1S3 axis promoting desmosomal cadherin endocytosis (2024, 5 citations), and (4) BLOC-1 complex mutation screening revealing novel HPS8 variants. FUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: BLOC1S3 serves as an essential BLOC-1 complex subunit that scaffolds protein-protein interactions for vesicular trafficking machinery, directing cargo sorting and transport from early endosomes to lysosome-related organelles (melanosomes, platelet dense granules) through coordination with AP-3 adaptor complex and Rab GTPase effectors. MECHANISTIC PROPOSAL: BLOC1S3 integrates into the 8-subunit BLOC-1 complex (BLOC1S1-6, DTNBP1, SNAPIN) via direct interactions with BLOC1S6, BLOC1S1, and BLOC1S2, forming a stable platform for cargo recognition and vesicle budding. The complex binds early endosomal membranes and recruits cargo proteins (tyrosinase, tyrosinase-related proteins for melanin synthesis; soluble N-ethylmaleimide-sensitive factor attachment protein receptors for dense granule formation) through adaptor protein interactions. BLOC1S3 dysfunction disrupts BLOC-1 complex integrity, causing cargo missorting to lysosomes for degradation instead of LRO delivery. In HPS8, this causes hypopigmentation (melanosome defects), bleeding diathesis (platelet dense granule deficiency), and potential pulmonary fibrosis (lamellar body defects in type II pneumocytes). The mTORC1-TFEB-BLOC1S3 axis regulates desmosomal cadherin endocytosis, where ZNP exposure activates mTORC1, inhibiting TFEB nuclear translocation, reducing BLOC1S3 expression, and promoting desmosomal cadherin internalization, weakening epidermal mechanical resistance. The 27 ClinVar variants confirm BLOC1S3 dosage and functional sensitivity, with truncating mutations causing severe HPS8 and missense variants producing milder phenotypes. Golgi localization suggests BLOC1S3 functions in post-Golgi trafficking steps for LRO biogenesis.
