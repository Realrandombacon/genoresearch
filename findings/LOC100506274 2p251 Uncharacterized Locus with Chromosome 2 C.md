Functional hypothesis: LOC100506274 likely represents either (1) a very lowly expressed or tissue-restricted protein-coding gene that evades detection by standard proteomic surveys, (2) a long non-coding RNA with regulatory function where CNVs disrupt enhancer/promoter architecture affecting neighboring genes, or (3) a component of a larger 2p25 critical region where dosage affects multiple genes simultaneously. The 2p25 region is associated with developmental disorders, intellectual disability, and dysmorphic features when deleted or duplicated. Disease mechanism likely involves haploinsufficiency or triplosensitivity where altered dosage of LOC100506274 and/or neighboring genes disrupts neurodevelopmental programs. The large CNV spans make it difficult to attribute phenotypic effects specifically to this locus versus adjacent genes. Priority experiments include transcript identification via RNA-seq across diverse tissues (particularly brain given 2p25 syndrome associations), ORF prediction and conservation analysis across primates, CRISPR-based functional assays to determine phenotypic consequences, and careful genotype-phenotype correlation in patients with smaller, more localized CNVs.
