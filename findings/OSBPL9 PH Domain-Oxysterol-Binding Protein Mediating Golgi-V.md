## Description
OSBPL9/Q96SU4 (736 aa) is a confident (AlphaFold pLDDT: 75.9) oxysterol-binding protein family member functioning as a critical regulator of intracellular lipid transport between Golgi apparatus and vesicular compartments through membrane contact site formation. InterPro analysis reveals canonical architecture containing an N-terminal pleckstrin homology PH domain (IPR001849/PF00169/G3DSA:2.30.29.30, residues 2-101/4-98/1-103) for phosphoinositide binding and membrane targeting, followed by a large C-terminal oxysterol-binding protein domain (IPR000648/PF01237/IPR037239, residues 5-725/378-671/369-730) with conserved sterol-binding signature site (IPR018494, residues 497-507) mediating lipid exchange activity (GO:0008289), classifying it within the oxysterol-binding protein superfamily. HPA data confirms ubiquitous expression (detected in all tissues) with low tissue specificity but cell type-enhanced pattern, classification as plasma proteins/predicted intracellular proteins with lipid transport biological process, localized to Golgi apparatus and vesicles consistent with intracellular lipid trafficking function. STRING interactions define an exceptionally high-confidence membrane contact site network linking OSBPL9 to VAPB (0.964), DNAJA1 (0.611), VAPA (0.558), PI4KB (0.528), OSBPL3 (0.476), OSBP (0.473), OSBP2 (0.427), OSBPL11 (0.406), OSBPL7 (0.403), and PLEK2 (0.400) - collectively representing the VAP-mediated ER-Golgi membrane contact machinery and oxysterol-binding protein family paralogs essential for non-vesicular lipid transfer. Literature (24 papers) establishes emerging neurodevelopmental disease mechanisms: 2025 landmark case report describes first known homozygous nonsense variant in OSBPL9 associated with fetal cerebral ventriculomegaly, cerebellar hypoplasia, and arthrogryposis multiplex, establishing the first monogenic OSBPL9-associated neurodevelopmental disorder; foundational work characterizes OSBPL9 as member of large eukaryotic lipid transport protein family mediating sterol and phospholipid exchange between trans-Golgi network and other compartments. ClinVar documents 12 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including chromosome 1p copy number alterations (1p34.1-22.2 gains, 1p33-32.2 gains, 1p34.1-32.3 losses, 1p32.3-32.2 losses, 1p33-32.3 losses, 1p36.33-q44 gains), single nucleotide variants (c.1377C>T p.Gly459=, c.1171-58A>T, c.319-5402T>G), and inversions implicating OSBPL9 dosage sensitivity in neurodevelopmental disorders though specific monogenic phenotypes emerging from 2025 case report suggest recessive loss-of-function causes severe brain malformation syndrome. The convergence of confident PH-oxysterol-binding architecture (pLDDT 75.9), exclusive VAP-mediated membrane contact site partnerships (VAPB-VAPA axis with exceptional 0.964 confidence), ubiquitous expression with Golgi/vesicular localization, 2025-established homozygous nonsense variant-associated neurodevelopmental syndrome with cerebral ventriculomegaly/cerebellar hypoplasia/arhrogryposis, oxysterol-binding protein family paralog network (OSBP/OSBPL3/OSBPL11/OSBPL7), and chromosome 1p dosage sensitivity suggests OSBPL9 functions as a phosphoinositide-targeted lipid transporter coordinating sterol/phospholipid exchange at ER-Golgi membrane contact sites through VAPB/VAPA tethering, maintaining Golgi membrane composition for proper vesicle budding and trafficking, and regulating lipid-dependent signaling pathways essential for neuronal migration, cerebellar development, and neuromuscular junction formation, with therapeutic potential in understanding neurodevelopmental disorder pathogenesis (cerebral ventriculomegaly, cerebellar hypoplasia, arthrogryposis multiplex), targeting membrane contact site defects in brain malformation syndromes, and modulating intracellular lipid trafficking in OSBPL9-associated congenital anomalies.
