## Description
ZNF702P (zinc finger protein 702 pseudogene, Gene ID: 79986) is a 129-amino acid protein encoded on chromosome 19q13.41 that represents a KRAB-less C2H2 zinc finger protein with remarkable structural integrity despite its pseudogene annotation. InterPro analysis reveals a canonical three-finger C2H2 zinc finger architecture: three tandem C2H2-type zinc finger domains (IPR013087, positions 6-33, 34-61, 62-89; PF00096 at positions 34-54, 62-84, 90-112) with conserved C2H2 signatures (PS00028, positions 36-56, 64-84, 92-112) and classification within diverse zinc finger transcription regulators (PTHR14003, positions 5-89). The zinc finger domains adopt the classic beta-beta-alpha fold (G3DSA:3.30.160.60; SSF57667) characteristic of sequence-specific DNA-binding proteins. AlphaFold structure prediction shows very high confidence (global pLDDT 89.5), indicating an exceptionally well-folded, stable protein with defined zinc-coordinating structures—unusually high for a pseudogene annotation. STRING database returned no interaction partners, and HPA has no expression data (ENSG00000293317 not in database), reflecting its understudied nature and potentially restricted expression pattern. ClinVar contains zero variants—no known pathogenic mutations or disease associations. Literature analysis shows only 2 total papers (VERY DARK status), with a notable 2022 study identifying ZNF702P as part of a ceRNA (competing endogenous RNA) network in glucocorticoid-induced osteoporosis (GIOP), where Zuogui Pill treatment modulates ZNF702P expression, and a 2013 study linking KRAB domain-containing zinc-finger genes to breast cancer (MDA-MB-231) stem cell maintenance. The absence of a KRAB domain is functionally significant: unlike canonical KRAB-ZNF repressors that recruit KAP1/TRIM28 for heterochromatin formation, ZNF702P may function as a transcriptional activator, compete with KRAB-ZNFs for DNA binding sites, or act at the RNA level as a ceRNA sponge for microRNAs. The three-finger configuration suggests recognition of ~9-10 bp DNA sequences. The osteoporosis association suggests potential roles in bone metabolism or osteoblast/osteoclast differentiation. Despite its pseudogene annotation, the high-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 89.5), intact zinc finger domains, and emerging disease associations suggest ZNF702P may retain functional capacity either as a DNA-binding protein or regulatory RNA. This represents a genuine dark gene in the largest human transcription factor family with potential roles in bone disease and cancer biology.
