## Description
ACTR6 (Actin-related protein 6, also known as Arp6) is a 396 aa nuclear protein representing a genuine dark gene with only 8 literature references, yet it plays a pivotal role in epigenetic regulation as a core structural component of multi-subunit chromatin remodeling complexes. Despite its actin fold, ACTR6 has lost polymerization capability and instead functions as a static scaffold within the SRCAP (Snf2-related CREBBP activator protein) and p400/EP400 complexes, which are responsible for exchanging the canonical histone H2A with the variant H2A.Z at specific genomic loci. InterPro analysis confirms a canonical actin family architecture (IPR004000/PF00022, aa 1-395) containing a conserved nucleotide-binding domain (NBD) (cd10210, aa 3-386) divided into two lobes (G3DSA:2.30.36.70 and G3DSA:3.90.640.10), characteristic of the actin-like ATPase superfamily (IPR043129). AlphaFold predicts an exceptionally high-confidence structure (pLDDT 94.3), reflecting the extreme evolutionary constraint on the actin fold required for precise integration into the remodeling machine. HPA shows ubiquitous expression (detected in all tissues) with localization to the nucleus and plasma membrane, consistent with roles in both chromatin dynamics and potential cytoskeletal crosstalk. STRING interactions reveal a tightly defined functional module: ACTR6 binds with near-perfect confidence to the core ATPases and chaperones of the H2A.Z deposition machinery, including SRCAP (0.999), RUVBL1/RUVBL2 (0.999/0.998, AAA+ ATPases), ZNHIT1 (0.998, H2A.Z chaperone), DMAP1 (0.962), EP400 (0.930), and the histone variant H2AZ1 itself (0.956). ClinVar lists 9 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, exclusively large copy number gains on chromosome 12q encompassing the ACTR6 locus, suggesting that gene dosage imbalance disrupts the stoichiometry of the remodeling complex and leads to developmental or oncogenic phenotypes. Recent literature (2025-2026) identifies ACTR6 as a critical regulator of hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) stemness, where it maintains the undifferentiated state of tumor cells by activating the Hedgehog signaling pathway through H2A.Z-mediated chromatin accessibility at key promoters. The scarcity of literature (8 papers) despite its fundamental role in transcription makes ACTR6 a paradigmatic dark gene: a 'housekeeping' epigenetic factor whose specific disease mechanisms remain largely unexplored.
