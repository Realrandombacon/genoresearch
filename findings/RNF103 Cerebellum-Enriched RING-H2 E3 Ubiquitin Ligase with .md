# RNF103: Cerebellum-Enriched RING-H2 E3 Ubiquitin Ligase with Neuropsychiatric Disease Associations

## Description
RNF103 (Ring Finger Protein 103, UniProt O00237) encodes a 685 amino acid E3 ubiquitin-protein ligase featuring a C-terminal RING-H2 zinc finger domain (PF13639, IPR001841, residues 620-663) that catalyzes ubiquitin transfer to substrate proteins. The RING domain architecture (C3HC4 zinc-binding motif) positions RNF103 within the ubiquitin-proteasome system, likely mediating K48-linked polyubiquitination for proteasomal degradation or K63-linked chains for signaling. HPA expression profiling shows ubiquitous detection across all tissues with immune cell enhancement, though gene_info notes particularly high expression in normal cerebellum versus cerebral cortex, suggesting specialized roles in cerebellar Purkinje cell maintenance or synaptic protein turnover. Rat ortholog studies demonstrate Rnf103 induction in frontal cortex and hippocampus following electroconvulsive treatment (ECT) and chronic antidepressant administration, implicating this ligase in activity-dependent neuronal plasticity and stress response pathways. ClinVar documents 13 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number losses and gains spanning 2p11.2, indicating dosage sensitivity where altered RNF103 levels disrupt ubiquitin homeostasis, potentially contributing to neurodevelopmental or neuropsychiatric phenotypes. The conserved RING-H2 finger (mouse Q9R1W3, 99% identity; rat Q9EPZ8, 98% identity) across vertebrates supports an essential role in protein quality control or receptor trafficking. Mechanistically, RNF103 likely functions as a substrate-specific E3 ligase targeting misfolded synaptic proteins or signaling receptors for degradation, with dysregulation leading to proteotoxic stress in cerebellar neurons.
