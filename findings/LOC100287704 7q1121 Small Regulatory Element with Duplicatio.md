# LOC100287704: 7q11.21 Small Regulatory Element with Duplication-Predominant Pathogenicity Near Williams-Beuren Syndrome Region

**Date:** 2026-03-19T03:02:04.184830

**Quality Score:** 3.6/10  (E=4.5, D=2.7) [MODERATE]

## Description
LOC100287704 is an uncharacterized genomic locus on chromosome 7q11.21 (genomic coordinates: 63349069-63351773 on NC_000007.14, spanning approximately 2.7 kb). This region lacks protein-coding evidence (no UniProt entry, no STRING interactions, no HPA protein expression data), indicating it is likely a non-coding regulatory element, long non-coding RNA, or structural genomic region rather than a protein-coding gene. ClinVar analysis identified 6 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants with duplication-predominant pathogenicity: 4 copy number gains (duplications) and 2 copy number losses (deletions), indicating triplosensitivity is the primary pathogenic mechanism with some haploinsufficiency. The 7q11.21 chromosomal region is adjacent to the Williams-Beuren syndrome critical region (7q11.23), which contains dosage-sensitive genes including ELN (elastin - supravalvular aortic stenosis), LIMK1 (cofilin kinase - cognitive phenotype), RFC2 (replication factor C), and BAZ1B (bromodomain adjacent to zinc finger protein 1B - Williams-Beuren syndrome transcription factor). The duplication-predominant pattern (67% gains vs 33% losses) contrasts with the deletion-predominant pattern seen in LOC101928211 (70% losses) and the balanced mixed pattern in LOC101928283, suggesting LOC100287704 may function as a triplosensitivity-critical regulatory element. Gain of this element likely causes overexpression of target genes in cis, potentially contributing to 7q11.21 duplication syndrome phenotypes including developmental delay, speech abnormalities, and behavioral issues. This 2.7 kb element may represent a compact enhancer or chromatin boundary element that regulates ELN or BAZ1B expression.
