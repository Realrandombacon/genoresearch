## Description
FAM72A (UniProt Q5TYM5) encodes a 149-amino acid intracellular protein characterized by a conserved Yippee homolog double zinc finger domain (InterPro IPR026768, residues 2-149; Pfam PF14976, residues 6-149), defining it as a member of the Yippee-like zinc finger protein family. AlphaFold prediction yields a high-confidence structure (global pLDDT 86.1), indicating a stable, well-folded conformation suitable for specific protein-protein interactions. STRING interaction analysis reveals a functional network centered on cell cycle regulation and mitotic spindle assembly, with medium-confidence partners including FAM72B (0.636), FAM72D (0.478), CDCA8 (Borealin, 0.472), DLGAP5 (CHMP1A/TPX2 partner, 0.449), KIF2C (MCAK kinesin, 0.454), TTK (MPS1 kinase, 0.448), and ARHGAP11B (0.467). Human Protein Atlas reports tissue-enhanced expression with peak levels in lymphoid tissue (10.3 nTPM) and ubiquitous detection across many tissues; subcellular localization includes cytosol, vesicles, and plasma membrane. ClinVar documents 17 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variants (large 1q21.1-q44 deletions and duplications) disrupting FAM72A, associated with 1q deletion/duplication syndromes featuring developmental delay, intellectual disability, and microcephaly.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: FAM72A functions as a regulatory scaffold within the chromosomal passenger complex (CPC) or mitotic spindle assembly checkpoint, leveraging its Yippee-type zinc finger domain to stabilize interactions between core mitotic regulators. The strong association with CDCA8 (Borealin), DLGAP5, KIF2C, and TTK suggests FAM72A modulates CPC localization to centromeres or regulates microtubule-kinetochore attachment stability. Specifically, FAM72A may act as a bridging factor that recruits or stabilizes Borealin (CDCA8) at the inner centromere, ensuring proper Aurora B kinase activity and error correction during metaphase. Its interaction with ARHGAP11B, a human-specific gene involved in basal progenitor amplification and cortical expansion, hints at a specialized role in neurogenesis where precise mitotic control is critical. Haploinsufficiency or overexpression via 1q CNVs disrupts this mitotic checkpoint, leading to aneuploidy, apoptotic dysregulation (consistent with GO annotation), and the neurodevelopmental phenotypes observed in 1q syndrome. The lymphoid expression peak further suggests a role in rapid proliferative contexts such as immune response.
