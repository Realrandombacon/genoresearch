## Description
TMEM119 encodes a 283-amino acid type I transmembrane protein (UniProt Q4V9L6) belonging to the TMEM119 family (InterPro IPR031453, Pfam PF15724, Panther PTHR28645, residues 25-277). The protein contains a single transmembrane domain and is evolutionarily conserved with mouse ortholog Q8R138 (280 aa, ~90% identity).

Expression profiling (HPA) reveals tissue-enhanced RNA expression with highest levels in cervix (57.2 nTPM) and detectable expression across multiple tissues. Gene ontology annotations link TMEM119 to ossification (GO:0001503), biomineralization, osteogenesis, differentiation, and spermatogenesis.

Protein interaction network (STRING-DB) identifies 10 high-confidence partners: GPR34 (0.737), CST7 (0.718), TREM2 (0.660), CX3CR1 (0.555), AIF1/IBA1 (0.502), C1QA (0.500), OLFML3 (0.487), P2RY12 (0.474), CSF1R (0.447), and SALL1 (0.418). Notably, TREM2, CX3CR1, AIF1, C1QA, and P2RY12 are established microglial markers, suggesting TMEM119 functions in microglial identity and phagocytic activity. GPR34 is a lysophosphatidylserine receptor implicated in microglial surveillance. CSF1R is the macrophage colony-stimulating factor receptor essential for microglial development.

Clinical significance (ClinVar): 10 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variants (gains and losses) mapped to 12q23-q24 region encompassing TMEM119, though specific disease phenotypes remain uncharacterized. The copy number alterations suggest dosage sensitivity.

Mechanistic hypothesis: TMEM119 functions as an osteoblast-specific transmembrane scaffold protein that coordinates bone mineralization through protein-protein interactions with mineralization pathway components. In parallel, TMEM119 serves as a microglial homeostatic marker, potentially modulating TREM2-dependent phagocytic signaling and lysophosphatidylserine recognition via GPR34 coupling. The dual tissue expression pattern (bone/immune) suggests ancestral role in calcium-dependent cellular processes co-opted for microglial surveillance function.
