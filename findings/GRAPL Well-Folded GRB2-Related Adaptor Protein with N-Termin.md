## Description
GRAPL (GRB2-related adaptor protein-like, UniProt Q8TC17) is a 118-amino acid intracellular protein that functions as a signal transduction adapter linking activated receptor tyrosine kinases or immune receptors to downstream signaling pathways. InterPro analysis reveals defining GRB2-like adapter architecture: N-terminal Src homology 3 (SH3) domain (IPR001452/G3DSA:2.30.30.40/cd11948/IPR035645, aa 1-58/1-60/2-55/2-55) mediating proline-rich motif binding, and C-terminal Src homology 2 (SH2) domain (IPR000980/G3DSA:3.30.505.10/IPR036860/PF00017, aa 58-118/61-108/61-108/60-103) recognizing phosphotyrosine motifs on activated receptors — this canonical SH3-SH2-SH3 architecture (though GRAPL appears to lack the second SH3) is characteristic of GRB2-family adapters that nucleate signaling complexes. GO annotations confirm protein binding (GO:0005515). AlphaFold structure prediction achieves high confidence (pLDDT 86.2), indicating a well-folded protein with stable SH3 and SH2 domain folds — this exceptional structural confidence reflects the highly conserved adapter domain architecture enabling reliable modeling of ligand-binding surfaces. Human Protein Atlas shows tissue-enhanced expression for GRAPL (ENSG00000189152) with top expression in lymphoid tissue at 24.2 nTPM and intestine at 10.7 nTPM, detected in many tissues, low cancer specificity, immune cell-enriched blood cell specificity, group-enriched single cell specificity, and centrosome subcellular localization — this lymphoid/immune enrichment strongly suggests roles in T-cell or B-cell receptor signaling, while centrosomal localization indicates potential functions in cell division or ciliary signaling. STRING interaction network reveals 4 partners with functional coherence centered on immune signaling: LCNL1 (0.487, lipocalin-like protein 1), WAS (0.418, Wiskott-Aldrich syndrome protein — actin cytoskeleton regulator critical for immune cell function and hematopoiesis), RASGRP2 (0.403, Ras guanyl releasing protein 2 — calcium-regulated Ras activator in lymphocytes), and LRRC24 (0.403, leucine rich repeat containing 24). The WAS and RASGRP2 interactions definitively establish GRAPL within lymphocyte signaling pathways, potentially functioning analogously to GRB2 in coupling antigen receptor activation to Ras-MAPK signaling cascades. ClinVar contains 99 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants consisting entirely of copy number variations spanning chromosome 17p11.2 region (17p11.2, 17p12-11.2, 17p13.3-11.2) — this is the critical region for Smith-Magenis syndrome (17p11.2 deletion) and Potocki-Lupski syndrome (17p11.2 duplication), neurodevelopmental disorders characterized by intellectual disability, behavioral abnormalities, and congenital anomalies, and while no specific disease is attributed solely to GRAPL, haploinsufficiency likely contributes to immune dysfunction and developmental phenotypes through disrupted adapter-mediated signaling. Conservation across vertebrates indicates evolutionary origin tied to adaptive immune system development. GRAPL likely functions as a phosphotyrosine-dependent adapter that uses its SH2 domain to bind activated immune receptors (potentially TCR or BCR components) and its SH3 domain to recruit proline-rich signaling effectors (such as SOS, VAV, or WAS), nucleating multiprotein complexes that propagate activation signals — the high structural confidence (pLDDT 86.2) enabling future structural studies to define specific ligand preferences and develop therapeutic modulators for immune disorders.
