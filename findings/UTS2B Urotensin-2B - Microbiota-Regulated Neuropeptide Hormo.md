## Description
UTS2B (Urotensin-2B, UniProt Q765I0) is a 119 amino acid secreted peptide hormone functioning as a novel regulator of the gut-brain axis, glucose homeostasis, and sensory neuron signaling. With only 10 literature references (genuine dark gene status), UTS2B represents a paralog of the potent vasoconstrictor Urotensin-II (UTS2) but with distinct expression patterns and physiological roles centered on enteroendocrine communication. The protein contains a conserved Urotensin II signature motif (IPR001483, PS00984, aa 113-118) featuring the characteristic cyclic hexapeptide structure (CFWKYC) critical for receptor binding, embedded within the larger Urotensin-2B family domain (IPR043255, PF28458, aa 2-101). AlphaFold predicts a medium-confidence structure (pLDDT 67.0), consistent with small secreted peptides that are often disordered in isolation but adopt defined conformations upon receptor binding or when part of a larger prohormone precursor. STRING interactions reveal functional links to G-protein coupled receptors and related peptide systems: CYSLTR1 (0.622 - Cysteinyl leukotriene receptor 1), CCKBR (0.572 - Cholecystokinin B receptor), AGTR1 (0.571 - Angiotensin II receptor type 1), CCK (0.549 - Cholecystokinin), UTS2 (0.488 - Urotensin II), and its putative receptor UTS2R (0.400). HPA confirms tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in retina (5.9 nTPM) and intestine (3.2 nTPM), classified as a predicted secreted protein with hormone function, aligning with its role in neuro-endocrine signaling. ClinVar documents 40 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains (3q22.1-29, 3q26.31-29) and deletions, though specific monogenic diseases directly attributed to UTS2B point mutations remain undefined, suggesting potential contribution to complex metabolic or neuropsychiatric disorders via dosage effects. Key literature findings: (1) 2022 landmark study identified UTS2B as a microbiota-regulated gene expressed specifically in vagal afferent neurons synapsing with CCK-producing enteroendocrine cells, establishing a direct gut-microbiota-neuron circuit (6 citations), (2) 2019 study defined UTS2B as a marker for a novel enteroendocrine cell population regulating GLP-1 secretion through SSTR5 (somatostatin receptor 5) in male mice, linking it to insulin release and glucose control (4 citations), (3) 2022 chick retina study showed UTS2B downregulation during lens-induced myopia, implicating it in ocular growth regulation (5 citations). Functional hypothesis: UTS2B functions as a paracrine/neurocrine ligand for GPCRs (likely UTS2R or related receptors like CCKBR/SSTR5) to modulate neuronal excitability and hormone secretion. Unlike systemic UTS2 which regulates vascular tone, UTS2B operates locally in the gut mucosa and retina. In the intestine, microbiota-derived signals induce UTS2B expression in vagal afferents, creating a feedback loop that enhances CCK release from neighboring EECs, thereby regulating satiety, gastric emptying, and pancreatic enzyme secretion. The interaction with SSTR5 suggests UTS2B may fine-tune GLP-1 release, positioning it as a potential target for type 2 diabetes therapy. In the retina, UTS2B may regulate emmetropization (eye growth) via local neuropeptide signaling, with dysregulation contributing to myopia. Disease mechanism: Dysregulation of UTS2B (via CNVs or altered microbiota) may contribute to metabolic syndrome (impaired GLP-1/insulin axis), obesity (altered satiety signaling), and vision disorders (myopia). The 40 ClinVar CNVs suggest that gross genomic instability at 3q29 (a known microdeletion/duplication syndrome locus) could disrupt UTS2B dosage, potentially contributing to the neurodevelopmental and metabolic phenotypes observed in 3q29 syndrome patients. Therapeutic implication: UTS2B agonists could enhance GLP-1 secretion for diabetes treatment, while antagonists might treat hyperinsulinemia; targeting the microbiota-UTS2B axis offers a novel probiotic/prebiotic strategy for metabolic health.
