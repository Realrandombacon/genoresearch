# MRAP2 (Melanocortin-2 Receptor Accessory Protein 2): Blood Vessel-Enriched Single-Pass Membrane Protein Potentiating Melanocortin Receptor Signaling (MC1R-MC5R), Regulating Energy Homeostasis and Appetite via MC4R/MC3R Interaction, with Severe Obesity Association and GHSR/Ghrelin Receptor Modulation

## Description
MRAP2 encodes a 205 aa single-pass membrane protein (UniProt Q96G30) that functions as an essential accessory protein modulating all five melanocortin receptors (MC1R-MC5R) through direct physical interaction, regulating receptor trafficking, cell surface expression, ligand binding, and G protein coupling. This well-studied gene (181 publications) has strong clinical significance for severe early-onset obesity. The protein features a simple MRAP family architecture: melanocortin-2 receptor accessory protein family domain (IPR028111/PF15183/PTHR28675, residues 1-205/13-99) defining the accessory protein function without enzymatic activity; classified as predicted membrane protein with disease involvement in obesity. STRING interactions reveal MRAP2 operates within melanocortin signaling and energy homeostasis networks: MC2R (0.941), adrenocorticotropic hormone receptor in adrenal steroidogenesis; MC4R (0.857), melanocortin-4 receptor controlling appetite and food intake; MC5R (0.835), melanocortin-5 receptor in exocrine gland function; PROKR1 (0.752), prokineticin receptor 1 in angiogenesis and neurogenesis; MC1R (0.706), melanocortin-1 receptor in pigmentation; POMC (0.629), proopiomelanocortin precursor of melanocortin ligands; AGRP (0.599), agouti-related peptide endogenous MC4R antagonist; MRAP (0.589), paralog MRAP1 specific for MC2R; MC3R (0.474), melanocortin-3 receptor in energy homeostasis; and GHSR (0.444), growth hormone secretagogue receptor (ghrelin receptor) linking to hunger signaling. HPA confirms tissue-enriched expression with blood vessel enrichment (97.2 nTPM), detected in many tissues, low cancer specificity, not detected in immune cells, classified as disease-related/predicted membrane protein with obesity disease involvement. ClinVar lists 18 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including missense mutations affecting transmembrane or cytoplasmic domains (c.521A>G p.Gln174Arg, c.289C>T p.Pro97Ser within PF15183 domain), frameshift deletion causing premature termination (c.116del p.Lys39fs), deep intronic variant (c.228-1835C>T), single allele deletion, and extensive copy number losses (6q14.1-14.3 82-86Mb x1, 6q14.1-15 83-88Mb x1, 6q13-14.2 73-84Mb x1) and gains (6q12-16.1 69-94Mb, 6q14.1-16.1 81-97Mb x3), demonstrating strong dosage sensitivity with obesity phenotype. AlphaFold structure prediction yields medium confidence (global pLDDT 57.1), indicating a partially structured protein with significant intrinsically disordered regions characteristic of single-pass membrane proteins with flexible cytoplasmic tails that undergo conformational changes upon receptor binding. Mechanistically, MRAP2 operates through integrated modes: (1) Melanocortin receptor potentiation—2025 studies demonstrate MRAP2 directly interacts with MC4R and MC3R to enhance cAMP signaling, modifying receptor oligomerization state and increasing cell surface expression; (2) Obesity pathogenesis—gene summary confirms MRAP2 mutations cause severe early-onset obesity through impaired melanocortin signaling in hypothalamic appetite control circuits; mouse knockouts exhibit severe obesity phenotype; (3) Conserved GPCR modulation—2025 study shows obesity-associated genetic variants disrupt conserved mechanisms of GPCR potentiation, suggesting MRAP2 represents therapeutic target for obesity treatment; (4) Ghrelin receptor interaction—GHSR connection suggests MRAP2 may modulate ghrelin-mediated hunger signaling beyond melanocortin system; (5) Prokineticin signaling—PROKR1 interaction links to angiogenesis and neurogenesis pathways potentially explaining vascular enrichment; (6) Paralog specificity—unlike MRAP1 which specifically regulates MC2R for adrenal function, MRAP2 broadly modulates all MC receptors with particular importance for MC3R/MC4R in energy balance. Loss-of-function causes defective melanocortin receptor trafficking and signaling, impaired appetite regulation, severe early-onset obesity, disrupted ghrelin response, and altered energy homeostasis.
