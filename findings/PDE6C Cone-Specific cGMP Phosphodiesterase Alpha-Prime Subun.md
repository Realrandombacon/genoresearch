# PDE6C: Cone-Specific cGMP Phosphodiesterase Alpha-Prime Subunit for Phototransduction Cascade in Achromatopsia Type 5 and Cone Dystrophy Type 4 via cGMP Hydrolysis and CNG Channel Regulation

## Description
PDE6C encodes an 858 aa cone cGMP-specific 3',5'-cyclic phosphodiesterase subunit alpha-prime (UniProt P51160), also known as ACHM5 or COD4, functioning as the catalytic homodimer component of cone photoreceptor phosphodiesterase that hydrolyzes cGMP during phototransduction, enabling color vision and high-acuity daylight vision. The protein contains two N-terminal GAF domains (IPR003018, aa75-234 and aa256-443) for cGMP binding and allosteric regulation, followed by a C-terminal HD/PDEase catalytic domain (IPR002073/IPR003607/cd00077, aa486-819) with conserved metal-binding motifs (IPR023174, aa602-613) coordinating zinc/magnesium ions for phosphodiester bond cleavage, belonging to the 3'5'-cyclic nucleotide phosphodiesterase superfamily (IPR023088/G3DSA:1.10.1300.10, aa482-819). AlphaFold prediction shows high confidence (pLDDT 88.2), indicating a well-folded multi-domain structure with GAF regulatory domains connected to catalytic core. HPA shows retina-enriched expression (21.4 nTPM) with tissue-enriched specificity, cell type-enriched pattern in photoreceptors, and disease involvement in vision disorders. STRING interactions reveal PDE6C forms the cone phototransduction complex: CNGB3 (0.984, cyclic nucleotide-gated channel beta-3), CNGA3 (0.976, CNG channel alpha-3), PDE6H (0.833, inhibitory gamma subunit), GNAT2 (0.420, cone transducin alpha), plus nucleotide metabolism enzymes GUK1 (0.931), APRT (0.906), GMPS/GMPR/HPRT1/ITPA (all 0.900). ClinVar contains 126 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including frameshifts (p.Glu712fs, p.Lys27fs, p.Thr580fs, p.Arg82fs, p.Gly525fs), splice site mutations (c.481-1G>C, c.480+2T>C, c.939+2T>C), missense mutations (p.Arg565Trp corresponding to R565Q primate model), and copy number gains. Literature (165 papers) demonstrates PDE6C mutations cause achromatopsia type 5 (ACHM5) featuring complete color blindness, nystagmus, photophobia, reduced visual acuity, and absent cone ERG responses; PDE6C mutations cause cone dystrophy type 4 (COD4) with progressive cone degeneration; rhesus macaque PDE6C R565Q mutation serves as natural animal model for gene therapy development; AAV-mediated gene therapy shows promise in nonhuman primate PDE6C achromatopsia models; PDE6C homodimer structure differs from rod PDE6A/B heterotetramer enabling faster cone response kinetics; and PDE6C variants co-segregate with hearing loss in PDZD7 dual-diagnosis cases. PDE6C functions as the effector enzyme in cone phototransduction—light-activated cone opsin triggers transducin (GNAT2) GTPase, which activates PDE6C homodimer to hydrolyze cGMP, causing CNG channel closure (CNGA3/CNGB3), membrane hyperpolarization, and reduced glutamate release. GAF domains bind cGMP allosterically, modulating catalytic activity and enabling rapid response recovery essential for cone temporal resolution. ACHM5/COD4 pathogenesis reflects PDE6C loss-of-function: absent cGMP hydrolysis causes constitutive CNG channel opening, depolarization block, calcium overload, and cone photoreceptor degeneration. The R565Q mutation in the catalytic domain reduces enzymatic activity while preserving protein stability, making it amenable to gene augmentation therapy. Retina-specific expression underscores PDE6C's exclusive role in cone vision, distinguishing it from rod-specific PDE6A/B.
