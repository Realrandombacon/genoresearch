## Description
FCSK encodes L-fucose kinase (fucokinase/FUK), a 1084 aa cytosolic enzyme (UniProt Q8N0W3) belonging to the GHMP (galacto-, homoserine, mevalonate, phosphomevalonate) kinase superfamily that catalyzes the ATP-dependent phosphorylation of free L-fucose to beta-L-fucose 1-phosphate, representing the committed first step in the fucose salvage pathway essential for GDP-fucose biosynthesis and subsequent protein/glycolipid fucosylation. InterPro reveals complex multi-domain architecture characteristic of eukaryotic fucose kinases: N-terminal GDP-fucose pyrophosphorylase domain (IPR012887/PF07959, residues 82-496) unexpectedly present despite distinct enzymatic function suggesting evolutionary domain fusion or regulatory role, central GHMP kinase N-terminal domain (IPR006204/PF00288, residues 817-889) containing the ATP-binding pocket with conserved nucleotide-binding motifs, C-terminal GHMP kinase domain (IPR013750/PF08544, residues 971-1046) completing the catalytic core, ribosomal protein uS5 domain 2-type superfamily (IPR020568/G3DSA:3.30.230.120, residues 705-901) potentially mediating protein-protein interactions, and full-length GHMP kinase-related enzyme family signature (IPR052203/PTHR32463, residues 1-1072) defining enzymatic classification with kinase/transferase activity. AlphaFold predicts very high confidence structure (pLDDT 92.1) indicating a highly stable, well-folded multi-domain enzyme requiring precise active site geometry for catalysis with potential interdomain flexibility enabling substrate channeling between catalytic sites. ClinVar lists 79 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including nonsense mutation truncating catalytic domains (c.1197C>A/p.Cys399Ter), frameshift deletion disrupting central region (c.860del/p.Pro287fs), missense variant affecting conserved residue (c.556C>T/p.His186Tyr), readthrough mutation extending C-terminus (c.3255A>G/p.Ter1085Trp), splice site mutations (c.412-2A>G), and duplication causing frameshift (c.503_527dup/p.Pro180fs) with FCSK-CDG (fucokinase deficiency-related congenital disorder of glycosylation) as the definitive monogenic phenotype characterized by intellectual disability, developmental delay, hypotonia, seizures, distinctive facial features, and impaired protein fucosylation detectable by transferrin isoelectric focusing. Functional mechanism: FCSK operates through ordered bi-bi kinetic mechanism typical of GHMP kinases: (1) ATP binding - GHMP N-terminal domain binds Mg2+-ATP complex positioning gamma-phosphate for transfer via conserved nucleotide-binding motifs; (2) Fucose recruitment - flexible interdomain regions capture free L-fucose from cytosolic pool generated by lysosomal degradation of fucosylated glycoconjugates; (3) Phosphotransfer - nucleophilic attack of fucose hydroxyl group on ATP gamma-phosphate forms beta-L-fucose 1-phosphate with ADP release; (4) Product channeling - fucose-1-P directly transferred to FPGT for conversion to GDP-fucose minimizing diffusion loss. Disease mechanism: FCSK-CDG results from impaired GDP-fucose production via salvage pathway causing hypofucosylation of critical substrates including Notch receptors (requiring O-fucose for ligand binding and signaling), selectins (E/P/L-selectins requiring fucosylated ligands for leukocyte trafficking), blood group antigens (ABO system), and mucins, explaining neurological phenotype given Notch signaling roles in neurogenesis and synaptic plasticity, immune dysfunction from impaired leukocyte homing, and developmental defects from disrupted cell-cell communication. Recent 2024-2026 literature reveals therapeutic advances: D-mannose supplementation bypasses fucose kinase defect by providing alternative substrate for GDP-fucose synthesis via mannose-fucose metabolic interconversion, zebrafish knockout models demonstrate neurodevelopmental defects validating pathogenicity and enabling drug screening, CRISPR cell models reveal defective fucosylation patterns and rescue strategies, bacterial homologs (fcsK in Streptococcus) suggest evolutionary conservation of fucose utilization pathways.
