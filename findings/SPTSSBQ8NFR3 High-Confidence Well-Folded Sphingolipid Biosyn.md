## Description
SPTSSB (Serine palmitoyltransferase small subunit B, also known as ADMP, C3orf57, SSSPTB) is a 76 amino acid small membrane protein (UniProt Q8NFR3) with high-confidence well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 86.5), characteristic of the small regulatory subunits of serine palmitoyltransferase (SPT). The protein defines the Small subunit of serine palmitoyltransferase-like family (IPR024512/PTHR28612, PF11779, residues 7-59/1-73), which associates with the catalytic SPT heterodimer to form the active holoenzyme. SPT catalyzes the first committed and rate-limiting step in sphingolipid biosynthesis: the condensation of L-serine and palmitoyl-CoA to form 3-ketodihydrosphingosine. SPTSSB stimulates SPT activity and confers acyl-CoA substrate preference, determining which sphingolipid species are produced. Molecular functions include lipid metabolism and sphingolipid metabolism, placing SPTSSB within the ceramide biosynthesis pathway.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals an exceptionally tight network of sphingolipid biosynthesis machinery: strongest interactions with SPTLC3 (0.998, Serine palmitoyltransferase long chain base subunit 3), ORMDL3 (0.935, ORM family lipid metabolism regulator 3, negative feedback regulator), and ZDHHC9 (0.718, S-acyltransferase). High confidence links include GOLGA7B (0.656), SPTSSA (0.651, paralogous small subunit), SPTLC2 (0.584), SPTLC1 (0.580), ORMDL2 (0.562), ORMDL1 (0.558), and GOLGA7 (0.553). This profile confirms SPTSSB as an essential regulatory component of the SPT complex, working alongside SPTLC1/2/3 catalytic subunits and ORMDL feedback regulators. The ORMDL interactions are particularly significant as they mediate homeostatic control of sphingolipid levels.\n\nHPA expression reveals tissue-enhanced pattern with highest expression in stomach (67.0 nTPM), urinary bladder (62.6 nTPM), and skin (58.9 nTPM). These tissues have high epithelial turnover and require robust sphingolipid production for membrane integrity and barrier function. Cancer-enhanced specificity suggests SPTSSB upregulation in tumors, potentially supporting rapid membrane synthesis for proliferation. Immune cell enrichment indicates roles in immune cell membrane dynamics.\n\nClinVar documents 20 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, exclusively large copy number gains and losses affecting chromosome 3q (3q22.1-29 duplications, 3q25.31-26.1 deletions). The variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity. While no specific Mendelian disease is annotated, SPTSSB haploinsufficiency or overexpression likely causes sphingolipid metabolism disorders. 3q CNVs are associated with developmental delay and neurological phenotypes; SPTSSB disruption may impair ceramide production, affecting myelination and neuronal function.\n\nConservation is strong across vertebrates: human (Q8NFR3, 76 aa), mouse (Q925E8, 76 aa), indicating evolutionary pressure to maintain the small subunit structure and SPT regulatory function.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: SPTSSB functions as a regulatory subunit that modulates SPT catalytic activity and substrate specificity. It binds to the SPTLC1/SPTLC2 or SPTLC1/SPTLC3 heterodimer, stabilizing the complex and enhancing catalytic efficiency. SPTSSB may determine acyl-CoA preference, influencing whether C16-, C18-, or C20-sphingolipids are produced. ORMDL proteins provide negative feedback when sphingolipid levels are sufficient, and SPTSSB may mediate this regulation. Loss of SPTSSB reduces SPT activity, depleting ceramides and downstream sphingolipids (sphingomyelin, glycosphingolipids), impairing membrane integrity, signaling, and myelination. Overexpression may cause sphingolipid accumulation, contributing to metabolic disorders or cancer progression. SPTSSB represents a potential biomarker for 3q CNV syndromes and a target for modulating sphingolipid metabolism in neurodegenerative diseases.
