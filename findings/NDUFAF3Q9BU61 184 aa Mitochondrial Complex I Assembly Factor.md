Functional hypothesis: NDUFAF3 acts as assembly chaperone binding nascent Complex I subunits via Mth938 domain, stabilizing intermediate assembly states during Q/P module formation, with NDUFAF4 providing parallel/backup assembly pathway. The testis-enriched expression suggests additional role in spermatogenesis where mitochondrial function is critical. Disease mechanism: NDUFAF3 loss-of-function causes incomplete Complex I assembly, reduced NADH oxidation, impaired oxidative phosphorylation, and tissue-specific bioenergetic crisis manifesting as Leigh syndrome (basal ganglia necrosis), leukoencephalopathy (white matter degeneration), and lactic acidosis.
