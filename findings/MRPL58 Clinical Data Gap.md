## Description
MRPL58 (Mitochondrial Ribosomal Protein L58) represents a classic "Clinical Data Gap" candidate: a well-characterized, essential mitochondrial release factor with high-confidence structural integrity (AlphaFold pLDDT 84.4) and core ribosomal network interactions (STRING >0.9), yet harboring 13 pathogenic copy number variants in ClinVar with zero cataloged named diseases. The literature (5 papers) describes it as "Dark" and poorly characterized regarding specific phenotypes. The functional hypothesis posits that MRPL58 is critical for mitochondrial protein synthesis termination; its loss-of-function variants likely cause broad, pleiotropic metabolic and neurodegenerative defects due to systemic mitochondrial dysfunction. These phenotypes are not cataloged as a single syndrome because they are either too heterogeneous, masked by larger CNVs affecting the entire 17q24 cluster, or grouped under general mitochondrial disorders (e.g., Leigh syndrome, MERRF) where MRPL58 is just one of many contributors. The lack of tissue specificity (HPA: Low) further explains the absence of a distinct organ-specific disease, as defects manifest as global energy failure. This finding highlights the "Clinical Data Gap" profile: high variant burden (13) + zero diseases + essential function + dark gene status.
