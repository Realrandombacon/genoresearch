## Description
ABHD18 encodes a 414 amino acid alpha/beta hydrolase fold protein localized to the nuclear membrane and mitochondrion. The protein contains a conserved ABHD18-specific domain (IPR019149, PF09752, residues 16-414) with characteristic alpha/beta hydrolase catalytic fold (G3DSA:3.40.50.1820, IPR029058) spanning residues 92-409, suggesting lipase/esterase activity.

AlphaFold structure prediction yields high confidence (pLDDT 80.5), indicating a well-folded stable tertiary structure with defined catalytic pocket. The protein shows ubiquitous expression across all tissues with low tissue specificity, consistent with housekeeping metabolic function.

Protein interaction network (STRING) reveals 10 partners with enrichment for ABHD family members: ABHD4 (0.502), ABHD2 (0.477), ABHD8 (0.458), ABHD16B (0.458), ABHD11 (0.454), ABHD14A (0.403), suggesting functional redundancy or complex formation within the ABHD hydrolase superfamily. Additional partners include TEX43 (0.645), MFSD8 (0.524), and LARP1B (0.447), linking to RNA metabolism and lysosomal trafficking.

ClinVar documents 31 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number losses, gains, and splice site mutations (c.92+3A>T), indicating dosage sensitivity and clinical relevance. Chromosome 4q28.2 location places it in a region susceptible to large CNVs.

Evolutionary conservation: mouse ortholog (Q8C1A9, 464 aa) and rat (Q4V7A8, 464 aa) show extended C-termini but conserved catalytic core; Drosophila (Q8IQI5, 510 aa) retains the hydrolase domain.

Mechanistic hypothesis: ABHD18 functions as a mitochondrial-associated membrane (MAM) hydrolase processing specific lipid substrates (likely lysophospholipids or endocannabinoids) at ER-mitochondria contact sites. The nuclear membrane localization suggests additional roles in nuclear lipid signaling or chromatin-associated lipid metabolism. Disease variants disrupt catalytic triad geometry or membrane anchoring, causing metabolic dysfunction linked to mitochondrial disease phenotypes.
