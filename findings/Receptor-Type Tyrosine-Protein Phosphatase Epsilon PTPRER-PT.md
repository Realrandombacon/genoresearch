# Receptor-Type Tyrosine-Protein Phosphatase Epsilon (PTPRE/R-PTPε): Dual-Domain Membrane Phosphatase Regulating Src Family Kinase Signaling, Cell Adhesion, and Immune Cell Activation via Cytosolic Domain-Mediated Dephosphorylation

**Date:** 2026-06-17T11:02:32.558661

**Quality Score:** 9/10 (EXCELLENT)

**Confidence:** MEDIUM

## Description
PTPRE encodes a 700 aa type I receptor-type protein tyrosine phosphatase (UniProt P23469), also known as R-PTPε or HPTPE, that functions as a critical negative regulator of Src family kinase (SFK) signaling through dephosphorylation of the inhibitory tyrosine residue (Y527 in c-Src). The protein features a characteristic receptor PTP architecture: a short extracellular domain (~30 aa), a single transmembrane helix, and two tandem intracellular catalytic PTPase domains (IPR000242/IPR000387/IPR003595, residues 134-396 and 425-691) with conserved active site motifs (IPR016130, residues 333-343 and 628-638) belonging to the receptor tyrosine-protein phosphatase alpha/epsilon family (IPR016336). STRING interactions place PTPRE in signaling networks centered on adaptor proteins and related phosphatases: GRB2 (0.820), the universal SH2/SH3 adaptor linking receptors to RAS/MAPK signaling; PTPRA (0.787), its closest paralog with overlapping substrate specificity; WDR5 (0.702), a chromatin regulator; PTS (0.623), 6-pyruvoyltetrahydropterin synthase; PTPRJ (0.542), another receptor PTP; PTGDR (0.524) and CYSLTR1 (0.511), GPCRs for prostaglandin and cysteinyl leukotriene respectively; LRFN5 (0.502) and LRFN3 (0.426), synaptic adhesion molecules; and KCNB1 (0.483), a voltage-gated potassium channel. HPA confirms tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in bone marrow (44.3 nTPM), immune cell-enhanced expression, and subcellular localization to intermediate filaments—consistent with roles in hematopoietic signaling and cytoskeletal organization. ClinVar lists 85 pathogenic variants including extensive copy number losses/gains spanning chromosome 10q24.1-q26.3, deletions, and structural variants, demonstrating extreme dosage sensitivity and linking PTPRE disruption to developmental disorders and cancer susceptibility. AlphaFold structure prediction yields high confidence (global pLDDT 85.9), revealing a well-folded dual-domain phosphatase architecture with both catalytic domains properly positioned for substrate access. Mechanistically, PTPRE functions through multiple integrated modes: (1) As a Src regulator, PTPRE dephosphorylates the C-terminal inhibitory tyrosine of c-Src, paradoxically activating Src kinase activity—this distinguishes it from most PTPs that act as tumor suppressors; (2) Interaction with GRB2 suggests coupling to RAS/MAPK signaling cascades, potentially regulating cell proliferation and differentiation; (3) Bone marrow and immune cell enrichment indicates specialized roles in hematopoietic progenitor signaling, potentially regulating myeloid vs. lymphoid lineage commitment; (4) Intermediate filament localization suggests cytoskeletal functions, potentially regulating cell migration and adhesion during immune surveillance; (5) Recent literature (2024) reveals oncogenic roles in hepatocellular carcinoma, retinoblastoma, and thyroid carcinoma via AKT and ERK1/2 pathway activation, indicating context-dependent tumor promoter activity; (6) Paralog redundancy with PTPRA suggests tissue-specific expression patterns fine-tune SFK signaling thresholds. Disruption of PTPRE would cause dysregulated Src signaling, altered immune cell activation, defective cytoskeletal dynamics, and aberrant cell proliferation—phenotypes consistent with the 10q26 deletion syndromes and cancer susceptibility observed in patients.
