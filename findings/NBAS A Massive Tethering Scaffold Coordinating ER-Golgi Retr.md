# NBAS: A Massive Tethering Scaffold Coordinating ER-Golgi Retrograde Transport and Membrane Fusion via the NRZ Complex

**Quality Score:** 10/10 (EXCELLENT)

**Confidence:** HIGH

## Description
NBAS (Neuroblastoma Amplified Sequence, UniProt A2RRP1) is a colossal 2,371-amino acid intracellular protein that serves as the central scaffold of the NRZ tethering complex, essential for retrograde vesicle transport from the Golgi apparatus to the endoplasmic reticulum (ER). InterPro analysis reveals a modular architecture designed for multi-protein assembly: an N-terminal YVTN repeat-like domain (IPR011044, G3DSA:2.130.10.10, aa 87-444) likely involved in protein-protein interactions, a central Sec39 domain (IPR013244, PF08314, aa 1025-1365) critical for tethering activity, and a unique C-terminal domain (IPR054751, PF22913, aa 1996-2120) specific to the NRZ complex. The AlphaFold predicted structure shows confident folding (pLDDT 74.1) across its massive length, supporting its role as a rigid structural bridge. STRING interaction data places NBAS at the heart of the SNARE machinery, with near-perfect associations to the syntaxin STX18 (0.994), the SNAP receptor NAPG (0.921), and the v-SNARE SEC22B (0.894), forming the core fusion engine. It also links strongly to SCFD2 (0.858), another tethering factor, and STX6 (0.790), indicating coordination with endosomal trafficking. HPA expression profiles confirm ubiquitous presence ('Detected in all') with specific localization to the nuclear membrane, nucleoli, and Golgi apparatus, consistent with its housekeeping role in membrane homeostasis. ClinVar records are striking, documenting 392 pathogenic variants including frameshifts (e.g., p.Glu664fs, p.Leu250fs), nonsense mutations (p.Glu200Ter), and splice defects, which cause Infantile Liver Failure Syndrome 2 (ILFS2), short stature, optic nerve atrophy, and Pelger-Huet anomaly. We hypothesize that NBAS acts as a 'molecular ruler' that physically bridges incoming COPI vesicles to the ER membrane, stabilizing the transient SNARE complex (STX18-SNAP23-SEC22B) to ensure precise fusion. Loss of NBAS function disrupts this tethering, leading to Golgi fragmentation, accumulation of misfolded proteins in the ER, and catastrophic failure of high-demand tissues like the liver and developing skeleton. The sheer number of pathogenic variants underscores its non-redundant, dosage-sensitive role in cellular viability.
