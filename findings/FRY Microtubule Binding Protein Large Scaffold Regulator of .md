## Description
FRY encodes a massive 3013 aa microtubule-binding protein (UniProt Q5TBA9) that functions as a conserved scaffold regulator of cell morphogenesis and neuronal development. InterPro analysis reveals a modular domain architecture characteristic of the Furry/Tao3/Mor2 family (IPR039867, PTHR12295): N-terminal cell morphogenesis domain (IPR025614/PF14222, aa 165-697), three central morphogenesis regions (IPR029473/PF14228 at aa 1209-1369, 1637-1698, 1781-1898), and dual C-terminal domains (IPR025481/PF14225 at aa 2042-2294; IPR045842/PF19421 at aa 2319-3013). STRING interactions reveal functional associations with NDR kinase family members STK38 (0.687) and STK38L (0.697), consistent with the conserved FRY-NDR signaling module controlling cell polarity and morphogenesis across eukaryotes. Additional partners include FRYL (0.487, paralog), GLIPR1L2 (0.457), and RXFP2 (0.433). HPA data shows low tissue specificity with expression detected in many tissues and enrichment in blood cell groups, suggesting broad cellular functions. ClinVar reports 24 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly chromosome 13q copy number variations (deletions and duplications spanning 13q11-34), indicating haploinsufficiency sensitivity. The absence of dedicated molecular biology papers in literature searches (results dominated by unrelated 'fry' terms) suggests this gene remains under-characterized despite its size and clinical variants. Mechanistically, FRY likely serves as a microtubule-associated scaffold that recruits NDR kinases to sites of polarized growth, regulating centrosome positioning, spindle orientation, and neurite extension. The hematologic enrichment and CNV pathogenicity suggest roles in blood cell development and chromosome 13q deletion syndromes. AlphaFold structure unavailable due to protein size, but domain conservation implies conserved structural mechanisms.
