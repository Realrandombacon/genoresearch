# ZNF595: Placenta-Enriched KRAB-Zinc Finger Transcriptional Repressor with High CNV Burden

**Date:** 2026-03-20T14:23:13.707847

**Quality Score:** 2.1/10  (E=3.4, D=0.8) [WEAK]

## Description
ZNF595 (zinc finger protein 595, UniProt Q8IYB9) encodes a 648 amino acid C2H2-type zinc finger transcription factor featuring an N-terminal KRAB repressor domain (IPR001909/PF01352, positions 3-75) that recruits heterochromatin machinery via TRIM28/KAP1, followed by three tandem C2H2 zinc finger DNA-binding motifs (IPR013087/PF00096, positions 145-278) recognizing specific GC-rich promoter sequences. HPA RNA-seq reveals striking placenta-specific enrichment (56.9 nTPM) with low expression elsewhere, suggesting specialized roles in trophoblast differentiation, placental barrier formation, or imprinted gene regulation during pregnancy. STRING interaction network identifies 10 medium-confidence partners led by TRIM28 (0.683, universal KRAB corepressor that recruits SETDB1 histone methyltransferase and HP1 for H3K9me3 heterochromatin), ANKRD20A2 (0.600, KRAB-ZNF family member), FOXD4L5 (0.535) and FOXD4L3 (0.481, forkhead transcription factors), FAM199X (0.508), SPOP (0.479, cullin3 ubiquitin ligase substrate adaptor), DHX32 (0.434, DEAD-box RNA helicase), and GCNT2 (0.429, glycosyltransferase), indicating participation in transcriptional silencing complexes, ubiquitin-mediated turnover, and potential RNA processing. ClinVar contains 138 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly copy number losses and gains spanning 4p16.3 (Wolf-Hirschhorn syndrome critical region), though no monogenic disease is specifically attributed to ZNF595 alone. The KRAB-ZNF architecture positions ZNF595 as sequence-specific DNA-binding repressor that silences target genes through TRIM28-mediated recruitment of histone methyltransferases and chromatin remodelers. Placental enrichment parallels other pregnancy-specific KRAB-ZNFs (e.g., ZNF99, ZNF98) that regulate endogenous retroviral elements and imprinted loci. High CNV burden at 4p16.3 suggests dosage sensitivity where haploinsufficiency or triplication disrupts placental gene regulatory networks, potentially contributing to pregnancy complications or developmental phenotypes in Wolf-Hirschhorn syndrome deletions.
