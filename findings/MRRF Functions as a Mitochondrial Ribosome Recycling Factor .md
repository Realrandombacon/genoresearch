## Description
MRRF (Mitochondrial Ribosome Recycling Factor) is a 262 aa essential mitochondrial protein representing a partially characterized gene with approximately 50 literature references (many representing acronym collision with climate modeling and image processing), functioning as the critical factor for mitochondrial ribosome disassembly after translation termination. Multi-omics data reveals MRRF as a vertebrate-conserved translation factor with defined RRF domain architecture yet emerging disease characterization through chromosome 9 copy number variants. UniProt accession Q96E11 confirms the 262 aa length with homologs across eukaryotes, indicating strong evolutionary conservation of the mitochondrial translation machinery. InterPro analysis confirms membership in the Ribosome Recycling Factor family (IPR002661, aa 1-262; PF01765, aa 99-261; IPR023584, aa 99-261; IPR036191, aa 82-261) containing a canonical three-domain RRF structure essential for ribosome disassembly. The RRF superfamily (IPR036191) and cathgene3d homologous superfamilies (G3DSA:1.10.132.20, G3DSA:3.30.1360.40) indicate functional specialization in translation termination. HPA shows ubiquitous expression (detected in all tissues) with low tissue specificity, microtubule and cytokinetic bridge localization, and protein biosynthesis annotation—consistent with a housekeeping translation factor. The cytokinetic bridge localization is intriguing and may indicate non-canonical roles in cell division.
