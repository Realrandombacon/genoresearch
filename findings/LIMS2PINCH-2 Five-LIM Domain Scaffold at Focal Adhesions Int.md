# LIMS2/PINCH-2: Five-LIM Domain Scaffold at Focal Adhesions Integrating ILK-Parvin Signaling for Cytoskeletal Organization and Muscular Dystrophy Pathogenesis

**Date:** 2026-06-26T15:30:10.429239

**Quality Score:** 7/10 (GOOD)

**Confidence:** MEDIUM-HIGH

## Description
LIMS2 (341 aa, UniProt Q7Z4I7), also known as PINCH-2, is a critical focal adhesion scaffold protein that organizes the integrin-linked kinase (ILK) signaling complex to regulate cell-matrix adhesion, cytoskeletal dynamics, and muscle integrity. The protein architecture features five tandem LIM zinc-finger domains (IPR001781/PF00412, aa15-71, 76-130, 140-192, 196-249, 257-310) that create multiple protein-protein interaction surfaces, characteristic of the PINCH family (IPR017351). Unlike its paralog LIMS1/PINCH-1, LIMS2 shows tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in intestine (473.9 nTPM) and immune cells, suggesting specialized roles in epithelial barrier function and immune cell migration. Mechanistically, LIMS2 forms the core of the IPP complex by binding ILK (STRING 0.999) and parvin family members PARVA/alpha-parvin (0.974), PARVB/beta-parvin (0.864), and PARVG/gamma-parvin (0.783), creating a molecular bridge between integrin cytoplasmic tails and the actin cytoskeleton. Additional interactions with RSU1 (0.923), vinculin/VCL (0.551), and FERMT2/kindlin-2 (0.449) position LIMS2 as a central hub for focal adhesion maturation and turnover. AlphaFold prediction (pLDDT 86.8) reveals a highly confident extended structure with five compact LIM domains connected by flexible linkers, enabling simultaneous binding to multiple partners. Clinical significance is underscored by 33 pathogenic/likely pathogenic ClinVar variants including frameshift mutations (p.His144fs) and large copy number losses/gains at 2q12.2-q21.2, with HPA explicitly linking LIMS2 to limb-girdle muscular dystrophy type 2W (LGMD2W). Recent 2022-2026 literature establishes LIMS2 as a tumor suppressor downregulated in osteosarcoma, lung adenocarcinoma, and cervical cancer, where it inhibits cell growth and migration while modulating cancer-associated fibroblast activation. The functional hypothesis posits that LIMS2 acts as a 'mechanosensitive scaffold': mechanical tension at focal adhesions induces conformational changes in the LIM domain array, exposing cryptic binding sites that recruit signaling molecules (RSU1, TERF1) to coordinate adhesion strengthening versus disassembly; in muscle tissue, loss of LIMS2 destabilizes the costamere structure linking sarcomeres to the extracellular matrix, leading to progressive muscle weakness characteristic of LGMD2W.
