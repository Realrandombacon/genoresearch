## Description
SOX17 (UniProt Q9H6I2) encodes a 414-amino acid transcription factor essential for definitive endoderm specification and vascular development. InterPro analysis identifies a canonical N-terminal HMG-box DNA-binding domain (PF00505/IPR009071, aa 69-136) that bends DNA, a central SOX-specific domain (PF12067/IPR033392, aa 203-253) unique to the SOX F-group, and a C-terminal transactivation domain (IPR021934, aa 280-413). AlphaFold predicts a structure with medium confidence (pLDDT: 57.4), reflecting a structured HMG core flanked by intrinsically disordered regions typical of transcriptional regulators.\n\nSTRING interactions reveal SOX17 as a central node in the Wnt/beta-catenin pathway, showing high-confidence links to CTNNB1 (0.967), TCF7L1 (0.944), TCF7L2 (0.918), and LEF1, alongside endodermal partners FOXA2 (0.942) and GATA4/6. Mechanistically, SOX17 acts as a context-dependent Wnt antagonist by sequestering beta-catenin or forming non-productive complexes with TCFs, thereby blocking canonical signaling.\n\nHPA data shows tissue-enhanced expression (adipose: 46.8 nTPM) with nucleoplasmic localization and cancer-enhanced expression patterns. ClinVar lists 49 pathogenic variants, including missense mutations in the HMG box (e.g., p.Lys143Thr, p.Arg138Pro) and truncations, linked to developmental defects. Despite 1,660 papers, emerging insights highlight its 'dark' side in disease: SOX17 enables immune evasion in early colorectal adenomas (2024, 83 citations) by suppressing immune recognition, drives cisplatin resistance in germ cell tumors via yolk-sac lineage induction (2025), and its haploinsufficiency is a primary driver of Pulmonary Arterial Hypertension (PAH) through endothelial dysfunction. This positions SOX17 not just as a developmental factor, but as a critical checkpoint in tumor immunity and vascular homeostasis.
