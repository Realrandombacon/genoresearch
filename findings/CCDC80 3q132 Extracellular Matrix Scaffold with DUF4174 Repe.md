## Description
CCDC80 (Coiled-Coil Domain Containing 80, UniProt Q76M96, Gene ID: 151887) is a 950 amino acid extracellular matrix (ECM) protein encoded on chromosome 3q13.2 (GRCh38: 112596796-112641142). InterPro analysis reveals a unique architecture dominated by three tandem repeats of the Domain of Unknown Function 4174 (DUF4174, IPR025232, PF13778): residues 142-270, 616-748, and 771-902. These domains comprise nearly 40% of the protein length and define the PTHR46792 family. Despite the "coiled-coil" nomenclature, the primary functional signature is this triplicated DUF, suggesting a modular scaffold role. Gene Ontology annotations predict fibronectin binding, heparin binding, and involvement in ECM organization and cell-substrate adhesion.\n\nSTRING interaction network reveals 10 partners (scores 0.499-0.917), forming a highly coherent functional cluster centered on ECM structure and signaling: BRS3 (0.917, Bombesin Receptor Subtype 3, GPCR), FAU (0.806, Finkel-Biskis-Reilly Murine Sarcoma virus-associated ubiquitously expressed protein, ribosomal/ubiquitin fusion), FBN1 (0.596, Fibrillin-1, microfibril scaffold), RBMS3 (0.594, RNA Binding Motif Protein Single Stranded Interacting 3), COL3A1 (0.583, Collagen Type III Alpha 1 Chain), COL1A2 (0.539, Collagen Type I Alpha 2 Chain), COL1A1 (0.511, Collagen Type I Alpha 1 Chain), MFAP5 (0.510, Microfibrillar Associated Protein 5), CCL19 (0.509, Chemokine Ligand 19), and SRPX (0.499, Sushi Repeat Containing Protein X-linked). The exceptionally high confidence link to BRS3 (0.917) and strong connections to fibrillins (FBN1, MFAP5) and collagens (COL1A1, COL1A2, COL3A1) strongly implicate CCDC80 as a critical cross-linking component of the microfibrillar-collagen network, potentially acting as a bridge between elastic fibers and the basement membrane.\n\nHuman Protein Atlas classifies CCDC80 as having low tissue specificity, detected in all tissues, with low cancer specificity. It is predicted to be secreted and located in the extracellular region, interstitial matrix, and basement membrane. This ubiquitous expression pattern is consistent with a structural housekeeping role in maintaining tissue integrity across diverse organ systems.\n\nClinVar analysis reveals a high burden of 27 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, exclusively large-scale copy number losses (deletions) and gains (duplications) spanning the 3q13 region. Variants include massive deletions (3q13.2-13.31, ~3-5Mb; 3q13.12-13.31, ~8-9Mb) and duplications (3q13.2-13.31, ~3.4Mb). These CNVs are associated with 3q13 deletion syndrome, characterized by developmental delay, intellectual disability, growth retardation, and distinctive facial features. The absence of specific point mutations suggests that haploinsufficiency of CCDC80, likely in concert with neighboring genes, drives the connective tissue and neurodevelopmental phenotypes.\n\nAlphaFold structure prediction unavailable (500 server error).\n\nHYPOTHESIS: CCDC80 functions as a multifunctional extracellular scaffold protein that stabilizes the microfibrillar-collagen network via its triplicated DUF4174 domains. The strong interaction with FBN1 and collagens suggests CCDC80 acts as a "molecular glue" or cross-linker, reinforcing the mechanical integrity of the ECM. The ultra-high confidence link to BRS3 (0.917) implies a potential regulatory role where CCDC80 modulates GPCR signaling by sequestering ligands or presenting them to the receptor, possibly linking mechanical stress to cellular responses. The ubiquitous expression supports a fundamental role in tissue architecture; loss of CCDC80 (via 3q13 deletions) likely weakens ECM cohesion, contributing to the hypotonia, growth failure, and craniofacial dysmorphism seen in 3q13 deletion syndrome. The DUF4174 repeats may specifically mediate fibronectin/heparin binding, anchoring the protein to the basement membrane and facilitating cell-matrix adhesion.
