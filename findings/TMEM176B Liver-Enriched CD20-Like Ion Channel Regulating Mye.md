## Description
TMEM176B (Gene ID: 51339, UniProt Q3YBM2) is a 270 amino acid transmembrane protein located on chromosome 7q34 (NC_000007.14). InterPro domain analysis identifies TMEM176B as a member of the CD20-like transmembrane domain family (IPR007237/PF04103, positions 66-228), characterized by four transmembrane helices forming a potential ion channel pore, and the specific Transmembrane protein 176A/B family (IPR009281/PTHR15756, positions 1-270). This structural classification places it within the LR8/HCA112 superfamily, suggesting evolutionary conservation as a membrane transporter or channel. AlphaFold structure prediction (AF-Q3YBM2-F1) yields a high-confidence model (global pLDDT 70.3) consistent with a compact, well-folded four-helix bundle topology typical of small membrane channels. Human Protein Atlas classifies TMEM176B as a predicted membrane protein and transporter involved in differentiation processes. Subcellular localization includes the nucleoplasm (potentially indicating nuclear signaling roles or trafficking intermediates), Golgi apparatus (site of processing), and plasma membrane (functional site). Expression is highly tissue-enriched in the liver (1555.3 nTPM), with lower but detectable levels in other tissues; it is cancer-enhanced and immune cell-enhanced, particularly in myeloid lineages. STRING database reveals a tight interaction network centered on its paralog TMEM176A (score 0.999, nearly identical sequence and function), alongside immune-specific partners: GIMAP8 (0.511), GIMAP6 (0.509), GIMAP5 (0.483), and GIMAP4 (0.474) — GTPases of immunity-associated proteins critical for T-cell survival and lymphocyte homeostasis. Additional partners include SP9 (0.552, transcription factor), APOE (0.522, lipid metabolism/Alzheimer's), MTREX (0.486, RNA exosome), ACRV1 (0.479, sperm acrosome), and ZNF775 (0.412, zinc finger). The strong GIMAP cluster suggests TMEM176B operates within an immune regulatory module, potentially modulating cation fluxes (Ca2+/K+) to control myeloid cell maturation and activation. ClinVar lists 73 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number losses and gains spanning 7q33-q36.3 (multi-megabase regions affecting multiple genes), with no single-nucleotide variants specifically attributed to TMEM176B alone yet; however, the dosage sensitivity of this region implies TMEM176B contribution to phenotypes when haploinsufficient or overexpressed. Conservation is evident across mammals (mouse Q9R1Q6, rat Q925D4). Functional hypothesis: TMEM176B functions as a non-selective cation channel or transporter regulating intracellular pH and Ca2+ concentrations in myeloid progenitors and hepatocytes. By modulating ion gradients, it controls downstream signaling cascades (e.g., calcineurin-NFAT, NF-kB) driving differentiation into mature macrophages/dendritic cells and maintaining liver metabolic homeostasis. The physical interaction with TMEM176A suggests heteromeric complex formation, possibly altering channel gating or selectivity. Disease mechanism: Dysregulation of TMEM176B expression (via CNVs or transcriptional misregulation) disrupts ion homeostasis, leading to defective myeloid differentiation (immunodeficiency or autoimmunity) and potentially contributing to liver pathologies (steatosis, fibrosis). Overexpression in cancers may promote tumor growth by inhibiting apoptosis via Ca2+-dependent survival pathways. Priority experiments include patch-clamp electrophysiology to define ion selectivity and conductance, CRISPR knockout in myeloid cell lines to assess differentiation blocks, co-IP validation of TMEM176A/GIMAP interactions, and investigation of TMEM176B's role in inflammasome activation.
