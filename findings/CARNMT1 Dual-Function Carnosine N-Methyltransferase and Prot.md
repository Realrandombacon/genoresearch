## Description
CARNMT1 (UniProt Q8N4J0), also known as CARMT1 or C9orf41, encodes a 409-amino acid S-adenosyl-L-methionine (SAM)-dependent methyltransferase belonging to the carnosine N-methyltransferase family (IPR012901/PF07942, aa 146-408) within the broader SAM-dependent methyltransferase superfamily (IPR029063/SSF53335, aa 148-408). The protein contains the Vaccinia Virus VP39-like fold (G3DSA:3.40.50.150, aa 151-399) characteristic of class I methyltransferases, with conserved SAM-binding motifs essential for methyl group transfer. AlphaFold predicts a well-folded catalytic domain (pLDDT: 89.4), among the highest confidence predictions indicating stable enzymatic architecture.\n\nSTRING interactions reveal dual metabolic roles: strongest links to carnosine synthase CARNS1 (0.976) and dipeptidases CNDP1 (0.941)/CNDP2 (0.935), establishing CARNMT1 within the carnosine/anserine metabolic pathway converting carnosine (β-alanyl-L-histidine) to anserine (β-alanyl-L-Nπ-methylhistidine) abundant in skeletal muscle. Additional connections include histidine methyltransferase SETD3 (0.605), tripeptidyl-peptidase TPP2 (0.598), zinc finger transcription factor ZBTB25 (0.560), nicotinamide riboside kinase NMRK1 (0.559), GARP repeat protein GARNL3 (0.537), proteasome subunit PSMD14 (0.533), and acetyltransferase NAT16 (0.418). HPA confirms ubiquitous expression across tissues with cell type-enhanced specificity.\n\nWith only 19 papers, CARNMT1 represents a genuinely emerging dark gene with transformative 2023-2025 discoveries: (1) identified as the major His N1-position-specific protein methyltransferase targeting C3H zinc finger proteins; (2) modulates RNA metabolism through zinc finger modification; (3) recent structural studies reveal substrate requirements for C3H zinc finger methylation. ClinVar contains 41 pathogenic variants (9q21.13 CNVs and SNVs including p.Gly77Asp). This positions CARNMT1 as a bifunctional enzyme bridging skeletal muscle dipeptide metabolism to epitranscriptomic regulation via protein histidine methylation.
