## Description
LRRN1 (Leucine-Rich Repeat Neuronal Protein 1) is a 716 aa type I transmembrane protein functioning as a cell adhesion molecule critical for neuronal development, sensory epithelial boundary formation, and cancer progression. With 55 literature references, LRRN1 represents a 'partially characterized' dark gene where its roles in cochlear development and tumor biology are emerging but mechanistic details remain incomplete. InterPro analysis reveals a classic cell adhesion molecule architecture: multiple leucine-rich repeats (LRRs; IPR001611/IPR003591, aa 94-373) forming a curved solenoid structure for protein-protein interactions, flanked by cysteine-rich regions (IPR000483, aa 371-423), followed by immunoglobulin-like domains (IPR003598/IPR013098, aa 424-517) and a fibronectin type III domain (IPR003961/cd00063, aa 525-617) typical of adhesion receptors. AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (pLDDT 81.9), indicating a well-folded extracellular module optimized for ligand binding. HPA shows brain-enriched expression (15.7 nTPM) with tissue-enhanced specificity, localizing to ER, Golgi, vesicles, and centriolar satellites—consistent with a secretory pathway protein undergoing trafficking to the cell surface. STRING interactions reveal connections to adhesion GPCRs (ADGRL1/2/3 latrophilins: 0.578/0.534/0.528), receptor tyrosine phosphatase PTPRD (0.485), and LRRC family members (LRRC4/1/4C: 0.476/0.473/0.471), placing LRRN1 in neuronal synapse organization and cell recognition pathways. ClinVar lists 84 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number gains/losses on chromosome 3p, deletions, and duplications, implicating LRRN1 dosage sensitivity in neurodevelopmental disorders, though specific monogenic diseases are not yet defined. Recent literature (2021-2024) establishes LRRN1 as: (1) Essential regulator of medial boundary formation in the developing mouse organ of Corti, separating sensory hair cells from nonsensory cells; (2) Oncogene in pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma where knockdown inhibits malignant phenotypes via HIF-1α/Notch pathway suppression; (3) Stroke risk gene in type 1 diabetes identified through whole-genome sequencing; (4) Cancer-enhanced expression across multiple tumor types.
