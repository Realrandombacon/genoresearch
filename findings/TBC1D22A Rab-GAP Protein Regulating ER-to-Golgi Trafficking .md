## Description
TBC1D22A (TBC1 Domain Family Member 22A, UniProt Q8WUA7) is a 517 aa intracellular Rab GTPase-activating protein (Rab-GAP) belonging to the TBC domain superfamily that regulates vesicular trafficking through RAB GTPase inactivation. InterPro analysis reveals a canonical Rab-GAP-TBC domain architecture (IPR000195, PF00566, positions 227-469; SM00164, positions 219-472) within the Rab-GAP-TBC domain superfamily (IPR035969, positions 208-506; G3DSA:1.10.472.80, positions 381-517; G3DSA:1.10.8.270, positions 251-380; SSF47923, positions 208-406/382-506) with TBC/rab GAP domain profile (PS50086, positions 222-446). The protein belongs to the Rab GTPase-activating protein family (PTHR22957, positions 191-487). AlphaFold structure shows high confidence (pLDDT 76.1), indicating a well-folded protein with stable TBC domain geometry for RAB binding and catalysis. HPA data confirms ubiquitous expression (detected in all tissues, low tissue specificity), nucleoplasm/Golgi apparatus/vesicles localization, classified as predicted intracellular protein with GTPase activation molecular function. STRING interactions reveal functional clustering with Golgi trafficking and mitochondrial calcium regulation: ACBD3 (0.867, Golgi resident protein GCP60), TBC1D20 (0.673, Rab-GAP family member), MICU1 (0.649, mitochondrial calcium uptake protein), GRTP1 (0.588), MRPS6 (0.583, mitochondrial ribosomal protein), TRABD (0.564), PPP6R2 (0.544, protein phosphatase 6 regulator), TBC1D21 (0.542, Rab-GAP family member), MICU2 (0.541, mitochondrial calcium uptake regulator), RAB43 (0.401, direct RAB substrate candidate). ClinVar contains 95 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly copy number losses at 22q13.31-13.33 affecting the TBC1D22A locus within the Phelan-McDermid syndrome critical region. Literature (28 papers) establishes TBC1D22A roles in: (1) ovarian serous cystadenocarcinoma poor prognosis biomarker (2024, 1 citation), (2) Phelan-McDermid syndrome novel gene beyond SHANK3 (2025, 3 citations), (3) chronic venous disease miRNA biomarker network (2024, 1 citation), and (4) related paralog TBC1D22B regulates ER-to-Golgi trafficking via RAB1B inactivation in breast cancer (2025, 1 citation). FUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: TBC1D22A serves as a Rab-GAP regulating ER-to-Golgi vesicular trafficking and mitochondrial calcium homeostasis through specific RAB GTPase substrate inactivation (likely RAB43 and related Golgi-associated RABs). MECHANISTIC PROPOSAL: The TBC domain catalyzes GTP hydrolysis on RAB GTPases, converting them from active GTP-bound to inactive GDP-bound states, thereby controlling vesicle budding, transport, and fusion. ACBD3 interaction suggests Golgi apparatus localization and function in secretory pathway trafficking. MICU1/MICU2 interactions indicate potential mitochondrial calcium regulation role, possibly coordinating ER-mitochondria contact sites. In ovarian cancer, TBC1D22A overexpression correlates with poor prognosis, suggesting oncogenic trafficking programs. In Phelan-McDermid syndrome, 22q13.31-13.33 deletions cause TBC1D22A haploinsufficiency contributing to neurodevelopmental phenotypes beyond SHANK3 loss. The 95 ClinVar CNVs indicate TBC1D22A dosage sensitivity in neurodevelopmental disorders. Paralog TBC1D22B's established role in RAB1B-mediated ER-to-Golgi trafficking suggests TBC1D22A performs analogous functions with distinct RAB substrate specificity.
