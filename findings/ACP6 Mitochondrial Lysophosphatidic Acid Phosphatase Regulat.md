## Description
ACP6/Q9NPH0 (428 aa) is a very well-folded (AlphaFold pLDDT: 90.9) mitochondrial histidine acid phosphatase functioning as a lysophosphatidic acid phosphatase that catalyzes dephosphorylation of lysophosphatidic acid (LPA) and related lipid phosphates, regulating cellular lipid metabolism and energy homeostasis. InterPro analysis reveals canonical histidine phosphatase architecture with N-terminal catalytic domain (IPR000560/IPR029033/IPR050645/PF00328/PTHR11567, residues 49-379/33-428/38-420/49-379/38-420) containing the conserved phosphohistidine active site signature (IPR033379/PS00616, residues 50-64) characteristic of branch-2 histidine acid phosphatases that form phosphorylated enzyme intermediates during catalysis, classifying it within the phosphoglycerate mutase-like superfamily (G3DSA:3.40.50.1240/SSF53254, residues 33-428/49-67/116-421). HPA data confirms ubiquitous expression across all tissues with low tissue specificity, intracellular localization consistent with mitochondrial metabolic functions, and involvement in lipid and phospholipid metabolism pathways. The convergence of conserved histidine phosphatase catalytic machinery, mitochondrial lipid phosphate hydrolysis function, AMPK/PRKAB2 metabolic partnerships, esophageal cancer prognostic value, hepatocellular carcinoma nitidine chloride targeting, and Parkinson's disease motor progression association suggests ACP6 functions as a metabolic checkpoint coordinating LPA signaling with cellular energy status and tumor progression, with therapeutic potential in esophageal cancer stratification, hepatocellular carcinoma pharmacological targeting, and Parkinson's disease motor symptom modulation.
