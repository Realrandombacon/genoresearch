## Description
GSTM4 encodes glutathione S-transferase mu 4 (GST Mu 4/GSTM4-4), a 218 aa cytosolic enzyme (UniProt Q03013) belonging to the mu class of soluble glutathione S-transferases that catalyzes conjugation of reduced glutathione (GSH) to electrophilic substrates including carcinogens, therapeutic drugs, and oxidative stress products. InterPro reveals canonical GST architecture: N-terminal thioredoxin-like domain (IPR004045/PF02797/cd03075/G3DSA:3.40.30.10, residues 1-88) containing the GSH-binding site (G-site) with conserved catalytic residues, C-terminal alpha-helical domain (IPR004046/cd03209/G3DSA:1.20.1050.10, residues 90-211) forming the hydrophobic substrate-binding pocket (H-site) determining substrate specificity for mu class enzymes, and full-length Mu class GST family signature (IPR003081/IPR040079/IPR050213, residues 2-201) defining enzymatic classification. AlphaFold predicts exceptional confidence structure (pLDDT 98.2) indicating a highly stable, well-folded dimeric enzyme typical of cytosolic GSTs requiring precise active site geometry for catalysis. STRING reveals functional clustering with phase II detoxification and antioxidant defense networks: EPHX1 (0.940, epoxide hydrolase 1 cooperating in xenobiotic metabolism), GSTM3 (0.927)/GSTM2 (0.920)/GSTM5 (0.917) (mu class paralogs with overlapping substrate specificity), GPX4 (0.925, glutathione peroxidase 4 reducing lipid hydroperoxides), CYP2E1 (0.922)/CYP1A2 (0.921) (cytochrome P450s generating reactive metabolites requiring GST conjugation), GSTZ1 (0.921, zeta class GST), GSR (0.450, glutathione disulfide reductase maintaining GSH pool), PRDX6 (0.405, peroxiredoxin-6 antioxidant enzyme). HPA shows tissue-enhanced expression with intestinal predominance (128.0 nTPM highest in dataset), detected in all tissues, immune cell-enriched pattern in blood cells, multiple subcellular localizations including cytosol (canonical), microtubules, cytokinetic bridge, and primary cilium suggesting roles beyond detoxification potentially in cell division and ciliary signaling, classified as enzyme/metabolic protein with lyase/transferase molecular functions involved in lipid metabolism. ClinVar lists 18 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including splice site mutation disrupting catalytic domain (c.259+1G>A), intronic variant (c.37-49A>G), and extensive copy number losses spanning 1p13.3-13.1/1p21.3-13.2/1p21.2-12 affecting gene dosage though no specific monogenic disease phenotype is currently definitively assigned suggesting contribution to complex disease susceptibility rather than Mendelian disorders. Functional mechanism: GSTM4 operates through induced-fit catalytic cycle: (1) GSH binding - N-terminal domain anchors GSH via hydrogen bonding network lowering pKa of GSH thiol to generate reactive thiolate anion at physiological pH; (2) Substrate recruitment - C-terminal helical domain creates hydrophobic pocket accommodating diverse electrophiles including polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbon epoxides, α,β-unsaturated carbonyls from lipid peroxidation (4-hydroxynonenal, acrolein), and chemotherapeutic alkylating agents; (3) Conjugation - nucleophilic attack of glutathionyl thiolate on electrophilic carbon forms thioether bond rendering substrate more water-soluble and less reactive; (4) Product release - conformational changes facilitate dissociation of GS-conjugate for export via MRP transporters. Recent 2025 literature reveals expanded disease relevance: migraine susceptibility through multi-omics Mendelian randomization identifying GSTM4 as potential drug target suggesting oxidative stress involvement in pathogenesis, breast cancer tumor suppression via regulation of redox homeostasis and apoptosis pathways with prognostic biomarker potential, oral cancer therapy target through genome-wide Mendelian randomization and network pharmacology revealing mechanistic insights, hepatic role in diet-induced obesity and dyslipidemia linking detoxification capacity to metabolic syndrome. Despite mechanistic understanding of catalytic activity and substrate range, complete substrate repertoire especially endogenous signaling molecules beyond xenobiotics, regulatory post-translational modifications controlling activity versus stability, tissue-specific partner proteins explaining primary cilium/microtubule localization, contribution of 1p CNVs to disease phenotypes beyond general genomic instability, polymorphic variation affecting catalytic efficiency across populations, isoform-specific functions from alternative splicing, therapeutic potential for GSTM4 activators enhancing detoxification versus inhibitors sensitizing tumors to chemotherapy remain incompletely characterized - representing genuine dark aspects of this critical detoxification enzyme bridging environmental exposure with disease susceptibility.
