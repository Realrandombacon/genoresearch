## Description
HMSD (Histocompatibility minor serpin domain containing, also known as ACC-6, ACC6, C18orf53, HSMD-v) is a 139 amino acid small secreted/intracellular protein (UniProt A8MTL9) with high-confidence well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 80.1), characteristic of the serpin (serine protease inhibitor) superfamily. The protein defines the Serpin family (IPR000215/PTHR11461, PF00079/SSF56574, residues 1-115) and contains an Antithrombin-like domain 2 (IPR042178/G3DSA:3.30.497.10, residues 1-134), indicating structural homology to antithrombin and other coagulation regulators. A splice variant (P0C7T4, 53 aa) encodes a minor histocompatibility antigen, suggesting immunogenic properties. Molecular functions include serine-type endopeptidase inhibitor activity and extracellular space localization, placing HMSD within the immune regulation and protease homeostasis pathway.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals a network of immune and serpin proteins: strongest interactions with ZNF419 (0.572, Zinc finger protein 419), IL2RG (0.565, Interleukin-2 receptor subunit gamma, critical for cytokine signaling), CTSH (0.564, Cathepsin H, lysosomal protease), and HLA-B (0.561, MHC class I antigen). Multiple serpin family members interact including SERPINA12 (0.501), SERPINH1 (0.495, HSP47 collagen chaperone), SERPINI2 (0.492), SERPINE1 (0.491, PAI-1), SERPINE2 (0.491), and SERPING1 (0.485, C1 inhibitor). This profile confirms HMSD as a functional serpin that likely regulates protease activity in immune cells, potentially modulating cytokine signaling (via IL2RG) or antigen presentation (via HLA-B).\n\nHPA expression reveals tissue-enriched pattern with brain expression (30.9 nTPM) and immune cell enrichment. Cancer-enhanced specificity suggests upregulation in tumors, possibly as an immune evasion mechanism. The myeloid lineage expression (per RefSeq) indicates HMSD functions in granulocytes, monocytes, or dendritic cells. Dual intracellular/secreted localization suggests it may act both intracellularly to regulate protease activity and extracellularly to modulate immune responses.\n\nClinVar documents 97 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, exclusively large copy number gains and losses affecting chromosome 18q (18q11.1-23 duplications, 18q21.2-23 deletions). The variant burden indicates extreme dosage sensitivity. While no specific Mendelian disease is annotated, HMSD haploinsufficiency or overexpression likely causes immunodeficiency or autoimmunity. 18q CNVs are associated with 18q deletion syndrome (de Grouchy syndrome), featuring developmental delay, immune dysfunction, and increased infection susceptibility; HMSD disruption may contribute to the immune phenotypes.\n\nConservation appears limited: human (A8MTL9, 139 aa), macaque (H9EY34, 100 aa), with BLAST showing no significant similarity to distant species, suggesting HMSD is a primate-specific serpin that evolved rapidly. This rapid evolution is typical of immune-related genes under positive selection.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: HMSD functions as a regulatory serpin that controls protease activity in myeloid cells, potentially inhibiting cathepsins (CTSH) or coagulation factors to prevent excessive inflammation. The interaction with IL2RG suggests HMSD may modulate cytokine receptor signaling by cleaving or protecting cytokines. HLA-B interaction indicates potential roles in antigen processing or MHC class I presentation. As a minor histocompatibility antigen, HMSD polymorphisms may trigger graft-versus-host disease in transplant recipients. The cancer-enhanced expression suggests tumors may exploit HMSD to suppress immune surveillance by inhibiting cytotoxic proteases. Loss of HMSD impairs protease homeostasis, leading to uncontrolled inflammation or autoimmunity. HMSD represents a potential biomarker for 18q deletion syndrome immune dysfunction and a target for modulating immune responses in cancer or transplantation.
