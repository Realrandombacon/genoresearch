## Description
ZNF502 (Gene ID: 91392, UniProt Q8TBZ5) is a 544 amino acid zinc finger protein located on chromosome 3p21.31 (NC_000003.12, positions 44712659-44723830, ~11.2kb region). InterPro domain analysis reveals a canonical C2H2-type zinc finger architecture with three tandem C2H2 zinc finger domains (IPR013087/PF00096/PS00028/PS50157 at positions 155-182, 183-210, 211-238) characteristic of sequence-specific DNA-binding transcription factors, membership in the Krüppel C2H2-type zinc-finger family (PTHR24394, positions 285-419), a zinc-finger double domain (PF13465, positions 450-474), and classification within the classic zinc finger superfamily (G3DSA:3.30.160.60/SSF57667, positions 149-179/180-208/209-235). AlphaFold structure prediction (AF-Q8TBZ5-F1) shows high confidence (global pLDDT 74.1), indicating a well-folded stable protein with defined secondary and tertiary structure typical of DNA-binding transcription factors. Human Protein Atlas classifies ZNF502 as a transcription factor localized to the nucleus, functioning in transcription regulation through DNA-binding with roles in host-virus interaction and viral release from host cells. Expression shows low tissue specificity with detection in many tissues and cell type-enhanced patterns, suggesting ubiquitous but regulated expression. STRING database identifies 2 medium-confidence protein-protein interactions: KAT8 (score 0.442) and NKAPL (0.402). KAT8 (lysine acetyltransferase 8) is a catalytic subunit of the MOZ/MORF histone acetyltransferase complexes involved in chromatin modification and transcriptional activation, while NKAPL (NK2-associated protein-like) is implicated in transcriptional regulation. ClinVar contains 5 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all large copy number gains affecting chromosome 3p/q regions including duplications (GRCh37/hg19 3p26.3-14.3 chr3:2263690-55016039x3, 3p24.3-21.31 chr3:16923595-45249923x2,3, 3p26.3-q29 chr3:61892-197851986x3, 3p22.2-21.31 chr3:37028313-49929220x3). Conservation across primates is evidenced by orthologs in chimpanzee (A0A6D2Y194, 544 aa) and Sumatran orangutan (A0A2J8WXG2, 544 aa). Functional hypothesis: ZNF502 functions as a sequence-specific DNA-binding transcriptional regulator that recognizes specific promoter/enhancer sequences through its C2H2 zinc finger array. The Krüppel-type zinc fingers mediate DNA recognition at target gene regulatory elements, while potential interactions with KAT8 suggest ZNF502 may recruit or cooperate with histone acetyltransferase complexes to modulate chromatin accessibility and transcriptional output. The documented role in host-virus interaction and viral release suggests ZNF502 may regulate expression of antiviral defense genes or cellular factors exploited by viruses during infection. Disease mechanism: Dosage sensitivity from chromosome 3p copy number gains causes ZNF502 overexpression, potentially leading to dysregulated transcriptional programs. The 3p21.31 region is frequently deleted in various cancers and haploinsufficiency of this region causes 3p deletion syndrome with developmental delay, intellectual disability, and congenital anomalies. ZNF502 dysregulation may contribute to these phenotypes through altered expression of target genes involved in development, cell cycle control, or immune response. Priority experiments include identification of DNA binding targets via ChIP-seq, characterization of KAT8-dependent chromatin modification mechanisms, assessment of target gene expression changes upon ZNF502 knockdown/overexpression, investigation of ZNF502's role in antiviral responses, and determination of crystal structure with bound DNA sequences.
