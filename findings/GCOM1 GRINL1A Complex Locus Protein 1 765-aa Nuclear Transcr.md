## Description
GCOM1 (GRINL1A Complex Locus Protein 1), also known as the GRINL1A complex locus protein, is a partially characterized gene with only 14 publications in Semantic Scholar, representing a genuine dark gene candidate with emerging clinical significance. UniProt accession H8Y6P7 identifies it as a 765-amino acid nuclear protein. InterPro scan reveals multiple conserved domains: IPR026213 (DNA-directed RNA polymerase II subunit GRINL1, residues 435-638), IPR051375 (Tuftelin/GRINL1A/MYZAP/CCD68 family, residues 25-763), PF15328 (Putative GRINL1B complex locus protein 2, residues 435-638), and PTHR23171 (Tuftelin/GRINL1A/MYZAP/CCD68 family). The troponin interactions (TNNT1/2/3) are particularly notable given GCOM1's disease associations with cardiomyopathy.\n\nHuman Protein Atlas localizes GCOM1 to the nucleoplasm with tissue-enhanced expression, detected in many tissues but highest in heart muscle (18.8 nTPM). It shows cancer-enriched and immune cell-enhanced expression patterns, suggesting potential roles in proliferating cells and immune regulation.\n\nClinVar reports 10 pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants: 4 nonsense mutations (p.Arg117Ter, p.Lys384Ter, p.Arg130Ter, p.Ser79Ter), 2 frameshift deletions/duplications (p.Leu377fs, p.His260fs), 1 splice site variant (c.933+1G>A), and 3 large copy number losses spanning 15q21.2-22.2. While no specific disease is named in ClinVar, the literature definitively links GCOM1 to fully penetrant recessive dilated cardiomyopathy (2021, 10 citations).\n\nLiterature reveals three key functional contexts: (1) Cardiomyopathy—GRINL1A complex transcription unit containing GCOM1, MYZAP, and POLR2M associates with fully penetrant recessive dilated cardiomyopathy in Finnish families (2021); (2) Neuronal interaction—GCOM1 complex interacts with NMDA receptor and internexin-alpha, suggesting neuronal roles (2018, 9 citations); (3) Neuroprotection—PSD expression and reduction of NMDA-induced toxicity by anti-GRINL1A Gcom1 antibodies suggest neuroprotective function (2004/2008, 10 citations).\n\nWe hypothesize that GCOM1 functions as a multifunctional transcription elongation regulator with tissue-specific roles: (1) Cardiac transcription hub—high heart expression (18.8 nTPM) and troponin interactions suggest GCOM1 regulates cardiac-specific gene expression programs essential for cardiomyocyte function; (2) Dilated cardiomyopathy driver—biallelic loss-of-function variants cause recessive DCM through disrupted transcription elongation of cardiac genes; (3) RNA polymerase II cofactor—interactions with POLR2C, POLR2J, and RPAP2 position GCOM1 within the transcription elongation complex; (4) Neuronal NMDA receptor modulator—interaction with NMDA receptor and internexin-alpha suggests dual neuronal function in synaptic plasticity and neuroprotection; (5) Troponin complex linkage—unexpected STRING interactions with TNNT1/2/3 suggest physical or functional coupling between transcription machinery and sarcomeric proteins; (6) Cancer/immune modifier—cancer-enriched and immune cell-enhanced expression implies roles in cell proliferation and immune regulation; (7) Dosage sensitivity—large 15q CNVs and truncating mutations indicate haploinsufficiency or complete loss causes disease; (8) Structural flexibility—medium-confidence AlphaFold structure enables dynamic interactions with transcription complexes and neuronal proteins. The combination of cardiac-specific expression, troponin interactions, recessive cardiomyopathy associations, neuronal NMDA receptor binding, and transcription elongation function positions GCOM1 as a high-priority candidate for functional characterization in cardiac genetics and neurobiology.
