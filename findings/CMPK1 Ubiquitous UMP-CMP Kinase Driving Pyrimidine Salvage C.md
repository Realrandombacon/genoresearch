## Description
CMPK1/P30085 (196 aa) is an exceptionally well-folded (AlphaFold pLDDT: 96.9) cytosolic/nucleoplasmic kinase functioning as a critical rate-limiting enzyme in pyrimidine nucleotide salvage pathways by phosphorylating UMP and CMP to their diphosphate forms for DNA/RNA synthesis. InterPro analysis reveals a canonical adenylate kinase fold (IPR000850/PF00406/MF_00235, residues 4-193/8-170/4-193) containing the signature P-loop NTP hydrolase motif (IPR027417/G3DSA:3.40.50.300, residues 1-196) and UMP-CMP kinase-specific catalytic site (IPR006266/MF_03172, residues 3-195/3-195) with conserved adenylate kinase signature (PS00113, residues 89-100) essential for nucleotide binding and phosphotransfer activity, classifying it within the nucleoside monophosphate kinase family. HPA data confirms ubiquitous expression across all tissues with low tissue specificity, exclusive nucleoplasmic localization consistent with its role in nuclear pyrimidine metabolism, and classification as an essential metabolic protein. The convergence of ultra-high confidence P-loop kinase structure, pyrimidine salvage enzyme partnerships, gemcitabine/5-FU chemotherapy response modulation, monocyte-mediated renal injury mechanisms, and T2DM biomarker potential suggests CMPK1 functions as a nucleotide homeostatic checkpoint coordinating DNA/RNA synthesis with chemotherapeutic efficacy, immune-metabolic inflammation, and glucose metabolism dysregulation, with therapeutic potential in overcoming nucleoside analog resistance, targeting renal ischemia-reperfusion injury, and predicting chemotherapy response in NSCLC and gastric cancer.
