## Description
SARS1 (UniProt P49591), also known as SERS or SERRS, encodes a 514 aa class II aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase functioning as an essential enzyme that catalyzes the ATP-dependent ligation of L-serine to its cognate tRNA(Ser) isoacceptors, ensuring translational fidelity for serine codons during protein synthesis. InterPro analysis reveals conserved class II aaRS architecture: SerRS catalytic core domain (cd00770/IPR033729/G3DSA:3.30.930.10, residues 152-460/137-476) forming the active site for serine activation and tRNA aminoacylation; N-terminal tRNA-binding domain (G3DSA:1.10.287.40/IPR010978/IPR015866/IPR042103, residues 1-136/3-144/2-76) recognizing tRNA(Ser) anticodon and acceptor stem; Class II aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase domain (IPR002314/IPR006195, residues 259-444/203-453) conferring ATP binding (GO:0005524), nucleotide binding (GO:0000166), and serine-tRNA ligase activity (GO:0004828); and Serine-tRNA ligase type 1 family signatures (IPR002317, residues 3-475) defining substrate specificity. AlphaFold predicts very high confidence structure (pLDDT 93.4) indicating an exceptionally well-folded enzyme with stable catalytic and tRNA-binding domains suitable for precise substrate recognition. The perfect PSTK score reveals SARS1 critical role in selenocysteine biosynthesis pathway beyond canonical translation. HPA classifies SARS1 as disease-related/enzymes/essential proteins/metabolic proteins with ubiquitous expression (detected in all tissues, low tissue specificity), localized to cytosol consistent with cytoplasmic translation machinery, molecular functions include aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase/DNA-binding/ligase activity, biological processes include protein biosynthesis/transcription/transcription regulation, with documented involvement in epilepsy and intellectual disability. ClinVar lists 27 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including missense mutations affecting catalytic residues (c.1231G>A p.Gly411Arg in catalytic core, c.1196C>T p.Thr399Met, c.1168C>T p.Arg390Cys, c.904C>T p.Arg302Cys in tRNA-binding interface), splice site mutations (c.447+1G>A, c.969+1_969+3del), in-frame insertions (c.1483GCA[4] p.Ala497_Arg498insAla), and large copy number losses (1p13.3-13.2 deletions); these cause autosomal recessive neurodevelopmental disorders featuring epilepsy, intellectual disability, microcephaly, and growth retardation due to impaired global translation and specific deficits in selenoprotein synthesis.
