## Description
SLAIN2 (SLAIN Motif-Containing Protein 2, UniProt Q9P270) is a 581 amino acid intracellular protein functioning as a microtubule plus-end tracking protein (+TIP) that regulates microtubule dynamics, ciliary function, and cancer metastasis. With 25 literature references (genuine dark gene status), SLAIN2 has emerging roles in colorectal cancer invasion, oral squamous cell carcinoma, subarachnoid hemorrhage recovery, and temporomandibular joint osteoarthritis. The protein contains a SLAIN motif domain (IPR026179, PF15301, aa 130-581) characteristic of microtubule-associated proteins. AlphaFold predicts a partially structured protein (pLDDT 53.7, medium confidence) across all 581 residues, indicating intrinsically disordered regions typical of +TIP proteins that undergo conformational flexibility during microtubule binding. STRING interactions reveal exceptionally high-confidence partnerships with microtubule regulators: CKAP5 (0.997 - cytoskeleton-associated protein 5/ch-TOG, a major microtubule polymerase), FAU (0.806 - ubiquitin-like protein), MAPRE1 (0.618 - microtubule-associated protein RP/EB family member 1/EB1), CLASP2 (0.540), CLASP1 (0.532 - cytoplasmic linker-associated proteins), and ZC3H18 (0.560). HPA confirms primary cilium tip, centrosome, basal body, and cytosol localization with ubiquitous expression across all tissues, classified as a predicted intracellular protein with low tissue/cancer specificity. ClinVar documents 17 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number gains across 4p16.3-q35.2 regions and single nucleotide variants (c.*413T>A), though specific SLAIN2-associated diseases require further characterization. Key literature findings: (1) 2019 landmark study showed MALAT1 sponges miR-106b-5p to promote colorectal cancer invasion/metastasis via SLAIN2-enhanced microtubule mobility (107 citations), (2) 2023 study demonstrated SLAIN2 attenuates brain injury following subarachnoid hemorrhage by controlling axonal microtubule structure in mice (2 citations), (3) 2023 work showed circ-SLAIN2 alleviates cartilage degradation and inflammation in TMJOA (11 citations), (4) 2025 Vietnamese study examined SLAIN2 immunohistochemical expression in oral squamous cell carcinoma patients. Functional hypothesis: SLAIN2 functions as a +TIP adaptor protein that bridges EB1/MAPRE1 at growing microtubule plus-ends with CKAP5/ch-TOG polymerase activity, promoting sustained microtubule growth and stability. The intrinsically disordered SLAIN motif domain (low pLDDT 53.7) enables dynamic conformational sampling required for rapid association/dissociation with microtubule ends during polymerization cycles. Through CKAP5 interaction (0.997 confidence), SLAIN2 recruits the microtubule polymerase to plus-ends, accelerating tubulin addition and suppressing catastrophe events. The centrosome/basal body/cilium tip localization suggests critical roles in ciliogenesis and ciliary microtubule organization, potentially explaining neurological phenotypes (subarachnoid hemorrhage recovery, axonal transport). Disease mechanism: In cancer (colorectal, oral squamous cell), SLAIN2 upregulation via lncRNA/miRNA sponging (MALAT1/miR-106b-5p axis) enhances microtubule dynamics, promoting cell migration, invasion, and metastasis. The 17 ClinVar variants suggest potential developmental disorders affecting ciliary function or neuronal microtubule stability. The circ-SLAIN2 finding in TMJOA reveals circular RNA-mediated regulation of cartilage homeostasis, adding post-transcriptional complexity to SLAIN2 biology.
