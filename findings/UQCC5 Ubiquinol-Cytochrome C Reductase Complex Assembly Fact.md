## Description
UQCC5 (Ubiquinol-Cytochrome C Reductase Complex Assembly Factor 5, UniProt Q8WVI0) is a 70 amino acid small integral membrane protein functioning as a putative assembly factor for mitochondrial Complex III (ubiquinol-cytochrome c reductase) of the electron transport chain. Functional hypothesis: UQCC5 functions as a transient assembly chaperone facilitating the incorporation of cytochrome b (MT-CYB) or other Complex III subunits into the mature bc1 complex during mitochondrial inner membrane biogenesis. Like other UQCC factors (UQCC1-3 characterized in Complex III assembly), UQCC5 likely binds nascent polypeptides emerging from mitochondrial ribosomes, preventing aggregation and guiding proper folding/insertion. The high expression in skeletal muscle (122.3 nTPM) and adrenal gland (116.7 nTPM) reflects the extraordinary energy demands of these tissues - muscle requires sustained ATP for contraction, while adrenal cortex demands ATP for steroid hormone biosynthesis. The mitochondrial localization suggests UQCC5 operates at the inner membrane where Complex III assembles. Disease mechanism: The 10 ClinVar CNVs suggest that gross genomic instability at 3p21 (a known tumor suppressor locus containing multiple cancer genes) could disrupt UQCC5 dosage, potentially contributing to mitochondrial myopathy, adrenal insufficiency, or metabolic crisis phenotypes observed in 3p deletion syndrome patients. Reduced UQCC5 function may impair Complex III assembly, leading to electron transport chain dysfunction, ROS overproduction, and bioenergetic failure - particularly devastating in high-energy tissues like muscle and adrenal gland. The medium-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 68.7) suggests UQCC5 may contain both structured transmembrane helices and flexible regions typical of assembly chaperones that must bind multiple partners. Therapeutic implication: UQCC5 represents a potential target for mitochondrial diseases where Complex III deficiency drives pathology (e.g., mitochondrial encephalomyopathy, lactic acidosis). Gene therapy or small molecule chaperones enhancing UQCC5 function could restore Complex III assembly in patients with assembly defects. The muscle-adrenal expression pattern suggests UQCC5 dysfunction may present with exercise intolerance, adrenal crisis, or metabolic myopathy - phenotypes warranting investigation in undiagnosed mitochondrial disease cohorts.
