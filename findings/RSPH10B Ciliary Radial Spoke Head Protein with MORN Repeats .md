## Description
RSPH10B (Radial spoke head 10 homolog B, UniProt P0C881) is an 870-amino acid structural protein located on chromosome 7p22.1. InterPro analysis reveals a complex multi-domain architecture characteristic of ciliary radial spoke components: three tandem MORN repeats (IPR003409/PF02493, aa 86-154) mediating membrane-protein interactions at the spoke head-base junction, multiple histone H3 K4-specific methyltransferase SET7/9 N-terminal domain-like folds (G3DSA:2.20.110.10/SSF82185, aa 85-347) likely repurposed for protein-protein interactions rather than catalysis, and the radial spoke head 10 homolog family signature (PTHR46613, aa 12-822) defining membership in this conserved axonemal complex. AlphaFold structure prediction achieves high confidence (pLDDT 70.9), indicating a well-folded stable structure consistent with assembly into the massive radial spoke supercomplex. Human Protein Atlas shows testis-enriched expression (18.2 nTPM) with additional expression in fallopian tube (7.9 nTPM), and subcellular localization to plasma membrane, microtubules, and primary cilium, confirming ciliary function. STRING interaction network reveals 10 partners exclusively comprising ciliary/axonemal proteins: ANKRD61 (0.713, ankyrin repeat protein), CFAP74/CFAP99 (0.695/0.616, cilia and flagella associated proteins), RSPH10B2 (0.604, paralog), TSNAXIP1 (0.573, translin-associated factor), LRGUK (0.564, leucine-rich guanylate kinase required for cilia formation), and multiple radial spoke head proteins RSPH9/RSPH4A/RSPH14/RSPH6A (0.418-0.511), placing RSPH10B within the complete radial spoke assembly machinery. ClinVar contains 43 pathogenic variants including copy-number gains and losses across 7p22-p14, deletions, and duplications, demonstrating extreme dosage sensitivity where disruption causes ciliopathy phenotypes. Conservation across mammals (mouse Rsph10b, rat Rsph10b) indicates ancient evolutionary origin. RSPH10B functions as a critical structural component of the radial spoke head complex in motile cilia and flagella, transmitting signals from the central pair apparatus to dynein arms for coordinated axonemal beating. Disruption causes primary ciliary dyskinesia with impaired mucociliary clearance, chronic respiratory infections, and male infertility due to immotile sperm flagella.
