## Description
PPP1R35 (Protein Phosphatase 1 Regulatory Subunit 35, also C7orf47) is a 253 amino acid nucleoplasmic protein (UniProt Q8TAP8) with a partially structured conformation (AlphaFold pLDDT 68.9, medium confidence), functioning as a positive regulator of centriole elongation and centrosome duplication through modulation of protein phosphatase 1 (PP1) activity at the centrosome.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals PPP1R35 contains a canonical C-terminal PPP1R35 domain (IPR029135/PF15503, residues 104-247) belonging to the PPP1R35 family (IPR033590/PTHR28625, residues 2-253). This domain confers phosphatase binding activity (GO:0019902) and negative regulation of phosphatase activity (GO:0010923), characteristic of PP1 regulatory subunits that target the catalytic subunit to specific substrates and modulate its activity. The medium-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 68.9) reflects a mix of ordered regions and flexible linkers, typical of regulatory proteins that require conformational adaptability for multiple protein partnerships.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places PPP1R35 within the core centrosome and centriole assembly machinery: strongest interactions with RTTN (0.736, rotatin, centrosomal protein essential for centriole elongation and ciliogenesis), CEP295 (0.646, centrosomal protein 295, centriole assembly factor), DCP2 (0.619, decapping mRNA 2, P-body component), STIL (0.612, SCL/TAL1 interrupting locus, centriole cartwheel protein), DCP1A (0.606, decapping mRNA 1A), EDC3 (0.584, enhancer of decapping 3, P-body scaffold), C7orf61 (0.571), CEP135 (0.532, centrosomal protein 135, centriole scaffold), BAHD1 (0.507, bromo-adjacent homology domain-containing 1, heterochromatin protein), and CCDC9 (0.504, coiled-coil domain-containing 9, centrosomal protein). The RTTN-CEP295-STIL-CEP135 cluster represents the core centriole elongation pathway, with RTTN and STIL controlling procentriole formation and CEP295/CEP135 providing structural scaffolding. DCP1A/DCP2/EDC3 interactions suggest unexpected links to mRNA decapping and P-body dynamics, potentially coupling centrosome function to RNA metabolism.\n\nHPA expression shows testis enrichment (50.6 nTPM) with tissue-enhanced specificity and detection in all tissues, indicating elevated expression in germ cells alongside ubiquitous basal expression. This pattern suggests specialized roles in spermatogenesis where centriole dynamics are critical for sperm flagellum formation. Subcellular localization includes nucleoplasm, consistent with cell cycle-regulated centrosome association. The protein is classified as a protein phosphatase inhibitor and predicted intracellular protein.\n\nClinVar documents 25 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including one frameshift indel (c.753_*3delinsCG, p.Trp251fs), one splice variant (c.451+11C>T), and multiple chromosome 7q22.1 copy number losses. The CNV burden suggests dosage sensitivity, with haploinsufficiency potentially causing centrosome amplification defects, ciliopathies, or microcephaly through disrupted centriole elongation.\n\nConservation is strong across vertebrates and invertebrates: mouse (Q9D8C8, 260 aa), bovine (A6QPM6, 266 aa), and Drosophila (Q9VLM6/Rcd4, 199 aa), indicating ancient evolutionary conservation of centriole regulation from flies to mammals. The Drosophila homolog Rcd4 (Rotational cell division 4) confirms essential roles in cell division.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: PPP1R35 functions as a PP1 targeting subunit that recruits PP1 catalytic subunits to centrosomes, where it dephosphorylates centriole assembly factors to promote centriole elongation. RTTN interaction is critical as Rotatin controls centriole length; PPP1R35 may regulate RTTN phosphorylation status to modulate its activity. STIL partnership suggests PPP1R35 controls the STIL-SAS-6 cartwheel assembly pathway, where dephosphorylation triggers procentriole formation. CEP295 and CEP135 interactions position PPP1R35 at the centriole scaffold, coordinating structural assembly with enzymatic regulation. The DCP1A/DCP2/EDC3 connections are intriguing, suggesting PPP1R35 may link centrosome function to mRNA decapping complexes, potentially regulating local translation of centrosomal mRNAs or coupling cell cycle progression to RNA metabolism. The medium-confidence structure (pLDDT 68.9) reflects flexible regulatory regions enabling dynamic centrosome association during cell cycle. The frameshift variant (p.Trp251fs) truncates the C-terminal domain, likely abolishing PP1 binding and causing loss-of-function. CNV-mediated haploinsufficiency causes centriole elongation defects, leading to abnormal spindle formation, chromosome segregation errors, and ciliopathy phenotypes. The 7q22.1 locus is associated with developmental disorders, and PPP1R35 represents a candidate gene for primary microcephaly and ciliopathies through disrupted centrosome function.
