## Description
RCBTB1 (RCC1 and BTB Domain-Containing Protein 1) is a 531-amino acid nuclear protein with emerging disease characterization (40 publications) that causes inherited retinal dystrophy with macular chorioretinal atrophy, though pathogenic mechanisms remain poorly understood. The protein features a distinctive domain architecture: an N-terminal RCC1 domain (IPR000408, residues 41-199 with three repeats; IPR009091, 11-328) characteristic of chromatin regulators and guanine nucleotide exchange factors, followed by a C-terminal BTB/POZ domain (IPR000210, cd18353, residues 350-466) and BACK domain (cd18528, 466-531). This BTB-BACK combination typically mediates CUL3 E3 ubiquitin ligase complex assembly, suggesting RCBTB1 may function as a substrate adaptor for targeted protein degradation. AlphaFold predicts an exceptionally well-folded structure (global pLDDT 93.2), indicating strong evolutionary constraint on both RCC1 and BTB-BACK folds.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals connections to chromatin regulation and retinal disease pathways: RCBTB2 (paralog, 0.698), NUDCD1 (0.624), NUDCD2 (0.607), PHF11 (0.624), ZNF408 (0.605 - causes familial exudative vitreoretinopathy), CUL3 (0.443 - direct BTB domain partner for ubiquitination), and SETDB2 (0.407 - histone methyltransferase). The RCBTB1-RCBTB2 paralogue interaction and CUL3 binding support a role in CUL3-dependent ubiquitination of chromatin substrates. ZNF408 association is particularly notable given its independent role in retinal vascular disease.\n\nHuman Protein Atlas classifies RCBTB1 as a chromatin regulator and transcription regulator with ubiquitous expression (detected in all tissues, low tissue specificity), localized to the nucleoplasm. This confirms nuclear function consistent with RCC1 domain-mediated chromatin interactions. ClinVar reports 103 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including frameshifts (p.Glu358fs, p.Lys128fs), splice site mutations (c.603+1G>A, c.711+1G>C), missense variants (p.Ala229Gly), and large chromosome 13q CNVs. Recent clinical phenotyping (2021-2025) describes RCBTB1-associated retinopathy as later-onset non-syndromic retinal dystrophy with macular atrophy and peripheral retinal degeneration, though mitochondrial dysfunction and impaired antioxidant responses in RPE cells have been observed in patient-derived cells.\n\nWe hypothesize that RCBTB1 functions as a CUL3 E3 ubiquitin ligase substrate adaptor that targets specific transcription factors or chromatin modifiers for degradation in retinal pigment epithelium and photoreceptors. The RCC1 domain may recruit RCBTB1 to specific chromatin loci via interaction with Ran-GTP or histone modifications, while the BTB-BACK domains assemble the CUL3 ubiquitination machinery. Loss of RCBTB1 disrupts turnover of key retinal transcription factors (potentially NRL, CRX, or NR2E3), leading to progressive photoreceptor degeneration. The ubiquitous expression but retina-specific disease phenotype suggests compensatory mechanisms in other tissues or unique RCBTB1 substrate requirements in retinal cells. The high pLDDT score indicates both domains are structurally critical, and variants disrupting either RCC1 chromatin binding or BTB-BACK CUL3 recruitment cause retinal dystrophy.
