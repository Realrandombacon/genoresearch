## Description
CLINT1 (Clathrin Interactor 1; UniProt Q14677) is a 625 amino acid endocytic adaptor protein containing an N-terminal ENTH (Epsin N-Terminal Homology) domain. With only 33 publications, CLINT1 remains a genuine dark gene despite its fundamental role in clathrin-mediated vesicle trafficking and emerging connections to viral infection and cancer migration.\n\nSTRUCTURAL FEATURES: InterPro analysis identifies a canonical ENTH domain (IPR013809/PF01417/SM00273, residues 16-149) within the ENTH/VHS superfamily (IPR008942), characteristic of epsin-family membrane curvature sensors. The protein belongs to the epsin family (PTHR12276, residues 8-577). AlphaFold prediction yields medium confidence (global pLDDT 56.8), indicating a structured ENTH domain coupled with extensive intrinsically disordered regions (~70% of the protein) - a hallmark of adaptor proteins that require conformational flexibility for multi-protein complex assembly.\n\nINTERACTION NETWORK: STRING-DB reveals CLINT1 embedded in core clathrin trafficking machinery with exceptionally high-confidence interactions: VTI1B (0.999, v-SNARE for endosomal fusion), CLTC (0.972, clathrin heavy chain), CLTCL1 (0.948, clathrin light chain-like), CLTB (0.772, clathrin light chain B), AP2A1 (0.739, adaptor protein complex 2), PICALM (0.535, phosphatidylinositol-binding clathrin assembly protein), AP1B1 (0.481), GGA2 (0.462), and AP1G1 (0.439). This positions CLINT1 at the intersection of clathrin coat assembly and SNARE-mediated vesicle fusion.\n\nEXPRESSION & LOCALIZATION: HPA shows ubiquitous expression across all tissues with immune cell enrichment, consistent with high vesicle trafficking demands in antigen presentation and cytokine secretion. Subcellular localization includes nucleoplasm and vesicles, suggesting potential nuclear functions beyond canonical endocytosis - possibly in transcriptional regulation or nuclear envelope dynamics.\n\nDISEASE ASSOCIATION: ClinVar contains 15 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all large copy number losses/gains spanning 5q33-35 that encompass CLINT1. No monogenic disease is yet defined, but 5q deletions cause various developmental syndromes. Recent 2026 literature identifies CLINT1 as a subtype-specific biomarker and downstream effector of mutant p53-R273H in lung adenocarcinoma migration, while 2022 studies demonstrate CLINT1 phosphorylation by BIKE kinase mediates dengue virus infection - suggesting roles in cancer metastasis and viral pathogenesis.\n\nCONSERVATION: Orthologs confirmed in mouse (Q99KN9, 631 aa) and cattle (A7Z035, 643 aa), indicating evolutionary conservation of the ENTH domain architecture.\n\nFUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: CLINT1 likely functions as a membrane curvature sensor and clathrin recruitment factor that bridges early endocytic coat assembly (via ENTH-phosphoinositide binding) with late vesicle fusion events (via VTI1B interaction). The extensive disordered regions enable dynamic scaffolding of multi-protein complexes. Immune cell enrichment suggests specialized roles in immunological synapse formation or MHC trafficking. The 5q33-35 CNVs without defined phenotypes warrant investigation for contributions to neurodevelopmental disorders, immunodeficiency, or cancer susceptibility.
