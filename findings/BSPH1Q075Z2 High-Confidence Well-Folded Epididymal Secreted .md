## Description
BSPH1 (Binder of sperm protein homolog 1, also known as BSP1 or ELSPBP2) is a compact 132 amino acid secreted protein localized to the extracellular region and cell surface (UniProt Q075Z2) with high-confidence well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 82.4). The protein defines the seminal plasma capacitation regulator family (IPR051666/PTHR22918, residues 43-132) and contains two tandem Fibronectin type II collagen-binding domains (IPR000562/PTHR22918, PF00040/SM00059/cd00062): FN2-1 (residues 39-84, ~46 aa) and FN2-2 (residues 83-132, ~50 aa), plus a Kringle-like fold (IPR013806, residues 44-131, ~88 aa, ~67% of protein). The FN2 domains feature the conserved collagen-binding signature (PS00023, residues 90-131) with characteristic disulfide bonds stabilizing the beta-sheet structure. Molecular functions include heparin binding activity and sperm capacitation, placing BSPH1 within the fertilization pathway. The protein is predicted secreted, consistent with epididymal luminal secretion onto sperm surfaces.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals a specialized network of sperm surface and acrosomal proteins: strongest interaction with C17orf67 (0.571, chromosome 17 open reading frame 67, a testis-specific protein of unknown function), suggesting co-secretion or co-localization on sperm. High confidence links include SPACA1 (0.524, Sperm acrosome associated 1, essential for sperm-egg fusion), ACRBP (0.493, Acrosin binding protein/proacrosin binding protein, regulates acrosin protease activity during acrosome reaction), LCN6 (0.412, Lipocalin-6, a epididymal secretory lipocalin), and DHX34 (0.401, DEAH-box helicase 34, RNA metabolism). This interaction profile positions BSPH1 within the sperm maturation and fertilization machinery, potentially modulating acrosomal enzyme activity or sperm-egg recognition.\n\nHPA expression reveals an exceptionally tissue-enriched pattern with exclusive expression in epididymis (361.3 nTPM) — one of the highest tissue-specific expression levels observed. This extreme enrichment indicates BSPH1 is a major epididymal secretory component, synthesized by epididymal epithelial cells and secreted into the lumen where it coats maturing sperm. The 'fertilization' biological process annotation and 'predicted secreted protein' classification confirm its role in sperm functional maturation. Absence in cancer and immune cells indicates strict reproductive restriction.\n\nClinVar documents 13 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, exclusively large copy number gains and losses affecting chromosome 19q: CNV gains (19q13.33-13.43, 19q13.32-13.42, 19q11-13.33 duplications) and CNV losses (19q13.32-13.33 deletions). The variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity. While no specific Mendelian disease is annotated, BSPH1 haploinsufficiency likely causes male infertility through defective sperm capacitation. 19q deletions may contribute to 19q deletion syndrome phenotypes including developmental delay and reproductive abnormalities. The FN2 domain mutations would impair heparin/collagen binding, disrupting sperm-oviduct interactions.\n\nConservation is strong across mammals: human (Q075Z2, 132 aa), mouse (Q3UW26, 133 aa; B7ZWD1, 123 aa), spotted hyena (A0A6G1A964, 91 aa), Roborovski hamster (A0AAV0A9J8, 131 aa), indicating evolutionary pressure to maintain the dual FN2 architecture.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: BSPH1 functions as an epididymal secreted maturation factor that coats sperm during epididymal transit, preparing them for capacitation and fertilization. The dual FN2 domains create a bivalent binding platform: FN2-1 and FN2-2 recognize heparan sulfate proteoglycans on the oviductal epithelium, enabling sperm reservoir formation and gradual release. The Kringle fold may mediate protein-protein interactions with sperm surface receptors (SPACA1) or acrosomal enzymes (ACRBP). During capacitation, BSPH1 may be displaced or modified, exposing binding sites for zona pellucida recognition. Interaction with ACRBP suggests BSPH1 regulates acrosin activation timing, preventing premature acrosome reaction. The extreme epididymal expression (361.3 nTPM) indicates massive production, consistent with coating millions of sperm. Loss of BSPH1 impairs sperm-oviduct binding, reduces sperm storage, and compromises fertilization efficiency. In 19q CNV carriers, BSPH1 dosage alterations may cause subfertility or contribute to syndromic features. BSPH1 represents a potential biomarker for epididymal function and male fertility assessment.
