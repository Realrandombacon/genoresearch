# SAT2: A GNAT Family Acetyltransferase Regulating Polyamine Homeostasis via N1-Acetylation of Spermidine and Spermine

**Date:** 2026-06-26T17:20:06.874408

**Quality Score:** 9/10 (EXCELLENT)

**Confidence:** MEDIUM

## Description
SAT2 (Spermidine/Spermine N1-Acetyltransferase Family Member 2, UniProt Q96F10), also known as SSAT-2, is a 170-amino acid cytosolic enzyme that catalyzes the N1-acetylation of polyamines (spermidine and spermine) using acetyl-CoA as a donor, a critical regulatory step in polyamine catabolism and homeostasis. Unlike its paralog SAT1 which targets the N1 position for export/degradation, SAT2 exhibits distinct substrate specificity potentially targeting alternative amine groups or functioning in specific cellular compartments to fine-tune local polyamine pools essential for DNA stabilization, cell proliferation, and stress response. InterPro analysis confirms membership in the GCN5-related N-acetyltransferase (GNAT) superfamily with a canonical GNAT domain (IPR000182, PS51186, aa 4-168) embedded within an acyl-CoA N-acyltransferase fold (IPR016181, SSF55729, G3DSA:3.40.630.30, aa 4-155); the protein belongs to the diverse substrate acetyltransferase family (IPR051016, PTHR10545). The AlphaFold predicted structure exhibits exceptional confidence (pLDDT 93.9), revealing a highly ordered α/β fold characteristic of GNAT enzymes with a deep central cleft accommodating both acetyl-CoA and polyamine substrates. STRING interaction data places SAT2 at the core of the polyamine metabolic network with very high confidence associations to: SAT1 (0.980), the primary catabolic acetyltransferase; SRM (0.941), spermidine synthase; AGMAT (0.909), agmatinase; MAOA/MAOB (0.904), monoamine oxidases degrading biogenic amines; AOC1 (0.900), diamine oxidase; PAOX (0.895), polyamine oxidase completing the back-conversion pathway; ODC1 (0.810), ornithine decarboxylase rate-limiting synthesis enzyme; and HIF1A (0.814), linking polyamine metabolism to hypoxia response. HPA expression profiles confirm ubiquitous detection across all tissues with cell type-enhanced expression, consistent with housekeeping roles in cellular metabolism. ClinVar records document 20 pathogenic variants predominantly consisting of large copy number gains and losses spanning 17p13.1-p13.3 (ranging from 1 Mb to whole arm duplications/deletions), implicating SAT2 dosage sensitivity in developmental disorders though specific phenotypes remain undefined. We hypothesize that SAT2 functions as a regulatory rheostat for polyamine pools: upon cellular stress or proliferative signals, SAT2 is induced or activated to acetylate excess spermidine/spermine; this acetylation either tags polyamines for export via specific transporters or alters their binding affinity to DNA/RNA targets; the interaction with HIF1A suggests SAT2 may modulate the hypoxic response by regulating polyamine levels required for HIF stability; distinct from SAT1, SAT2 may operate in specific subcellular niches (e.g., nucleus vs cytosol) to control local polyamine concentrations affecting chromatin structure or translation; loss of SAT2 would disrupt polyamine balance, leading to aberrant cell growth, oxidative stress susceptibility, and developmental defects.
