## Description
TASP1 (Threonine Aspartase 1) is a 420 aa threonine-type endopeptidase functioning as a unique protease that cleaves specific transcription factors and chromatin regulators to control gene expression programs during development. InterPro analysis reveals a canonical N-terminal nucleophile (Ntn) hydrolase architecture with a Peptidase T2/asparaginase 2 domain (IPR000246/PF01112, aa 42-398) containing the catalytic threonine residue, and a Taspase 1 family signature (IPR037464/cd04514, aa 42-416) defining its substrate specificity. AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (pLDDT 86.8) consistent with a stable α/β fold characteristic of Ntn hydrolases. STRING interactions place TASP1 in transcriptional regulatory networks with high-confidence partners: KMT2A/MLL1 (0.841), the histone methyltransferase that TASP1 cleaves to activate HOX genes; ASPG (0.877), a related asparaginase; HOXA7 (0.585) and HOXA9 (0.566), downstream targets; MEN1 (0.603) and FH (0.671), tumor suppressors; and RBBP5 (0.426), a MLL complex member. HPA expression shows tissue-enhanced patterns with maximal expression in blood vessels (27.3 nTPM), detected in all tissues, and subcellular localization to the plasma membrane and nucleus. ClinVar contains 35 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including missense mutations (p.Ser252Asn, p.Pro139Ala) and copy number variations on chromosome 20p, causing Suleiman-El-Hattab syndrome—a rare autosomal recessive disorder characterized by intellectual disability, distinctive facial features, dental anomalies, and growth retardation. Literature (39 papers) establishes TASP1's role in cleaving MLL1 to generate active fragments, processing TFIIA into subunits, and regulating HOX gene expression critical for embryonic patterning.
