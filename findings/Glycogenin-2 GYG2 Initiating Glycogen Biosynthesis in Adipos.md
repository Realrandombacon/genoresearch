## Description
GYG2 (Glycogenin-2, UniProt O15488) is a 501-amino acid glycosyltransferase functioning as the priming enzyme for glycogen biosynthesis, initiating glycogen particle formation through self-glucosylation. InterPro analysis confirms canonical glycogenin architecture: Glycogenin GT8 family domain (cd02537, aa 37-288) belonging to glycosyltransferase family 8; Glycosyl transferase family 8 (IPR002495/PF01501, aa 40-255) with GO annotation for glycosyltransferase activity (GO:0016757); Nucleotide-diphospho-sugar transferases superfamily (IPR029044/G3DSA:3.90.550.10, aa 34-296) characteristic of sugar nucleotide-binding enzymes; and GNT1/Glycosyltransferase 8 family membership (IPR050587/PTHR11183, aa 27-432). Functional hypothesis: GYG2 serves as the adipose tissue-specific glycogenin isoform that initiates glycogen particle biosynthesis through autocatalytic self-glucosylation, attaching the first glucose residues from UDP-glucose to its own tyrosine residue (catalytic Tyr) to create a primer for glycogen synthase (GYS1/GYS2) elongation, with specialized roles in adipose energy storage, aging-related metabolic changes, and potential X-linked disease mechanisms. Mechanistic proposal: The GT8 glycosyltransferase domain (aa 37-288) contains the catalytic site binding UDP-glucose and catalyzing transfer of glucose to Tyr194 (conserved autocatalytic tyrosine); GYG2 self-glucosylates to create an alpha-1,4-glucan primer of ~8 glucose units; this primer is then extended by glycogen synthases (GYS1 in muscle, GYS2 in liver/adipose); GBE1 introduces alpha-1,6 branches creating mature glycogen particles; TRIM7 interaction suggests ubiquitin-mediated regulation of GYG2 stability; adipose-specific high expression (146.8 nTPM) indicates specialized role in fat tissue energy storage and glucose homeostasis; mitochondrial localization signals and Leigh syndrome association suggest potential dual localization affecting mitochondrial glycogen metabolism; X-chromosome location (Xp22.33) means hemizygous mutations in males could cause severe metabolic phenotypes; cancer-enhanced expression pattern suggests glycogen metabolism reprogramming in tumors; GYG2-GYG1 paralogs show tissue-specific expression (GYG1 ubiquitous, GYG2 adipose-enriched) providing metabolic flexibility; copy number variants affecting GYG2 may contribute to Xg blood group null phenotype through contiguous gene deletion.
