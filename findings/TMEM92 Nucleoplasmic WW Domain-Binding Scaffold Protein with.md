## Description
Transmembrane protein 92 (TMEM92) is a 159-amino acid nucleoplasmic protein containing a WW domain-binding motif (IPR021684, PF11669, residues 33-141) that likely functions as a protein-protein interaction scaffold. Despite its "transmembrane" name, HPA data confirms nucleoplasmic localization with tissue-enhanced RNA expression, highest in intestine (17.9 nTPM) and stomach (10.4 nTPM). AlphaFold predicts a partially structured conformation (pLDDT 67.2), suggesting flexible binding interfaces typical of scaffold proteins. STRING interactions reveal connections to E3 ubiquitin ligases (ITCH score 0.502, NEDD4 score 0.438), zinc finger proteins (ZSCAN4 0.587, ZNF618 0.491), and chromatin regulators (TRIM28 0.414), positioning TMEM92 within ubiquitination and transcriptional regulation networks. The WW domain-binding fold suggests TMEM92 recognizes proline-rich PPxY motifs in partner proteins, potentially recruiting ubiquitination machinery to specific substrates. Clinical significance is evidenced by 13 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants in ClinVar, predominantly copy number losses and gains affecting 17q21.33, implicating TMEM92 dosage sensitivity in disease. Conservation across vertebrates (mouse ortholog Q96Q80 homolog, 162 aa) indicates functional constraint. I hypothesize TMEM92 serves as an adaptor protein bridging WW domain-containing E3 ligases to nuclear substrates, modulating protein turnover of transcription factors or chromatin modifiers in gastrointestinal tissues.
