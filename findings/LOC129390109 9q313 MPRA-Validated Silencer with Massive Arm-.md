## Description
LOC129390109 is a 200bp cis-regulatory element (CRE) located on chromosome 9q31.3 (GRCh38: 114604754-114604954), functionally validated as a transcriptional silencer through STARR-seq massively parallel reporter assays (MPRAs) in K562 erythroleukemia cells. This locus lacks canonical promoter, enhancer, or CTCF-bound insulator chromatin signatures but demonstrates robust repressive activity against the super core promoter SCP1. As a non-coding regulatory sequence, it has no UniProt accession, protein domains, STRING interaction partners, or Human Protein Atlas expression data (query failed to resolve Ensembl ID).\n\nClinVar analysis reveals a striking burden of 13 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variants disrupting this region. The variant spectrum is dominated by massive arm-level events spanning nearly the entire chromosome 9. Duplications (copy number gains) are highly prevalent (approx. 9:4 ratio vs deletions), including whole-arm gains (9p24.3-q34.3, ~138Mb) and large segmental duplications (9p11.2-q34.3, ~138Mb; 9p24.3-q34.3, ~138Mb). Deletions include large losses spanning 9q22.33-33.1 (~15.9Mb) and 9q31.2-33.1 (~10.4Mb). These CNVs are associated with complex 9p/9q deletion/duplication syndromes, characterized by severe developmental delay, intellectual disability, trigonocephaly (often linked to 9p deletion), and skeletal anomalies. The locus resides in close proximity to critical genes such as PTCH1 (Patched-1, the receptor for Sonic Hedgehog) and SMO (Smoothened), key components of the Hedgehog signaling pathway which is vital for embryonic patterning and neural tube development.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: LOC129390109 functions as a critical distal silencer within the 9q31 topologically associating domain (TAD), specifically regulating the Hedgehog signaling pathway components PTCH1 and/or SMO. Given its validation in K562 cells, it likely acts as a repression module to prevent ectopic Hedgehog activation in non-target tissues or during specific cell cycle phases. The mechanism involves recruitment of co-repressor complexes (e.g., PRC2, NuRD) to maintain target genes in a silenced state. The high burden of massive duplications suggests a "hyper-silencing" or "position effect" mechanism: triplication of this silencer (or disruption of TAD boundaries leading to ectopic contacts) causes profound downregulation of PTCH1/SMO, mimicking loss-of-function mutations and leading to Hedgehog pathway defects (e.g., holoprosencephaly-like features, skeletal dysplasia). Conversely, large deletions may remove the silencer, leading to constitutive overexpression of these targets, also disrupting the precise gradient required for development. The sheer size of the CNVs implies that LOC129390109 is part of a larger dosage-sensitive regulatory landscape on 9q, where stoichiometric balance of multiple CREs is essential for normal neurodevelopment.
