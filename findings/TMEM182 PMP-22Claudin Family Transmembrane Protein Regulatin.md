## Description
TMEM182 (UniProt Q6ZP80) encodes a 229 amino acid multi-pass transmembrane protein belonging to the PMP-22/EMP/MP20/Claudin superfamily (IPR004031, PF13903, residues 15-220). This domain family is characterized by four transmembrane helices that form tight junction strands or membrane scaffolds, mediating cell-cell adhesion and paracellular barrier function in epithelial and endothelial tissues.\n\nHPA expression profiling reveals striking skeletal muscle enrichment (141.0 nTPM) with elevated expression in tongue (119.3 nTPM) and heart muscle (91.4 nTPM). Gene ontology annotations implicate TMEM182 in myogenesis, specifically negative regulation of myoblast differentiation and myoblast fusion—suggesting it functions as a brake on terminal muscle differentiation. Subcellular localization in nucleoplasm and vesicles indicates potential dual roles in transcriptional regulation and membrane trafficking.\n\nClinVar documents 15 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number gains/losses on chromosome 2 and splice site mutations (c.469+2T>C), indicating clinical relevance though specific disease phenotypes remain undefined. The PMP-22/EMP/MP20/Claudin domain architecture (G3DSA:1.20.140.150 fold) is conserved across vertebrates (mouse, bovine, chicken, Xenopus), confirming functional constraint.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: TMEM182 functions as a tetraspan membrane scaffold that organizes specialized membrane microdomains in myoblasts, potentially sequestering pro-differentiation receptors (e.g., IGF1R, MET) away from lipid rafts or recruiting inhibitory signaling complexes. By maintaining myoblasts in a proliferative state, TMEM182 ensures adequate muscle progenitor pool expansion before terminal differentiation. Dysregulation could cause premature differentiation (depleting satellite cells) or blocked differentiation (causing myopathy).
