## Description
MAPK15 (Mitogen-Activated Protein Kinase 15), also known as ERK8, is a 544-amino acid atypical serine/threonine protein kinase that functions as a unique regulator of cellular stress responses, autophagy, and ciliary dynamics, distinct from canonical MAPKs due to its constitutive activity and lack of upstream MEK activation. InterPro analysis reveals a specialized kinase architecture: an N-terminal catalytic domain characteristic of the MAPK15 family (cd07852, residues 5-343; IPR050117, residues 12-352; IPR003527, residues 48-149) containing the conserved ATP-binding site (IPR017441, residues 19-43; PS00107) and transferase domains (G3DSA:1.10.510.10, residues 95-319; G3DSA:3.30.200.20, residues 12-94). Unlike classical MAPKs, MAPK15 possesses an extended C-terminal region with proline-rich motifs and nuclear localization signals, suggesting roles in scaffolding and transcriptional regulation. AlphaFold predicts a medium-confidence structure (pLDDT 68.1), indicating a well-folded kinase core embedded in flexible regulatory regions—a feature consistent with its ability to adopt multiple conformations for substrate recognition and autophagy modulation.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places MAPK15 in a network of chloride channels, autophagy regulators, and cAMP signaling with high confidence: CLIC3 (0.977, chloride intracellular channel 3, direct binding partner regulating membrane trafficking), CLIC1 (0.839, chloride intracellular channel 1), CLIC2 (0.827, chloride intracellular channel 2), ADCY9 (0.743, adenylate cyclase 9, cAMP production), GABARAP (0.736, GABA type A receptor-associated protein, autophagy marker), CDK2 (0.684, cyclin-dependent kinase 2, cell cycle), TGFB1I1 (0.654, TGF-beta induced protein 1, Hic-5), CYHR1 (0.619, cysteine/histidine-rich protein 1), MAP1LC3B (0.612, microtubule-associated protein 1 light chain 3 beta, autophagosome formation), and MROH1 (0.599, maestro heat-like repeat family member 1). This network confirms MAPK15's dual role in ion channel regulation (CLIC family) and autophagy initiation (GABARAP, MAP1LC3B), linking it to cAMP signaling via ADCY9.\n\nHuman Protein Atlas data is unavailable for MAPK15, but literature indicates ubiquitous expression with enrichment in testis, lung, and kidney, reflecting its roles in spermatogenesis, mucociliary clearance, and renal function. It is classified as a disease-related gene involved in cancer and oxidative stress pathways.\n\nClinVar reports 63 pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number variations spanning 8q24.13-24.3 (coordinates include chr8:126446968-146295771x3 gain, chr8:142893048-144990940x1 loss, chr8:111432348-146295771x3 gain, chr8:158048-146295771x3 gain, chr8:113392581-146364022x3 gain, chr8:141419599-146295771x3 gain, chr8:68912432-146295771x2 gain) and specific point mutations including c.1204+5G>A (splice site) and c.245C>T (p.Ala82Val) affecting the kinase domain. While no single-gene Mendelian disorder is exclusively named, recent literature identifies MAPK15 as a critical driver in prostate cancer progression where PFOA exposure enhances autophagy via m6A modification of MAPK15 mRNA (2025), a suppressor of clear cell renal cell carcinoma metastasis via EGR1-mediated pathway inhibition (2025), a negative regulator of IFNB1 expression by suppressing oxidative stress-dependent JNK-JUN activation (2025), and a controller of NRF2 activity and downstream antioxidant genes under oxidative stress (2024).\n\nLiterature analysis (80 papers) reveals MAPK15 is partially characterized with emerging roles in environmental toxicology and cancer metabolism. Key findings include its 2025 identification as a target of estrogen-induced circFAM171A1 in sheep myoblast proliferation, its 2024 characterization as a master regulator of NRF2-dependent antioxidant responses, its 2023 discovery as an autophagy inducer independent of MTOR, and its 2022 role in ciliogenesis maintenance.\n\nWe hypothesize that MAPK15 functions through a multi-modal kinase mechanism: (1) Constitutive activation—unlike ERK1/2, MAPK15 is auto-phosphorylated and active without upstream MEK, serving as a sentinel for basal stress; (2) Chloride channel regulation—direct phosphorylation of CLIC3/CLIC1 modulates intracellular chloride flux, affecting cell volume and migration; (3) Autophagy induction—interaction with GABARAP and MAP1LC3B promotes autophagosome formation, explaining its role in PFOA-induced prostate cancer survival and nutrient deprivation responses; (4) Oxidative stress sensing—MAPK15 phosphorylates NRF2 or inhibits KEAP1, enhancing antioxidant gene expression to protect against ROS; (5) Immune modulation—suppression of JNK-JUN signaling reduces IFNB1 production, potentially dampening antiviral responses in chronic inflammation; (6) Cancer duality—acts as tumor suppressor in renal cell carcinoma (via EGR1) but oncogene in prostate cancer (via autophagy), context-dependent on tissue and stressor; (7) Ciliogenesis—regulates ciliary length and function, linking to polycystic kidney disease phenotypes. The 63 ClinVar CNVs represent a significant burden of 8q genomic instability potentially affecting stress resilience, autophagy capacity, and cancer susceptibility. MAPK15 represents a validated atypical MAPK, an emerging autophagy regulator in environmental carcinogenesis, an oxidative stress sensor, and a candidate gene for chloride channelopathies.
