# NDUFC1: Compact 76-aa Complex I Accessory Subunit with 40 ClinVar Variants, Muscle-Enhanced Expression, and Oncogenic Roles in Hepatocellular and Gastric Cancer

**Quality Score:** 9/10 (EXCELLENT)

**Confidence:** MEDIUM

## Description
NDUFC1 (NADH:Ubiquinone Oxidoreductase Subunit C1; UniProt O43677) is a compact 76 amino acid mitochondrial accessory subunit of respiratory Complex I with only 21 publications, classifying it as a genuine dark gene despite essential roles in oxidative phosphorylation and emerging functions in cancer metabolism.\n\nSTRUCTURAL ARCHITECTURE: InterPro analysis identifies NDUFC1 as a canonical member of the NADH-ubiquinone oxidoreductase subunit C1 family (IPR026192/PTHR17097, residues 1-76) with a Pfam domain spanning residues 28-76 (PF15088). The protein localizes to mitochondrion (GO:0005739) and respiratory chain complex I (GO:0045271). AlphaFold predicts high confidence structure (global pLDDT 82.3), indicating a well-folded compact accessory subunit optimized for integration into the ~45-subunit Complex I holoenzyme.\n\nINTERACTION NETWORK: STRING-DB reveals ultra-high confidence interactions (all scores 0.998-0.999) exclusively with Complex I subunits: NDUFB4 (0.999), NDUFA2 (0.999), NDUFB5 (0.999), NDUFS6 (0.999), NDUFA7 (0.999), NDUFV2 (0.999), NDUFA12 (0.999), NDUFV3 (0.999), NDUFB3 (0.999), and NDUFC2 (0.998). This exclusive interaction profile confirms NDUFC1's role as an integral, non-exchangeable accessory component of the membrane arm of Complex I, likely contributing to structural stability rather than catalytic activity.\n\nEXPRESSION & LOCALIZATION: HPA shows group-enriched expression with highest levels in skeletal muscle (251.3 nTPM), tongue (148.5 nTPM), and heart muscle (78.4 nTPM)—tissues with high oxidative metabolic demands. Expression is detected in all tissues, consistent with universal requirement for mitochondrial respiration. Subcellular localization is strictly mitochondrial. Classified as a metabolic protein involved in electron transport and respiratory chain function.\n\nDISEASE LANDSCAPE: ClinVar contains 40 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains and losses at 4q27-35 encompassing NDUFC1. No monogenic disease is yet definitively assigned, but 4q deletions cause developmental anomalies. Recent 2021-2022 literature establishes oncogenic roles: NDUFC1 is upregulated in gastric cancer where it regulates cell proliferation, apoptosis, cell cycle progression, and migration; knockdown in hepatocellular carcinoma inhibits proliferation, migration, and invasion. This suggests NDUFC1 supports the metabolic reprogramming (Warburg effect reversal or oxidative phosphorylation dependence) required for tumor growth in certain cancers.\n\nCONSERVATION: Orthologs confirmed in mouse (Q9CQY9, 76 aa), bovine (Q02376, 76 aa), orangutan (P0CB70, 77 aa), and chimpanzee (Q0MQF6, 76 aa), indicating extreme evolutionary constraint on this compact subunit across mammals.\n\nFUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: NDUFC1 functions as a structural accessory subunit that stabilizes the membrane arm of Complex I, facilitating proper assembly of the ~800 kDa holoenzyme and ensuring efficient electron transfer from NADH to ubiquinone. Unlike catalytic core subunits, accessory subunits like NDUFC1 likely serve as molecular scaffolds that protect against oxidative damage, regulate supercomplex formation with Complex III/IV, or mediate interactions with mitochondrial membranes. The muscle-enriched expression reflects high ATP demand in contractile tissues. In cancer contexts, NDUFC1 upregulation may support oxidative phosphorylation-dependent tumor metabolism, particularly in gastric and liver cancers that retain mitochondrial function. The 40 ClinVar CNVs warrant investigation for haploinsufficiency effects on neurodevelopment and metabolic disease, potentially contributing to mitochondrial encephalopathy or Leigh syndrome-like phenotypes.
