## Description
MTX3 (Metaxin-3) is a 312 amino acid mitochondrial outer membrane protein (UniProt Q5HYI7) with high-confidence well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 84.2). The protein defines the Metaxin family (IPR017410/PTHR12289, residues 1-303) and contains an N-terminal Glutathione S-transferase (GST) domain (IPR040079/IPR036282/IPR033468, PF17171/cd03078, residues 5-77/163-241), which in metaxins has evolved away from enzymatic activity to serve a structural role in protein-protein interactions. It also features a C-terminal alpha-helical domain characteristic of the SAM37/metaxin subfamily (IPR019564/PF10568/cd03212, residues 23-142/105-241), essential for docking to the Sorting and Assembly Machinery (SAM) complex. Molecular functions include mitochondrion organization, protein transport, and structural constituent of the SAM complex, placing MTX3 within the mitochondrial biogenesis pathway.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals a tightly integrated network of mitochondrial import machinery: strongest interactions with TOMM40 (0.933, Translocase of outer mitochondrial membrane 40, the main pore), CHCHD3 (0.898, Coiled-coil-helix-coiled-coil-helix domain containing 3, a MICOS component), and SAMM50 (0.895, Sorting and assembly machinery component 50, the core of the SAM complex). High confidence links include DNAJC11 (0.847), APOOL (0.824), TOMM22 (0.783), TOMM7 (0.747), and its paralog MTX2 (0.671). This profile confirms MTX3 as a peripheral component of the SAM complex, likely acting as a receptor or regulatory subunit that facilitates the insertion of beta-barrel proteins (like TOMM40 and VDAC) into the outer membrane. The interaction with CHCHD3 suggests crosstalk between the SAM complex and the MICOS complex for cristae maintenance.\n\nHPA expression shows low tissue specificity with detection in all tissues, consistent with the universal requirement for mitochondrial function across cell types. Subcellular localization is strictly mitochondrial. The ubiquitous expression pattern underscores its role as a housekeeping gene essential for cellular respiration and energy metabolism.\n\nClinVar documents 5 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, exclusively large copy number gains and losses affecting chromosome 5q (5q13.2-15 duplications, 5p15.1-q35.2 deletions). The variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity. While no specific Mendelian disease is annotated, MTX3 haploinsufficiency or overexpression likely causes mitochondrial dysfunction, leading to metabolic disorders, myopathy, or neurodegeneration. 5q CNVs are associated with spinal muscular atrophy (SMA) region deletions and other developmental syndromes; MTX3 disruption may contribute to the metabolic phenotypes observed in these conditions.\n\nConservation is strong across vertebrates: human (Q5HYI7, 312 aa), Xenopus laevis (Q3KPT9, 309 aa), Danio rerio (Q4VBW0, 313 aa), indicating evolutionary pressure to maintain the GST-like fold and SAM-interaction interface.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: MTX3 functions as a regulatory subunit of the SAM complex, specifically facilitating the recognition and insertion of beta-barrel precursors delivered by the TOM complex. Unlike MTX1/MTX2 which form the core receptor, MTX3 may stabilize the SAM-MICOS supercomplex or regulate the release of assembled barrels into the membrane. The GST-like domain provides a scaffold for binding chaperones or precursor proteins, while the C-terminal helical domain anchors it to SAMM50. Loss of MTX3 impairs beta-barrel biogenesis, leading to defective outer membrane integrity, reduced respiratory chain assembly, and increased oxidative stress. This manifests clinically as multi-system metabolic failure. MTX3 represents a potential biomarker for mitochondrial diseases and a target for enhancing mitochondrial health in aging.
