## Description
ARHGEF19 (UniProt: Q8IW93, alias WGEF) encodes an 802 amino acid Rho guanine nucleotide exchange factor representing a partially characterized dark gene with only 18 publications in Semantic Scholar. InterPro analysis reveals canonical Dbl-family GEF architecture with four structured domains: IPR000219 (Dbl homology/DH domain, residues 376-560, GO:0005085 guanyl-nucleotide exchange factor activity), IPR001849 (Pleckstrin homology/PH domain, residues 592-706), IPR001452 (SH3 domain, residues 715-776, GO:0005515 protein binding), plus superfamily classifications IPR035899 (DH domain superfamily, residues 355-578), IPR011993 (PH-like domain superfamily, residues 585-700), and IPR036028 (SH3-like domain superfamily, residues 716-778). The DH-PH tandem is the catalytic module for Rho GTPase activation, while the C-terminal SH3 domain mediates protein-protein interactions. AlphaFold structural prediction yields medium confidence (global pLDDT: 64.7), indicating a mixed architecture with ordered catalytic domains and potentially disordered N-terminal regulatory regions (residues 1-354). STRING interaction analysis identifies 10 protein partners with high-to-medium confidence: DAAM1 (dishevelled-associated activator of morphogenesis 1, score 0.875), RHOA (0.869), ARHGEF35 (0.770), CDC42 (0.694), DVL1 (0.654), RAC1 (0.596), ALS2CL (0.562), KIAA1109 (0.541), ARHGEF38 (0.541), and PLEKHG4B (0.530). The strong interactions with RHOA, CDC42, and RAC1 confirm its role as a multi-specific Rho GEF, while DAAM1 and DVL1 connections suggest involvement in WNT signaling and cytoskeletal remodeling. Human Protein Atlas lacks expression data, confirming dark gene status. ClinVar contains 15 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number losses, gains, and deletions on chromosome 1p36, though no specific disease phenotypes are definitively established. Literature reveals ARHGEF19 promotes tumor progression in breast cancer and small cell lung cancer through BRAF interaction and MAPK/ERK pathway activation. Based on domain architecture (DH-PH catalytic module + SH3 interaction domain), interaction partners (RHOA/CDC42/RAC1 GTPases + DAAM1 formin), and cancer literature, ARHGEF19 likely functions as a cytoskeletal regulator that coordinates Rho GTPase activation with MAPK signaling, potentially linking cell morphology changes to proliferative signals in developing tissues and cancers. The SH3 domain may recruit ARHGEF19 to specific subcellular locations or scaffold complexes containing BRAF, explaining its oncogenic activity.
