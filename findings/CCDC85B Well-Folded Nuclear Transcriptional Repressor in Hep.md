## Description
CCDC85B (alias DIPA) encodes a 202-amino acid coiled-coil protein (UniProt Q15834) localized to the nucleus. The protein belongs to the CCDC85 family (InterPro IPR019359, PF10226, PTHR13546) with conserved regions spanning residues 5-196. AlphaFold predicts a well-folded structure (pLDDT 80.6, high confidence), indicating stable tertiary structure unlike its paralog CCDC85C (pLDDT 68.1).

FUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: CCDC85B functions as a nuclear transcriptional repressor that restricts hepatitis delta virus (HDV) replication through direct interaction with viral hepatitis delta antigen (HDAg). The protein shares homology with HDAg and acts as a dominant-negative inhibitor by sequestering viral replication machinery. Beyond antiviral defense, CCDC85B regulates cellular differentiation and growth through transcriptional repression activity, potentially modulating chromatin accessibility at developmentally regulated promoters. The high brain expression (367.2 nTPM, 9.4× higher than CCDC85C at 36.7 nTPM) suggests specialized roles in neuronal differentiation or synaptic plasticity.

STRUCTURAL INSIGHT: The well-folded AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 80.6) contrasts sharply with the partially structured CCDC85C paralog, suggesting divergent functional mechanisms despite shared domain architecture. CCDC85B likely forms stable coiled-coil homodimers or heterodimers, enabling precise protein-protein recognition rather than the disordered scaffolding mechanism of CCDC85C. This structural stability supports specific molecular recognition of HDAg and cellular transcription factors.

EXPRESSION & DISEASE: HPA classifies CCDC85B as a repressor involved in differentiation, growth regulation, host-virus interaction, and transcription regulation. Tissue-enhanced expression with brain enrichment (367.2 nTPM) indicates neuronal specialization. ClinVar documents 11 pathogenic variants including chromosome 11q CNVs (11q12.1-13.3, 11q12.2-13.5 gains), focal deletions (11p13-q25, 11q13.1-13.2), and duplications, suggesting dosage sensitivity in neurodevelopmental or metabolic disorders.

CONSERVATION: Strong orthology with mouse (Q6PDY0, 202 aa, 100% length conservation) indicates purifying selection across mammals. The CCDC85 family architecture is vertebrate-conserved, supporting essential regulatory functions.
