## Description
PLPPR4 (Phospholipid Phosphatase Related 4, also known as PRG-1) is a neuron-specific membrane protein with only 19 publications, recently characterized (2020-2025) as a critical regulator of synaptic plasticity whose haploinsufficiency causes neurodevelopmental disorders including intellectual disability through mTOR signaling disruption. This 763-amino acid postsynaptic density protein (UniProt Q7Z2D5) contains a PAP2-type phosphatidic acid phosphatase domain (IPR000326/PF01569, positions 174-323; cd03384, positions 174-323) within the phosphatidic acid phosphatase type 2/haloperoxidase superfamily (G3DSA:1.20.144.10). AlphaFold predicts medium-confidence structure (pLDDT 55.9), indicating a structured catalytic core with flexible regulatory regions typical of membrane-associated lipid enzymes.\n\nSTRING interactions reveal PLPPR4 operates within lipid signaling and neuronal development networks: EPHX2 (0.751, epoxide hydrolase 2 involved in lipid mediator metabolism), GNAS (0.570, G-protein alpha subunit coupling receptors to cAMP), MCF2L2 (0.502, Rho guanine nucleotide exchange factor), LPAR2 (0.448, lysophosphatidic acid receptor 2), plus COQ2 (0.457, coenzyme Q biosynthesis), STX16 (0.428, syntaxin-16 vesicle trafficking), and CALM3 (0.415, calmodulin-3 calcium signaling). This positions PLPPR4 at the intersection of lipid phosphate metabolism, GPCR signaling, and synaptic vesicle dynamics.\n\nHPA shows brain-enriched expression (50.1 nTPM) with tissue-enhanced specificity and cancer-enhanced expression patterns. Subcellular localization to postsynaptic density of glutamatergic synapses confirms neuronal function. ClinVar contains 10 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number losses and gains on chromosome 1p21-22, though specific disease associations remain under investigation. Conservation across mouse (766 aa) and rat (766 aa) indicates functional constraint.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: PLPPR4 is a postsynaptic lipid phosphatase that dephosphorylates lysophosphatidic acid (LPA) and related phospholipids to regulate excitatory synapse formation and plasticity. The PAP2 catalytic domain suggests enzymatic conversion of LPA to monoacylglycerol, terminating LPA receptor (LPAR2) signaling that controls dendritic spine morphology. Recent evidence linking PLPPR4 haploinsufficiency to mTOR pathway dysregulation suggests it may coordinate lipid signaling with protein synthesis machinery at synapses. The EPHX2 interaction indicates cross-talk with epoxyeicosatrienoic acid (EET) metabolism, potentially linking PLPPR4 to neuroinflammatory responses. Pathogenic CNVs disrupting PLPPR4 dosage may cause synaptic hyperexcitability and impaired learning/memory through constitutive LPA-mTOR activation, contributing to intellectual disability and autism spectrum disorders.
