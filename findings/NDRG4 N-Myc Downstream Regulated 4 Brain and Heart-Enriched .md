## Description
NDRG4 encodes a 352 aa cytoplasmic protein (UniProt Q9ULP0), also known as BDM1 or SMAP-8, that functions as a critical regulator of cell cycle progression and survival in neural and cardiac tissues. The protein features a conserved alpha/beta-hydrolase fold architecture (G3DSA:3.40.50.1820, IPR029058, residues 6-285) characteristic of the NDRG family (IPR004142, PF03096, residues 7-346), though it lacks catalytic activity and instead serves as a structural scaffold for protein-protein interactions. STRING interactions place NDRG4 in networks linking cytoskeletal organization, oncogenic signaling, and epigenetic regulation: SEPTIN9 (0.697), a GTPase involved in cytokinesis and cancer; MYCN (0.600), the proto-oncogene that transcriptionally represses NDRG4; BVES (0.585), a tight junction protein regulating Wnt signaling; SDC2 (0.543), a syndecan used alongside NDRG4 as a dual methylation biomarker for colorectal cancer; RNF207 (0.507), an E3 ubiquitin ligase; TFPI2 (0.476), a protease inhibitor; BMP3 (0.462) and SFRP2 (0.447), TGF-beta/Wnt pathway modulators; and KRAS (0.437), a key oncogene. HPA confirms extreme tissue enrichment in brain (347.4 nTPM) and heart muscle (326.5 nTPM), with subcellular localization to the plasma membrane and cytosol, annotated as a predicted intracellular/membrane protein—consistent with roles in neuronal survival and cardiac function. ClinVar lists 23 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including extensive copy number losses/gains spanning chromosome 16q11.2-q24.3, deletions, duplications, and splice site mutations (c.177+5G>T), demonstrating dosage sensitivity and linking NDRG4 disruption to developmental disorders and cancer susceptibility. AlphaFold structure prediction yields high confidence (global pLDDT 84.5), revealing a well-folded compact architecture typical of the NDRG family scaffold. Mechanistically, NDRG4 functions through multiple integrated modes: (1) As a Myc-repressed target, NDRG4 expression is silenced in proliferating cells but induced upon differentiation, acting as a brake on cell cycle progression; (2) In colorectal cancer, promoter hypermethylation of NDRG4 (often co-methylated with SDC2) serves as a highly specific diagnostic biomarker, suggesting its loss drives tumorigenesis; (3) Recent studies (2026) reveal NDRG4 overexpression protects against apoptosis after intracerebral hemorrhage via activation of the PI3K/Akt/GSK3β signaling pathway, indicating neuroprotective functions; (4) Interaction with SEPTIN9 suggests roles in cytoskeletal dynamics during cell division and neuronal morphogenesis; (5) Heart-specific expression implies potential roles in cardiomyocyte survival and response to stress. Disruption of NDRG4 would cause uncontrolled cell proliferation, increased apoptosis susceptibility in neural/cardiac tissues, and elevated cancer risk—phenotypes consistent with the 16q deletion syndromes and colorectal cancer observed in patients.
