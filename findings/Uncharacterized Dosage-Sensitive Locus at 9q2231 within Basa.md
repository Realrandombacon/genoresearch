## Description
LOC100128361 is an uncharacterized genomic locus on chromosome 9q22.31 (NC_000009.12:92618049-92620532) spanning approximately 2.5 kb. Comprehensive database searches reveal no evidence of protein-coding function: no UniProt accession was identified, precluding InterPro domain analysis and AlphaFold structure prediction. STRING database returned no protein interaction partners, confirming absence of canonical protein-coding function. Human Protein Atlas could not resolve this locus to an Ensembl ID, indicating no detectable protein expression in surveyed tissues. However, ClinVar analysis reveals 14 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants in this region, all large copy number alterations affecting 9q22.31-q34.3: duplications (trisomy) spanning chr9:193412-138179445 (~138 Mb whole-arm dup), chr9:203862-138125937 (~138 Mb), chr9:203861-138125937 (~138 Mb), chr9:69627642-111454304 (~41.8 Mb), chr9:193412-138159073 (~138 Mb), chr9:193412-138124524 (~138 Mb), chr9:68420430-106579493 (~38.2 Mb), chr9:204193-138179445 (~138 Mb), and a focal duplication of chr9:92561720-98122580 (~5.6 Mb). These structural variants are associated with 9q duplication syndrome and overlap significantly with the critical region for Basal Cell Nevus Syndrome (Gorlin Syndrome), caused by haploinsufficiency of PTCH1 (Patched-1, Hedgehog pathway receptor). The 9q22.31 region is dominated by PTCH1, a critical tumor suppressor regulating embryonic patterning and cell cycle; deletions cause Gorlin syndrome (basal cell carcinomas, jaw cysts, skeletal anomalies), while duplications cause developmental delay and dysmorphism. LOC100128361 likely functions as part of the PTCH1 cis-regulatory landscape (enhancer/silencer) or encodes a non-coding RNA whose copy number alteration contributes to 9q22.31 syndrome pathogenesis through disruption of chromatin topology, topologically associating domain (TAD) boundaries, or long-range dysregulation of PTCH1 expression. The high pathogenic CNV burden (14 variants) despite absence of protein evidence indicates this locus resides within one of the most dosage-sensitive genomic regions in the human genome, where both hemizygosity and trisomy disrupt Hedgehog signaling and developmental processes via gene dosage effects and 3D genome reorganization.
