## Description
CAMTA2 (Calmodulin-Binding Transcription Activator 2; UniProt O94983) is a large 1202 amino acid transcriptional regulator with only 43 publications, classifying it as a genuine dark gene in human biology despite essential roles in cardiac development, calcium-responsive transcription, and chromatin remodeling.\n\nSTRUCTURAL ARCHITECTURE: InterPro analysis reveals a complex multi-domain architecture specialized for calcium-regulated DNA binding. The N-terminal region contains a CG-1 DNA-binding domain (IPR005559/PF03859/PS51437, residues 30-155/39-149) that recognizes specific promoter sequences with GO annotation for DNA binding (GO:0003677). An IPT/TIG domain (IPR002909/PF01833, residues 534-614) adopts an immunoglobulin-like fold (IPR013783/IPR014756/G3DSA:1.25.40.20, residues 529-618) for protein-protein interactions. A central ankyrin repeat region (IPR036770/G3DSA:2.60.40.10, residues 699-813) mediates scaffold assembly with partner proteins. The C-terminal region contains an IQ motif (PS50096, residues 1101-1127) that binds calmodulin in a calcium-dependent manner, plus additional immunoglobulin-like domains (G3DSA:1.20.5.190, residues 1042-1087/1099-1142). The protein belongs to the calmodulin-binding transcription activator family (PTHR23335, residues 33-1185). Biological processes include transcription and transcription regulation. Molecular function is annotated as transcriptional activator. Classified as a predicted intracellular protein with cell type-enhanced single cell specificity.\n\nDISEASE LANDSCAPE: ClinVar contains 26 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number losses at 17p13.3-13.2, 17p13.2, and 17p13.3-11.2 encompassing CAMTA2, copy number gains at 17p13.2-12 and 17p13.3-12, plus the coding missense variant c.2287G>A (p.Ala763Thr) within the ankyrin repeat region. No monogenic disease is yet definitively assigned, but 17p13 CNVs cause developmental anomalies including Miller-Dieker syndrome features. The NKX2-5 interaction suggests CAMTA2 dysfunction may cause congenital heart defects through disrupted cardiac transcriptional networks. The HDAC5 partnership implies roles in cardiac hypertrophy pathways where HDAC5-MEF2 signaling is critical. Plant literature (wheat grain weight via GCN5 histone acetyltransferase interaction, Arabidopsis aluminum resistance via CML24-WRKY46-ALMT1 pathway) reveals conserved mechanisms of calmodulin-targeted transcriptional control that likely operate in human tissues.\n\nCONSERVATION: Orthologs confirmed in mouse (Q80Y50, 1208 aa) and Arabidopsis thaliana (Q6NPP4, 1050 aa), indicating strong evolutionary constraint on the CG-1-IPT-Ankyrin-IQ modular architecture across kingdoms.\n\nFUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: CAMTA2 functions as a calcium/calmodulin-responsive transcription factor that integrates intracellular calcium signals into gene expression programs. In resting cells, CAMTA2 resides in the nucleus bound to target promoters via its CG-1 DNA-binding domain. Upon calcium influx, calmodulin binds the C-terminal IQ motif, inducing conformational changes transmitted through the ankyrin repeat scaffold to modulate transcriptional activity. The NKX2-5 interaction positions CAMTA2 as a co-activator for cardiac-specific genes (NPPA, ion channels, contractile proteins), explaining potential congenital heart disease phenotypes. HDAC5 recruitment suggests CAMTA2 can both activate and repress targets depending on calcium status—potentially switching between growth-promoting and differentiation programs. The PSME3/PSME3IP1 interactions link CAMTA2 to proteasomal degradation, possibly regulating its own turnover or that of partner transcription factors. In cancer contexts, CAMTA2 dysregulation may alter calcium-responsive proliferation genes. The 26 ClinVar variants warrant investigation for cardiac conduction defects, cardiomyopathy, and neurodevelopmental disorders involving calcium signaling defects.
