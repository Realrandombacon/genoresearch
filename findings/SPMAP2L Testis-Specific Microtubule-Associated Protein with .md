## Description
SPMAP2L (Sperm Microtubule Associated Protein 2-Like, alias THEGL) is a 465-amino acid testis-specific protein that functions as a structural component of the sperm flagellar principal piece, where it stabilizes microtubule doublets essential for sperm motility. With zero publications in the literature, this gene represents a genuine 'dark gene' despite having clinical variant data. The protein contains multiple tandem Testicular Haploid Expressed Gene (THEG) repeats (IPR006623 at residues 173-192, 210-279, 303-352; PF14912 at 210-277, 303-350, 369-401; SM00705 at 173-192, 214-233, 260-279), which are conserved structural motifs found exclusively in haploid-expressed testicular proteins across metazoans. These repeats likely mediate microtubule binding through electrostatic interactions with tubulin, analogous to classical microtubule-associated proteins (MAPs) but with testis-specific adaptation.\n\nThe Human Protein Atlas confirms strict testis enrichment (3.3 nTPM) with no expression in somatic tissues or cancers, and subcellular localization to the principal piece of the sperm tail - the region containing the axonemal microtubule core. AlphaFold predicts a partially disordered structure (global pLDDT 63.4), consistent with intrinsically disordered microtubule-binding proteins that wrap around microtubule surfaces. The medium confidence score suggests flexible linker regions between structured repeat domains, enabling conformational adaptation during flagellar bending.\n\nClinVar reports 16 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number variations (deletions and duplications spanning 4q12), though no specific monogenic disease is yet assigned. Given the testis-specific expression and microtubule-associated function, SPMAP2L deficiency likely causes male infertility through asthenozoospermia (reduced sperm motility) rather than systemic disease. The absence of STRING interactions is consistent with structural proteins that function as static axonemal components rather than dynamic signaling hubs. Conservation across vertebrates (mouse Q9DA15, rat Q5PQR6) confirms evolutionary pressure to maintain flagellar structure. This gene exemplifies a 'dark' structural protein with clear mechanistic function but limited clinical recognition due to reproductive-specific phenotypes that evade standard disease ascertainment.
