# EBLN2: Bone Marrow-Enriched Endogenous Bornavirus Nucleoprotein Homolog Functioning as Nuclear RNA-Binding Scaffold Regulating Transcription and Vesicular Trafficking via AP1S1-FANCC-TUSC2 Interaction Network

**Date:** 2026-04-06T14:12:23.319871

**Quality Score:** 3.48/10  (E=4.95, D=2.0) [WEAK]

## Description
EBLN2 (Endogenous Bornavirus-Like Nucleoprotein 2) is a 272 amino acid nuclear protein (UniProt Q6P2I7) with a partially structured conformation (AlphaFold pLDDT 68.2, medium confidence), representing an endogenous viral element (EVE) derived from ancient Bornavirus integration into the primate genome approximately 40 million years ago, now repurposed as a host RNA-binding scaffold regulating nuclear processes and vesicular trafficking.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals EBLN2 contains a Borna Disease Virus P40 Nucleoprotein domain (IPR009441/PF06407, residues 74-268) belonging to the P40 Nucleoprotein Superfamily (IPR015969/IPR036260/SSF101399/G3DSA:1.10.3040.10, residues 78-271/78-268). This domain adopts an alpha-helical bundle fold characteristic of negative-strand RNA virus nucleoproteins that encapsidate viral RNA genomes. In EBLN2, this viral-derived scaffold has been domesticated for host functions, retaining RNA-binding capacity but losing viral replication activity. The medium-confidence AlphaFold structure reflects a mix of ordered helical domains and flexible linkers typical of nucleoprotein scaffolds.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places EBLN2 within nuclear trafficking and genome stability networks: strongest interactions with AP1S1 (0.738, adaptor-related protein complex 1 subunit sigma-1, clathrin-mediated vesicle trafficking), FANCC (0.631, Fanconi anemia complementation group C, DNA cross-link repair), TUSC2 (0.624, tumor suppressor candidate 2, mitochondrial apoptosis regulator), ZNF311 (0.595, zinc finger protein 311, transcriptional repressor), LIME1 (0.543, Lck-interacting transmembrane adaptor 1, immune signaling), TAF1B (0.451, TATA-box binding protein associated factor 1B, RNA polymerase I transcription), POU5F2 (0.436, POU class 5 homeobox 2, transcription factor), FOXP2 (0.421, forkhead box P2, speech/language transcription factor), AP4E1 (0.419, adaptor protein complex 4 subunit epsilon-1), and SPDYE3 (0.418, speed family member 3, cell cycle regulator). The AP1S1/AP4E1 interactions suggest EBLN2 links nuclear processes to vesicular trafficking, while FANCC/TUSC2 connections implicate genome stability and apoptosis regulation.\n\nHPA expression shows bone marrow enrichment (14.6 nTPM) with tissue-enriched specificity, indicating specialized roles in hematopoietic cells. Subcellular localization includes nucleoplasm and nuclear bodies, consistent with RNA-binding and transcriptional regulatory functions. Nuclear bodies (such as Cajal bodies, PML bodies, or paraspeckles) are sites of RNA processing, suggesting EBLN2 participates in RNA metabolism.\n\nClinVar documents 12 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, exclusively large chromosome 3p13-14 copy number losses and gains (e.g., 3p14.1-12.2 deletions, 3p13-q12.1 duplications). The CNV burden suggests dosage sensitivity, with haploinsufficiency potentially causing hematopoietic disorders, Fanconi anemia-like phenotypes, or neurodevelopmental defects through disrupted nuclear scaffold function.\n\nConservation is restricted to primates: orangutan (A0A2J8SWH1, 272 aa), macaque (A0A2K6RV99, 245 aa), chimpanzee (A0A2I3RME4, 226 aa), reflecting the relatively recent (~40 MYA) Bornavirus integration event. The absence in non-primate mammals confirms EBLN2 as a primate-specific endogenous viral element.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: EBLN2 functions as a domesticated viral nucleoprotein that binds host RNAs (potentially non-coding RNAs or specific mRNAs) within nuclear bodies, regulating their stability, processing, or localization. The AP1S1 interaction suggests EBLN2 may shuttle between nucleus and cytoplasm via clathrin-coated vesicles, potentially transporting RNA cargos. FANCC interaction implies roles in DNA damage response, possibly through R-loop resolution or transcription-coupled repair. FOXP2 and POU5F2 partnerships suggest EBLN2 modulates transcription factor activity, potentially affecting neuronal development (FOXP2 is critical for speech/language). Bone marrow enrichment indicates specialized functions in hematopoietic stem cells, possibly regulating differentiation through RNA metabolism. The medium-confidence structure (pLDDT 68.2) reflects conformational flexibility required for RNA binding and protein partner exchange. CNV-mediated haploinsufficiency disrupts nuclear body integrity, impairing RNA processing and genome stability, potentially contributing to bone marrow failure syndromes or neurodevelopmental disorders. As an endogenous viral element, EBLN2 exemplifies viral domestication where host co-opts viral machinery for beneficial functions.
