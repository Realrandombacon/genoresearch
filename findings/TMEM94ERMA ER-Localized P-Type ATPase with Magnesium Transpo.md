# TMEM94/ERMA: ER-Localized P-Type ATPase with Magnesium Transport Activity, Nuclear Body Localization, and Intellectual Disability Association

**Date:** 2026-03-20T11:20:24.639382

**Quality Score:** 4.2/10  (E=6.6, D=1.8) [MODERATE]

## Description
TMEM94 (Transmembrane Protein 94, also known as ERMA/Endoplasmic Reticulum Magnesium ATPase, UniProt Q12767) encodes a 1356-amino acid P-type ATPase superfamily member (IPR023298, residues 63-1334) with high structural confidence (AlphaFold pLDDT: 72.9). The protein contains multiple transmembrane helical bundles characteristic of calcium-transporting ATPases (G3DSA:1.20.1110.10, residues 1070-1335; SSF81665 at positions 63-134, 559-665, 1082-1334) and belongs to the TMEM94 family (IPR039720/PTHR13219, residues 10-1356). Despite its annotation as an ER magnesium transporter, HPA data reveals unexpected nucleoplasmic and nuclear body localization with ubiquitous expression across all tissues, suggesting dual ER/nuclear functions. STRING interaction network identifies 10 partners including FIBP (0.810, fibroblast growth factor-interacting protein), CDK5 (0.765, cyclin-dependent kinase 5), CASKIN2 (0.625, neuronal scaffold protein), ZMYM3 (0.540, zinc finger transcriptional regulator), and TMEM88 (0.528, Wnt signaling inhibitor) - the CDK5 and CASKIN2 interactions strongly suggest neuronal functions consistent with intellectual disability phenotypes. ClinVar contains 56 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including nonsense mutations (p.Arg466Ter, p.Lys732Ter, p.Arg1335Ter) and missense changes (p.Lys204Arg, p.Pro512Leu, p.Ile788Met, p.Asp1016Asn) plus copy number gains spanning 17q24.3-25.3, confirming disease relevance. Mouse (Q7TSH8, 1360 aa) and rat (F1LUG5, 1360 aa) orthologs show strong conservation. Mechanistically, TMEM94 likely functions through: (1) P-type ATPase catalytic cycle transporting Mg2+ from cytosol into ER lumen using phosphorylation-driven conformational changes, (2) nuclear body recruitment via FIBP/CDK5 interactions for cell cycle regulation or DNA damage response, (3) neuronal scaffolding through CASKIN2 binding at postsynaptic densities, and (4) Wnt pathway modulation via TMEM88 coordination. Truncating mutations cause haploinsufficiency leading to intellectual disability through disrupted Mg2+ homeostasis affecting protein folding, kinase activity, and chromatin regulation.
