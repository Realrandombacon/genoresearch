## Description
METTL25B (Methyltransferase-like protein 25B, also known as RRNAD1, C1orf66, CGI-41) is a 475 amino acid nuclear enzyme (UniProt Q96FB5) with very-high-confidence well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 91.3), characteristic of S-adenosyl-L-methionine (SAM)-dependent methyltransferases. The protein belongs to the METTL25 family (IPR052220/PTHR12496, residues 13-460) and contains a conserved methyltransferase domain (IPR025714/PF13679, residues 133-291) within the SAM-dependent methyltransferase superfamily (IPR029063/SSF53335, residues 139-195). Critically, METTL25B harbors the ribosomal RNA adenine dimethylase signature (IPR020596/PS01131, residues 157-185), indicating enzymatic activity toward rRNA substrates. Molecular functions include rRNA (adenine-N6,N6-)-dimethyltransferase activity (GO:0000179) and rRNA modification (GO:0000154), placing METTL25B within the ribosome biogenesis and RNA processing pathways.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals a network exclusively composed of methyltransferases and translation factors: strongest interactions with METTL17 (0.594, Methyltransferase-like protein 17, 18S rRNA methylation), CSKMT (0.489, Calmodulin lysine methyltransferase), METTL11B (0.484, Methyltransferase-like protein 11B), METTL26 (0.476, Methyltransferase-like protein 26), SETD4 (0.469, SET domain-containing protein 4, histone methyltransferase), KRBA1 (0.467, Kringle domain-containing protein), METTL27 (0.461), EEF1AKNMT (0.440, Elongation factor 1-alpha lysine methyltransferase), METTL9 (0.430), and EEF1AKMT3 (0.410). This profile confirms METTL25B functions within the methyltransferase ecosystem, specifically targeting ribosomal RNA or translation-associated proteins. The METTL17 interaction is particularly informative, as METTL17 is a known 18S rRNA methyltransferase, suggesting METTL25B may modify complementary rRNA sites or function in the same ribosome assembly pathway.\n\nHPA expression reveals ubiquitous detection across all tissues with low tissue specificity, consistent with a housekeeping role in ribosome biogenesis. Subcellular localization is nucleolar and cytosolic, with nucleolar enrichment expected for rRNA-modifying enzymes. The nucleolar localization directly supports the predicted rRNA methylation function, as this is the site of ribosome assembly. Protein class includes predicted intracellular and membrane proteins.\n\nClinVar documents 15 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, exclusively large copy number gains and losses affecting chromosome 1q (1q21.1-44 duplications, 1q22-23.2 gains, 1q21.3-23.1 deletions, 1q12-23.1 amplifications). The variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity. While no specific Mendelian disease is annotated, 1q CNVs are associated with developmental delay, microcephaly, intellectual disability, and growth abnormalities. METTL25B disruption likely causes ribosome biogenesis defects (ribosomopathy), leading to impaired protein synthesis, nucleolar stress, and p53 activation. This mechanism parallels known ribosomopathies like Diamond-Blackfan anemia and Treacher Collins syndrome.\n\nConservation is strong across vertebrates: human (Q96FB5, 475 aa), mouse (Q8BZG5, 475 aa), rat (Q6AYG0, 475 aa), cow (Q5E9V4, 475 aa), fish (A0A667ZLT7, 586 aa). The length variation in fish reflects lineage-specific extensions while maintaining the catalytic core.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: METTL25B functions as a nucleolar rRNA N6,N6-dimethyltransferase that modifies specific adenine residues in 18S or 28S rRNA during ribosome assembly. The enzyme likely recognizes a conserved rRNA sequence motif via its methyltransferase domain, using SAM as the methyl donor. Interaction with METTL17 suggests coordinated modification of adjacent rRNA sites, potentially creating a methylation 'signature' required for ribosome maturation or translational fidelity. Loss of METTL25B causes hypomethylation of target rRNA sites, impairing ribosome subunit assembly, reducing translational efficiency, and triggering nucleolar stress responses. This activates p53-dependent cell cycle arrest or apoptosis, particularly in rapidly dividing tissues, explaining the developmental phenotypes associated with 1q CNVs. METTL25B represents a candidate ribosomopathy gene and a potential biomarker for 1q duplication/deletion syndromes.
