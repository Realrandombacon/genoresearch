## Description
LOC129390485 is a 200bp cis-regulatory element (CRE) located on chromosome 12q15 (GRCh38: 70008694-70008894), functionally validated as a transcriptional silencer through STARR-seq massively parallel reporter assays (MPRAs) in K562 erythroleukemia cells. This locus lacks canonical promoter, enhancer, or CTCF-bound insulator chromatin signatures but demonstrates robust repressive activity against the super core promoter SCP1. As a non-coding regulatory sequence, it has no UniProt accession, protein domains, STRING interaction partners, or Human Protein Atlas expression data (query failed to resolve Ensembl ID).\n\nClinVar analysis reveals 4 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variants disrupting this region. All variants are large-scale structural alterations: (1) a massive whole-arm duplication (12p13.33-q24.33, ~133Mb), (2) a large deletion spanning 12q14.3-15 (~5.6Mb), and two overlapping deletions in the 12q15-21.2 region (~7.2Mb and ~7.4Mb). The exclusive presence of deletions (and one massive duplication) suggests haploinsufficiency intolerance where silencer loss causes pathological derepression of target genes. These CNVs are associated with 12q15 deletion syndrome phenotypes, including developmental delay, intellectual disability, and growth abnormalities. The 12q15 region is gene-dense, containing critical dosage-sensitive genes such as HMGA2 (High Mobility Group AT-hook 2, growth regulation) and CDK4 (Cyclin Dependent Kinase 4, cell cycle control).\n\nHYPOTHESIS: LOC129390485 functions as a critical distal silencer within the 12q15 topologically associating domain (TAD), specifically regulating the expression of HMGA2 or CDK4 during development. The silencer likely recruits co-repressor complexes (e.g., PRC2, NCoR/SMRT) to deposit repressive histone marks (H3K27me3) at target promoters. The exclusive deletion burden (4:0 ratio for focal events) indicates that silencer loss leads to catastrophic overexpression of HMGA2 (driving tissue overgrowth or tumorigenesis) or CDK4 (causing cell cycle dysregulation), contributing to the severe phenotypes of 12q deletion syndrome. Conversely, the massive 12p-q duplication may be tolerated due to buffering mechanisms or may cause mild underexpression. The K562 validation suggests potential roles in hematopoietic lineage specification, possibly silencing oncogenes in erythroid progenitors. The location near HMGA2, a known architectural transcription factor, suggests LOC129390485 may fine-tune HMGA2 levels to prevent aberrant chromatin remodeling.
