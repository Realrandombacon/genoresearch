# CLEC5A: Myeloid C-Type Lectin Receptor MDL-1 Orchestrating SYK-DAP12 Signaling for Innate Immune Activation in Sepsis-Induced Vascular Leakage, Atherosclerosis, and Osteosarcoma Metastasis Suppression

**Date:** 2026-06-17T23:54:10.069699

**Quality Score:** 9/10 (EXCELLENT)

**Confidence:** MEDIUM

## Description
CLEC5A (C-type Lectin Domain Family 5 Member A, MDL-1, Q9NY25) encodes a 188 aa type II transmembrane receptor functioning as a critical pattern recognition receptor (PRR) on myeloid cells that detects viral envelope proteins and damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs) to trigger pro-inflammatory cytokine storms via SYK-DAP12 signaling. Structural architecture reveals a single C-type lectin-like domain (CTLD) (IPR001304/IPR033992/PF00059/PS50041/SM00034/cd03593, residues 71-185/78-184/89-185/71-184/71-185) forming a characteristic beta-sandwich fold with conserved calcium-binding site (G3DSA:3.10.100.10/SSF56436/IPR016186/IPR016187, residues 70-187/54-187) essential for ligand recognition; AlphaFold prediction (pLDDT 85.2) confirms high-confidence well-folded CTLD structure consistent with stable receptor-ligand interactions. STRING interactions define innate immune signaling networks: spleen tyrosine kinase SYK (0.854) mediating downstream phosphorylation cascades, hematopoietic cell signal transducer HCST/DAP10 (0.839) and FCER1G/FcRγ (0.650) serving as adaptor subunits for surface expression and ITAM signaling, maltase-glucoamylase MGAM (0.740), tetraspanin TSPAN6 (0.677) organizing membrane microdomains, CLEC1B/CLEC-2 (0.675) related receptor, Fc gamma receptor FCGR3A/CD16a (0.613), G-protein coupled receptor GPR84 (0.609), triggering receptor TREM1 (0.461), and TYROBP/DAP12 (0.419) key ITAM-bearing adaptor. HPA localizes CLEC5A to plasma membrane/cytosol as immune cell-enriched protein with tissue-enriched expression in bone marrow (39.0 nTPM) and involvement in host-virus interaction/innate immunity. ClinVar documents 49 pathogenic variants including extensive 7q33-36.3 copy number losses (chr7:137521595-159119707, chr7:136304444-148292957, chr7:122190535-149944340 deletions) and gains (chr7:137456457-159119707 duplications). Literature (211 papers) demonstrates critical disease mechanisms: endothelial CLEC5A drives barrier dysfunction and vascular leakage responsible for lung injury in bacterial pneumonia and sepsis (10x citations, 2025), epigenetic upregulation contributes to monocyte/macrophage dysfunction in coronary artery disease (2x citations, 2025), causal inference in atherosclerosis via Mendelian randomization (1x citation, 2025), suppression of osteosarcoma cell growth and metastasis via interference with calcineurin/NFATc1 signaling (1x citation, 2025), dengue virus NS1 protein binding triggers cytokine storm, CLEC5A blockade protects against lethal influenza and enterovirus 71 infection. Mechanistic hypothesis: CLEC5A forms homodimers or heterodimers with related CLEC receptors on myeloid cell surfaces; unlike classical C-type lectins, CTLD binds ligands in calcium-independent manner recognizing specific glycan structures on viral envelopes (Dengue NS1, Influenza HA) or endogenous DAMPs released during tissue injury; upon ligand binding, CLEC5A associates non-covalently with ITAM-bearing adaptors DAP12 (TYROBP) and/or DAP10 (HCST) via charged residue in transmembrane domain; this recruits SYK kinase initiating phosphorylation cascade activating PLCγ, NFAT, NF-κB, and MAPK pathways leading to massive IL-1β, IL-6, TNF-α production; in sepsis, excessive CLEC5A activation on endothelial cells and macrophages causes vascular hyperpermeability and organ failure; in atherosclerosis, epigenetic dysregulation enhances CLEC5A expression promoting plaque inflammation; in osteosarcoma, CLEC5A acts as tumor suppressor by inhibiting calcineurin/NFATc1 axis reducing metastatic potential; 7q CNVs alter CLEC5A dosage affecting immune surveillance.
