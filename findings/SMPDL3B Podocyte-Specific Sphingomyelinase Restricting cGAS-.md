## Description
SMPDL3B (Sphingomyelin Phosphodiesterase Acid-Like 3B) is a partially characterized sphingolipid-metabolizing enzyme with 92 publications, recently emerged (2025) as a critical dual-function regulator of antiviral innate immunity and podocyte homeostasis. This 455-amino acid secreted/membrane-associated protein (UniProt Q92485) contains a catalytic calcineurin-like phosphoesterase domain (IPR004843/PF00149, positions 22-280) with metallophosphatase activity (cd00842, positions 23-323), and a C-terminal sphingomyelin phosphodiesterase domain (IPR045473/PF19272, positions 293-430) characteristic of acid sphingomyelinase-like enzymes. AlphaFold predicts very high-confidence structure (pLDDT 92.7), indicating a well-folded stable enzyme typical of secreted hydrolases requiring precise active site geometry.\n\nSTRING interactions reveal SMPDL3B operates within sphingolipid metabolism and podocyte-specific networks: CERK (0.639, ceramide kinase), SMPD2 (0.573, neutral sphingomyelinase 2), NPHS2/podocin (0.572, podocyte slit diaphragm protein), XKR8 (0.513, scramblase involved in apoptosis), SPTLC1 (0.511, serine palmitoyltransferase), SYNPO (0.507, synaptopodin - podocyte actin regulator), NPHS1/nephrin (0.476, slit diaphragm component), CERS2 (0.474, ceramide synthase), UGCG (0.462, glucosylceramide synthase), and SMPD4 (0.452, sphingomyelinase 4). This interaction profile positions SMPDL3B at the nexus of sphingolipid remodeling and podocyte structural integrity.\n\nHPA shows tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in pancreas (46.6 nTPM) and intestine (22.6 nTPM), with group-enriched expression in blood cells. Subcellular localization includes Golgi apparatus and cytosol, consistent with secretory pathway processing. Biological processes span immunity, inflammatory response, innate immunity, and lipid metabolism/degradation. ClinVar contains 5 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all large copy number gains on chromosome 1p36.33-35.1, though specific monogenic diseases remain uncharacterized. Conservation across mouse (456 aa) indicates strong functional constraint.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: SMPDL3B functions as a context-dependent sphingolipid rheostat that couples membrane lipid composition to both antiviral defense and glomerular filtration. Recent breakthrough studies reveal SMPDL3B restricts cGAS-STING signaling by directly degrading cGAMP (the second messenger produced by cGAS upon cytosolic DNA sensing), thereby preventing excessive interferon production during viral infection. This positions SMPDL3B as a negative feedback regulator preventing immunopathology. In podocytes, SMPDL3B maintains actin cytoskeleton stability by inhibiting store-operated Ca2+ entry (SOCE) via choline production from sphingomyelin hydrolysis. Loss of SMPDL3B causes minimal change disease-like podocytopathy due to SOCE hyperactivation and cytoskeletal collapse. The NPHS1/NPHS2/SYNPO interactions confirm SMPDL3B integration into the slit diaphragm complex. In myalgic encephalomyelitis, circulating SMPDL3B levels define metabolic endophenotypes with subclinical kidney alterations, suggesting systemic sphingolipid dysregulation. Pathogenic CNVs may cause either immunodeficiency (via excessive cGAMP accumulation and chronic interferonopathy) or proteinuric kidney disease (via podocyte cytoskeletal destabilization).
