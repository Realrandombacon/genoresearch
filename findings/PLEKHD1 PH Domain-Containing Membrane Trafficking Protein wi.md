## Description
PLEKHD1 (Pleckstrin homology domain-containing family D member 1, UniProt A6NEE1) is a 506-amino acid intracellular protein located on chromosome 14q23.1. InterPro analysis reveals canonical pleckstrin homology (PH) domain architecture: single PH domain (IPR001849/PF00169/SM00233, aa 28-138/32-134/29-138) forming a β-sandwich fold with C-terminal α-helix (G3DSA:2.30.29.30/SSF50729/IPR011993, aa 20-144/22-134) characteristic of phosphoinositide-binding modules that target proteins to specific membrane compartments. The protein belongs to the PH domain-containing GEF family (PTHR14383, aa 26-462), suggesting potential guanine nucleotide exchange factor activity or regulation of small GTPases. GO annotations indicate roles in membrane targeting and signal transduction. AlphaFold structure prediction achieves high confidence (pLDDT 81.6), indicating a well-folded stable protein with defined PH domain structure optimal for lipid binding and membrane recruitment. Human Protein Atlas shows tissue-enhanced expression (detected in some tissues) with highest expression in brain (9.5 nTPM), followed by retina (8.1 nTPM) and parathyroid gland (5.6 nTPM), cancer-enriched pattern, and immune cell-enriched specificity — consistent with roles in neuronal membrane trafficking, synaptic vesicle dynamics, and potential oncogenic functions. This interactome suggests PLEKHD1 functions within membrane trafficking, ion transport, and cytoskeletal organization networks. ClinVar contains 9 pathogenic variants consisting of large copy-number gains and losses across 14q11.2-q32.33, demonstrating dosage sensitivity where genomic rearrangements disrupting chromosome 14q cause disease phenotypes potentially affecting neurodevelopment and cellular metabolism. Conservation across vertebrates and invertebrates (mouse B2RPU2, 505 aa; rat B1WBU8, 505 aa; C. elegans O45420, 570 aa) indicates ancient evolutionary origin tied to fundamental membrane trafficking mechanisms. PLEKHD1 likely functions as a phosphoinositide-binding adaptor protein, using its PH domain to localize to specific membrane compartments (plasma membrane, endosomes, or Golgi) where it recruits or regulates partner proteins including zinc transporters and trafficking machinery — mechanisms critical for neuronal signaling, synaptic transmission, and cellular zinc homeostasis, frequently dysregulated in cancers and neurological disorders.
