## Description
GALNTL5 (Polypeptide N-acetylgalactosaminyltransferase-like protein 5) is a 443 aa testis-specific protein representing a partially characterized dark gene with only 19 literature references, functioning as a critical regulator of sperm motility and fertilization despite lacking catalytic glycosyltransferase activity. Multi-omics data reveals GALNTL5 as a 'molecular mimic' that has evolved from an enzymatic ancestor into a structural scaffold essential for male fertility. InterPro analysis confirms its evolutionary heritage: it retains the canonical pp-GalNAc-T domain architecture including the catalytic domain fold (IPR001173/PF00535, aa 137-317), the nucleotide-diphospho-sugar transferase superfamily fold (IPR029044/G3DSA:3.90.550.10/SSF53448, aa 65-442), and the N-acetylgalactosaminyltransferase domain (IPR045885/cd02510, aa 137-433). However, key catalytic residues are mutated, rendering it enzymatically inactive ('Inactive polypeptide N-acetylgalactosaminyltransferase-like'). AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (pLDDT 85.4), indicating a stable, well-folded protein that likely uses its preserved GT-A fold surface for specific protein-protein interactions rather than sugar transfer. HPA shows strict tissue-enriched expression exclusively in the testis (10.5 nTPM), with biological processes linked to spermatogenesis and differentiation, and no expression in cancers or immune cells. STRING interactions reveal a specialized network of glycosylation-related proteins and testis-specific factors: ST6GALNAC1 (0.913, sialyltransferase), B3GNT6 (0.901, beta-1,3-N-acetylglucosaminyltransferase), C1GALT1/C1GALT1C1 (0.548/0.506, T-synthase complex), MUC13 (0.613, mucin), and crucially SPATA16 (0.564, spermatogenesis-associated protein 16), linking GALNTL5 to the machinery of sperm maturation. ClinVar lists 79 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number variations on chromosome 7q (losses: chr7:137521595-159119707x1, chr7:142941022-156102711x1; gains: chr7:142491993-159119707x3, chr7:141690279-159119707x3) and duplications, implicating GALNTL5 dosage sensitivity in reproductive disorders. The 19 literature references document its unique biology: (1) A 2025 study demonstrating GALNTL5 binds GalNAc and is strictly required for sperm migration through the uterotubal junction (UTJ) and binding to the zona pellucida; (2) A 2024 mouse study showing GALNTL5 localizes to the endoplasmic reticulum (ER) in spermatids and impairs ER function by directly interacting with ER chaperone proteins, suggesting a role in quality control or stress response during sperm development; (3) A 2020 human study identifying GALNTL5 mutations in patients with asthenozoospermia (reduced sperm motility). Unlike active GALNT enzymes that build mucin-type O-glycans, GALNTL5 acts as a regulatory decoy or scaffold.
