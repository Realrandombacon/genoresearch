## Description
PACSIN3 (Protein Kinase C and Casein Kinase Substrate in Neurons 3; UniProt Q9UKS6) is a 424 amino acid F-BAR domain-containing membrane curvature sensor with 28 publications, representing a partially characterized endocytic regulator with newly defined roles in skeletal muscle integrity, cancer chemoresistance, and growth factor signaling through ADAM metalloprotease interactions.\n\nSTRUCTURAL ARCHITECTURE: InterPro analysis reveals modular architecture optimized for membrane binding and protein-protein interactions. The N-terminal region contains an F-BAR domain (IPR031160/PF00611/PS51741, residues 10-280/22-96) belonging to the Fes/CIP4 homology domain family that senses and induces membrane curvature for vesicle formation. An FCH domain (IPR001060, residues 14-102) mediates interactions with dynamin and other endocytic machinery. The C-terminal region contains an SH3 domain (IPR001452/PF14604/PS50002, residues 363-424/370-420) that binds proline-rich motifs in partner proteins. The full-length protein belongs to the F-BAR domain-containing protein family (IPR027267/PTHR23065, residues 1-302/18-342). GO annotations include protein binding (GO:0005515) and endocytosis. AlphaFold predicts high confidence structure (global pLDDT 86.1), indicating a well-folded elongated crescent-shaped F-BAR domain typical of membrane curvature sensors—consistent with PACSIN3's role in clathrin-mediated endocytosis and vesicle scission.\n\nINTERACTION NETWORK: STRING-DB identifies high-confidence interactions with endocytic machinery, cytoskeletal regulators, and ion channels: PACSIN1 (0.945, closest paralog, neuronal F-BAR protein), COBLL1 (0.858, Cobl-like protein 1, actin nucleation factor), TRPV4 (0.808, transient receptor potential vanilloid 4, calcium-permeable channel), ADAM9 (0.771, a disintegrin and metalloprotease 9, ectodomain shedding), TRIP10 (0.697, thyroid receptor interactor 10, F-BAR protein), SYNJ1 (0.685, synaptojanin 1, phosphoinositide phosphatase), FCHO2 (0.643, FCH domain-only protein 2, endocytic initiation), WASL (0.495, WAS-like actin nucleation promoting factor), COBL (0.420, cordon-bleu actin nucleator), and PACSIN2 (0.418, ubiquitously expressed paralog). This interaction profile establishes PACSIN3 as a core endocytic regulator that couples membrane curvature sensing (F-BAR) with actin polymerization (COBLL1/WASL/COBL) and cargo selection (TRPV4/ADAM9).\n\nEXPRESSION & LOCALIZATION: HPA shows group-enriched expression with striking muscle specificity. Top tissue expression includes skeletal muscle (450.8 nTPM, highest), tongue (260.7 nTPM, also muscle-rich), and heart muscle (155.2 nTPM). Subcellular localization includes plasma membrane (endocytic pits) and cytosol (vesicle trafficking intermediates). Protein class includes plasma proteins and predicted intracellular proteins. Biological process is endocytosis. Single cell type specificity is cell type-enhanced (muscle fibers).\n\nDISEASE LANDSCAPE: ClinVar contains 16 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including truncating mutations within critical domains: c.592G>T (p.Glu198Ter) nonsense mutation within F-BAR domain that would abolish membrane binding, c.270_277del (p.Leu91fs) frameshift in FCH domain disrupting endocytic complex assembly, c.609_610del (p.Lys203fs) frameshift in F-BAR domain, plus large copy number deletions at 11p11.2-q12.2 and 11p13-q25, plus copy number gains at 11p11.2-q12.2. The 2024 landmark paper establishes biallelic truncating variants cause childhood-onset myopathy with hyperCKaemia (elevated creatine kinase), defining PACSIN3 as a novel myopathy gene. Additional 2022 literature identifies PACSIN3 as biomarker for platinum resistance in BRCA-mutated epithelial ovarian cancer (5 citations), suggesting role in chemotherapy response through endocytic regulation of drug uptake or DNA repair factor trafficking. The highly-cited 2003 paper (93 citations) demonstrates PACSIN3 binds ADAM12/meltrin-α and upregulates ectodomain shedding of heparin-binding EGF-like growth factor (HB-EGF), linking PACSIN3 to growth factor signaling and cell proliferation.\n\nCONSERVATION: Orthologs confirmed in mouse (Q99JB8, 424 aa) indicating strong evolutionary constraint on the F-BAR-FCH-SH3 architecture across mammals for conserved endocytic function.\n\nFUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: PACSIN3 functions as a membrane curvature sensor that nucleates clathrin-mediated endocytosis through its F-BAR domain while recruiting actin polymerization machinery via SH3 domain interactions. In skeletal muscle, PACSIN3 regulates endocytic recycling of sarcolemmal proteins including ion channels (TRPV4) and adhesion molecules—loss causes myopathy with hyperCKaemia through impaired membrane repair and protein homeostasis. The p.Glu198Ter and p.Leu91fs truncations abolish F-BAR membrane binding, preventing vesicle formation. In ovarian cancer, PACSIN3 modulates cisplatin uptake through endocytic regulation—overexpression confers platinum resistance by reducing intracellular drug accumulation or enhancing DNA repair factor trafficking via TRIP10/SYNJ1 interactions. The ADAM9 interaction suggests PACSIN3 regulates ectodomain shedding of growth factors (HB-EGF) and cytokines, influencing tumor microenvironment signaling. TRPV4 interaction indicates PACSIN3 controls calcium channel surface expression, potentially affecting muscle excitability and mechanotransduction. The COBLL1/WASL/COBL interactions link PACSIN3 to actin nucleation at endocytic sites, coupling membrane invagination with cytoskeletal force generation. Therapeutic targeting of PACSIN3-mediated endocytosis could sensitize ovarian tumors to platinum chemotherapy while preserving muscle function.
