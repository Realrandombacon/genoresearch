## Description
LRRC55 (Leucine-rich repeat-containing protein 55, UniProt Q6ZSA7) is a 298 amino acid transmembrane auxiliary subunit encoded on chromosome 11q12.1. InterPro analysis reveals a canonical leucine-rich repeat (LRR) architecture characteristic of protein-protein interaction scaffolds: an N-terminal LRR domain (IPR000372, SM00013, residues 37-69), multiple typical LRR repeats (IPR001611, IPR003591, PF13855, residues 68-148), a C-terminal cysteine-rich flanking region (IPR000483, SM00082, residues 196-250), and classification within the voltage/calcium-activated potassium channel auxiliary protein family (IPR051432, PTHR46473, residues 12-294). The LRR solenoid structure mediates specific binding to potassium channel pore-forming subunits, while the cysteine-rich region may stabilize extracellular domains or mediate disulfide bonding.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places LRRC55 within a high-confidence potassium channel regulatory network. Top partners include KCNMB1 (beta-1 regulatory subunit, 0.719), KCNU1 (Slo3 sperm channel, 0.535), and KCNMA1 (Slo1 alpha pore subunit, 0.501), confirming its role as a BK channel auxiliary factor. Additional interactions with KCNMB3/4 (0.475/0.472, tissue-specific beta subunits), LRRC26 (0.408, gamma-1 auxiliary subunit), and ADGRL1/2/3 (0.514-0.517, adhesion GPCRs) suggest LRRC55 may integrate channel activity with cell adhesion signaling. The LRR-KCNMB network indicates LRRC55 functions as a tissue-specific modulator of calcium-activated potassium currents.\n\nHuman Protein Atlas classifies LRRC55 as "Tissue enhanced" with expression detected in some tissues, showing peak expression in brain (3.7 nTPM) and lymphoid tissue (1.9 nTPM). It is annotated as a membrane transporter involved in ion transport, with cancer-enriched expression suggesting potential oncogenic roles. Single-cell data indicates cell type enhancement, consistent with specialized neuronal or immune functions.\n\nClinVar records 9 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, comprising a striking duplication bias: 8 copy number gains (duplications spanning 11q12.1-13.3 ~7-13 Mb, 11p11.12-q12.2 ~10 Mb, whole-arm 11p13-q25 ~102 Mb, and 11q11-12.1 ~3.7 Mb) versus only 1 deletion. This 8:1 ratio indicates dosage sensitivity favoring overexpression pathogenicity. These CNVs are associated with 11q duplication syndromes, characterized by developmental delay, intellectual disability, and dysmorphic features.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: LRRC55 functions as a tissue-specific auxiliary subunit that modulates calcium-activated potassium (BK) channel gating properties in neuronal and lymphoid tissues. The LRR domain binds the extracellular turret region of KCNMA1 (Slo1) or KCNU1 (Slo3) pore subunits, altering voltage/calcium sensitivity or surface expression. Interaction with KCNMB1 suggests LRRC55 may compete with or cooperate with beta subunits to fine-tune channel kinetics. In brain, LRRC55 likely regulates neuronal excitability, neurotransmitter release, or action potential repolarization in specific neuronal subtypes (e.g., cerebellar Purkinje cells, hippocampal interneurons). In lymphoid tissue, it may modulate T-cell activation thresholds via potassium current regulation. The duplication bias indicates that LRRC55 overexpression causes hyperpolarization or altered firing patterns, disrupting neural circuit development or immune cell function. The ADGRL interactions suggest LRRC55 may couple channel activity to adhesion signaling during synaptogenesis or immune synapse formation. Cancer enrichment raises the possibility that LRRC55 overexpression promotes tumorigenesis via altered membrane potential or calcium signaling.
