# DCPS: A Histidine Triad Family Scavenger Decapping Enzyme Completing the 3'-5' mRNA Decay Pathway by Hydrolyzing Residual m7GpppN Caps

**Date:** 2026-06-26T17:29:31.444831

**Quality Score:** 10/10 (EXCELLENT)

**Confidence:** HIGH

## Description
DCPS (Decapping Enzyme Scavenger, UniProt Q96C86), also known as DCS1, HINT5, or m7GpppX diphosphatase, is a 337-amino acid hydrolase that functions as the terminal enzyme in the 3'-to-5' mRNA decay pathway by removing residual cap structures from short oligonucleotides generated after exosome-mediated degradation. Unlike the major decapping enzyme DCP2 which removes caps from intact mRNAs to initiate 5'-to-3' decay, DCPS acts as a 'scavenger' cleaning up cap remnants (m7GpppN where N is typically 1-10 nucleotides) left after 3'-to-5' exonucleolytic digestion, hydrolyzing the triphosphate linkage to yield m7GMP and ppN products. InterPro analysis reveals a bipartite domain architecture characteristic of the histidine triad (HIT) superfamily: an N-terminal domain (IPR011145, G3DSA:3.30.200.40, PF05652, aa 37-146) involved in substrate recognition and oligomerization; a C-terminal HIT-like domain (IPR008594, G3DSA:3.30.428.10, IPR036265, PF11969, aa 147-337) containing the catalytic histidine triad motif (PS00892, IPR019808, aa 264-282) with the signature H-X-H-X-H sequence essential for phosphoramidate bond hydrolysis; DCPS belongs to the scavenger mRNA decapping enzyme family (PIRSF028973, PTHR12978). The AlphaFold predicted structure exhibits high confidence (pLDDT 88.4), revealing a well-folded homodimeric architecture typical of HIT proteins with each monomer contributing to a shared active site cleft. STRING interaction data places DCPS at the convergence of mRNA decay pathways with very high confidence associations to: NDOR1 (0.953), an oxidoreductase potentially linking redox state to RNA metabolism; DCP2 (0.881), the primary decapping enzyme for 5'-3' decay; XRN1 (0.867), the 5'-3' exoribonuclease completing cytoplasmic mRNA degradation; EIF4E (0.716), the cap-binding translation initiation factor representing the competing pathway; POLR3K (0.631), RNA polymerase III subunit; FZR1 (0.625), cell cycle regulator; XRN2 (0.584), nuclear 5'-3' exoribonuclease; EDC3 (0.580), enhancer of decapping complex; NT5C3B (0.574), nucleotidase; and SMN2 (0.570), survival motor neuron protein linking to RNA processing. HPA expression profiles confirm ubiquitous detection across all tissues with tissue-enhanced expression peaking in liver (79.5 nTPM), cancer-enhanced patterns, and immune cell enrichment; subcellular localization spans both nucleoplasm and cytosol consistent with roles in nuclear surveillance and cytoplasmic decay; classified as a hydrolase involved in mRNA processing/splicing with disease associations including intellectual disability. ClinVar records document 85 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including nonsense mutations (p.Glu14Ter causing complete loss-of-function), missense variants affecting catalytic regions (p.Pro6Ser in N-domain, p.Ser45Cys near active site), splice site mutations (c.522+6T>C, c.523-106G>T), small duplications/deletions disrupting the HIT motif (c.745_747+1dup, c.739_747+22del), and large copy number losses spanning 11q24.1-25 (up to 11 Mb deletions). We hypothesize that DCPS functions as an essential quality control enzyme preventing accumulation of toxic cap remnants: during normal 3'-5' decay, the exosome degrades deadenylated mRNAs processively until reaching the cap structure; DCPS recognizes these short m7GpppN oligonucleotides via its N-terminal domain; dimerization brings two HIT domains into proximity creating the catalytic pocket; the conserved histidine triad coordinates a nucleophilic attack on the α-phosphate of the cap triphosphate bridge; hydrolysis releases m7GMP which can be recycled through salvage pathways while ppN fragments are further degraded; this prevents competitive inhibition of EIF4E by free cap analogs and maintains cellular nucleotide pools; the intellectual disability phenotype suggests neuronal sensitivity to aberrant RNA metabolite accumulation; cancer-enhanced expression indicates DCPS may be upregulated in rapidly proliferating cells with high transcriptional turnover; coordination with DCP2 and XRN1 suggests integrated regulation of bidirectional mRNA decay pathways; loss of DCPS would cause cap remnant accumulation, potentially sequestering cap-binding proteins and disrupting translation homeostasis.
