## Description
TSNAX (Translin-Associated Factor X, also known as TRAX) is a 290 amino acid nucleic acid-binding protein that forms a heteromeric complex with Translin (TSN) to create the C3PO (Complex 3 Promoting Oligonucleotide Processing) complex, functioning in RNA interference, mRNA trafficking, and chromosomal translocation breakpoint recognition. InterPro analysis identifies canonical Translin family domains (IPR002848, PF01997, residues 51-264; PTHR10741, residues 21-274) with sequence-specific DNA binding capability (GO:0043565), organized into N-terminal (G3DSA:1.20.58.190, IPR016068, residues 32-186) and C-terminal (G3DSA:1.20.58.200, IPR016069, residues 187-272) subdomains within the Translin superfamily (IPR036081, SSF74784, residues 35-266). AlphaFold structure (AF-Q99598-F1) predicts high confidence (pLDDT 82.6) across the full 290 residue sequence, indicating a stable folded protein suitable for nucleic acid recognition and protein complex assembly. HPA data classifies TSNAX as a predicted intracellular protein with low tissue specificity (detected in all tissues), localized to nucleoplasm, Golgi apparatus, and cytosol, with immune cell-enhanced expression and roles in differentiation and spermatogenesis. STRING interactions reveal an exceptionally tight functional partnership with TSN (Translin, 0.993), confirming obligate heteromeric complex formation; strong connections to RNAi machinery including AGO2 (0.952), DICER1 (0.837), and AGO1 (0.708); association with DISC1 (Disrupted in Schizophrenia 1, 0.915), positioning TSNAX at the intersection of RNA metabolism and psychiatric disease pathways; links to TSNAXIP1 (0.884), a dedicated interaction partner; KIF2A (0.813), a kinesin involved in neuronal migration; PTGER3 (0.706), a prostaglandin receptor; and GOLGA3 (0.496), consistent with Golgi localization. ClinVar lists 40 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants predominantly consisting of large copy number gains and losses on chromosome 1q42-44, encompassing the DISC1-TSNAX locus, implicating dosage sensitivity in neurodevelopmental disorders though specific monogenic phenotypes remain incompletely defined. Literature (37 papers) establishes TSNAX as essential for C3PO complex endonuclease activity that removes siRNA passenger strands during RISC loading, mediates translocation breakpoint junctions in lymphoid malignancies through the TSN-TSNAX complex recognizing DNA crossover sites, and forms a fusion transcript TSNAX-DISC1 via intergenic splicing in endometrial carcinoma. Genetic association studies link TSNAX/DISC1 locus variants to schizophrenia, bipolar disorder, and major depression with cognitive impairment, while recent work identifies roles in spermatogenesis and cellular differentiation. As a dark gene with established molecular function but incompletely characterized disease mechanisms, TSNAX represents a critical node connecting RNA silencing, DNA recombination, and neurodevelopmental signaling through its partnership with both Translin and DISC1.
