## Description
HNRNPAB (Heterogeneous Nuclear Ribonucleoprotein A/B, UniProt Q99729) is a 332 amino acid nucleoplasmic RNA-binding protein with moderate characterization (63 papers) that functions as a member of the hnRNP family involved in mRNA processing, splicing, and stability regulation with emerging roles in cancer metastasis and ferroptosis. The protein contains two RNA recognition motif domains (IPR000504/PF00076, residues 69-154 and 153-233) within the RRM superfamily (IPR035979/G3DSA:3.30.70.330, residues 67-263), an N-terminal CARG-binding factor domain (IPR012956/PF08143, residues 1-69), and belongs to the RNA-binding and regulatory protein family (PTHR48033, residues 15-332), placing it in the nucleotide-binding alpha-beta plait domain superfamily (IPR012677, residues 58-142/143-267). STRING interactions reveal high-confidence partnerships with hnRNP family members HNRNPC (0.958), HNRNPA2B1 (0.905), HNRNPU (0.889), SYNCRIP (0.886), HNRNPA1 (0.844), HNRNPH1 (0.769), and RNA-binding regulators YBX1 (0.898), PTBP1 (0.875), APOBEC1 (0.772), and PABPN1 (0.634), placing HNRNPAB in mRNA processing and stability complexes. HPA data shows ubiquitous expression (detected in all tissues), low tissue and cancer specificity, nucleoplasmic localization, consistent with housekeeping RNA-binding functions. ClinVar contains 28 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number losses at 5q35.2-35.3, copy number gains at 5q35.1-35.3, and splice site variants (c.670-249G>A, c.378+6C>G), though no specific diseases are directly assigned—suggesting potential developmental, cancer, or neurological phenotypes. AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 64.4) indicates medium confidence with partially structured RRM domains and disordered regions typical of RNA-binding proteins. Functional hypothesis: HNRNPAB acts as an mRNA stability and splicing regulator through its dual RRM domains that bind AU-rich elements and specific mRNA targets, where recent 2025 studies demonstrate: (1) KAP1 binding to HNRNPAB activates Hippo/YAP1 signaling promoting gastric adenocarcinoma progression and peritoneal carcinomatosis, (2) tRNA-derived fragment tRF-22 inhibits HNRNPAB ubiquitination causing immunosuppression in esophageal squamous cell carcinoma, (3) HNRNPAB mediates NF-κB/Lcn2 axis regulating ferroptosis following traumatic brain injury, (4) HNRNPAB stabilizes MYC mRNA promoting pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma extravasation and liver metastasis (2024, 3 citations), (5) exosomal HNRNPAB mediates silica nanoparticle-induced pulmonary epithelial-mesenchymal transition, suggesting HNRNPAB functions as a context-dependent RNA-binding hub that stabilizes oncogenic transcripts (MYC, YAP1 targets) in cancer, regulates inflammatory responses (NF-κB/Lcn2) in neurotrauma, and modulates immune checkpoint sensitivity through ubiquitination-dependent mechanisms, with 5q copy number variants potentially contributing to developmental disorders through RNA processing defects, making HNRNPAB a potential therapeutic target for metastatic cancers and ferroptosis-modulating conditions.
