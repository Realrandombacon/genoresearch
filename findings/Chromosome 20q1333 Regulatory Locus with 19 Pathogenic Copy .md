## Description
FLJ16779 (LOC100192386) is an uncharacterized genetic locus on chromosome 20q13.33 (NC_000020.11, positions 63253977-63261614, ~7.6 kb region) with substantial clinical significance despite lacking protein-coding features. No UniProt accession exists, preventing InterPro domain analysis and AlphaFold structure prediction. No mRNA transcript was identified in NCBI nucleotide databases, and Human Protein Atlas found no expression data (ENSG00000275620). STRING-DB detected no protein-protein interactions. However, ClinVar contains 19 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all representing large-scale copy number variations spanning the 20q13 region (variants include 3235944, 2579288, 1341868, 160985, 155517, 153305, 153207, 152840, 150212, 148973). These CNVs range from focal deletions to multi-megabase duplications (200 kb to 13.5 Mb). The 20q13.33 chromosomal location is clinically notable as this terminal region is associated with 20q13.33 deletion/duplication syndrome, characterized by developmental delay, intellectual disability, autism spectrum disorder, growth abnormalities, and dysmorphic features. The region contains multiple dosage-sensitive genes including AURKA (aurora kinase A, cell cycle regulation), HNF4A (hepatocyte nuclear factor 4-alpha, maturity-onset diabetes of the young), MACROD2 (ADP-ribosylhydrolase, neurodevelopment), and TBX3 (T-box transcription factor, ulnar-mammary syndrome). Functional hypothesis: FLJ16779 likely represents a cis-regulatory element or chromatin boundary controlling expression of neighboring dosage-sensitive genes in the 20q13.33 critical region. The 19 pathogenic CNVs indicate extreme dosage sensitivity where copy number alterations disrupt normal transcriptional regulation of developmental genes. Disease mechanism likely involves haploinsufficiency or triplosensitivity of AURKA, HNF4A, MACROD2, or TBX3 through enhancer disruption, silencer deletion, or chromatin topology alteration. The absence of protein-coding features combined with strong ClinVar evidence and neurodevelopmental disease association suggests this locus functions in embryonic development and metabolic regulation through position effects on critical regulatory networks.
