# ILDR1: Immunoglobulin-Like Domain Receptor Causing DFNB42 Hearing Loss via Hair Cell Stereocilia Defects and Emerging Roles in Cancer Progression, Influenza A Virus Replication through PLSCR1 Binding, and Tight Junction Regulation

## Description
ILDR1 (Immunoglobulin-Like Domain-Containing Receptor 1, also known as DFNB42, ILDR1alpha/alpha'/beta, UniProt Q86SU0) is a 546-amino acid type I transmembrane receptor protein belonging to the lipolysis-stimulated receptor (LSR) family that functions as a critical regulator of auditory hair cell development and tight junction organization, with emerging roles in cancer progression and viral replication. The protein architecture features an N-terminal signal peptide followed by two immunoglobulin-like domains (IPR003599 Ig subtype aa 30-166, IPR013783 Ig-like fold aa 21-169, IPR036179 Ig-like domain superfamily aa 26-166, G3DSA:2.60.40.10 immunoglobulins aa 21-71 and 102-169, SM00409 Ig domain aa 30-166) forming the extracellular ligand-binding region, a LISCH7 domain (IPR008664, PF05624 LSR family aa 167-214) characteristic of the tricellulin-associated protein family, and a C-terminal cytoplasmic tail of unknown function. The AlphaFold predicted structure shows medium confidence (pLDDT 59.0), indicating a partially structured protein with ordered immunoglobulin domains embedded within flexible regions potentially enabling conformational adaptation for diverse partner recognition. STRING interaction network places ILDR1 within auditory function and epithelial barrier machinery with medium-confidence associations to hearing loss genes: TMPRSS3 (0.588), transmembrane protease serine 3 mutated in autosomal recessive deafness; CLDN14 (0.579), claudin-14 tight junction protein causing deafness when mutated; LRIT3 (0.576), leucine-rich repeat transmembrane neuronal protein 3; TRA2A (0.571) and TRA2B (0.536), transformer-2 alpha/beta homologs involved in alternative splicing; TMIE (0.571), transmembrane inner ear protein defective in deafness; OCLN (0.563), occludin tight junction protein; MYO15A (0.545), myosin-XVA essential for stereocilia development; TMC1 (0.544), transmembrane channel-like protein 1 causing deafness; and MARVELD2 (0.447), tricellulin paralog. HPA expression profiles demonstrate tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in parathyroid gland (14.7 nTPM), detected in many tissues, classification as predicted membrane protein with receptor molecular function, low cancer specificity at RNA level, immune cell-enhanced expression in blood, and disease involvement in deafness/non-syndromic deafness. ClinVar records document 139 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including frameshift deletions (c.316del p.Arg106fs), missense mutations affecting Ig domains (c.340G>C p.Gly114Arg, c.419A>T p.His140Leu, c.298C>T p.Arg100Cys, c.363G>C p.Lys121Asn), splice site mutations (c.379+8C>T), and C-terminal variants (c.932G>A p.Gly311Asp, c.1199G>T p.Ser400Ile, c.1289C>T p.Ala430Val), all causing autosomal recessive non-syndromic sensorineural hearing loss DFNB42 through disruption of tricellular tight junctions in cochlear hair cells. Literature analysis (71 papers) reveals multi-context functionality: ILDR1 localizes to tricellular tight junctions (tTJs) where three epithelial cells meet, recruiting tricellulin (MARVELD2) to organize tTJ structure essential for maintaining endolymph composition and hair cell stereocilia integrity; Ildr1 knockout mice show progressive hearing loss with disorganized stereocilia bundles; ILDR1 deficiency protects against diet-induced obesity and hyperglycemia suggesting metabolic regulation via cholecystokinin-positive enteroendocrine cells; ILDR1 promotes influenza A virus replication through direct binding to PLSCR1 (phospholipid scramblase 1) enhancing viral entry or assembly; ILDR1 expression correlates with gastric cancer progression and poor prognosis; presbycusis-associated SNPs identified in ILDR1 locus. We hypothesize that ILDR1 functions through a multi-mechanism model: (1) In auditory hair cells, the extracellular Ig domains mediate homophilic or heterophilic adhesion at tricellular contacts, recruiting tricellulin/MARVELD2 to seal tTJs preventing endolymph leakage and maintaining ion homeostasis essential for mechanotransduction; (2) Mutations in Ig domains (p.Gly114Arg, p.His140Leu, p.Arg100Cys) disrupt folding or partner binding causing tTJ defects and hair cell degeneration; (3) The LISCH7/LSR domain mediates interactions with claudins (CLDN14) and occludin (OCLN) organizing bicellular tight junctions; (4) In cancer, ILDR1 may promote EMT or metastasis through tight junction remodeling enabling cell migration; (5) Viral replication enhancement via PLSCR1 binding suggests ILDR1 functions as a host factor facilitating viral entry/assembly at membrane microdomains; (6) TMPRSS3 interaction suggests proteolytic processing may regulate ILDR1 shedding or activation; (7) Alternative splicing generates ILDR1alpha/alpha'/beta isoforms with distinct cytoplasmic tails enabling tissue-specific functions; (8) Medium-confidence structure suggests flexible linkers between Ig domains enabling adaptation to tricellular geometry; (9) Parathyroid expression suggests potential roles in calcium homeostasis linking to auditory calcium signaling. Therapeutic targeting of ILDR1-tricellulin interface could treat DFNB42 hearing loss, while blocking ILDR1-PLSCR1 interactions may inhibit influenza A replication.
