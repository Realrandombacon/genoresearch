## Description
PRCD (Photoreceptor disc component, also known as RP36) is a 54 amino acid intracellular membrane protein (UniProt Q00LT1) with medium-confidence partially-structured architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 64.0), characteristic of small photoreceptor-specific proteins that function through protein-protein interactions and membrane association in photoreceptor outer segment discs. The protein belongs to the photoreceptor disk component PRCD family (IPR027937, residues 1-54; PTHR38501, residues 1-54) and is classified as a progressive rod-cone degeneration protein (PF15201, residues 1-54). PRCD is located on chromosome 17q25.1 and is predicted to be located in photoreceptor outer segment membrane (GO:0042622), involved in sensory transduction and vision. While no specific Mendelian disease name is annotated in ClinVar summary, PRCD mutations cause autosomal recessive retinitis pigmentosa (RP36) in humans and progressive rod-cone degeneration in dogs, confirming conserved photoreceptor function across mammals. Given PRCD's photoreceptor outer segment localization and phototransduction interactions, loss-of-function causes defective disc structure, impaired phototransduction, or disrupted outer segment maintenance leading to progressive photoreceptor degeneration.\n\nConservation is evident across mammals: human (Q00LT1, 54 aa), Mus musculus (Q00LT2, 53 aa), Canis lupus familiaris (Q00LT9, 54 aa), Bos taurus (E1B7R9, 54 aa), indicating the PRCD architecture is highly conserved across mammals with nearly identical protein length, suggesting strong purifying selection on photoreceptor outer segment function. The conservation in dogs is particularly notable as PRCD mutations cause progressive rod-cone degeneration in multiple dog breeds, establishing PRCD as a conserved photoreceptor maintenance factor.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: PRCD functions as a photoreceptor outer segment disc component that maintains disc structure and phototransduction efficiency through protein-protein interactions within the photoreceptor membrane.
