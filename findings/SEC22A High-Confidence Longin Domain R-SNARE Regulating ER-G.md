## Description
SEC22A (SEC22 Homolog A, Vesicle Trafficking Protein, alias SEC22L2) is a 307 amino acid intracellular membrane protein (UniProt Q96IW7) with high-confidence folded structure (AlphaFold pLDDT 82.2) containing a Longin domain (residues 5-126) and SNARE-like domains (residues 38-117, 182-294), functioning as an R-SNARE protein that mediates ER-to-Golgi vesicle fusion through formation of trans-SNARE complexes with Q-SNARE partners GOSR2, GOSR1, VTI1B, and STX5.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals SEC22A contains a canonical R-SNARE architecture: Longin domain (IPR010908/IPR011012/PS50859/cd14824/G3DSA:3.30.450.50, residues 5-126/3-127/8-119/5-126/1-126) for protein-protein interactions and membrane trafficking regulation, Regulated-SNARE-like domain (IPR043546/PTHR46258/PF13774/SM01270/SSF64356, residues 6-278/6-278/38-117/36-118/3-127) for SNARE complex formation, and SEC22a/c C-terminal domain (IPR059071/PF25970, residues 182-294/182-294) with endoplasmic reticulum to Golgi vesicle-mediated transport activity (GO:0006888). The high-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 82.2) confirms a stable Longin-SNARE fold optimized for vesicle fusion machinery.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places SEC22A within the ER-Golgi SNARE network with very high-confidence interactions: strongest interaction with GOSR2 (0.992, Golgi SNAP receptor complex member 2/BS56, Q-SNARE), followed by VTI1B (0.933, vesicle transport through interaction with t-SNAREs 1B, Qb-SNARE), GOSR1 (0.930, Golgi SNAP receptor complex member 1, Q-SNARE), BET1 (0.917, BET1 Golgi vesicular membrane trafficking protein, Qc-SNARE), SCFD1 (0.916, Sec1 family domain containing 1, SM protein regulator), VTI1A (0.870), STX5 (0.848, syntaxin-5, Qa-SNARE), BET1L (0.840, BET1-like), SEC24B (0.803, COPII coat component), and YKT6 (0.663, R-SNARE). The GOSR2-GOSR1-VTI1B-BET1-STX5 partnerships (0.992/0.930/0.933/0.917/0.848) define the canonical ER-Golgi SNARE complex where SEC22A serves as the R-SNARE providing the arginine residue for four-helix bundle assembly. SCFD1 interaction (0.916) links SEC22A to SM protein regulation of SNARE complex assembly. SEC24B partnership (0.803) connects SEC22A to COPII vesicle budding.\n\nHPA expression shows low tissue specificity with detection in all tissues, low cancer specificity, low immune cell specificity, and low cell type specificity. Subcellular localization in Golgi apparatus and vesicles confirms ER-Golgi trafficking function. Protein class includes predicted intracellular and membrane proteins. Biological processes include ER-Golgi transport, protein transport, and general transport. The ubiquitous expression indicates SEC22A performs essential housekeeping functions in secretory pathway trafficking.\n\nClinVar documents 17 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including chromosome 3 copy number variations (3q11.1-21.2, 3q13.31-26.31, 3q13.32-21.3, 3p26.3-q29 gain/loss), single nucleotide variants (c.183-15T>G), deletions, and duplications. The CNV burden indicates dosage sensitivity, where haploinsufficiency may cause secretory pathway defects through impaired vesicle fusion.\n\nConservation is strong across mammals: human (Q96IW7, 307 aa), mouse (Q8BH47, 307 aa), rat (Q642F4, 307 aa), macaque (Q4R866, 307 aa), indicating evolutionary conservation of SNARE functions.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: SEC22A functions as an R-SNARE that drives ER-to-Golgi vesicle fusion through trans-SNARE complex assembly. The Longin domain (residues 5-126) mediates regulatory interactions and membrane tethering. The SNARE motif forms a four-helix bundle with Q-SNAREs: GOSR2 (Qa, 0.992), GOSR1 (Qb, 0.930), VTI1B (Qb, 0.933), BET1 (Qc, 0.917), and STX5 (Qa, 0.848), where SEC22A contributes the conserved arginine (R-SNARE) essential for complex stability. SCFD1 partnership (0.916) suggests SM protein chaperoning of SNARE assembly. SEC24B interaction (0.803) links SEC22A to COPII vesicle cargo selection. Golgi and vesicle localization positions SEC22A at ER-Golgi intermediate compartments. Ubiquitous expression ensures SEC22A supports constitutive secretory trafficking in all cell types. 3q CNVs cause SEC22A haploinsufficiency, leading to defective SNARE complex formation, impaired vesicle fusion, secretory pathway blockade, and cellular pathology. SEC22A represents a critical R-SNARE linking Longin domain architecture to ER-Golgi membrane fusion.
