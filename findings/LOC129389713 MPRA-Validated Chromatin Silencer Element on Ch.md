# LOC129389713: MPRA-Validated Chromatin Silencer Element on Chromosome 6q25 with High CNV Burden

**Date:** 2026-03-23T11:24:33.256107

**Quality Score:** 4.8/10  (E=5.3, D=4.3) [MODERATE]

## Description
LOC129389713 is a non-coding cis-regulatory DNA element classified as a transcriptional silencer, validated through STARR-seq massively parallel reporter assays (MPRAs) in K562 erythroleukemia cells. This 200bp genomic region (chr6:160884042-160884242, GRCh38) at 6q25.3 demonstrates capacity to repress super core promoter (SCP1) activity, confirming silencer function. As a regulatory DNA element, it lacks protein-coding features: no UniProt accession, no InterPro domains, no STRING interactions, and no HPA expression data. However, clinical significance is substantial: ClinVar documents 14 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variants (CNVs) spanning this locus—higher burden than the chromosome 14 silencer (LOC129390646, 6 CNVs). These include both duplications (x3) and deletions (x1) across 6q25.2-27 regions, indicating strong dosage sensitivity. Hypothesis: LOC129389713 functions as a chromatin silencer recruiting repressive complexes (Polycomb PRC2, HDACs, or KRAB-ZNF co-repressors) to silence target genes on 6q25, a region harboring immunologically important loci (e.g., HLA region proximity). The elevated CNV burden (14 variants) suggests this element regulates critical genes where dosage disruption causes disease—potentially autoimmune disorders, hematologic malignancies, or developmental syndromes. K562 erythroid validation indicates relevance to blood cell development, where 6q25 genes participate in immune cell differentiation. The silencer may coordinate long-range chromatin looping to repress multiple target genes simultaneously, and CNVs disrupt this three-dimensional genome architecture.
