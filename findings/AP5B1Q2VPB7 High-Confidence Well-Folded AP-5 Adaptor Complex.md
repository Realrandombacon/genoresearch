## Description
AP5B1 (Adaptor related protein complex 5 subunit beta 1, also known as AP-5 or PP1030) is a large 878 amino acid intracellular protein localized to the nucleoplasm, Golgi apparatus, and vesicles (UniProt Q2VPB7) with high-confidence well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 79.8). The protein defines the AP-5 complex subunit beta-1 family (IPR038741/PTHR34033, residues 4-668) and contains four distinct structural domains: N-terminal domain (IPR048978/PF21587, residues 35-104, ~70 aa), middle domain (IPR048979/PF21588, residues 263-627, ~365 aa, the largest domain), beta-barrel domain (IPR048980/PF21589, residues 690-759, ~70 aa), and C-terminal domain (IPR048981/PF21590, residues 781-873, ~93 aa). This multi-domain architecture creates a scaffold for vesicle coat assembly. Molecular functions include endosomal transport (GO:0016197), protein transport, and structural constituent of AP-type membrane coat adaptor complex, placing AP5B1 within the lysosomal trafficking pathway.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals an exceptionally tight network within the AP-5 adaptor complex: near-perfect interactions with AP5S1 (0.999, AP-5 complex subunit sigma-1, the small subunit), AP5Z1 (0.998, AP-5 complex subunit zeta-1, the large subunit), and AP5M1 (0.838, AP-5 complex subunit mu-1, the medium subunit), confirming AP5B1 as an integral core component of the pentameric AP-5 holoenzyme. High confidence links include other adaptor complex subunits AP3S2 (0.921), AP4B1 (0.920), AP4M1 (0.918), AP1S1 (0.908), indicating evolutionary and functional relationships among adaptor complexes. The interaction with SPG11 (0.501, Spatacsin, mutated in hereditary spastic paraplegia type 11) is particularly significant, as SPG11 forms a complex with AP-5 (the 'spastizin' complex) for lysosomal repair and autophagosome maturation.\n\nHPA expression reveals a tissue-enhanced pattern with top expression in bone marrow (41.5 nTPM), detected in all tissues with immune cell enrichment. This ubiquitous expression reflects the essential housekeeping role of AP-5 in endosomal-lysosomal trafficking across all cell types. Subcellular localization to nucleoplasm, Golgi, and vesicles confirms involvement in multiple trafficking steps. Low cancer specificity indicates physiological rather than oncogenic function.\n\nClinVar documents 16 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including nonsense mutations (p.Arg731Ter in the C-terminal domain, p.Arg155Ter in the middle domain), frameshift deletions (p.Gln288fs, p.Leu104fs), and large CNVs (11q12.1-13.3 duplications, 11p13-q25 duplications, 11q deletions). The variant burden indicates strong functional sensitivity. Loss-of-function mutations cause hereditary spastic paraplegia (HSP) type 48, characterized by progressive lower limb spasticity, weakness, and cognitive impairment. The AP-5/SPG11 complex is essential for lysosomal membrane repair and autophagosome clearance; its dysfunction leads to lysosomal storage defects and axonal degeneration in corticospinal tracts.\n\nConservation is strong across vertebrates: human (Q2VPB7, 878 aa), mouse (Q80U40, 876 aa), rat (D3ZVB0, 876 aa), cow (G3MZC5, 877 aa), indicating evolutionary pressure to maintain the AP-5 architecture.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: AP5B1 functions as the beta subunit scaffold of the AP-5 adaptor complex, which coats vesicles budding from endosomes toward lysosomes. The middle domain (365 aa) likely provides the primary interaction surface for cargo recognition and membrane binding, while the beta-barrel and C-terminal domains mediate complex assembly with AP5S1/AP5Z1/AP5M1. AP5B1 recruits SPG11/spastizin to damaged lysosomes, enabling membrane repair via ESCRT machinery. During autophagy, AP-5 facilitates autophagosome-lysosome fusion and cargo degradation. Loss of AP5B1 destabilizes the AP-5 complex, impairing lysosomal function and causing accumulation of undigested material in neurons. This leads to axonal transport defects and corticospinal tract degeneration in HSP. The bone marrow expression suggests additional roles in hematopoietic cell trafficking. 11q CNVs disrupt AP5B1 dosage, potentially contributing to 11q deletion/duplication syndrome phenotypes including developmental delay and spasticity.
