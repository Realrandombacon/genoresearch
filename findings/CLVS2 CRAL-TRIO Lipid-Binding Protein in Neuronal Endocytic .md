## Description
CLVS2 (Clavesin-2) is a 327 amino acid intracellular lipid-binding protein (UniProt Q5SYC1) with minimal characterization (9 papers) that functions in neuronal vesicular trafficking and lipid homeostasis. The protein contains a canonical CRAL-TRIO lipid-binding domain (IPR001251/PF00650, residues 96-257) and Sec14p-like lipid-binding domain (cd00170, residues 101-251), indicating capacity for binding and transferring small hydrophobic molecules such as retinoids, tocopherols, or phosphoinositides. STRING interactions reveal partnerships with clathrin heavy chain CLTC (0.556), AP1 adaptor complex subunits AP1G1/AP1S1/AP1S2/AP1S3/AP1M1 (all 0.506-0.535), and CCDC175 (0.624), strongly suggesting roles in clathrin-mediated endocytosis and Golgi-to-endosome vesicular trafficking. HPA data shows group-enriched expression in brain (16.5 nTPM) and retina (7.6 nTPM), consistent with neuronal and visual system functions. ClinVar contains 21 pathogenic copy number variants (losses and gains) at 6q21-23.2, though no specific diseases are yet assigned—suggesting potential neurological or ophthalmological phenotypes. AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 85.9) indicates a well-folded protein with stable CRAL-TRIO domain architecture. Functional hypothesis: CLVS2 acts as a lipid transfer protein that binds phosphoinositides or retinoids at clathrin-coated vesicles, facilitating lipid exchange between endosomal membranes and regulating neuronal endocytic trafficking, with loss-of-function potentially causing neurodevelopmental or retinal degeneration disorders through disrupted lipid homeostasis and vesicle maturation.
