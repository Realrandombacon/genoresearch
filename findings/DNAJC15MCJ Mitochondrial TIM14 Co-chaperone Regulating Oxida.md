## Description
DNAJC15 (UniProt Q9Y5T4), also known as MCJ (Methylation-controlled J protein) or DnaJ homolog subfamily C member 15, encodes a 150 aa mitochondrial inner membrane protein belonging to the evolutionarily conserved TIM14/PAM16 family of J-domain co-chaperones, functioning as a critical negative regulator of oxidative phosphorylation efficiency, ferroptosis vulnerability, and T-cell metabolic fitness through its unique role in the TIM23 protein import complex. InterPro analysis reveals a compact architecture: an N-terminal transmembrane anchor domain (aa 45-77, IPR060698, PF28356) that embeds the protein in the mitochondrial inner membrane; a canonical DnaJ/J-domain (aa 95-149, IPR001623, PS50076, SM00271) with the conserved HPD motif required for Hsp70 ATPase stimulation; and classification within the TIM14/PAM16 family (PTHR12763) linking it functionally to mitochondrial protein import and quality control. AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (global pLDDT 78.9), indicating a well-folded, stable J-domain with precise geometry for HSPA9 binding, consistent with dedicated co-chaperone function. STRING interactions define DNAJC15 as a core component of the TIM23 translocase: PAM16 (0.955, TIM16 homolog), TIMM44 (0.931, translocase subunit), HSPA9 (0.891, mitochondrial Hsp70), TIMM17B (0.874), TIMM23 (0.842, core translocase), TIMM17A (0.823), TIMM50 (0.795, intermembrane space receptor), DNAJC19 (0.794, DCMA syndrome protein), TIMM21 (0.786), and GRPEL1 (0.786, nucleotide exchange factor), confirming its essential role in mitochondrial protein import and respiratory chain assembly. HPA expression shows low tissue specificity with detection in all tissues, classified as a predicted membrane protein with chaperone function involved in protein transport and translocation, consistent with housekeeping mitochondrial roles. ClinVar reports 24 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly chromosome 13q copy number losses and gains (spanning the DNAJC15 locus at 13q13.1-21.32), indicating genomic instability at this region potentially linked to mitochondrial disorders and cancer susceptibility, though no specific monogenic disease has been definitively assigned to DNAJC15 alone. Literature (55 papers) highlights: (1) OXPHOS Negative Regulator—DNAJC15 limits mitochondrial respiration efficiency by modulating complex I assembly, and its absence enhances brown adipose thermogenesis; (2) Ferroptosis Modulator—Promotes vulnerability to ferroptosis in chemoresistant ovarian cancer cells through mitochondrial lipid metabolism regulation; (3) CAR-T Cell Metabolism—Silencing DNAJC15 enhances mitochondrial respiration and improves CD8+ CAR-T cell therapy efficacy against leukemia/lymphoma; (4) OMA1 Substrate—Undergoes stress-regulated degradation by OMA1 peptidase to adapt mitochondrial protein import during cellular stress. Functional hypothesis: DNAJC15 functions as a: (1) TIM23 Complex Co-chaperone—Uses its J-domain to stimulate HSPA9 ATPase activity, driving protein translocation through the TIM23 channel; (2) Respiratory Chain Brake—Limits complex I assembly and OXPHOS efficiency to prevent excessive ROS production under basal conditions; (3) Ferroptosis Sensitizer—Regulates mitochondrial lipid metabolism and iron handling to modulate cellular susceptibility to lipid peroxidation. Mechanistic proposal: DNAJC15 anchors to the mitochondrial inner membrane via its N-terminal transmembrane domain, positioning its J-domain to interact with HSPA9 (STRING 0.891) at the TIM23 translocase. The high-confidence structure (pLDDT 78.9) supports stable J-domain folding for efficient Hsp70 recruitment. Under stress, OMA1 cleaves DNAJC15 to enhance protein import capacity. In cancer, DNAJC15 overexpression limits OXPHOS and protects against ferroptosis; silencing enhances mitochondrial respiration and CAR-T cell persistence. Chromosome 13q CNVs disrupt DNAJC15 dosage, potentially altering mitochondrial function and metabolic flexibility. The combination of a well-folded J-domain, TIM23 complex integration, OXPHOS regulation, ferroptosis modulation, and emerging therapeutic relevance (CAR-T enhancement) positions DNAJC15 as a borderline dark gene where mitochondrial co-chaperone function bridges protein import, metabolic control, and cell death regulation.
