## Description
RPP14 (Ribonuclease P protein subunit p14, also known as POP5 in yeast nomenclature) is a partially characterized dark gene with 23 publications, primarily from foundational studies in 1999-2002 that established its role in the RNase P complex. UniProt accession O95059 identifies it as a 124-amino acid protein that is a core component of ribonuclease P, the endonuclease responsible for cleaving the 5'-leader sequence from precursor tRNAs (pre-tRNAs), a critical step in tRNA maturation. This function is essential for protein synthesis and cellular viability.\n\nInterPro scan reveals membership in the Pop5/Rpp14/Rnp2-like family (IPR002759, residues 35-108; PF01900) with a conserved RNase P subunit domain (G3DSA:3.30.70.3250, residues 13-117; IPR038085 superfamily). GO annotations include tRNA 5'-leader removal (GO:0001682) and ribonuclease P complex (GO:0030677). The protein is classified under PTHR15441 (Ribonuclease P/MRP protein subunit) and SSF160350 (Rnp2-like fold), indicating structural conservation across eukaryotes and archaea.\n\nAlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (global pLDDT 79.4), indicating a well-folded, stable protein. This is consistent with its role as a structural component of the RNase P holoenzyme, where it likely contributes to RNA binding and catalytic complex assembly. The structured nature (residues 13-117 forming the core domain) suggests RPP14 makes specific contacts with other RNase P subunits and the pre-tRNA substrate.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals 10 high-confidence partners, all components of the RNase P or RNase MRP complexes: POP5 (0.999 - the yeast ortholog/paralog), RPP30 (0.999 - RNase P subunit p30), RPP21 (0.999 - RNase P subunit p21), POP4 (0.999 - RNase P subunit p29), RPP25 (0.996 - RNase P subunit p25), POP1 (0.993 - RNase P subunit p1), RPP40 (0.992 - RNase P/MRP subunit p40), POP7 (0.991 - RNase P subunit p7), RPP38 (0.991 - RNase P subunit p38), and AGO2 (0.900 - Argonaute-2, linking to RNA interference pathways). Low cancer specificity and low immune cell specificity indicate constitutive expression.\n\nClinVar reports 10 pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants, all large copy number variations on chromosome 3p (e.g., chr3:51149374-59265315)x1, chr3:57076136-65716956)x1, chr3:61892-197851986)x3).
