## Description
RNF157 (Q96PX1, 679 aa), also known as Ring Finger Protein 157, is a RING-type E3 ubiquitin-protein ligase belonging to the MGRN1/RNF157-like family (PTHR22996). InterPro identifies comprehensive RING finger architecture: modified RING finger HC subclass C3HC5-type (cd16817, aa 271-330), RING-type zinc finger (IPR001841, PF13920, PS50089, SM00184, aa 275-319), RING/FYVE/PHD-type superfamily (IPR013083, G3DSA:3.30.40.10, aa 253-326), E3 ubiquitin-protein ligase MGRN1/RNF157-like family (IPR045194, aa 1-343), and RNF157-like N-terminal domain (IPR058981, PF26192, aa 88-246). AlphaFold predicts medium-confidence structure (pLDDT 59.9, residues 1-679) consistent with partially structured protein containing ordered RING domain (aa 253-326) connected to extensive intrinsically disordered regions typical of regulatory E3 ligases. HPA shows brain-enriched expression (brain 73.6 nTPM, skeletal muscle 69.2 nTPM, retina 54.7 nTPM, tissue enhanced); localized to Golgi apparatus and vesicles; classified as enzyme (transferase) involved in Ubl conjugation pathway; cancer enhanced. Functional hypothesis: RNF157 functions as a Golgi-associated E3 ubiquitin ligase that catalyzes ubiquitin transfer from E2 conjugating enzymes to substrate proteins involved in neuronal development, dendrite extension, and apoptosis regulation. The C-terminal RING domain (aa 253-326) coordinates zinc ions via C3HC5 motif to form catalytic core for E2 binding and ubiquitin transfer. The N-terminal MGRN1-like domain (aa 88-246) mediates protein-protein interactions and substrate recognition. Brain enrichment (73.6 nTPM) reflects critical role in neuronal differentiation and synaptic development. BORCS8 interaction (0.478) suggests role in lysosomal trafficking via BLOC-1 complex. Golgi/vesicle localization indicates involvement in membrane protein trafficking and receptor turnover. Pathogenic CNVs disrupt ubiquitination machinery causing impaired neuronal development, altered apoptosis regulation, and potential neurodevelopmental disorders. Conservation across vertebrates (mouse 685 aa, rat 682 aa) confirms essential neuronal role.
