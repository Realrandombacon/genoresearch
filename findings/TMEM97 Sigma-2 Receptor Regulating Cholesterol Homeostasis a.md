## Description
TMEM97 encodes a 176-amino acid transmembrane protein functioning as the sigma-2 intracellular receptor (UniProt Q5BJF2). InterPro analysis reveals the EXPERA domain (IPR033118, PF05241, residues 43-161) characteristic of the expanded EBP superfamily involved in sterol sensing and membrane lipid regulation. The protein localizes to the endoplasmic reticulum according to HPA data, with highest expression in pancreas (303.3 nTPM) and liver (105.9 nTPM), consistent with roles in cholesterol biosynthesis. STRING interactions show high-confidence partnerships with NPC1 (0.978) - the Niemann-Pick C1 cholesterol transporter, SIGMAR1 (0.943) - the sigma-1 receptor ER chaperone, and TMBIM4 (0.830) - a transmembrane BAX inhibitor. Additional interactions with SREBF2 (0.748), the master transcriptional regulator of cholesterol synthesis, suggest TMEM97 operates within sterol homeostasis networks. ClinVar documents 6 pathogenic copy number gain variants on chromosome 17 affecting TMEM97, indicating clinical relevance though specific disease associations remain under investigation. Orthologs in mouse (Q8VD00) and bovine (Q3MHW7) share identical 176 aa length, demonstrating strong evolutionary conservation. AlphaFold structure prediction was unavailable (API error). FUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: TMEM97 acts as an ER-resident sigma-2 receptor that modulates cholesterol trafficking and biosynthesis through direct interaction with NPC1 and coordination with SREBF2 signaling. The EXPERA domain likely mediates sterol binding or membrane curvature sensing. MECHANISTIC PROPOSAL: TMEM97 forms a heteromeric complex with SIGMAR1 at ER membrane contact sites, where it senses luminal cholesterol levels and regulates NPC1-mediated lysosomal cholesterol export. Under ER stress conditions, TMEM97 may recruit chaperones to prevent protein aggregation, linking lipid metabolism to proteostasis. Dysregulation contributes to metabolic disease and potentially neurodegeneration via cholesterol trafficking defects.
