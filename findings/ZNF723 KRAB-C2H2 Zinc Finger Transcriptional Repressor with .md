# ZNF723: KRAB-C2H2 Zinc Finger Transcriptional Repressor with Testis-Enriched Expression

**Date:** 2026-03-23T00:30:11.881030

## Description
ZNF723 encodes a 513 amino acid Krüppel-associated box (KRAB) zinc finger protein functioning as a sequence-specific DNA-binding transcriptional repressor. The protein architecture features a canonical N-terminal KRAB domain (residues 3-75, IPR001909, PF01352) which recruits the corepressor KAP1 (TRIM28) complex to silence target genes via heterochromatin formation through H3K9me3 histone methylation. The C-terminal region contains multiple C2H2-type zinc finger arrays (IPR013087, PF00096) at positions 173-195, 201-223, 229-251, and 299-324 (PF13465), providing modular DNA recognition specificity for GC-rich promoter/enhancer sequences.

**Expression Profile**: Human Protein Atlas reveals testis-enriched expression (3.1 nTPM) with tissue-specific distribution, classified as "cancer enriched" and "cell type enhanced" in single-cell analysis. This expression pattern suggests potential roles in germ cell development or spermatogenesis, consistent with many KRAB-ZNF proteins that regulate transposable elements in the germline.

**Clinical Significance**: ClinVar documents 4 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all large copy number gains spanning 19p13.12-q13.31 regions (chr19:11,227,942-44,626,354 and overlapping intervals). These CNVs suggest dosage sensitivity, where ZNF723 amplification may contribute to transcriptional dysregulation of target genes or chromatin architecture disruption.

**HYPOTHESIS**: ZNF723 functions as a germline-specific transcriptional silencer targeting endogenous retroviral elements (ERVs) or developmentally regulated genes. The KRAB domain mediates heterochromatin recruitment via KAP1/HP1α interactions, while the tandem C2H2 zinc fingers confer DNA sequence specificity. Testis-enriched expression aligns with the known role of KRAB-ZNF proteins in piRNA pathway-mediated transposon silencing during spermatogenesis. Chromosomal 19p12 amplifications in cancer may lead to ZNF723 overexpression, causing aberrant silencing of tumor suppressor loci or cell cycle regulators.
