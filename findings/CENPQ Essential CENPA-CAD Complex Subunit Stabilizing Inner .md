# CENPQ: Essential CENPA-CAD Complex Subunit Stabilizing Inner Kinetochore Assembly and Chromosome Segregation with Cancer Prognostic Value

## Description
CENPQ (Centromere Protein Q) is a partially characterized kinetochore component with only 19 publications, emerging as a critical structural subunit of the CENPA-CAD (CENP-A Distal) complex that stabilizes inner kinetochore assembly during mitosis and shows prognostic value in hepatocellular carcinoma and esophageal squamous cell carcinoma. This 268-amino acid nuclear protein (UniProt Q7L2Z9) contains a conserved CENP-Q family domain (IPR025212, positions 1-268) within the CENPA-CAD centromere complex subunit family (PF13094, positions 116-266). AlphaFold predicts high-confidence structure (pLDDT 73.2), indicating a well-folded stable protein typical of constitutive centromere components that must maintain structural integrity throughout the cell cycle.\n\nSTRING interactions reveal CENPQ operates at the core of the constitutive centromere-associated network (CCAN) with exceptionally high confidence scores: CENPP (0.999), CENPO (0.999), CENPK (0.999), ITGB3BP/CENPQ-interacting protein (0.999), CENPN (0.999), CENPL (0.999), CENPI (0.999), CENPU (0.998), CENPH (0.997), and CENPM (0.994). This interaction network confirms CENPQ as an integral component of the 16-subunit CCAN complex that bridges CENPA-containing nucleosomes to outer kinetochore microtubule-binding machinery.\n\nHPA shows ubiquitous expression detected in all tissues with nucleoplasmic localization and cell type-enhanced specificity, consistent with essential housekeeping function in chromosome segregation. ClinVar contains 10 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number losses and gains on chromosome 6p12.3-21.1, though specific disease associations remain uncharacterized. Conservation across mouse (267 aa) and rat (270 aa) indicates strong functional constraint.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: CENPQ forms a heterotrimeric subcomplex with CENPP and CENPO that serves as a structural wedge within the CCAN, stabilizing the interface between CENPA nucleosomes and downstream kinetochore assembly. Unlike enzymatic kinetochore proteins, CENPQ likely functions as a rigid scaffold maintaining proper spacing and orientation of kinetochore components. The ITGB3BP interaction suggests potential cross-talk between kinetochore assembly and integrin signaling pathways, possibly coordinating cell division with adhesion status. Recent cancer biomarker studies indicate CENPQ overexpression correlates with poor prognosis in hepatocellular and esophageal carcinomas, suggesting elevated kinetochore stability may confer chromosomal instability tolerance in aggressive tumors. Pathogenic CNVs disrupting CENPQ dosage may cause mitotic errors leading to aneuploidy, potentially contributing to developmental disorders or cancer predisposition through defective chromosome segregation fidelity.
