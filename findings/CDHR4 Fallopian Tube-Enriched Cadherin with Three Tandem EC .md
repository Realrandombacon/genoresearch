## Description
CDHR4 (Cadherin-related family member 4, UniProt A6H8M9) is a 788-amino acid type I transmembrane protein located on chromosome 3p21.31 within the cadherin gene cluster. InterPro analysis reveals a canonical cadherin architecture with three tandem extracellular cadherin (EC) repeat domains (IPR002126/PF00028/SM00112, aa 237-338, 338-449, 444-554) forming the characteristic cadherin fold (G3DSA:2.60.40.60/SSF49313, aa 232-547) that mediates calcium-dependent homophilic cell-cell adhesion. The protein contains conserved cadherin signature motifs (PS00232, aa 538-548) critical for calcium coordination and adhesive function, classified within the cadherin domain-containing protein family (IPR050971/PTHR24025, aa 306-649). AlphaFold structure prediction achieves high confidence (pLDDT 78.6), indicating a well-folded stable extracellular region with properly arranged EC domains ready for calcium binding and intercellular adhesion. Human Protein Atlas reveals fallopian tube-enriched expression (55.8 nTPM) with tissue-enriched RNA specificity detected in some tissues, plus cancer-enriched expression and immune cell enhancement, suggesting roles in reproductive epithelial integrity and potential dysregulation in malignancy. STRING interaction network reveals 9 partners spanning adhesion, signaling, and trafficking functions: IP6K1 (0.527, inositol hexakisphosphate kinase regulating phosphate signaling), RNF123 (0.522, E3 ubiquitin ligase), CDH23 (0.482, protocadherin in stereocilia adhesion), TRAIP (0.466, TRAF-interacting protein), ZACN (0.452, zinc finger protein), MON1A (0.440, endosomal trafficking regulator), KNCN (0.432, keratinocyte-associated protein), CDHR2 (0.431, paralogous cadherin), and CAMKV (0.430, calmodulin kinase-like protein). This interactome positions CDHR4 within networks governing cell adhesion, membrane trafficking, and signal transduction. ClinVar contains 11 pathogenic variants including multiple copy-number gains and losses across 3p26.3-q29 and 3p21.31-21.2, deletions, and duplications, demonstrating dosage sensitivity where genomic rearrangements disrupting CDHR4 cause disease phenotypes. Conservation across birds (Amazon parrot A0A7L0M9J8, 742 aa; ostrich A0A7L1IW76, 708 aa; albatross Urocynchramus A0A7K5TEB8, 707 aa; razorbill A0A7K6ZBK5, 710 aa) indicates ancient evolutionary origin tied to epithelial adhesion. CDHR4 likely functions as a calcium-dependent homophilic adhesion receptor mediating cell-cell junctions in fallopian tube epithelium, potentially regulating ciliary function, oocyte transport, or epithelial barrier integrity through interactions with trafficking regulators and signaling kinases.
