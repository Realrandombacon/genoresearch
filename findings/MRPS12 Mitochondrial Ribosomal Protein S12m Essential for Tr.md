## Description
MRPS12 encodes a 138 aa mitochondrial ribosomal protein S12m (UniProt O15235), a critical structural and functional component of the mitochondrial 30S ribosomal subunit. InterPro analysis confirms its canonical ribosomal architecture: N-terminal OB-fold domain (IPR012340, aa 31-138) and central catalytic core (PF00164, aa 49-132), with conserved signature motifs (PS00055, aa 71-78) essential for mRNA decoding and tRNA binding. AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (pLDDT 86.4), indicating a stable, rigid conformation typical of ribosomal proteins required for precise translation. STRING interactions reveal a highly cohesive mitochondrial ribosome complex: MRPS9 (0.999), MRPS5 (0.999), MRPS11 (0.999), MRPS2 (0.999), MRPS10 (0.998), MRPS7 (0.998), MRPL17 (0.994), MRPL27 (0.985), MRPS25 (0.981), and MRPL32 (0.970), all with near-perfect confidence scores, confirming its role as a core structural scaffold. HPA expression shows low tissue specificity but universal detection ('Detected in all'), consistent with essential housekeeping functions in mitochondrial translation across all cell types. ClinVar reports 7 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including a frameshift c.74G>A (p.Trp25Ter) and multiple large 19q/19p copy number gains, suggesting dosage sensitivity; the p.Trp25Ter variant is particularly notable as it likely disrupts the decoding center, impairing translation fidelity. Literature (24 papers) details emerging mechanistic roles: (1) Ovarian Cancer Metastasis - MRPS12 overexpression correlating with poor prognosis and metastatic potential in ovarian cancer, potentially driving aberrant mitochondrial translation of oncogenic factors; (2) Aminoglycoside Sensitivity - specific mutations in MRPS12 (e.g., Leber hereditary optic neuropathy-associated) altering aminoglycoside binding affinity, causing resistance or hypersensitivity; (3) Mitochondrial Stress Response - NF-YA regulation of the bidirectional MRPS12/SARS promoter under stress, linking translation capacity to cellular energy homeostasis. Functional hypothesis: MRPS12 functions through: (1) Structural Assembly - maintaining the integrity of the 30S subunit to ensure accurate codon-anticodon recognition; (2) Fidelity Control - preventing misreading of mitochondrial mRNAs which are highly structured and prone to errors; (3) Oncogenic Drive - overexpression in ovarian cancer promoting the synthesis of mitochondrial proteins required for rapid proliferation and metastasis; (4) Drug Resistance - mutation-induced conformational changes altering the binding pocket for aminoglycosides. Disease implications include mitochondrial myopathies, hearing loss, Leber hereditary optic neuropathy (LHON), ovarian cancer prognosis, and aminoglycoside toxicity resistance. Despite 24 papers, the precise impact of p.Trp25Ter on mitochondrial translation kinetics and the full extent of MRPS12's role in ovarian cancer metastasis mechanisms remain underexplored.
