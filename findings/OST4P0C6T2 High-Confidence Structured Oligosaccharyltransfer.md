## Description
OST4 (Oligosaccharyltransferase complex subunit 4, non-catalytic, FinGER10) is a 37 amino acid membrane protein localized to the ER membrane and Golgi apparatus (UniProt P0C6T2) with very high-confidence structured architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 90.2) forming a compact, stable scaffold essential for the oligosaccharyltransferase (OST) complex. The protein contains the Oligosaccharyltransferase family domain (IPR018943/PF10215, residues 1-33) and the Oligosaccharyltransferase subunit ost4p superfamily (IPR036330/SSF103464, residues 1-35), which function as a catalytic platform for transferring oligosaccharides from dolichyl-phosphate to asparagine residues on nascent proteins during N-linked glycosylation. OST4 acts as a non-catalytic subunit, likely providing structural integrity to the OST complex (OSTA, OSTB, OSTC) and facilitating substrate recognition or complex assembly.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places OST4 at the core of a high-confidence trafficking and glycosylation machinery: strongest interaction with TMEM258 (0.999, Transmembrane protein 258, involved in ER-Golgi transport), suggesting a direct link between glycosylation and vesicular trafficking. High confidence links include DDOST (0.994, the catalytic core of the OST complex), DAD1 (0.993, a glycosyltransferase), and OSTC (0.986, another OST subunit), confirming its role in complex assembly. Notable connections to RPN2 (0.984, ER-associated degradation) and RPN1 (0.984, ERAD receptor) suggest a role in quality control, where misfolded glycoproteins are retro-translocated. Links to MAGT1 (0.875, Mannosyltransferase 1) and STT3A/B (0.994/0.944, S-tubulin chaperones) further integrate OST4 into lipid metabolism and protein folding networks.\n\nHPA expression shows low tissue specificity but universal detection ('Detected in all'), consistent with a fundamental housekeeping function in protein synthesis and folding. The 'cell type enhanced' specificity and 'low cancer specificity' suggest its role is critical for general cellular viability rather than specific oncogenic pathways, though dysregulation could impact secretory capacity in tumors.\n\nClinVar documents 15 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains affecting the 2p23.3-2p25.3 region (e.g., chr2:12771-35541353, chr2:12771-242783384) and one copy number loss. The high variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity. Haploinsufficiency would likely destabilize the OST complex, leading to defective N-glycosylation, accumulation of misfolded proteins, and activation of the unfolded protein response (UPR), potentially causing developmental delays or neurodegeneration. Duplications may saturate the glycosylation machinery or cause toxic gain-of-function via aberrant glycan processing.\n\nConservation is strict across eukaryotes: human (P0C6T2, 37 aa), mouse (Q99LX8, 37 aa), yeast (Q99380, 36 aa), indicating strong evolutionary pressure to maintain the OST scaffold.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: OST4 forms a stable, well-folded scaffold (pLDDT 90.2) that recruits DDOST and other OST subunits to the ER membrane, positioning the catalytic site for efficient N-glycosylation of secretory and membrane proteins. Its interaction with TMEM258 and RPN2 links glycosylation status to ER-Golgi trafficking and ERAD, ensuring that only properly glycosylated proteins proceed to the Golgi. Dysregulation via 2p CNVs disrupts this quality control, leading to proteostasis collapse and the phenotypes observed in 2p deletion/duplication syndromes.
