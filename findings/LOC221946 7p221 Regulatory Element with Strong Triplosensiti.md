# LOC221946: 7p22.1 Regulatory Element with Strong Triplosensitivity in Critical Developmental Region

## Description
LOC221946 is an uncharacterized genomic locus on chromosome 7p22.1 (genomic coordinates: 5513853-5525544 on NC_000007.14, spanning approximately 11.7 kb). This region lacks protein-coding evidence (no UniProt entry, no STRING interactions, no HPA protein expression data), indicating it is likely a non-coding regulatory element, long non-coding RNA, or structural genomic region rather than a protein-coding gene. ClinVar analysis identified 20 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants with strong duplication-predominant pathogenicity: 19 copy number gains (duplications) and 1 copy number loss (deletion), indicating extreme triplosensitivity where increased copy number is the primary pathogenic mechanism. The 7p22 chromosomal region is critical for development and contains dosage-sensitive genes including ACTB (beta-actin - cytoskeleton, Baraitser-Winter syndrome), ARPC1B (actin-related protein - immune deficiency, thrombocytopenia), C7orf55, and multiple developmental regulators. The extreme duplication bias (95% gains vs 5% losses) is the strongest triplosensitivity signal observed in this research series, contrasting with LOC101928211 (70% deletions), LOC100287704 (67% duplications), and balanced regions like LOC101928283. This suggests LOC221946 functions as a triplosensitivity-critical cis-regulatory element where precise dosage is essential. Gain of this element likely causes overexpression of target genes in cis, contributing to 7p22 duplication syndrome phenotypes including developmental delay, intellectual disability, dysmorphic features, and potential immune/thrombocytopenia manifestations (via ARPC1B pathway). This 11.7 kb element may represent a potent enhancer cluster or chromatin boundary that regulates ACTB or ARPC1B expression during embryonic development.
