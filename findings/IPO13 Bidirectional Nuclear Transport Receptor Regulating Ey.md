# IPO13: Bidirectional Nuclear Transport Receptor Regulating Eye Development, Insulin Secretion, and Neuronal Differentiation via RAN-GTPase Cycling

## Description
IPO13 (Importin-13, UniProt O94829) is a 963-amino acid bidirectional nuclear transport receptor belonging to the importin-beta superfamily, uniquely capable of mediating both nuclear import and export of specific cargo proteins through RAN-GTPase dependent mechanisms. The protein architecture features an N-terminal importin-beta domain (IPR001494, PF03810, aa 45-111) mediating RAN-GTP binding and cargo release, followed by extensive armadillo-type helical repeats (IPR011989, IPR016024, G3DSA:1.25.10.10, aa 72-957) forming the characteristic curved solenoid structure that wraps around cargo proteins, and four specialized TPR-like repeat domains unique to Importin-13/Transportin-3 subfamily (IPR057941-IPR058537, aa 301-762) conferring cargo specificity. The AlphaFold predicted structure shows very high confidence (pLDDT 90.5), indicating a well-folded stable solenoid architecture optimal for cargo binding and conformational changes during transport. STRING interaction network places IPO13 within the nuclear transport and RNA processing machinery with high-confidence associations to: RAN (0.988), Ras-related nuclear protein GTPase driving directionality of nucleocytoplasmic transport; EIF1AX (0.981), eukaryotic translation initiation factor 1A (X-linked) the known export cargo; RBM8A (0.972), RNA binding motif protein 8A component of exon junction complex; UBE2I (0.942), SUMO-conjugating enzyme E2 mediating SUMOylation of transport complexes; MAGOH (0.938) and MAGOHB (0.846), mago nashi homologs forming exon junction complex with RBM8A; BAIAP3 (0.813), BAI1-associated protein 3; KCTD2 (0.804), potassium channel tetramerization domain containing 2; PAX6 (0.804), paired box protein 6 critical eye development transcription factor; and XPO4 (0.559), exportin-4 paralogous export receptor. HPA expression profiles demonstrate tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in skeletal muscle (121.1 nTPM), ubiquitous detection across all tissues indicating housekeeping transport function, low cancer specificity suggesting normal physiological role, classification as essential protein and transporter, nucleocytoplasmic localization consistent with shuttling receptor function. ClinVar records document 10 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number losses on 1p34.1-34.2 (40.5-45.0 Mb), deletions spanning 44.2-44.5 Mb, inversions, and large gains on 1p36.33-q44; these mutations cause autosomal recessive ocular coloboma, microphthalmia, and cataract syndrome affecting eye development through disrupted PAX6 nuclear import. Literature analysis (53 papers) reveals multi-context functionality: IPO13 maintains glucose homeostasis by regulating insulin secretion and pancreatic α-cell expansion through unknown cargo trafficking; serves as central mediator of efficient neuronal differentiation potentially via neurogenic transcription factor transport; mediates nuclear export of eIF1A linking translation initiation to nuclear events; responds to oxidative stress in embryonic stem cells altering transcriptional profiles; interacts with SUMO pathway (UBE2I) suggesting regulation by post-translational modification; and respiratory syncytial virus nonstructural proteins may hijack IPO13-mediated transport. We hypothesize that IPO13 functions through a bidirectional transport mechanism: (1) For nuclear import, cytoplasmic IPO13 binds cargo proteins (potentially PAX6, neurogenic factors, metabolic regulators) via armadillo repeats, translocates through nuclear pore complex, and releases cargo upon RAN-GTP binding in the nucleus; (2) For nuclear export, nuclear IPO13 binds eIF1A and other export cargos, translocates to cytoplasm where RAN-GTP hydrolysis triggers release; (3) In pancreatic β-cells, IPO13 may traffic insulin granule components or transcription factors (PDX1, MAFA) regulating insulin secretion; (4) In eye development, IPO13-PAX6 interaction ensures proper nuclear accumulation of this master regulator for optic cup formation; (5) The four unique TPR domains may confer specificity for distinct cargo subsets compared to other importins; (6) SUMOylation via UBE2I may regulate IPO13 stability, localization, or cargo affinity; (7) In neurons, IPO13 may transport activity-dependent transcription factors (CREB, MEF2) enabling differentiation programs. The very high-confidence structure suggests potential for small molecule inhibitors targeting the cargo-binding groove for therapeutic modulation in diabetes or ocular disorders.
