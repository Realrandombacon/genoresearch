## Description
CEP135 (Centrosomal Protein 135 kDa, UniProt Q66GS9) is a 1140 aa coiled-coil structural protein belonging to the centriole and basal body structural protein family (IPR051877/PTHR20544, positions 1-1137) that functions in centriole duplication, microtubule nucleation, and sperm flagellum assembly. The molecular architecture consists of an N-terminal coiled-coil microtubule binding domain (cd22292, positions 81-142) for direct microtubule interaction, followed by extensive coiled-coil regions (G3DSA:1.10.287.1490, positions 195-338 and 650-813) forming an elongated scaffold for centrosome protein assembly. Literature (111 papers) establishes CEP135 roles in: (1) CEP135 loss causing oligoasthenoteratozoospermia and male infertility in mice through defective sperm centriole formation (2025), (2) LZTS2 negatively regulating CEP135 levels and microtubule nucleation at centrosomes (2025), (3) CP110 and CEP135 localizing near proximal centriolar remnants in mouse spermatozoa (2024), (4) CEP135+ inflammatory endothelial subtype associated with glioma progression (2026), (5) CEP135 mutations causing primary microcephaly and intellectual disability. HPA confirms low tissue specificity with detection in many tissues, disease involvement in intellectual disability and primary microcephaly, and subcellular localization to sperm structures (acrosome, mid piece, principal piece, annulus), consistent with centrosome and flagellum functions. STRING interactions reveal centriole duplication network: SASS6 (0.999) - SAS-6 homolog for centriole cartwheel assembly; CENPJ (0.996) - centromere protein J/CPAP for microtubule binding; PLK4 (0.988) - polo-like kinase 4, master regulator of centriole duplication; CEP152 (0.968) - centrosomal protein 152 for PLK4 recruitment; CCP110 (0.964) - centriolar coiled-coil protein 110 for distal end capping; STIL (0.963) - SCL/TAL1 interrupting locus for cartwheel assembly; CEP295 (0.936) - centrosomal protein 295 for procentriole formation; PLK1 (0.905) - polo-like kinase 1 for cell cycle regulation; CNTLN (0.887) - centrin-2 for centrosome function; WRAP73 (0.862) - WD repeat-containing protein 73 for centrosome integrity. ClinVar contains 102 pathogenic variants including nonsense mutations (p.Gln469Ter, p.Arg418Ter, p.Tyr456Ter, p.Ser496Ter, p.Arg1109Ter), frameshift deletions (p.Glu88fs, p.Ser483fs, p.Lys409fs, p.Asn834fs), and splice variants (c.305-2A>T), causing primary microcephaly, intellectual disability, and male infertility phenotypes. AlphaFold shows high-confidence structure (pLDDT 76.9) indicating well-folded elongated coiled-coil architecture typical of centrosomal scaffold proteins. Conservation: mouse Cep135 (Q6P5D4) is 1140 aa, Drosophila Cep135 (Q960T6) is 1059 aa - highly conserved across metazoans. Functional hypothesis: CEP135 functions as a structural scaffold that bridges the centriole cartwheel to the microtubule wall, stabilizing procentriole assembly during duplication and anchoring microtubules for nucleation. The mechanistic model proposes: (1) CEP135 N-terminal coiled-coil domain binds SASS6 cartwheel hub at centriole proximal end, (2) central coiled-coil regions form elongated rod connecting cartwheel to CENPJ/CPAP microtubule-binding complex at distal end, (3) CEP135-CEP152-PLK4 complex regulates centriole duplication licensing during S phase, (4) CEP135 stabilizes microtubule triplets in sperm flagellum basal body, essential for sperm motility, (5) LZTS2-mediated CEP135 degradation controls microtubule nucleation capacity. In primary microcephaly, CEP135 loss-of-function reduces neural progenitor centrosome number, causing premature cell cycle exit and reduced brain size. In male infertility, CEP135 deficiency disrupts sperm flagellum assembly, causing asthenoteratozoospermia. In glioma, CEP135+ endothelial cells promote tumor angiogenesis through inflammatory signaling. Pathogenic variants truncate the coiled-coil scaffold, disrupting centrosome integrity and microtubule anchoring. CEP135 represents an understudied centrosomal scaffold linking centriole biogenesis to neurodevelopment and reproduction.
