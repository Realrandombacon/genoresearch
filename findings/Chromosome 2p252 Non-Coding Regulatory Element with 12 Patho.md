## Description

Functional hypothesis: LOC400940 likely represents a critical regulatory element within the 2p25.2 dosage-sensitive region, potentially functioning as a neural progenitor-specific enhancer coordinating SOX11 expression in developing cortex (neurogenesis, neuronal migration) or as an interferon-responsive enhancer coordinating RSAD2/CMPK2 expression in immune cells (antiviral defense, innate immunity). The 12 pathogenic CNVs indicate extreme regional dosage sensitivity where altered copy number disrupts multiple gene expression programs simultaneously. Disease mechanism involves SOX11 haploinsufficiency causing Coffin-Siris syndrome (developmental delay, hypoplastic fifth fingernail, coarse facies, intellectual disability), SOX11 overexpression driving mantle cell lymphoma oncogenesis, RSAD2 deficiency impairing antiviral restriction (viral susceptibility), RSAD2 overexpression causing excessive inflammation (autoimmunity), CMPK2 dysfunction altering mitochondrial nucleotide pools (metabolic defects, immune dysfunction), NRIR loss causing interferon hyperactivation (autoimmune disease, interferonopathy), and MIR7515 loss promoting tumorigenesis. The 2p25.2 deletion syndrome presents with developmental delay, intellectual disability, and dysmorphic features. The 2p25.2 duplication syndrome may present with neurodevelopmental abnormalities, immune dysregulation, and cancer predisposition. The absence of protein-coding features combined with ClinVar evidence (12 CNVs ranging 347 kb-55.6 Mb) and SOX11-RSAD2-CMPK2-NRIR-MIR7515 gene cluster suggests this locus functions as a tissue-specific super-enhancer or topologically associating domain (TAD) boundary critical for neurodevelopment-interferon-nucleotide metabolism coordination.
