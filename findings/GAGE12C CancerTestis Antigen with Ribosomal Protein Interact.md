FUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: GAGE12C functions as a cancer/testis antigen (CTA) that regulates ribosome biogenesis and protein translation in germ cells and is aberrantly reactivated in tumors. The GAGE domain (aa 1-117) defines a family of immunogenic tumor antigens normally expressed only in immune-privileged testis germ cells but silenced in somatic tissues. The extraordinary interaction pattern with ribosomal proteins (RPL5, RPL11, RPL15, RPL19, RPL34, RPS11, RPS15, RPS18) and FAU (ubiquitin-ribosomal fusion protein) suggests GAGE12C may: (1) regulate ribosome assembly or stability through direct binding to ribosomal subunits, (2) modulate translation efficiency in germ cells during spermatogenesis, (3) function as a ribosome quality control factor, or (4) serve as an adaptor linking ribosomes to ubiquitin-mediated degradation pathways via FAU interaction. The testis-enriched expression (22.1 nTPM) confirms canonical CTA pattern. Cancer-enhanced expression indicates transcriptional reactivation in tumors through epigenetic mechanisms (DNA hypomethylation, histone modifications) characteristic of CTAs. The 155 ClinVar variants (chromosome X CNVs and deletions) suggest GAGE12C locus instability may contribute to disease through dosage effects on ribosome function or immune recognition. The partially structured AlphaFold prediction (pLDDT 63.2) supports flexible conformational states enabling multiple protein-protein interactions. GAGE12C represents an immunotherapeutic target candidate as CTAs are highly immunogenic and tumor-specific. The ribosomal protein interaction network suggests unexplored roles in translational control during germ cell development and tumorigenesis. Experimental priorities include: validating ribosome binding (co-IP, ribosome profiling), determining crystal structure of GAGE domain, assessing immunogenicity (T-cell epitope mapping), and evaluating therapeutic vaccine potential in GAGE12C-positive cancers.
