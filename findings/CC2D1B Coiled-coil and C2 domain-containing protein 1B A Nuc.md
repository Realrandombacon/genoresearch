# CC2D1B (Coiled-coil and C2 domain-containing protein 1B): A Nuclear Envelope Remodeling Factor with ESCRT-III Activity

## Description
CC2D1B is a well-characterized transcriptional repressor and nuclear envelope remodeling factor, characterized by its ability to coordinate ESCRT-III complex activity during mitotic NE reformation. The gene encodes an 858 amino acid protein featuring two distinct structural domains: (1) A C-terminal Freud-like domain containing a conserved C2 motif (IPR037772; PF00168), which mediates DNA binding and transcriptional repression activity, specifically targeting RNA polymerase II-specific promoters (GO:0001227); (2) An N-terminal coiled-coil domain with DM14-like motifs (IPR006608; PF21528), facilitating protein-protein interactions. Functional evidence includes its critical role in coordinating ESCRT-III assembly to re-establish nuclear envelope integrity post-mitosis, a process essential for cellular viability and preventing genomic instability. CC2D1B interacts with key cytoskeletal and endosomal proteins including CHMP4C (STRING score: 0.831), CHMP4A (0.777), IST1 (0.712), and DPP9, suggesting a role in vesicular trafficking alongside nuclear envelope dynamics. Expression is ubiquitous but enhanced in immune cells (HPA data). Clinically significant pathogenic variants exist on chromosome 1p36-p32, associated with intellectual disability syndromes despite lacking specific disease annotations in current databases. Conservation analysis shows high identity (>85%) between human and mouse orthologs, indicating essential evolutionary conservation of its nuclear envelope function.
