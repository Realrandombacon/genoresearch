## Description
LOC400541 is an uncharacterized genomic locus on chromosome 16q22.1 (NC_000016.10:70173447-70156339) spanning approximately 17.1 kb. Comprehensive database searches reveal no evidence of protein-coding function: no UniProt accession was identified, precluding InterPro domain analysis and AlphaFold structure prediction. STRING database returned no protein interaction partners, confirming absence of canonical protein-coding function. Human Protein Atlas could not resolve this locus to an Ensembl ID, indicating no detectable protein expression in surveyed tissues. However, ClinVar analysis reveals 23 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants in this region, comprising predominantly copy number gains (duplications: chr16:46432879-90294753, ~43.9 Mb whole-arm trisomy; chr16:62746020-84485022, ~21.7 Mb; chr16:46503968-90155062, ~43.7 Mb; chr16:61524229-90155062, ~28.6 Mb; chr16:54416050-72453266, ~18.0 Mb; chr16:85881-90155062, ~90.1 Mb whole-chromosome) and copy number losses (deletions: chr16:69709450-73535741, ~3.8 Mb; chr16:68971067-74823560, ~5.9 Mb). These structural variants encompass the 16q22 region and are associated with 16q22 microdeletion syndrome and 16q22 microduplication syndrome featuring developmental delay, intellectual disability, craniofacial dysmorphism, cardiac defects, and skeletal anomalies. The 16q22.1 region contains several dosage-sensitive genes including CDH1 (cadherin-1/E-cadherin, a tumor suppressor critical for cell adhesion and epithelial integrity), MLYCD (malonyl-CoA decarboxylase, essential for fatty acid metabolism), and MPHOSPH9 (M-phase phosphoprotein 9, involved in centrosome function and cell cycle regulation). LOC400541 likely functions as part of the 16q22.1 cis-regulatory landscape or encodes a non-coding RNA whose copy number alteration contributes to 16q22 syndrome pathogenesis through disruption of chromatin topology, topologically associating domain (TAD) boundaries, or long-range regulation of dosage-sensitive genes like CDH1 and MLYCD. The absence of protein-coding features combined with a high burden of pathogenic structural variants (23 variants) indicates this locus resides within a critical dosage-sensitive genomic region where both deletions and duplications disrupt developmental processes via gene dosage effects and 3D genome reorganization.
