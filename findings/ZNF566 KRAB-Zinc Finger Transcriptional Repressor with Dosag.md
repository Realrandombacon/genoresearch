## Description
ZNF566 (Gene ID: 84924, UniProt Q969W8) encodes a 418 aa C2H2-type zinc finger transcription factor mapping to chromosome 19q13.12 (36.5-36.4 Mbp). Multiple evidence lines support classification as a KRAB-domain repressor with emerging disease relevance: (1) InterPro domains confirm canonical KRAB-ZFP architecture: KRAB box (PF01352, aa 5-46) for transcriptional repression via KAP1/TRIM28 recruitment, plus 3+ C2H2 zinc fingers (PF00096, aa 199-333) for sequence-specific DNA binding; (2) AlphaFold structure shows medium confidence (pLDDT=68.6), consistent with structured zinc finger domains flanked by flexible linker regions; (3) HPA data confirms ubiquitous expression across all tissues with nuclear localization and transcription factor classification; (4) ClinVar contains 11 pathogenic copy number variants (large 19q deletions/duplications spanning 8-30 Mb), linking ZNF566 dosage to chromosomal instability syndromes; (5) STRING interactions failed (gene not in database)—typical for understudied ZNFs. Mechanistic hypothesis: ZNF566 functions as a sequence-specific transcriptional repressor that recruits the KAP1/TRIM28/SETDB1 heterochromatin complex to silence target genes via H3K9me3 deposition. The KRAB domain mediates protein-protein interactions while C2H2 fingers recognize degenerate DNA motifs (likely 5-GTGT-3 variants). Dosage sensitivity suggests ZNF566 regulates critical developmental or cell-cycle genes requiring precise expression levels. As a member of the primate-specific KRAB-ZFP expansion (~400 human genes), ZNF566 may have evolved to repress endogenous retroviruses or regulate placental development. This represents a "dark" transcription factor—annotated but functionally uncharacterized regarding target genes and biological pathways.
