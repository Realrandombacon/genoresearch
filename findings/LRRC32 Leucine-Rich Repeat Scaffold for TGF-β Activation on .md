# LRRC32: Leucine-Rich Repeat Scaffold for TGF-β Activation on Regulatory T Cells

## Description
LRRC32 (Leucine Rich Repeat Containing 32), also known as GARP (Glycoprotein A Repetitions Predominant), encodes a 662 amino acid type I membrane protein (UniProt Q14392) characterized by an extensive extracellular domain containing 20 leucine-rich repeats (LRRs). InterPro analysis identifies multiple LRR motifs (IPR000372, IPR001611, PF01462, PF13855) spanning residues 19-428, forming a solenoid structure typical of protein-protein interaction scaffolds.

AlphaFold prediction yields a high-confidence structure (global pLDDT 86.1), confirming the stability of the LRR solenoid which likely serves as a binding platform. HPA data indicates immune cell-enhanced expression, specifically localized to the nucleoplasm in resting states but functioning as a membrane protein upon activation. The protein is classified under "Disease related genes" with involvement in intellectual disability.

ClinVar reports 12 pathogenic variants, including copy number gains/losses on 11q13 and truncating mutations (e.g., p.Arg544Ter), suggesting dosage sensitivity and functional criticality. While STRING interactions timed out, the established biological role of GARP is as a high-affinity receptor for latent TGF-β1 and TGF-β3 on the surface of regulatory T cells (Tregs).

FUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: LRRC32 functions as a critical co-receptor that presents latent TGF-β complexes to integrins (such as αVβ8 or αVβ6) on neighboring cells, facilitating the conformational change required for TGF-β activation. The LRR domain provides the structural rigidity necessary to hold the latent complex in an activation-competent orientation. Dysregulation of LRRC32 disrupts the immunosuppressive function of Tregs, leading to autoimmunity or inflammatory disorders, while germline variants may contribute to neurodevelopmental phenotypes via dysregulated TGF-β signaling in the CNS.
