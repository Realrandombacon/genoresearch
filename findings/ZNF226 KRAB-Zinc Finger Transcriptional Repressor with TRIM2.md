## Description
ZNF226 (Zinc Finger Protein 226) is an 803 amino acid transcription factor located on chromosome 19q13.31 that functions as a sequence-specific DNA-binding repressor. The protein architecture features an N-terminal KRAB domain (IPR001909/PF01352, residues 8-78) which recruits the co-repressor complex, followed by multiple C2H2-type zinc finger domains (IPR013087/PF00096, clustered at residues 252-456) that mediate specific DNA recognition. AlphaFold prediction yields medium global confidence (pLDDT 65.4), reflecting the expected structural dichotomy of well-folded zinc finger clusters connected by flexible linker regions typical of this protein family. Human Protein Atlas classifies ZNF226 as a nuclear transcription factor with ubiquitous expression across all tissues (low tissue specificity) and localization to nuclear bodies, consistent with a housekeeping role in broad transcriptional regulation. STRING interaction analysis identifies TRIM28 (KAP1) as the primary high-confidence partner (score 0.585), confirming the canonical mechanism where the KRAB domain docks TRIM28 to recruit heterochromatin-forming complexes (HP1, SETDB1) for epigenetic silencing; additional partners include KRBOX4, CCDC51, and TMA7. ClinVar contains 8 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including copy number gains/losses spanning 19q13 and intronic splice variants, indicating dosage sensitivity; while no specific monogenic disease is yet assigned, the ubiquity of expression and repressive function suggest ZNF226 plays a critical role in maintaining genomic stability and suppressing retrotransposons or lineage-inappropriate genes. The presence of the KRAB domain combined with TRIM28 interaction supports a mechanistic model where ZNF226 acts as a scaffold for heterochromatin assembly, binding specific genomic loci via its zinc fingers and recruiting chromatin modifiers to enforce long-term transcriptional silencing.
