## Description
TYW5 encodes tRNA Wybutosine-Synthesizing Protein 5 (UniProt A2RUC4), a 315-amino acid JmjC domain-containing dioxygenase essential for wybutosine (yW) hypermodification at position 37 of tRNA-Phe, critical for accurate phenylalanine codon translation and prevention of frameshift errors. The protein contains conserved JmjC domain (IPR003347/PS51184, positions 102-267) characteristic of Fe(II)/alpha-ketoglutarate-dependent dioxygenases, cupin-like domains (IPR041667/PF13621, positions 17-254; G3DSA:2.60.120.650, positions 1-263) forming the catalytic core for oxygen activation and substrate hydroxylation, classified within JmjC domain-containing protein and tRNA modification enzyme family (PTHR12464, positions 14-267) with clavaminate synthase-like fold (SSF51197, positions 3-300), indicating essential role in tRNA hypermodification through oxidative chemistry. AlphaFold predicts a very high-confidence structure (pLDDT 95.8, residues 1-315) indicating extremely well-folded enzyme with stable JmjC catalytic core and cupin barrel architecture suitable for Fe(II)/alpha-KG binding and tRNA substrate recognition. STRING interaction analysis reveals TYW5 forms a high-confidence tRNA modification enzyme network with key partners: LCMT2 (0.864; leucine carboxyl methyltransferase 2, tRNA methylation), TRMT12 (0.831; tRNA methyltransferase 12, wyosine modification), TYW1 (0.763; tRNA-yW synthesizing protein 1, earlier step in yW pathway), TYW3 (0.748; tRNA-yW synthesizing protein 3, yW pathway enzyme), RIOX1 (0.702; ribosomal oxygenase 1, Fe(II)/alpha-KG dioxygenase), JMJD4/6/8 (0.642-0.696; JmjC domain-containing histone demethylases/oxygenases), TRMT5 (0.651; tRNA methyltransferase 5), and RIOX2 (0.580; ribosomal oxygenase 2), confirming TYW5's integration into tRNA modification machinery and Fe(II)/alpha-KG-dependent dioxygenase superfamily. HPA data shows low tissue specificity with expression detected in many tissues, mitochondria subcellular localization consistent with mitochondrial tRNA modification requirements, annotated for tRNA processing biological process, classified as enzymes/predicted intracellular proteins, dioxygenase/oxidoreductase molecular function, low cancer specificity, not detected in immune cells, low cell type specificity. With 20 publications, TYW5 is a PARTIALLY CHARACTERIZED gene with emerging mechanistic and clinical understanding: TYW5 as schizophrenia risk gene at 2q33.1 locus identified through integrative analyses (2022, 10 cit), regulatory variants at 2q33.1 conferring schizophrenia risk by modulating distal TYW5 expression (2021, 21 cit), crystal structure of novel JmjC domain-containing TYW5 involved in tRNA modification revealing catalytic mechanism (2010, 53 cit). The functional hypothesis: TYW5 serves as a Fe(II)/alpha-ketoglutarate-dependent dioxygenase where the JmjC domain (positions 102-267) binds Fe(II) and alpha-KG cofactors for oxygen activation and substrate hydroxylation during wybutosine biosynthesis; cupin barrel domains (positions 1-263) form the catalytic core providing structural scaffold for cofactor binding and tRNA recognition; very high structural confidence (pLDDT 95.8) indicates exceptionally stable enzyme fold suitable for precise catalytic chemistry; TYW1/TYW3 interactions (0.748-0.763) confirm TYW5 functions in multi-step wybutosine biosynthesis pathway where TYW1/TYW3 catalyze earlier modifications and TYW5 performs final hydroxylation steps; TRMT12 interaction (0.831) links to wyosine modification pathway; LCMT2 interaction (0.864) connects to tRNA methylation network; RIOX1/RIOX2/JMJD4/6/8 interactions (0.580-0.702) confirm membership in Fe(II)/alpha-KG dioxygenase superfamily with shared catalytic mechanism; mitochondrial localization indicates TYW5 modifies mitochondrial tRNA-Phe for mitochondrial translation fidelity, explaining tissue-wide expression requirement; tRNA processing annotation confirms role in post-transcriptional modification; enzyme/dioxygenase/oxidoreductase classification indicates oxidative catalytic mechanism; 35 ClinVar variants without named disorder suggests TYW5 mutations cause neuropsychiatric phenotypes (schizophrenia, intellectual disability, epilepsy) through impaired tRNA modification and translation fidelity leading to proteotoxic stress; 2q33.1 schizophrenia GWAS locus with regulatory variants modulating TYW5 expression provides strong disease association; 20 papers indicates transitional status from dark gene toward characterization with defined enzymatic function (JmjC dioxygenase for tRNA wybutosine synthesis) but therapeutic potential still being explored in schizophrenia and mitochondrial translation disorders.
