## Description
CCDC22 encodes a 627 amino acid intracellular scaffold protein (UniProt O60826) featuring extensive coiled-coil domains (IPR008530/PF05667, residues 128-597) that mediate protein-protein interactions within multi-subunit complexes, plus an N-terminal domain (IPR048349/PF21674, residues 1-109) of unknown specific function. The protein belongs to the JM1 protein family (PTHR15668) with annotated functions in protein transport and vesicular trafficking. The AlphaFold predicted structure shows high confidence folding (global pLDDT 78.6) across the full-length protein, consistent with stable coiled-coil helical bundles typical of scaffold proteins that organize multi-protein complexes. Human Protein Atlas reveals ubiquitous expression detected in all tissues with low tissue specificity, classified as a transporter localized to endoplasmic reticulum and vesicles, with disease association to intellectual disability. STRING interaction network identifies 10 high-confidence partners forming a coherent functional module: CCDC93 (score 0.999, coiled-coil domain-containing protein 93 — the obligate heterodimerization partner), COMMD8 (0.986), COMMD2 (0.984), COMMD3 (0.980), COMMD6 (0.955), COMMD1 (0.953), COMMD4 (0.917), COMMD10 (0.862) — all members of the COMMD (Copper metabolism MURR1 domain) protein family, plus VPS26C (0.845, vacuolar protein sorting-associated protein 26C — retromer complex component) and VPS35L (0.793, retromer complex subunit). This interaction pattern definitively places CCDC22 within the CCC (COMMD/CCDC22/CCDC93) complex, a recently characterized endosomal trafficking regulator that interfaces with the retromer complex to control cargo recycling from endosomes to the trans-Golgi network and plasma membrane. ClinVar contains 195 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including missense mutations (e.g., p.His295Gln, p.Val49Ala, p.Lys289Arg, p.Arg436Gly, p.Leu374Pro) and splice site variants distributed across the coiled-coil domain region, suggesting disruption of CCC complex assembly or stability causes disease through defective endosomal trafficking potentially affecting neuronal development given the intellectual disability phenotype. The combination of ubiquitous ER/vesicle localization, coiled-coil scaffold architecture, definitive CCC complex membership via COMMD and CCDC93 interactions, retromer complex association via VPS26C/VPS35L, and 195 disease-causing variants suggests CCDC22 functions as an essential structural scaffold that nucleates CCC complex assembly to regulate endosomal cargo sorting and trafficking, with haploinsufficiency or dominant-negative mutations disrupting neuronal protein homeostasis leading to intellectual disability.
