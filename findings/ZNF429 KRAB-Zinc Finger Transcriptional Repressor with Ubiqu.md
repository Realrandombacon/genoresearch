## Description
ZNF429 encodes a 674 aa Kruppel-associated box (KRAB) zinc finger protein (UniProt Q86V71) functioning as a sequence-specific transcriptional repressor. The protein contains an N-terminal KRAB-A domain (PF01352, IPR001909, aa 3-75) that recruits the corepressor TRIM28/KAP1 to initiate heterochromatin formation, followed by multiple C2H2 zinc finger motifs (PF00096, IPR013087, aa 173-279 and beyond) for specific DNA binding. AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 72.5) indicates a confident global fold with structured zinc finger clusters and a defined KRAB domain, suggesting stable DNA-protein interactions. HPA localizes ZNF429 to the nucleoplasm with ubiquitous expression across all tissues (low tissue specificity), consistent with a housekeeping role in maintaining basal transcriptional repression. STRING interactions reveal a functional association with TRIM28 (0.532), the canonical cofactor for KRAB-ZFPs that recruits SETDB1 and HP1 to deposit H3K9me3 marks. Additional partners include CYP20A1 (0.516), GK5 (0.511), and ICA1L (0.502), though their specific roles in ZNF429 complexes remain unclear. ClinVar documents 7 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, exclusively large copy number gains (triplications) on chromosome 19p-q encompassing the ZNF429 locus, suggesting dosage sensitivity where overexpression may disrupt chromatin architecture or silencing of target genes. The combination of ubiquitous expression, nuclear localization, and TRIM28 interaction supports a mechanism where ZNF429 binds specific genomic loci to recruit repressive chromatin machinery, potentially silencing retrotransposons or lineage-inappropriate genes to maintain cellular identity.
