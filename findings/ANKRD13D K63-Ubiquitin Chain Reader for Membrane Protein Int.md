## Description
ANKRD13D encodes a 605-amino acid integral membrane protein (UniProt Q6ZTN6) functioning as a ubiquitin chain reader that targets membrane proteins for endocytosis. The protein contains three defining structural features: (1) N-terminal ankyrin repeat domain (IPR002110, PF12796, residues 13-101) mediating protein-protein interactions; (2) three C-terminal ubiquitin-interacting motifs (UIMs, IPR003903, PS50330, SM00726 at residues 482-501, 528-547, 564-583) that specifically bind Lys-63-linked polyubiquitin chains; and (3) a family-specific C-terminal domain (PF11904, IPR055285, residues 155-469) of unknown function. AlphaFold predicts a well-folded structure (pLDDT 72.8) spanning all 605 residues. STRING interaction network reveals 10 high-confidence partners including UBA52 (score 0.797, ubiquitin-60S ribosomal protein L40 fusion), TEPSIN (0.644, epsin-related trafficking factor), LRRC73 (0.625), and SLC12A9 (0.546, solute carrier), suggesting roles in vesicular trafficking and membrane protein sorting. HPA classifies ANKRD13D as an intracellular protein with low tissue specificity but widespread expression across many tissues. ClinVar documents 13 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variants (duplications, deletions, gains/losses) though no specific disease phenotypes are yet assigned. The mechanistic hypothesis: ANKRD13D acts as an endosomal membrane adaptor that recognizes K63-ubiquitinated cargo proteins via its triple UIM array, recruiting them into internalization vesicles through ankyrin-mediated scaffolding interactions with trafficking machinery (TEPSIN, UBA52). This positions ANKRD13D as a specificity factor in the ubiquitin-dependent endocytic pathway, potentially regulating receptor downregulation, nutrient transporter turnover, or immune receptor signaling termination.
