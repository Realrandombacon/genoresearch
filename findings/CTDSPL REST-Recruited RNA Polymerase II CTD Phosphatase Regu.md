# CTDSPL: REST-Recruited RNA Polymerase II CTD Phosphatase Regulating Neuronal Gene Silencing and Cell Cycle

**Date:** 2026-03-18T18:33:01.551998

**Quality Score:** 3.48/10  (E=5.15, D=1.8) [WEAK]

## Description
CTDSPL (CTD small phosphatase-like protein, UniProt O15194) encodes a 276-amino acid phosphatase that functions as an epigenetic regulator of neuronal gene expression and cell cycle control. The protein contains a catalytic HAD (haloacid dehalogenase) superfamily phosphatase domain (IPR004274, IPR011948, PF03031, residues 102-270) that confers RNA polymerase II CTD heptapeptide repeat phosphatase activity (GO:0008420), preferentially dephosphorylating Ser-5 residues of the CTD heptad repeats. CTDSPL is recruited by the transcriptional repressor REST (RE1-silencing transcription factor) to neuronal genes containing RE-1 silencing elements, mediating epigenetic silencing in non-neuronal cells to prevent aberrant neuronal gene expression. STRING interaction data reveals high-confidence partnerships with chromatin regulators CTDP1 (0.749), CTDSP1 (0.711), CTDSP2 (0.640), the cell cycle regulator CDCA3 (0.861), and general transcription factor GTF2F1 (0.775), positioning CTDSPL within a multiprotein complex coordinating transcription elongation and G1/S cell cycle checkpoint control. HPA data shows ubiquitous expression across all tissues with nucleoplasmic and vesicular localization, consistent with its nuclear role in transcription regulation. AlphaFold predicts a well-folded structure (global pLDDT 77.8), supporting stable enzymatic function. ClinVar contains 11 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large chromosomal deletions and copy number gains on chromosome 3p, implicating CTDSPL dosage sensitivity in disease pathogenesis, particularly given its 3p22.2 location within a cancer-associated chromosomal region. Strong evolutionary conservation is evident with mouse (P58465, 276 aa, 100% length conservation) and Xenopus (Q7T227, 276 aa) orthologs, indicating purifying selection on this regulatory phosphatase. The mechanistic model proposes that CTDSPL acts as a REST co-repressor, dephosphorylating RNAPII CTD to block transcriptional elongation at neuronal genes in non-neuronal tissues, while simultaneously regulating cell cycle progression through CDCA3 interaction.
