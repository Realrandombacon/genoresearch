## Description
SLC35D4 (also known as TMEM241) encodes a 296 amino acid UDP-N-acetylglucosamine transporter (UniProt Q24JQ0), a member of the SLC35 nucleotide sugar transporter family. InterPro analysis identifies it as part of the IPR050186 (Triose Phosphate/Phosphate Translocator) and PTHR11132 families, suggesting a conserved mechanism for transporting phosphorylated sugars across membranes. AlphaFold structure (AF-Q24JQ0-F1) predicts an exceptionally stable 3D structure with very high confidence (global pLDDT 94.1), indicating a rigid, well-folded membrane protein likely critical for function. HPA expression reveals low tissue specificity but ubiquitous detection in many tissues, with specific localization to the Golgi apparatus and vesicles, consistent with its role in supplying UDP-GlcNAc for glycosylation processes. Immune cell enhancement suggests a potential role in immune cell glycoprotein synthesis. Critically, literature search returned ZERO publications, confirming SLC35D4 as a genuine 'dark gene' with no functional characterization beyond sequence homology. Despite this lack of study, ClinVar contains a massive burden of 34 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains and losses spanning chromosome 18q11-q23, implying that dosage sensitivity of this region (and potentially SLC35D4 itself) is clinically significant, though no specific monogenic disease has yet been attributed solely to SLC35D4 mutations. STRING interactions were unavailable, limiting network context. The combination of ultra-high structural confidence, Golgi localization, zero literature, and heavy clinical CNV burden suggests SLC35D4 functions as an essential housekeeping transporter for UDP-GlcNAc into the Golgi lumen, regulating global protein glycosylation, with haploinsufficiency or overexpression contributing to the severe developmental phenotypes observed in 18q deletion/duplication syndromes.
