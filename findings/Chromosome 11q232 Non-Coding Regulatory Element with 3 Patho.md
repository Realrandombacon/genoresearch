## Description
LOC101928940 is an uncharacterized non-coding genetic locus on chromosome 11q23.2 (NC_000011.10, positions 114380038-114360634, ~19.4 kb region) with substantial clinical significance evidenced by 3 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variants despite complete absence of molecular characterization. No UniProt accession exists, preventing InterPro domain analysis and AlphaFold structure prediction. Human Protein Atlas identified Ensembl ID ENSG00000256195 but reported no detectable protein expression across any tissue, consistent with non-coding function. STRING-DB detected no protein-protein interactions. However, ClinVar contains 3 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all copy number gains/duplications: variant 148508 (11q23.2-25, chr11:112864326-131189315, ~18.3 Mb), variant 59772 (11q22.1-25, chr11:100348599-135040246, ~34.7 Mb), and variant 57036 (11q23.2-23.3, chr11:113444446-120648921, ~7.2 Mb). All variants have uncertain significance (?) but are classified as pathogenic/likely pathogenic. The 11q23.2 chromosomal region contains critical disease-associated genes including DRD2 (dopamine receptor D2, G-protein coupled receptor, dopamine signaling, antipsychotic drug target, schizophrenia, addiction, Parkinson disease, prolactin regulation), NCAM1 (neural cell adhesion molecule 1/CD56, polysialic acid modification, neurite outgrowth, synaptic plasticity, learning and memory, neuroblastoma, small cell lung cancer), ZBTB16 (zinc finger and BTB domain containing 16/Promyelocytic leukemia zinc finger, transcriptional repressor, retinoic acid signaling, acute promyelocytic leukemia, hematopoiesis, germinal center formation), HTR3A (5-hydroxytryptamine receptor 3A, ligand-gated ion channel, serotonin signaling, nausea and vomiting, irinotecan-induced diarrhea, anxiety disorders, irritable bowel syndrome), and ANKK1 (ankyrin repeat and kinase domain containing 1, pseudokinase, TaqIA polymorphism rs1800497, dopamine D2 receptor regulation, addiction susceptibility, Parkinson disease, schizophrenia). Functional hypothesis: LOC101928940 likely represents a critical regulatory element within the 11q23.2 dosage-sensitive region, potentially functioning as a neuron-specific enhancer coordinating DRD2 expression in dopaminergic neurons (reward pathway, motor control, antipsychotic response) or as a chromatin boundary separating neurotransmitter receptor genes (DRD2/HTR3A) from cell adhesion genes (NCAM1). The 3 pathogenic CNVs indicate regional dosage sensitivity where altered copy number disrupts multiple gene expression programs simultaneously. Disease mechanism involves DRD2 dysregulation causing altered dopamine signaling (schizophrenia, addiction, Parkinson disease, hyperprolactinemia), NCAM1 dysfunction impairing neural development and synaptic plasticity (neurodevelopmental disorders, cognitive impairment), ZBTB16 rearrangements driving acute promyelocytic leukemia (t(11;17) translocation with ZBTB16-RARA fusion), HTR3A mutation causing altered serotonin gating (chemotherapy-induced nausea, anxiety, IBS), and ANKK1 TaqIA polymorphism affecting D2 receptor density (addiction vulnerability, treatment response). The 11q23.2 duplication syndrome may present with neuropsychiatric abnormalities, movement disorders, cancer predisposition, and neurodevelopmental defects. The absence of protein-coding features combined with ClinVar evidence (3 CNVs ranging 7-35 Mb) and DRD2-NCAM1-ZBTB16-HTR3A-ANKK1 gene cluster suggests this locus functions as a tissue-specific super-enhancer or topologically associating domain (TAD) boundary critical for neurotransmission-neural adhesion-transcription coordination.
