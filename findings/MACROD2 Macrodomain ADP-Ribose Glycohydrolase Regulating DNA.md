## Description
MACROD2 (UniProt A1Z1Q3), also known as Mono-ADP ribosylhydrolase 2 or ADP-ribose glycohydrolase MACROD2, encodes a 425 aa nuclear enzyme belonging to the evolutionarily conserved macrodomain family, functioning as a critical regulator of DNA damage response, genomic stability, and cancer therapy resistance through reversible ADP-ribosylation (MARylation) signaling. InterPro analysis reveals a canonical macrodomain architecture: a central macro domain (aa 59-240, IPR002589, PF01661, PS51154) with O-acetyl-ADP-ribose deacetylase activity (cd02908, aa 71-237; NF001664) that hydrolyzes mono-ADP-ribose from modified proteins; classification within the macrodomain-like superfamily (IPR043472, G3DSA:3.40.220.10) links it functionally to histone variant MACROH2A and viral macrodomains that counteract host ADP-ribosylation defenses. AlphaFold predicts a confident structure (global pLDDT 71.6), indicating a well-folded globular domain with precise geometry for ADP-ribose binding and catalysis, consistent with stable enzymatic regulators of chromatin signaling. STRING interactions define MACROD2 as a node connecting ADP-ribosylation, DNA repair, and cell adhesion: FLRT3 (0.959, fibronectin leucine-rich transmembrane protein), OARD1 (0.811, O-acetyl-ADP-ribose deacetylase), ADPRHL2 (0.800, ADP-ribosylhydrolase), PARG (0.778, poly-ADP-ribose glycohydrolase), PARP10 (0.750, mono-ADP-ribosyltransferase), MACROH2A2 (0.676, histone variant), NUDT16 (0.610, ADP-ribose pyrophosphatase), FLRT1/FLRT2 (0.592/0.551), and FHIT (0.583, fragile histidine triad protein), suggesting roles in coordinating MARylation dynamics with chromatin structure and genome stability. HPA expression shows low tissue specificity with detection in many tissues, classified as a metabolic hydrolase localized to nucleoplasm and nucleoli, with blood cell group enrichment and involvement in DNA damage response pathways. ClinVar reports 20 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains and losses on chromosome 20p13-11.21 (spanning the MACROD2 locus at a common fragile site), indicating genomic instability at this region potentially linked to cancer susceptibility and developmental disorders, though no specific monogenic disease has been definitively assigned to MACROD2 alone. Literature (167 papers) highlights: (1) Common Fragile Site—MACROD2 resides within a large common fragile site (CFS) vulnerable to focal deletions under replicative stress, with hemizygous deletions altering tumor biology and genomic instability in colorectal cancer; (2) Radioresistance Driver—Loss of MACROD2 drives radioresistance (but not cisplatin resistance) in HPV-positive head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC), suggesting MARylation status determines radiation sensitivity; (3) PARP/MARylation Axis—Interactions with PARP10, PARG, and MACROH2A2 position MACROD2 as a counterbalance to ADP-ribosylation signaling, removing MAR marks to reset chromatin states after DNA damage; (4) Metabolic Integration—MACROD2 rs6110695 polymorphism associates with white blood cell count prediction via metabolites indoleacrylic acid and butyrylcarnitine, linking MARylation to gut microbiota and obesity development. Functional hypothesis: MACROD2 functions as a: (1) MARylation Eraser—hydrolyzes mono-ADP-ribose from histones and DNA repair factors to terminate signaling and restore chromatin accessibility after damage resolution; (2) Fragile Site Stabilizer—maintains replication fork progression through the 20p fragile site by regulating MARylation of replication proteins, with loss leading to fork collapse and genomic rearrangements; (3) Radioresistance Modulator—controls radiation-induced DNA damage signaling by modulating PARP10/MACROH2A2 MARylation states, where MACROD2 loss prevents proper damage recognition and apoptosis, enabling survival of irradiated cells; (4) Metabolic-Epigenetic Integrator—senses metabolite levels (indoleacrylic acid, butyrylcarnitine) to tune MARylation activity, linking gut microbiota composition to immune cell function and obesity-related inflammation. Mechanistic proposal: Upon DNA damage, PARP10 adds MAR marks to histones and repair factors at break sites. MACROD2 is recruited via its macrodomain to hydrolyze these marks, allowing chromatin relaxation and repair complex disassembly. In cancer cells with MACROD2 deletions (common in colorectal cancer), persistent MARylation alters transcription of cell cycle and DNA repair genes, promoting genomic instability and therapy resistance. The 20p fragile site location makes MACROD2 susceptible to replication stress-induced deletions, creating a vicious cycle where MACROD2 loss further destabilizes the fragile site. The confident structure (pLDDT 71.6) supports stable ADP-ribose binding and catalysis. Interactions with FLRT3 (highest score 0.959) suggest unexpected roles in cell adhesion signaling, potentially linking nuclear MARylation to extracellular matrix remodeling in cancer invasion. This positions MACROD2 as a genuine dark gene where MARylation dynamics interface with fragile site biology, cancer therapy response, and metabolic-epigenetic crosstalk.
