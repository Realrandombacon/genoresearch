## Description
SYNGR3 encodes a 229 aa synaptic vesicle protein (UniProt O43761) localized to the Golgi apparatus and synaptic vesicles, functioning as a critical regulator of neurotransmitter release, vesicle trafficking, and neurodegeneration. InterPro analysis confirms its canonical Synaptogyrin architecture: N-terminal MARVEL domain (IPR008253, aa 20-172) mediating membrane association and oligomerization, and C-terminal Synaptogyrin domain (IPR016579, aa 1-229) essential for vesicle tethering. AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (pLDDT 80.3), indicating a stable, well-folded scaffold capable of precise subcellular localization. STRING interactions reveal a specialized synaptic network: SLC6A3 (0.944, dopamine transporter, primary partner), SYP (0.774, synaptophysin), SYT1 (0.715, synaptotagmin), SV2A (0.688, vesicle-associated protein), SNCA (0.651, α-synuclein), and PICK1 (0.674, protein interacting with C-kinase). HPA expression shows extreme brain specificity (125.4 nTPM in brain) with cancer enhancement, consistent with roles in dopaminergic neurons. ClinVar reports 34 pathogenic variants, predominantly large 16p13.3 copy number gains (x3) and deletions, suggesting dosage sensitivity; specific SNVs may disrupt MARVEL domain function. Literature (25 papers) confirms emerging roles: (1) Parkinson's Disease - SYNGR3 accelerating α-synuclein aggregation and neurodegeneration; (2) Dopamine Transport - interaction with SLC6A3 regulating vesicular dopamine loading; (3) Vesicle Trafficking - Golgi-to-synapse transport defects; (4) Epigenetic Regulation - NURR1-dependent transcriptional control. Functional hypothesis: SYNGR3 functions through: (1) Vesicle Tethering - anchoring synaptic vesicles via MARVEL domain oligomerization to facilitate neurotransmitter release; (2) Dopamine Regulation - stabilizing SLC6A3 to ensure adequate vesicular dopamine loading for dopaminergic signaling; (3) α-Synuclein Pathology - promoting α-synuclein aggregation and seeding propagation in Parkinson's disease; (4) Dosage Sensitivity - 16p13.3 amplification driving neurodegeneration by disrupting vesicle dynamics. Disease implications include Parkinson's Disease, Synucleinopathies, and Dopamine Deficiency disorders. Despite clinical variant burden and high structural confidence, the precise mechanism of α-synuclein aggregation promotion and full signaling partners remain partially unexplored.
