# BOLA2B: BolA-Like Protein Paralog in Iron-Sulfur Cluster Biogenesis Complex with GLRX3, Regulating Intracellular Iron Homeostasis and Implicated in 16p11.2 Copy Number Variation Disorders

## Description
BOLA2B (A0A499FJE1) encodes a 152 amino acid BolA-like protein belonging to the evolutionarily conserved BolA family that functions as a component of the cytosolic iron-sulfur (Fe-S) cluster assembly machinery. InterPro analysis reveals a compact BolA fold architecture: a BolA protein domain (IPR002634/PF01722, positions 78-145) classified within the BolA-like superfamily (IPR036065/SSF82657, positions 55-151) and specifically as BolA-like protein 2 (IPR045115/PTHR12735, positions 67-152) with Gene Ontology annotations for 2 iron 2 sulfur cluster binding (GO:0051537), intracellular iron ion homeostasis (GO:0006879), and protein maturation (GO:0051604). A CATH-Gene3D homologous superfamily match (G3DSA:3.10.20.90, positions 51-152) to phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase catalytic subunit domain 1 suggests structural similarity to nucleotide-binding folds. AlphaFold predicts a partially structured architecture (pLDDT 68.0, medium confidence) consistent with small Fe-S cluster binding proteins that often exhibit conformational flexibility during cluster transfer. STRING interactions demonstrate exclusive partnerships with the cytosolic Fe-S cluster assembly (CIA) machinery: GLRX3 (0.999), glutaredoxin-3 forming a complex with BOLA2B for Fe-S cluster transfer; BOLA3 (0.994), paralogous BolA protein functioning in mitochondrial Fe-S cluster biogenesis; GLRX5 (0.879), mitochondrial glutaredoxin-5 essential for Fe-S cluster assembly; CIAPIN1 (0.844), anamorsin acting as electron transfer component in CIA pathway; BOLA1 (0.810), another BolA family paralog; ISCU (0.764), iron-sulfur cluster scaffold protein providing the assembly platform; FDX2 (0.743), ferredoxin-2 electron donor; NFS1 (0.739), cysteine desulfurase providing sulfur for cluster synthesis; LYRM4 (0.708), LyR motif-containing protein 4 in mitochondrial complex I assembly; and STAT4 (0.750), signal transducer suggesting potential immune signaling cross-talk. HPA shows ubiquitous expression across all tissues with low specificity, nucleoplasm localization consistent with cytosolic Fe-S cluster assembly function, classified under predicted intracellular proteins. ClinVar contains 95 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants predominantly large copy number losses and gains spanning the 16p11.2 region (e.g., chr16:29584162-30204300 deletions, chr16:29568701-30291027 duplications, chr16:28824858-30321320 losses), highlighting dosage sensitivity of this locus in neurodevelopmental disorders including autism spectrum disorder and schizophrenia, though specific monogenic disease associations for BOLA2B alone remain undefined. Literature (4 papers) demonstrates BOLA2B as a recently discovered protein-coding gene with pan-cancer expression patterns impacting immune response and gene mutation profiles; DNA methylation alterations at BOLA2B locus observed during transition from in situ to invasive cervical cancer. The mechanistic hypothesis: BOLA2B functions as a Fe-S cluster transfer protein partnering with GLRX3 to receive nascent Fe-S clusters from the CIA targeting complex and deliver them to client apoproteins requiring Fe-S cofactors for activity; the BolA fold provides a conserved structural platform for coordinating [2Fe-2S] clusters through cysteine residues while maintaining conformational flexibility (medium pLDDT 68.0) enabling cluster handoff between assembly factors and target enzymes; high-confidence interactions with BOLA3, GLRX5, and CIAPIN1 suggest functional redundancy or tissue-specific specialization among BolA paralogs partitioned between cytosolic (BOLA2B-BOLA1) and mitochondrial (BOLA3) compartments; ISCU/NFS1/FDX2 interactions place BOLA2B downstream of initial cluster synthesis on the scaffold receiving mature clusters for distribution; 16p11.2 copy number variations alter BOLA2B dosage potentially disrupting cellular Fe-S homeostasis affecting numerous Fe-S-dependent enzymes including DNA repair proteins, metabolic dehydratases, and respiratory chain components explaining pleiotropic neurodevelopmental phenotypes; pan-cancer expression patterns suggest BOLA2B upregulation may support tumor metabolism through enhanced Fe-S enzyme activity enabling rapid proliferation; cervical cancer methylation changes indicate epigenetic regulation of BOLA2B during malignant transformation.
