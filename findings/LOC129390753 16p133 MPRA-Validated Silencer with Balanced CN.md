## Description
LOC129390753 is a 200bp cis-regulatory element (CRE) located on chromosome 16p13.3 (GRCh38: 1295654-1295854), functionally validated as a transcriptional silencer through STARR-seq massively parallel reporter assays (MPRAs) in K562 erythroleukemia cells. This locus lacks canonical promoter, enhancer, or CTCF-bound insulator chromatin signatures but demonstrates robust repressive activity against the super core promoter SCP1. As a non-coding regulatory sequence, it has no UniProt accession, protein domains, STRING interaction partners, or Human Protein Atlas expression data (query not applicable for non-coding locus).\n\nClinVar analysis reveals 16 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variants disrupting this region. Critically, the variant spectrum shows a BALANCED pattern: approximately 7-8 duplications (copy number gains spanning 16p13.3-13.11 ~15 Mb, 16p13.3 ~0.4-1.9 Mb, 16p13.3-13.12 ~13 Mb, 16p13.3-13.13 ~11 Mb) and 7-8 deletions (copy number losses spanning 16p13.3 ~0.1-4.2 Mb, 16p13.3 ~1.0-1.5 Mb, 16p13.3 ~0.1-1.7 Mb). This ~1:1 ratio indicates TRUE dosage sensitivity where both haploinsufficiency and overexpression are pathogenic, similar to LOC129390578 (5:5 balanced) but contrasting with duplication-biased silencers like LOC129389913 (23:1) or LOC129388743 (5:0). These CNVs are associated with 16p13.3 deletion/duplication syndromes, characterized by developmental delay, intellectual disability, craniofacial dysmorphisms, and cardiac defects.\n\nThe genomic context is highly notable: 16p13.3 is a clinically critical region containing CREBBP (CREB binding protein, Rubinstein-Taybi syndrome histone acetyltransferase), PKD1 (polycystin-1, polycystic kidney disease), CLEC18A (C-type lectin), and multiple genes associated with 16p13.3 microdeletion/duplication syndrome. The locus also resides near telomeric sequences and subtelomeric gene clusters.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: LOC129390753 functions as a critical distal silencer within the 16p13.3 topologically associating domain (TAD), specifically regulating developmental genes (CREBBP, PKD1) or chromatin modifiers in embryonic and hematopoietic contexts. The balanced CNV pattern indicates this silencer operates within a narrow optimal dosage range: haploinsufficiency (50% activity) causes target gene derepression/overexpression, while triplication (150% activity) causes excessive repression. Both states disrupt precise gene expression levels required for normal development. The mechanism likely involves recruitment of context-specific co-repressor complexes (PRC2, NuRD, HDACs, or SIN3A) that fine-tune target gene expression levels. Unlike silencers with duplication bias (where only overexpression is pathogenic), LOC129390753 appears to regulate a gene or gene network where precise expression levels are critical - too much or too little silencing causes disease. CREBBP is particularly dosage-sensitive (haploinsufficiency causes Rubinstein-Taybi syndrome), suggesting LOC129390753 may directly or indirectly regulate CREBBP expression. The K562 validation indicates it functions in hematopoietic lineages as well, possibly regulating 16p13.3 genes involved in erythroid differentiation or myeloid development. The 16p13.3 location near telomeres raises the possibility of telomere position effect modulation.
