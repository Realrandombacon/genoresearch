## Description
HMGCLL1 (Q8TB92) encodes a 370 amino acid cytoplasmic enzyme homologous to mitochondrial HMG-CoA lyase (HMGCL), the final step enzyme in ketone body biosynthesis. Despite its clear enzymatic classification, HMGCLL1 remains poorly characterized with only 16 publications—primarily as a biomarker in chronic myeloid leukemia (CML) response to imatinib therapy.\n\nSTRUCTURAL FEATURES: InterPro analysis reveals a canonical HMG-CoA lyase family architecture (IPR043594, PF00682) with a catalytic TIM barrel domain (cd07938, residues 80-353) characteristic of aldolase-type lyases (G3DSA:3.20.20.70). The pyruvate carboxyltransferase domain (IPR000891, 78-351) confirms oxo-acid-lyase activity (GO:0016833). AlphaFold prediction shows high-confidence folding (pLDDT 84.7) across all 370 residues, indicating a stable, functional enzyme.\n\nINTERACTION NETWORK: STRING-DB reveals HMGCLL1 operates within the ketone body metabolic pathway through high-confidence interactions: AUH (0.977, methylglutaconyl-CoA hydratase), BDH1 (0.946, beta-hydroxybutyrate dehydrogenase), HMGCS2 (0.744, mitochondrial HMG-CoA synthase), ACAA1 (0.734), ACAT1 (0.719), ACAT2 (0.645), HMGCS1 (0.539), OXCT1 (0.484), and OXCT2 (0.430). This interaction profile strongly suggests HMGCLL1 functions as a cytoplasmic counterpart to mitochondrial ketogenesis, potentially enabling extra-mitochondrial ketone production.\n\nEXPRESSION & DISEASE: HPA shows brain-enriched expression (9.1 nTPM top tissue), tissue-enhanced pattern, and lipid metabolism classification. This neuronal expression is striking given ketone bodies are critical brain energy substrates during fasting. ClinVar contains 9 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants (all copy number gains/losses spanning 6p12.1), yet no specific disease phenotype is defined—suggesting either embryonic lethality, rare metabolic disorders, or unidentified neurological conditions.\n\nFUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: HMGCLL1 likely performs cytoplasmic HMG-CoA cleavage to acetoacetate and acetyl-CoA, providing localized ketone production independent of mitochondrial β-oxidation. This would be particularly important in neurons during metabolic stress. The CML biomarker association suggests dysregulation affects cellular metabolism in hematopoietic cells. The 9 ClinVar CNVs without defined phenotypes represent a critical knowledge gap—systematic metabolic screening of carriers could reveal novel inborn errors of ketone metabolism.
