## Description
CENPX (Centromere Protein X; UniProt A8MT69) is an ultra-compact 81 amino acid histone-fold protein with only 13 publications, classifying it as a genuine dark gene despite essential roles in DNA double-strand break repair, kinetochore assembly, and Fanconi anemia pathway integration.\n\nSTRUCTURAL ARCHITECTURE: InterPro analysis reveals a canonical histone-fold domain (cd22921, residues 10-81) that enables heterodimerization with CENPS/MHF1 to form the MHF1-MHF2 (CENPS-CENPX) complex. The protein belongs to the Centromere protein X family (IPR018552/PTHR28680, residues 6-81) with GO annotations for DNA repair (GO:0006281) and kinetochore assembly (GO:0051382). The CENP-S associating domain (PF09415, residues 12-81) mediates stable heterodimer formation. AlphaFold predicts very high confidence structure (global pLDDT 92.6), indicating an exceptionally well-folded histone-fold motif despite minimal sequence length—among the highest confidence predictions for proteins under 100 amino acids.\n\nINTERACTION NETWORK: STRING-DB identifies exclusively high-confidence interactions (score 0.999) with Fanconi anemia core complex proteins and centromere components: FANCE (Fanconi anemia complementation group E), FANCC (complementation group C), CENPS/MHF1 (centromere protein S, obligate heterodimer partner), FANCF (complementation group F), FAAP100 (FA-associated polypeptide 100), CENPW (centromere protein W), FANCG (complementation group G), FANCA (complementation group A), FANCL (FA core complex E3 ubiquitin ligase), plus medium-confidence FANCM (0.489, FA helicase). This interaction profile establishes CENPX as integral to both the FA DNA repair pathway and centromere/kinetochore structure.\n\nEXPRESSION & LOCALIZATION: HPA shows ubiquitous expression detected in all tissues with low tissue specificity, consistent with fundamental requirements for genome maintenance in all dividing cells. Biological processes include cell cycle, cell division, DNA damage response, DNA repair, and mitosis. Molecular function is DNA-binding. Classified as a predicted intracellular protein with cell type-enhanced single cell specificity.\n\nDISEASE LANDSCAPE: ClinVar contains 23 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number gains at 17q24.1-25.3 and 17q25.1-25.3 encompassing CENPX, copy number losses at 17q25.3, deletions, and coding variant c.176C>T (p.Ala59Val) within the histone-fold domain. No monogenic disease is yet definitively assigned, but 17q CNVs cause developmental anomalies. The p.Ala59Val substitution occurs at a critical position within the histone-fold dimerization interface, likely disrupting CENPS binding and MHF complex stability. Given the exclusive FA pathway interactions, CENPX deficiency may cause Fanconi anemia-like phenotypes with chromosomal instability, bone marrow failure, and cancer predisposition.\n\nCONSERVATION: Orthologs confirmed in mouse (Q8C4X1, 78 aa), bovine (Q2NKU0, 79 aa), and chicken (P0DJH7, 80 aa), indicating extreme evolutionary constraint on the 80-amino-acid histone-fold architecture across vertebrates.\n\nFUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: CENPX functions as the MHF2 subunit of the MHF1-MHF2 (CENPS-CENPX) histone-fold heterodimer that serves dual roles: (1) At DNA double-strand breaks, RSF1 recruits MHF1-MHF2 to damaged chromatin as an early event required for both non-homologous end joining (NHEJ) and homologous recombination (HR), where the complex stabilizes replication forks and facilitates DNA end processing; (2) At centromeres, MHF1-MHF2 integrates into the CENP-A nucleosome-associated complex (CAN) for kinetochore assembly during mitosis. The exclusive high-confidence STRING interactions with FA core complex proteins (FANCA/C/E/F/G/L) suggest CENPX may be a previously unrecognized component of the FA pathway, potentially linking replication fork protection to interstrand crosslink repair. In FA-deficient cells, CENPX dysfunction could cause genomic instability characteristic of FA syndrome. The 23 ClinVar variants warrant investigation for FA-like phenotypes, particularly the p.Ala59Val missense mutation which may act as a dominant-negative by disrupting heterodimer formation.
