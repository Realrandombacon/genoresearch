## Description
TMEM208 (Transmembrane protein 208, UniProt Q9BTX3) is a 173 amino acid endoplasmic reticulum (ER) membrane protein encoded on chromosome 16q22.1. It belongs to the SRP-independent targeting (SND) protein family, specifically identified as SND2 (hSND2), which mediates the post-translational insertion of tail-anchored proteins into the ER membrane via the GET (Guided Entry of Tail-anchored proteins) pathway. InterPro analysis confirms its classification within the SRP-independent targeting protein 2/TMEM208 family (IPR008506, PF05620, residues 8-171), encompassing nearly the entire protein sequence, indicating a conserved functional core.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals TMEM208 operates within a network of autophagy and ER membrane biogenesis factors. High-confidence partners include ATG9A (0.840) and ATG9B (0.840), the only transmembrane autophagy proteins essential for autophagosome formation, and SWI5 (0.960), a homolog of yeast ER membrane protein complex components. Additional links to GET3 (0.627) and GET1 (0.605) confirm its role in the GET pathway, while RTN4 (0.722, Reticulon-4) association suggests involvement in ER shaping and membrane curvature. Human Protein Atlas data classifies TMEM208 as a transporter involved in autophagy, with ubiquitous expression ("Detected in all" tissues) and low tissue specificity, consistent with a housekeeping ER function.\n\nClinVar records 27 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including a mix of large-scale copy number gains (e.g., whole arm 16q duplications ~44 Mb, 16q21-24.3 ~28 Mb) and losses (e.g., 16q22.1 focal deletions ~1 Mb, 16q21-22.1 ~4.5 Mb), plus at least one intronic single nucleotide variant (c.300-33A>T). The high burden of both deletions and duplications suggests dosage sensitivity, where perturbation of TMEM208 levels disrupts ER proteostasis. These CNVs are associated with 16q deletion/duplication syndromes, characterized by developmental delay, intellectual disability, and growth abnormalities.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: TMEM208 functions as an ER membrane insertase or chaperone that facilitates the post-translational targeting of tail-anchored proteins (including autophagy factors like ATG9) via the GET pathway. By ensuring proper membrane integration of these substrates, TMEM208 maintains ER homeostasis and prevents unfolded protein response (UPR) activation. Knockdown studies showing increased autophagy and ER stress support this model: loss of TMEM208 impairs GET pathway efficiency, leading to accumulation of mislocalized tail-anchored proteins, ER stress, and compensatory upregulation of autophagy to clear aggregates. Haploinsufficiency (via 16q deletions) likely causes chronic ER stress in developing neurons, contributing to neurodevelopmental phenotypes. Conversely, duplication may saturate the GET machinery or alter ER membrane composition, also disrupting proteostasis. The ubiquitous expression pattern underscores its essential role in cellular homeostasis across all tissue types.
