## Description
CYTH2 (Cytohesin-2) is a partially characterized dark gene with only 18 publications, representing a critical but understudied guanine nucleotide exchange factor (GEF) that regulates ARF GTPases to control membrane trafficking and cytoskeletal dynamics. UniProt accession Q99418 identifies it as a 400-amino acid intracellular protein. InterPro scan reveals a canonical cytohesin architecture composed of two functional domains: an N-terminal Sec7 domain (IPR000904/PF00169, residues 54-243) responsible for catalyzing the exchange of GDP for GTP on ARF proteins, and a C-terminal Pleckstrin Homology (PH) domain (IPR001849/PF00169, residues 259-378) that targets the protein to specific phosphoinositide-rich membranes. This domain arrangement classifies CYTH2 as a member of the cytohesin family, essential for vesicle budding, cell adhesion, and immune synapse formation.\n\nAlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (global pLDDT 84.6), indicating a rigid, well-folded molecule where the Sec7 and PH domains are precisely oriented for coordinated membrane recruitment and enzymatic activation. However, single-cell data indicates enhancement in specific cell types, likely reflecting demand for rapid membrane remodeling in immune or neuronal contexts.\n\nClinVar reports 14 pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants, including large copy number gains spanning chromosome 19q13 (e.g., chr19:48905537-51614930)x3, chr19:28271106-49213832)x3). The combination of universal expression, specific viral/neuronal roles, and pathogenic CNVs positions CYTH2 as a high-priority candidate for functional characterization in virology and neuroscience.
