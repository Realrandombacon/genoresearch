## Description
PIK3R4 encodes a 1358 aa multidomain scaffold protein (UniProt Q99570), also known as VPS15 or p150, that functions as an atypical serine/threonine kinase essential for class III phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase (PI3K) complex assembly and autophagosome biogenesis. The protein features a sophisticated multi-domain architecture: an N-terminal serine/threonine protein kinase domain (IPR000719, residues 26-324) with a conserved active site motif (IPR008271, residues 144-156) that exhibits regulatory rather than catalytic function; two armadillo-like helical domains (IPR011989, residues 370-706) providing structural scaffolding; and multiple C-terminal WD40 repeats (IPR001680, residues 982-1169) forming a beta-propeller structure for protein-protein interactions, embedded within YVTN repeat-like scaffolds (G3DSA:2.130.10.10, residues 961-1358). STRING interactions place PIK3R4 at the absolute core of the autophagy machinery with near-perfect confidence partnerships: BECN1 (0.999) and BECN2 (0.999), the essential autophagy initiators; AMBRA1 (0.998), a Beclin-1 regulator; PIK3C3 (0.997), the catalytic subunit of class III PI3K that generates PI3P; ULK2 (0.996) and ULK1 (0.782), the autophagy-initiating kinases; ATG14 (0.984), specifying autophagosomal vs. endosomal localization; UVRAG (0.736), regulating endosomal maturation; NRBF2 (0.607) and RUBCN (0.421), additional autophagy regulators. HPA confirms ubiquitous expression across all tissues with subcellular localization to vesicles, microtubules, cytokinetic bridge, primary cilium, and basal body—consistent with roles in membrane trafficking and ciliary function. ClinVar lists 14 pathogenic variants including copy number losses/gains spanning chromosome 3q13.31-q26.31, deletions, and point mutations (c.733G>T p.Gly245Cys, c.1586-2A>T splice variant), demonstrating dosage sensitivity and linking PIK3R4 disruption to developmental disorders. AlphaFold structure prediction yields high confidence (global pLDDT 78.1), revealing a well-folded elongated architecture with distinct kinase and WD40 domains positioned for complex assembly. Mechanistically, PIK3R4 functions through multiple integrated modes: (1) As a scaffolding kinase, PIK3R4 recruits and stabilizes PIK3C3 (VPS34) within the class III PI3K complex, enabling localized production of phosphatidylinositol 3-phosphate (PI3P) on nascent autophagosomal membranes; (2) The WD40 beta-propeller mediates interactions with BECN1/2, AMBRA1, and ATG14, determining complex specificity for autophagy vs. endosomal sorting; (3) ULK1/ULK2 phosphorylation of PIK3R4 triggers complex activation during nutrient starvation, linking mTOR signaling to autophagy initiation; (4) Ciliary and basal body localization suggests roles in ciliogenesis and ciliary signaling, potentially explaining developmental phenotypes in patients with 3q deletions; (5) Cytokinetic bridge localization indicates functions in cell division and abscission. Disruption of PIK3R4 would cause defective autophagosome formation, impaired clearance of protein aggregates and damaged organelles, aberrant ciliary signaling, and cell division defects—phenotypes consistent with neurodevelopmental disorders and cancer susceptibility observed in patients.
