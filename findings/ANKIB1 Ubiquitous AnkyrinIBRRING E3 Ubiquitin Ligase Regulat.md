## Description
ANKIB1 (Ankyrin Repeat and IBR Domain Containing 1) is a 1089 amino acid cytosolic/plasma membrane protein (UniProt Q9P2G1) with a partially structured conformation (AlphaFold pLDDT 65.4, medium confidence), functioning as a multi-domain E3 ubiquitin ligase that targets substrates for proteasomal degradation through its RING-IBR-RING (RBR) architecture and ankyrin repeat-mediated protein-protein interactions.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals ANKIB1 contains N-terminal ankyrin repeats (IPR002110/G3DSA:1.25.40.20, residues 45-77/136-194/3-203) that mediate protein binding (GO:0005515), a central RBR E3 ligase module composed of RING finger (IPR001841/G3DSA:3.30.40.10, residues 333-381/328-415), IBR domain (IPR002867, residues 402-478/501-565 with zinc ion binding GO:0008270), and second RING finger (residues 519-643). The RING domains (cd16774) coordinate zinc ions for E2 ubiquitin-conjugating enzyme recruitment, while the IBR domain (BRcat cd20346 residues 409-487, Rcat cd20361 residues 508-569) forms the catalytic core for ubiquitin transfer. A C-terminal ubiquitin-interacting motif (IPR003903/UIM, residues 851-870) recognizes polyubiquitin chains on substrates or regulates ligase activity through ubiquitin binding.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals ANKIB1 functions within ubiquitin-dependent proteolysis and chromatin regulation networks: strongest interactions with GATAD1 (0.816, GATA zinc finger domain-containing protein 1, chromatin remodeling), AKAP11 (0.655, A-kinase anchoring protein 11, PKA signaling scaffold), RNF216 (0.575, ring finger protein 216, E3 ubiquitin ligase), CWF19L2 (0.552, cell cycle control protein 19-like 2), FNIP2 (0.537, folliculin-interacting protein 2, mTOR regulation), CUL7 (0.532, cullin 7, SCF ubiquitin ligase scaffold), CLOCK (0.530, circadian locomotor output cycles kaput, circadian rhythm), RBM48 (0.488, RNA-binding protein 48), ZFP62 (0.488, zinc finger protein 62), and RALGAPB (0.457, Ral GTPase-activating protein subunit beta). The GATAD1 interaction links ANKIB1 to NuRD chromatin remodeling complexes, while CUL7 partnership suggests integration into SCF-type ubiquitin ligase assemblies.\n\nHPA expression shows ubiquitous low tissue specificity with detection in all tissues, consistent with a housekeeping ubiquitin ligase maintaining protein homeostasis. Subcellular localization includes cytosol and plasma membrane. The protein is classified under enzymes (transferase activity) and predicted intracellular proteins, participating in Ubl conjugation pathways.\n\nClinVar documents 15 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including splice site mutations (c.1486+5G>C), chromosome 7q21.2 deletions, and large 7q11.23-22.1/7p22.3-q36.3 copy number gains/losses. These CNVs suggest dosage sensitivity potentially causing developmental disorders, intellectual disability, or cancer through disrupted ubiquitin-mediated proteolysis.\n\nConservation is evident from mouse (Q6ZPS6, 1085 aa) and zebrafish (Q1L8G6, 1060 aa) orthologs, indicating ancient evolutionary conservation of RBR E3 ligase mechanisms.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: ANKIB1 functions as a RBR-type E3 ubiquitin ligase that receives ubiquitin from E2 conjugating enzymes via its first RING domain, transfers it through the IBR catalytic cysteine (forming an E3~ubiquitin thioester intermediate), and conjugates it to substrate lysines via the second RING domain. Ankyrin repeats recruit specific substrates through protein-protein interactions, while the UIM domain may regulate processivity by binding polyubiquitin chains. GATAD1 interaction suggests ANKIB1 targets chromatin regulators for degradation during transcriptional reprogramming. AKAP11 partnership links ANKIB1 to cAMP/PKA signaling, potentially phosphorylating ANKIB1 to modulate ligase activity. CUL7 interaction indicates ANKIB1 may function within larger cullin-RING ligase complexes or regulate CUL7 stability. CLOCK interaction suggests circadian regulation of protein turnover. The medium-confidence structure (pLDDT 65.4) reflects flexible linkers between domains enabling conformational changes during catalytic cycles. Ubiquitous expression indicates ANKIB1 maintains basal proteostasis, while CNV-mediated haploinsufficiency causes substrate accumulation and proteotoxic stress. The 7q21.2 location places ANKIB1 near imprinted regions, potentially contributing to Silver-Russell or Beckwith-Wiedemann syndrome phenotypes through epigenetic dysregulation.
