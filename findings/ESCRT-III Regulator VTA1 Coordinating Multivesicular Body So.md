## Description
VTA1 (Vacuolar Protein Sorting-Associated Protein VTA1 Homolog) is a 307 aa cytoplasmic protein functioning as a critical co-factor for the AAA-ATPase VPS4, essential for the disassembly of ESCRT-III complexes during multivesicular body (MVB) sorting, cytokinesis abscission, and viral budding. InterPro analysis reveals a bipartite domain architecture: an N-terminal Vta1/callose synthase domain (IPR039431/PF04652, aa 16-158) responsible for ESCRT-III binding, and a C-terminal Vta1 domain (IPR041212/PF18097, aa 266-303) that mediates oligomerization and VPS4 recruitment. AlphaFold predicts a confident structure (pLDDT 78.2) consistent with an elongated scaffolding protein. STRING interactions define VTA1 as a central hub in the ESCRT machinery with ultra-high confidence partners: CHMP1B (0.999), CHMP1A (0.998), IST1 (0.996), VPS4A (0.988), and CHMP2A (0.979), alongside LYST (0.948), linking it to lysosomal trafficking disorders. HPA expression shows ubiquitous detection across all tissues with low specificity, localized to nucleoplasm, vesicles, and cytosol, reflecting its housekeeping role in membrane remodeling. ClinVar contains 16 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains/losses on chromosome 6q, suggesting dosage sensitivity, though specific monogenic syndromes are not yet fully delineated. Literature (80 papers) confirms VTA1's role in regulating VPS4 ATPase activity, facilitating ESCRT-III turnover, and its emerging significance as a prognostic marker in lung adenocarcinoma and breast cancer, as well as a host factor for viral entry (e.g., baculovirus).
