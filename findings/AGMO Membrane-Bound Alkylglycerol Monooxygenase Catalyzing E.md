## Description
AGMO (Alkylglycerol Monooxygenase, UniProt Q6ZNB7) is a 445-amino acid membrane-bound di-iron oxidoreductase that functions as the sole known enzyme capable of cleaving the ether bond of 2-alkylglycerophospholipids, representing a critical and previously orphan step in ether lipid catabolism with emerging roles in immune cell function and lipid metabolism. Functional hypothesis: AGMO functions as the rate-limiting enzyme in ether lipid catabolism where its membrane-embedded di-iron center (within fatty acid hydroxylase domain, aa 119-249) activates molecular oxygen to cleave the ether bond at the sn-1 position of 2-alkylglycerophospholipids, producing fatty aldehydes and glycerol-3-phosphate; the fatty aldehyde product is subsequently oxidized by ALDH3A2 (STRING 0.952) to fatty acids for beta-oxidation or recycling; the C-terminal domain (aa 335-422) provides substrate specificity for alkylglycerol recognition.
