## Description
DYNC2I2 (Cytoplasmic Dynein 2 Intermediate Chain 2, also known as Dynein-2 IC2) is a 536 aa intermediate chain protein functioning as an essential structural component of the dynein-2 motor complex that drives retrograde intraflagellar transport (IFT) in primary cilia. With only 5 literature references, DYNC2I2 represents a genuine 'dark gene' where its specific contribution to dynein-2 complex assembly and ciliopathy pathogenesis is only recently emerging. InterPro analysis reveals a domain architecture dominated by multiple WD40 repeats (IPR001680/PF00400, at aa 207-246, 255-299, 381-421, 482-510) that form β-propeller structures for protein-protein interactions, embedded within a larger YVTN repeat-like superfamily (IPR015943, aa 128-532) characteristic of dynein intermediate chains. AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (pLDDT 82.1), indicating a well-folded scaffold optimized for stable complex assembly. HPA confirms ubiquitous expression with low tissue specificity, localizing to the primary cilium, centrosome, nucleoplasm, and nucleoli—consistent with dual roles in ciliary trafficking and nuclear functions. STRING interactions reveal an extremely tight functional cluster dedicated to dynein-2 complex integrity: DYNC2I2 binds almost exclusively to the dynein-2 heavy chain DYNC2H1 (0.999), the WD40 subunit WDR60 (0.999), the light intermediate chain DYNC2LI1 (0.997), and multiple dynein light chains (DYNLL1: 0.997, DYNLT3: 0.978, DYNLRB2: 0.956). This network confirms DYNC2I2's role as a central adapter linking the motor domain to cargo-binding subunits. ClinVar lists 116 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including frameshift mutations (p.Phe403fs, p.Lys477fs, p.Pro427fs), nonsense mutations (p.Ser50Ter), and large copy number losses/gains on chromosome 9q, definitively linking DYNC2I2 to short-rib thoracic dysplasia 11 (SRTD11), a lethal skeletal ciliopathy characterized by polydactyly, shortened limbs, and narrow thorax. Recent literature (2022-2026) establishes DYNC2I2 as: (1) Cause of prenatal short-rib thoracic dysplasia with compound heterozygous variants presenting polydactyly and limb shortening; (2) Component of skeletal ciliopathies where mutations disrupt ciliary trafficking; (3) Differentially expressed in cervical cancer, suggesting potential oncogenic roles beyond cilia.
