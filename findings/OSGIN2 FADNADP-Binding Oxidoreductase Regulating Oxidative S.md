## Description
OSGIN2 (Oxidative Stress-Induced Growth Inhibitor Family Member 2) encodes a 505 aa intracellular oxidoreductase (UniProt Q9Y236) with emerging roles in oxidative stress response, cancer progression, and bone metabolism. InterPro analysis reveals canonical FAD/NAD(P)-binding oxidoreductase architecture: IPR029731 (OSGIN 1/2 family, aa 1-496), IPR036188/G3DSA:3.50.50.60/SSF51905 (FAD/NAD(P)-binding domain superfamily, aa 16-368 with subdomains at aa 16-37, 124-201, 251-334), PTHR15192 (Protein CBG05349 family, aa 1-496). The FAD/NAD(P)-binding domain indicates redox enzyme function with monooxygenase and oxidoreductase activities (GO molecular functions). STRING interactions show medium-confidence partnerships with calcium-binding proteins CALB1/calbindin (0.691) and CALB2/calretinin (0.663), DNA damage response protein NBN/nibrin (0.535), FRMPD3 (0.455), DECR1 (0.437, 2,4-dienoyl-CoA reductase), CA7 (0.434, carbonic anhydrase 7), S100G (0.431, calbindin-D9k), MSL1 (0.407), and GPR146 (0.401, G-protein coupled receptor), suggesting roles in calcium homeostasis, DNA repair, and metabolic regulation. HPA shows ubiquitous expression (detected in all tissues) with low tissue specificity, nucleoli localization, blood cell group enrichment, and cell type-enhanced expression patterns. ClinVar contains 35 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including point mutations (c.620+153C>T) and copy number variations at 8p23.3-q24.3 and 8q21.13-22.1, though no specific disease names are listed. AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 87.7) indicates high confidence with well-folded FAD/NAD-binding domain and stable tertiary structure. Literature (16 papers) shows OSGIN2 promoting gastric cancer proliferation and serving as prognostic biomarker (4 citations), regulating osteogenesis of jawbone bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells in osteoporotic rats through oxidative stress modulation (7 citations), mediating silver nanoparticle-induced apoptosis and mitochondrial damage in dental pulp stem cell-derived neurons (17 citations), and being regulated by miR-199a-5p alongside HIF-1α in soft tissue sarcoma prognosis (20 citations). FUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: OSGIN2 functions as a FAD/NAD(P)-dependent oxidoreductase that regulates cellular redox homeostasis, DNA damage response, and calcium signaling in oxidative stress conditions. The FAD/NAD(P)-binding domain (aa 16-368) catalyzes redox reactions that modulate reactive oxygen species (ROS) levels, influencing cell proliferation and survival. Nucleoli localization suggests roles in ribosome biogenesis regulation under stress. CALB1/CALB2 interactions indicate OSGIN2 coordinates calcium signaling with redox status, potentially regulating calcium-dependent enzymes and transcription factors. NBN interaction links OSGIN2 to the MRN complex (MRE11-RAD50-NBN) for DNA double-strand break repair, suggesting OSGIN2 redox activity modulates DNA repair efficiency. In gastric cancer, OSGIN2 overexpression promotes proliferation through ROS-mediated signaling pathway activation (possibly PI3K/AKT or MAPK). In osteoporosis, OSGIN2 regulates jawbone BMSC osteogenic differentiation through oxidative stress modulation, with estrogen deficiency altering OSGIN2 expression and impairing bone formation. The 35 ClinVar variants at 8q21-24 suggest OSGIN2 haploinsufficiency or dosage effects contribute to cancer susceptibility, bone disorders, or developmental conditions through disrupted redox homeostasis and DNA repair. High-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 87.7) indicates a stable oxidoreductase suitable for substrate binding studies and potential therapeutic targeting in cancer and osteoporosis.
