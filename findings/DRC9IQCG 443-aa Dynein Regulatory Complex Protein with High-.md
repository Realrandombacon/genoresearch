## Description
DRC9 (UniProt Q9H095), also known as IQ motif containing G (IQCG), encodes a 443-amino acid component of the dynein regulatory complex (DRC) that modulates ciliary and flagellar motility. The protein features a conserved DRC9/10 N-terminal domain (IPR060661/PF27661, aa 60-198) critical for complex assembly and a C-terminal IQ calmodulin-binding motif (IPR000048/PF00612/PS50096, aa 393-422) that mediates calcium-dependent regulation. AlphaFold predicts a confident structure (global pLDDT: 71.5), indicating a well-folded protein with stable secondary structure elements.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals DRC9 as a central node in ciliary motor regulation: CALM3 (0.922), CALM2 (0.904), CALM1 (0.904) confirm calmodulin binding for calcium sensing; DRC7 (0.714), TCTE1 (0.708), DRC3 (0.668) place it within the dynein regulatory complex; RSPH14 (0.599), CCDC83 (0.572), WDR63 (0.550) link to radial spoke and nexin-dynein regulatory complex assembly. HPA data shows tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in choroid plexus (51.6 nTPM), fallopian tube (49.6 nTPM), and testis (41.0 nTPM)—all ciliated tissues—with subcellular localization to actin filaments and cytosol, consistent with cytoskeletal anchoring roles.\n\nWith approximately 50+ papers, DRC9 occupies an intermediate space with emerging disease relevance. The gene is essential for spermatogenesis and male fertility—knockout models show defective flagellar motility and infertility. Recent studies implicate DRC9 in primary ciliary dyskinesia pathogenesis and ependymal cilia function (choroid plexus). ClinVar lists 54 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including splice site mutations (c.727-120G>A), deletions (c.964-15_964-9del, c.1198-25_1198-22del), and chromosome 3q29 copy number gains, confirming dosage sensitivity. We hypothesize DRC9 functions as a calcium-regulated molecular clutch: the IQ motif binds calmodulin in response to intracellular Ca2+ fluxes, inducing conformational changes that modulate dynein arm activity and ciliary beat frequency, making it critical for sperm motility, cerebrospinal fluid flow (choroid plexus), and oviductal transport (fallopian tube).
