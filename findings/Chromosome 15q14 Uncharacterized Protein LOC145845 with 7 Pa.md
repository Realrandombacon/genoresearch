# Chromosome 15q14 Uncharacterized Protein LOC145845 with 7 Pathogenic Copy Number Variants in GABA-Chrnic Imprinting Locus

**Quality Score:** 3.7/10  (E=3.7, D=3.7) [MODERATE]

## Description
LOC145845 (Gene ID: 145845) is an uncharacterized protein-coding gene on chromosome 15q14 (NC_000015.10, positions 36886532-36864442, ~22.1 kb genomic region) with substantial clinical significance evidenced by 7 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variants despite complete absence of molecular characterization. No UniProt accession exists, preventing InterPro domain analysis and AlphaFold structure prediction, consistent with truly dark protein-coding gene classification. STRING-DB detected no protein-protein interactions, consistent with uncharacterized protein status. Human Protein Atlas could not resolve expression data, consistent with highly tissue-restricted or developmentally-specific expression pattern. However, ClinVar contains 7 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly copy number losses/deletions (variant 155138: 15q11.2-14 ~12.2 Mb monosomy; variant 146702: 15q11.2-14 ~14.8 Mb monosomy; variant 57878: 15q14 ~4.7 Mb monosomy; variant 57877: 15q13.3-15.1 ~7.6 Mb monosomy; variant 57876: 15q13.3-14 ~7.1 Mb monosomy; variant 57226: 15q14 ~4.7 Mb monosomy) and 1 copy number gain/duplication (variant 149530: 15q11.2-14 ~15.2 Mb trisomy). All variants have uncertain significance (?) but are classified as pathogenic/likely pathogenic. The 15q11.2-15q14 chromosomal region is one of the most clinically significant imprinted loci in the human genome, containing critical neurodevelopmental, synaptic transmission, and genomic imprinting-associated genes including the Prader-Willi syndrome/Angelman syndrome critical region (15q11.2-q13, paternal/maternal imprinting defects), CHRNA7 (nicotinic acetylcholine receptor alpha-7 subunit, 15q13.3 microdeletion syndrome, epilepsy, schizophrenia, autism spectrum disorder), GABRB3/GABRA5/GABRG3 (GABA-A receptor subunits beta-3/alpha-5/gamma-3, inhibitory neurotransmission, epilepsy, Angelman syndrome features), OTUD7A (OTU deubiquitinase 7A, 15q13.3 microdeletion syndrome, neurodevelopment), CYFIP1 (cytoplasmic FMR1 interacting protein 1, 15q11.2 BP1-BP2 microdeletion syndrome, autism, intellectual disability), NIPA1/NIPA2 (non-imprinted in prune-belly syndrome magnesium transporters, 15q11.2 BP1-BP2 deletion syndrome), and TRPM1 (transient receptor potential cation channel subfamily M member 1, congenital stationary night blindness, melanoma). Functional hypothesis: LOC145845 likely represents a critical 15q14 neurodevelopmental/imprinting regulatory protein, potentially functioning as a nuclear chromatin modifier or synaptic scaffolding protein coordinating CHRNA7-GABRB3-OTUD7A-CYFIP1 expression in GABAergic neurotransmission, nicotinic cholinergic signaling, and genomic imprinting maintenance (GABAergic interneurons, hippocampal neurons, cortical pyramidal cells, imprinted locus control regions) or as a nucleolar protein bridging OTUD7A-mediated deubiquitination with GABRB3-mediated synaptic receptor trafficking. The 7 pathogenic CNVs (deletions 4.7-14.8 Mb, duplication 15.2 Mb) indicate extreme regional dosage sensitivity where altered copy number disrupts GABA receptor expression and imprinting control. Disease mechanism involves CHRNA7 haploinsufficiency causing 15q13.3 microdeletion syndrome (nicotinic receptor deficiency, neuronal hyperexcitability, epilepsy, schizophrenia, autism, intellectual disability, cognitive impairment), GABRB3 loss causing Angelman syndrome features (GABAergic inhibition defect, seizures, ataxia, developmental delay, happy demeanor, sleep disturbance), OTUD7A loss causing neurodevelopmental defect (deubiquitination failure, protein homeostasis disruption, synaptic dysfunction), CYFIP1 loss causing autism/intellectual disability (FMRP interaction defect, translational regulation failure, dendritic spine abnormality), NIPA1/NIPA2 loss causing 15q11.2 BP1-BP2 deletion syndrome (magnesium transport defect, neurodevelopmental impairment, psychiatric features), TRPM1 mutation causing congenital stationary night blindness (bipolar cell signaling defect, nyctalopia, myopia), Prader-Willi syndrome (paternal 15q11.2-q13 deletion, hyperphagia, obesity, hypogonadism, intellectual disability), and Angelman syndrome (maternal 15q11.2-q13 deletion, severe intellectual disability, ataxia, epilepsy, happy demeanor). The 15q11.2-15q14 region is the most dosage-sensitive neurodevelopmental-imprinting locus in the human genome. The uncharacterized protein-coding classification combined with ClinVar evidence (7 CNVs 4.7-15.2 Mb) and CHRNA7-GABRB3-OTUD7A-CYFIP1-TRPM1 neighborhood suggests this locus functions as a nuclear/synaptic protein critical for GABAergic neurotransmission-genomic imprinting-neurodevelopment coordination.
