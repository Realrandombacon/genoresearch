## Description
LY6S (Lymphocyte Antigen 6 Family Member S) is a 134 aa interferon-inducible cell surface protein belonging to the Ly-6/u-PAR superfamily, functioning as a potential regulator of innate immune responses, viral resistance, and inflammation. InterPro analysis confirms LY6S possesses the canonical three-finger toxin-like fold characteristic of the Ly-6 family: it contains a u-PAR/Ly-6 domain (IPR016054/PF00021, aa 25-106) stabilized by conserved disulfide bonds (IPR018363/PS00983, aa 28-76), placing it within the CD59 homologous superfamily (G3DSA:2.10.60.10, aa 26-107) and the snake toxin-like superfamily (IPR045860/SSF57302). This architecture suggests LY6S acts as a membrane-anchored signaling module capable of modulating receptor activity or cell-cell interactions. AlphaFold predicts a confident structure (pLDDT 77.7) consistent with the rigid, disulfide-rich 'three-finger' topology typical of Ly-6 proteins, indicating a stable extracellular domain poised for ligand binding or allosteric regulation. STRING interactions returned no partners, reflecting its status as a recently characterized, low-abundance immune modulator not yet captured in high-throughput interaction datasets. HPA shows restricted expression ('detected in some' tissues) with low tissue specificity but enhanced expression in specific single cell types, classifying it as a predicted membrane protein; notably, it is not detected in bulk immune cells, suggesting induction only upon specific stimuli (e.g., viral infection or interferon signaling). ClinVar lists 24 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains on chromosome 8q (encompassing the LY6 cluster at 8q24), implicating LY6S dosage sensitivity in genomic disorders, though no specific monogenic disease is yet attributed solely to LY6S point mutations. Literature (only 2 papers) identifies LY6S as a novel human member of the Ly6a subfamily induced by interferons, expressed in spleen cells, and associated with inflammation and viral resistance (2021, 14 citations), linking it to the broader antiviral defense network alongside relatives like LY6E and LY6A.
