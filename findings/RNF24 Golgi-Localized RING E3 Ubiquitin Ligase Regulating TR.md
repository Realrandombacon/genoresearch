## Description
RNF24 (UniProt Q9Y225, 148 aa) is a genuine dark gene with only 17 literature papers, encoding a small RING-type E3 ubiquitin ligase with a critical role in regulating transient receptor potential canonical (TRPC) channel trafficking. The seminal 2008 study (42 citations) established RNF24 as a novel TRPC-interacting protein that causes intracellular retention of TRPC channels at the Golgi apparatus, preventing their plasma membrane expression. InterPro reveals a canonical RING-type zinc finger domain (IPR001841, aa 77-119; IPR011016, RING-CH-type) embedded within the HRD1 family E3 ubiquitin-protein ligase architecture (IPR050731, aa 41-140; PTHR22763), with the characteristic C3HC4 zinc-binding motif (G3DSA:3.30.40.10, aa 47-123; PF13639, aa 77-119) essential for E3 ligase catalytic activity. The cd16675 domain specifically identifies the RNF24 H2 subclass RING finger (aa 76-128), distinguishing it from other RING proteins. AlphaFold predicts a confident structure (global pLDDT 70.6), with the RING domain well-folded despite the small protein size, indicating stable E3 ligase function. HPA shows tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in bone marrow (48.9 nTPM), immune cell enhancement, and Golgi apparatus subcellular localization—consistent with its role in regulating channel trafficking from the secretory pathway. STRING interactions reveal strong connections to TRPC family members: TRPC4 (0.691), TRPC6 (0.688), TRPC1 (0.605), TRPC3 (0.504), TRPC5 (0.495), confirming the literature-based TRPC regulatory function. Additional partners include PANK2 (0.592, pantothenate kinase linking to coenzyme A metabolism), PCED1A (0.499), TRPM2 (0.499, related TRP channel), RWDD1 (0.499, SUMOylation regulator), and UBOX5 (0.479, another E3 ligase). ClinVar contains 28 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains/losses spanning 20p13-11.21 regions, though specific RNF24 point mutations remain of uncertain significance (?), suggesting haploinsufficiency or dosage effects may contribute to disease phenotypes. Conservation is strong: mouse Q8BGI1 (148 aa, identical length), indicating evolutionary constraint on TRPC regulation. Functional hypothesis: RNF24 functions as a Golgi-resident RING-type E3 ubiquitin ligase that negatively regulates TRPC channel surface expression by promoting their retention in the secretory pathway. The mechanism likely involves RNF24-mediated ubiquitination of TRPC channels (or associated trafficking proteins) with K63-linked chains that alter sorting signals, preventing forward trafficking to the plasma membrane and retaining channels in the Golgi/ER. This regulatory function is critical for controlling store-operated calcium entry (SOCE) and receptor-operated calcium influx, with implications for immune cell activation (bone marrow enrichment), neuronal excitability, and smooth muscle contraction. The TRPC4/TRPC6 specificity suggests roles in endothelial permeability, podocyte function, and vascular tone. The PANK2 interaction hints at metabolic regulation of channel trafficking via CoA availability. The small size (148 aa) and Golgi localization suggest RNF24 acts as a specialized trafficking regulator rather than a general E3 ligase. Priority research: (1) identify direct ubiquitination substrates (TRPC channels vs. trafficking adaptors), (2) characterize ubiquitin chain linkage type (K48 vs. K63), (3) determine RNF24 expression in immune cells and role in calcium-dependent activation, (4) investigate RNF24 variants in calcium channelopathies or immunodeficiency, (5) structural analysis of RNF24-TRPC interaction interface, (6) test whether RNF24 regulates other membrane proteins beyond TRPC family.
