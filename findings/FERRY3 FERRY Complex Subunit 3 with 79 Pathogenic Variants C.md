## Description
FERRY3 (Ferry Endosomal RAB5 Effector Complex Subunit 3, previously C12orf4) encodes a 552-amino acid protein that is a core component of the five-subunit FERRY complex, a newly described RAB5-GAP brake essential for endo-lysosomal homeostasis. This GENUINE DARK gene has only 5 literature references despite clear clinical significance in neurodevelopmental disorders. A landmark 2026 study identified the TBCK-PPP1R21-FERRY3/C12orf4 complex as a RAB5-GAP regulatory brake, linking FERRY3 dysfunction to TBCK syndrome—a severe neurodevelopmental disorder characterized by hypotonia, intellectual disability, and progressive neurodegeneration. InterPro analysis reveals FERRY3-specific family domains (IPR019311, PF10154, PTHR16525) spanning residues 6-547, indicating a conserved structural role. AlphaFold predicts a well-folded structure with high confidence (pLDDT 83.9), consistent with stable complex formation. Human Protein Atlas shows ubiquitous expression across all tissues with low specificity, localized to nuclear bodies, with disease involvement classified as intellectual disability. STRING interactions (via C12orf4 alias) reveal the complete FERRY complex: FERRY3 binds TBCK (0.892, high confidence), PPP1R21 (0.818, high confidence), and CRYZL1 (0.627, medium confidence), plus GATD1 as the fifth subunit. ClinVar documents 79 pathogenic variants including frameshift deletions (p.Arg13fs, p.Glu14fs), nonsense mutations (p.Arg335Ter, p.Gln67Ter, p.Glu14Ter), missense variants (p.His427Arg), and copy number gains at 12p13.33-11.1. Functional hypothesis: FERRY3 serves as a scaffold subunit anchoring the FERRY complex to early endosomes, enabling TBCK's RAB5-GAP activity to terminate RAB5 signaling and maintain endo-lysosomal homeostasis. Mechanistic proposal: Loss-of-function mutations disrupt FERRY complex assembly, preventing proper RAB5-GAP regulation, causing constitutive RAB5 activation, endosomal trafficking defects, and impaired lysosomal degradation—leading to neuronal accumulation of toxic substrates, neurodegeneration, and intellectual disability. The nuclear bodies localization suggests additional roles in RNA processing or ribonucleoprotein complex assembly, potentially explaining multi-system phenotypes in TBCK syndrome.
