# PSMC3: AAA+ ATPase Motor Subunit of the 26S Proteasome Regulatory Particle with Roles in Neurodevelopmental Disorders and Cataract Formation

## Description
PSMC3 (Proteasome 26S subunit, ATPase 3, UniProt P17980) is a 439-amino acid AAA+ ATPase that forms part of the hexameric ring within the 19S regulatory particle of the 26S proteasome. It functions as a molecular motor that unfolds substrate proteins and translocates them into the 20S catalytic core for degradation. InterPro analysis reveals a canonical AAA+ ATPase domain (IPR003593, aa 219-358) containing the Walker A/B motifs essential for ATP hydrolysis, flanked by two OB-fold domains (IPR012340, IPR032501, aa 111-174) that mediate substrate recognition and binding. The AlphaFold predicted structure shows high confidence (pLDDT 80.6), confirming a stable folded architecture typical of AAA+ family members. STRING interaction data demonstrates PSMC3's integration into the proteasome holoenzyme, with strongest partnerships to other regulatory subunits PSMD7 (0.999), PSMC1 (0.999), PSMC4 (0.998), and core particle subunits PSMA2 (0.999) and PSMB7 (0.997). HPA expression profiles show ubiquitous distribution across nucleoplasm, nuclear bodies, and cytosol, consistent with housekeeping proteostasis functions. Notably, ClinVar records document 49 pathogenic variants including missense mutations in critical functional residues (p.Met259Val, p.Ala237Val, p.Glu383Lys) and in-frame deletions (p.Glu51del, p.Asp390del). HPA disease associations link PSMC3 to cataract, deafness, intellectual disability, and neuropathy, suggesting tissue-specific vulnerabilities to proteasome dysfunction. We hypothesize that PSMC3 mutations impair ATP-dependent substrate unfolding, leading to toxic protein aggregation particularly in long-lived cells like lens fiber cells (cataract) and neurons (intellectual disability, neuropathy). The cochlea's high metabolic demand may render it especially sensitive to proteostatic stress, explaining the deafness phenotype. This positions PSMC3 as a potential therapeutic target for proteasome-related neurodegenerative conditions.
