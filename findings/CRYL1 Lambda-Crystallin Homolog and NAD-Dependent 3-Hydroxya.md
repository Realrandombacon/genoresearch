## Description
CRYL1 (UniProt Q9Y2S2), also known as Lambda-crystallin homolog or Crystallin lambda 1, encodes a compact 319 aa metabolic enzyme belonging to the evolutionarily conserved short-chain dehydrogenase/reductase (SDR) and 3-hydroxyacyl-CoA dehydrogenase families, functioning as a critical regulator of fatty acid beta-oxidation, cuproptosis susceptibility, and auditory development through its NAD-dependent oxidoreductase activity. AlphaFold predicts an exceptionally high-confidence structure (global pLDDT 96.4), indicating a rigid, well-folded protein with precise geometry typical of stable metabolic enzymes, featuring a classic bi-lobed architecture. InterPro analysis reveals a modular catalytic core: an N-terminal Rossmann-fold domain (aa 1-192, G3DSA:3.40.50.720, IPR006176) for NAD+/NADH binding; a C-terminal catalytic domain (aa 193-319, G3DSA:1.10.1040.10, IPR006108, IPR013328) containing the active site for substrate recognition; and a conserved catalytic motif (aa 189-213, IPR006180) characteristic of 3-hydroxyacyl-CoA dehydrogenases involved in fatty acid metabolism (GO:0006631). STRING interactions define CRYL1 as a hub in mitochondrial and peroxisomal fatty acid oxidation: ACAA1 (0.933, Acetyl-CoA acyltransferase 1), AKR1A1 (0.927, Aldehyde reductase), HSDL2 (0.901, Hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase-like protein 2), ACAT2 (0.879, Acetyl-CoA acetyltransferase 2), SCP2 (0.878, Sterol carrier protein 2), HADHB (0.872, Trifunctional enzyme subunit beta), ACAA2 (0.742), ACAT1 (0.724), CARTPT (0.648), and ACOT8 (0.422), confirming its integration into lipid catabolic pathways. HPA expression shows strong tissue enhancement in kidney (307.7 nTPM) and liver (237.5 nTPM), classified as an enzyme and metabolic protein with oxidoreductase activity, localized to nucleoplasm, nucleoli, Golgi apparatus, and plasma membrane, suggesting potential moonlighting functions beyond canonical mitochondrial beta-oxidation. ClinVar reports 37 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including large deletions on chromosome 13q12.11 (spanning the CRYL1 locus), splice site mutations (c.276+2T>C), and copy number losses/gains, recently linked to DFNB1A non-syndromic sensorineural hearing loss in recessive inheritance patterns, establishing CRYL1 as a novel deafness gene. Literature (33 papers) highlights: (1) Fatty Acid Oxidation—Functions as a 3-hydroxyacyl-CoA dehydrogenase converting 3-hydroxyacyl-CoA to 3-ketoacyl-CoA using NAD+; (2) Cuproptosis Biomarker—Identified as a potential prognostic biomarker in clear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC) correlated with immune infiltration and copper-induced cell death; (3) Hearing Loss—Recurrent 200 kb deletions encompassing CRYL1 cause autosomal recessive deafness (DFNB1A); (4) Chimeric Transcripts—Recurrent CRYL1-IFT88 fusion transcripts identified in hepatocellular carcinoma. Functional hypothesis: CRYL1 functions as a: (1) Beta-Oxidation Enzyme—Catalyzes the NAD+-dependent oxidation step in fatty acid breakdown, essential for energy production in high-metabolic tissues like kidney and liver; (2) Cuproptosis Modulator—Regulates cellular sensitivity to copper toxicity, potentially through interaction with lipoylated TCA cycle enzymes or mitochondrial metabolism; (3) Auditory Development Factor—Required for cochlear hair cell survival or function, where defects in fatty acid metabolism lead to progressive hearing loss. Mechanistic proposal: CRYL1 utilizes its Rossmann fold to bind NAD+ and its C-terminal domain to bind 3-hydroxyacyl-CoA substrates. The very high-confidence structure (pLDDT 96.4) supports a rigid catalytic cleft optimized for efficient hydride transfer. In the kidney and liver, it drives fatty acid catabolism for energy. In the inner ear, local lipid metabolism defects caused by CRYL1 loss disrupt hair cell homeostasis, leading to DFNB1A deafness. In cancer, altered CRYL1 expression modulates cuproptosis vulnerability, possibly by affecting mitochondrial lipid intermediates that sensitize cells to copper aggregation. The combination of a stable dehydrogenase structure, fatty acid oxidation network integration, kidney/liver enrichment, hearing loss association, and emerging role in cuproptosis positions CRYL1 as a genuine dark gene where metabolic enzyme function bridges energy metabolism, metal toxicity, and sensory biology.
