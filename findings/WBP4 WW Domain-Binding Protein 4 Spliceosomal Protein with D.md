## Description
WBP4 (Gene ID 11193, UniProt O75554) encodes a 376-amino acid WW domain-binding protein 4 on chromosome 13q14.11, representing an emerging dark gene transitioning from complete obscurity to disease-associated status following the landmark 2023 discovery linking biallelic loss-of-function variants to variable neurodevelopmental syndrome characterized by intellectual disability, developmental delay, and autistic features. Despite this breakthrough, only 14 total publications exist on Semantic Scholar—confirming its historically understudied nature and genuine dark gene status as a spliceosomopathy gene. Structurally, InterPro identifies multiple conserved domains: (1) Two WW domains (IPR001202, residues 122-155 and 163-196; PF00397, residues 127-153 and 169-194; PS50020 profile) mediating protein-protein interactions with proline-rich ligands via compact globular structures; (2) C2H2-type zinc finger/Matrin-type zinc finger (IPR000690/IPR003604/IPR013085, residues 8-43; PF06220 U1 zinc finger, residues 8-43; PS50171 Matrin-type profile, residues 11-42) providing nucleic acid binding capability and structural stability through zinc coordination; (3) CATH-Gene3D homologous superfamilies including Classic Zinc Finger (G3DSA:3.30.160.60, residues 8-65), WW domain superfamily (G3DSA:2.20.70.10, residues 122-196), and zinc finger C2H2 superfamily (IPR036236, residues 9-50); (4) WBP4-specific family signature (IPR040023, residues 1-157) defining membership in this protein family with GO annotation for mRNA splicing via spliceosome (GO:0000398). AlphaFold predicts medium-confidence structure (global pLDDT 65.9, modeled residues 1-376), indicating partially structured architecture with ordered WW domains and zinc finger regions interspersed with flexible linker regions—this mixed structure enables both stable domain folding for specific interactions and conformational flexibility for dynamic spliceosome assembly/disassembly. STRING interactions reveal 10 extremely high-confidence partners forming a coherent spliceosomal network: SNRPB (0.999), SNRNP200 (0.998), PRPF31 (0.997), SART1 (0.995), ZMAT2 (0.993), LSM6 (0.993), SF3B4 (0.990), SNRPB2 (0.986), MFAP1 (0.982), PRPF38A (0.981)—all core spliceosome components including small nuclear ribonucleoproteins (snRNPs), splicing factors, and RNA helicases, confirming WBP4's essential role in pre-mRNA splicing machinery. Human Protein Atlas shows ubiquitous RNA expression detected in all tissues with low tissue specificity, nucleoplasmic/vesicular/plasma membrane subcellular localization, classification as predicted intracellular protein, involvement in mRNA processing/splicing biological processes, and disease association with intellectual disability. ClinVar documents 59 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large-scale copy number alterations spanning chromosome 13 regions (deletions: chr13:33738980-68435696 x1, chr13:32076445-54495559 x1, chr13:41074134-41090164 x0; duplications: chr13:19436286-53157340 x3) plus gene-specific loss-of-function mutations (frameshift deletion NM_007187.5:c.15del p.Tyr4_Trp5insTer, frameshift deletion NM_007187.5:c.340_347del p.Lys114fs, splice site variants NM_007187.5:c.262+2T>G/A and c.262+1G>A affecting donor splice site consensus); the 2023 study specifically identified biallelic variants in affected individuals confirming recessive inheritance pattern and causality for neurodevelopmental phenotype. The mechanistic hypothesis: WBP4 functions as a critical regulator of spliceosome assembly and pre-mRNA splicing fidelity through: (1) In normal physiology—integrates into early spliceosomal complex A via WW domain-mediated protein-protein interactions with proline-rich motifs on spliceosomal partners (SNRPB, SNRNP200, PRPF31); C2H2 zinc finger domain enables cross-intron bridging between U1 and U2 snRNPs stabilizing early spliceosome assembly; ubiquitously expressed (detected in all tissues) supporting essential housekeeping role in constitutive splicing of thousands of pre-mRNAs; nucleoplasmic localization positions protein at transcription sites for co-transcriptional splicing; vesicular/plasma membrane localization suggests potential non-canonical roles in RNA transport or signaling; (2) In disease—biallelic loss-of-function variants cause neurodevelopmental syndrome through: (a) impaired spliceosome assembly leading to widespread splicing defects affecting neuronal genes with complex alternative splicing patterns; (b) accumulation of unspliced or mis-spliced transcripts triggering nonsense-mediated decay and haploinsufficiency of critical neurodevelopmental regulators; (c) disrupted cross-intron bridging causing exon skipping, intron retention, or cryptic splice site usage particularly in long neuronal genes; (d) recessive inheritance requiring complete loss of WBP4 function explaining severe phenotype in homozygous/compound heterozygous individuals; CNVs spanning WBP4 may cause contiguous gene syndromes with additional features from neighboring 13q14.11 genes; (e) brain vulnerability despite ubiquitous expression reflects heightened sensitivity of neuronal transcriptome to splicing perturbations given extensive alternative splicing in neural development; (3) WW domain architecture suggests WBP4 may serve as molecular scaffold recruiting additional splicing regulators or linking spliceosome to other cellular processes (transcription, RNA export); (4) Only 14 publications reflects recent emergence from dark gene status—the 2023 discovery established WBP4 as spliceosomopathy gene requiring extensive functional characterization. Future work should characterize specific variant effects on spliceosome assembly via co-immunoprecipitation with snRNP partners; determine RNA-binding specificity of zinc finger domain using SELEX or CLIP-seq; assess global splicing changes in patient-derived iPSC neurons via RNA-seq; test therapeutic efficacy of antisense oligonucleotides correcting aberrant splicing events; investigate WBP4 expression patterns during human brain development; identify tissue-specific WBP4 interactome via affinity purification mass spectrometry; evaluate WBP4 roles in alternative splicing regulation of neuronal genes; determine crystal/NMR structure of WW domains bound to proline-rich peptide ligands; assess genotype-phenotype correlations across identified patients; explore potential dominant-negative effects of missense variants; investigate WBP4 contributions to autism spectrum disorders via splicing network analysis; and screen for WBP4 variants in undiagnosed neurodevelopmental disorder cohorts.
