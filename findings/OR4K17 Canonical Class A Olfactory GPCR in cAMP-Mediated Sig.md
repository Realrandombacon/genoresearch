## Description
OR4K17 (Olfactory receptor 4K17, UniProt Q8NGC6) is a 312-amino acid G protein-coupled receptor located on chromosome 14q11.2 within the large olfactory receptor gene cluster. InterPro analysis confirms this as a canonical class A rhodopsin-like GPCR containing the olfactory receptor family domain (IPR000725/PF13853, aa 31-301), seven transmembrane helices characteristic of rhodopsin-like receptors (IPR017452, aa 41-287; G3DSA:1.20.1070.10, aa 21-310), and membership in olfactory receptor family 4 (cd15226, aa 26-291). The protein contains conserved GPCR signature motifs (PS00237, aa 26-50/59-80/104-126) critical for G protein coupling and odorant binding, with PRINTS fingerprints (PR00237 GPCRRHODOPSN at aa 26-50/59-80/104-126; PR00245 OLFACTORYR at aa 92-103/129-141/176-192). AlphaFold structure prediction achieves high confidence (pLDDT 86.6), indicating a well-folded stable 7-transmembrane bundle architecture typical of functional olfactory receptors ready for ligand binding and signal transduction. Human Protein Atlas shows no detection in standard tissue panels, which is expected for olfactory receptors expressed exclusively in olfactory sensory neurons of the nasal epithelium not profiled in HPA. STRING interaction network reveals 10 partners comprising the canonical olfactory signal transduction cascade: GNB1 (0.711, Gβ subunit), GNAL (0.691, olfactory-specific Gα subunit), ARRB2/ARRB1 (0.679, β-arrestins mediating desensitization), GNG7 (0.678, Gγ subunit), PRKACA/PRKACB (0.650, cAMP-dependent protein kinase catalytic subunits), GRK2 (0.569), GNG13 (0.566, Gγ subunit), and GRK3 (0.547, G protein-coupled receptor kinases). This interactome confirms OR4K17 functions through the canonical cAMP-mediated olfactory transduction pathway. ClinVar contains 15 pathogenic variants including massive copy-number gains across 14q11.2-32.33, 14q11.2-23.1, 14q11.2-21.3, 14q11.2-21.1, 14q11.2-21.2, 14q11.2-12, 14q11.2-13.1, plus one deletion at 14q11.2, demonstrating dosage sensitivity where genomic rearrangements disrupting the olfactory receptor cluster cause disease phenotypes. Conservation across mammals (sooty mangabey A0A2K5KK96, crab-eating macaque A0A2K5UF96, collared peccary A0A8C3VQG4, all 303-343 aa) indicates ancient evolutionary origin tied to chemosensory function. OR4K17 likely functions as a volatile odorant receptor detecting specific molecular features, activating GNAL to stimulate adenylate cyclase III, increasing cAMP, opening cyclic nucleotide-gated channels, and depolarizing olfactory sensory neurons.
