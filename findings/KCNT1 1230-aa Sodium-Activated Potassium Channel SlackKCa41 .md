## Description
KCNT1 (Potassium Sodium-Activated Channel Subfamily T Member 1; UniProt Q5JUK3), also known as Slack or KCa4.1, is a 1230 amino acid voltage-gated potassium channel with extensive clinical significance, representing a moderately characterized sodium-activated potassium channel that regulates neuronal firing patterns, circadian rhythms, and neurotransmitter release through intracellular sodium sensing and RCK domain-mediated gating mechanisms.\n\nSTRUCTURAL ARCHITECTURE: InterPro analysis reveals a complex multi-domain architecture optimized for sodium-dependent potassium channel gating. The N-terminal region contains a Regulator of K+ conductance (RCK) N-terminal lobe (IPR003148/PS51201/PF22614, residues 351-488/352-488/351-470) forming the first sodium-sensing domain, with a second RCK domain (residues 780-921/781-921/780-900) creating a tandem gating apparatus characteristic of Slo family channels. The pore-forming region contains a Potassium channel domain (IPR013099/PF07885, residues 260-327) with the conserved selectivity filter motif. The channel belongs to the Calcium-activated BK potassium channel alpha subunit family (IPR003929/PF03493, residues 485-579) and the broader Slowpoke-like channel superfamily (IPR047871/PTHR10027, residues 66-1195). The structure contains two NAD(P)-binding Rossmann-like domains (G3DSA:3.40.50.720, residues 346-488 and 775-917) that form the RCK gating ring.
