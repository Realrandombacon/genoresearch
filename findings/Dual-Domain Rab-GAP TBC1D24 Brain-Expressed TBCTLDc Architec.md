## Description
TBC1D24 (UniProt Q9ULP9), also known as EIEE16, DEE16, DFNA65, DFNB86, or DOORS syndrome protein, encodes a 559 aa dual-domain protein functioning as a Rab GTPase-activating protein (Rab-GAP) that regulates vesicular trafficking critical for neuronal development and function. InterPro analysis reveals a bipartite architecture characteristic of the TBC-TLDc protein family: an N-terminal Rab-GAP-TBC domain (IPR000195/PF00566/SM00164, residues 42-259/49-253) containing the catalytic TBC domain with conserved arginine finger and glutamine finger residues for GTP hydrolysis acceleration within the Ypt/Rab-GAP superfamily (G3DSA:1.10.472.80/SSF47923, residues 109-159/180-293/167-314); and a C-terminal TLDc domain (IPR006571/PF07534/SM00584/PS51886, residues 340-554/340-445/493-554/343-554) belonging to the TLDc oxidoreductase family (PTHR23354, residues 96-555) though catalytic activity remains unconfirmed, potentially serving structural or protein-interaction roles. AlphaFold predicts high confidence structure (pLDDT 84.1) indicating a well-folded protein where the TBC and TLDc domains are connected by a linker enabling coordinated regulation of trafficking and potential redox sensing. STRING interactions define a neuronal trafficking-excitability network: ARF6 (0.553, ADP-ribosylation factor 6, small GTPase regulating endocytic recycling), RAB35 (0.542, Rab GTPase controlling cytokinesis and neurite outgrowth), KCNT1 (0.632, potassium sodium-activated channel subfamily T member 1, epilepsy-associated ion channel), SCN1A (0.597, voltage-gated sodium channel alpha subunit 1, Dravet syndrome gene), PRRT2 (0.586, proline-rich transmembrane protein 2, paroxysmal kinesigenic dyskinesia), NEU1 (0.615, neuraminidase 1, lysosomal enzyme), SLC25A22 (0.614, mitochondrial glutamate transporter, early infantile epileptic encephalopathy), CDC16 (0.677, cell division cycle 16, anaphase-promoting complex subunit), NANP (0.695), and USP6 (0.598, ubiquitin-specific peptidase). The ARF6/RAB35 connections strongly support Rab-GAP function while KCNT1/SCN1A/PRRT2 links explain epilepsy phenotypes. HPA classifies TBC1D24 as tissue-enhanced with highest expression in salivary gland (15.5 nTPM), detected in many tissues, cancer-enhanced, localized to plasma membrane and cell junctions, reflecting roles in membrane trafficking and synaptic organization. ClinVar lists 284 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including frameshift deletions (c.61del p.Asp21fs, c.400del p.Leu134fs), nonsense mutations (c.1198C>T p.Gln400Ter, c.937C>T p.Gln313Ter), missense variants affecting catalytic residues (c.707G>T p.Gly236Val, c.758A>G p.Lys253Arg, c.214A>G p.Ser72Gly in TBC domain), and splice site mutations (c.1526-1G>C) causing a spectrum of disorders: DOORS syndrome (Deafness, Onychodystrophy, Osteodystrophy, intellectual disability, Seizures), developmental and epileptic encephalopathy-16 (DEE16/EIEE16), nonsyndromic deafness (DFNB86 recessive, DFNA65 dominant), and familial infantile myoclonic epilepsy (FIME). Functional hypothesis: TBC1D24 operates through integrated trafficking-excitability mechanisms: (1) Rab-GAP catalysis - Accelerates GTP hydrolysis on ARF6/RAB35 switching them from active GTP-bound to inactive GDP-bound state, controlling endocytic recycling of neurotransmitter receptors and adhesion molecules at synapses; (2) Synaptic vesicle trafficking - Regulates presynaptic vesicle pool maintenance and postsynaptic receptor density through ARF6-dependent pathways; (3) Neuronal migration - Controls cortical neuron positioning during development via RAB35-mediated cytokinesis and process extension; (4) Ion channel modulation - Physical or functional interaction with KCNT1/SCN1A channels modulating neuronal excitability thresholds; (5) Disease mechanism - Loss-of-function variants impair synaptic trafficking causing hyperexcitability (epilepsy) and sensory deficits (deafness); dominant-negative missense mutations may trap Rab substrates in inactive state; TLDc domain mutations could disrupt protein stability or partner binding; (6) Conservation - Mouse Tbc1d24 (Q3UUG6, 561 aa), Rat Tbc1d24 (A0A0G2K5B0, 562 aa) indicate essential neuronal function. Dark aspects requiring investigation include: identification of specific Rab substrate repertoire beyond ARF6/RAB35, structural basis for TLDc domain function (oxidoreductase vs scaffold), mechanism linking trafficking defects to specific epilepsy phenotypes, genotype-phenotype correlations explaining syndrome vs isolated deafness, therapeutic strategies targeting Rab-GAP activity, role in non-neuronal tissues (salivary gland enrichment), post-translational modifications regulating GAP activity, and crosstalk with other epilepsy genes (SCN1A, KCNT1) in shared pathways.
