## Description
ITLN1 encodes a 313 amino acid secreted lectin (UniProt Q8WWA0), also known as Intelectin-1 or Omentin-1, that functions as a calcium-dependent microbial pattern recognition receptor and adipokine linking gut immunity to systemic metabolism. With 158 publications, ITLN1 is moderately characterized—its roles in inflammatory bowel disease and metabolic syndrome are established but mechanistic details of glycan recognition remain incompletely understood.\n\nSTRUCTURAL FEATURES: AlphaFold predicts a very high-confidence structure (pLDDT 92.4) across all 313 residues, indicating a well-folded stable protein with defined tertiary structure characteristic of fibrinogen-like lectins. While no specific monogenic disease is yet annotated, recent literature links ITLN1 to Crohn's disease pathogenesis and atherosclerosis progression.\n\nFUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: ITLN1 functions through two coordinated mechanisms: (1) Microbial glycan recognition—the fibrinogen-like domain (residues 17-238) binds calcium and recognizes microbial glycans including galactofuranose-containing carbohydrates on bacterial cell walls, acting as a pattern recognition receptor in innate immunity. (2) Metabolic regulation—as an adipokine (omentin-1), ITLN1 enhances insulin sensitivity through AMPK activation and suppresses inflammation via TNF-α inhibition. Recent literature (2025-2026) reveals critical disease roles: ITLN1 exacerbates Crohn's colitis by driving ZBP1-dependent PANoptosis in intestinal epithelial cells through antagonizing TRIM8-mediated CAPN2 ubiquitination (11 citations); ITLN1+ foam cells interact with SPP1+ macrophages in atherosclerotic plaques (9 citations); ITLN1 orchestrated by IFNγ-IRF1 axis suppresses hepatocellular carcinoma proliferation via ERK1/2 activation; genetic links between obesity/insulin resistance and breast cancer through ITLN1 SNPs. Mechanistically, the conserved YCDxxxxGGGW motif (residues 40-82) likely coordinates calcium binding essential for glycan recognition. Loss-of-function may impair microbial clearance but reduce intestinal inflammation; gain-of-function drives PANoptosis in Crohn's disease and promotes foam cell formation in atherosclerosis.
