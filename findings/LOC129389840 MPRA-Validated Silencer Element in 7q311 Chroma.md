## Description
LOC129389840 (Gene ID: 129389840) is a functionally validated cis-regulatory silencer element mapping to chromosome 7q31.1 (108,274,580-108,274,780 bp, ~200 bp span). RefSeq annotation explicitly states: "MPRA-validated peak6682 silencer" - validated by STARR-seq massively parallel reporter assays in K562 erythroleukemia cells as repressing super core promoter SCP1 activity. Multiple evidence lines confirm non-coding regulatory function: (1) No UniProt accession (not a protein); (2) Genomic span of 200 bp is far too small for protein-coding gene; (3) ClinVar contains 5 pathogenic copy number variants spanning 7q22.1-31.31, 7q21.3-36.3, and 7p22.3-q36.3 regions (deletions 102-121 Mb, 54 kb-159 Mb; duplication 97-159 Mb whole chromosome arm), associated with 7q deletion/duplication syndromes featuring intellectual disability, developmental delay, growth abnormalities, craniofacial dysmorphism, cardiac/renal defects, and skeletal anomalies. The 7q31 region contains critical dosage-sensitive genes including MET (hepatocyte growth factor receptor, cell proliferation, cancer), BRAF (serine/threonine kinase, RAS-MAPK signaling, cardiofaciocutaneous syndrome), and FOXP2 (forkhead box P2, speech/language development, verbal dyspraxia); (4) No HPA expression or STRING interactions (non-coding). Mechanistic hypothesis: LOC129389840 functions as a transcriptional silencer that represses gene expression in hematopoietic or neural contexts. The element likely recruits co-repressor complexes (e.g., Polycomb repressive complex 2, NuRD, or HDAC-containing complexes) via sequence-specific DNA-binding proteins to establish repressive chromatin (H3K27me3, H3K9me3, DNA methylation). Pathogenic 7q CNVs disrupt silencer-enhancer balance and topologically associating domain (TAD) architecture, causing long-range dysregulation of MET, BRAF, FOXP2, and other 7q genes leading to neurodevelopmental phenotypes and growth disorders. MET is a critical growth regulator - silencer disruption may alter MET expression contributing to developmental overgrowth and cancer predisposition in 7q deletion carriers. FOXP2 mutations cause speech/language disorders - altered regulation may contribute to verbal dyspraxia phenotypes. This represents a critical class of "dark genes" that are actually functional non-coding regulatory elements misannotated as genes due to automated pipeline predictions. The MPRA validation provides direct functional evidence of regulatory activity in erythroid lineage.
