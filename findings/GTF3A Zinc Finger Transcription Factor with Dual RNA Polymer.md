## Description
GTF3A encodes a 365 aa transcription factor IIIA (UniProt Q92664) located at 13q11, functioning as a dual-specificity regulator of RNA polymerase III (Pol III) transcription and innate antiviral immunity. InterPro analysis reveals a complex zinc finger architecture: nine C2H2-type zinc fingers (IPR013087/PF00096/PS00028, aa 40-125) forming the classic DNA-binding domain, plus a unique beta-beta-alpha zinc finger domain (IPR054599/PF22110, aa 178-208) that confers RNA-binding specificity to 5S rRNA. AlphaFold predicts medium-confidence structure (pLDDT 69.7), suggesting a partially ordered protein with potential intrinsically disordered regions. STRING interactions confirm core Pol III machinery: GTF3C1 (0.991), BRF1 (0.887), BDP1 (0.871), GTF3C5 (0.777), TBP (0.772), and POLR3C (0.684), establishing GTF3A as a central scaffold in the Pol III pre-initiation complex. HPA shows ubiquitous expression (detected in all tissues), nucleoplasmic localization, and classification as an essential protein involved in ribosome biogenesis and transcription regulation. ClinVar reports 16 pathogenic variants including missense (p.Leu87Val), splice site, and multiple 13q11-34 copy number gains. Literature (29 papers) details: (1) GTF3A mutations predisposing to herpes simplex encephalitis (HSE) by disrupting biogenesis of the host-derived RIG-I ligand RNA5SP141 (24 citations, 2022); (2) GTF3A mutations impairing antiviral immunity (0 citations, 2022); (3) teleost gene duplication with ovarian-specific gtf3ab expression (6 citations, 2020); (4) molecular cloning and chromosomal mapping (25 citations, 1995). Mechanistically, GTF3A functions through: (1) 5S rRNA Binding - nine zinc fingers bind 5S rRNA with high specificity; (2) Pol III Recruitment - bridging 5S rRNA to the Pol III machinery via GTF3C1/BRF1 interactions; (3) Innate Immunity Regulation - generating RNA5SP141, a non-coding RNA that activates RIG-I-mediated type I interferon response against HSV-1; (4) Transcriptional Autoregulation - feedback control of its own expression. The dual role in ribosome biogenesis and antiviral defense represents a critical nexus between cellular metabolism and immune surveillance. Disease implications include: herpes simplex encephalitis susceptibility, potential cancer links (13q11-34 amplifications in various cancers), and developmental disorders from Pol III dysfunction. This represents a partially characterized gene where the Pol III role is established but the immunological functions and disease mechanisms remain underexplored.
