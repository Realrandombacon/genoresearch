# LRRC8E: Volume-Regulated Anion Channel Subunit with Leucine-Rich Repeat Extracellular Domain and 19p13 Dosage Sensitivity

## Description
LRRC8E (Gene ID: 80131, UniProt Q6NSJ5) is a 796 amino acid transmembrane protein located on chromosome 19p13.2 (NC_000019.10, positions 7888509-7902015, ~13.5kb genomic region). This gene encodes an essential subunit of the volume-regulated anion channel (VRAC) complex, which mediates regulatory volume decrease (RVD) in response to cell swelling. InterPro domain analysis reveals a sophisticated multi-domain architecture: N-terminal pannexin-like transmembrane region (IPR021040/PF12534, positions 1-331, ~331 residues forming 4-6 transmembrane helices), followed by C-terminal leucine-rich repeat (LRR) extracellular domain comprising multiple LRR motifs (IPR001611/PF00560/PF13855 at positions 584-642, 654-675, 677-734; IPR003591 typical subtype at 604-628, 629-651, 652-675), and ribonuclease inhibitor-like superfamily folds (G3DSA:3.80.10.10 at positions 387-653 and 654-779). The bipartite topology places the TM region in the membrane pore and the LRR domain in the extracellular space, suggesting roles in both ion conduction and ligand sensing. GO annotations include ion channel activity (GO:0005261), ion transport (GO:0006811), and protein binding (GO:0005515). STRING interaction network identifies 10 medium-confidence partners forming the VRAC interactome: LRRC8A (0.522, essential VRAC subunit required for channel function), LRRC8B (0.634, VRAC subunit B), LRRC8D (0.523, VRAC subunit D), BEST1 (0.576, bestrophin-1 calcium-activated chloride channel), TTYH1 (0.547, tweety homolog 1 chloride channel), TTYH2 (0.570), TTYH3 (0.501), CLCN3 (0.524, chloride channel protein 3, endosomal/lysosomal), TRIM2 (0.650, tripartite motif-containing protein 2, E3 ubiquitin ligase), and KERA (0.486, keratocan, corneal proteoglycan). The LRRC8A/B/D interactions confirm VRAC complex assembly—LRRC8A is obligatory while LRRC8C/E determine substrate selectivity. Human Protein Atlas classifies LRRC8E (ENSG00000171017) as a predicted membrane protein and transporter with ion channel molecular function and ion transport biological process. RNA expression shows low tissue specificity with detection in many tissues, low cancer specificity, low immune cell specificity, but cell type-enhanced single cell specificity—indicating ubiquitous housekeeping expression with cell type modulation consistent with fundamental osmoregulatory function. AlphaFold structure prediction (AF-Q6NSJ5-F1) yields high-confidence model (global pLDDT 84.1), indicating a well-folded stable architecture across all 796 residues—among the highest confidence structures for dark genes, suggesting strong evolutionary constraint and defined tertiary structure. The structure likely comprises N-terminal transmembrane helices forming the ion pore and C-terminal solenoid LRR domain for extracellular interactions. ClinVar lists 6 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including one missense mutation (NM_025061.6:c.1777G>A, p.Gly593Arg in LRR domain) and 5 large chromosomal copy number gains spanning chromosome 19p13 regions (e.g., chr19:3120160-9732820 triplication, chr19:260912-58956888 duplication/triplication, chr19:1972245-9648879 triplication); no specific monogenic disease is attributed solely to LRRC8E, suggesting dosage sensitivity within larger chromosomal syndromes. Conservation is strong across vertebrates: mouse (Q66JT1, 795 aa), rat (Q3KRC6, 795 aa), Xenopus laevis (Q6NU09, 806 aa), Xenopus tropicalis (Q68F79, 806 aa)—indicating critical functional importance. Functional hypothesis: LRRC8E functions as an auxiliary VRAC subunit that heteromerizes with obligatory LRRC8A and other LRRC8 paralogs (B, C, D) to form functional volume-regulated anion channels. The pannexin-like TM region (residues 1-331) forms the ion-conducting pore with 4-6 transmembrane helices, while the extensive LRR extracellular domain (residues 584-779) mediates protein-protein interactions, substrate selectivity, and potentially ligand sensing. LRRC8E incorporation into VRAC complexes determines permeability to organic osmolytes (taurine, glutamate) versus inorganic anions (Cl-). TRIM2 interaction suggests ubiquitin-mediated regulation of channel turnover or trafficking. BEST1 and TTYH family interactions indicate functional coupling with other chloride channel systems. High-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 84.1) enables precise mapping of disease variants—the p.Gly593Arg mutation falls within the LRR domain and may disrupt protein folding or inter-subunit interactions. Ubiquitous expression reflects fundamental role in cellular osmoregulation across all cell types. Disease mechanism: LRRC8E haploinsufficiency or overexpression from chromosome 19p13 CNVs may cause osmoregulatory defects contributing to neurological phenotypes (19p13 deletion syndrome features include intellectual disability, seizures, macrocephaly). The p.Gly593Arg missense variant in the LRR domain could impair channel assembly or gating. VRAC dysfunction affects cell volume homeostasis, apoptosis regulation, neurotransmitter release (glutamate, taurine), and drug transport (cisplatin uptake). LRRC8E may contribute to chemotherapy resistance (reduced cisplatin uptake), epilepsy (altered glutamate release), or developmental disorders (impaired cell migration requiring volume changes). Priority experiments include patch-clamp electrophysiology of LRRC8E-containing VRAC complexes to measure ion selectivity and conductance, co-IP validation of LRRC8A/B/D heteromerization, cryo-EM structure determination leveraging high-confidence AlphaFold model (pLDDT 84.1), investigation of p.Gly593Arg variant effects on channel function via mutant expression studies, assessment of LRRC8E role in cisplatin sensitivity using knockout models, and analysis of LRRC8E expression in 19p13 deletion syndrome patient cells.
