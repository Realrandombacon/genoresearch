## Description
NT5DC2 (5'-Nucleotidase Domain Containing 2) is a 520 amino acid cytosolic hydrolase belonging to the cNT5-II family that catalyzes nucleotide dephosphorylation. InterPro analysis reveals a canonical HAD (Haloacid Dehalogenase) superfamily fold (IPR023214, IPR036412, G3DSA:3.40.50.1000, SSF56784) spanning residues 211-407, with a specialized cytosolic 5'-nucleotidase II domain (cd07522, residues 57-471) characteristic of purine nucleotide metabolism enzymes. The protein belongs to the 5' nucleotidase family (PF05761, residues 56-496; IPR008380, residues 37-496) and purine 5'-nucleotidase superfamily (IPR016695, PIRSF017434, residues 40-509). AlphaFold structure (AF-Q9H857-F1) predicts high confidence (pLDDT 86.1) across all 520 residues, indicating a stable catalytic core typical of HAD hydrolases with conserved active site architecture. HPA data confirms ubiquitous expression with immune cell enhancement and specific subcellular localization patterns; the protein functions as an intracellular hydrolase. STRING interactions reveal functional coupling to its paralog NT5DC3 (0.639), the transmembrane protein SMIM4 (0.512), STIMATE/STIM1 (0.510) involved in calcium signaling, centromere protein CENPW (0.494), ubiquitin-conjugating enzyme UBE2T (0.493), and tyrosine hydroxylase TH (0.443), the rate-limiting enzyme in catecholamine biosynthesis. ClinVar lists 11 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including splice site mutations (c.1119+2T>G) and large copy number losses/gains on chromosome 3p, though specific monogenic diseases remain undefined. Recent literature demonstrates NT5DC2 knockdown suppresses monoamine oxidase activity while increasing catecholamine levels in PC12D cells, and promotes triple-negative breast cancer progression through EGFR pathway activation and glycolysis enhancement. With only 56 publications despite clear metabolic and oncogenic functions, NT5DC2 represents an undercharacterized nucleotide hydrolase linking purine metabolism to neurotransmitter synthesis and tumor metabolism.
