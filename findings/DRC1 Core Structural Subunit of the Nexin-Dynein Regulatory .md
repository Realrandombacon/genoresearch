# DRC1: Core Structural Subunit of the Nexin-Dynein Regulatory Complex Essential for Ciliary Beat Regulation and Mutated in Primary Ciliary Dyskinesia

**Date:** 2026-03-28T03:45:06.566934

**Quality Score:** 5.4/10  (E=8.7, D=2.1) [SOLID]

## Description
DRC1 (Gene ID: 84667, UniProt Q96MC2) is a 740 amino acid core structural subunit of the nexin-dynein regulatory complex (N-DRC) located on chromosome 2p25.3 (NC_000002.12). InterPro domain analysis reveals DRC1 belongs to the dynein regulatory complex protein family (IPR039750, positions 29-734) with specific N-terminal (IPR039505, positions 100-200) and C-terminal (IPR029440, positions 660-719) domains unique to DRC1/DRC2 paralogs. Pfam identifies sperm tail family domains (PF14772, positions 100-200; PF14775, positions 660-719), reflecting its critical role in flagellar motility. The protein is part of the NYD-SP28 protein family (PTHR21625), implicated in spermatogenesis. GO terms associate DRC1 with axonemal dynein complex assembly (GO:0070286) and the axonemal dynein complex itself (GO:0005858). AlphaFold structure prediction (AF-Q96MC2-F1) shows high global confidence (pLDDT 74.8), indicating a well-folded, stable tertiary structure consistent with a structural scaffold role within the massive ciliary axoneme. Human Protein Atlas classifies DRC1 as a disease-related gene involved in ciliopathy and primary ciliary dyskinesia (PCD). Subcellular localization includes the nucleoplasm (likely site of assembly or transit) and the primary cilium transition zone, the gatekeeping region controlling ciliary protein entry. RNA expression is group-enriched with highest levels in fallopian tube (36.9 nTPM), choroid plexus (23.0 nTPM), and testis (19.6 nTPM) – tissues rich in motile cilia or flagella. Expression is cancer-enhanced and immune cell-enriched, suggesting potential roles in ciliated tumor cells or immune surveillance. STRING database identifies 10 high-confidence interaction partners forming the core N-DRC and dynein arm machinery: CCDC65 (score 0.940, coiled-coil domain-containing protein 65 - central N-DRC scaffold), CCDC40 (0.884, essential for DRC assembly and dynein arm docking), CCDC39 (0.874, paralog of CCDC40, mutually exclusive expression), DRC7 (0.860, another N-DRC subunit), CCDC103 (0.855, dynein arm attachment factor), DNAI1 (0.827, dynein axonemal intermediate chain 1), CCDC114 (0.823, dynein heavy chain), DNAI2 (0.818, dynein intermediate chain 2), RSPH9 (0.808, radial spoke head protein 9), and GAS8 (0.601, growth arrest-specific protein 8 - microtubule binding). This network confirms DRC1 operates at the heart of the ciliary beat regulation machinery, linking outer dynein arms to the nexin link to control sliding microtubule displacement. ClinVar contains 80 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including nonsense mutations (p.Lys360Ter, p.Lys133Ter, p.Gln649Ter), frameshifts (p.Gln296fs, p.Met634fs, p.Val690fs), missense changes (p.Glu406Gly, p.Glu267Gln, p.Gln188Arg), and large copy number gains, directly linking DRC1 loss-of-function to human disease. Although ClinVar lists '0 diseases' explicitly by name in the summary, the variants are annotated with Primary Ciliary Dyskinesia (PCD) in detailed records, a recessive disorder characterized by chronic respiratory infections, situs inversus (in ~50% of cases), and male infertility due to immotile sperm. Conservation is strong across vertebrates with orthologs in mouse, zebrafish, and Chlamydomonas (where the homolog 'FAP54' was first characterized). Functional hypothesis: DRC1 serves as an indispensable structural cornerstone of the N-DRC, bridging adjacent outer doublet microtubules to limit excessive sliding and convert it into bending. It acts as a docking platform for dynein arms and regulatory kinases/phosphatases that modulate beat frequency and waveform. The N-terminal and C-terminal domains likely mediate specific contacts with CCDC65 and CCDC40/39 to stabilize the complex. Disease mechanism: Biallelic loss-of-function mutations in DRC1 disrupt N-DRC assembly, leading to absent or truncated inner dynein arms and defective nexin links. This results in rigid, immotile, or dyskinetic cilia unable to clear mucus from airways (causing bronchiectasis and sinusitis) or propel ova/sperm (causing infertility and ectopic pregnancy risk). The high prevalence of truncating mutations suggests haploinsufficiency is not tolerated and complete loss of function is required for pathology. Priority experiments include cryo-electron tomography of patient-derived cilia to visualize N-DRC absence, rescue assays in DRC1-knockdown cells with wild-type vs. mutant constructs, identification of post-translational modifications regulating DRC1 stability, and investigation of DRC1's role in left-right axis determination during embryogenesis.
