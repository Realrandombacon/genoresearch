## Description
ZNF490 encodes a 529 amino acid Krüppel-associated box (KRAB) C2H2 zinc finger transcription factor. InterPro analysis reveals a canonical KRAB repression domain (IPR001909, PF01352, residues 57-97) followed by three tandem C2H2 zinc finger DNA-binding domains (IPR013087, PF00096, residues 157-244), establishing the archetypal KRAB-ZNF repressor architecture. The STRING interaction network identifies 10 medium-confidence partners (0.429-0.589), notably including TRIM28 (0.567), the universal KRAB domain co-repressor that recruits heterochromatin protein 1 (HP1) and histone methyltransferases for transcriptional silencing. Additional partners include NBPF3 (0.589), PRADC1 (0.571), and ZNF283 (0.460), suggesting homomeric or heteromeric zinc finger clustering. Human Protein Atlas classifies ZNF490 as a transcription factor with DNA-binding activity, detected in many tissues with cancer-enriched expression, consistent with roles in cell proliferation control. ClinVar documents 20 pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number losses and gains spanning 19p13.2, demonstrating extreme dosage sensitivity typical of haploinsufficient regulatory genes. The 19p13.2 region is associated with developmental phenotypes when disrupted. The convergence of KRAB repression domain, TRIM28 interaction, C2H2 zinc finger DNA recognition, and CNV pathogenicity supports a model where ZNF490 functions as a sequence-specific transcriptional silencer, recruiting the TRIM28/HP1 heterochromatin machinery to target promoters for epigenetic repression, with copy number alterations disrupting gene regulatory networks and contributing to developmental or oncogenic phenotypes.
