## Description
PSMG2 (Proteasome Assembly Chaperone 2, also known as PAC2) is a partially characterized dark gene with only 23 publications, representing a critical but under-studied component of the proteasome assembly machinery with emerging roles in autoimmunity and cancer therapy resistance. UniProt accession Q969U7 identifies it as a 264-amino acid intracellular chaperone. InterPro scan reveals a distinctive dual-domain architecture characteristic of the PAC2 family: G3DSA:3.40.50.10900 (PAC-like subunit, residues 14-166 and 180-264, indicating two structurally similar domains), IPR016562 (Proteasome assembly chaperone 2, eukaryotic, 1-264), IPR019151 (Proteasome assembly chaperone 2, 17-230), IPR038389 (Proteasome assembly chaperone 2 superfamily, 14-166/180-264), PF09754 (PAC2 family, 17-230), PIRSF010044 (Uncharacterised conserved protein, 16-256), PTHR12970 (Proteasome assembly chaperone 2, 1-264), and SSF159659 (Cgl1923-like, 9-125). The two PAC-like domains likely form a V-shaped scaffold that binds proteasome precursors.\n\nAlphaFold predicts a very high-confidence structure (global pLDDT 92.5), indicating PSMG2 is an extremely well-folded, stable protein with precise tertiary structure required for specific chaperone-substrate recognition. The high confidence across both domains suggests a rigid structural role in complex assembly.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals 10 partners with exceptionally high confidence, defining a dedicated proteasome assembly module: PSMG1 (0.999 - Proteasome assembly chaperone 1, partner in heterodimer), PSMG4 (0.998 - Proteasome assembly chaperone 4, functional paralog), POMP (0.997 - Proteasome maturation protein, sequential assembly factor), PSMA1 (0.995 - Proteasome subunit alpha type-1, direct substrate), PSMB2 (0.981 - Proteasome subunit beta type-2), PSMB4 (0.899 - Proteasome subunit beta type-4), PSMA3 (0.818), PSMA5 (0.696), PSMA4 (0.450), and PSMG3 (0.426). RNA expression shows 'Low tissue specificity' and is 'Detected in all' tissues, consistent with the ubiquitous requirement for proteasome biogenesis in all cell types. Single-cell analysis indicates 'Cell type enhanced' status, suggesting variable expression levels correlating with proteasome demand.\n\nClinVar reports 109 pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants. While many are large CNVs on 18p11, specific loss-of-function mutations in PSMG2 cause CANDLE/PRAAS4 (Chronic Atypical Neutrophilic Dermatosis with Lipodystrophy and Elevated Temperature / Proteasome-Associated Autoinflammatory Syndromes type 4), a rare autoinflammatory interferonopathy. The 2019 landmark study (97 citations) identified additive loss-of-function mutations causing proteasome dysfunction, type I interferon upregulation, and severe systemic inflammation. Recent case reports (2024) describe disseminated BCG disease in children with novel PSMG2 deletions, highlighting immune susceptibility. Other variants include splice site mutations (c.230-2A>G) and intronic changes affecting expression.\n\nLiterature (23 papers) reveals three key functional contexts: (1) Autoinflammation—PSMG2 mutations cause CANDLE/PRAAS4 via proteasome insufficiency and interferonopathy (2019, 97 citations); (2) Cancer therapy resistance—PSMG2-controlled proteasome-autophagy balance mediates tolerance to MEK-targeted therapy in triple-negative breast cancer (2022, 22 citations), where PSMG2 knockdown sensitizes cells by disrupting proteostasis; (3) Tumorigenesis—recent work (2025) links PSMG2 to stemness, protein accumulation, reticulum stress, and autophagy regulation. The 2025 paper proposes PSMG2 drives dedifferentiation toward cancer stem cells.\n\nWe hypothesize that PSMG2 functions as a mandatory structural scaffold in proteasome biogenesis with critical homeostatic roles: (1) Alpha-ring nucleation—PSMG2 heterodimerizes with PSMG1 (0.999) to bind nascent alpha-subunits (PSMA1/3/4/5), preventing aggregation and ensuring correct ring topology; (2) Chaperone handoff—after alpha-ring closure, PSMG2-PSMG1 dissociates, allowing PSMG3-PSMG4 and POMP (0.997) to recruit beta-subunits (PSMB2/4); (3) Proteostasis gatekeeper—by controlling proteasome abundance, PSMG2 regulates the proteasome-autophagy balance; low PSMG2 shifts cells toward autophagy-dependent survival, conferring resistance to MEK inhibitors in TNBC; (4) Interferon regulation—PSMG2 haploinsufficiency reduces proteasome capacity, causing ubiquitinated protein accumulation, ER stress, and cGAS-STING-mediated type I interferon production (CANDLE pathogenesis); (5) Immune defense—PSMG2 deficiency impairs immunoproteasome assembly, increasing susceptibility to intracellular pathogens (disseminated BCG); (6) Cancer stemness—elevated PSMG2 in tumors maintains high proteasome activity, clearing damaged proteins to support rapid proliferation and stem cell identity; (7) Nuclear body function—localization to nuclear bodies suggests roles in degrading nuclear substrates (e.g., transcription factors, misfolded nuclear proteins); (8) Therapeutic target—PSMG2 inhibition could sensitize TNBC to MEK inhibitors or reduce interferonopathy in CANDLE via proteasome boosting; (9) Dosage sensitivity—109 ClinVar variants indicate strict stoichiometric requirement; even partial loss causes disease; (10) Evolutionary conservation—dual PAC domain structure (pLDDT 92.5) is conserved from yeast (Cgl1923) to humans, underscoring fundamental role. The combination of very high-confidence structure, essential chaperone interactions, CANDLE/PRAAS4 causality, cancer therapy resistance mechanism, and ubiquitous expression positions PSMG2 as a high-priority candidate for therapeutic modulation in autoimmunity and oncology.
