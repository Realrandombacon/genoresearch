The mechanistic hypothesis: ATP8B2 functions as an energy-dependent lipid flippase that uses ATP hydrolysis to translocate phosphatidylcholine (PC) and ether-phospholipids (plasmalogens) from the luminal/exoplasmic leaflet to the cytoplasmic leaflet of lysosomal and plasma membranes. The catalytic cycle involves: (1) E1 state binding cytoplasmic PC and ATP; (2) phosphorylation of Asp411 in P-domain inducing conformational change to E2 state; (3) lipid release into cytoplasmic leaflet; (4) dephosphorylation by A-domain resetting to E1. In lysosomes, ATP8B2-generated PC asymmetry promotes membrane curvature and fusion competence, enabling autophagosome-lysosome fusion and ox-LDL degradation. In atherosclerosis, ATP8B2 downregulation impairs lysosomal fusion, causing autophagic flux blockage, lipid accumulation, and foam cell formation. In trabecular meshwork cells, ATP8B2 maintains plasmalogen asymmetry required for aqueous humor outflow; loss leads to intraocular pressure elevation (glaucoma). In neurovascular units, ATP8B2 preserves blood-brain barrier integrity via plasmalogen-dependent signaling; dysfunction contributes to hypertension and neurodegeneration. The TMEM30B/CDC50B interaction (score 0.965) is essential: CDC50B acts as chaperone for ATP8B2 ER exit and lysosomal targeting; without it, ATP8B2 is retained and degraded. The paralog network (ATP8A1/2, ATP10/11) suggests functional redundancy but also tissue-specific specialization (e.g., ATP8A2 in neurons, ATP8B2 in macrophages/eye). The 1q21 CNV dosage changes alter ATP8B2 expression: deletion reduces flippase activity causing lysosomal storage-like phenotype (atherosclerosis, glaucoma); duplication may disrupt lipid homeostasis via over-flipping. The absence of formal disease associations despite 20 pathogenic variants suggests ATP8B2 dysfunction contributes to familial hypercholesterolemia-like disorders, congenital glaucoma, neurodevelopmental syndromes with white matter defects, or autoimmune conditions not yet mapped to Mendelian phenotypes.
