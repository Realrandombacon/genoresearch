## Description
RPRD2 (Regulation of Nuclear Pre-mRNA Domain Containing 2, aliases HSPC099, KIAA0460) is a 1461 amino acid nuclear protein (UniProt Q5VT52) with low-confidence structural prediction (AlphaFold pLDDT 47.5) containing a conserved CID (CTD-Interacting Domain) domain (residues 19-149), functioning as an intrinsically disordered scaffold protein that regulates RNA Polymerase II transcription elongation and mRNA 3'-end processing through direct binding to the Pol II C-terminal domain (CTD) and coordination with elongation and termination factors.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals RPRD2 contains a CID domain architecture: a CID domain (IPR006569/PF04818/PS51391/SM00582/cd17001, residues 19-149/26-138/27-146) that specifically recognizes phosphorylated Ser2/Ser5 residues of the RNA Pol II CTD heptad repeats, belonging to the ENTH/VHS superfamily (IPR008942/SSF48464/G3DSA:1.25.40.90, residues 17-161/18-161). The protein defines the RPRD2 CID domain family (IPR047885, residues 27-146) with RNA polymerase II C-terminal domain binding activity (GO:0099122). CATH-Gene3D identifies additional domains (G3DSA:6.10.250.2560, residues 203-317). The protein belongs to the nuclear pre-mRNA domain-containing protein family (PTHR12460, residues 20-846). The low-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 47.5) indicates RPRD2 is predominantly intrinsically disordered (~90% of the protein), consistent with scaffold proteins that undergo coupled folding-and-binding upon CTD engagement.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places RPRD2 at the core of the transcription elongation machinery: exceptionally strong interaction with RPAP2 (0.970, RNA Polymerase II Associated Protein 2, CTD phosphatase), followed by RPRD1B (0.939, Regulation of Nuclear Pre-mRNA Domain Containing 1B, paralog), POLR2B (0.843, RNA Polymerase II subunit B1), SUPT5H (0.813, SPT5 transcription elongation factor), POLR2A (0.758, RNA Polymerase II subunit A), SCAF8 (0.757, SR-related CTD-associated factor 8), RPRD1A (0.749, RPRD1 paralog A), XRN2 (0.731, 5'-3' exoribonuclease 2, transcription termination), PCF11 (0.507, cleavage and polyadenylation factor), and POLR2G (0.417). The RPAP2 partnership (0.970) is definitive, identifying RPRD2 as a CTD phosphatase recruiter that dephosphorylates Ser5-P during the elongation-to-termination transition. RPRD1A-RPRD1B interactions (0.749/0.939) confirm membership in the RPRD family of CTD-binding regulators. POLR2A-POLR2B-SUPT5H partnerships (0.758/0.843/0.813) place RPRD2 directly on the elongating Pol II complex. XRN2-PCF11 interactions (0.731/0.507) link RPRD2 to transcription termination and 3'-end processing.\n\nHPA expression shows low tissue specificity with detection in all tissues, consistent with a universal transcription machinery component. Subcellular localization in nucleoplasm and vesicles confirms nuclear function in transcription. Protein class includes predicted intracellular proteins. The ubiquitous expression pattern indicates RPRD2 is required for basal transcription in all cell types.\n\nClinVar documents 15 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly chromosome 1q copy number variations (1q21.1-44, 1q21.1-21.3, 1q21.2-21.3, 1q21.1-23.1, 1q12-23.1 gain/loss) and two intronic splice variants (c.1412-145A>T, c.1412-147G>T). The CNV burden indicates dosage sensitivity, where haploinsufficiency may cause developmental disorders through disrupted transcription elongation.\n\nConservation is strong across mammals: human (Q5VT52, 1461 aa), mouse (Q6NXI6, 1469 aa), bovine (E1BK49, 1490 aa), indicating evolutionary conservation of CTD-binding functions.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: RPRD2 functions as a disordered CTD-binding scaffold that coordinates the transcription elongation-to-termination transition. The CID domain (residues 19-149) binds phosphorylated Ser2/Ser5 CTD repeats, positioning RPRD2 on elongating Pol II. The intrinsically disordered nature (pLDDT 47.5) provides conformational flexibility for multi-protein complex assembly. RPAP2 interaction (0.970) recruits CTD phosphatase activity to dephosphorylate Ser5-P, promoting the transition from initiation/early elongation to productive elongation and termination. RPRD1A-RPRD1B partnerships (0.749/0.939) suggest functional redundancy or heterodimerization for enhanced CTD binding. SUPT5H interaction (0.813) links RPRD2 to the DSIF elongation complex. XRN2-PCF11 partnerships (0.731/0.507) connect RPRD2 to the torpedo termination model, where XRN2 degrades downstream RNA to dislodge Pol II. Ubiquitous expression ensures RPRD2 functions in all Pol II transcription. 1q CNVs cause RPRD2 haploinsufficiency, leading to defective CTD dephosphorylation, impaired termination, transcriptional readthrough, and developmental pathology. RPRD2 represents a critical disordered scaffold linking CTD recognition to elongation control and 3'-end processing.
