# LOC129389920: 7q35 MPRA-Validated Silencer Element with High CNV Burden (33 Variants) and Hematopoietic Regulation

## Description
LOC129389920 is a 200bp cis-regulatory element (CRE) located on chromosome 7q35 (GRCh38: 146781422-146781622), functionally validated as a transcriptional silencer through STARR-seq massively parallel reporter assays (MPRAs) in K562 erythroleukemia cells. This locus lacks canonical promoter, enhancer, or CTCF-bound insulator chromatin signatures but demonstrates robust repressive activity against the super core promoter SCP1. As a non-coding regulatory sequence, it has no UniProt accession, protein domains, STRING interaction partners, or Human Protein Atlas expression data (query failed to resolve Ensembl ID).\n\nClinVar analysis reveals a high burden of 33 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variants disrupting this region. The variant spectrum includes multiple focal deletions specifically targeting the 7q35 locus (e.g., chr7:146774271-146839904, ~65kb; chr7:146713626-146913128, ~200kb) as well as massive whole-arm duplications spanning 7q32-36 (~20-28Mb). The presence of both focal deletions and large amplifications suggests strong dosage sensitivity where silencer loss leads to pathological derepression of target genes. These CNVs are associated with 7q deletion/duplication syndrome phenotypes, including developmental delay, intellectual disability, and distinctive facial features. The 7q35 region is gene-dense, containing critical loci such as SHH (Sonic Hedgehog, though more distal, often affected in large rearrangements), GLI3, and various homeobox genes. However, the focal nature of some deletions suggests LOC129389920 may regulate a specific nearby gene essential for hematopoiesis or neurodevelopment.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: LOC129389920 functions as a critical distal silencer within the 7q35 topologically associating domain (TAD), specifically regulating genes involved in erythroid differentiation or neural crest development. Given its validation in K562 cells, it likely represses proto-oncogenes or lineage-inappropriate transcription factors (e.g., HOX cluster members or specific signaling modulators) in normal erythroid progenitors. The mechanism involves recruitment of co-repressor complexes (e.g., PRC2, NuRD) to deposit repressive histone marks (H3K27me3) at target promoters. Loss of this silencer (via focal 7q35 deletions) leads to aberrant overexpression of target genes, contributing to the hematologic defects and developmental anomalies seen in 7q syndrome. The high frequency of focal deletions (unlike the other silencers analyzed which mostly had large CNVs) implies that this specific 200bp element is a critical vulnerability point in the 7q35 architecture, potentially acting as a boundary or anchor for a repressive chromatin loop. Duplication of this region may lead to ectopic silencing of essential genes, exacerbating the phenotype.
