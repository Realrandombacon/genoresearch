## Description
PAAF1 (Proteasomal ATPase-Associated Factor 1, also known as Rpn14 in yeast) is a 392 aa nucleoplasmic/cytosolic protein representing a genuine dark gene with only 18 literature references despite essential roles in proteasome assembly, HIV-1 transcription, and neurodegeneration. Multi-omics data reveals PAAF1 as a highly conserved WD40-repeat scaffold protein orchestrating 26S proteasome holoenzyme formation. InterPro analysis confirms a dominant WD40-repeat architecture: seven canonical WD40 repeats (IPR001680/PF00400, aa 90-204) forming a β-propeller structure, embedded within a YVTN repeat-like domain superfamily (G3DSA:2.130.10.10, aa 76-246 and 247-392) characteristic of proteasome regulatory particles. The PAC1/LIS1-like WD-40 repeat motif (IPR020472, aa 107-205) suggests functional similarity to dynein regulatory complexes. AlphaFold predicts exceptionally high-confidence structure (pLDDT 95.9), indicating a rigid, well-organized seven-bladed β-propeller ideal for protein-protein interactions. HPA shows brain-enriched expression (110.8 nTPM, highest of all tissues) with ubiquitous detection across tissues, consistent with essential housekeeping function in protein homeostasis and neuronal proteostasis. STRING interactions reveal exclusive association with 26S proteasome subunits: AAA+ ATPases PSMC5/Rpt6 (0.998) and PSMC4/Rpt3 (0.997), PSMC2/Rpt1 (0.954), and non-ATPase subunits PSMD10/gankyrin (0.975), PSMD7/S8 (0.971), PSMD12/S5a (0.971), PSMD14/Poh1 (0.970), PSMD13/S11 (0.950), PSMD3/S3 (0.949), and ADRM1/Rpn13 (0.953). ClinVar lists 8 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly 11q12.2-13.5 and 11p15.5-q25 copy number alterations (gains: chr11:59923608-76272324x3, chr11:32799481-134938470x3, chr11:71588805-116680918x3; losses: chr11:71969881-78232895x1, chr11:71928796-77064521x1), suggesting PAAF1 dosage sensitivity. Literature (2007-2024) identifies PAAF1 as: (1) A proteasome assembly chaperone promoting 26S holoenzyme formation by facilitating RP-CP association; (2) A dissociation factor converting 26S to 19S regulatory particles for HIV-1 LTR transcriptional elongation via Spt6 stabilization; (3) A mediator of α-synuclein-induced proteasome inhibition in Parkinson's disease; (4) A host factor for HIV-1 Tat-mediated transactivation. The scarcity of literature (18 papers) despite clear mechanistic roles makes PAAF1 a paradigmatic dark gene awaiting comprehensive characterization.
