## Description
CLEC4D encodes C-type lectin domain family 4 member D (Dectin-3/MCL/MPCL/CD368), a 215 aa type II transmembrane pattern recognition receptor (UniProt Q8WXI8) belonging to the C-type lectin/C-type lectin-like domain (CTL/CTLD) superfamily. InterPro reveals canonical C-type lectin architecture: C-type lectin-like domain (IPR001304/cd03590, residues 84-208), C-type lectin fold (IPR016187, residues 64-211), C-type lectin-like/link domain superfamily (IPR016186/G3DSA:3.10.100.10, residues 60-215), CD209-like C-type lectin domain (IPR033989, residues 84-208), Pfam Lectin C-type domain (PF00059, residues 102-208), and SMART CTL/CRD domain (SM00034, residues 84-208) mediating carbohydrate recognition and pathogen binding. AlphaFold predicts high confidence structure (pLDDT 86.1) consistent with stable CTLD fold for ligand interactions. HPA shows bone marrow-enriched expression (61.1 nTPM, tissue enriched), cancer-enriched pattern, and group enrichment in blood cells with adaptive/innate immunity annotation. ClinVar lists 41 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number gains at 12p13.33-11.1, duplications, and deletions though specific monogenic diseases remain undefined. Functional mechanism: Dectin-3 forms heterodimers with Dectin-2 (CLEC6A) on myeloid cells (macrophages, dendritic cells, monocytes) to recognize α-mannans on fungal cell walls (Candida albicans, Aspergillus species), triggering ITAM-mediated signaling through FcRγ-associated SYK kinase and CARD9 adaptor to activate NF-κB, MAPK, and cytokine production (IL-1β, IL-6, TNF-α, IL-23). Recent literature reveals expanded disease relevance: sarcopenia pathogenesis via JAK/STAT pathway inhibition (2025), tuberculosis biomarker identification in monocytes via machine learning (2025), ZEB2-regulated Clec4d+ macrophage state in early cardiac hypertrophy inflammation (2025), and Prader-Willi syndrome transcriptional dysregulation (2025). Despite mechanistic understanding of antifungal immunity, specific ligand repertoire beyond fungal mannans, regulatory mechanisms controlling receptor expression in non-immune tissues, contribution to sterile inflammation in metabolic/cardiac disease, and precise role in human immunodeficiency syndromes remain incompletely characterized - representing genuine dark aspects of this emerging therapeutic target for infectious disease, autoimmunity, and cancer immunotherapy.
