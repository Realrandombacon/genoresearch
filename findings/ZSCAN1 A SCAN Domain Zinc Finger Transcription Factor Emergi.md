# ZSCAN1: A SCAN Domain Zinc Finger Transcription Factor Emerging as the Autoantigen Target in ROHHAD Syndrome with Roles in Pituitary-Hypothalamic Development and Transcriptional Regulation

**Date:** 2026-06-28T13:40:53.207187

**Quality Score:** 9/10 (EXCELLENT)

**Confidence:** MEDIUM

## Description
ZSCAN1 (Zinc Finger and SCAN Domain Containing 1) is a 408-amino acid transcription factor characterized by an N-terminal SCAN oligomerization domain (IPR003309/IPR038269/PF02023, positions 34-146; cd07936, 35-118) within the SCAN domain superfamily (G3DSA:3.30.710.10, 31-121) mediating protein-protein interactions and homo/hetero-oligomerization, and three C-terminal C2H2-type zinc finger DNA-binding domains (IPR013087/IPR036236/PF00096/PS00028/PS50157, positions 292-319, 320-347, 380-407) classified under the Classic Zinc Finger superfamily (G3DSA:3.30.160.60) and the SCAN domain + C2H2-type zinc finger family (IPR050916, 12-317). STRING interactions reveal a tight functional network with other SCAN/ZNF transcription factors: ZNF24 (0.825, high confidence), ZSCAN18 (0.808, high confidence), PGBD1 (0.783, high confidence; another SCAN-ZNF protein we characterized), ZNF213 (0.777, high confidence), PLVAP (0.728, plasmalemma vesicle-associated protein), ZNF174 (0.630), MAZ (0.620, MYC-associated zinc finger), TGFB1 (0.575), MZF1 (0.554), and USF1 (0.543), suggesting roles in transcriptional regulatory complexes. HPA data shows tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in pituitary gland (15.5 nTPM), brain (10.0 nTPM), and testis (8.3 nTPM), cancer-enhanced specificity, not detected in immune cells, cell type-enhanced single cell specificity, and subcellular localization to nucleoplasm and nucleoli, classified as a transcription factor with DNA-binding function involved in transcription regulation. ClinVar contains 14 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains spanning 19q13.33-13.43 and 19p13.3-q13.43 regions, though specific monogenic disease phenotypes remain under characterization (significance marked '?' for most); the genomic location overlaps with regions implicated in neuroendocrine disorders. AlphaFold structure (AF-Q8NBB4-F1) predicts medium-low confidence folding (pLDDT 59.4) consistent with a partially structured transcription factor containing ordered SCAN and zinc finger domains coupled with flexible linker regions typical of DNA-binding proteins. With only 16 papers, ZSCAN1 represents a 'gray' gene rapidly transitioning from dark due to a major 2024 clinical breakthrough: multiple independent studies identified anti-ZSCAN1 autoantibodies as a feasible diagnostic marker for ROHHAD syndrome (Rapid-onset Obesity with Hypoventilation, Hypothalamic dysfunction, and Autonomic Dysregulation), a rare life-threatening neuroendocrine disorder affecting children and adults, particularly in non-paraneoplastic cases not associated with tumors. The mechanistic hypothesis posits that ZSCAN1 functions as a sequence-specific transcriptional regulator that oligomerizes via its SCAN domain with partner proteins (ZNF24, ZSCAN18, PGBD1) forming repressor or activator complexes targeting genes involved in pituitary-hypothalamic development and function; its enrichment in pituitary gland (15.5 nTPM) and brain (10.0 nTPM) aligns perfectly with ROHHAD syndrome phenotypes including hypothalamic dysfunction, obesity, and autonomic dysregulation; the presence of autoantibodies suggests ZSCAN1 may be exposed to the immune system during neuroendocrine cell stress or turnover, making it both a disease biomarker and potential pathogenic driver through antibody-mediated disruption of transcriptional programs controlling energy homeostasis and respiratory drive.
