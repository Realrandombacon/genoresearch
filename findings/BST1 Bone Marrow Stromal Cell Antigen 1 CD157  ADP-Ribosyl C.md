## Description
BST1 (Bone marrow stromal cell antigen 1, also known as CD157) encodes a 318 amino acid GPI-anchored ectoenzyme (UniProt Q10588) belonging to the CD38/ADP-ribosyl cyclase family. InterPro analysis reveals a canonical ADP-ribosyl cyclase domain (IPR003193, PF02267, cd04759, residues 11-288) with NAD+ nucleosidase activity and cyclic ADP-ribose generating capability (GO:0061809). The structure contains an N-(deoxy)ribosyltransferase-like fold (SSF52309, residues 34-282) and NAD(P)-binding Rossmann-like domain (G3DSA:3.40.50.720), characteristic of enzymes that process nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide substrates. Unlike CD38 which has both cyclase and hydrolase activity, BST1 primarily functions as an ADP-ribosyl cyclase with weaker hydrolase activity.\n\nAlphaFold prediction shows high global confidence (pLDDT 89.6) across all 318 aa, indicating a well-folded stable structure typical of the ADP-ribosyl cyclase family. The high confidence suggests BST1 maintains a rigid catalytic core optimized for NAD+ substrate binding and cADPR production.\n\nSTRING interactions reveal BST1 operates within the NAD+ metabolism and sirtuin signaling network with exceptionally high confidence scores: NMNAT1 (0.960) and NMNAT2 (0.951) are nicotinamide mononucleotide adenylyltransferases that synthesize NAD+; SIRT3 (0.934), SIRT6 (0.928), SIRT4 (0.624), and SIRT1 (0.460) are NAD+-dependent deacylases linking BST1 to mitochondrial function, DNA repair, and metabolic regulation; NNMT (0.931) is nicotinamide N-methyltransferase; ENPP1 (0.930) is ectonucleotide pyrophosphatase; NAMPT (0.914) is nicotinamide phosphoribosyltransferase, the rate-limiting enzyme in NAD+ salvage; PNP (0.930) is purine nucleoside phosphorylase. This interaction network positions BST1 as a central regulator of cellular NAD+ homeostasis, connecting extracellular NAD+ metabolism to intracellular sirtuin signaling.\n\nHPA expression shows BST1 is bone marrow-enriched (131.9 nTPM) with group-enriched RNA specificity, also detected in intestine (45.7 nTPM). It shows immune cell-enhanced blood cell specificity and cell type-enhanced single cell specificity, consistent with its role as a leukocyte surface marker (CD157). BST1 is classified as a CD marker, enzyme, FDA-approved drug target, metabolic protein, and plasma protein with hydrolase and transferase molecular functions.\n\nClinVar reports 53 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly 4p16.3-15.2 copy number losses and gains, plus intronic variants (c.792-261G>T, c.535-22A>G). While no specific monogenic disease is definitively assigned, the 4p16 locus overlaps with Wolf-Hirschhorn syndrome region. The CNV sensitivity suggests BST1 dosage affects immune and neurological function.\n\nLiterature (223 papers) reveals emerging roles in inflammation and neurodegeneration: BST1 serves as a diagnostic biomarker for pediatric sepsis (2025, machine learning validation); the rs4698412 variant affects Parkinson's disease progression (2025, longitudinal study); BST1/CD157 and CD38 double knockout mice show aberrant humoral immune responses and intestinal homeostasis defects (2025); BST1 links to neutrophil extracellular traps in acute liver failure (2025); fission yeast Bst1 regulates ER-Golgi transport and cytokinesis. BST1 is a mammalian ADP-ribosyl cyclase family member alongside CD38.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: BST1 functions as a cell surface NAD+ glycohydrolase that regulates extracellular NAD+ levels and generates cyclic ADP-ribose (cADPR), a potent calcium-mobilizing second messenger. As a GPI-anchored ectoenzyme, BST1 cleaves extracellular NAD+ to produce cADPR and ADP-ribose, which can signal through purinergic receptors or be internalized to modulate intracellular calcium release from ER stores via ryanodine receptors. The strong STRING interactions with NMNAT1/2 and sirtuins (SIRT1/3/4/6) suggest BST1 coordinates extracellular NAD+ metabolism with intracellular NAD+-dependent signaling: by controlling NAD+ availability, BST1 influences sirtuin activity, thereby regulating mitochondrial function (SIRT3/4), DNA repair and metabolism (SIRT6), and transcriptional programs (SIRT1). In bone marrow, BST1 likely modulates hematopoietic stem cell niche signaling through cADPR-mediated calcium flux. In Parkinson's disease, the rs4698412 variant may alter BST1 enzymatic activity, disrupting NAD+ homeostasis in dopaminergic neurons and accelerating neurodegeneration. In sepsis and acute liver failure, BST1 upregulation on neutrophils may enhance NETosis through calcium signaling. The CD38/BST1 double knockout phenotype suggests functional redundancy with partial compensation. Therapeutic targeting of BST1 could modulate NAD+ metabolism in neurodegeneration, inflammation, and cancer.
