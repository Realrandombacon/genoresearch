## Description
DPM3 (Dolichyl-phosphate mannosyltransferase subunit 3, regulatory, also known as CDG1O, MDDGB15) is a 92 amino acid membrane protein (UniProt Q9P2X0) with high-confidence well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 88.9), characteristic of the DPM3 family (IPR013174/PF08285/PTHR16433, residues 1-91). Molecular functions include dolichyl-phosphate mannosyltransferase activity (GO:0004584) and glycoprotein biosynthetic process (GO:0009101), placing DPM3 within the N-linked glycosylation and GPI anchor biosynthesis pathways. The protein serves as a critical regulatory subunit that stabilizes the catalytic DPM1 enzyme within the endoplasmic reticulum (ER) membrane.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals an exceptionally tight network dedicated to dolichol metabolism and glycosylation: strongest interactions with DPM1 (0.999, Dolichyl-phosphate mannosyltransferase catalytic subunit), DOLK (0.982, Dolichol kinase), DOLPP1 (0.950, Dolichyl pyrophosphatase 1), ALG3 (0.948, Asparagine-linked glycosylation 3 homolog), DPAGT1 (0.945, Dolichyl-phosphate N-acetylglucosaminephosphotransferase), DPM2 (0.925, DPM1 stabilizing subunit), PIGV (0.907, Phosphatidylinositol glycan anchor biosynthesis class V), ALG5 (0.586), PIGM (0.495), and ALG1 (0.437). The near-perfect confidence score with DPM1 confirms DPM3's role as an obligate partner; without DPM3, DPM1 is unstable and degraded, abolishing dolichol-phosphate mannose (Dol-P-Man) synthesis. Interactions with PIG genes link DPM3 directly to GPI anchor assembly, while ALG connections tie it to N-glycan core formation.\n\nHPA expression reveals ubiquitous detection across all tissues with low tissue specificity, consistent with an essential housekeeping role in protein glycosylation. The protein is classified as essential, disease-related (Congenital Disorder of Glycosylation type Io, Congenital Muscular Dystrophy-Dystroglycanopathy type B15), and a predicted membrane protein. Loss of function leads to defective surface expression of GPI-anchored proteins and hypoglycosylation of serum proteins.\n\nClinVar documents 33 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including specific loss-of-function mutations like c.54G>A (p.Trp18Ter) and c.205del (p.Asp69fs), as well as numerous large copy number gains and losses affecting chromosome 1q (1q21.1-44 duplications, 1q21.3-22 gains, 1q21.3-23.1 deletions). The presence of truncating mutations confirms haploinsufficiency or recessive loss-of-function mechanisms. Clinical phenotypes associated with DPM3 defects include congenital disorders of glycosylation (CDG), muscular dystrophy, intellectual disability, and skeletal abnormalities, reflecting the broad requirement for proper glycosylation in development.\n\nConservation is strong across eukaryotes: human (Q9P2X0, 92 aa), mouse (Q9D1Q4, 92 aa), cow (Q3ZC71, 92 aa), indicating an ancient and indispensable function in ER glycosylation.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: DPM3 functions as a membrane-embedded chaperone-like subunit that binds and stabilizes the catalytic DPM1 protein, preventing its proteasomal degradation and facilitating its integration into the ER membrane. The DPM1-DPM2-DPM3 complex synthesizes Dol-P-Man from GDP-mannose and dolichol-phosphate on the cytosolic leaflet of the ER. Dol-P-Man is then flipped to the lumen to serve as the mannose donor for GPI anchor assembly (via PIG enzymes) and N-glycan maturation (via ALG enzymes). Loss of DPM3 destabilizes DPM1, depleting cellular Dol-P-Man pools. This causes a dual defect: failure to attach GPI anchors to proteins (leading to their secretion instead of membrane retention) and incomplete N-glycan processing. The resulting hypoglycosylation disrupts cell signaling, adhesion (via dystroglycan), and protein folding, triggering the multisystem phenotypes observed in CDG-Io and muscular dystrophy. DPM3 represents a critical bottleneck in the glycosylation pathway and a validated target for congenital glycosylation disorders.
