# SPECC1: A Cytoskeletal-Nuclear Scaffold Linking Actin Dynamics to Chromatin Regulation via Calponin Homology Domain

## Description
SPECC1 (Sperm antigen with calponin homology and coiled-coil domains 1, UniProt Q5M775), also known as Cytospin-B, is a 1,068-amino acid multifunctional scaffold protein that bridges cytoskeletal organization with nuclear processes. InterPro analysis identifies a C-terminal calponin homology (CH) domain (IPR001715, PF00307, aa 962-1067) characteristic of actin-binding proteins, embedded within a larger F-actin monooxygenase MICAL-like family architecture (IPR050540, aa 571-1067). The AlphaFold predicted structure shows medium confidence (pLDDT 66.6), reflecting a modular architecture with an ordered CH domain and extensive intrinsically disordered regions suitable for dynamic protein-protein interactions. STRING interaction data reveals SPECC1's position at the intersection of cytoskeletal and nuclear regulatory networks: strong partnerships with the signaling adapter SH2D3C (0.927), the chromatin modifier HDAC2 (0.904), and the cohesin subunit SMC1A (0.734). Additional links to the myosin motor MYO1C (0.606), small GTPase RAC3 (0.595), and protein phosphatase PPP1CA (0.686) suggest roles in force transmission and signal transduction. HPA expression profiles show ubiquitous distribution with enhancement in brain (42.9 nTPM) and immune cells, with subcellular localization spanning nucleoplasm, nucleoli fibrillar center, vesicles, and cytosol—consistent with shuttling between compartments. ClinVar records document 101 pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number losses and gains at 17p11.2, and chromosomal translocations with PDGFRB cause juvenile myelomonocytic leukemia (JMML). We hypothesize that SPECC1 functions as a mechanotransduction scaffold that couples actin cytoskeletal tension to nuclear events: the CH domain anchors SPECC1 to F-actin networks while coiled-coil regions recruit chromatin regulators (HDAC2, SMC1A) to modulate gene expression in response to mechanical cues. In hematopoietic cells, SPECC1-PDGFRB fusion creates a constitutively active kinase-scaffold chimera that dysregulates both cytoskeletal dynamics and transcriptional programs, driving leukemogenesis. The nucleolar localization suggests additional roles in ribosome biogenesis coordination with cell cycle progression.
