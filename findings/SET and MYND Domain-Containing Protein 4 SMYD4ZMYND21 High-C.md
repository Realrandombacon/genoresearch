## Description
SMYD4 (UniProt Q8IYR2), also known as ZMYND21, encodes an 804 aa protein-lysine N-methyltransferase functioning as an epigenetic regulator involved in heart development through histone modification and chromatin remodeling. InterPro analysis reveals conserved multi-domain methyltransferase architecture: catalytic SET domain (cd10536/IPR001214/IPR044421/IPR046341/PF00856, residues 233-602/240-602/244-295+336-574/404-602/489-573) mediating lysine methyltransferase activity (GO:0005515 protein binding) for histone tail modification; MYND-type zinc finger (IPR002893/PF01753/PS01360, residues 296-335) providing protein-protein interaction surfaces for recruitment to specific chromatin contexts; and dual tetratricopeptide repeat (TPR) domains (G3DSA:1.25.40.10/IPR011990, residues 70-225+582-794) forming helical scaffolds for multiprotein complex assembly. AlphaFold predicts high confidence structure (pLDDT 85.0) indicating a well-folded enzyme with stable catalytic core suitable for substrate recognition and methyl transfer chemistry. STRING interactions define high-confidence epigenetic regulatory network: SMYD5 (0.845, paralog lysine methyltransferase), SETD4 (0.588, another SET domain methyltransferase), HDAC1 (0.537, histone deacetylase 1 confirming gene_info prediction of HDAC binding), SMYD3 (0.536, oncogenic methyltransferase), EHMT1 (0.520, euchromatic histone-lysine N-methyltransferase), KMT2E (0.517, H3K4 methyltransferase), L3MBTL2 (0.500, chromatin compaction factor), METTL8 (0.495, RNA methyltransferase), PRDM15 (0.462, PR/SET domain protein), and PRDM4 (0.425, transcriptional regulator). The strong HDAC1 link validates gene_info prediction and suggests SMYD4 coordinates methylation-deacetylation for gene silencing. HPA classifies SMYD4 as predicted intracellular/membrane protein with methyltransferase/transferase activity, low tissue specificity (detected in all tissues), localized to nucleoplasm/Golgi apparatus/vesicles/cytosol consistent with dual chromatin and secretory pathway roles, cell type enhanced expression pattern. ClinVar lists 80 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants predominantly consisting of large copy number losses (17p13.3-13.2x1 deletions of varying sizes: chr17:525-5728870, chr17:240638-1939562, chr17:9475-3793447, chr17:1505119-3010963, chr17:9474-6017500) and copy number gains (17p13.3x4, 17p13.3x3); while no single-gene disorder name is consistently assigned, 17p13.3 deletions cause Miller-Dieker syndrome and related neurodevelopmental disorders where SMYD4 haploinsufficiency may contribute to cardiac phenotypes given its heart development role. Functional hypothesis: SMYD4 operates through integrated epigenetic mechanisms: (1) Catalytic mechanism - SET domain transfers methyl groups from S-adenosylmethionine (SAM) to specific lysine residues on histone tails (likely H3K4, H3K9, or H3K36 based on SMYD family homology) altering chromatin accessibility; (2) HDAC coordination - MYND domain recruits HDAC1 enabling coupled deacetylation-methylation for stable gene repression; (3) TPR scaffolding - Dual TPR domains (N-terminal 70-225, C-terminal 582-794) assemble larger chromatin remodeling complexes bridging methyltransferase activity with other epigenetic modifiers; (4) Cardiac development - Specific role in heart morphogenesis through epigenetic regulation of cardiac transcription factors (NKX2-5, GATA4, TBX5) or structural genes; (5) Subcellular dynamics - Nucleoplasmic localization for chromatin modification plus Golgi/vesicle association suggesting potential non-canonical roles in secretory pathway or protein trafficking; (6) Disease mechanism - 17p13.3 CNVs cause dosage imbalance: deletions reduce SMYD4 impairing cardiac development and potentially contributing to Miller-Dieker syndrome cardiac defects; duplications may cause aberrant gene silencing; missense variants in SET domain (e.g., c.117C>G p.His39Gln) may disrupt catalytic activity; (7) Conservation - Mouse Smyd4 (Q8BTK5, 799 aa), Zebrafish smyd4 (Q08C84, 753 aa), Chicken SMYD4 (Q5F3V0, 742 aa) indicate essential conserved epigenetic function across vertebrates. Dark aspects requiring investigation include: precise histone substrate specificity (which lysine residue(s)), structural mechanism of SET domain catalysis, identity of non-histone methylation targets, contribution to specific 17p13.3 syndrome phenotypes especially cardiac defects, therapeutic potential of SMYD4 modulation in congenital heart disease, role in adult heart function vs. development, crosstalk between SMYD4 and other SMYD family members (SMYD3/5), involvement in cancer through epigenetic dysregulation (SMYD3 is oncogenic), mechanism linking Golgi localization to chromatin function, regulation by post-translational modifications, tissue-specific target genes beyond heart, potential synthetic lethality with HDAC inhibitors, and whether SMYD4 acts as tumor suppressor or oncogene in different contexts.
