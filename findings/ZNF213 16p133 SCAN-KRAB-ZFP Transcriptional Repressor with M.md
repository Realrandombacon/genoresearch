## Description
ZNF213 (Zinc Finger Protein 213, UniProt O14771, Gene ID: 7760), also known as ZKSCAN21 or ZSCAN53, is a 459 amino acid transcriptional regulator encoded on chromosome 16p13.3. InterPro analysis reveals a tripartite domain architecture characteristic of the ZKSCAN subfamily: an N-terminal SCAN oligomerization domain (residues 41-145, IPR003309, PF02023) mediating homo- or hetero-dimerization; a central KRAB-A repressor domain (residues 202-292, IPR001909, PF01352) recruiting KAP1/TRIM28 co-repressors; and a C-terminal cluster of three C2H2-type zinc fingers (residues 317-400, IPR013087, PF00096) for sequence-specific DNA binding. This modular structure allows ZNF213 to act as a sequence-specific scaffold that recruits chromatin silencing machinery to target loci.\n\nSTRING interaction network confirms ZNF213 operates within a dedicated repressor module, showing high-confidence associations with other SCAN-KRAB-ZFPs such as ZKSCAN4 (0.795), ZSCAN32 (0.785), ZNF397 (0.784), and PGBD1 (0.783). The strong link to PGBD1 (PiggyBac transposase derived) suggests a potential role in silencing transposable elements or regulating genomic stability. Additional partners include ZSCAN1, ZSCAN18, and SCAND1, reinforcing its function in a larger nuclear complex dedicated to epigenetic regulation.\n\nHuman Protein Atlas classifies ZNF213 as a ubiquitous transcription factor with "Low tissue specificity" but "Cell type enhanced" expression patterns. Subcellular localization is dynamic, detected in the nucleoplasm, nuclear membrane, vesicles, and cytosol, suggesting shuttling behavior or association with nuclear pore complexes. AlphaFold structure prediction (AF-O14771-F1) yields a global pLDDT score of 65.1 (Medium Confidence). The model likely shows well-defined structures for the SCAN and Zinc Finger domains, while the linker regions and potentially the KRAB domain exhibit higher flexibility or disorder, consistent with intrinsically disordered regions (IDRs) often found in transcriptional regulators to facilitate promiscuous protein interactions.\n\nClinVar analysis identifies a high burden of 35 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variants. The spectrum is dominated by large-scale duplications of 16p13.3 (e.g., ~4-5Mb gains, whole-arm 16p gains), but notably includes at least one focal deletion (~2.8Mb loss). This mix of loss and gain events, heavily skewed towards duplication (approx. 34:1), indicates extreme dosage sensitivity. These CNVs are associated with 16p13.3 duplication/deletion syndromes, characterized by neurodevelopmental disorders, intellectual disability, and autism spectrum traits. The presence of both deletions and duplications suggests that precise stoichiometry of ZNF213 is critical; haploinsufficiency may fail to repress toxic targets (e.g., retrotransposons), while overexpression may lead to widespread hyper-silencing of essential neurodevelopmental genes.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: ZNF213 functions as a critical epigenetic silencer that maintains genomic integrity and neural development by repressing specific families of endogenous retroviruses (ERVs) or lineage-inappropriate genes via its SCAN-mediated oligomerization and KRAB-dependent recruitment of heterochromatin. The medium-confidence AlphaFold structure suggests a flexible linker allowing the repressor domain to sample various chromatin states. The high CNV burden, particularly the prevalence of duplications, implies that ZNF213 acts as a "dosage rheostat"; triplication leads to excessive recruitment of repressive complexes (HP1, SETDB1) to off-target sites, causing global transcriptional suppression and neurodevelopmental deficits. Conversely, deletion may unleash aberrant expression of silenced elements, triggering innate immune responses or genomic instability. Its interaction with PGBD1 further supports a role in monitoring transposon activity, linking ZNF213 dysfunction to the etiology of 16p13.3 syndromes.
