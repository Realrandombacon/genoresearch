# TCAF2: TRPM8 Channel-Associated Factor with M60 Peptidase-Like Domains Driving Cancer Metastasis via JAK2/STAT3 Signaling

## Description
TCAF2 encodes a 919 aa TRPM8 channel-associated factor (UniProt A6NFQ2) with a complex multi-domain architecture. InterPro analysis reveals: (1) N-terminal TRPM8-binding domain (IPR060286, PF27016, aa 162-407) for cold-sensing channel interaction; (2) alpha-helical domain (IPR060292, PF27027, aa 411-511); (3) M60 peptidase-like domains (IPR031161, PF13402, aa 524-889) with enhancin-like folds (G3DSA:1.10.390.30, G3DSA:3.40.390.80); (4) Class I glutamine amidotransferase-like region (IPR029062, aa 96-305). AlphaFold predicts a very high-confidence structure (pLDDT 92.6), indicating stable folding despite the gene's 'dark' status. STRING interactions confirm functional partnership with TRPM8 (0.537), the cold-sensing ion channel, plus PTPN20 (0.457), CTAGE6 (0.447), and NPDC1 (0.422). HPA shows low tissue specificity with detection in many tissues, localized to plasma membrane and cell junctions. ClinVar reports 33 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly 7q34-36.3 copy number losses/gains, though specific disease associations remain undefined. Literature (18 papers, 2023-2025) reveals emerging oncogenic roles: (1) TCAF2 in pericytes promotes colorectal cancer liver metastasis by inhibiting TRPM8 cold-sensing channel (20 citations); (2) drives glioma migration/invasion via STAT3 signaling; (3) gastric cancer progression through PDE12/TCAF2/JAK2/STAT3 axis; (4) associated with immune microenvironment and poor prognosis in glioma. Mechanistically, TCAF2 likely serves as a scaffold regulating TRPM8 channel activity at cell junctions, with the M60 peptidase-like domains potentially cleaving extracellular matrix components or signaling peptides. In cancer, TCAF2 overexpression appears to suppress TRPM8-mediated cold sensing (which normally inhibits migration), thereby enabling metastatic spread through JAK2/STAT3 activation. The high-confidence structure and cancer associations suggest TCAF2 as a potential therapeutic target for metastatic disease.
