# FAM90A3: Primate-Specific Zinc Knuckle Protein with Nucleic Acid Binding Function

**Date:** 2026-03-20T13:11:52.921959

**Quality Score:** 2.75/10  (E=4.5, D=1.0) [WEAK]

## Description
FAM90A3 encodes a 464 amino acid primate-specific protein belonging to the FAM90A gene family (subfamily II), which originated from multiple duplications and rearrangements on chromosome 8p23.1. InterPro analysis reveals two key domains: the FAM90 family domain (IPR039213, aa 2-462) spanning nearly the entire protein, and a CCHC-type zinc knuckle motif (IPR041670/PF15288, aa 41-82) characteristic of nucleic acid-binding proteins.\n\nThe zinc knuckle domain (C-X2-C-X4-H-X4-C motif) is a classic RNA-binding fold found in retroviral nucleocapsid proteins and cellular RNA chaperones, suggesting FAM90A3 functions in RNA recognition, binding, or remodeling. The PANTHER family assignment (PTHR16035) links FAM90A3 to FAM90A1, indicating functional conservation within this primate-specific gene family.\n\nClinVar contains 34 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly copy number losses and gains affecting 8p23.1-23.3 regions (ranging from 7-24 Mb deletions/duplications). The 8p23.1 locus is a well-known genomic instability hotspot containing beta-defensin gene clusters and olfactory receptor arrays, prone to non-allelic homologous recombination (NAHR). The dosage sensitivity of this region suggests FAM90A3 copy number changes contribute to 8p23.1 deletion/duplication syndrome phenotypes.\n\nThe absence of STRING interactions and HPA expression data reflects the limited characterization of this primate-restricted gene family. However, the conserved zinc knuckle architecture across FAM90A paralogs suggests a role in post-transcriptional gene regulation, potentially as an RNA chaperone or translational modulator specific to primate biology.\n\nConservation is restricted to primates—FAM90A genes are absent in mouse and zebrafish genomes, consistent with their emergence from primate-specific segmental duplications. This lineage-restricted evolution suggests FAM90A3 may contribute to primate-specific traits or adaptations.
