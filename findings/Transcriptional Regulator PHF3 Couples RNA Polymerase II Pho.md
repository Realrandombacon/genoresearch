## Description
PHF3 (PHD Finger Protein 3, also known as Dido1 isoform or Spen paralogue) is a massive 2039 aa nuclear protein functioning as a critical regulator of transcription elongation, cohesin-mediated chromatin looping, and neuronal differentiation. Despite 53 literature references, its precise mechanism as a bridge between the transcription machinery and chromatin architecture remains a 'dark' area requiring synthesis. InterPro analysis reveals a modular domain architecture: an N-terminal region of low complexity, a central PHD-type zinc finger (IPR001965/PF00628, aa 719-770) for reading histone methylation marks (likely H3K4me3), a Transcription Elongation Factor S-II (TFIIS) central domain (IPR003618, aa 925-1046) suggesting direct interaction with stalled RNA Polymerase II, and a C-terminal SPOC domain (IPR012921/PF14467, aa 1200-1349) characteristic of the SPEN family of transcriptional repressors. AlphaFold predicts a globally disordered structure (pLDDT 43.0), consistent with a large intrinsically disordered protein (IDP) that functions as a flexible scaffold or 'hub' rather than a rigid enzyme, allowing it to sample multiple conformational states during transcription cycles. STRING interactions place PHF3 at the nexus of transcription and chromatin organization: ultra-high confidence partner NIPBL (0.976, cohesin loader essential for loop extrusion), POLR2A (0.877, RPB1 subunit of RNA Pol II), SETD2 (0.766, H3K36me3 methyltransferase), SETX (0.559, senataxin helicase), and BPTF (0.680, nucleosome remodeling factor). HPA confirms ubiquitous nuclear expression with low tissue specificity, localizing to the nucleoplasm. ClinVar lists 210 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, though many appear to be mapping artifacts to the neighboring EYS gene; however, true PHF3 loss-of-function mutations are implicated in neurodevelopmental disorders and retinitis pigmentosa-like phenotypes due to its role in neuronal gene regulation. Recent literature (2023) explicitly links PHF3 (via its SPOC domain) and its paralog DIDO3 to the co-regulation of gene expression programs driving neuronal differentiation, likely by recruiting the NIPBL-cohesin complex to specific promoters to establish long-range enhancer-promoter loops.
