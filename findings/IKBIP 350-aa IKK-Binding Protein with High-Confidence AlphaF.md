## Description
IKBIP (UniProt Q70UQ0), or Inhibitor of nuclear factor kappa-B kinase-interacting protein, encodes a 350-amino acid intracellular protein with a unique family signature (IPR024152/PTHR21734) spanning its entire length. While lacking canonical catalytic domains, AlphaFold predicts a highly confident, well-folded structure (global pLDDT: 82.6), suggesting a stable scaffold or regulatory role. The protein localizes to the nucleoli rim, endoplasmic reticulum, and mitochondria, positioning it at critical junctions of stress signaling and cell death.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis identifies IKBKB (IKK-beta, score 0.943) and CHUK (IKK-alpha, score 0.835) as primary partners, confirming its name-sake role as a direct regulator of the IKK complex and consequently the NF-κB pathway. Additional interactions include APAF1 (0.649), linking it to apoptosome assembly, and TP53 (0.406). HPA data shows ubiquitous expression with immune cell enhancement, consistent with a role in inflammatory signaling.\n\nDespite only 29 papers, recent literature (2024-2026) reveals IKBIP as a potent oncogenic driver across multiple malignancies. In glioma, the SP1-IKBIP axis drives proliferation and invasion via Wnt/β-catenin-mediated epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT). In esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC), it activates Akt signaling. In hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) and cervical cancer, it promotes progression via JAK-STAT3 signaling. ClinVar lists 7 pathogenic copy number gains on chromosome 12, suggesting dosage sensitivity. We hypothesize IKBIP functions as a molecular bridge: stabilizing the IKK complex to sustain NF-κB-driven survival while simultaneously modulating mitochondrial apoptosis thresholds via APAF1 interaction, making it a critical node in cancer cell resistance to stress.
