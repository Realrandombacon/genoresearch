## Description
STARD5 (StAR-related lipid transfer protein 5, UniProt Q9NSY2) is a 213 aa lipid transfer protein with a canonical START domain (IPR002913, PF01852, positions 7-213) for sterol binding and transport. AlphaFold structure shows very high confidence (pLDDT 96.9), indicating an exceptionally stable, well-folded lipid-binding pocket. HPA data reveals low tissue specificity with ubiquitous expression, localized to vesicles and cytosol. STRING interactions identify partners in lipid metabolism: PCTP (0.940, phosphatidylcholine transfer protein), STARD10 (0.685), CERT1 (0.647, ceramide transfer), STARD7 (0.645), SREBF1 (0.615, sterol regulatory element-binding transcription factor), NR1H3/LXRα (0.567, liver X receptor), and other START family members (STARD8 0.577, STARD3 0.543, STAR 0.533). ClinVar contains 20 pathogenic variants at 15q24.1-26.3. Literature (69 papers) reveals STARD5 as a critical ER stress survival protein that binds cholesterol and 25-hydroxycholesterol (25-HC), modulates B cell cholesterol synthesis and IgG1 plasma cell differentiation, correlates with steatosis and liver fibrosis progression, and promotes colorectal cancer stem cell phenotypes through cholesterol homeostasis regulation. FUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: STARD5 acts as an ER-to-membrane cholesterol shuttle that integrates unfolded protein response (UPR) signaling with cellular sterol distribution, enabling cells to survive ER stress by redistributing cholesterol to maintain membrane integrity. MECHANISTIC PROPOSAL: Under ER stress, STARD5 expression is upregulated via the UPR pathway. The START domain binds cholesterol or 25-HC in the ER membrane and transfers it to the plasma membrane or other organelles. This activity modulates SREBF1 processing (STRING 0.615) and LXRα signaling (NR1H3, STRING 0.567), creating a feedback loop that balances cholesterol synthesis with membrane demand. In cancer stem cells, elevated STARD5 maintains high plasma membrane cholesterol, promoting Wnt and Hedgehog signaling pathway activity. In B cells, STARD5-mediated cholesterol transfer affects membrane raft composition required for IgG1 class switching.
