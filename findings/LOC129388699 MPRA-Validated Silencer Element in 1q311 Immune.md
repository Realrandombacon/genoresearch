## Description
LOC129388699 (Gene ID: 129388699) is a functionally validated cis-regulatory silencer element mapping to chromosome 1q31.1 (191,226,305-191,226,505 bp, ~200 bp span). RefSeq annotation explicitly states: "MPRA-validated peak596 silencer" - validated by STARR-seq massively parallel reporter assays in K562 erythroleukemia cells as repressing super core promoter SCP1 activity. Multiple evidence lines confirm non-coding regulatory function: (1) No UniProt accession (not a protein); (2) Genomic span of 200 bp is far too small for protein-coding gene; (3) ClinVar contains 4 pathogenic copy number variants spanning 1q31.1-32.1, 1q25.2-31.3, 1q25.2-32.1, and 1q31.1-42.11 regions (deletions 10.6-20.3 Mb, duplication 37.2 Mb), associated with 1q rearrangement syndromes featuring intellectual disability, developmental delay, craniofacial dysmorphism, cardiac defects, and immune dysfunction. The 1q31 region contains critical dosage-sensitive genes including F3 (coagulation factor III/tissue factor, coagulation cascade initiation), IL6R (interleukin-6 receptor, inflammatory cytokine signaling, tocilizumab target), RGS1 (regulator of G-protein signaling 1, lymphocyte chemotaxis), PEX19 (peroxisome biogenesis factor 19), and multiple immune regulatory genes; (4) No HPA expression (non-coding - Ensembl ID could not be resolved); (5) No STRING interactions (non-coding - timeout expected). Mechanistic hypothesis: LOC129388699 functions as a transcriptional silencer that represses gene expression in hematopoietic or immune contexts. The element likely recruits co-repressor complexes (e.g., Polycomb repressive complex 2, NuRD, or HDAC-containing complexes) via sequence-specific DNA-binding proteins to establish repressive chromatin (H3K27me3, H3K9me3, DNA methylation). K562 erythroleukemia validation suggests activity in erythroid/myeloid lineage. The 1q31 region forms a topologically associating domain (TAD) with extensive long-range chromatin interactions critical for immune and coagulation function. Pathogenic 1q CNVs disrupt silencer-enhancer balance and TAD architecture, causing long-range dysregulation of F3, IL6R, RGS1, and PEX19. F3 dysregulation affects coagulation cascade and thrombosis risk. IL6R dysregulation affects inflammatory cytokine signaling - critical for immune homeostasis and therapeutic targeting (tocilizumab). This represents a critical class of "dark genes" that are actually functional non-coding regulatory elements misannotated as genes due to automated pipeline predictions. The MPRA validation provides direct functional evidence of regulatory activity in erythroid lineage.
