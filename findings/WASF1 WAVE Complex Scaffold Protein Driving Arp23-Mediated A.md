# WASF1: WAVE Complex Scaffold Protein Driving Arp2/3-Mediated Actin Polymerization in Neuronal Development — Causative Gene for Neurodevelopmental Disorder with Absent Language and Variable Seizures (NEDALVS) Through WH2 Domain Disruption

## Description
WASF1 (WASP Family Member 1, also known as WAVE1) is a 559-amino acid actin-binding scaffold protein characterized by an N-terminal SCAR/WAVE family domain (IPR028288/PTHR12902, positions 1-188) containing the SCAR helical domain (PF27569, positions 3-180) mediating complex assembly, and a C-terminal WH2/WASP homology 2 domain (IPR003124/IPR036892/PF02205/PS51082/SM00246/cd22071, positions 485-559 with core WH2 motif at 495-520) binding actin monomers for polymerization nucleation, classified under actin cytoskeleton organization (GO:0030036) and actin binding (GO:0003779). STRING interactions reveal an exceptionally tight functional network exclusively with WAVE regulatory complex (WRC) components: NCKAP1 (0.999, NCK-associated protein 1 - highest confidence), ABI1 (0.999, Abl interactor 1), BRK1/HSPC300 (0.999), CYFIP1 (0.999, cytoplasmic FMR1-interacting protein 1), CYFIP2 (0.999), WASF2 (0.990, paralogue), ABI2 (0.970), BAIAP2 (0.901, brain-specific angiogenesis inhibitor 1-associated protein 2), ARPC3 (0.665, actin-related protein 2/3 complex subunit 3), and NCKAP1L (0.513), indicating WASF1 functions as the catalytic core of the pentameric WAVE complex activating Arp2/3-mediated actin nucleation. HPA data shows tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in brain (99.1 nTPM) and testis (73.4 nTPM), detected in all tissues, low cancer specificity, group-enriched blood cell specificity, classified as a predicted intracellular actin-binding protein with disease involvement in epilepsy and intellectual disability. ClinVar contains 60 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including frameshift deletions (c.270_271del, c.1429del/p.Gln477fs, c.1422del/p.Pro475fs, c.412_415del/p.Thr138fs, c.1257dup/p.Val420fs), nonsense mutations (c.304C>T/p.Arg102Ter), missense variants (c.481T>C/p.Trp161Arg, c.1171C>T/p.Pro391Ser, c.484A>G/p.Lys162Glu), and splice site variants (c.894-2A>G); these cause Neurodevelopmental Disorder with Absent Language and Variable Seizures (NEDALVS, OMIM #618707), an autosomal dominant condition featuring motor development delay, intellectual disability, wide-based gait, and epilepsy. AlphaFold structure (AF-Q92558-F1) predicts medium confidence folding (pLDDT 66.2) consistent with a partially structured scaffold protein containing ordered SCAR and WH2 domains coupled with flexible linker regions typical of actin regulatory proteins that undergo conformational activation. With 52 papers, WASF1 represents a 'gray' gene rapidly transitioning from dark due to definitive disease gene discovery (2023-2026): recurrent nonsense variant p.Arg506Ter identified in multiple Chinese families with NEDALVS; first description of polymicrogyria-causing pathogenic variant in monozygotic twins; asymptomatic truncation revealing pathogenic mechanism and therapeutic strategy; WASF1 deletion attenuating tau hyperphosphorylation, microglial state transition, and cognitive deficits in P301S tau mice linking to Alzheimer's pathology. The mechanistic hypothesis posits that WASF1 functions as the catalytic core of the WAVE complex that: (1) remains autoinhibited in cytoplasm until Rac1 GTPase signaling releases conformational constraint through Sra1/CYFIP subunits; (2) uses its WH2 domain to bind G-actin monomers positioning them for Arp2/3 complex-mediated nucleation creating branched actin networks; (3) drives lamellipodia formation and growth cone dynamics essential for neuronal migration, axon guidance, and synapse formation explaining brain-enriched expression (99.1 nTPM) and neurodevelopmental phenotypes when disrupted; (4) regulates activity-dependent actin remodeling in mature neurons linking to tau pathology and cognitive function; truncating variants causing haploinsufficiency disrupt WRC assembly or actin nucleation capacity producing NEDALVS through impaired neuronal development and circuit formation.
