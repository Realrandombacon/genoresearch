## Description
Calsyntenin-2 (CLSTN2) is a 955-amino acid type I transmembrane protein belonging to the calsyntenin family of postsynaptic adhesion molecules. InterPro analysis reveals a modular domain architecture characteristic of the calsyntenin subfamily: two extracellular cadherin repeats (IPR002126, residues 43-171/161-280; PF00028, residues 166-253; G3DSA:2.60.40.60, residues 36-157/158-256) mediating calcium-dependent homophilic cell-cell adhesion, a concanavalin A-like lectin domain (IPR013320, residues 330-541; G3DSA:2.60.120.200, residues 334-521), a calsyntenin-specific N-terminal immunoglobulin-like domain (IPR060323/PF28399, residues 36-152), and a large calsyntenin C-terminal cytoplasmic domain (IPR045588/PF19699, residues 551-892) that mediates intracellular protein interactions. AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (pLDDT 78.9), consistent with a well-folded multi-domain adhesion receptor with defined extracellular ligand-binding regions and an intracellular signaling tail.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places CLSTN2 within the vesicle transport and synaptic organization network with highest confidence for NMNAT3 (0.902, nicotinamide nucleotide adenylyltransferase 3, a NAD biosynthetic enzyme localized to vesicles), followed by kinesin light chain family members KLC2 (0.707), KLC4 (0.700), KLC1 (0.696), and KLC3 (0.569). This interaction profile strongly supports a role as a kinesin-1 cargo receptor, where the cytoplasmic C-terminal domain binds KLCs to link membrane vesicles to microtubule-based transport machinery. Additional partners include GRIP1 (0.660, glutamate receptor-interacting protein 1, a PDZ domain scaffold), NRXN2 (0.490, neurexin-2, a presynaptic adhesion molecule), CDH17 (0.543, cadherin-17), and SLC12A9 (0.488, solute carrier). The KLC1-4 interactions are particularly notable, as calsyntenins are established cargo adaptors for anterograde axonal transport of synaptic vesicle precursors.\n\nHuman Protein Atlas classifies CLSTN2 as 'tissue enhanced' with top expression in ovary (39.7 nTPM) and brain (19.7 nTPM), consistent with dual roles in reproductive biology and neuronal function. It is categorized as a predicted membrane protein involved in cell adhesion, with subcellular localization to vesicles and the cytokinetic bridge. The ovarian enrichment is distinctive among calsyntenins and suggests specialized functions in folliculogenesis or ovulation, supported by literature linking CLSTN2 to sheep ovulation genes (Fec locus).\n\nClinVar reports 27 pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number variations spanning 3q13.31-29 (coordinates: chr3:116620308-172042292 gain, chr3:131235568-150065289 loss, chr3:132561657-197851986 gain, chr3:138173683-162494699 gain). While no specific monogenic disease is named solely for CLSTN2, the 3q22.1-29 region is associated with neurodevelopmental disorders, and CNVs in this locus may contribute to cognitive impairment, autism spectrum disorders, or cardiovascular phenotypes given the gene's expression pattern.\n\nLiterature analysis (72 papers) indicates CLSTN2 is moderately characterized with emerging functional insights. Recent 2025 work identified CLSTN2 expression as associated with carotid plaque presence independently from classical risk factors, suggesting vascular roles. Additional 2025 research demonstrated myocardin-mediated CLSTN2 regulation facilitating kinesin-1-dependent PAI-1 transport in pleural mesothelial cells, directly linking CLSTN2 to cargo trafficking. A 2024 study identified Lnc-CLSTN2-1:1 as promoting osteosarcoma progression via PI3K/AKT signaling and redox balance disruption. GWAS data linked CLSTN2 to HDL-mediated cholesterol efflux capacity. In reproductive biology, CLSTN2 is implicated in sheep ovulation (Fec gene cluster).\n\nWe hypothesize that CLSTN2 functions as a dual-purpose cargo adaptor and adhesion receptor. In neurons, it likely operates analogously to CLSTN1/3, binding kinesin light chains via its cytoplasmic C-terminal domain to transport synaptic vesicle precursors, neurotransmitter receptors, or mitochondria along axons. The cadherin ectodomains may mediate trans-synaptic adhesion with presynaptic neurexins (NRXN2 interaction supports this), organizing synaptic architecture. The high NMNAT3 interaction score suggests CLSTN2 may transport NAD biosynthetic machinery to distal axons, supporting axonal maintenance and preventing degeneration. In ovary, CLSTN2 may regulate follicular cell adhesion, oocyte-cumulus communication, or vesicle trafficking during ovulation. The cytoplasmic domain may also recruit signaling scaffolds (GRIP1) to couple adhesion to downstream pathways (PI3K/AKT). The 3q CNVs may cause dosage-sensitive phenotypes affecting neurodevelopment (altered axonal transport), cardiovascular health (endothelial adhesion/cholesterol efflux), or reproduction. CLSTN2 represents a candidate biomarker for atherosclerosis, a potential modifier of axonal transport in neurodegeneration, and an uncharacterized regulator of ovarian function.
