## Description
ARL5B (UniProt Q96KC2), or ADP-ribosylation factor-like protein 5B, encodes a compact 179-amino acid small GTPase belonging to the ARF/SAR superfamily (IPR006689/IPR024156). The protein features a canonical P-loop NTPase fold (IPR027417/G3DSA:3.40.50.300) and a specific Arf-like GTP-binding domain (IPR005225/SM00177, aa 1-179) containing the conserved switch I/II regions essential for GTP/GDP cycling. AlphaFold predicts an exceptionally confident structure (global pLDDT: 92.7), indicating a rigid, well-defined conformation typical of active GTPases.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places ARL5B at the nexus of vesicular trafficking and motor protein recruitment: KIF1A (0.766), PLEKHM2 (0.749), KLC2 (0.715), TOM1 (0.710), KLC1 (0.707), and BORCS8 (0.628). These partners link ARL5B to the kinesin-1 motor complex (via KLCs) and the HOPS/CORVET tethering complexes (via PLEKHM2, BORCS8), suggesting a role in directing lysosomal motility and fusion. HPA data confirms ubiquitous expression with cell-type enhancement, consistent with a housekeeping trafficking role.\n\nWith only 36 papers, ARL5B remains a partially characterized dark gene with emerging oncogenic significance. Recent 2025 studies identify ARL5B as a driver of esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) progression via the ROCK1–SREBP1 axis, mediating lipid metabolic reprogramming. In retinoblastoma, ARL5B-associated lysosomal trafficking is inhibited by the ribosomal peptide RPL41 to suppress tumor growth. ClinVar lists 14 pathogenic variants, including copy number gains/losses on chromosome 10p and a splice variant (c.492-5T>G), implying dosage sensitivity. We hypothesize ARL5B functions as a GTP-dependent switch that recruits kinesin motors to lysosomes via KLC binding, facilitating their perinuclear clustering or peripheral dispersal to modulate lipid metabolism and mTOR signaling in cancer cells.
