## Description
KYAT1 (Kynurenine--oxoglutarate transaminase 1, also known as KAT-I) is a partially characterized metabolic enzyme with only 24 publications despite its role in the clinically important kynurenine pathway. This 422-amino acid cytosolic protein (UniProt Q16773) is a PLP-dependent aminotransferase containing class I/II aminotransferase domains (PF00155, positions 29-409) and pyridoxal phosphate-dependent transferase domains (IPR015424, positions 7-418). AlphaFold predicts an exceptionally well-folded structure with global pLDDT of 97.9 (very high confidence), indicating a stable enzymatic fold.\n\nSTRING interactions reveal KYAT1 operates within a tight kynurenine pathway network: AADAT (0.999, another kynurenine transaminase), KMO (0.983, kynurenine 3-monooxygenase), KYNU (0.980, kynureninase), AFMID (0.955, arylformamidase), plus sulfur metabolism enzymes CTH (0.963) and CBS (0.935). This positions KYAT1 at a metabolic branch point between neuroactive kynurenine metabolites and sulfur amino acid metabolism.\n\nHPA shows expression in some tissues with cytosolic localization. ClinVar contains 35 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including splice site mutations (c.202-2A>T) and missense variants (p.Lys189Asn), though specific diseases remain uncharacterized. Conservation across mouse (424 aa) and rat (423 aa) indicates functional constraint.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: KYAT1 produces kynurenic acid (KYNA), an endogenous NMDA receptor antagonist and α7-nicotinic acetylcholine receptor antagonist, linking tryptophan metabolism to neuropsychiatric function. The strong interaction with sulfur metabolism enzymes (CTH, CBS) suggests KYAT1 may coordinate kynurenine and transsulfuration pathways during oxidative stress. Pathogenic variants may disrupt KYNA production, potentially contributing to schizophrenia, depression, or neurodegenerative diseases where kynurenine pathway imbalance is implicated.
