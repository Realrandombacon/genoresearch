## Description
OBI1 (ORC Ubiquitin Ligase 1, also known as RNF219, Ensembl ENSG00000152193) encodes a 726-amino acid nuclear E3 ubiquitin ligase (UniProt Q5W0B1, ~82 kDa) representing a partially characterized dark gene with only 23 publications despite its fundamental role in DNA replication initiation and connections to cancer progression. Structurally, OBI1 contains multiple conserved domains defining its enzymatic function: RING-type zinc finger (InterPro IPR001841/Zinc finger RING-type, residues 18-56; IPR035691/ORC ubiquitin ligase 1 RING finger, residues 15-59; PF13923/Zinc finger C3HC4 type RING finger, residues 18-55; PS50089/Zinc finger RING-type profile, residues 18-56), CATH-Gene3D G3DSA:3.30.40.10/Zinc-RING finger domain C3HC4 (residues 2-124), SSF57850/RING-U-box superfamily (residues 17-64), and OBI1 family domain (IPR039209/ORC ubiquitin ligase 1, residues 1-726) with GO annotations for ubiquitin-protein transferase activity (GO:0004842), regulation of DNA replication (GO:0006275), and protein monoubiquitination (GO:0006513). AlphaFold predicts a medium-confidence structure (global pLDDT 57.0, modeled residues 1-726), indicating a partially ordered protein with structured RING domain and extensive intrinsically disordered regions consistent with its role as a dynamic E3 ligase interacting with multiple substrates. Strong vertebrate conservation is evident from orthologs: Mus musculus (722 aa), Pongo abelii (orangutan, 726 aa)—maintaining nearly identical length across mammals, suggesting critical functional constraints. Functionally, Human Protein Atlas data reveals 'Low tissue specificity' with expression 'Detected in all' tissues, 'Low cancer specificity', 'Low immune cell specificity', and 'Low cell type specificity'—consistent with a housekeeping role in fundamental cellular processes; subcellular localization includes nucleoplasm, nuclear bodies, and cytosol; protein class is 'Enzymes' and 'Predicted intracellular proteins'; biological process is 'Ubl conjugation pathway' and molecular function is 'Transferase'. STRING interactions reveal two distinct functional networks: (1) DNA replication/origin firing complex—ORC3 (0.697, medium confidence), ORC5 (0.656), LRWD1 (0.679, origin recognition complex-associated protein); (2) CNOT deadenylase complex—CNOT10 (0.778, high confidence), CNOT11 (0.771), CNOT1 (0.647), CNOT7 (0.639), CNOT3 (0.626)—suggesting OBI1 bridges DNA replication and mRNA decay pathways; additional partners include TOB1 (0.651, transducer of ERBB2 antiproliferative protein) and WRAP73 (0.618, WD repeat-containing protein). The landmark 2019 study (cited 30x) demonstrated OBI1 promotes DNA replication origin firing by ubiquitinating ORC complex components, establishing its essential role in replication initiation. Recent 2022 studies identified OBI1-AS1 (antisense lncRNA) as an astrocyte marker with possible roles in glioma recurrence and progression from low-grade glioma to glioblastoma. ClinVar documents 79 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large-scale copy number losses spanning chromosome 13q11-q33.2 (e.g., chr13:54206989-88417670 x1, chr13:56450978-93582180 x1, chr13:64825656-103641349 x1, chr13:49547974-98214905 x1), copy number gains (chr13:19436287-92292639 x3), large deletions (NC_000013.10:g.[71038137_86760324del;86910325_89586915del]), and splice region variants (c.301-65T>G, c.209-10_209-9del, NR_047001.1(OBI1-AS1):n.768-3C>G); while no specific monogenic disease is formally linked solely to OBI1 mutations, the chromosome 13q location overlaps with retinoblastoma (RB1) locus and 13q deletion syndrome regions causing developmental delay, intellectual disability, and cancer predisposition—suggesting OBI1 haploinsufficiency may contribute to replication stress-induced genomic instability and tumorigenesis. The mechanistic hypothesis: OBI1 functions as a nuclear E3 ubiquitin ligase that: (1) In normal physiology—localizes to nucleoplasm and nuclear bodies where its RING domain recruits E2 ubiquitin-conjugating enzymes to monoubiquitinate ORC complex subunits (ORC3, ORC5, LRWD1), promoting pre-replicative complex activation and DNA replication origin firing during S-phase entry; interaction with CNOT deadenylase complex suggests coordination between DNA replication and mRNA turnover, potentially regulating stability of replication factors or cell cycle transcripts; (2) In cancer—OBI1 overexpression may drive replication stress and genomic instability via excessive origin firing, contributing to glioma progression (as suggested by OBI1-AS1 glioma studies); conversely, OBI1 haploinsufficiency from 13q deletions may impair replication origin firing, causing replication fork stalling and DNA damage accumulation predisposing to retinoblastoma and other 13q deletion syndrome malignancies; (3) The medium-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 57.0) with extensive disordered regions suggests conformational flexibility enabling interaction with diverse substrates (ORC complex, CNOT complex) analogous to other RING E3 ligases; (4) The 79 ClinVar CNVs suggest OBI1 is dosage-sensitive, consistent with roles in replication requiring precise expression levels; (5) Connection to TOB1 (antiproliferative protein) suggests OBI1 may integrate proliferation signals with replication licensing. Future work should identify direct OBI1 ubiquitination substrates via ubiquitin remnant profiling (K-ε-GG proteomics); map OBI1-ORC interaction interface via co-IP/MS and mutagenesis; determine whether OBI1 monoubiquitinates vs. polyubiquitinates ORC subunits and functional consequences; characterize OBI1 knockout phenotypes in mice for replication defects and cancer susceptibility; test whether OBI1 variants contribute to 13q deletion syndrome phenotypes beyond RB1 loss; investigate OBI1-AS1 lncRNA function in glioma and whether it regulates OBI1 expression; assess OBI1 expression as prognostic biomarker across cancer types; determine cell cycle regulation of OBI1 activity (phosphorylation, degradation); solve crystal structure of RING domain with E2 enzyme; explore therapeutic potential of OBI1 inhibition in cancers with replication stress; and test whether OBI1 coordinates replication with transcription via CNOT complex interaction.
