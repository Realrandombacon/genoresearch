## Description
ARHGAP33 (Rho GTPase-Activating Protein 33) is a 1,287-amino acid multidomain scaffold protein that functions as a critical regulator of intracellular trafficking for the neurotrophin receptor TrkB, thereby controlling hippocampal development and cognitive function. With 24 publications, it remains partially characterized, particularly regarding its structural integration of lipid sensing and GTPase regulation. The protein architecture features three distinct functional modules: an N-terminal Phox (PX) domain (IPR036871, residues 49-172) for phosphatidylinositol binding and membrane recruitment, a central Src Homology 3 (SH3) domain (IPR001452, residues 186-248) for protein-protein interactions, and a catalytic C-terminal RhoGAP domain (IPR000198, PF00620, residues 315-510) that accelerates GTP hydrolysis on Rho family GTPases. AlphaFold predicts a partially structured conformation (global pLDDT 53.3), consistent with a flexible scaffold where ordered catalytic domains are connected by long disordered linkers to facilitate dynamic complex assembly.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals a network centered on cytoskeletal and trafficking regulators, including direct GTPase substrates CDC42 (score 0.645) and RAC1 (0.503), the sorting receptor SORT1 (0.499) which mediates TrkB transport, and the kinase NEK6 (0.632). Notably, interactions with SYNGAP1 (0.471) and NTRK1 (0.479) link ARHGAP33 to synaptic plasticity and broader neurotrophin signaling. Human Protein Atlas data indicates ubiquitous expression ('detected in all') with enhancement in specific cell types and a 'cancer enhanced' profile, aligning with recent findings linking ARHGAP33 overexpression to poor prognosis in kidney renal clear cell carcinoma and hepatocellular carcinoma. Subcellular localization spans the plasma membrane, actin filaments, and cytosol, reflecting its role as a shuttle between membrane compartments.\n\nClinVar reports 13 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, exclusively large copy number losses and gains spanning chromosome 19q, suggesting dosage sensitivity is critical for normal development. While no single Mendelian disorder is yet assigned, mouse and zebrafish models demonstrate that ARHGAP33 deficiency leads to impaired TrkB surface expression, defective hippocampal circuit formation, cognitive deficits, and abnormal ocular development. We hypothesize that ARHGAP33 acts as a 'traffic light' at the intersection of endosomal sorting and cytoskeletal dynamics: its PX domain anchors it to specific PI(3)P-rich endosomes, where its GAP activity locally inactivates CDC42/RAC1 to permit vesicle uncoating or fusion, ensuring efficient TrkB delivery to the synapse. Dysregulation disrupts neurotrophic support, contributing to neuropsychiatric disorders and enabling cancer cell invasion through aberrant cytoskeletal remodeling.
