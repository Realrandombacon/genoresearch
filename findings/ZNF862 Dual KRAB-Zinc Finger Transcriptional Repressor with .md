# ZNF862: Dual KRAB-Zinc Finger Transcriptional Repressor with Mitochondrial Localization and RNase-like Domain

## Description
ZNF862 encodes a 1169 amino acid zinc finger protein (UniProt O60290) with a distinctive domain architecture combining transcriptional repression and potential RNA processing functions. The protein contains two canonical KRAB domains (PF01352, IPR001909) at residues 10-37 and 332-373, which recruit KAP1/TRIM28 corepressors to silence target genes through heterochromatin formation. Six C2H2-type zinc fingers (IPR006580, TTF-type) at positions 135-218 and 461-544 enable sequence-specific DNA binding to GC-rich promoter elements. Notably, ZNF862 harbors a C-terminal hAT dimerization domain (IPR008906, PF05699, residues 1004-1068) enabling homodimerization or heterodimerization with related family members. Most remarkably, a FAM200/ZBED5 RNase-like domain (PF27073, residues 596-714) suggests potential RNA cleavage activity, positioning ZNF862 at the intersection of transcriptional silencing and post-transcriptional RNA regulation. HPA data indicates ubiquitous expression across all tissues with mitochondrial subcellular localization, an unusual combination for a KRAB-ZFP that typically functions in the nucleus. STRING interactions reveal functional connections to FOXN4 (0.617), PGBD2 (0.603), PGBD1 (0.576), and THAP9 (0.538)—all DNA-binding chromatin regulators—supporting a role in chromatin remodeling complexes. ClinVar documents 69 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly copy number losses and gains at 7q34-36.3, implicating ZNF862 dosage sensitivity in human disease. The mechanistic hypothesis proposes that ZNF862 functions as a bifunctional epigenetic regulator: KRAB domains recruit repressive chromatin machinery to silence developmental genes, while the RNase-like domain may process non-coding RNAs or nascent transcripts at target loci, coupling transcriptional repression with RNA turnover.
