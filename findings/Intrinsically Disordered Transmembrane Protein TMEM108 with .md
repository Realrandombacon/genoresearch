## Description
TMEM108 (UniProt Q6UXF1) is a 575 amino acid transmembrane protein representing an understudied component of the human dark genome with emerging roles in membrane trafficking, cytoskeletal organization, and neuronal development. The protein architecture is defined by membership in the IPR031508 Transmembrane protein 108 family (residues 1-575), PF15759 TMEM108 Pfam family (residues 62-575), and PTHR28673 TRANSMEMBRANE PROTEIN 108 family (residues 1-575), indicating evolutionary conservation across vertebrates but no homology to characterized functional domains—suggesting TMEM108 represents a unique protein module awaiting biochemical characterization. AlphaFold predicts a low-confidence structure (global pLDDT 45.3) spanning the full 1-575 residue length, indicating a predominantly intrinsically disordered protein or non-globular architecture—this is highly informative as it suggests TMEM108 functions through flexible protein-protein interactions rather than enzymatic catalysis, consistent with scaffold or adaptor protein roles in multi-protein complexes. HPA classifies TMEM108 as both a predicted intracellular and membrane protein with tissue-enhanced RNA expression showing specificity for retina (32.3 nTPM, top tissue) and skeletal muscle (21.7 nTPM), with detection in many other tissues at lower levels. This retina-enrichment suggests specialized function in photoreceptor development, synaptic transmission in retinal neurons, or visual signal processing. Subcellular localization is remarkably diverse: nucleoli, vesicles, plasma membrane, and cytosol—this multi-compartment distribution suggests TMEM108 may shuttle between cellular locations or exist in distinct isoforms serving different functions. STRING interaction network reveals high-confidence associations with functionally coherent partners involved in membrane trafficking and cytoskeletal dynamics: SH3GL2 (0.809, endophilin-A1, critical for clathrin-mediated endocytosis and synaptic vesicle recycling), DYNC1H1 (0.690, dynein cytoplasmic 1 heavy chain 1, minus-end directed microtubule motor for retrograde axonal transport), DCTN1 (0.655, dynactin subunit 1, activates dynein motor function), DST (0.549, dystonin, cytoskeletal linker protein), DLX3 (0.547, distal-less homeobox 3, transcription factor for ectodermal development), GAS2 (0.521, growth arrest-specific 2, microtubule-actin crosslinker), MSX2 (0.505, msh homeobox 2, transcription factor), SYNDIG1L (0.498, syndig1-like, uncharacterized), CDV3 (0.453, cell division cycle associated 3), SYNDIG1 (0.438, syndig1, uncharacterized). The presence of SH3GL2/endophilin strongly implicates TMEM108 in endocytic pathways, while DYNC1H1/DCTN1 connections suggest involvement in dynein-mediated transport along microtubules—particularly relevant for neuronal axonal transport. ClinVar contains 17 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number gains and losses affecting chromosome 3q regions (chr3:132561657-197851986, chr3:131235568-150065289, chr3:116620308-172042292, chr3:132642704-136360844, chr3:132716978-144784743) and deletions. The 3q region is dosage-sensitive and alterations cause neurodevelopmental disorders, though no specific disease is currently attributed solely to TMEM108 mutations. Evolutionary conservation across mammals (mouse Q8BHE4, 574 aa; bovine A6QLF8, 583 aa) indicates strong functional constraint. The mechanistic model positions TMEM108 as an intrinsically disordered scaffold protein using its flexible architecture (pLDDT 45.3) for dynamic protein-protein interactions linking endocytic machinery (SH3GL2/endophilin) to dynein-dynactin motor complexes (DYNC1H1, DCTN1) for coordinated membrane trafficking and vesicle transport. Retina-enriched expression (32.3 nTPM) suggests specialized role in photoreceptor synaptic vesicle cycling or opsin trafficking. Multi-compartment localization (nucleoli, vesicles, plasma membrane, cytosol) suggests potential moonlighting functions or regulated shuttling. Loss-of-function through 3q deletions would disrupt endocytic-transport coupling, impairing neuronal development and contributing to neurodevelopmental phenotypes observed in 3q deletion/duplication syndromes.
