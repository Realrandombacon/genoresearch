## Description
TMEM133 (transmembrane protein 133, Gene ID: 83935) is a predicted 129-amino acid protein encoded on chromosome 11q22.1 that represents a very dark gene with only 1 publication and significant uncertainty regarding its existence and function. RefSeq notes this is an intronless gene with evidence of transcription but the protein remains predicted (not experimentally validated). Literature analysis reveals only 1 paper: a 2021 study demonstrating that triazole/thiadiazole-substituted 4'-demethylepipodophyllotoxin derivatives induced apoptosis in HeLa cells by up-regulating TMEM133 (cited 1x), suggesting a potential role in cancer cell death pathways. No human UniProt accession exists—only chimpanzee ortholog H2Q4L8 (129 aa) is available. AlphaFold structure prediction for the chimpanzee ortholog shows very low confidence (global pLDDT 39.7), indicating a mostly disordered, non-globular protein that may function through protein-protein interactions rather than enzymatic activity or may represent a non-functional gene model. InterPro scan failed, suggesting no conserved domains. HPA expression data could not be resolved, further indicating poor characterization. STRING interactions returned data for ARHGAP42 (a different gene), highlighting annotation confusion around TMEM133. ClinVar contains 15 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all copy number variations spanning 11q14.1-q22.3 regions, though no specific monogenic disease is attributed solely to TMEM133; these CNVs are associated with various developmental and neurological phenotypes. The single literature link to apoptosis in HeLa cells, combined with the disordered protein structure and 11q22.1 location (near ATM and other cancer-associated genes), suggests TMEM133 may function as a regulatory scaffold or signaling modulator in cell death pathways, potentially interacting with apoptotic machinery through intrinsically disordered regions. However, the lack of human protein validation, failed domain detection, and low structural confidence raise questions about whether TMEM133 represents a bona fide protein-coding gene or a transcriptional artifact. This represents a very dark gene requiring experimental validation of protein existence before functional characterization can proceed.
