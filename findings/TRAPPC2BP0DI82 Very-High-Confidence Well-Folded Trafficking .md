## Description
TRAPPC2B (Trafficking protein particle complex subunit 2B, also known as MIP-2A, SEDLP, SEDLP1, TRAPPC2P1) is a 140 amino acid intracellular protein (UniProt P0DI82) with very-high-confidence well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 92.2), characteristic of stable structural subunits within multi-protein trafficking complexes. The protein contains a Sedlin N-terminal conserved region (IPR006722/PF04628/cd14825, residues 9-136) that defines the sedlin family of TRAPP complex subunits, belongs to the trafficking protein particle complex subunit 2 family (IPR006722/PTHR12403, residues 5-138) involved in endoplasmic reticulum to Golgi vesicle-mediated transport (GO:0006888), contains a Longin-like domain superfamily fold (IPR011012/G3DSA:3.30.450.70/SSF64356, residues 1-138) that mediates membrane trafficking, and has a SNARE-like structural fold (SSF64356, residues 1-138) for vesicle fusion. TRAPPC2B is located on chromosome 19q13.43 and enables transcription corepressor binding activity, transcription regulator inhibitor activity, and transmembrane transporter binding activity, functioning in positive regulation of gene expression and skeletal system development as part of the TRAPP complex.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals an exceptionally high-confidence network of TRAPP complex subunits: TRAPPC1 (0.979, Trafficking protein particle complex subunit 1), TRAPPC5 (0.979, Trafficking protein particle complex subunit 5), TRAPPC8 (0.978, Trafficking protein particle complex subunit 8), TRAPPC2L (0.976, Trafficking protein particle complex subunit 2-like), TRAPPC4 (0.971, Trafficking protein particle complex subunit 4), TRAPPC6B (0.968, Trafficking protein particle complex subunit 6B), TRAPPC3 (0.965, Trafficking protein particle complex subunit 3), TRAPPC9 (0.962, Trafficking protein particle complex subunit 9), TRAPPC6A (0.928, Trafficking protein particle complex subunit 6A), and TRAPPC11 (0.919, Trafficking protein particle complex subunit 11). These interactions confirm TRAPPC2B functions as an integral subunit of the TRAPP (trafficking protein particle) complex that acts as a guanine nucleotide exchange factor (GEF) for Rab GTPases during ER-to-Golgi vesicle tethering and fusion.\n\nHPA expression reveals TRAPPC2B has low tissue specificity and is detected in all tissues, consistent with a housekeeping role in constitutive vesicular trafficking across cell types. Subcellular localization includes nucleoplasm, endoplasmic reticulum, and vesicles, consistent with TRAPP complex function at ER-Golgi intermediate compartments. The protein is classified as a predicted intracellular protein involved in ER-Golgi transport, transcription, and transport biological processes. Cell type-enhanced single cell specificity suggests specialized functions in secretory cell types with high vesicular trafficking demand.\n\nClinVar documents 10 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all consisting of copy number gains on chromosome 19q13: 19q13.41-13.43, 19q13.33-13.43 amplifications (multiple overlapping duplications). The variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity. While no specific Mendelian disease is annotated, 19q CNVs are associated with developmental delay, intellectual disability, and skeletal anomalies. Given TRAPPC2B's role in ER-Golgi transport and skeletal system development, loss-of-function or dosage imbalance may cause skeletal dysplasia phenotypes through impaired collagen trafficking and secretion, similar to TRAPPC2-related spondyloepiphyseal dysplasia tarda (SEDT).\n\nConservation is strong across vertebrates: human (P0DI82, 140 aa), mouse, indicating an ancient and essential function in vesicular trafficking conserved in mammals.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: TRAPPC2B functions as a core subunit of the TRAPP II/III complex that activates Rab1/Rab11 GTPases to drive ER-to-Golgi vesicle tethering and fusion. The well-folded Longin-like domain (pLDDT 92.2) provides a stable structural scaffold that interacts with other TRAPP subunits (TRAPPC1/3/4/5/6A/6B/8/9/11) to form the active GEF complex. The Sedlin domain mediates specific interactions with TRAPPC2L and may regulate complex assembly. Through interactions with the full TRAPP network, TRAPPC2B positions the complex at ER exit sites to capture COPII vesicles and facilitate their tethering to the Golgi membrane. Nucleoplasmic localization suggests TRAPPC2B may also regulate transcription of trafficking-related genes. 19q CNVs cause TRAPPC2B dosage imbalance, potentially disrupting TRAPP complex stoichiometry and impairing collagen/glycoprotein trafficking, leading to skeletal dysplasia phenotypes. TRAPPC2B represents a candidate gene for spondyloepiphyseal dysplasia and congenital disorders of glycosylation.
