## Description
MRPL36 (Mitochondrial Ribosomal Protein L36, UniProt Q9P0J6) is a 103 amino acid mitochondrial ribosomal protein representing a genuine dark gene with only 9 literature references despite essential cellular function. This well-folded protein (AlphaFold pLDDT 71.5) is a structural component of the mitochondrial large ribosomal subunit (39S), containing the conserved ribosomal protein L36 domain (IPR000473, PF00444, residues 66-103) critical for mitochondrial translation. MRPL36 exhibits ubiquitous expression across all tissues (HPA: detected in all, low tissue specificity) with mitochondrial and nuclear body localization, consistent with its role as an essential housekeeping protein in oxidative phosphorylation. STRING interactions reveal 10 high-confidence partners (all scores 0.973-0.999) exclusively within the mitochondrial translation apparatus: MRPL17, MRPL33, MRPL13, MRPL32, MRPL27 (large subunit proteins), MRPS10, MRPS5 (small subunit proteins), and cytosolic ribosomal proteins RPL35, RPS3, RPS11 - suggesting coordinated regulation between mitochondrial and cytosolic translation systems. ClinVar documents 135 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly copy number losses and gains spanning 5p15.33-15.2 (chr5:113462-13341742), the critical region for Cri-du-chat syndrome (5p deletion syndrome). While these CNVs encompass multiple genes, MRPL36's essential mitochondrial function suggests it may contribute to the metabolic and developmental phenotypes of 5p deletion. Foundational literature (2004-2009, 9 papers total) establishes MRPL36's molecular mechanisms: (1) Assembly competent protein generation during mitochondrial translation (cited 52x), (2) LETM1-MRPL36 complex regulating mitochondrial ATP production and necrotic cell death (cited 77x), (3) Highly diverged L31 ribosomal protein homolog with additional functional domains in yeast mitochondria (cited 18x). The LETM1 interaction is particularly significant - LETM1 deletions cause Wolf-Hirschhorn syndrome, and the MRPL36-LETM1 complex couples mitochondrial translation to ATP synthesis and cell death pathways. Functional hypothesis: MRPL36 serves as a (1) Mitoribosome assembly factor - the L36 domain stabilizes large subunit structure during mitochondrial ribosome biogenesis, enabling efficient translation of 13 mitochondrially-encoded OXPHOS subunits; (2) Metabolic checkpoint - through LETM1 interaction, MRPL36 links mitochondrial translation status to ATP production and necrotic cell death decisions, potentially acting as a sensor for mitochondrial stress; (3) Dosage-sensitive essential gene - 135 ClinVar variants indicate extreme sensitivity to gene copy number, suggesting MRPL36 functions in stoichiometric complexes where precise expression is critical for mitoribosome assembly; (4) Cri-du-chat contributor - while 5p deletions cause characteristic phenotypes (high-pitched cry, intellectual disability, microcephaly, cardiac defects), MRPL36 haploinsufficiency may contribute to metabolic dysfunction, growth retardation, and developmental delay through impaired mitochondrial protein synthesis. Disease mechanism: MRPL36 deficiency impairs mitochondrial translation, reducing synthesis of Complex I, III, IV, and V subunits encoded by mtDNA. This causes OXPHOS dysfunction, ATP depletion, and potential activation of necrotic cell death via LETM1-MRPL36 complex disruption. In Cri-du-chat syndrome, MRPL36 hemizygosity may exacerbate metabolic stress in developing tissues with high energy demands (brain, heart, muscle). The 135 pathogenic variants suggest both deletions (haploinsufficiency) and duplications (dosage imbalance) cause disease, indicating MRPL36 requires precise expression levels. Therapeutic implication: MRPL36 represents a candidate modifier of Cri-du-chat syndrome severity and mitochondrial disease phenotypes. Its essential function makes it unsuitable for direct targeting, but understanding MRPL36-LETM1 interactions could reveal therapeutic approaches for mitochondrial disorders. Validation priorities include: determining MRPL36's precise position in mitoribosome structure, characterizing LETM1-MRPL36 complex stoichiometry, assessing tissue-specific sensitivity to MRPL36 deficiency, and correlating 5p deletion breakpoints with metabolic phenotypes to determine if MRPL36 loss correlates with specific clinical features.
