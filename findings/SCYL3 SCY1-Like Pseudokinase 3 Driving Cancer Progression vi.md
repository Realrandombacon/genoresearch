## Description
SCYL3 (SCY1 Like Pseudokinase 3), also known as PACE (Protein-Associating with the Carboxyl-terminal domain of Ezrin), is a 742-amino acid intracellular protein (UniProt Q8IZE3) representing a genuine dark gene with only 15 publications despite emerging roles in cancer metastasis and neuronal viability. Structurally, SCYL3 contains an N-terminal protein kinase-like domain (IPR000719/IPR001245/PF07714, residues 1-245) classified as catalytically inactive (pseudokinase) within the Catalytically Inactive Kinase-Related Protein family (IPR051177), followed by armadillo-like helical repeats (IPR011989/IPR016024/G3DSA:1.25.10.10, residues 243-425) and HEAT type 2 repeats (IPR021133, residues 338-375) characteristic of proteins involved in nucleocytoplasmic transport and vesicular trafficking. The protein belongs to the SCY1-like family alongside SCYL1 and SCYL2. AlphaFold predicts medium-confidence structure (pLDDT 69.4), consistent with a partially structured protein containing ordered kinase-like and armadillo domains connected by flexible linkers typical of scaffold/adaptor proteins. Functionally, SCYL3 localizes to the Golgi apparatus and cytosol with ubiquitous expression across all tissues (HPA: detected in all, low tissue specificity), suggesting housekeeping roles in intracellular transport. STRING interactions reveal connections to cytoskeletal organizers and trafficking machinery: EZR (0.874, ezrin—original binding partner linking to actin-membrane dynamics), BCDIN3D (0.623, tRNA methyltransferase), TXN2 (0.594, mitochondrial thioredoxin), COPG1/G2 (0.474-0.531, coatomer subunits for COPI vesicle trafficking), COPA (0.467), XPOT (0.447, exportin for tRNA), SCYL2 (0.442, paralog), BTBD9 (0.439, restlessness syndrome protein), and COPZ1 (0.433). Critically, ClinVar documents 28 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number gains/losses spanning 1q21-25 (multiple entries affecting megabase regions) and intronic SNVs (c.166-59T>G, c.351+92C>T, c.1313-18T>G), yet ZERO diseases are formally associated—a significant gap given the cancer and neuronal phenotypes. Recent literature establishes emerging oncogenic roles: SCYL3 knockdown enhances chemotherapy sensitivity in triple-negative breast cancer (2025); promotes hepatocellular carcinoma progression as a novel binding partner and regulator of ROCK2 (14 citations, 2022); overlaps with SCYL1 in maintaining motor neuron viability (21 citations, 2018); forms a novel SCYL3-NTRK1 fusion in colorectal cancer (20 citations, 2017); and shows differential expression in ovarian cancer and periodontitis. The mechanistic hypothesis: SCYL3 functions as a pseudokinase scaffold that coordinates ROCK2-mediated cytoskeletal remodeling with COPI vesicular trafficking at the Golgi, facilitating cancer cell invasion and metastasis. Through its ezrin-binding capacity, SCYL3 may bridge membrane-cytoskeleton interfaces required for invadopodia formation and extracellular matrix degradation. The pseudokinase domain likely serves regulatory rather than catalytic functions, potentially acting as a phospho-switch or competitive inhibitor of active kinases. In neurons, SCYL3 may cooperate with SCYL1 (mutations cause SCAR20/spinocerebellar ataxia) to maintain axonal transport and neuronal survival, suggesting that SCYL3 loss could contribute to neurodegenerative phenotypes not yet mapped. The 1q21 copy number variant syndromes may include SCYL3 haploinsufficiency contributing to developmental or neurological features. Future work should define SCYL3's role in ROCK2 signaling cascades, identify phosphorylation-dependent interaction partners, and screen patients with 1q21 CNVs or early-onset ataxia for SCYL3 mutations.
