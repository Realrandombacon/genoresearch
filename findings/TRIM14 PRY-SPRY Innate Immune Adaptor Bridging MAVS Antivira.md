# TRIM14: PRY-SPRY Innate Immune Adaptor Bridging MAVS Antiviral Signaling to NF-κB and STAT1 Inflammatory Responses

**Date:** 2026-03-20T14:40:00.635703

**Quality Score:** 2.05/10  (E=4.1, D=0.0) [WEAK]

## Description
TRIM14 encodes a 442 amino acid cytoplasmic E3 ubiquitin ligase adaptor (UniProt Q14142) belonging to the TRIM family with a distinctive domain architecture: N-terminal B-box type zinc finger (IPR000315, residues 19-61) followed by a C-terminal PRY-SPRY domain (IPR001870/IPR003877/PF00622, residues 249-442). The PRY-SPRY domain mediates protein-protein interactions critical for innate immune signaling. STRING interactions reveal TRIM14 operates as a high-confidence innate immune scaffold with strongest partners: MAVS (0.905, mitochondrial antiviral signaling protein), PPP6C (0.888, protein phosphatase 6 catalytic subunit), DDX58/RIG-I (0.688, cytosolic RNA helicase), STAT1 (0.662, signal transducer and activator of transcription), TRIM44 (0.630, TRIM family paralog), and IKBKG (0.627, NF-κB essential modulator). HPA data shows ubiquitous expression across all tissues with cell type-enhanced patterns, consistent with broad innate immune surveillance function. ClinVar documents 58 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including 3-prime UTR variants (c.*130C>A) and chromosomal 9q22.33 copy number alterations, though many variants map to the adjacent NANS gene due to genomic proximity. TRIM14 likely functions as a positive regulator of antiviral defense by recruiting the MAVS signalosome to mitochondrial membranes upon RIG-I/MDA5 detection of viral RNA, facilitating TBK1/IKKε activation and subsequent IRF3/STAT1 phosphorylation. The PRY-SPRY domain may directly bind viral proteins or host restriction factors, while the B-box mediates TRIM14 oligomerization for signal amplification. TRIM14 may also modulate NF-κB activation via IKBKG interaction, coordinating inflammatory cytokine production with antiviral state establishment.
