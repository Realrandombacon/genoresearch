# ZNF221: KRAB-Zinc Finger Transcriptional Repressor with Nuclear Body Localization and Dosage-Sensitive Clinical Variants

## Description
ZNF221 encodes a 617 amino acid Krüppel-associated box (KRAB) zinc finger protein localized to chromosome 19q13.31 (NC_000019.10:43951227-43981612). InterPro analysis reveals a canonical domain architecture: an N-terminal KRAB repressor domain (PF01352, IPR001909, residues 30-100) followed by at least six C2H2-type zinc finger DNA-binding domains (PF00096, IPR013087, residues 170-375). The KRAB domain recruits TRIM28/KAP1 corepressor complex to silence target genes via histone H3 lysine 9 trimethylation and heterochromatin formation. STRING interactions confirm functional partnerships: TRIM28 (score 0.522) as the canonical KRAB cofactor, ZNF225 (0.532) suggesting heterodimerization with related zinc fingers, ZNF284 (0.429) and PRDM10 (0.430) indicating collaboration with other chromatin regulators. HPA data shows localization to nuclear bodies (distinct from diffuse nucleoplasm), with expression detected in many tissues (low tissue specificity) but not detected in immune cells, suggesting tissue-restricted regulatory roles. ClinVar documents 6 pathogenic copy number variants including large deletions (19q13.2-13.32) and duplications (19p13.3-q13.43, 19q11-13.33), indicating dosage sensitivity where gene copy number alterations cause disease phenotypes. The combination of KRAB repression domain, extended zinc finger array for specific DNA recognition, TRIM28 interaction, nuclear body localization, and clinical CNVs supports a model where ZNF221 functions as a sequence-specific transcriptional repressor that may form higher-order repressive complexes in nuclear bodies, with haploinsufficiency or overexpression disrupting target gene silencing programs.
