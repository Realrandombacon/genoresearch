## Description
ARRDC2 (UniProt Q8TBH0), also known as Alpha-Arrestin-2 or CLONE24945, encodes a 407 aa cytoplasmic protein functioning as a specialized adaptor in the ubiquitin-proteasome system, likely recruiting specific substrates to NEDD4-family E3 ubiquitin ligases for degradation or trafficking. InterPro analysis reveals a canonical bipartite arrestin fold characteristic of the α-arrestin subfamily: an N-terminal arrestin-like domain (IPR011021/PF00339, residues 21-158) forming the core scaffold; and a C-terminal arrestin-like domain (IPR011022/PF02752/SM01017, residues 180/181-307) completing the two-lobe structure; all embedded within the broader arrestin domain-containing protein family (IPR050357/PTHR11188, residues 5-392) and the immunoglobulin E-set superfamily (IPR014756/G3DSA:2.60.40.640/SSF81296, residues 8-163/165-338) providing structural stability. Unlike visual/beta-arrestins, α-arrestins like ARRDC2 typically lack C-terminal tails for GPCR binding but retain PPxY motifs (not explicitly mapped here but implied by family) for interacting with WW domains of HECT E3 ligases. AlphaFold predicts high confidence structure (pLDDT 81.8) indicating a well-folded adaptor where the two lobes are oriented to create a versatile substrate-binding cleft. STRING interactions define a focused ubiquitination network dominated by HECT E3 ligases: WWP1 (0.679, NEDD4-like E3 ligase), TMA16 (0.490), SYDE2 (0.481), WWP2 (0.476), NEDD4 (0.474), SMURF2 (0.465), RASL11B (0.449), TRIB1 (0.439), HECW2 (0.433), and CNKSR1 (0.432). The strong connectivity to WWP1, WWP2, NEDD4, and SMURF2 strongly supports the hypothesis that ARRDC2 acts as a substrate recruiter for these ligases. HPA classifies ARRDC2 as tissue-enhanced with highest expression in skeletal muscle (145.8 nTPM), detected in all tissues, low cancer specificity, localized to nucleoplasm/cytoplasmic vesicles/plasma membrane, reflecting roles in muscle homeostasis and general protein trafficking. ClinVar lists 8 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants consisting entirely of large copy number gains (19p13.12-12x3, 19p13.3-q13.43x3, 19p13.2-q13.31x3, 19p13.12-q11x3, 19p13.11-q13.11x3) and one loss (19p13.11x1) though no single-gene disorder is definitively assigned suggesting contribution to 19p duplication syndromes or neuromuscular phenotypes. Functional hypothesis: ARRDC2 operates through a conserved α-arrestin mechanism: (1) Adaptor function - Binds specific transmembrane or cytosolic substrates via its arrestin domains; (2) Ubiquitination recruitment - Recruits NEDD4-family E3 ligases (WWP1/2, NEDD4, SMURF2) via PY motifs (hypothesized) to ubiquitinate substrates, targeting them for lysosomal degradation or endocytic trafficking; (3) Muscle plasticity - High skeletal muscle expression suggests regulation of muscle-specific receptors or signaling proteins (e.g., IGF1R, integrins) controlling hypertrophy/atrophy responses to mechanical overload (supported by 2019/2023 studies showing divergent expression with ARRDC3); (4) Cancer prognosis - 2022 studies identify ARRDC2 as a prognostic indicator in ovarian cancer, potentially modulating immune microenvironment by regulating checkpoint proteins or cytokine receptors; (5) Disease mechanism - CNV gains cause overexpression potentially disrupting substrate turnover leading to signaling dysregulation; loss may impair clearance of toxic aggregates or oncogenic receptors; 19p13.11 location places it in a region associated with developmental delays; (6) Conservation - Mouse Arrdc2 (Q8VDI4) indicates preserved function. Dark aspects requiring investigation include: identification of specific protein substrates (distinct from other α-arrestins like ARRDC1/3/4), structural mapping of substrate-binding interfaces, validation of PY motifs and E3 ligase binding kinetics, role in specific muscle diseases (myopathies, sarcopenia), contribution to ovarian cancer progression mechanisms, genotype-phenotype correlations in 19p syndromes, crosstalk with ARRDC3 in muscle stimuli, and potential therapeutic targeting in cancer or muscle wasting.
