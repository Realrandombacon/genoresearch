## Description
MRPL19 encodes a 292-amino acid large ribosomal subunit protein bL19m (UniProt P49406), a well-folded mitochondrial ribosomal protein functioning in mitochondrial translation and oxidative phosphorylation. The protein contains a large ribosomal subunit protein bL19 domain (IPR001857, positions 40-205; PF01245, positions 106-193; PR00061, positions 124-153/154-177) characteristic of mitochondrial ribosome structural components, classified within the ribosomal protein L19 family (PTHR15680, positions 40-205; IPR038657, positions 84-205) with translation protein SH3-like domain superfamily fold (IPR008991, positions 93-226; SSF50104, positions 93-226; G3DSA:2.30.30.790, positions 84-205). AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (pLDDT 83.9, residues 1-292) indicating stable folding consistent with ribosomal protein architecture. STRING interaction analysis reveals MRPL19 forms part of the mitochondrial ribosome network with ultra-high confidence interactions: MRPL17 (0.999, mitochondrial ribosomal protein L17), MRPL33 (0.999, mitochondrial ribosomal protein L33), MRPL13 (0.999, mitochondrial ribosomal protein L13), MRPS9 (0.998, mitochondrial ribosomal protein S9), MRPL32 (0.997, mitochondrial ribosomal protein L32), MRPL27 (0.993, mitochondrial ribosomal protein L27), MRPS10 (0.982, mitochondrial ribosomal protein S10), RPL35 (0.975, cytoplasmic ribosomal protein L35), RPS11 (0.973, cytoplasmic ribosomal protein S11), and MRPL3 (0.971, mitochondrial ribosomal protein L3), confirming its central role in mitochondrial translation machinery. HPA data shows ubiquitous expression across all tissues with low tissue specificity, mitochondrial subcellular localization consistent with mitochondrial ribosome function, annotated for ribonucleoprotein/ribosomal protein molecular functions. ClinVar contains 13 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number losses (2p13.3-12, 2p25.1-q13, 2p13.1-12, 2p13.1-11.2) and gains (2p25.3-q37.3, 2p25.3-q37.3), plus single nucleotide variants (c.*3677G>T, c.658-12A>T), though specific Mendelian disorders remain undefined. With 57 publications, MRPL19 is moderately studied but specific molecular mechanisms remain unclear: lung adenocarcinoma prognosis marker (2023, 7 cit), dyslexia susceptibility locus at 2p12 containing co-regulated MRPL19 and C2ORF3 genes (2007, 132 cit; 2013, 14 cit), diabetic retinopathy association (2023). The functional hypothesis: MRPL19 serves as a structural component of the mitochondrial 39S large ribosomal subunit, essential for mitochondrial protein synthesis and oxidative phosphorylation complex assembly; the bL19 domain mediates rRNA binding and intersubunit bridging during translation elongation; mitochondrial localization and interaction with both mitochondrial (MRPL/MRPS) and cytoplasmic (RPL/RPS) ribosomal proteins suggests potential coordination between nuclear and mitochondrial translation systems; dyslexia association at 2p12 locus implies MRPL19 dosage sensitivity affecting neuronal energy metabolism and synaptic function through impaired mitochondrial translation; cancer prognostic value in lung adenocarcinoma suggests MRPL19 overexpression supports tumor metabolic reprogramming through enhanced oxidative phosphorylation; 2p CNVs may disrupt MRPL19 dosage, causing mitochondrial dysfunction contributing to neurodevelopmental disorders (dyslexia) or metabolic diseases; co-regulation with C2ORF3 suggests shared regulatory mechanisms potentially through bidirectional promoter or common transcription factors.
