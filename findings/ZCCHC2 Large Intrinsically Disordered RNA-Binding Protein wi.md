# ZCCHC2: Large Intrinsically Disordered RNA-Binding Protein with SMAUG-like PHAT Domain Regulating mRNA Stability and Nuclear RNA Processing via CCHC Zinc Finger-Mediated Nucleic Acid Interactions

**Date:** 2026-04-06T13:23:21.739297

**Quality Score:** 2.0/10  (E=3.7, D=0.3) [WEAK]

## Description
ZCCHC2 (Zinc Finger CCHC-Type Containing 2, also C18orf49) is a 1178 amino acid nucleoplasmic, nucleolar, and cytosolic protein (UniProt Q9C0B9) with a predominantly intrinsically disordered conformation (AlphaFold pLDDT 49.0, low confidence), characteristic of large scaffold proteins that function through dynamic protein-protein and protein-RNA interactions rather than stable enzymatic folds.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals ZCCHC2 contains a modular domain architecture: an N-terminal RNA-binding protein vts1-like alpha-helical domain (IPR057327/PF25479, residues 96-140) for RNA recognition, a central SMAUG/ZCCHC2-like PHAT domain (IPR058599/PF26034, residues 145-290) characteristic of the SMAUG family of mRNA stability regulators, a Phox-like/PX domain superfamily (IPR036871/G3DSA:3.30.1520.10, residues 342-468/342-478) conferring phosphatidylinositol binding (GO:0035091) for membrane association, and a C-terminal CCHC-type zinc finger/knuckle (IPR001878/IPR036875/PF00098/PS50158/G3DSA:4.10.60.10, residues 1109-1161/1120-1148/1132-1148) for nucleic acid binding (GO:0003676) and zinc ion binding (GO:0008270). The protein belongs to the Zinc finger CCHC domain-containing protein 2 family (IPR042793, residues 1-1178).\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals ZCCHC2 functions within RNA processing and nuclear export networks: strongest interactions with RELCH (0.690, RING finger and CH zinc finger domain-containing protein), ZNF518A (0.573, zinc finger protein 518A), N4BP1 (0.531, NEDD4-binding protein 1, ubiquitin ligase adaptor), TENT4B (0.491, terminal nucleotidyltransferase 4B, RNA tailing enzyme), MTREX (0.486, mRNA turnover and export factor, RNA exosome cofactor), TENT4A (0.481, terminal nucleotidyltransferase 4A, PAPD5/7), ZNF891 (0.485), NKAPD1 (0.481, NKAP domain-containing protein 1), CYLC2 (0.465, cylicin-2), and ARMC8 (0.454, armadillo repeat-containing protein 8). The TENT4A/TENT4B interactions link ZCCHC2 to RNA 3'-end tailing and stability regulation, while MTREX suggests roles in nuclear RNA surveillance and export.\n\nHPA expression shows low tissue specificity with detection in all tissues and nucleoplasm/nucleoli/nucleoli rim/cytosol subcellular localization, consistent with housekeeping RNA processing functions. The protein is classified as predicted intracellular with cell type enhanced single cell specificity.\n\nClinVar documents 88 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all large chromosome 18q11.1-23 copy number variations (gains and losses spanning 18q11.1-23 to 18q21.2-23 regions). These CNVs suggest dosage sensitivity potentially causing developmental disorders through impaired RNA metabolism and nucleolar function.\n\nConservation is evident from mouse orthologs, indicating functional importance in mammalian RNA processing.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: ZCCHC2 acts as a large intrinsically disordered scaffold protein that coordinates mRNA stability, nuclear RNA surveillance, and nucleolar RNA processing through dynamic interactions with RNA-binding partners and tailing enzymes. The SMAUG-like PHAT domain suggests functional homology to Drosophila SMAUG, which binds specific mRNA sequences (Smaug Recognition Elements) and recruits deadenylase complexes to destabilize target mRNAs. The CCHC zinc finger binds single-stranded RNA or DNA, potentially recognizing specific sequence motifs or secondary structures. The PX domain mediates membrane association at phosphatidylinositol-rich compartments, possibly linking RNA processing to membrane trafficking. TENT4A/TENT4B interactions suggest ZCCHC2 recruits these non-canonical poly(A) polymerases to add heteropolymeric tails (A/G) that stabilize or destabilize specific mRNAs. MTREX interaction links ZCCHC2 to the nuclear exosome for degradation of aberrant RNAs. Nucleolar localization suggests roles in rRNA processing or ribosome biogenesis. The intrinsically disordered nature (pLDDT 49.0) enables ZCCHC2 to function as a hub within biomolecular condensates, facilitating phase-separated RNA processing granules. CNV-mediated haploinsufficiency disrupts RNA homeostasis, causing nucleolar stress and developmental defects.
