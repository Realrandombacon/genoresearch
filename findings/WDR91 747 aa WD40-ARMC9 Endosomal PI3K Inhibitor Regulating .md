## Description
WDR91 (UniProt A4D1P6, 747 aa) is a moderately characterized WD40 repeat protein emerging as a critical regulator of endosomal trafficking, lysosomal function, and autophagy, with 41 ClinVar pathogenic variants lacking defined disease associations. InterPro reveals a bipartite domain architecture: an N-terminal ARMC9/CTLH-like domain (IPR056327, PF23138, aa 47-169) of unknown function, followed by a C-terminal region containing multiple WD40 repeats (IPR001680, PF00400, aa 392-641) forming a β-propeller structure for protein-protein interactions, and YVTN repeat-like domains (G3DSA:2.130.10.10, aa 400-746). The WDR91 family signature (IPR039724, aa 1-745) confers phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase inhibitor activity (GO:0141039), early endosome to late endosome transport (GO:0045022), and negative regulation of PI3K/AKT signaling (GO:0051898). AlphaFold predicts a confident structure (global pLDDT 74.2), with well-folded WD40 β-propeller domains suitable for scaffolding protein complexes. HPA classifies WDR91 as tissue-enhanced with highest expression in choroid plexus (17.5 nTPM), consistent with roles in CNS endosomal trafficking and cerebrospinal fluid homeostasis. STRING interactions reveal a tight functional partnership with WDR81 (0.972, highest confidence), forming the WDR91-WDR81 complex that acts as a Rab7 effector regulating endosome maturation. Additional partners include BECN1 (0.774, autophagy initiator), CCZ1/CCZ1B (0.607/0.604, endosome-lysosome tethering), PIK3C3 (0.558, class III PI3K producing PI3P), CDC42 (0.545, Rho GTPase), and VPS18/MON1A (0.444/0.432, HOPS complex components), placing WDR91 at the intersection of endosomal maturation, autophagy, and PI3P signaling. ClinVar contains 41 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including nonsense mutations (p.Gln334Ter), missense variants (p.Trp125Ser, p.Asn233Ser, p.Thr22Met), splice site mutations (c.1768+1G>A, c.511+1A>G), and large copy number losses (7q31.31-36.1 deletions), but zero specific disease names—indicating clinical significance without established genotype-phenotype correlations. Recent literature (2024-2025) reveals WDR91 as a Rab7 effector that inhibits PI3K activity on endosomes to promote endosome maturation, manipulates endosome-to-cytosol transport during African swine fever virus infection, promotes productive antisense oligonucleotide (ASO) activity in melanoma through endosomal maturation, and forms a complex with WDR81 to modulate phosphoinositide conversion. A 2025 case report describes a previously unrecognized clinical entity caused by WDR91 deficiency. Functional hypothesis: WDR91 functions as an endosomal PI3K brake that binds active Rab7-GTP via its WD40 β-propeller, recruiting WDR81 to inhibit local PIK3C3/VPS34 activity and reduce PI3P levels, thereby triggering the transition from early (PI3P-rich) to late (PI3P-poor) endosomes. This mechanism coordinates endosome maturation with autophagosome formation (via BECN1 interaction) and lysosomal fusion (via CCZ1-MON1-HOPS). The 41 pathogenic variants likely cause neurodevelopmental disorders, ciliopathies, or lysosomal storage diseases through loss of endosomal maturation control. The ARMC9/CTLH-like domain may mediate membrane association or additional protein interactions. The choroid plexus expression suggests roles in cerebrospinal fluid production or blood-CSF barrier function. The high-confidence structure (pLDDT 74.2) and conserved domain architecture (mouse Q7TMQ7 748 aa, rat B2RYI0 747 aa) indicate the WD40-ARMC9 arrangement is essential for coordinating Rab7 binding with PI3K inhibition—a mechanism still being elucidated.
