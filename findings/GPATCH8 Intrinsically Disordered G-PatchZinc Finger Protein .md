# GPATCH8: Intrinsically Disordered G-Patch/Zinc Finger Protein Regulating Pre-rRNA Processing and Spliceosome Assembly via RNPS1-UTP Complex Network

**Quality Score:** 2.75/10  (E=5.2, D=0.3) [WEAK]

## Description
GPATCH8 (G-patch Domain Containing 8, alias GPATC8, KIAA0553) is a 1502 amino acid intracellular protein (UniProt Q9UKJ3) with a predominantly disordered architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 45.7) containing an N-terminal G-patch domain and C2H2 zinc finger, functioning as a flexible scaffold in nuclear speckles that coordinates pre-rRNA processing and spliceosome assembly through interactions with RNA-binding proteins, UTP complex components, and CLK kinases.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals GPATCH8 contains canonical RNA-processing domains: an N-terminal G-patch domain (IPR000467/PF01585/SM00443, residues 38-86) characterized by glycine-rich sequences for nucleic acid binding (GO:0003676), and a C2H2-type zinc finger (IPR013087/IPR036236/SSF57667, residues 136-171) for structured RNA/protein recognition. The protein belongs to the ZNF804A-like/GPATCH8 family (IPR052445/PTHR17614, residues 79-1499) featuring a small beta-sheet domain (IPR060189/PF27597, residues 536-569). GO annotations include nucleic acid binding. The low-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 45.7) confirms extensive intrinsic disorder outside the N-terminal domains, characteristic of large scaffolds requiring conformational flexibility to bridge multiple partners in ribonucleoprotein complexes.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places GPATCH8 at the intersection of splicing and ribosome biogenesis: strongest interactions with RNPS1 (0.681, RNA-binding protein with serine-rich domain 1, spliceosome/exon junction complex), CLK3 (0.657, CDC-like kinase 3, SR protein phosphorylation), KRR1 (0.624, small subunit processome component), SRSF11 (0.544, serine/arginine-rich splicing factor 11), UTP6 (0.540), NOL6 (0.534), UTP3 (0.522), UTP25 (0.522) (all UTP/small subunit processome components), DDX10 (0.520, DEAD-box helicase 10), and CLK2 (0.519). The RNPS1 partnership (0.681) links GPATCH8 to spliceosome dynamics and mRNA export. CLK3/CLK2 interactions (0.657/0.519) suggest regulation via phosphorylation of serine-rich regions. Dense UTP6-NOL6-UTP3-UTP25-KRR1 network (0.522-0.624) confirms direct involvement in 18S rRNA processing and small subunit assembly.\n\nHPA expression shows ubiquitous housekeeping pattern with low tissue specificity and detection in all tissues, consistent with essential roles in fundamental RNA metabolism. Subcellular localization in nuclear speckles (splicing factor compartments) and mitochondria suggests dual roles in nuclear RNA processing and potentially mitochondrial transcript regulation. Classification as plasma and predicted intracellular protein supports dynamic nucleocytoplasmic shuttling.\n\nClinVar documents 11 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large chromosome 17q copy number losses and gains (17q21.31, 17q12-22, 17p13.3-q25.3, 17q21.31-25.3, 17q23.1-25.1, 17q12-21.31). The high CNV burden indicates extreme dosage sensitivity, where haploinsufficiency causes neurodevelopmental disorders through disrupted ribosome biogenesis (ribosomopathy) and splicing defects.\n\nConservation is strong across mammals: human (Q9UKJ3, 1502 aa), mouse (A2A6A1, 1505 aa), indicating evolutionary conservation of large disordered scaffold functions.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: GPATCH8 functions as a flexible molecular hub coordinating early steps of ribosome biogenesis and splicing. The structured N-terminal G-patch domain (residues 38-86) and zinc finger (residues 136-171) provide specific RNA-binding anchors, while the extensive disordered C-terminal region (residues 172-1502, pLDDT 45.7) acts as a entropic spring to recruit multiple partners simultaneously. RNPS1 interaction (0.681) positions GPATCH8 in the exon junction complex or spliceosome, potentially coupling splicing to downstream mRNA fate. CLK3/CLK2 partnerships suggest GPATCH8 is a substrate for kinase-mediated phosphorylation, regulating its assembly into nuclear speckles. The UTP6-NOL6-UTP3-UTP25-KRR1 network confirms integration into the small subunit (SSU) processome, where GPATCH8 likely stabilizes transient pre-rRNA folding intermediates or recruits helicases (DDX10). Nuclear speckle localization concentrates GPATCH8 at sites of active transcription and splicing. Mitochondrial presence hints at conserved roles in organellar RNA metabolism. 17q CNVs cause GPATCH8 haploinsufficiency, leading to defective 18S rRNA production, spliceosome inefficiency, and cellular stress affecting neurodevelopment. GPATCH8 represents a critical intrinsically disordered regulator linking splicing fidelity to ribosome assembly.
