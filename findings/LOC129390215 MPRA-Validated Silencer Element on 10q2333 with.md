# LOC129390215: MPRA-Validated Silencer Element on 10q23.33 with Haplinsufficient Tumor Suppressor Function

## Description
LOC129390215 is a 200bp cis-regulatory element (CRE) located on chromosome 10q23.33 (GRCh38: 94377498-94377698), functionally validated as a transcriptional silencer through STARR-seq massively parallel reporter assays (MPRAs) in K562 erythroleukemia cells. This element lacks canonical promoter, enhancer, or CTCF-bound insulator chromatin signatures but demonstrates robust repressive activity against the super core promoter SCP1. As a non-coding regulatory locus, it has no UniProt accession, protein domains, or STRING interaction partners. ClinVar analysis reveals 3 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number losses disrupting this region: 10q23.33-24.1 deletion (chr10:92643919-95471137, ~2.8Mb), 10q23.33-24.32 deletion (chr10:93181201-101356779, ~8.2Mb), and 10q23.33-24.2 deletion (chr10:92626680-97755102, ~5.1Mb). These recurrent deletions are associated with 10q deletion syndromes featuring developmental delay, intellectual disability, and predisposition to malignancies including glioblastoma and prostate cancer. \n\nHYPOTHESIS: LOC129390215 functions as a critical distal silencer within the 10q23.33 topologically associating domain (TAD), likely regulating expression of neighboring tumor suppressor genes such as PTEN, MXI1, or SFRP1 through long-range chromatin interactions. The silencer recruits co-repressor complexes (e.g., Polycomb Repressive Complex 2, NCoR/SMRT, HDACs) to deposit repressive histone marks (H3K27me3) at target promoters, maintaining basal repression of proto-oncogenes in proliferating cells. Haploinsufficiency via 10q deletions abolishes this repressive function, leading to ectopic overexpression of oncogenic targets and disrupted cell cycle control. The recurrent nature of pathogenic CNVs encompassing this locus suggests strong selective pressure against silencer loss, positioning LOC129390215 as a non-coding tumor suppressor element whose disruption contributes to 10q deletion syndrome phenotypes and cancer predisposition.
