# SLC38A10: Ubiquitous Golgi-Localized Amino Acid Transporter Regulating Bone Development and Intracellular Amino Acid Homeostasis via SLC38A9-MAN1B1 Interaction Network

**Quality Score:** 3.7/10  (E=7.1, D=0.3) [MODERATE]

## Description
SLC38A10 (Solute Carrier Family 38 Member 10, also SNAT10 or PP1744) is a large 1119 amino acid transmembrane protein (UniProt Q9HBR0) with a partially structured conformation (AlphaFold pLDDT 54.2, low-medium confidence), functioning as an L-amino acid transporter localized to the Golgi apparatus where it regulates intracellular amino acid flux, bone development, and potentially mTORC1 signaling.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals SLC38A10 contains a canonical Transmembrane Amino Acid Transporter domain (IPR013057/PF01490, residues 6-382) belonging to the Amino Acid/Polyamine Transporter 2 family (PTHR22950, residues 4-393). This domain typically comprises 8-11 transmembrane helices forming a substrate-binding pocket for neutral amino acids. The unusually large C-terminal region (residues ~400-1119) lacks defined domains in InterPro, suggesting it may act as a long regulatory tail, scaffold, or intrinsically disordered region involved in protein-protein interactions or subcellular targeting. The low global pLDDT score (54.2) supports the presence of significant flexible or disordered regions outside the core transporter domain.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places SLC38A10 within the amino acid sensing and trafficking machinery: strongest interactions with SLC38A9 (0.541, solute carrier family 38 member 9, lysosomal amino acid sensor regulating mTORC1), MAN1B1 (0.462, mannosidase alpha class 1B member 1, ER/Golgi glycoprotein processing), TBC1D22A (0.450, Rab GTPase activating protein), CTAGE8 (0.450, cutaneous T-cell lymphoma-associated antigen 8, ER export), ZNF516 (0.446), PRPSAP2 (0.445), NPEPL1 (0.437), KIF26B (0.428, kinesin family member), NEK6 (0.415), and KIF18A (0.413). The SLC38A9 partnership is critical; while SLC38A9 senses arginine on lysosomes to activate mTORC1, SLC38A10 in the Golgi may supply amino acids to this pool or regulate parallel signaling pathways. MAN1B1 and CTAGE8 links confirm Golgi/ER localization and roles in secretory pathway quality control.\n\nHPA expression shows ubiquitous detection in all tissues with low tissue specificity, consistent with a housekeeping transporter maintaining basal amino acid homeostasis. Subcellular localization is explicitly assigned to the Golgi apparatus, distinguishing it from plasma membrane-localized SNATs. It is classified as a transporter, predicted membrane protein, and plasma protein.\n\nClinVar documents 19 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including one splice site variant (c.501+16G>A) and multiple large copy number gains and losses spanning 17q24-q25. These CNVs suggest dosage sensitivity, potentially causing skeletal dysplasias, intellectual disability, or growth retardation through disrupted amino acid transport and bone development defects.\n\nConservation is evident in mouse (Q5I012, 1090 aa), rat (E9PT23, 1099 aa), and orangutan (Q5RC98, 1121 aa), indicating strong evolutionary pressure to maintain the large protein structure and transporter function across mammals.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: SLC38A10 functions as a Golgi-resident amino acid transporter that imports neutral amino acids (e.g., glutamine, alanine, serine) into the Golgi lumen or exports them to the cytosol, regulating local concentrations for glycosylation enzymes or signaling complexes. Unlike plasma membrane SNATs, its Golgi localization suggests a role in 'intracrine' sensing rather than uptake from blood. The interaction with SLC38A9 implies a coordinated network where SLC38A10 supplies amino acids to the lysosomal/vacuolar system or modulates mTORC1 activity indirectly. The massive C-terminal tail may recruit regulatory kinases (NEK6) or motor proteins (KIF26B, KIF18A) to position the transporter near specific Golgi subdomains or facilitate vesicle trafficking. Disruption of SLC38A10 impairs bone development, possibly by limiting amino acid availability for collagen synthesis or osteoblast differentiation signals. The low AlphaFold confidence in the tail region suggests it acts as a flexible hub for dynamic interactome assembly.
