## Description
LRCH4 (Leucine-Rich Repeats and Calponin Homology Domain Containing 4) is a 683 amino acid intracellular scaffold protein that integrates cytoskeletal organization with innate immune signaling. InterPro analysis reveals a bipartite domain architecture: an N-terminal region containing multiple leucine-rich repeats (LRRs; IPR001611, IPR003591, PF13855, residues 31-258) forming a curved solenoid structure typical for protein-protein interactions, and a C-terminal calponin homology (CH) domain (IPR001715, PF00307, residues 533-649) known for binding actin filaments. AlphaFold structure (AF-O75427-F1) predicts medium confidence (pLDDT 64.2), reflecting the structured LRR and CH domains connected by a flexible linker region, consistent with a dynamic scaffold capable of conformational changes upon ligand binding. HPA data confirms ubiquitous expression with localization to the nucleoplasm, cytosol, and focal adhesion sites, placing LRCH4 at the interface of nuclear signaling and cell-matrix adhesion. STRING interactions highlight a specific functional network: strong association with DOCK8 (0.835), a guanine nucleotide exchange factor regulating immune cell migration; paralogs LRCH3 (0.800) and LRCH1 (0.757), suggesting functional redundancy or complex formation; and connections to adhesion G-protein coupled receptors ADGRL1-3 (0.523-0.548) and synaptic organizers LRRTM3/4 (0.494-0.517), implying roles in cell-cell recognition. ClinVar lists 26 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large deletions on chromosome 7q22.1 and missense mutations (p.Glu372Ala, p.Arg394Gln), though no specific monogenic syndrome is yet defined. Literature (16 papers) identifies LRCH4 as a critical regulator of Toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4) signaling in lipid rafts, where it facilitates the assembly of signaling complexes required for NF-kappaB activation and cytokine production. Recent studies demonstrate LRCH4 promotes colorectal cancer progression by activating YAP and TGF-beta/Smad pathways, remodeling the tumor microenvironment to favor immune evasion. As a dark gene with limited mechanistic characterization despite clear roles in immunity and oncology, LRCH4 represents a specialized scaffold bridging pathogen recognition to cytoskeletal reorganization and transcriptional output.
