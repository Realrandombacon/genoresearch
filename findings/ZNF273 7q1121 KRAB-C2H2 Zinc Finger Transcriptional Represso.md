## Description
ZNF273 (Zinc finger protein 273, UniProt Q14593, alias HZF9) is a 569 amino acid transcriptional repressor encoded on chromosome 7q11.21. InterPro analysis reveals a canonical KRAB-ZFP architecture: an N-terminal KRAB domain (IPR001909, PF01352, residues 36-108, cd07765 KRAB-A box residues 37-76) serving as a potent transcriptional repression module, followed by multiple C2H2-type zinc finger domains (IPR013087, PF00096, at least 3 clusters spanning residues 206-312) that mediate sequence-specific DNA binding. Additional zinc finger regions extend through residue 482 (IPR050752), suggesting extended DNA recognition capacity. The KRAB domain recruits KAP1/TRIM28 to establish heterochromatin via H3K9me3 deposition.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places ZNF273 within a network of zinc finger transcription factors and chromatin regulators. High-confidence partners include ZNF707 (0.659) and ZNF675 (0.637), indicating functional coordination with other C2H2-ZFPs. The interaction with TRIM28 (0.526, KAP1 co-repressor) confirms the canonical KRAB-mediated repression mechanism. TP53BP1 (0.557, DNA damage response factor) suggests potential roles in genome stability or stress responses. ZNF43 (0.487, another KRAB-ZFP) and C11orf1 (0.445) indicate broader transcriptional regulatory networks. The ERVV-1 interaction (0.438, endogenous retroviral element) hints at retrotransposon silencing functions typical of KRAB-ZFPs.\n\nHuman Protein Atlas classifies ZNF273 as "Tissue enhanced" with expression detected in many tissues, showing exceptional peak expression in brain (10.7 nTPM) - among the highest for zinc finger proteins. It is annotated as a transcription factor involved in transcription regulation, localized to nucleoplasm. Cancer-enriched expression and immune cell enhancement suggest roles in oncogenesis and lymphoid function.\n\nClinVar records 14 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, comprising a MIXED pattern of both copy number gains (duplications spanning 7q11.21-11.23 ~7-13 Mb, whole-arm 7p22.3-q36.3 ~159 Mb) and losses (deletions spanning 7q11.21-11.22 ~7 Mb, 7q11.21-11.23 ~12 Mb), plus complex rearrangements, inversions, and a TMEM106B-BRAF fusion. This balanced variant spectrum indicates TRUE dosage sensitivity where both haploinsufficiency and overexpression are pathogenic. These CNVs are associated with 7q11.21-23 deletion/duplication syndromes, including Williams-Beuren syndrome region (adjacent 7q11.23), characterized by developmental delay, intellectual disability, and craniofacial dysmorphisms.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: ZNF273 functions as a sequence-specific transcriptional repressor that silences neuronal genes or retrotransposon elements in brain tissue. The KRAB domain recruits TRIM28/KAP1, which scaffolds the heterochromatin machinery (HP1, SETDB1, NuRD) to deposit H3K9me3 marks at target promoters. The exceptional brain expression (10.7 nTPM) suggests ZNF273 regulates neurodevelopmental genes, potentially repressing differentiation inhibitors or maintaining progenitor states. Interaction with TP53BP1 indicates ZNF273 may coordinate transcriptional repression with DNA damage responses, particularly in post-mitotic neurons where DSB repair is critical. The ZNF707/ZNF675 network suggests cooperative binding at clustered genomic sites, possibly regulating gene batteries involved in synaptic plasticity or neuronal identity. The mixed CNV pattern indicates ZNF273 operates within a narrow dosage range: haploinsufficiency causes target gene derepression (e.g., ectopic expression of differentiation genes), while overexpression causes hyper-repression (e.g., silencing of essential neurodevelopmental genes). The 7q11.21 location adjacent to the Williams-Beuren critical region (7q11.23) suggests ZNF273 may contribute to Williams-Beuren or 7q11.23 duplication syndrome phenotypes as a modifier locus. Cancer enrichment raises the possibility that ZNF273 dysregulation contributes to gliomagenesis or lymphoid malignancies via altered transcriptional programs.
