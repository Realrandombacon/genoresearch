# PSD4: A Sec7-PH Domain Guanine Nucleotide Exchange Factor Activating ARF6 in Membrane Trafficking and Cancer Metastasis

**Quality Score:** 9/10 (EXCELLENT)

**Confidence:** MEDIUM

## Description
PSD4 (Pleckstrin and Sec7 Domain Containing 4, UniProt Q8NDX1), also known as EFA6B or TIC, is a 1,056-amino acid cytosolic guanine nucleotide exchange factor (GEF) that specifically activates ARF6 GTPase to regulate endosomal recycling, plasma membrane ruffling, and cell migration. PSD4 belongs to the EFA6 subfamily of ARF-specific GEFs characterized by a C-terminal catalytic Sec7 domain flanked by regulatory PH domains that mediate membrane recruitment via phosphoinositide binding. InterPro analysis reveals a modular domain architecture: a catalytic Sec7 domain (IPR000904, cd00171, IPR035999, aa 555-738) responsible for GDP-GTP exchange on ARF proteins; multiple PH domains including a spectrin-type PH domain (IPR001605, aa 779-798/847-864/867-885) and canonical PH domain (IPR001849, IPR011993, cd13295, aa 776-894) that bind phosphatidylinositol lipids for membrane targeting; the Sec7 C-terminal domain superfamily (IPR023394, G3DSA:1.10.1000.11, aa 629-748) forms the nucleotide-binding site opener. The AlphaFold predicted structure shows medium confidence (pLDDT 54.8), consistent with a multi-domain protein containing ordered catalytic regions interspersed with flexible linkers and intrinsically disordered segments that may facilitate conformational changes during GEF cycling. STRING interaction data places PSD4 within the ARF signaling network with high confidence associations to: ARF6 (0.808), the primary substrate GTPase regulating endocytic recycling; ARL14 (0.813), an ARF-like GTPase involved in melanosome transport; LDLRAP1 (0.573), LDL receptor adaptor linking to cholesterol metabolism; NUMB (0.463) and NUMBL (0.429), cell fate determinants regulating asymmetric division; AP3M1 (0.454), adaptor protein complex subunit for vesicle trafficking; and ARFRP1 (0.438), ARF-related protein. HPA expression profiles confirm detection across many tissues with low tissue specificity and cell type-enhanced expression patterns, consistent with roles in fundamental membrane trafficking processes; classified as a guanine-nucleotide releasing factor with predicted intracellular localization. ClinVar records document 13 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants predominantly consisting of large copy number losses and gains spanning 2q13-14.3 and broader 2p25.3-q37.3 regions (ranging from 240 kb to whole-arm CNVs), though specific disease phenotypes remain undefined suggesting haploinsufficiency or dosage sensitivity effects. Literature analysis reveals 26 publications with emerging roles in cancer: DDR1 recruits PSD4 to activate ARF6 promoting hepatocellular carcinoma metastasis (41 citations); TNF-α/NF-κB signaling epigenetically represses PSD4 transcription in alcohol-related HCC (10 citations); PSD4 methylation facilitates vasculogenic mimicry in breast cancer brain metastases through ferroptotic resistance (4 citations). We hypothesize that PSD4 functions as a spatially regulated ARF6 activator: upon growth factor stimulation or integrin engagement, PSD4 is recruited to plasma membrane ruffles via PH domain-phosphoinositide interactions; the Sec7 domain catalyzes GDP release from membrane-bound ARF6 allowing GTP binding and conformational activation; active ARF6-GTP promotes endosomal recycling of integrins and growth factor receptors back to the leading edge of migrating cells; this creates positive feedback loops sustaining directional migration and invasion; in cancer contexts, DDR1-mediated PSD4 recruitment hyperactivates ARF6 driving metastatic dissemination; epigenetic silencing of PSD4 in alcohol-related HCC may represent context-dependent tumor suppressor functions; the medium-confidence AlphaFold structure suggests flexible interdomain linkers enabling autoinhibitory conformations relieved by membrane binding or protein partners; loss of PSD4 would impair ARF6-dependent trafficking pathways affecting cell motility, cytokinesis, and receptor recycling.
