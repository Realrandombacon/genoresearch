## Description
S100PBP (S100P-Binding Protein), also known as ASKOPOS, is a 408-amino acid intracellular protein (UniProt Q96BU1) representing a genuine dark gene with only 23 publications despite its critical roles in meiotic recombination, reproductive biology, and cancer suppression. Structurally, S100PBP belongs exclusively to the S100P-binding protein family (IPR026097/PF15427, residues 1-408/22-402) within the broader ASKOPOS family (PTHR14455, residues 1-408), annotated with calcium-dependent protein binding activity (GO:0048306). Unlike well-characterized calcium sensor proteins with EF-hand domains, S100PBP lacks recognizable calcium-binding motifs, suggesting it may bind S100P through a novel interface or require conformational changes for partner recognition. AlphaFold predicts medium-confidence structure (pLDDT 53.8), consistent with a partially structured protein containing ordered domains connected by intrinsically disordered regions—typical of scaffold proteins that undergo conformational flexibility during multi-protein complex assembly. Functionally, S100PBP localizes to nuclear speckles and cytosol with ubiquitous expression across all tissues (HPA: detected in all, low tissue specificity, low cancer specificity, cell type enhanced), classified as a predicted intracellular protein. STRING interactions reveal connections to calcium signaling, RNA processing, and nuclear transport: S100P (0.982, S100 calcium-binding protein P—the primary binding partner regulating proliferation and metastasis), PCED1B (0.616, PC-esterase domain containing 1B), ANGEL2 (0.530, angel homolog 2 involved in mRNA decay), ZG16B (0.517, zymogen granule protein 16-like B), FAM89A (0.478, family with sequence similarity 89 member A), LRIG2 (0.451, leucine-rich repeats and immunoglobulin-like domains protein 2), TCEANC2 (0.436, transcription elongation factor A N-terminal and central domain containing 2), and NUP43 (0.402, nucleoporin 43 kDa—connecting to recent meiosis findings). This interaction network positions S100PBP at the intersection of calcium signaling, nuclear transport, and RNA metabolism. Critically, ClinVar documents 12 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number gains spanning 1p36.33-q44 (chr1:849467-249224684 multiple entries), 1p35.1-33 (chr1:33285582-47891811), 1p36.11-34.2 (chr1:24381206-41401517), copy number losses (chr1:32859415-36454915), yet ZERO diseases are formally associated—a profound gap given the essential meiotic and cancer suppression functions. Recent literature establishes emerging roles: S100PBP interacts with nucleoporin TPR to facilitate XY crossover formation during mouse meiosis ensuring faithful chromosome segregation (2025); lncRNA S100PBP promotes granulosa cell proliferation and steroid hormone synthesis by sponging miR-2285bc-BMPR2 in bovine ovarian follicles (5 citations, 2024); and mutant KRAS drives loss of S100PBP expression enhancing pancreatic cancer progression with tumor suppressor activity (multiple studies 2020-2024, 7 citations on downstream pathways). The mechanistic hypothesis: S100PBP functions as a calcium-regulated scaffold protein that bridges S100P signaling to nuclear processes. During meiosis, S100PBP may recruit TPR nucleoporin to chromosomal sites facilitating homologous recombination machinery assembly for crossover formation—critical for fertility and preventing aneuploidy. In granulosa cells, S100PBP could regulate BMPR2 signaling through miRNA sponging mechanisms affecting follicle development and steroidogenesis. In pancreatic cancer, S100PBP loss downstream of KRAS mutation may解除 growth suppression by altering S100P-mediated metastasis pathways or nuclear transport of tumor suppressors. The NUP43 interaction suggests S100PBP may regulate nucleocytoplasmic trafficking of transcription factors or RNA-processing machinery. The intrinsically disordered regions enable dynamic interactions with multiple partners, while calcium-dependent binding provides signal-responsive regulation. The absence of formal disease associations despite 12 pathogenic variants suggests S100PBP dysfunction contributes to infertility (meiotic defects), ovarian disorders (PCOS, premature ovarian insufficiency), or pancreatic cancer susceptibility not yet mapped to Mendelian phenotypes. Future work should define S100PBP's calcium-binding mechanism, identify chromosomal localization sites during meiosis, investigate whether S100PBP mutations cause infertility or recurrent pregnancy loss, and screen pancreatic cancer patients for germline S100PBP variants.
