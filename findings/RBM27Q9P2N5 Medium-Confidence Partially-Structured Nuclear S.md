## Description
RBM27 (RNA binding motif protein 27, also known as ARRS1, Psc1) is a 1060 amino acid nuclear protein (UniProt Q9P2N5) with medium-confidence partially-structured architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 54.9), characteristic of large RNA-binding proteins with multiple structured domains embedded in disordered regions. The protein contains a canonical RNA recognition motif (RRM) domain (IPR000504/PF00076, residues 599-674/615-667) for RNA binding (GO:0003723), a CCCH-type zinc finger (IPR000571/PF00642, residues 273-301/276-299) for metal ion binding and RNA recognition, and a PWI domain (IPR002483/PF01480, residues 7-74/3-79) characteristic of splicing factors. The PWI domain is specifically found in spliceosomal proteins and mediates protein-protein interactions within the spliceosome. The strong interaction with ZFC3H1 and connections to exosome components (ZC3H18, MTREX) suggest RBM27 functions in coupling splicing to RNA surveillance and decay. The SRPK1 interaction indicates regulation by phosphorylation, common for spliceosomal proteins.\n\nHPA expression reveals ubiquitous detection in all tissues with low tissue specificity, localized to nuclear speckles - the subnuclear compartments where splicing factors concentrate. The protein is classified as a predicted intracellular protein with RNA-binding molecular function. Nuclear speckle localization strongly supports a role in pre-mRNA splicing or mRNA maturation.\n\nClinVar documents 14 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including large copy number losses (5q32 deletions, 5q31.3-32 losses, 5p15.1-q35.2 deletions) and gains (5p15.33-q35.3 duplications, 5q31.3-32 amplifications), as well as intronic variants (c.3100-141G>T). The variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity. Given RBM27's role in RNA processing, dosage imbalance may cause widespread splicing defects affecting multiple genes.\n\nConservation is strong across vertebrates: human (Q9P2N5, 1060 aa), mouse (Q5SFM8, 1060 aa), indicating an ancient and essential function in RNA metabolism.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: RBM27 functions as a nuclear speckle-localized splicing regulator that coordinates pre-mRNA splicing with RNA surveillance. The RRM domain binds specific RNA sequences or structures in pre-mRNA substrates, while the CCCH zinc finger may recognize AU-rich elements or facilitate RNA-protein complex assembly. The PWI domain mediates interactions with spliceosomal components, potentially recruiting RBM27 to active spliceosomes. Through interactions with ZFC3H1 and the nuclear exosome machinery (ZC3H18, MTREX), RBM27 may couple splicing to RNA quality control, targeting aberrant transcripts for degradation. SRPK1-mediated phosphorylation may regulate RBM27's subnuclear localization or RNA-binding affinity. Loss of RBM27 causes splicing defects, accumulation of unspliced pre-mRNA, and impaired RNA surveillance, leading to cellular stress and developmental abnormalities. Dosage sensitivity explains the association with 5q32 CNV syndromes. RBM27 represents a candidate splicing factor and RNA metabolism regulator.
