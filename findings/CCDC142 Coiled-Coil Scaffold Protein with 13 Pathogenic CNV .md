## Description
CCDC142 (Gene ID: 84865, UniProt Q17RM4) encodes a 750 amino acid coiled-coil domain-containing protein mapping to chromosome 2p13.1 (74,472,831-74,482,997 bp on GRCh38). InterPro analysis reveals extensive coiled-coil architecture: (1) IPR026700 - Coiled-coil domain-containing protein 142 family (aa 1-749); (2) IPR055350 - Coiled-coil protein 142 C-terminal domain (aa 309-726, PF14923); (3) IPR056901 - CC142 N-terminal domain (aa 86-283, PF25081); (4) PTHR23436 family membership. HPA expression profile: low tissue specificity, detected ubiquitously across all tissues, classified as predicted intracellular protein with low cancer/immune cell specificity - suggests constitutive housekeeping or structural role. STRING interaction network (10 partners, medium confidence 0.402-0.601) reveals associations with: C2orf81 (0.601, chromosome 2 open reading frame 81, uncharacterized), DQX1 (0.503, DEAH-box helicase, RNA metabolism), TTC31 (0.482, tetratricopeptide repeat protein, protein scaffolding), MRPL53 (0.474, mitochondrial ribosomal protein L53, translation), CCDC192 (0.474, coiled-coil protein 192, cilia/centrosome), M1AP (0.459, meiosis-specific coiled-coil protein, homologous recombination), LBX2 (0.435, ladybird homeobox 2, transcription factor), DDX39A (0.431, DEAD-box helicase, mRNA export/spliceosome), INO80B (0.416, INO80 chromatin remodeling complex subunit), WDR54 (0.402, WD-repeat protein, ubiquitination/assembly factor). Interaction pattern suggests CCDC142 functions in nuclear/chromatin contexts - associations with INO80B (chromatin remodeling), DDX39A/DDX1 (helicases, transcription/RNA processing), and multiple coiled-coil partners indicate role in macromolecular complex assembly. ClinVar contains 13 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants - predominantly large copy number variations affecting 2p region: (1) 2p13.3-12 deletion (71076472-76368354, ~5.3 Mb); (2) 2p13.1-11.2 deletion (74527522-89125488, ~14.6 Mb); (3) 2p25.1-q13 deletion (11504318-111365996, ~99.9 Mb); (4) 2p25.3-q37.3 duplication (1-243199373, entire 2p arm, ~243 Mb); (5) Multiple single allele deletions/duplications. These CNVs cause 2p deletion/duplication syndromes featuring developmental delay, intellectual disability, dysmorphic features, cardiac defects, and growth abnormalities. Critical 2p region genes include: ALK (anaplastic lymphoma kinase, neurodevelopment), ACVR1B (activin receptor, TGF-beta signaling), BCL11A (B-cell lymphoma 11A, hemoglobin switching), PAX3 (paired box 3, neural crest development), and SIX3 (six homeobox 3, forebrain development). Mechanistic hypothesis: CCDC142 functions as coiled-coil scaffold protein that nucleates assembly of multi-protein complexes involved in chromatin remodeling and transcriptional regulation. The extensive coiled-coil domains (spanning ~640 aa of 750 aa total) provide dimerization/oligomerization interfaces for recruiting partner proteins. Interaction with INO80B suggests participation in INO80 chromatin remodeling complex (ATP-dependent nucleosome sliding, DNA double-strand break repair, transcriptional regulation). Coiled-coil mediated heterodimerization with CCDC192 may localize complex to centrosomes/cilia during cell division. Association with M1AP (meiosis-specific) suggests potential role in meiotic chromosome dynamics and homologous recombination. DDX39A/DDX1 helicase interactions implicate CCDC142 in transcription elongation, mRNA splicing, or R-loop resolution. The ubiquituous HPA expression pattern supports constitutive structural/regulatory role across cell types. Pathogenic CNVs disrupting CCDC142 may cause: (1) haploinsufficiency of scaffold protein destabilizing INO80/chromatin remodeling complexes; (2) disrupted coiled-coil mediated protein-protein interactions affecting nuclear architecture; (3) dosage sensitivity altering stoichiometry of multi-protein complex assembly. The 13 ClinVar variants indicate both deletion haploinsufficiency and duplication triplosensitivity mechanisms. This represents a "dark gene" class of coiled-coil scaffold proteins with critical structural roles in nuclear complexes - understudied despite clear clinical significance through chromosomal rearrangements.
