## Description
ZC3H3 (Zinc Finger CCCH-Type Containing 3) is a 948-amino acid nuclear protein belonging to the CCCH-type zinc finger protein family, characterized by multiple C-x8-C-x5-C-x3-H motif arrays that coordinate zinc ions for RNA binding. InterPro analysis reveals three tandem CCCH-type zinc finger domains (IPR000571, residues 667-695/695-722/723-749; PF00642, residues 669-694/776-794; PS50103 profile at 667-695/699-722/723-749) within a larger CCCH-type zinc finger superfamily architecture (IPR036855, residues 669-696/776-798; G3DSA:4.10.1000.10, residues 696-749/750-812). These domains are characteristic of RNA-binding proteins that recognize specific sequence motifs in target RNAs, particularly AU-rich elements or poly(A) tails. AlphaFold predicts a medium-confidence structure (global pLDDT 51.7), consistent with a partially structured protein containing ordered zinc finger domains embedded within intrinsically disordered regions that may facilitate dynamic protein-RNA and protein-protein interactions in ribonucleoprotein complexes.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places ZC3H3 at the heart of the mRNA 3' end processing and nuclear RNA decay machinery with highest confidence for CPSF1 (0.842, cleavage and polyadenylation specificity factor subunit 1, the scaffold of the CPSF complex), followed by FIP1L1 (0.790, FIP1 pre-mRNA 3' end processing factor), NUDT21 (0.763, cleavage factor Im subunit 2/CPSF5), CPSF2 (0.739, cleavage and polyadenylation specificity factor subunit 2), SYMPK (0.738, symplekin, a scaffold for 3' end processing), PCF11 (0.732, cleavage and polyadenylation factor subunit), CSTF1 (0.725, cleavage stimulation factor subunit 1), VIRMA (0.723, KIAA1429, an m6A methyltransferase complex component), CSTF2 (0.723, cleavage stimulation factor subunit 2), and CLP1 (0.708, cleavage factor polyribonucleotide 5'-kinase subunit). This interaction profile is remarkably specific: ZC3H3 physically associates with the core cleavage and polyadenylation machinery (CPSF, CstF, CFI, CFII complexes) and the m6A methylation complex (VIRMA), positioning it as a critical coordinator between mRNA 3' end processing and nuclear RNA surveillance.\n\nHuman Protein Atlas classifies ZC3H3 as an essential protein with low tissue specificity (detected in all tissues), consistent with a fundamental housekeeping role in RNA metabolism. It localizes to the nucleoplasm and is involved in mRNA transport. The single-cell classification as 'cell type enhanced' suggests elevated expression in specific cell types, potentially those with high transcriptional activity or specialized RNA processing demands.\n\nClinVar reports 62 pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number variations spanning 8q24 (coordinates include chr8:126446968-146295771 gain, chr8:142893048-144990940 loss, chr8:111432348-146295771 gain, chr8:113392581-146364022 gain, chr8:141419599-146295771 gain). The 8q24 region is a well-known cancer susceptibility locus containing MYC, and CNVs in this region are associated with various malignancies and developmental disorders. While no specific monogenic disease is named solely for ZC3H3, the pathogenic variant classification and essential protein status suggest ZC3H3 dosage is clinically relevant, potentially contributing to 8q24 duplication/deletion syndrome phenotypes or cancer predisposition.\n\nLiterature analysis (22 papers) confirms ZC3H3 as a partially characterized dark gene with emerging functional insights. The landmark 2020 study (cited 72 times) established that ZC3H3 forms a complex with RBM26 and RBM27 as part of the PAXT (Poly(A) Tail eXosome Targeting) connection, which recruits the nuclear RNA exosome to polyadenylated nuclear RNAs for degradation. This positions ZC3H3 as a critical adaptor linking 3' end processing factors to the RNA decay machinery, ensuring proper turnover of aberrant or surplus nuclear transcripts. Recent work links ZC3H3 to m6A modification biology (VIRMA interaction) and heterochromatin assembly through nuclear condensate formation.\n\nWe hypothesize that ZC3H3 functions as a molecular bridge between mRNA 3' end processing and nuclear RNA surveillance. The CCCH zinc fingers likely recognize specific RNA features (poly(A) tails, AU-rich elements, or m6A-modified sequences) on nuclear transcripts that require degradation. Through its extensive interactions with CPSF, CstF, CFI, and CFII complexes, ZC3H3 may couple 3' end processing quality control to exosome recruitment: improperly processed or surplus pre-mRNAs are flagged by ZC3H3 and delivered to the PAXT-exosome machinery for degradation. The VIRMA interaction suggests ZC3H3 may also participate in m6A-dependent RNA fate decisions, potentially recognizing m6A-modified nuclear RNAs for decay. The intrinsically disordered regions may facilitate phase separation into nuclear condensates (PAXT bodies or nuclear speckles), concentrating RNA processing and decay factors. The 8q24 CNVs may cause dosage-sensitive phenotypes affecting global RNA metabolism, potentially contributing to developmental disorders or cancer through altered transcriptome homeostasis. ZC3H3 represents a candidate regulator of nuclear RNA quality control, a potential biomarker for 8q24 syndromes, and an uncharacterized link between m6A modification and RNA decay.
