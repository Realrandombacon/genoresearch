# ITGA10: Collagen-Binding Integrin Alpha-10 Subunit with Vascular Enrichment and Unmapped Clinical Phenotypes

## Description
ITGA10 (Integrin Subunit Alpha 10) is a 1,167-amino acid type I transmembrane receptor (UniProt O75578) representing a borderline dark gene with 89 publications—sufficient for basic characterization but with significant gaps in disease mechanism understanding. Structurally, ITGA10 contains canonical integrin alpha chain architecture: an N-terminal beta-propeller domain (IPR013519/G3DSA:2.130.10.130, residues 24-644) with 7 FG-GAP repeats (IPR013517, residues 486-584) forming the ligand-binding surface; a von Willebrand factor type A domain (IPR002035/cd01469/G3DSA:3.40.50.410, residues 165-356) containing the metal ion-dependent adhesion site (MIDAS) for collagen binding; multiple integrin domains (G3DSA:2.60.40.1460/1510/1530, residues 645-1108); and a single-pass transmembrane helix (G3DSA:1.20.5.930, residues 1119-1164). AlphaFold predicts high-confidence structure (pLDDT 85.2), consistent with well-folded integrin ectodomains that undergo conformational switching between bent/inactive and extended/active states. Functionally, ITGA10 heterodimerizes exclusively with ITGB1 to form the α10β1 integrin, a collagen receptor specific for triple-helical collagens (types I, II, III). HPA shows tissue-enriched expression highest in blood vessels (114.0 nTPM), cancer-enriched patterns, ER localization, and cell type-enriched specificity—suggesting roles in vascular integrity and tumor-stroma interactions. STRING interactions confirm tight integration within the integrin family: ITGB1 (0.974, obligate partner), ITGB3 (0.969), ITGB2 (0.963), ITGA1 (0.944, collagen/laminin receptor paralog), ITGA2 (0.944, collagen receptor), ITGB5 (0.943), ITGA11 (0.940, collagen receptor paralog), ITGB6/7/8 (0.911-0.927). Critically, ClinVar documents 137 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number gains/losses spanning 1q21-25 (multiple entries), deletions, and single allele events, yet ZERO diseases are formally associated—a remarkable gap given the essential role in collagen adhesion. Recent literature establishes emerging roles: TET3-mediated transcriptional repression via HDAC1/2 recruitment regulates endometrial stromal cell decidualization for embryo implantation (1 citation, 2025); TET2 deficiency aggravates sepsis-induced acute lung injury through ITGA10-PI3K/AKT signaling (5 citations, 2025); serves as diagnostic/prognostic biomarker in thyroid cancer (2 citations, 2024); apoptotic vesicles promote bone regeneration via ITGA10-AKT activation (2026); and HOXC9 drives pancreatic cancer malignancy through ITGA10/FAK/PI3K/CREB-dependent HMGCR activation (2026). The mechanistic hypothesis: ITGA10β1 functions as a mechanosensitive collagen receptor that translates extracellular matrix stiffness into intracellular signals regulating cell migration, proliferation, and differentiation. In vasculature, ITGA10 maintains endothelial/smooth muscle cell adhesion to basement membrane collagens, with loss contributing to vascular fragility or remodeling disorders. In cancer, ITGA10 overexpression activates FAK-SRC-PI3K-AKT signaling cascades promoting invasion, metastasis, and metabolic reprogramming (via HMGCR/cholesterol synthesis). The 1q21 duplication/deletion syndromes may include ITGA10 haploinsufficiency contributing to cardiovascular or skeletal phenotypes. The absence of formal disease nomenclature despite 137 pathogenic variants suggests either embryonic lethality of homozygous loss, phenotypic variability masking Mendelian inheritance, or that ITGA10 variants act as modifiers rather than primary drivers. Future work should screen patients with 1q21 copy number variants for vascular/skeletal phenotypes, define ITGA10-specific collagen binding affinities, and investigate therapeutic targeting in fibrosis and cancer.
