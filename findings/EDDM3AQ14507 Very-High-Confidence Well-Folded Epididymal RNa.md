## Description
EDDM3A (Epididymal secretory protein E3-alpha, also known as HE3A, EP3A, RAM1) is a 147 amino acid small secreted protein (UniProt Q14507) with very-high-confidence well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 91.2), characteristic of the Ribonuclease A superfamily. The protein defines the Ribonuclease A domain (IPR023412/PF00074, residues 31-128), Ribonuclease A-like superfamily (IPR036816/G3DSA:3.10.130.10/SSF54076, residues 25-139), and the Epididymal secretory protein E3-alpha/beta family (IPR042402/PTHR16788, residues 1-147). Despite retaining the catalytic fold of pancreatic RNases, EDDM3A has likely evolved specialized functions in the male reproductive tract, potentially involving RNA processing, antimicrobial defense, or sperm surface modification rather than canonical ribonucleolytic activity. Molecular functions include hydrolase activity and potential RNA binding, placing EDDM3A within the post-testicular sperm maturation pathway.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals a network of epididymal secretory proteins and reproductive factors: strongest interactions with PTGER3 (0.583, Prostaglandin E receptor 3, smooth muscle contraction), CRISP1 (0.536, Cysteine-rich secretory protein 1, sperm-egg fusion), WFDC9 (0.500, WAP four-disulfide core domain protein 9, epididymal secretory), GOLGA8F (0.479, Golgin subfamily A member 8F), PTGER4 (0.476, Prostaglandin E receptor 4), DEFB134 (0.465, Beta-defensin 134, antimicrobial), WFDC11 (0.454, WAP four-disulfide core domain protein 11), PTGER1 (0.448, Prostaglandin E receptor 1), EDDM3B (0.437, Epididymal secretory protein E3-beta, paralog), and RNASE4 (0.429, Ribonuclease 4, angiogenic RNase). This profile confirms EDDM3A as a core component of the epididymal secretome, interacting with proteins involved in sperm motility (CRISP1), antimicrobial defense (DEFB134, WFDCs), and prostaglandin signaling (PTGERs).\n\nHPA expression reveals extreme tissue specificity: exclusively enriched in the epididymis (600.6 nTPM), with no detection in other tissues, cancers, or immune cells. This restricted pattern underscores its specialized role in sperm maturation within the epididymal lumen. The protein is classified as a predicted secreted protein, consistent with its function as a luminal factor.\n\nClinVar documents 28 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, exclusively large copy number gains and losses affecting chromosome 14q11.2 (14q11.2 duplications, 14q11.2-23.1 gains, 14q11.2 deletions). The variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity. While no specific Mendelian disease is annotated, EDDM3A disruption likely causes male infertility due to defective sperm maturation. 14q CNVs are associated with developmental delay and reproductive abnormalities; EDDM3A haploinsufficiency may impair the acquisition of sperm motility or fertilization capacity.\n\nConservation is strong across vertebrates: human (Q14507, 147 aa), chimpanzee (A0A663D6B5, 147 aa), orangutan (H2NKI4, 147 aa), indicating evolutionary pressure to maintain the RNase-like fold in the reproductive context.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: EDDM3A functions as a secreted epididymal protein that modifies sperm surface properties during transit through the epididymis. While structurally homologous to RNase A, it may have lost catalytic activity or gained new substrate specificities (e.g., binding to sperm surface glycans or RNA remnants). Interaction with CRISP1 suggests a role in preparing sperm for egg recognition. Interaction with WFDC9 and DEFB134 indicates participation in the antimicrobial shield protecting sperm in the reproductive tract. Paralog interaction with EDDM3B suggests functional redundancy or heterodimer formation. Loss of EDDM3A prevents proper sperm maturation, leading to asthenozoospermia (lack of motility) or inability to fertilize. Overexpression may disrupt the delicate balance of epididymal secretions. EDDM3A represents a potential biomarker for male infertility and a target for contraceptive development.
