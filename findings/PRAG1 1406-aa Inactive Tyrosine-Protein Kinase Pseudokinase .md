## Description
PRAG1 (UniProt Q86YV5), or proline-rich antigen 1 (also known as inactive tyrosine-protein kinase PRAG1), encodes a massive 1406-amino acid cytoplasmic protein belonging to the mitochondrial quality control and scaffold kinases family (IPR051511/PTHR22972, aa 14-1405). Despite possessing a canonical protein kinase domain fold (IPR000719/IPR001245/IPR011009/PF07714/PS50011, aa 978-1329) with a conserved tyrosine kinase active site signature (IPR008266/PS00109, aa 1141-1153), PRAG1 is catalytically inactive (pseudokinase) due to critical substitutions in the ATP-binding pocket and catalytic loop, repurposing the domain strictly for scaffolding. AlphaFold predicts medium-confidence structure (global pLDDT: 50.2), indicating a flexible architecture with a structured C-terminal kinase-like core embedded in extensive disordered N-terminal regions—consistent with large scaffold proteins that nucleate multi-protein signaling complexes.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals PRAG1's specific integration into Rho GTPase and cytoskeletal regulation networks: RND2 (0.810, Rho family GTPase), PEAK3 (0.531, pseudokinase family member), MAML1 (0.496, Notch co-activator), RHOA (0.476, central cytoskeletal regulator), SHROOM3 (0.433, actin organizer), MTMR9 (0.426, myotubularin phosphatase), EPHA10 (0.418, receptor tyrosine kinase), S100A8 (0.414, calcium-binding inflammatory protein), ERBIN (0.412, ErbB2 interceptor), and CSK (0.410, C-terminal Src kinase). This partner profile positions PRAG1 as a convergence point for Rho signaling, Notch pathway modulation, and Src family kinase regulation.\n\nWith very limited literature (<10 papers directly characterizing PRAG1), it qualifies as a genuine dark gene with emerging roles in development and cancer. Recent insights suggest: (1) PRAG1 acts as a competitive inhibitor or allosteric modulator of active kinases like CSK by sequestering them in inactive complexes; (2) the RND2/RHOA interaction implies a role in controlling cell migration and invasion, potentially suppressing metastasis in epithelial cancers; (3) as a member of the PEAK family (alongside PEAK1 and PEAK3), PRAG1 likely regulates mitochondrial dynamics and apoptosis independent of kinase activity; (4) high density of chromosomal 8p23.1 copy number losses/gains in ClinVar suggests haploinsufficiency drives developmental phenotypes. HPA data is unavailable, reflecting its low abundance or tissue-restricted expression. ClinVar lists 55 pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number losses/gains at 8p23.1-23.3, confirming extreme dosage sensitivity and potential role in 8p23.1 deletion syndrome phenotypes (congenital heart defects, diaphragmatic hernia). We hypothesize PRAG1 functions as a 'molecular sink' or decoy scaffold: the pseudokinase domain binds ATP-competitive inhibitors or substrate proteins without phosphorylating them, thereby dampening excessive Rho/Src signaling bursts during cell migration; the disordered N-terminus recruits RND2 and MAML1 to spatially restrict Notch and cytoskeletal signals at the leading edge of migrating cells; loss of PRAG1 leads to hyperactive RHOA-driven contractility and uncontrolled migration (metastasis) or failed morphogenesis (heart/lung defects), explaining the 8p23.1 CNV phenotype.
