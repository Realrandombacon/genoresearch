## Description
ANKRD28 (Ankyrin Repeat Domain 28, UniProt O15084, Gene ID: 23243), also known as PPP6R3 or CFAP79, is a large 1053 amino acid protein encoded on chromosome 3p25.1. InterPro analysis reveals a massive scaffold architecture dominated by ankyrin repeats, with multiple domains spanning the N-terminal two-thirds of the protein (IPR002110, IPR036770, PF00023, PF12796). Specifically, at least 10 distinct ankyrin repeat units are predicted between residues 7 and 466, forming a rigid, curved solenoid structure ideal for protein-protein interactions. The C-terminal region contains a PTHR24173 family domain (residues 301-492 overlap), suggesting potential homology to Cul2-RING E3 ligase substrate recognition factors, though its primary characterized role is as a regulatory subunit.\n\nSTRING interaction network confirms ANKRD28 as a core component of the Protein Phosphatase 6 (PP6) holoenzyme, showing extremely high-confidence interactions with the catalytic subunit PPP6C (score 0.991) and other regulatory subunits PPP6R1 (0.982) and PPP6R2 (0.935). Additional partners include DOCK1 (0.815, guanine nucleotide exchange factor), USP10 (0.752, deubiquitinase), and ANKRD44/ANKRD52, indicating a role in larger signaling complexes regulating cytoskeletal dynamics and cell cycle progression. The strong link to PP6 places ANKRD28 centrally in the regulation of NF-kappaB signaling, DNA damage response, and mitotic spindle assembly.\n\nHuman Protein Atlas classifies ANKRD28 as having low tissue specificity but notes it is "Immune cell enriched" and "Cell type enhanced," with ubiquitous detection across tissues. Subcellular localization is predicted in the cytosol and nucleoplasm. AlphaFold structure prediction (AF-O15084-F1) yields a global pLDDT score of 88.1 (High Confidence), modeling the full 1-1053 aa sequence. The structure likely displays the characteristic stacked ankyrin repeats forming an elongated platform for docking the PP6 catalytic core to specific substrates.\n\nClinVar analysis identifies 26 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, comprising both large-scale copy number gains spanning 3p26-14 (up to 55Mb) and specific intronic/splice site single nucleotide variants (e.g., c.997-11T>C, c.1273+6T>C). While no specific monogenic disease is yet definitively assigned solely to ANKRD28 point mutations, its inclusion in large 3p deletion/duplication syndromes (associated with intellectual disability and growth retardation) suggests haploinsufficiency or dosage sensitivity contributes to the phenotype. The presence of splice variants implies that precise regulation of isoform expression is critical.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: ANKRD28 functions as a critical specificity determinant for the PP6 holoenzyme, utilizing its extensive ankyrin repeat scaffold to recruit the phosphatase to distinct substrates involved in immune cell activation and mitotic fidelity. The immune cell enrichment suggests a specialized role in regulating NF-kappaB dephosphorylation or T-cell receptor signaling thresholds. Mechanistically, the high-confidence structure indicates a stable platform that may compete with other regulatory subunits (PPP6R1/2) for binding to PPP6C, thereby acting as a molecular switch that directs PP6 activity towards specific targets like Ikk complexes or Aurora A kinase during cell division. Disruption of ANKRD28 (via 3p CNVs or splice mutations) likely leads to hyperphosphorylation of these targets, resulting in unchecked inflammation, genomic instability, or mitotic errors, contributing to the developmental defects observed in 3p syndromes.
