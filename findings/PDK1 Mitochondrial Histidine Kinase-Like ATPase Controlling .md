# PDK1: Mitochondrial Histidine Kinase-Like ATPase Controlling Pyruvate Dehydrogenase Complex Activity and Warburg Metabolism in Cancer and Fibrosis

## Description
PDK1 (436 aa, UniProt Q15118), pyruvate dehydrogenase kinase isozyme 1, is a mitochondrial serine/threonine kinase that phosphorylates and inactivates the pyruvate dehydrogenase complex (PDC) to regulate the metabolic switch between glycolysis and oxidative phosphorylation. The protein architecture features an N-terminal domain (G3DSA:1.20.140.20/IPR018955, aa56-220) for substrate recognition and dimerization, connected to a C-terminal histidine kinase-like ATPase domain (IPR003594/IPR005467/PF02518, aa268-393) containing the catalytic core with ATP-binding pocket characteristic of the GHKL ATPase superfamily (IPR036890). The full PDK/BCKDK protein kinase family signature spans aa41-402 (IPR039028). HPA confirms ubiquitous expression across all tissues with low tissue specificity, consistent with fundamental metabolic regulation, and classification as an FDA-approved drug target reflecting therapeutic relevance. Mechanistically, PDK1 forms ultra-high-confidence interactions with PDC components: PDHB pyruvate dehydrogenase E1 beta subunit (STRING 0.961), PDHA1 E1 alpha subunit (0.712), PDHX E3 binding protein (0.544), and DLAT E2 component (0.543), positioning it as the primary negative regulator of acetyl-CoA production from pyruvate. PDK1 also interacts with its isoforms PDK2 (0.961) and PDK3 (0.833) suggesting heterodimerization possibilities, while connections to LDHA (0.851) link it to lactate production, AKT1 (0.848) embeds it in PI3K signaling, and HIF1A (0.782) places it under hypoxic control. AlphaFold prediction (pLDDT 85.9) reveals high-confidence bilobal architecture with well-defined N-terminal regulatory domain and C-terminal catalytic domain connected by a short linker, consistent with autoinhibited conformation recently described in 2024 literature. Clinical significance includes 23 pathogenic/likely pathogenic ClinVar variants predominantly consisting of large copy number alterations at 2q31.1-32.2 (chr2:171436894-189531954 deletions, chr2:162692199-174452488 losses, chr2:169829974-215521436 gains), though no specific monogenic syndrome has been definitively established. Conservation across mammals (mouse Q8BFP9 434 aa, rat Q63065 434 aa) indicates essential metabolic function maintained over ~90 million years. Literature (4019 papers) establishes PDK1 as a central cancer metabolism regulator: 2025 studies describe PDK1-degrading PROTACs that reverse Warburg effect and induce immunogenic cell death in breast cancer; 2024 work reveals FTO/YTHDF3/m6A axis activates PDK1 to upregulate PD-L1 and promote immunosuppression under hypoxia; Smurf1-mediated PDK1 degradation blocks AKT signaling in KRAS-mutant colorectal cancer; Akt2-PDK1 signaling drives lactate accumulation in pulmonary fibrosis; structural studies captured autoinhibited PDK1 conformations informing novel inhibitor design. The functional hypothesis posits that PDK1 acts as a 'metabolic checkpoint kinase': under normoxic conditions, basal PDK1 activity maintains partial PDC inhibition balancing glycolytic and oxidative metabolism; during hypoxia or oncogenic transformation, HIF1α transcriptionally upregulates PDK1 while AKT-mediated phosphorylation enhances its catalytic activity, causing hyperphosphorylation of PDH E1α subunit at Ser293/Ser300 sites that blocks pyruvate entry into TCA cycle; this forces aerobic glycolysis (Warburg effect) generating lactate via LDHA while accumulating biosynthetic intermediates for rapid proliferation; in fibrosis, chronic Akt2-PDK1 activation in fibroblasts promotes myofibroblast differentiation through metabolic reprogramming; therapeutic strategies targeting PDK1 include dichloroacetate (DCA, FDA-approved), novel ATP-competitive inhibitors, and emerging PROTAC degraders that restore oxidative metabolism and sensitize tumors to immunotherapy.
