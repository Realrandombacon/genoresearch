## Description
ANKRD54 (Gene ID: 129138, UniProt: Q6NXT1) encodes a 299-amino acid ankyrin repeat-containing protein localized to the nucleoplasm, microtubules, and midbody. InterPro analysis reveals 3 canonical ankyrin repeat arrays (IPR002110, PF00023, positions 109-244) forming a curved solenoid scaffold (CATH G3DSA:1.25.40.20) typical of protein-protein interaction hubs. This structural architecture enables ANKRD54 to function as a molecular scaffold coordinating signaling complexes.\n\nSTRING interaction network (10 partners, confidence 0.510-0.942) reveals ANKRD54 connects to: (1) Src-family tyrosine kinases LYN (0.548), TXK (0.525), BLK (0.516), BTK (0.510) - all hematopoietic signaling kinases; (2) cytoskeletal regulators LIMA1 (0.546), DBNL (0.527), LASP1 (0.552); (3) metabolic enzyme LIAS (0.942, highest confidence); (4) membrane trafficking protein SNX33 (0.591); (5) tubby-family protein TULP3 (0.834). This interaction profile positions ANKRD54 at the intersection of kinase signaling and cytoskeletal dynamics.\n\nHPA data shows ubiquitous RNA expression with cell type-enhanced specificity, consistent with housekeeping scaffold function. Subcellular localization to the midbody is particularly notable - the midbody is a transient structure formed during cytokinesis where abscission occurs. Ankyrin proteins at the midbody typically regulate abscission timing and fidelity.\n\nClinVar documents 27 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including 10 copy number variants (deletions and duplications spanning 22q13.1), indicating strong dosage sensitivity. The 22q13.1 region is associated with neurodevelopmental phenotypes when disrupted. The combination of midbody localization, kinase interactions, and dosage sensitivity suggests ANKRD54 regulates cell division fidelity in hematopoietic lineages, with haploinsufficiency potentially causing cytokinesis defects and aneuploidy.\n\nFunctional hypothesis: ANKRD54 acts as a cytokinetic scaffold protein that recruits Src-family kinases (LYN, TXK, BLK, BTK) to the midbody via ankyrin repeat-mediated protein-protein interactions, phosphorylating abscission machinery components to ensure proper daughter cell separation. Dosage reduction impairs this checkpoint, leading to binucleation and genomic instability.
