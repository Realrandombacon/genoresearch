## Description
SWI5 (SWI5 homologous recombination repair protein, also known as SAE3, C9orf119) is a 235 amino acid intracellular protein (UniProt Q1ZZU3) with medium-confidence partially-structured architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 58.4), characteristic of DNA repair proteins that function through protein-protein interactions with both ordered domains and flexible disordered regions for homologous recombination-mediated double-strand break repair. The protein belongs to the DNA repair protein Swi5 homolog family (PTHR28529, residues 59-235), contains a Swi5 DNA repair domain (IPR010760, residues 59-235; PF07061, residues 160-234), and a CATH-Gene3D homologous superfamily domain (G3DSA:1.20.5.170, residues 153-215). SWI5 is located on chromosome 9q34.11 and is predicted to be involved in cellular response to ionizing radiation and double-strand break repair via homologous recombination, located in nucleoplasm (GO:0005654) and cytosol. The protein is classified as a predicted intracellular protein with DNA repair molecular function.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals an exceptionally high-confidence network of homologous recombination repair factors: SFR1 (0.999, SWI5-SFR1 complex subunit SFR1, obligate heterodimer partner), XRCC3 (0.952, X-ray repair cross-complementing protein 3, RAD51 paralog), RAD51D (0.951, RAD51 paralog D, homologous recombination), RAD51C (0.948, RAD51 paralog C, homologous recombination), RAD51B (0.942, RAD51 paralog B, homologous recombination), XRCC2 (0.911, X-ray repair cross-complementing protein 2, RAD51 paralog), RAD51 (0.883, DNA repair protein RAD51 homolog 1, strand exchange protein), SMARCA2 (0.776, SWI/SNF-related matrix-associated actin-dependent regulator of chromatin subfamily A member 2, chromatin remodeler), MND1 (0.486, Meiotic nuclear divisions protein 1 homolog, meiotic recombination), and TMEM208 (0.425, Transmembrane protein 208). These interactions confirm SWI5 functions within the homologous recombination repair machinery, with SFR1 as the near-maximal confidence partner (0.999) reflecting the obligate Swi5-Sfr1 heterodimeric complex formation. RAD51 and RAD51 paralog interactions (XRCC3, RAD51D, RAD51C, RAD51B, XRCC2; 0.952-0.883) link SWI5 to the core strand exchange machinery. SMARCA2 interaction (0.776) suggests chromatin remodeling coordination for DNA repair access.\n\nHPA expression reveals SWI5 has tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in testis (149.3 nTPM), consistent with specialized roles in meiotic recombination and spermatogenesis where homologous recombination is essential for proper chromosome segregation. This extreme testis enrichment (149.3 nTPM) indicates critical functions in germ cell DNA repair during meiosis. Detected in all tissues indicates ubiquitous basal expression for somatic DNA repair. Low cancer specificity indicates expression across cancer types. Low immune cell specificity indicates expression across hematopoietic lineages. Subcellular localization to nucleoplasm and cytosol confirms its role in nuclear DNA repair and potential cytoplasmic functions. The protein is classified as a predicted intracellular protein.\n\nClinVar documents 39 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including extensive copy number losses (9q33.3-34.11 deletions 128.1-133.3 Mb, 9q34.11 deletions 130.4-132.8 Mb) and gains (9q33.2-34.3 amplifications 124.1-141.0 Mb, 9p24.3-q34.3 whole chromosome arm amplifications 0.2-141.0 Mb, 9p24.3-q34.11 amplifications 0.2-131.6 Mb, 9q32-34.11 amplifications 116.4-131.7 Mb, 9q33.3-34.11 amplifications 128.5-132.6 Mb), plus promoter variant (c.-30C>A). The variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity with both haploinsufficiency and overexpression effects. While no specific Mendelian disease is annotated, 9q CNVs are associated with developmental delay, neurological disorders, growth abnormalities, and intellectual disability. Given SWI5's essential role in homologous recombination repair and testis-enriched expression, loss-of-function may cause defective double-strand break repair, impaired meiotic recombination, genomic instability, or disrupted spermatogenesis leading to infertility and cancer predisposition.\n\nConservation is evident across eukaryotes: human (Q1ZZU3, 235 aa), Saccharomyces cerevisiae (P08153 Swi5, 709 aa), Schizosaccharomyces pombe (Q9UUB7 swi5, 85 aa), Mus musculus (Q8K3D3, 89 aa), Salmo salar (B5X601, 138 aa), indicating the Swi5 DNA repair architecture is conserved from yeast to humans with variable protein length, suggesting strong purifying selection on homologous recombination functions despite structural divergence.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: SWI5 functions as a homologous recombination repair factor that forms an obligate heterodimeric complex with SFR1 to promote RAD51-mediated strand exchange during double-strand break repair. The medium-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 58.4) indicates a partially structured protein with a C-terminal Swi5 domain (residues 160-234) providing a structured interaction surface for SFR1 binding and RAD51 recruitment, connected by an N-terminal region (residues 1-159) likely containing intrinsically disordered segments for conformational flexibility and multiple protein interactions. Through SFR1 interaction (0.999), SWI5 forms the Swi5-Sfr1 complex that stimulates RAD51 filament formation and strand exchange activity. XRCC3, RAD51D, RAD51C, RAD51B, and XRCC2 interactions (0.952-0.911) link SWI5 to the RAD51 paralog complex (BCDX2) for RAD51 loading and stabilization. RAD51 interaction (0.883) indicates direct coordination with the core strand exchange protein. SMARCA2 interaction (0.776) suggests chromatin remodeling coordination for DNA repair access. MND1 interaction (0.486) links SWI5 to meiotic recombination. Nucleoplasm localization positions SWI5 at sites of DNA damage. Testis enrichment (149.3 nTPM) indicates specialized functions in meiotic recombination during spermatogenesis. 9q CNVs cause SWI5 haploinsufficiency or overexpression, potentially disrupting homologous recombination and causing genomic instability, cancer predisposition, or male infertility. SWI5 represents a candidate gene for 9q deletion/duplication syndrome phenotypes and may serve as a therapeutic target for homologous recombination-deficient cancers.
