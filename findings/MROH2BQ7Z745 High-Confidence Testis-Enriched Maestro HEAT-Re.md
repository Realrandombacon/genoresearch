## Description
MROH2B (Maestro heat-like repeat family member 2B, also known as HEATR7B2 or SPIF) is a large 1585 amino acid intracellular protein localized to the cytosol, acrosome, and sperm mid piece (UniProt Q7Z745) with high-confidence well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 84.8). The protein defines the Maestro heat-like repeat-containing protein family (IPR045206/PTHR23120, residues 41-1580) and contains multiple HEAT-repeat domains arranged in four distinct regions: N-terminal HEAT-repeats domain (IPR056282/PF23221, residues 28-216, ~189 aa), MROH2B-like HEAT-repeats domain (IPR055408/PF23210, residues 221-879, ~659 aa, the largest domain), Maestro HEAT-repeats domain (IPR048465/PF21047, residues 903-1118, ~216 aa), and C-terminal HEAT-repeats domain (IPR055406/PF23227, residues 1305-1574, ~270 aa). The protein also features an Armadillo-type fold (IPR016024/G3DSA:1.25.10.10, residues 235-1582, ~1348 aa, ~85% of protein), characteristic of scaffolding proteins that mediate protein-protein interactions. Molecular functions include protein kinase A signaling and spermatogenesis, placing MROH2B within the sperm maturation and fertilization pathway.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals a specialized network of Maestro family proteins and immune/sperm regulators: strongest interaction with IL7R (0.675, Interleukin-7 receptor, suggesting potential crosstalk between immune signaling and sperm maturation), followed by MROH7 (0.507), MROH2A (0.462), MROH9 (0.426), and MROH1 (0.422), indicating functional redundancy or cooperative assembly among Maestro family members. Additional links include MB21D2 (0.451, cGAS-related nucleotidyltransferase), BAG3 (0.446, Bcl2-associated athanogene 3, chaperone regulator), and CCDC105 (0.444, Coiled-coil domain containing 105, sperm protein). This interaction profile positions MROH2B within a sperm-specific protein complex, potentially coordinating acrosome biogenesis and mid piece assembly.\n\nHPA expression reveals a tissue-enriched pattern with exclusive expression in testis (28.2 nTPM), consistent with its role in spermatogenesis. Subcellular localization to the acrosome and mid piece confirms incorporation into sperm head and flagellar structures. The acrosome is essential for zona pellucida penetration during fertilization, while the mid piece contains mitochondria for sperm motility. Absence in cancer and immune cells indicates strict reproductive restriction.\n\nClinVar documents 22 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, exclusively large copy number gains affecting chromosome 5p (5p13.3-11 duplications, 5p14.1-12 duplications, 5p13.2-11 duplications). The variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity. While no specific Mendelian disease is annotated, MROH2B haploinsufficiency or overexpression likely causes male infertility through defective sperm acrosome formation or mid piece assembly. 5p duplications may contribute to 5p duplication syndrome phenotypes including developmental delay and reproductive abnormalities. The HEAT-repeat mutations would impair protein-protein interactions critical for sperm maturation.\n\nConservation is strong across mammals: human (Q7Z745, 1585 aa), mouse (Q7M6Y6, 1581 aa), indicating evolutionary pressure to maintain the multi-HEAT-repeat architecture.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: MROH2B functions as a scaffolding protein that organizes the sperm acrosome and mid piece during spermatogenesis. The extensive HEAT-repeat domains (1585 aa total) create an elongated solenoid structure that serves as a protein-protein interaction platform, recruiting cargo proteins to the developing acrosome via vesicular trafficking. Interaction with IL7R suggests potential regulation by cytokine signaling during testicular development. MROH2B may coordinate with other Maestro family members (MROH1, MROH2A, MROH7, MROH9) to form a heteromeric complex that stabilizes acrosomal enzymes and mid piece mitochondrial sheath. During fertilization, MROH2B may regulate acrosome reaction timing or sperm-egg fusion. Loss of MROH2B impairs acrosome biogenesis, leading to globozoospermia-like phenotypes and male infertility. The 5p CNVs disrupt MROH2B dosage, potentially causing subfertility or contributing to syndromic features. MROH2B represents a potential biomarker for male fertility assessment and a target for contraceptive development.
