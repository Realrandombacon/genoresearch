# VSTM4: Cancer-Enriched Immunoglobulin Superfamily V-Set Protein Regulating Endothelial Cell Proliferation and Vasculature Development via LSAMP-TMEM273 Cell Adhesion Network

## Description
VSTM4 (V-Set and Transmembrane Domain Containing 4, also C10orf72) is a 320 amino acid membrane/secreted protein (UniProt Q8IW00) with a well-folded immunoglobulin architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 74.9, high confidence), functioning as a cell surface receptor that regulates endothelial cell proliferation, retina blood vessel maintenance, and vasculature development through V-set domain-mediated cell-cell adhesion and signaling.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals VSTM4 contains a canonical Immunoglobulin V-set domain (IPR013106/PF07686/PS50835/SM00409, residues 34-134) embedded within a larger Immunoglobulin-like domain (IPR007110/IPR003599/IPR036179/G3DSA:2.60.40.10/SSF48726, residues 18-155). The V-set domain adopts a beta-sandwich fold characteristic of immunoglobulin superfamily members, mediating homophilic or heterophilic cell-cell interactions. VSTM4 belongs to the Immunoglobulin Superfamily V-set and Transmembrane Domain-containing Protein family (IPR051102/PTHR12207, residues 12-274). The high-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 74.9) confirms a stable immunoglobulin fold optimized for extracellular ligand binding.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places VSTM4 within the cell adhesion and membrane protein network: strongest interactions with LSAMP (0.518, limbic system-associated membrane protein, IgLON family cell adhesion molecule), TMEM273 (0.479, transmembrane protein 273), LGALS12 (0.476, galectin-12, adipocyte-specific lectin), MSANTD4 (0.435, Myb/SANT-like DNA-binding domain-containing protein 4), AGAP6 (0.430, ArfGAP with GTPase-like domain and ankyrin repeat-containing protein 6), and ANTXRL (0.418, anthrax toxin receptor-like protein). The LSAMP interaction is particularly significant as LSAMP belongs to the IgLON family of GPI-anchored neural cell adhesion molecules, suggesting VSTM4 functions in similar cell-cell recognition pathways. TMEM273 and ANTXRL partnerships indicate VSTM4 operates within transmembrane receptor complexes.\n\nHPA expression shows low tissue specificity with detection in many tissues, indicating ubiquitous basal expression. Cancer-enriched specificity suggests VSTM4 is upregulated in tumors, potentially promoting angiogenesis and tumor vascularization. Subcellular localization includes nucleoplasm and vesicles, suggesting dynamic trafficking between intracellular compartments and the cell surface. The protein is classified as a predicted membrane and secreted protein, consistent with roles in cell-cell communication.\n\nClinVar documents 65 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including one splice site variant (c.457+85G>A) and multiple chromosome 10q11.22-11.23 copy number losses and gains. The high CNV burden indicates dosage sensitivity, with haploinsufficiency or overexpression causing vascular development defects, retinal vasculopathy, or cancer predisposition through disrupted endothelial cell proliferation.\n\nConservation is evident across vertebrates: mouse (T1NXB5, 319 aa, 99% identity), indicating strong evolutionary conservation of vasculature regulation functions.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: VSTM4 functions as a cell surface receptor that mediates endothelial cell-cell adhesion and transduces proliferative signals during angiogenesis. The V-set domain binds homophilically to VSTM4 on adjacent cells or heterophilically to LSAMP family members, triggering intracellular signaling cascades that promote endothelial proliferation and vessel stabilization. LSAMP interaction suggests VSTM4 may function in neural-vascular coupling or blood-retina barrier maintenance. TMEM273 partnership indicates VSTM4 operates within multi-protein receptor complexes. The cancer-enriched expression pattern suggests VSTM4 is co-opted by tumors to promote angiogenesis, providing nutrients for tumor growth. The well-folded structure (pLDDT 74.9) confirms a stable extracellular domain optimized for ligand binding. The splice variant (c.457+85G>A) may disrupt V-set domain folding, impairing cell adhesion. CNV-mediated haploinsufficiency causes retinal vasculopathy and vascular malformations through reduced endothelial proliferation. VSTM4 overexpression in cancers promotes tumor angiogenesis and metastasis. VSTM4 represents a candidate therapeutic target for anti-angiogenic cancer therapy and vascular disorders.
