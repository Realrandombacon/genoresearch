## Description
SMU1 (UniProt Q2TAY7), or WD40 repeat-containing protein SMU1, encodes a 513-amino acid essential spliceosomal factor belonging to the SMU1 family (IPR045184/PTHR13186). The protein architecture features a canonical LisH motif (IPR006594, aa 6-38) and CTLH C-terminal domain (IPR006595, aa 40-92) mediating protein-protein interactions, plus a large C-terminal WD40 repeat β-propeller domain (IPR001680/IPR019775/PF00400, aa 202-512) forming a stable 7-bladed scaffold for spliceosome assembly. AlphaFold predicts high-confidence structure (global pLDDT: 82.9), indicating a well-folded protein with a rigid WD40 β-propeller connected to an N-terminal dimerization domain—consistent with its role as a structural scaffold in the spliceosomal B-complex.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals SMU1's central integration into core spliceosome machinery: IK/RED (0.999, forms obligate heterodimer with SMU1), MFAP1 (0.995, microfibrillar-associated protein 1/spliceosomal component), PRPF8 (0.993, U5 snRNP core protein), SF3B3 (0.989, U2 snRNP subunit), SNRPD3/SNRPF (0.983, Sm core proteins), SNRNP200 (0.982, U5 snRNP helicase), PRPF38A (0.981, pre-mRNA processing factor), LSM8 (0.980, LSm ring component), and ZMAT2 (0.979, zinc finger matrin-type). This partner profile confirms SMU1's role at the heart of spliceosome assembly and activation.\n\nWith only 55 papers, SMU1 qualifies as a genuine dark gene with breakthrough mechanisms emerging in 2024-2025. Recent studies reveal: (1) SMU1-mediated splicing of CAK (cyclin-dependent kinase-activating kinase) genes is essential for embryonic pattern formation and cell specification in Arabidopsis—demonstrating deep conservation of cell cycle regulation via splicing control; (2) SMU1 knockdown suppresses gastric carcinoma growth, migration, and invasion by modulating cell cycle progression, implicating it as a cancer dependency; (3) the RED-SMU1 heterodimer is required for activation of spliceosomal B-complexes assembled on short introns—a mechanistic insight explaining substrate specificity; (4) destabilization of the human RED-SMU1 complex serves as a host-directed anti-influenza strategy, linking spliceosome function to viral replication control. HPA data confirms ubiquitous expression (detected in all tissues, low tissue specificity) with vesicular localization, classified in mRNA processing, splicing, and host-virus interactions. ClinVar lists 69 pathogenic variants—including chromosome 9p24.3-q21.13 copy number gains and duplications—confirming dosage sensitivity. We hypothesize SMU1 functions as a molecular gatekeeper for short intron splicing: the LisH/CTLH domain mediates RED heterodimerization while the WD40 β-propeller docks onto the U5/U2 snRNP interface during B-complex formation, stabilizing the catalytic core specifically when intron length is limiting; this explains both the embryonic lethality phenotype (CAK gene mis-splicing disrupts cell cycle timing) and cancer dependency (tumor cells require efficient splicing of proliferation genes)—the influenza connection suggests viral RNAs exploit SMU1-dependent short intron processing, making the RED-SMU1 interface a therapeutic target for broad-spectrum antivirals.
