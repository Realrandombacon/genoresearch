# FAM43A: PTB Domain Adapter Protein Localized to Vesicles with Potential Roles in Phosphatase-Mediated Signaling and Membrane Trafficking

## Description
FAM43A (Gene ID: 23305, UniProt Q8N2R8) is a 423 amino acid intracellular protein located on chromosome 3q22.1 (NC_000003.12). InterPro domain analysis reveals a canonical phosphotyrosine-binding (PTB) domain architecture characteristic of adapter proteins involved in signal transduction and membrane trafficking: PTB/PI domain (IPR006020/PF14719/SM00462, positions 65-256), FAM43A/B-specific PTB domain (IPR033930, positions 63-191), and membership in the Adapter and Engulfment Domain-Containing Protein family (IPR011993/PTHR11233, positions 44-354). The PTB domain typically recognizes NPxY motifs in target proteins, mediating protein-protein interactions in signaling cascades. AlphaFold structure prediction (AF-Q8N2R8-F1) yields medium-confidence model (global pLDDT 62.9), indicating FAM43A contains both ordered PTB domains and intrinsically disordered regions—a characteristic common in adapter/scaffold proteins that require conformational flexibility for multiple binding partners. Human Protein Atlas classifies FAM43A as a predicted intracellular protein localized to vesicles; RNA expression is tissue-enhanced with highest levels in lymphoid tissue (47.6 nTPM), detected in many tissues with low cancer specificity, and immune cell enhanced at single-cell resolution. This expression pattern suggests roles in immune cell function and vesicular trafficking. STRING database identifies 10 interaction partners revealing connections to phosphatase signaling and membrane dynamics: PPP2R5B (score 0.610, protein phosphatase 2A regulatory subunit B beta), PPP2R5A (0.591, PP2A regulatory subunit B alpha), LSG1 (0.549, large subunit GTPase 1 involved in ribosome biogenesis), TMEM44 (0.509, transmembrane protein 44), SFT2D1 (0.496, SFT2 domain-containing protein 1, Golgi trafficking), SNX29 (0.479, sorting nexin 29, endosomal trafficking), PPP2R5C (0.465, PP2A regulatory subunit B gamma), XXYLT1 (0.464, xylosyltransferase 1, glycosylation enzyme), ZNF22 (0.441, zinc finger protein 22), and CLASP2 (0.422, cytoplasmic linker-associated protein 2, microtubule plus-end tracking). The PPP2R5A/B/C interactions are particularly significant—PP2A is a major serine/threonine phosphatase regulating cell cycle, apoptosis, and signal transduction. SFT2D1/SNX29 interactions suggest vesicular trafficking roles consistent with HPA vesicle localization. ClinVar lists 38 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all large copy number losses or gains spanning 3q regions (multi-megabase CNVs from ~132-197Mb affecting many genes); no specific monogenic disease is attributed solely to FAM43A, but 3q deletions/duplications cause various developmental syndromes. Conservation is evident across mammals with mouse ortholog Q8BUP8 (Fam43a, 424 aa, ~95% identity), indicating strong evolutionary constraint on PTB domain structure. Functional hypothesis: FAM43A functions as a PTB domain-containing adapter protein that bridges phosphorylated signaling molecules with PP2A phosphatase complexes via PPP2R5 subunit interactions, potentially regulating dephosphorylation of key signaling nodes in immune cells. The vesicle localization and SFT2D1/SNX29 interactions suggest additional roles in Golgi-to-endosome trafficking, possibly coordinating signal transduction with membrane dynamics. PTB domains typically bind NPxY motifs—FAM43A may recognize phosphorylated tyrosine-containing sequences in receptors or adaptors during immune activation. Disease mechanism: Given absence of specific FAM43A point mutations in ClinVar (only large CNVs), this gene is unlikely to cause monogenic disease when disrupted alone; however, FAM43A haploinsufficiency within 3q deletion syndromes may contribute to immunological or developmental phenotypes. Priority experiments include co-IP validation of PPP2R5A/B/C/SFT2D1/SNX29 interactions, phosphoproteomics to identify FAM43A-dependent dephosphorylation targets, immunofluorescence colocalization with vesicle markers (EEA1, Rab proteins), and CRISPR knockout in immune cells to assess effects on signaling pathway activation and vesicle trafficking.
