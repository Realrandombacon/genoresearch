## Description
ZCCHC10 (Zinc Finger CCHC-Type Containing 10) is a 192 amino acid nuclear protein (UniProt Q8TBK6) with a partially structured conformation (AlphaFold pLDDT 62.8, medium confidence), characteristic of zinc finger proteins with ordered DNA/RNA-binding domains connected by flexible linker regions.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals ZCCHC10 contains a canonical CCHC-type zinc finger/knuckle domain (IPR036875/PF13917, residues 38-66/40-76; SSF57756, residues 38-66) conferring nucleic acid binding (GO:0003676) and zinc ion binding (GO:0008270) activities. The protein belongs to the ZCCHC10 family (IPR039715/PTHR13491, residues 10-192). The CCHC zinc finger adopts a retrovirus zinc finger-like fold that typically binds single-stranded nucleic acids, suggesting roles in RNA recognition or DNA binding during transcription.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals ZCCHC10 functions within a transcriptional and RNA processing network: strongest interaction with THAP1 (0.856, high confidence; THAP domain-containing protein 1, a transcription factor regulating endothelial cell proliferation and angiogenesis), followed by ARGLU1 (0.687, arginine and glutamate-rich protein 1, transcriptional coactivator), STAC3 (0.601, SH3 and cysteine-rich domain-containing protein 3), FRA10AC1 (0.585), SLU7 (0.575, pre-mRNA-splicing factor), DHX8 (0.572, DEAH-box helicase 8, RNA helicase), PITX1 (0.570, paired-like homeodomain transcription factor 1), FASN (0.557, fatty acid synthase), PRPF38A (0.548, pre-mRNA processing factor 38A), and RBM39 (0.413, RNA binding motif protein 39). The THAP1 interaction suggests transcriptional regulation roles, while SLU7, DHX8, and PRPF38A interactions indicate involvement in pre-mRNA splicing.\n\nHPA expression shows ubiquitous detection across all tissues with low tissue specificity and nucleoplasm/nucleoli rim subcellular localization, consistent with housekeeping transcriptional and RNA processing functions. The nucleolar rim localization suggests roles in ribosome biogenesis or nucleolar RNA metabolism. The protein is classified as predicted intracellular with cell type enhanced single cell specificity.\n\nClinVar documents 17 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including both single nucleotide variants (c.108-74A>T, c.312-16C>G) and large chromosome 5q23.2-q31.2 copy number variations (deletions and duplications). These CNVs suggest dosage sensitivity potentially causing developmental disorders through impaired transcriptional regulation and RNA processing.\n\nConservation is evident from the ZCCHC family expansion across vertebrates, with CCHC zinc fingers representing an ancient nucleic acid-binding motif.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: ZCCHC10 acts as a nucleolar transcriptional co-regulator that bridges transcription factors with the RNA processing machinery. The CCHC zinc finger binds nascent RNA transcripts or specific DNA sequences to recruit or stabilize transcriptional complexes. The strong THAP1 interaction suggests ZCCHC10 functions as a THAP1 cofactor, potentially modulating THAP1-dependent transcription of genes involved in cell proliferation and angiogenesis. Nucleolar rim localization indicates roles in coordinating transcription with ribosome biogenesis or pre-rRNA processing. Interactions with splicing factors (SLU7, DHX8, PRPF38A) suggest ZCCHC10 may couple transcription elongation with co-transcriptional splicing. The partially structured nature (pLDDT 62.8) indicates intrinsically disordered regions that facilitate dynamic protein-protein interactions within transcriptional condensates. CNV-mediated dosage imbalance disrupts transcriptional networks, contributing to developmental phenotypes through misregulation of THAP1 target genes or impaired RNA processing.
