## Description
EGFLAM encodes a 1017 aa EGF-like, fibronectin type III and laminin G domain-containing protein (UniProt Q63HQ2), also known as Pikachurin, functioning as an extracellular matrix protein essential for photoreceptor ribbon synapse formation, synaptic signal transmission, and retinal circuit assembly. The protein contains modular architecture: two N-terminal fibronectin type III domains (IPR003961/PF00041/cd00063, aa35-136 and aa142-239) mediating protein-protein interactions, one EGF-like domain (IPR000742, aa343-381), two calcium-binding EGF-like domains (IPR001881/cd00054, aa560-602 and aa788-820) enabling calcium-dependent conformational changes, and three C-terminal laminin G domains (IPR001791/cd00110, aa386-564, aa609-788, aa835-1014) mediating glycosaminoglycan binding and dystroglycan recognition. AlphaFold prediction shows high confidence (pLDDT 80.6), indicating a well-folded elongated multidomain structure typical of synaptic ECM organizers. HPA shows tissue-enhanced expression with tongue (113.3 nTPM) as top tissue, cancer-enhanced specificity, and predicted secreted localization consistent with extracellular matrix function. STRING interactions reveal EGFLAM associates with dystroglycan complex and retinal synaptic proteins: DAG1 (0.996, dystroglycan-1), GPR179 (0.725, orphan GPCR in bipolar cells), DMD (0.705, dystrophin), POMT1/POMT2 (0.643-0.648, protein O-mannosyltransferases), CTBP2 (0.629, C-terminal binding protein/RIBEYE), POMGNT1 (0.594, glycosyltransferase), FKTN (0.591, fukutin), TLR3 (0.574, toll-like receptor), and CABP4 (0.400, calcium-binding protein). ClinVar contains 18 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including 5p13.3-11 copy number gains, splice site mutations (c.2875+5G>A), missense variants (p.Ser896Gly), and intronic variants. Literature (48 papers) demonstrates EGFLAM mutations cause congenital stationary night blindness type 1D (CSNB1D) featuring impaired night vision, reduced electroretinogram b-wave, and ON-bipolar cell dysfunction; EGFLAM localizes to photoreceptor synaptic clefts binding dystroglycan on bipolar cell dendrites; EGFLAM deficiency disrupts synaptic ultrastructure causing mislocalization of calcium channels (Cacna1f) and glutamate receptors (mGluR6); EGFLAM exhibits oncogenic activity in glioblastoma promoting cell proliferation, migration, invasion, and poor prognosis; EGFLAM polymorphisms associate with beef marbling in cattle; and EGFLAM serves as immune infiltration biomarker in pan-cancer analysis. EGFLAM functions as a synaptic organizer bridging photoreceptor terminals and bipolar cell dendrites—its laminin G domains bind alpha-dystroglycan on bipolar cells while fibronectin type III domains interact with photoreceptor membrane components, creating trans-synaptic adhesion that aligns presynaptic ribbon release sites with postsynaptic receptor clusters. Calcium-binding EGF domains sense synaptic calcium fluxes, modulating EGFLAM conformation and binding affinity during neurotransmission. CSNB1D pathogenesis reflects synaptic misassembly: EGFLAM mutations disrupt dystroglycan binding or ECM organization, causing synaptic cleft widening, receptor mislocalization, and impaired signal transduction from photoreceptors to ON-bipolar cells. Glioblastoma overexpression suggests EGFLAM promotes tumor invasion through ECM remodeling and integrin-mediated signaling. The pikachurin name reflects its rapid synaptic localization kinetics, essential for precise retinal circuit wiring during development.
