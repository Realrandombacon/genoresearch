## Description
RESP18 (regulated endocrine-specific protein 18) is a 228 amino acid predicted intracellular protein (UniProt Q5W5W9) with low-confidence disordered architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 45.2) containing a unique RESP18 domain family signature including RESP18 domain (IPR029403/PF14948/SM01305, residues 79-155/79-166) forming a ~77-88 aa module (~34-39%) defining the RESP18 family, Regulated endocrine-specific protein 18 family (IPR024833/PTHR17314, residues 43-190) encompassing the central region (~65%), and molecular functions including regulation of prohormone processing, insulin secretion modulation, intracellular localization, tissue enriched expression (brain 46.4 nTPM), detected in some tissues distribution, not detected in cancer/immune cells, cell type enriched single cell type specificity, predicted intracellular proteins classification, functioning as a brain-enriched small low-confidence disordered regulator that modulates neuroendocrine function and insulin homeostasis through interaction with prohormone convertases and insulin signaling components.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals RESP18 defines its own unique protein family (IPR024833, PTHR17314) with no homology to known enzymatic or structural superfamilies outside of this specific group. The core RESP18 domain (residues 79-155) is the defining feature. The lack of other recognizable domains suggests a specialized regulatory role, possibly as a scaffold or inhibitor. The low-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 45.2) indicates the protein is largely intrinsically disordered (IDP), a common feature of regulatory proteins that function via flexible protein-protein interactions, post-translational modification sites, or phase separation, rather than stable enzymatic pockets.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places RESP18 within the neuroendocrine/insulin processing network with medium confidence interactions: strongest interaction with PCSK2 (0.565, proprotein convertase subtilisin/kexin type 2), a key enzyme responsible for cleaving prohormones (including proinsulin and proglucagon) into their active forms. This strongly suggests RESP18 acts as a regulator of PCSK2 activity, substrate availability, or trafficking. Additional interactions include INS (0.422, insulin) and FABP9 (0.413, fatty acid binding protein 9, also known as M-FABP, expressed in muscle/testis but linked here potentially via metabolic contexts). The PCSK2 link is mechanistically critical: by associating with the convertase, RESP18 could fine-tune the rate of hormone maturation in neuroendocrine cells.\n\nHPA expression shows a striking tissue enriched pattern with the brain as the dominant site of expression (46.4 nTPM), significantly higher than any other tissue. This strong brain enrichment, combined with the 'endocrine' name and PCSK2 interaction, points to a specific role in the neuroendocrine system—perhaps in the hypothalamus, pituitary, or pancreatic innervation—where it regulates the production of neuropeptides or hormones controlling metabolism and stress responses.\n\nClinVar documents 29 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, exclusively large copy number gains and losses affecting the 2q31.2-37.3/2q35 region: CNV gains (2q33.3-37.3 chr2:206965837-242783384x3, 2q35-37.3 chr2:216815496-242782258x3, 2p25.3-q37.3 whole arm gain) and CNV losses (2q35-37.3 chr2:219606537-239217703, 2q34-35 chr2:215122019-220397907x1). Disruption of RESP18 levels (haploinsufficiency in deletions, overexpression in duplications) likely perturbs the delicate balance of prohormone processing, leading to altered levels of active peptides (like insulin or neuropeptides), which could manifest as metabolic disorders (diabetes, obesity) or neurodevelopmental phenotypes (given the high brain expression).\n\nConservation is observed in mammals: human (Q5W5W9, 228 aa), mouse (P47939, 175 aa), rat (P47940, 175 aa). While the human protein is longer, the core domain is conserved, suggesting functional preservation of the regulatory mechanism across species.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: RESP18 functions as an intrinsically disordered regulatory hub in neuroendocrine cells, specifically modulating the activity of PCSK2 to control the maturation of peptide hormones. Its disordered nature (pLDDT 45.2) allows it to act as a flexible 'rheostat,' potentially binding to PCSK2 or its substrates (like proinsulin) to inhibit or enhance cleavage efficiency in response to cellular signals. The high brain expression (46.4 nTPM) suggests a primary role in regulating neuropeptide precursors essential for synaptic transmission or neuroendocrine axis control (e.g., CRH, TRH, or insulin-like growth factors in the brain). The interaction with INS supports a direct link to insulin pathway regulation, possibly in pancreatic beta-cells or insulin-sensitive neurons. Dysregulation via 2q35 CNVs disrupts this fine-tuning, causing either insufficient hormone activation (in deletions) or excessive/unregulated processing (in duplications), contributing to the clinical phenotypes of 2q35 syndrome, which often includes developmental delay and metabolic anomalies.
