## Description
KRTAP13-1 (Keratin Associated Protein 13-1, also KAP13.1) is a 172 amino acid intracellular protein (UniProt Q8IUC0) with a predominantly disordered conformation (AlphaFold pLDDT 46.2, low confidence), functioning as a high-sulphur matrix protein that forms extensive disulfide cross-links with hair keratin intermediate filaments, providing mechanical strength and structural integrity to the hair shaft.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals KRTAP13-1 belongs to the Keratin High-Sulphur Matrix Protein family (IPR007659, residues 1-166) and Keratin-Associated Protein PMG Type family (IPR007951/PF05287, residues 1-170). These proteins are characterized by high cysteine content (typically 10-30%) enabling extensive disulfide bond formation with keratin intermediate filaments. The PMG (Progressive Macular Hypomelanosis-associated) classification reflects homology to bacterial PMG proteins. KRTAP13-1 belongs to the KRTAP3-3-related subfamily (PTHR23260, residues 1-166). GO annotations include structural molecule activity (GO:0005198) and keratin filament association (GO:0045095). The low-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 46.2) is expected for KAPs, which are intrinsically disordered proteins that function through flexible protein-protein interactions rather than defined enzymatic folds.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places KRTAP13-1 within the keratin-associated protein network: strongest interactions with KRTAP7-1 (0.927, keratin associated protein 7-1), KRTAP3-1 (0.817), KRTAP20-1 (0.756), KRTAP8-1 (0.720), SCYGR1 (0.696, scyesion grey 1, hair protein), KRTAP26-1 (0.695), KRTAP19-3 (0.691), KRTAP24-1 (0.533), KRTAP20-2 (0.459), and KRTAP6-2 (0.400). This network represents the hair keratin matrix proteome, where multiple KAPs co-assemble with keratin filaments to form the hair shaft cortex and cuticle. The high-confidence interactions (KRTAP7-1 at 0.927) suggest co-expression and co-assembly during hair differentiation.\n\nHPA expression shows skin enrichment (8.9 nTPM in skin 1) with tissue-enriched specificity and single-tissue distribution, confirming KRTAP13-1 as a skin/hair-specific structural protein. Expression occurs in differentiated keratinocytes of the hair follicle, particularly the hair shaft cortex where KAPs cross-link keratin filaments. The protein is classified as a predicted intracellular protein, consistent with cytoplasmic localization in terminally differentiated hair cells.\n\nClinVar documents 55 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, exclusively chromosome 21q11.2-22.3 copy number gains (duplications/triplications). The 21q locus includes the Down syndrome critical region, and KRTAP cluster duplications may contribute to hair/skin phenotypes in trisomy 21. The high CNV burden reflects the repetitive nature of KRTAP gene clusters on chromosome 21, prone to non-allelic homologous recombination.\n\nConservation is limited to mammals, with KRTAP genes evolving rapidly through gene duplication and divergence. KRTAP13-1 orthologs exist in primates and some mammals but show high sequence divergence, reflecting species-specific hair adaptations.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: KRTAP13-1 functions as a matrix protein that embeds within the keratin intermediate filament network of the hair shaft, forming intermolecular disulfide bonds that cross-link adjacent keratin filaments. The high cysteine content enables covalent stabilization of the hair matrix, conferring mechanical resistance to breakage and chemical damage. KRTAP13-1 co-assembles with other KAPs (KRTAP7-1, KRTAP3-1, KRTAP20-1) during hair differentiation, creating a heterogeneous cross-linked matrix. The intrinsically disordered nature (pLDDT 46.2) enables conformational flexibility for multi-site cross-linking. SCYGR1 interaction suggests coordination with other hair-specific proteins. KRTAP gene clusters on chromosome 21 are co-regulated during hair follicle differentiation, with expression peaking in the hair shaft cortex. CNV-mediated duplications may alter KAP stoichiometry, potentially affecting hair texture, strength, or growth patterns. In Down syndrome (trisomy 21), KRTAP overexpression may contribute to characteristic hair phenotypes including sparse, fine hair. KRTAP13-1 represents a well-characterized structural component of the hair proteome, though individual KAP functions remain partially redundant within the large KAP family.
