# ZKSCAN5: A KRAB-SCAN Zinc Finger Transcription Factor Mediating Sequence-Specific Gene Silencing via Chromatin Remodeling Complexes

## Description
ZKSCAN5 (Zinc finger with KRAB and SCAN domains 5, UniProt Q9Y2L8), also known as ZFP95 or ZNF914, is an 839-amino acid transcriptional regulator belonging to the Krüppel-associated box (KRAB) zinc finger protein family—the largest family of transcription factors in vertebrates. InterPro analysis reveals a canonical tripartite domain architecture: an N-terminal SCAN oligomerization domain (IPR003309, cd07936, aa 46-155) that mediates homo- and heterodimerization with other SCAN-containing proteins; a central KRAB-A repression domain (IPR001909, cd07765, aa 218-291) that recruits the KAP1/TRIM28 co-repressor complex; and multiple C-terminal C2H2-type zinc finger DNA-binding domains (IPR013087, IPR036236, aa 346-429 and beyond) that recognize specific genomic sequences. The AlphaFold predicted structure shows medium confidence (pLDDT 58.0), reflecting the characteristic architecture of KRAB-ZFPs with structured domains separated by intrinsically disordered linker regions that provide conformational flexibility for chromatin engagement. STRING interaction data places ZKSCAN5 within the transcriptional silencing network: strongest associations with related zinc finger proteins ZSCAN20 (0.773), ZNF232 (0.705), and ZSCAN21 (0.658)—suggesting formation of heteromeric complexes via SCAN domain interactions; links to mRNA export factors THOC5 (0.634) and THOC3 (0.606) indicate coupling of transcriptional repression to RNA processing; and association with CNOT10 (0.633), a component of the CCR4-NOT deadenylase complex, suggests post-transcriptional regulatory roles. HPA expression profiles show ubiquitous distribution ('Detected in all') with low tissue specificity and subcellular localization to intermediate filaments, consistent with dynamic nucleocytoplasmic shuttling. ClinVar records document 23 pathogenic variants including large deletions at 7q22.1 and frameshift mutations (p.Gln405fs), implying dosage sensitivity and functional importance. We hypothesize that ZKSCAN5 functions as a sequence-specific transcriptional silencer: the SCAN domain mediates oligomerization into higher-order complexes that increase binding avidity and specificity; the KRAB domain recruits KAP1/TRIM28, which scaffolds SETDB1 histone methyltransferase and HP1 proteins to establish heterochromatin via H3K9me3 marks; and the C2H2 zinc fingers recognize specific DNA motifs in target gene promoters or enhancers. This positions ZKSCAN5 as an epigenetic regulator of developmental genes, potentially including imprinted loci or transposable elements. The intermediate filament association suggests coordination with cytoskeletal dynamics during cell division or differentiation. Loss of ZKSCAN5 may derepress target genes involved in cell cycle control or lineage specification, contributing to the phenotypes associated with 7q22.1 deletions.
