## Description
LOC283683 (Gene ID: 283683) is an uncharacterized genetic locus located on chromosome 15q11.2 (NC_000015.10, positions 22757856-22778736, ~21kb region). This locus resides within the well-known 15q11.2 critical region associated with Prader-Willi syndrome, Angelman syndrome, and 15q11.2 microdeletion/duplication syndrome (Burnside-Butler syndrome). Despite having an Entrez Gene ID, LOC283683 lacks protein-level characterization across major databases. No UniProt entry exists, preventing domain analysis via InterPro and structure prediction via AlphaFold. Human Protein Atlas has no expression data for this gene (ENSG00000274253), indicating the gene is either not expressed at detectable levels in surveyed tissues or may not encode a stable protein product. STRING database returns no protein-protein interactions, consistent with absence of a characterized protein. However, ClinVar contains 60 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants - predominantly copy number variations spanning the 15q11.2 region including deletions (chr15:22582283-23102621)x1, duplications (chr15:22582283-23102621)x3, and other CNVs ranging from ~500kb to ~1.3Mb. Two single nucleotide variants are also catalogued (NR_040057.1:n.139-145C>G and n.242-80C>A), with NR_ accession suggesting non-coding RNA annotation. The 15q11.2 region contains imprinted genes and is subject to parent-of-origin effects. Functional hypothesis: LOC283683 likely represents either (1) a long non-coding RNA with regulatory function in the 15q11.2 imprinting control region, where CNVs disrupt chromatin architecture or enhancer-promoter interactions affecting neighboring imprinted genes (SNRPN, NDN, MAGEL2), or (2) a very lowly expressed or brain-restricted protein-coding gene involved in neurodevelopment. The extensive ClinVar evidence strongly suggests functional importance despite lack of molecular characterization. Disease mechanism likely involves dosage sensitivity where deletions cause haploinsufficiency and duplications cause triplosensitivity, disrupting the delicate balance of imprinted gene expression critical for normal neurodevelopment. The 15q11.2 BP1-BP2 microdeletion syndrome phenotype includes developmental delay, speech impairment, and behavioral abnormalities. Priority experiments include transcript identification via RNA-seq across brain tissues, imprinting status determination, conservation analysis across mammals, and CRISPR-based functional assays in neuronal models.
