## Description
ANAPC11 encodes anaphase-promoting complex subunit 11 (APC11), a compact 84 aa catalytic core component (UniProt Q9NYG5) of the multi-subunit anaphase-promoting complex/cyclosome (APC/C) E3 ubiquitin ligase that functions as the essential RING-H2 finger subunit responsible for recruiting E2 ubiquitin-conjugating enzymes and catalyzing the final ubiquitin transfer to substrates controlling cell cycle progression, specifically the metaphase-to-anaphase transition and mitotic exit. InterPro reveals a singular but critical domain architecture: RING finger H2 subclass specific to APC11 (cd16456, residues 20-82) defining the catalytic center, Zinc/RING finger C3HC4 superfamily (G3DSA:3.30.40.10, residues 1-84) providing the canonical cross-brace zinc coordination, RING-type zinc finger (IPR001841/PF00097, residues 34-77) with GO annotations for zinc ion binding (GO:0008270), ubiquitin protein ligase activity (GO:0061630), and cullin family protein binding (GO:0097602), APC11-specific RING-H2 finger domain (IPR024991/PF12861, residues 20-84) distinguishing it from other RING proteins, RING-box E3 Ubiquitin Ligase family (IPR051031/PTHR11210, residues 2-84) confirming functional class, and RING/U-box superfamily (SSF57850, residues 1-83) indicating structural homology to other ubiquitin ligases. AlphaFold predicts very high confidence structure (pLDDT 92.4) indicating a rigid, well-folded small protein with stable zinc-coordinating geometry essential for precise E2 positioning and catalytic efficiency. Functional mechanism: ANAPC11 operates as the catalytic engine of the APC/C holoenzyme: (1) Complex assembly - ANAPC11 binds ANAPC2 (cullin homolog) via N-terminal region forming the catalytic module; (2) E2 recruitment - RING-H2 domain (20-82) specifically recruits E2 enzymes UBE2C (0.991) and UBE2S (0.985) through hydrophobic and electrostatic interfaces positioning the E2~Ub thioester near substrate lysines; (3) Substrate recognition - Activators CDC20 (0.999) or CDH1 bind APC/C recognizing destruction boxes (D-box) or KEN boxes on substrates (cyclin B, securin, PLK1); (4) Ubiquitin transfer - ANAPC11 catalyzes transfer of ubiquitin from E2 to substrate initiating polyubiquitination (K11-linked chains by UBE2S); (5) Proteasomal degradation - Polyubiquitinated substrates degraded by 26S proteasome triggering anaphase onset (securin degradation releases separase) and mitotic exit (cyclin B degradation inactivates CDK1); (6) FOXO3 regulation - Recent 2023 literature identifies ANAPC11-mediated FOXO3 degradation promoting bladder cancer proliferation and metastasis linking cell cycle machinery to transcriptional control; (7) LncRNA regulation - BCAR4 sponges miR-1261 upregulating ANAPC11 in liver cancer (2020). Biological roles include: (a) Cell cycle control - Essential driver of metaphase-anaphase transition; (b) Mitotic exit - Triggers cyclin degradation for CDK1 inactivation; (c) Genomic stability - Prevents premature sister chromatid separation; (d) Cancer progression - Overexpression drives proliferation via FOXO3 suppression; (e) Development - Required for embryonic cell divisions. Disease mechanism: ANAPC11 variants cause disease through: (1) Haploinsufficiency reducing APC/C activity causing mitotic delay, chromosome missegregation, and aneuploidy potentially leading to developmental defects in 17q syndromes; (2) Dosage effects from duplications potentially causing hyperactive APC/C, premature substrate degradation, and genomic instability; (3) 17q syndromes typically include developmental delay and organ defects consistent with disrupted cell cycle control; (4) Cancer progression through elevated ANAPC11 expression enhancing FOXO3 degradation (bladder cancer 2023) or BCAR4-mediated upregulation (liver cancer 2020) promoting uncontrolled proliferation and metastasis; (5) Potential synthetic lethality with other cell cycle defects in tumors. Conservation: Strong evolutionary conservation from yeast (Apc11p) to humans with mouse Q9CPX9 (84 aa), bovine Q3ZCF6 (84 aa), and even Dictyostelium Q54L48 (87 aa) indicating ancient origin of the RING-H2 catalytic mechanism in eukaryotic cell cycle control.
