## Description
POLR2K (RNA Polymerase II Subunit K, also known as RPABC4, UniProt P53803) is a 58 aa essential subunit shared by RNA polymerases I, II, and III that functions in transcription elongation complex stabilization and catalytic activity. The molecular architecture consists of a compact zinc-binding fold (IPR006591/IPR029040/IPR039747, positions 1-58) within the RNA polymerase subunit RPABC4 family (PF03604, positions 17-48; SM00659, positions 15-58; G3DSA:2.20.28.30/SSF63393, positions 14-58) containing conserved cysteine residues for structural zinc coordination. Literature (24 papers) establishes POLR2K roles in: (1) POLR2K loss being lethal - no homozygotes with large deletion encompassing SPAG1 and POLR2K found in primary ciliary dyskinesia patients, suggesting embryonic lethality (2025), (2) POLR2K expression and regulatory webwork in bladder carcinogenesis (2020), (3) pan-cancer analysis revealing RNA polymerase subunits including POLR2K dysregulated in multiple cancer types (2023). HPA confirms ubiquitous expression across all tissues with low tissue specificity, nucleoli fibrillar center localization, and classification as essential protein, consistent with fundamental role in transcription. STRING interactions reveal RNA polymerase complex network: POLR1D (0.999) - RNA polymerase I subunit D paralog; POLR2L (0.999) - RNA polymerase II subunit L shared across Pol I/II/III; POLR2C (0.934) - RNA polymerase II subunit C; POLR2I (0.718) - RNA polymerase II subunit I; POLR2D (0.675) - RNA polymerase II subunit D; POLR2G (0.665) - RNA polymerase II subunit G; POLR2J (0.609) - RNA polymerase II subunit J; POLR3B (0.602) - RNA polymerase III subunit B; POLR1A (0.518) - RNA polymerase I largest subunit; TWISTNB (0.476) - twist neighbor protein. ClinVar contains 38 pathogenic variants consisting of large copy number losses at 8q22.2 (chr8:100138992-100172318)x1 and gains spanning 8p23.3-q24.3, though specific single-nucleotide variant disease associations remain under investigation due to essential gene status. AlphaFold shows high-confidence structure (pLDDT 85.8) indicating well-folded compact domain with stable zinc-binding core, typical of essential RNA polymerase subunits. Conservation: mouse Polr2k (Q63871) is 58 aa, bovine (Q3ZBC0) is 58 aa - highly conserved across vertebrates. Functional hypothesis: POLR2K functions as an essential structural subunit that stabilizes the RNA polymerase II/III elongation complex through zinc-mediated protein-protein interactions with POLR2L and other shared subunits. The mechanistic model proposes: (1) POLR2K binds at the polymerase clamp domain interface via its compact zinc-binding fold, (2) conserved cysteine residues coordinate structural zinc ion maintaining subunit stability, (3) POLR2K-POLR2L heterodimer forms core scaffold for assembly of larger polymerase complex, (4) POLR2K facilitates transcription elongation by stabilizing DNA-RNA hybrid within active site cleft. In bladder cancer, POLR2K dysregulation alters transcriptional programs driving carcinogenesis. The absence of POLR2K homozygous deletions in PCD cohorts indicates embryonic lethality, consistent with essential transcription function. Pathogenic CNVs at 8q22 disrupt POLR2K dosage, potentially causing haploinsufficiency phenotypes. POLR2K represents an understudied essential transcription subunit linking polymerase assembly to developmental viability and cancer.
