## Description
IPO4 (UniProt: Q8TEX9, aliases Importin-4, Importin subunit beta-4) encodes a 1081 amino acid nuclear transport receptor representing a moderately characterized dark gene with 36 publications in Semantic Scholar. Located on chromosome 14q11.2, IPO4 is a member of the importin-beta family of karyopherins mediating nucleocytoplasmic transport. InterPro domain analysis reveals IPO4-specific architecture: Importin-beta N-terminal domain (IPR001494, residues 24-90, GO:0031267 small GTPase binding, GO:0006886 intracellular protein transport), Armadillo-like helical superfamily (IPR011989, residues 3-1079), HEAT type 2 repeats (IPR021133, residues 391-431 and 900-938), TOG domain (IPR034085, residues 339-583), Importin beta family (IPR040122, residues 6-1011, GO:0006606 protein import into nucleus), IPO4/5-like TPR repeats domain (IPR057672, residues 100-249), and Importin subunit beta-1/Transportin-1-like TPR repeats (IPR058584, residues 392-528). The HEAT/Armadillo repeat architecture (residues 3-1079) forms a superhelical structure characteristic of karyopherins, creating a flexible scaffold for cargo binding and RAN-GTPase regulation. AlphaFold structural prediction yields very high confidence (global pLDDT: 90.2), indicating an exceptionally well-folded stable protein with defined tertiary structure suitable for nuclear pore complex transit and cargo recognition. IPO4 exemplifies nuclear transport receptors that couple histone supply to DNA replication demands, with implications for cancer (bladder cancer chemoresistance, glioma) and viral pathogenesis (PCV2 hijacking IPO4 for nuclear entry).
