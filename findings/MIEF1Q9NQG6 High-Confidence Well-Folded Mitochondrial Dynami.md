## Description
MIEF1 (Mitochondrial elongation factor 1, also known as MID51, Mitochondrial dynamics protein MIEF1, OPA14, SMCR7L, D3A) is a 463 amino acid intracellular protein (UniProt Q9NQG6) with high-confidence well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 78.1), characteristic of stable scaffold proteins that function in mitochondrial dynamics regulation. The protein contains a MAB21L/Cyclic GMP-AMP synthase-like receptor domain (IPR024810/SM01265, residues 189-455) that may enable nucleotide binding, belongs to the mitochondrial dynamics protein MID49/MID51 family (IPR045909/PTHR16451, residues 6-461) involved in mitochondrion organization, contains a Mab-21-like HhH/H2TH-like domain (IPR046906/PF20266, residues 359-446) for DNA/RNA binding potential, and a MID51-like C-terminal domain (IPR049097/PF21297, residues 153-345) unique to this protein family. MIEF1 is located on chromosome 22q13.1 and enables identical protein binding activity, mitochondrial large ribosomal subunit binding activity, and purine ribonucleotide binding activity, functioning in mitochondrial large ribosomal subunit assembly, mitochondrion organization, and regulation of mitochondrion organization.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals a high-confidence network of mitochondrial dynamics regulators: DNM1L (0.998, Dynamin-1-like protein/DRP1, mitochondrial fission), MFN1 (0.848, Mitofusin-1, mitochondrial fusion), FIS1 (0.809, Mitochondrial fission 1 protein), MFN2 (0.809, Mitofusin-2), WDR90 (0.734, WD repeat-containing protein 90, centriole/cilia), MFF (0.676, Mitochondrial fission factor, DRP1 receptor), DNM1 (0.644, Dynamin-1), DENR (0.477, Density-regulated protein, translation), OPA1 (0.435, Optic atrophy protein 1, mitochondrial fusion), and TOMM20 (0.405, Translocase of outer mitochondrial membrane 20). These interactions confirm MIEF1 functions as a central regulator of mitochondrial fission/fusion balance, recruiting DRP1 to mitochondrial fission sites.\n\nHPA expression reveals MIEF1 has low tissue specificity and is detected in all tissues, consistent with a housekeeping role in mitochondrial maintenance across cell types. Subcellular localization includes mitochondria and intermediate filaments. The protein is classified as a predicted intracellular and membrane protein. Group-enriched single cell type specificity suggests specialized functions in cells with high mitochondrial demand.\n\nClinVar documents 16 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including copy number gains (22q13.1-13.31, 22q11.1-13.33, 22q12.2-13.33 amplifications) and missense single nucleotide variants (c.436C>T p.Arg146Trp, c.718T>A p.Tyr240Asn). The variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity. While no specific Mendelian disease is annotated, 22q13 deletions cause Phelan-McDermid syndrome featuring developmental delay, intellectual disability, and hypotonia. Given MIEF1's role in mitochondrial dynamics and OPA14 alias, loss-of-function may contribute to optic atrophy and neurodegeneration through impaired mitochondrial quality control.\n\nConservation is strong across vertebrates: human (Q9NQG6, 463 aa), mouse (Q8BGV8, 463 aa), indicating an ancient and essential function in mitochondrial dynamics conserved in mammals.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: MIEF1/MID51 functions as a mitochondrial outer membrane receptor that recruits DRP1 (DNM1L) to initiate mitochondrial fission. The well-folded MAB21L-like domain (pLDDT 78.1) may bind nucleotides (GTP/cGAMP) to regulate DRP1 recruitment in response to metabolic signals. Through interactions with MFN1/MFN2 and OPA1, MIEF1 coordinates fission with fusion to maintain mitochondrial network homeostasis. FIS1 and MFF interactions position MIEF1 within the fission machinery at ER-mitochondria contact sites. The intermediate filament localization suggests MIEF1 may link mitochondrial dynamics to cytoskeletal organization for mitochondrial trafficking. Ubiquitous expression indicates MIEF1 is essential for mitochondrial quality control in all tissues. 22q CNVs and missense variants cause MIEF1 dysfunction, potentially disrupting mitochondrial dynamics and contributing to neurodegeneration, optic atrophy, and Phelan-McDermid syndrome features through impaired mitochondrial fragmentation and mitophagy. MIEF1 represents a candidate gene for primary optic atrophy and mitochondrial diseases.
