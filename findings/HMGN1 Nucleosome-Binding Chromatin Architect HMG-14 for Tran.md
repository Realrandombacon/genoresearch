## Description
HMGN1 encodes a 100 aa high mobility group nucleosome-binding domain-containing protein 1 (UniProt P05114), also known as HMG-14 or non-histone chromosomal protein HMG-14, functioning as an abundant nuclear protein that binds nucleosomal DNA at the entry/exit sites, maintaining open chromatin configuration around transcribable genes and facilitating transcriptional elongation. The protein contains a single HMGN nucleosome-binding domain (IPR000079/PF01101/SM00527, aa2-95) with conserved HMG14/17 signature motif (PS00355, aa19-28) and nucleosomal DNA binding interface (GO:0031492), belonging to the high mobility group nucleosome-binding family (PTHR23087, aa1-90). AlphaFold prediction shows medium confidence (pLDDT 63.2), consistent with intrinsically disordered chromatin-binding proteins that adopt structure upon nucleosome engagement. HPA shows ubiquitous expression across all tissues with low tissue specificity, nucleoplasmic localization, and cell type-enhanced pattern consistent with universal chromatin regulatory function. STRING interactions reveal HMGN1 associates with chromatin remodeling and transcription machinery: FAU (0.988, ubiquitin-like protein), TCEA1 (0.975, transcription elongation factor SII), ribosomal proteins RPS3/RPL5/RPS18/RPS15 (0.941-0.943), HMGN2 (0.940, paralog), ERCC6 (0.933, DNA repair), HMGN5 (0.906, HMGN family), and EP300 (0.420, histone acetyltransferase). ClinVar contains 30 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including multiple 21q11.2-22.3/21q22.11-22.3 copy number gains (trisomy 21 dosage) and losses. Literature (251 papers) demonstrates HMGN1 overexpression in trisomy 21 drives myocardial reprogramming underlying congenital heart defects in Down syndrome; HMGN1 binds acetylated histones and H2A.Z-containing nucleosomes regulating chromatin state and transcription; HMGN1 reduces nucleosome stability facilitating transcription factor access; HMGN1 knockout mice show altered chromatin structure, reduced histone acetylation, and impaired transcriptional activation; HMGN1 serves as plasma biomarker in rheumatoid arthritis; HMGN1 combined with anti-TNFR2 enhances TIL cell therapy in colorectal cancer; and RUNX1::HMGN1 fusion identified in therapy-related acute myeloid leukemia. HMGN1 functions as a chromatin accessibility factor—its basic nucleosome-binding domain interacts with linker DNA and histone H3 tails, competing with histone H1 for nucleosome binding sites, thereby preventing chromatin compaction and maintaining transcriptionally permissive states. HMGN1 recruits histone acetyltransferases (EP300/CBP) and facilitates histone H3K14/K23 acetylation, creating positive feedback for transcriptional activation. Down syndrome cardiac pathogenesis reflects HMGN1 gene dosage: trisomy 21 causes HMGN1 overexpression, altering chromatin architecture in cardiac progenitors, dysregulating cardiac transcriptional programs (NKX2-5, GATA4, TBX5), and impairing myocardial differentiation. Cancer associations suggest HMGN1 modulates tumor suppressor/oncogene expression through chromatin remodeling. The medium-confidence AlphaFold structure reflects HMGN1's intrinsically disordered nature, enabling conformational flexibility for nucleosome engagement and dynamic chromatin interactions.
