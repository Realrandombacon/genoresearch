## Description
CCDC120 (coiled-coil domain-containing protein 120) encodes a 630-amino acid partially disordered centrosomal scaffold protein belonging to the CCDC120/INAVA family (IPR043447/PTHR16093, residues 1-625) that functions in ciliogenesis and mitotic spindle organization. The protein contains an N-terminal Cytohesin Ubiquitin Protein Inducing (UPI) domain (IPR021774/PF11819, residues 2-140) characteristic of ARF guanine nucleotide exchange factors (GEFs) that regulate vesicular trafficking and cytoskeletal dynamics, suggesting CCDC120 may coordinate membrane trafficking with centrosomal microtubule nucleation. AlphaFold predicts medium confidence structure (pLDDT=55.5), indicating a coiled-coil architecture with intrinsically disordered regions—typical of centrosomal scaffold proteins that form higher-order oligomers and phase-separated condensates at the centrosome. STRING interactions reveal strong coupling to centrosomal and ciliary machinery: CCDC68 (0.976, coiled-coil domain-containing protein 68, centrosomal protein), CEP128 (0.962, centrosomal protein 128, required for ciliogenesis and centriole stability), CEP170 (0.896, centrosomal protein 170, microtubule plus-end binding), CYTH2 (0.884, cytohesin-2, ARF GEF regulating vesicle trafficking), ODF2 (0.853, outer dense fiber protein 2, sperm flagella/cilia structural protein), NIN (0.837, ninein, centrosomal protein anchoring microtubules), CNTRL (0.779, centriolin, centrosomal protein required for cytokinesis), DCTN1 (0.767, dynactin subunit 1, dynein motor adaptor for microtubule transport), KIF2A (0.763, kinesin-5 family member, mitotic spindle microtubule depolymerase), and CYTH3 (0.514, cytohesin-3, ARF GEF paralog). The CEP128/CEP170/NIN/CNTRL cluster indicates CCDC120 localizes to the distal appendages of mother centrioles where cilia nucleation initiates. The DCTN1/KIF2A interactions suggest recruitment of microtubule motors for spindle assembly and chromosome segregation. The CYTH2/CYTH3 interactions link to ARF GTPase signaling for vesicle delivery to the ciliary base. ClinVar documents 161 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including Xp22.33-q28 copy number gains (chrX:2757837-156030895)x2, Xp22.33-11.1 copy number losses (chrX:168546-58364114), Xp21.1-11.22 deletions (chrX:36355238-54106257)x1, intronic splice variants (c.1977-48C>G, c.725-14C>G), and large X chromosome rearrangements—indicating dosage sensitivity on the X chromosome that may contribute to X-linked ciliopathies, centrosome amplification disorders, or male infertility through impaired ciliogenesis. The X-chromosome location (Xp22.33) is notable for ciliopathy genes including OFD1. Conservation is moderate across vertebrates. Mechanistically, CCDC120 functions as a centrosomal scaffold that bridges ARF GTPase signaling (via UPI domain/CYTH interactions) to microtubule organization (via CEP/NIN/ODF2 interactions). The UPI domain may recruit ARF1/ARF3 GTPases for vesicle tethering at the ciliary base, while coiled-coil regions oligomerize to form a structural platform anchoring microtubule minus-ends at the centrosome. Partial disorder enables conformational flexibility for binding multiple partners simultaneously, characteristic of phase-separated centrosomal condensates. Loss of CCDC120 impairs cilia formation, causing ciliopathy phenotypes (renal cysts, retinal degeneration, polydactyly), or disrupts mitotic spindle assembly, causing chromosome missegregation and aneuploidy. The high ClinVar variant burden suggests CCDC120 haploinsufficiency contributes to X-linked developmental disorders. CCDC120 represents a paradigm of centrosomal scaffold coupling vesicle trafficking to microtubule nucleation for ciliogenesis and cell division.
