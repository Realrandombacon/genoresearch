# MKS1: A B9 Domain-Containing Scaffold Protein Essential for Ciliary Transition Zone Assembly and Diffusion Barrier Formation

**Date:** 2026-06-26T17:06:09.847990

**Quality Score:** 9/10 (EXCELLENT)

**Confidence:** HIGH

## Description
MKS1 (Meckel Syndrome Type 1 protein, UniProt Q9NXB0), also known as Tectonic-like complex member 1 or BBS13, is a 559-amino acid structural component of the ciliary transition zone (TZ), a specialized diffusion barrier at the base of the primary cilium that regulates protein entry and exit. InterPro analysis reveals a dominant B9-type C2 domain architecture (IPR010796, PF07162, PS51381, aa 46-556) that spans nearly the entire protein length; this domain is characteristic of the 'MKS module' and is distinct from classical calcium-binding C2 domains, instead mediating critical protein-protein interactions within the TZ scaffold. The AlphaFold predicted structure shows confident folding (pLDDT 73.6), consistent with a stable structural role. STRING interaction data places MKS1 at the core of the MKS-JBTS ciliopathy network with near-perfect associations to other TZ components: CC2D2A (0.997), TCTN1 (0.997), TMEM67 (0.997), CEP290 (0.993), B9D1 (0.987), TMEM216 (0.987), TCTN2/TCTN3 (0.979), B9D2 (0.970), and TMEM231 (0.880); these proteins collectively form the 'Y-links' that anchor the ciliary membrane to the axoneme. HPA expression profiles confirm ubiquitous detection across tissues with localization to the basal body, nucleoplasm, and nucleoli, reflecting its fundamental role in ciliogenesis in diverse cell types. ClinVar records document 268 pathogenic variants including nonsense mutations (p.Glu189Ter, p.Ser426Ter), frameshifts (p.Leu454fs), splice defects (c.1095+1G>C), and missense changes (p.Ile125Asn, p.Phe126Leu), causing severe ciliopathies: Meckel syndrome type 1 (lethal multi-organ disorder with cystic kidneys, polydactyly, encephalocele), Joubert syndrome type 28 (cerebellar vermis hypoplasia, ataxia), and Bardet-Biedl syndrome type 13 (obesity, retinal dystrophy, polydactyly). We hypothesize that MKS1 functions as a central architectural hub within the MKS complex: its extensive B9 domain mediates heterodimerization with B9D1/B9D2 and recruitment of TCTN/TMEM proteins to assemble the Y-link structure; this assembly creates a size-exclusion filter and diffusion barrier that compartmentalizes the ciliary membrane, ensuring proper localization of signaling receptors (e.g., Smoothened, PDGFRα) while excluding non-ciliary proteins; loss of MKS1 disrupts barrier integrity, leading to aberrant Hedgehog and Wnt signaling, defective ciliary trafficking, and the pleiotropic developmental defects observed in ciliopathies.
