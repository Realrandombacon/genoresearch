# AGBL5/CCP5: Cytosolic Carboxypeptidase 5 Tubulin Deglutamylase Essential for Ciliogenesis and Mutated in Retinitis Pigmentosa with Hearing Loss

**Quality Score:** 9/10 (EXCELLENT)

**Confidence:** HIGH

## Description
AGBL5 encodes an 886-amino acid cytosolic carboxypeptidase-like protein 5 (UniProt Q8NDL9), also known as CCP5, a zinc-dependent metallocarboxypeptidase that functions as an α-tubulin deglutamylase cleaving the γ-carboxyl-linked branching point of glutamylated tubulin. The protein contains a catalytic M14 peptidase domain (IPR000834, PF00246, positions 157-570) with zinc-binding motifs (IPR034286, positions 183-574) characteristic of carboxypeptidase A family enzymes, plus an N-terminal domain (IPR040626, PF18027, positions 10-141) for substrate recognition, belonging to the cytosolic carboxypeptidase family (IPR050821, positions 5-711). AlphaFold predicts a medium-confidence structure (pLDDT 69.8, residues 1-886) with partially ordered catalytic domains and flexible regions consistent with enzyme conformational dynamics during tubulin binding. STRING interaction analysis reveals AGBL5 associates with tubulin tyrosine ligase-like proteins: TTLL6 (0.702, tubulin polyglutamylase), TTLL4 (0.688), TTLL10 (0.544), TTLL5 (0.477), TTLL1 (0.466), and TTL (0.402, tubulin tyrosine ligase), plus paralog AGBL4 (0.518) and CGAS (0.626), confirming its role in tubulin post-translational modification cycle. HPA data shows testis-enriched expression (180.4 nTPM) with moderate parathyroid gland expression (46.9 nTPM), subcellular localization to microtubules, cytokinetic bridge, primary cilium, centriolar satellite, and basal body, annotated for carboxypeptidase/hydrolase/metalloprotease activity, with disease involvement in retinitis pigmentosa. ClinVar contains 70 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including splice site mutations (c.2242+1G>A), frameshifts (p.Leu247fs, p.Ala107fs), nonsense mutations (p.Arg487Ter, p.Trp592Ter), missense variants (p.Phe449Leu, p.Arg193Trp), and copy number variations at 2p25-23, causing autosomal recessive retinitis pigmentosa with hearing loss. With 28 publications, AGBL5 is partially characterized: recent studies identify AGBL5−/− cell model rescue of ciliogenesis and hyperglutamylation mutant phenotype in retinitis pigmentosa (2025), variants responsible for autosomal recessive retinitis pigmentosa with hearing loss (2024, 2 cit), Iranian family evaluation establishing AGBL5 contribution (2024), mutations associated with retinitis pigmentosa (2023, 4 cit), and novel mutations in presumed unilateral retinitis pigmentosa (2021, 1 cit). The functional hypothesis: AGBL5/CCP5 removes branched glutamate side chains from polyglutamylated tubulin in ciliary axonemes and basal bodies, maintaining optimal tubulin glutamylation levels for ciliary stability and intraflagellar transport; disease variants disrupt catalytic activity causing tubulin hyperglutamylation, ciliary dysfunction in photoreceptors (retinal degeneration) and cochlear hair cells (hearing loss), explaining retinitis pigmentosa phenotype through defective cilia maintenance in sensory cells; testis enrichment reflects high ciliary content in spermatogenesis.
