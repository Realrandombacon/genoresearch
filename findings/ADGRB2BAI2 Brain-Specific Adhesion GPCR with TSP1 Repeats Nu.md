# ADGRB2/BAI2: Brain-Specific Adhesion GPCR with TSP1 Repeats, Nuclear Transport Interactions, and Angiogenesis Inhibitory Function

## Description
ADGRB2 (Adhesion G Protein-Coupled Receptor B2, also known as BAI2, UniProt O60241) encodes a 1585-amino acid class B2 adhesion GPCR with moderate structural confidence (AlphaFold pLDDT: 61.4). The protein architecture features three thrombospondin type-1 repeats (IPR000884, residues 309-472) conferring anti-angiogenic activity, a GPCR proteolysis site (GPS motif, IPR000203, residues 870-923) enabling autoproteolytic cleavage characteristic of adhesion GPCRs, and a 7-transmembrane secretin-like receptor domain (IPR000832, residues 933-1205) for G protein signaling. HPA data demonstrates brain-enriched expression (74.7 nTPM) with plasma membrane localization, consistent with its designation as brain angiogenesis inhibitor 2. STRING interaction network reveals 10 medium-to-high confidence partners including RANBP2 (0.808), RANGAP1 (0.700), and MAGI1 (0.693) - the RANBP2/RANGAP1 interactions suggest potential nucleocytoplasmic trafficking or nuclear signaling functions beyond canonical membrane GPCR activity, while MAGI1 binding indicates scaffolding at postsynaptic densities. Additional partners DOCK1 (0.686) and KPNB1 (0.561) further support nuclear transport connections. ClinVar contains 18 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including missense mutations (p.Pro147Ala, p.Val448Met, p.Pro287Ala) and copy number gains spanning 1p35.2-35.1, though specific disease phenotypes remain undefined. Mouse ortholog Q8CGM1 (1561 aa) shows strong conservation. Mechanistically, ADGRB2 likely functions through: (1) TSP1-mediated inhibition of angiogenesis via integrin/CD36 binding, (2) GPS-mediated autoproteolysis generating N-terminal fragments for ligand binding and C-terminal 7TM for Gα12/13 signaling, (3) MAGI1 scaffolding organizing postsynaptic protein complexes in neurons, and (4) potential nuclear translocation of cleaved intracellular domain for transcriptional regulation. The nuclear transport interactome is unusual for GPCRs and may represent a novel signaling paradigm for adhesion GPCRs.
