## Description
CCDC137 (Coiled-Coil Domain Containing 137), also known as RaRF, is a 289-amino acid essential intracellular protein encoded on chromosome 17q25.3 (UniProt Q6PK04). InterPro analysis reveals membership in the CCDC137 protein family (IPR026680, residues 12-275; PF28348, residues 47-271; PTHR21838) with extensive coiled-coil architecture enabling protein oligomerization and scaffold formation. Human Protein Atlas classifies CCDC137 as an essential protein with ubiquitous expression across all tissues (low tissue specificity, detected in all), dual nucleoplasmic, nucleolar, and mitotic chromosome localization, consistent with its annotated RNA binding activity and chromosomal association. STRING database identifies 10 high-to-medium confidence protein interaction partners including NOL12 (0.742, high confidence; nucleolar protein involved in ribosome biogenesis), TMEM69 (0.652), DDX27 (0.635; DEAD-box RNA helicase), NPM1 (0.622; nucleophosmin, nucleolar chaperone), NIFK (0.601; nucleolar protein), CBLN3 (0.573), RPL8 (0.561; ribosomal protein), GPATCH4 (0.558), RRP8 (0.546; rRNA processing), and ZC3H10 (0.533; zinc finger protein). This interaction network strongly implicates CCDC137 in nucleolar function, ribosome biogenesis, and RNA metabolism. ClinVar documents 21 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants encompassing CCDC137, including large-scale copy number gains and losses on chromosome 17 (17q24.3-25.3, 17q25.1-25.3, 17q24.1-25.3, 17q24.2-25.3 regions), deletions, and single allele losses, indicating strong dosage sensitivity. The coiled-coil domain architecture, nucleolar localization, RNA binding activity, and physical interactions with ribosome biogenesis factors (NOL12, DDX27, NPM1, RRP8) suggest CCDC137 functions as a nucleolar scaffold protein that organizes ribosomal RNA processing complexes or mediates nucleolar structural integrity during cell division (mitotic chromosome localization). Given essential protein classification, ubiquitous expression, and extensive clinical CNV burden, CCDC137 likely performs fundamental cellular functions in ribosome assembly or nucleolar organization, with haploinsufficiency or overexpression potentially contributing to developmental disorders through disrupted ribosome biogenesis or nucleolar stress responses.
