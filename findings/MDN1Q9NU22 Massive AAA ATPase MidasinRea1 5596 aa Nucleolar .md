## Description
MDN1 (Midasin AAA ATPase 1, also known as Rea1) is a massive 5596 amino acid intracellular chaperone protein (UniProt Q9NU22), one of the largest human proteins, with multiple AAA+ ATPase domains characteristic of dynein-related ATPases that function through ATP-dependent conformational changes to remodel ribonucleoprotein complexes. The protein contains multiple AAA+ ATPase domains (IPR003593, residues 321-634/669-912/1076-1221; IPR011704, residues 325-448/672-756/816-902; cd00009, residues 1368-1543/1746-1896; G3DSA:3.40.50.300, residues 309-486/656-717/809-920/1063-1233; IPR027417, residues 307-591/656-978/1048-1316) enabling ATP binding (GO:0005524) and ATP hydrolysis activity (GO:0016887), a von Willebrand factor type A domain (IPR002035, residues 5382-5583; IPR036465; G3DSA:3.40.50.410, residues 5379-5545; cd01460 vWA_midasin, residues 5323-5593) for protein interactions, and belongs to the midasin family (IPR012099, residues 19-5593). MDN1 is located on chromosome 6q15 and is involved in ribosomal large subunit assembly (GO:0000027), located in nucleoplasm (GO:0005654), nucleoli (GO:0005730), intermediate filaments (GO:0005882), and cytosol (GO:0005829). The protein is classified as an essential protein/plasma protein/predicted intracellular protein with chaperone molecular function.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals an extremely high-confidence network of ribosome biogenesis factors: NLE1 (0.982, Nuclear export-like protein 1, 60S subunit assembly), GNL2 (0.962, G protein nucleolar 2, GTPase involved in ribosome biogenesis), NSA2 (0.934, Nucleolar protein NSUN4-associated protein 2, 60S assembly), WDR18 (0.933, WD repeat-containing protein 18, ribosome biogenesis), MRTO4 (0.920, MRT4 homolog, 60S subunit assembly), TEX10 (0.919, Testis-expressed protein 10, ribosome biogenesis), WDR74 (0.895, WD repeat-containing protein 74, 60S assembly), GTPBP4 (0.886, GTP-binding protein 4, nucleolar GTPase), WDR12 (0.829, WD repeat-containing protein 12, PeBoW complex), and SDAD1 (0.728, SDA1 domain-containing protein 1, 60S export). These interactions confirm MDN1 functions within the ribosome biogenesis pathway, with NLE1 as the highest-confidence partner (0.982) reflecting direct cooperation in 60S pre-ribosome maturation. All interaction partners are established ribosome assembly factors, indicating MDN1 operates within the nucleolar ribosome assembly machinery.\n\nHPA expression reveals MDN1 has low tissue specificity with ubiquitous expression detected in many tissues, consistent with essential roles in fundamental ribosome biogenesis required in all proliferating cells. Cancer enhanced specificity indicates elevated expression in cancer types with high proliferation rates. Not detected in immune cells suggests restricted expression in hematopoietic lineages or low expression in quiescent immune cells. Subcellular localization to nucleoplasm and nucleoli confirms its role in ribosome assembly within nuclear compartments. Intermediate filament and cytosol localization suggests additional cytoplasmic functions in late ribosome maturation steps. The protein is classified as an essential protein and chaperone.\n\nClinVar documents 19 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including large copy number losses (6q14.3-22.31 deletion 85.9-120.5 Mb, 6q12-16.1 deletion 69.9-94.3 Mb, 6q14.1-16.1 deletion 81.2-97.7 Mb) and gains (6q12-16.1 amplification 69.9-94.3 Mb, 6q14.1-16.1 amplification 81.2-97.7 Mb, 6p25.3-q27 amplification spanning entire chromosome arm), plus point mutations (c.4449-11T>G splice variant, c.9882-25G>T splice variant, c.9911A>G p.Asp3304Gly missense, c.16395+4A>C splice variant). The variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity with both haploinsufficiency and overexpression effects. While no specific Mendelian disease is annotated, 6q CNVs are associated with developmental delay, neurological disorders, growth abnormalities, and intellectual disability. Given MDN1's essential role in ribosome biogenesis and nucleolar localization, loss-of-function may cause defective 60S subunit assembly, impaired ribosome production, or disrupted nucleolar function leading to ribosomopathy phenotypes.\n\nConservation is evident across eukaryotes: human (Q9NU22, 5596 aa), Arabidopsis (A0A1P8AUY4, 5400 aa), Saccharomyces cerevisiae (Q12019, 4910 aa), Schizosaccharomyces pombe (O94248, 4717 aa), Giardia (Q8T5T1, 4835 aa), indicating the midasin architecture with AAA+ domains and vWA domain is highly conserved across eukaryotes with similar massive protein length, suggesting strong purifying selection on ATP-dependent ribosome remodeling functions.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: MDN1 functions as a massive AAA+ ATPase molecular chaperone that drives ribosomal large subunit (60S) maturation through ATP-dependent conformational changes and protein remodeling. The multiple AAA+ domains (residues 307-1316) provide ATPase motor activity for mechanical work, while the C-terminal vWA domain (residues 5323-5593) mediates substrate binding to pre-60S particles. Through NLE1 interaction (0.982), MDN1 cooperates with nuclear export-like proteins for 60S subunit export. GNL2 interaction (0.962) links MDN1 to nucleolar GTPase activity for assembly checkpoint control. NSA2, WDR18, MRTO4, TEX10, WDR74, and WDR12 interactions (0.934-0.829) position MDN1 within the PeBoW complex and related 60S assembly machinery. GTPBP4 interaction (0.886) connects MDN1 to nucleolar GTPase cycles. SDAD1 interaction (0.728) links MDN1 to 60S export factors. Nucleolar localization indicates MDN1 acts during early-mid 60S assembly stages. Ubiquitous expression reflects essential ribosome biogenesis requirements. Cancer enhanced expression indicates elevated demand in proliferating cells. 6q CNVs cause MDN1 haploinsufficiency or overexpression, potentially disrupting ribosome assembly and causing ribosomopathy phenotypes. MDN1 represents a strong candidate gene for 6q deletion syndrome and may serve as a therapeutic target for ribosomopathies and cancers with elevated ribosome biogenesis.
