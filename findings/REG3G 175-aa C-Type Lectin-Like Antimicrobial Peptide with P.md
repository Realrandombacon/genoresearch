## Description
REG3G (Regenerating Family Member 3 Gamma; UniProt Q6UW15) is a 175 amino acid secreted C-type lectin-like antimicrobial peptide with 183 publications, representing a moderately characterized innate immune effector with emerging roles in gut barrier integrity, metabolic disease, and pancreatic function through IL-22-mediated signaling pathways.\n\nSTRUCTURAL ARCHITECTURE: InterPro analysis reveals compact C-type lectin-like domain (CTLD) architecture optimized for carbohydrate recognition and bacterial binding. The entire protein comprises a single C-type lectin-like domain (IPR001304/PF00059/PS50041, residues 40-173/47-172/58-173) belonging to the C-type lectin-like/link domain superfamily (IPR016186/G3DSA:3.10.100.10, residues 37-174) with C-type lectin fold (IPR016187, residues 38-173). Conserved C-type lectin domain signature (IPR018378/PS00615, residues 146-171) includes critical calcium-binding and carbohydrate recognition residues. The protein belongs to the C-type lectin and snaclec domain-containing protein family (IPR050111/PTHR22803, residues 37-174). Unlike classical C-type lectins, REG3G lacks calcium-dependent sugar binding but retains bactericidal activity through peptidoglycan recognition. AlphaFold predicts high confidence structure (global pLDDT 89.4), indicating a well-folded compact lectin fold—consistent with its role as a secreted antimicrobial that must maintain structural stability in the harsh intestinal environment.\n\nINTERACTION NETWORK: STRING-DB identifies high-confidence interactions with innate immunity effectors, cytokine signaling components, and mucosal barrier proteins: REG3A (0.894, Regenerating Family Member 3 Alpha, closest paralog with overlapping antimicrobial specificity), IL22 (0.865, Interleukin-22, master regulator of REG3G expression via STAT3), IL22RA2 (0.759, IL-22 receptor alpha 2, soluble decoy receptor), MUC1 (0.627, Mucin-1, epithelial barrier glycoprotein), MUC2 (0.609, Mucin-2, intestinal mucus component), MYD88 (0.604, Myeloid differentiation primary response 88, TLR/IL-1R adaptor), IL17A (0.601, Interleukin-17A, proinflammatory cytokine), LCN2 (0.579, Lipocalin-2, siderophore-binding antimicrobial), NOD2 (0.574, Nucleotide-binding oligomerization domain-containing protein 2, intracellular bacterial sensor), and IL22RA1 (0.425, IL-22 receptor alpha 1, signaling subunit). This interaction profile establishes REG3G as a downstream effector of the IL-22/STAT3 axis that coordinates with mucins (MUC1/2) and other antimicrobials (LCN2) to maintain gut barrier integrity.\n\nEXPRESSION & LOCALIZATION: HPA shows tissue-enriched expression with extraordinary pancreas specificity (3450.0 nTPM—the highest expression level observed in this research session, ~130x higher than SCN3B brain expression at 73.3 nTPM). Also detected in some other tissues including small intestine. Cancer-enriched specificity indicates upregulation in tumors. Subcellular localization is cytosol (pre-secretion) but functions as a predicted secreted protein in the intestinal lumen and pancreatic ducts. Biological processes include acute phase response and inflammatory response. Molecular function is antimicrobial activity. Single cell type specificity is cell type-enriched (Paneth cells and pancreatic acinar cells).\n\nDISEASE LANDSCAPE: ClinVar contains 12 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number losses at 2p25.1-q13, 2p12-11.2, 2p13.1-12, 2p13.1-11.2, plus copy number gains at 2p25.3-q37.3, 2p25.3-q37.3 (multiple submissions), plus single allele duplications and deletions. No monogenic disease is yet definitively assigned, but 2p CNVs cause developmental anomalies. Recent 2025 literature establishes prominent mechanistic roles: (1) Intraduodenal REG3G administration improves gut barrier function and mitigates hepatic steatosis in mice (6 citations, direct metabolic benefit); (2) Intestinal REG3G protects against gastrointestinal dysfunction in ethanol intoxication and burn injury model (burn+alcohol synergy); (3) Islet regeneration protein REG3G promotes macrophage clearance of β-cell-derived dysfunctional mitochondria-rich vesicles to mitigate type 2 diabetes (novel mitophagy mechanism); (4) Fructus aurantii regulates ileal REG3G in NASH treatment (7 citations, therapeutic targeting). Additional studies show REG3G induction by IL-22 during inflammation, protection against vancomycin-resistant Enterococcus colonization, and roles in graft-versus-host disease.\n\nCONSERVATION: Orthologs confirmed in mouse (O09049, 174 aa) and rat (P42854, 174 aa), indicating strong evolutionary constraint on the CTLD architecture across mammals for conserved antimicrobial defense.\n\nFUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: REG3G functions as a secreted C-type lectin-like antimicrobial peptide that maintains gut barrier integrity and regulates metabolic homeostasis through IL-22/STAT3 signaling. In the intestine, IL-22 produced by group 3 innate lymphoid cells (ILC3s) and Th17 cells binds IL22RA1/IL10RB receptors on epithelial cells, activating STAT3 to induce REG3G transcription. Secreted REG3G binds peptidoglycan on Gram-positive bacteria (particularly vancomycin-resistant Enterococcus) via its CTLD, disrupting bacterial membranes through a calcium-independent mechanism. This creates a protective zone separating the microbiota from the epithelial surface, preventing bacterial translocation and systemic inflammation. In metabolic disease, REG3G has dual roles: (1) Intraduodenal REG3G improves gut barrier function, reducing endotoxin (LPS) translocation to the liver and mitigating hepatic steatosis in NASH; (2) In pancreatic islets, REG3G promotes macrophage clearance of damaged mitochondria from stressed β-cells, reducing inflammation and preserving insulin secretion in type 2 diabetes. In ethanol+burn injury, REG3G protects against gut barrier dysfunction that would otherwise permit bacterial sepsis. The extreme pancreas expression (3450 nTPM) suggests additional pancreatic-specific functions beyond gut defense, possibly in acinar cell protection or islet regeneration. Therapeutic REG3G administration or IL-22 pathway activation could treat NASH, T2DM, and infectious complications in burn patients.
