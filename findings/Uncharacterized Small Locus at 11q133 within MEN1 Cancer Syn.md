## Description
LOC105369367 is an uncharacterized genomic locus on chromosome 11q13.3 (NC_000011.10:69234933-69228748) spanning approximately 6.2 kb. Comprehensive database searches reveal no evidence of canonical protein-coding function: no UniProt accession was identified, precluding InterPro domain analysis and AlphaFold structure prediction. STRING database returned no protein interaction partners, confirming absence of characterized interactome. Human Protein Atlas could not resolve this locus to an Ensembl ID, indicating no detectable protein expression or antibody reactivity in surveyed tissues. However, ClinVar analysis reveals 2 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants - both copy number losses (deletions): (1) GRCh38 chr11:68031693-71593495 deletion (~3.6 Mb, 11q13.2-13.4), (2) GRCh38 chr11:67217264-69448598 deletion (~2.2 Mb, 11q13.2-13.3). The 11q13.3 region is the critical locus for multiple endocrine neoplasia type 1 (MEN1) syndrome and various epithelial cancers, containing dosage-sensitive tumor suppressors and oncogenes including MEN1 (menin, histone methyltransferase complex, pituitary/pancreatic/parathyroid tumors), PYGM (myophosphorylase, McArdle disease, glycogen metabolism), C11orf65 (chromosome 11 open reading frame 65), and multiple cell cycle regulatory elements. LOC105369367 likely functions as: (1) a cis-regulatory enhancer controlling 11q13.3 MEN1 tumor suppressor expression, (2) a long non-coding RNA (lncRNA) with epigenetic regulatory function in endocrine tumorigenesis, (3) a chromatin boundary/insulator element within the 11q13.3 topologically associating domain (TAD), or (4) a recombination hotspot boundary affecting 11q13 genomic stability. The moderate pathogenic CNV burden (2 variants) with both deletions suggests: (1) this locus may be haploinsufficient for MEN1 regulation, (2) deletions may disrupt 11q13.3 chromatin architecture leading to MEN1 loss of function, (3) copy number losses may alter endocrine enhancer activity contributing to pituitary adenomas, pancreatic neuroendocrine tumors, or parathyroid hyperplasia, or (4) this represents a non-coding "dark" locus with tumor suppressor regulatory effects. The 11q13.3 region is critical for cell cycle control and endocrine development, with MEN1 mutations being the defining lesion in multiple endocrine neoplasia type 1 syndrome.
