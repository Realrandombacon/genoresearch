## Description
LOC129390466 is a 200bp cis-regulatory element (CRE) located on chromosome 12q13.3-14.1 (GRCh38: 57420331-57420531), functionally validated as a transcriptional silencer through STARR-seq massively parallel reporter assays (MPRAs) in K562 erythroleukemia cells. This locus lacks canonical promoter, enhancer, or CTCF-bound insulator chromatin signatures but demonstrates repressive activity against the super core promoter SCP1. As a non-coding regulatory sequence, it has no UniProt accession, protein domains, STRING interaction partners, or Human Protein Atlas expression data (query not applicable for non-coding locus).\n\nClinVar analysis reveals 3 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variants disrupting this region, representing LOW CNV burden (compared to LOC129390592: 9 variants, LOC129390117: 31 variants, LOC101928475: 39 variants, LOC129389033: 8 variants, LOC129388445: 14 variants). The variant spectrum shows BALANCED dosage sensitivity with slight deletion bias: 1 duplication (copy number gain spanning 12p13.33-q24.33, ~133 Mb) versus 2 deletions (copy number losses spanning 12q13.3-14.1 ~3.2 Mb and 12q13.3-14.2 ~6 Mb). This ~1:2 ratio (33% duplications, 67% deletions) indicates MODERATE loss-of-function dosage sensitivity where reduced silencing activity is somewhat more pathogenic than increased silencing, contrasting sharply with LOC129390592 (~8:1 duplication bias) and LOC129389033 (~7:1 duplication bias).\n\nThe genomic context places LOC129390466 within 12q13.3-14.1, a region associated with 12q deletion syndromes (characterized by intellectual disability, growth retardation, skeletal abnormalities, and cardiac defects). The 12q13-14 region is critical for neurodevelopment, metabolic regulation, and epigenetic chromatin modification.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: LOC129390466 functions as a distal silencer within the 12q13.3-14.1 topologically associating domain (TAD), potentially regulating PAH, GALNT11, or chromatin modifier genes in hepatic and neuronal contexts. The balanced ~1:2 deletion bias suggests that both LOSS-of-silencing (haploinsufficiency, 50% activity) and GAIN-of-silencing (triplication, 150% activity) cause pathology, with deletions slightly more severe. This indicates the target gene(s) require OPTIMAL repression - deviations in either direction disrupt homeostasis. The mechanism likely involves recruitment of co-repressor complexes that maintain precise expression levels of metabolic enzymes (PAH) or epigenetic regulators (KMT2D). In K562 cells, this silencer may regulate erythroid-specific transcription factors. The 12q13.3-14.1 location near PAH (phenylketinuria gene) and KMT2D (Kabuki syndrome gene) raises the possibility that LOC129390466 prevents ectopic silencing of essential metabolic and chromatin regulatory genes. The balanced CNV burden (3 variants) indicates this element has modest mutational target size, consistent with 12q13-14 deletion syndrome being a recognized genomic disorder.
