## Description
LYSMD2 (LysM domain-containing protein 2, UniProt Q8IV50) is a 215-amino acid intracellular protein located on chromosome 15q21.1 within a region prone to extensive copy-number variations. InterPro analysis reveals canonical LysM (lysine motif) domain architecture characteristic of peptidoglycan-binding proteins: single LysM domain (IPR018392/PF01476/SM00257/PS51782, aa 71-116/73-116/72-116) belonging to the LysM domain superfamily (IPR036779/G3DSA:3.10.350.10/SSF54106, aa 66-123/71-116) that forms a compact βααβ fold optimized for binding N-acetylglucosamine-containing glycans including bacterial peptidoglycan and chitin. The protein belongs to the LysM and putative peptidoglycan-binding domain-containing protein family (IPR045030/PTHR20932, aa 10-215). GO annotations are limited but protein class indicates predicted intracellular localization. AlphaFold structure prediction achieves medium confidence (pLDDT 66.9), indicating a partially structured protein with an ordered LysM domain connected by flexible regions — architecture consistent with glycan-binding proteins that require conformational flexibility for substrate recognition. Human Protein Atlas shows low tissue specificity with ubiquitous expression (detected in all tissues), low cancer and immune cell specificity, cell type-enhanced patterns, and remarkable dual subcellular localization to both nuclear bodies and mitochondria — suggesting potential roles in both nuclear processes (chromatin regulation, transcription) and mitochondrial function (metabolism, apoptosis, or innate immune signaling). STRING interaction network reveals only 3 partners but all from the same LysM family: LYSMD4 (0.626, highest confidence — LysM domain-containing protein 4), TNFAIP8L3 (0.444, TNF-alpha-induced protein 8-like protein 3 involved in apoptosis regulation and immune response), and LYSMD3 (0.432, LysM domain-containing protein 3). This highly specific interactome confirms LYSMD2 functions within the LysM protein family network, potentially forming homo/hetero-oligomers for glycan recognition. ClinVar contains 20 pathogenic variants consisting predominantly of large copy-number gains and losses across 15q21.1-q22.2, deletions, and duplications, demonstrating dosage sensitivity where genomic rearrangements disrupting chromosome 15 cause disease phenotypes potentially affecting development and cellular homeostasis. Conservation across mammals indicates ancient evolutionary origin tied to fundamental glycan recognition mechanisms. LYSMD2 likely functions as a peptidoglycan/glycan-binding protein, using its LysM domain to recognize N-acetylglucosamine-containing structures either from bacterial pathogens (innate immune surveillance) or endogenous glycoconjugates — with dual nuclear-mitochondrial localization suggesting roles in coordinating immune responses, metabolic regulation, or quality control pathways where glycan recognition signals cellular stress or infection, potentially linking mitochondrial dysfunction to inflammatory diseases when chromosome 15q rearrangements disrupt LYSMD2 dosage.
