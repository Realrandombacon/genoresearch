## Description
ISOC2 (Isochorismatase Domain-Containing Protein 2, UniProt Q96AB3) is a 205-amino acid mitochondrial/cytosolic protein belonging to the isochorismatase-like hydrolase superfamily (IPR000868, PF00857, positions 16-162). Despite its enzymatic domain classification, ISOC2's catalytic activity remains unconfirmed; instead, it functions as a protein-protein interaction module regulating tumor suppressor stability. AlphaFold predicts an exceptionally confident structure (pLDDT 95.0) with a well-folded isochorismatase-like hydrolase fold. ISOC2 shows liver-enriched expression (HPA: 262.8 nTPM in liver, detected in all tissues) with dual mitochondrial and cytosolic localization. STRING interactions reveal functional partnerships with cell cycle regulators: CDKN2A/p16INK4a (0.790, high confidence), OPLAH (0.758), NAPRT (0.748), MROH6 (0.742), and NAD biosynthesis enzymes NMNAT1/2/3 (0.564-0.571). Mechanistically, ISOC2 was originally identified as a p16INK4a-interacting protein (2007, 21 citations). Recent 2025 research establishes ISOC2 as a critical node in the WSB1-OTUD4-ISOC2-p16INK4a axis: WSB1 (SOCS-box E3 ligase) promotes prostate cancer malignancy through OTUD4-mediated ISOC2 stabilization, which suppresses p16INK4a and drives cell cycle progression. This positions ISOC2 as a molecular bridge between ubiquitin signaling and tumor suppressor regulation. ClinVar documents 17 pathogenic variants (mostly 19q CNVs), though no specific monogenic syndrome is defined. With only 9 literature references despite clear oncogenic function, ISOC2 represents a genuine dark gene with therapeutic potential in prostate cancer and cell cycle disorders.
