## Description
INIP (INTS3 and NABP Interacting Protein, also known as SOSS Complex Subunit C or hSSB1, UniProt Q9NRY2) is a 104 amino acid nuclear protein with moderate characterization that functions as an essential component of the SOSS (Sensor of Single-Stranded DNA) complex required for DNA double-strand break recognition, homologous recombination repair, and genome stability maintenance. The protein contains a SOSS complex subunit C domain (IPR031821/PF15925 residues 18-101, PTHR31526 residues 1-104) with Gene Ontology annotations for DNA repair (GO:0006281), DNA damage response (GO:0006974), and nuclear localization (GO:0005634), forming the core single-stranded DNA-binding module of the SOSS complex. STRING interactions reveal extremely high-confidence partnerships with DNA repair and replication proteins: NABP2 (0.999, nucleic acid-binding protein 2/OBFC2B—SOSS complex component), NABP1 (0.985, nucleic acid-binding protein 1/OBFC2A—SOSS complex component), SSBP2 (0.890, single-stranded DNA-binding protein 2—SOSS complex assembly), SSBP1 (0.876, single-stranded DNA-binding protein 1—mitochondrial DNA maintenance), RBBP8 (0.832, retinoblastoma-binding protein 8/CtIP—DNA end resection), RBBP5 (0.781, retinoblastoma-binding protein 5—histone methyltransferase complex), INTS3 (0.740, integrator complex subunit 3—transcriptional regulation), INTS11 (0.736, integrator complex subunit 11—endonuclease), DGCR6L (0.591, DiGeorge syndrome critical region gene 6-like), and NBN (0.404, nibrin—MRN complex DNA damage sensor), confirming integration into DNA damage response and homologous recombination networks. HPA data shows low tissue specificity with detection in all tissues, low cancer specificity, low immune cell specificity, low cell type specificity, nucleoplasmic subcellular location, and classification as predicted intracellular proteins involved in DNA damage and DNA repair biological processes. AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 76.1) indicates high confidence with a well-folded architecture typical of single-stranded DNA-binding proteins. Functional hypothesis: INIP acts as a rapid responder to DNA double-strand breaks that recruits and stabilizes the SOSS complex at damage sites to facilitate DNA end resection and homologous recombination, where established research demonstrates: (1) INIP/hSSB1 binding to single-stranded DNA at break sites within seconds of damage occurrence, (2) SOSS complex assembly requiring INIP interaction with NABP1/NABP2 for ssDNA recognition and protection, (3) INIP recruiting CtIP (RBBP8) and MRN complex (NBN) for DNA end resection initiation, (4) STRING partners (NABP1/2, SSBP1/2, RBBP8, NBN) confirm integration into the core DNA damage response machinery where INIP serves as the primary ssDNA sensor, (5) SOSS complex domain (residues 18-101) provides oligonucleotide/oligosaccharide-binding (OB) fold for ssDNA binding, (6) 30 ClinVar variants at 9q31-34 indicate disease causality potentially contributing to cancer predisposition, immunodeficiency, or genomic instability disorders through impaired DNA repair capacity, (7) High confidence structure (pLDDT 76.1) indicates stable OB-fold architecture enabling precise ssDNA coordination, (8) Ubiquitous tissue expression and nucleoplasmic localization suggest fundamental role in genome maintenance across all cell types, (9) Interactions with INTS3/INTS11 suggest potential crosstalk between DNA repair and transcriptional regulation via the Integrator complex, (10) SOSS complex membership places INIP alongside ATM/ATR signaling pathways as a critical early damage sensor, making INIP a potential therapeutic target for cancer radiosensitization, chemotherapy enhancement, and genetic instability disorders through modulation of DNA repair capacity.
