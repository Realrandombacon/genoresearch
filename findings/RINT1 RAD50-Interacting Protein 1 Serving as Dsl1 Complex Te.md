## Description
RINT1 encodes a 792 amino acid RAD50-interacting protein 1 (UniProt Q6NUQ1) that functions as an essential tethering factor in the Dsl1/NZR complex mediating retrograde vesicular transport from Golgi to endoplasmic reticulum. The protein contains a conserved RINT-1/Tip20 domain family (IPR007528, positions 47-792) with a characteristic RINT1/TIP20 domain profile (PS51386, positions 220-792), PF04437 repeat region (positions 304-784), and C-terminal Dsl1p vesicle tethering complex domain (G3DSA:1.20.58.670, positions 591-715; IPR042044). These structural elements enable RINT1 to function as a molecular bridge between ER SNARE complexes and incoming Golgi-derived vesicles.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals RINT1 forms an exceptionally tight complex with Dsl1 machinery components: NBAS (score 0.998), STX18 (0.994), USE1 (0.985), SEC22B (0.943), RAB18 (0.935), SCFD1 (0.920), SCFD2 (0.889), and ZW10 (0.749). This positions RINT1 at the critical interface of ER-Golgi trafficking, coordinating vesicle docking through SNARE complex assembly with syntaxin-18 (STX18) and use1 (USE1).\n\nHPA expression data shows RINT1 is ubiquitously expressed across all tissues with low tissue specificity, classified as an essential protein involved in cell cycle, ER-Golgi transport, and protein transport. Subcellular localization is predominantly Golgi apparatus. AlphaFold structure prediction (AF-Q6NUQ1-F1) yields a global pLDDT score of 85.1 (high confidence), indicating a well-folded stable structure across all 792 residues.\n\nClinVar documents 106 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including nonsense mutations (p.Asn49Ter, p.Glu371Ter, p.Ser280Ter, p.Arg501Ter), frameshifts (p.Asp403fs, p.Gln610fs), splice site mutations (c.690-2A>G), and large deletions at 7q23. Despite this extensive variant catalog, ClinVar lists 0 associated diseases—though recent literature (2024-2025) identifies RINT1 deficiency as causing recurrent acute liver failure (RALF), infantile liver dysfunction, neutropenia, and cranial dysmorphism.\n\nStrong conservation across species: mouse Q8BZ36 (792 aa), Drosophila Q9VS46 (724 aa), Arabidopsis MAG2 (795 aa). With only 69 publications (vs. 328 for KDM3A, 393 for HUS1), RINT1 represents partially characterized biology—its precise mechanism in linking ER stress responses to vesicle tethering, role in RAD50-mediated DNA repair (suggested by name), and genotype-phenotype correlations in liver failure patients remain incompletely understood.
