## Description
RBPJL encodes Recombination Signal Binding Protein for Immunoglobulin Kappa J Region-Like (UniProt Q9UBG7), a 517 aa transcription factor functioning as a pancreas-specific modulator of the Notch signaling pathway. InterPro analysis reveals a complex multi-domain architecture: an N-terminal RBP-J/Cbf11/Cbf12 DNA-binding domain (IPR015351, aa 68-206) sharing homology with the p53-like transcription factor superfamily (IPR008967), a central Beta-trefoil DNA-binding domain (IPR015350, aa 207-359) mediating specific cis-regulatory element recognition, and a C-terminal IPT domain (cd01176, aa 381-477) within an Immunoglobulin E-set fold (IPR014756) typical of cytokine receptors and signaling scaffolds. AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (pLDDT 80.2), indicating a stable, well-folded protein capable of precise DNA-protein and protein-protein interactions. STRING interactions confirm tight integration with the canonical Notch machinery: PTF1A (0.994, pancreatic master regulator), MAML2 (0.959), MAML3 (0.946), MAML1 (0.938) as transcriptional co-activators, and direct binding to NOTCH1 (0.942), NOTCH4 (0.933), NOTCH3 (0.811), NOTCH2 (0.777), plus the homologous repressor RBPJ (0.930). HPA shows extreme tissue specificity, with pancreas-enriched expression (919.9 nTPM), far exceeding all other tissues, and classification as 'cancer enhanced', suggesting a critical role in acinar cell identity. ClinVar reports 8 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, primarily large copy number losses/gains at 20q11.21-q13.13, implicating dosage sensitivity in developmental or neoplastic contexts. Literature (40 papers) establishes roles in: (1) Bone formation via Fam102a-mediated translocation of RBPJL to facilitate Osterix expression; (2) Acute pancreatitis protection through blockade of the Arid5a/IL-6/STAT3 axis; (3) Distinctive inability to respond to Notch activation despite repressing Notch targets, acting as a constitutive repressor or context-dependent modulator distinct from RBPJ. Functional hypothesis: RBPJL functions through: (1) Pancreatic Acinar Specification - high expression suggests it locks acinar cells in a differentiated state by repressing progenitor genes or preventing dedifferentiation; (2) Notch Pathway Divergence - unlike RBPJ, RBPJL may lack the conformational switch for NICD (Notch Intracellular Domain) binding, instead recruiting alternative co-repressors (e.g., SHARP, CIR1) to silence Notch targets permanently in the pancreas; (3) Inflammatory Brake - suppression of IL-6/STAT3 signaling during pancreatitis prevents excessive inflammation and tissue damage; (4) Competitive Inhibition - high affinity for Notch response elements (NREs) allows RBPJL to outcompete RBPJ, effectively 'locking' the chromatin in a repressed state independent of Notch ligand availability; (5) Disease Mechanism - 20q CNVs disrupting RBPJL dosage may lead to acinar-to-ductal metaplasia (ADM), a precursor to pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma, or predispose to severe acute pancreatitis due to unchecked inflammation. The high-confidence structure and unique interaction profile position RBPJL as a specialized 'brake' on Notch signaling in the exocrine pancreas.
