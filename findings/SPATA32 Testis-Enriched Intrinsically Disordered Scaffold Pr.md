## Description
SPATA32 (Spermatogenesis-Associated Protein 32) is a 384 amino acid testis-specific protein located on chromosome 17q21.31 that functions as a molecular scaffold during spermatogenesis, particularly in meiotic recombination and chromatin organization. The protein contains a spermatogenesis-associated domain (IPR029297, residues 1-384) and a vitamin A-deficiency signaling region (PF15310, residues 138-384), but lacks canonical enzymatic domains. AlphaFold prediction reveals low global confidence (pLDDT 42.3), indicating the protein is predominantly intrinsically disordered—a characteristic feature of scaffold proteins that function through dynamic protein-protein interactions rather than structured catalytic activity. Human Protein Atlas shows testis-enriched expression (43.5 nTPM) with no detection in other tissues or cancers, confirming its specialized role in germ cell development. STRING interaction analysis identifies SPATA31A7 as the highest-confidence partner (score 0.854), a paralogous spermatogenesis factor; additional partners include TEX11 (0.460) and REC8 (0.460), both critical meiotic recombination proteins—TEX11 is an X-linked meiosis-specific protein required for crossover formation, while REC8 is a cohesin subunit essential for sister chromatid cohesion during meiosis. This interaction network positions SPATA32 within the meiotic recombination machinery, likely serving as a flexible scaffold that bridges cohesin complexes with recombination factors. Additional partners include CATSPER1 (0.418), a sperm calcium channel, and PARP9 (0.437), an ADP-ribosyltransferase involved in DNA damage response. ClinVar contains 11 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number variants (deletions and duplications) spanning 17q12-q25, suggesting dosage sensitivity and potential association with male infertility or spermatogenic failure, though specific phenotypes remain uncharacterized. The conservation pattern (mouse ortholog Q8C5V0, 334 aa; rat ortholog Q66H17, 348 aa) indicates moderate sequence divergence but functional conservation across mammals. The intrinsically disordered nature combined with testis-specific expression and meiotic protein interactions supports a model where SPATA32 acts as a flexible molecular hub that facilitates assembly of multi-protein complexes during meiotic prophase, potentially regulating chromatin accessibility for recombination or stabilizing cohesin-DNA interactions.
