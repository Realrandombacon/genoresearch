## Description
ASRGL1 encodes a 308 aa cytoplasmic enzyme (UniProt Q7L266), also known as ALP, ALP1, or CRASH, that functions as a dual-specificity N-terminal nucleophile (Ntn) hydrolase catalyzing the hydrolysis of L-asparagine to L-aspartate and ammonia while simultaneously repairing damaged proteins by cleaving isoaspartyl peptides formed via spontaneous deamidation. This well-studied gene (51 publications) has critical clinical significance for amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS), hepatocellular carcinoma, nasopharyngeal carcinoma, and retinal degeneration. The protein features a highly conserved type 2 asparaginase architecture: Metazoan L-Asparaginase type 2 domain (IPR033844/cd04702, residues 2-291) containing the catalytic core; Peptidase T2 family signature (IPR000246/PF01112, residues 1-298/1-293) defining the asparaginase 2 class; N-terminal nucleophile aminohydrolase fold (IPR029055/SSF56235/G3DSA:3.60.20.30, residues 18-295/168-305) providing the characteristic alpha-beta sandwich structure with catalytic threonine; classified within L-asparaginase family (PTHR10188) with GO terms for asparaginase activity, beta-aspartyl-peptidase activity, hydrolase activity, and asparagine catabolic process. STRING interactions reveal ASRGL1 operates within high-confidence asparagine metabolism networks: ASPG (0.936), asparaginase homolog; GOT1 (0.908)/GOT2 (0.902)/GOT1L1 (0.906), glutamate oxaloacetate transaminases linking aspartate production to TCA cycle; ASNS (0.794), asparagine synthetase performing reverse reaction; ASS1 (0.519), argininosuccinate synthase in urea cycle; ASPA (0.900), aspartoacylase in aspartate metabolism; IL4I1 (0.906), amino acid oxidase; ADSS1 (0.900)/ADSS2 (0.903), adenylosuccinate synthases utilizing aspartate. HPA confirms tissue-enhanced expression with striking brain enrichment (123.8 nTPM), cervix (114.4 nTPM), testis (93.5 nTPM), detected in all tissues, cancer-enhanced expression, immune cell-enhanced in blood, nucleoplasm/microtubules/cytokinetic bridge subcellular localization, classified as enzyme/metabolic protease. ClinVar lists 18 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including missense mutations affecting catalytic core (c.280G>A p.Ala94Thr, c.280G>T p.Ala94Ser within cd04702, c.407G>A p.Gly136Glu, c.743C>T p.Ala248Val), stop codon readthrough variant (c.926A>C p.Ter309Ser extending C-terminus), single allele deletion, and extensive copy number gains (11q12.1-13.3 56-69Mb x3, 11p11.12-q13.1 50-63Mb x3, 11q12.2-13.5 59-76Mb x3, 11p13-q25 32-134Mb x3), demonstrating strong dosage sensitivity though specific monogenic diseases remain undefined. Mechanistically, ASRGL1 operates through integrated modes: (1) TDP-43 proteinopathy suppression—2024 landmark study (22 citations) demonstrates loss of ASRGL1 triggers TDP-43 aggregation in ALS via dysregulated human endogenous retrovirus HML-2 expression, establishing causal link between metabolic enzyme and neurodegeneration; (2) Photoreceptor survival—2022 study (4 citations) shows Asrgl1 deletion causes progressive photoreceptor degeneration in mice, indicating essential role in retinal protein homeostasis; (3) Hepatocellular carcinoma progression—2022 study (13 citations) identifies ASRGL1 downregulation suppresses HCC tumorigenesis in CDK1-dependent manner, suggesting oncogenic role; (4) Nasopharyngeal carcinoma metabolism—2025 study reveals ASRGL1 drives metabolic reprogramming and invasion in NPC cells; (5) Asparagine catabolism—converts extracellular/intracellular asparagine to aspartate, potentially starving asparagine-dependent tumors or regulating neurotransmitter pools; (6) Protein repair—cleaves isoaspartyl linkages formed by spontaneous deamidation of asparaginyl/aspartyl residues, preventing accumulation of damaged proteins in long-lived cells like neurons and photoreceptors; (7) Conservation—high sequence identity with mouse/rat/zebrafish orthologs indicating fundamental metabolic role. Loss-of-function causes TDP-43 proteinopathy, ALS-like neurodegeneration, photoreceptor death, reduced HCC/NPC progression, altered asparagine/aspartate balance, and accumulation of isoaspartyl-damaged proteins.
