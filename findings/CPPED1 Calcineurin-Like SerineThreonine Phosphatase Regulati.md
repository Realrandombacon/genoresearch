## Description
CPPED1 (calcineurin-like phosphoesterase domain-containing 1) encodes a compact 314 amino acid serine/threonine protein phosphatase (UniProt Q9BRF8) belonging to the metallo-dependent phosphatase superfamily. InterPro analysis reveals a canonical calcineurin-like phosphoesterase catalytic domain (IPR004843, PF00149, residues 69-250) with the characteristic metallophosphatase fold (G3DSA:3.60.21.10, residues 56-310). The CPPED1-specific metallophosphatase domain (IPR041867, residues 29-295) distinguishes it from classical calcineurin/PP2B phosphatases. AlphaFold prediction shows exceptional structural confidence (pLDDT 95.2) across the entire 314 aa length, indicating a highly stable, well-folded catalytic domain without disordered regions.\n\nSTRING interactions reveal a striking interaction network dominated by cystatin family protease inhibitors: CST1 (0.940), CST2 (0.917), and CST3 (0.758) - all at high confidence. Additional partners include NUDT12 (0.734, NAD metabolite hydrolase), NADK (0.690, NAD kinase), PICALM (0.506, clathrin assembly protein), SEPSECS (0.506, selenocysteine synthase), PUDP (0.497), FAM172A (0.486), and ALDH18A1 (0.411). The cystatin interactions suggest CPPED1 may regulate protease inhibitor phosphorylation states, potentially controlling extracellular matrix remodeling or antigen presentation.\n\nHPA expression shows CPPED1 is ubiquitously expressed (detected in all tissues) with low tissue specificity but immune cell enhancement. Subcellular localization spans plasma membrane and cytosol, suggesting potential roles in both intracellular signaling and membrane-associated processes. The immune cell enhancement aligns with emerging roles in neutrophil extracellular trap (NET) formation.\n\nClinVar reports 18 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly chromosome 16p copy number gains, though specific monogenic disease associations remain undefined. The copy number alterations suggest dosage sensitivity where CPPED1 overexpression may contribute to disease phenotypes.\n\nRecent literature (26 papers) reveals emerging disease associations: CPPED1 links neutrophil extracellular traps to diabetic nephropathy (2025), promotes triple-negative breast cancer progression, regulates laryngeal squamous cell carcinoma metastasis via circCDK1/IGF2BP2/m6A axis and PI3K/AKT signaling (2023), and contributes to restenosis after percutaneous coronary intervention (2022).\n\nHYPOTHESIS: CPPED1 functions as a metabolic-immune signaling node that dephosphorylates cystatin family members to modulate protease activity during inflammatory responses. The exceptional CST1/CST2/CST3 interactions suggest CPPED1 directly regulates cystatin phosphorylation, controlling their inhibitory capacity against cathepsins. In cancer, CPPED1 may promote metastasis by enhancing cystatin-mediated cathepsin suppression, facilitating ECM invasion. The NUDT12/NADK interactions suggest CPPED1 integrates NAD metabolic status with phosphatase activity, potentially linking cellular energy state to immune function. In diabetic nephropathy, CPPED1 upregulation may drive NET formation through cystatin-dependent neutrophil activation. The 16p copy number gains in ClinVar suggest CPPED1 overexpression contributes to 16p duplication syndrome phenotypes through hyperactive phosphatase signaling.
