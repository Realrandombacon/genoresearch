## Description
DNAJB5 encodes a 348 aa DnaJ heat shock protein family (Hsp40) member B5 (UniProt O75953), functioning as a co-chaperone that regulates Hsp70 ATPase activity and substrate protein folding through its canonical J-domain architecture. The protein contains an N-terminal DnaJ/J-domain (IPR001623, IPR018253, IPR036869, PF00226, cd06257, G3DSA:1.10.287.110, aa3-79/4-65/2-110) with the highly conserved HPD motif (IPR018253, aa45-64) essential for stimulating Hsp70 ATPase activity, and a C-terminal substrate-binding domain (IPR002939, IPR008971, PF01556, cd10747, G3DSA:2.60.260.20, aa169-341/172-330/164-341) that recognizes and binds unfolded or misfolded client proteins. AlphaFold prediction shows high confidence structure (pLDDT 80.1), indicating a well-folded two-domain architecture typical of type II Hsp40 co-chaperones. Human Protein Atlas classifies DNAJB5 as tissue-enhanced with highest expression in tongue (203.7 nTPM) and skeletal muscle (154.1 nTPM), detected in all surveyed tissues, localized to nucleoplasm and cytosol, with cell type-enhanced specificity—this ubiquitous expression pattern suggests housekeeping chaperone function with elevated demand in metabolically active tissues. STRING interactions reveal DNAJB5 integrates into the core Hsp70 chaperone network with 10 high-confidence partners: HSPA8 (0.943, heat shock protein family A member 8—constitutive Hsp70, primary interaction partner), HSPH1 (0.898, heat shock protein family H member 1—Hsp105/110 nucleotide exchange factor), HSPA1B (0.873, inducible Hsp70), HSPA2 (0.841, testis-specific Hsp70), HSPA4 (0.804, Hsp70 family member), P2RX6 (0.804, purinergic receptor P2X6—unexpected interaction suggesting potential membrane signaling role), DNAJB1 (0.794, Hsp40 co-chaperone paralog), DNAJB4 (0.743, Hsp40 co-chaperone paralog), HSPA6 (0.599, stress-inducible Hsp70), HSPA1L (0.533, Hsp70-like protein). The 0.943 HSPA8 interaction is canonical—DNAJB5 delivers unfolded substrates to HSPA8's ATPase domain, the J-domain HPD motif stimulates ATP hydrolysis, triggering conformational change that traps substrate in HSPA8's binding cleft for folding. ClinVar contains 39 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number variations on chromosome 9p24.3-q21.13 including duplications (9p24.3-q21.13 chr9:208455-72054336x3, 9p24.3-13.1 chr9:208455-38787483x3, 9p24.3-q34.3 chr9:203861-141020389x3) and deletions (9p24.3-q34.11 chr9:203861-131603223del), though no specific DNAJB5-associated disease is yet assigned—this suggests dosage sensitivity where gene copy number alterations cause disease phenotypes, potentially through chaperone network imbalance. Literature (23 papers) documents DNAJB5 roles in: (1) cervical cancer biomarker—DNAJB5/hsc40 elevated expression correlates with tumor progression; (2) zebrafish dnajb5 critical for myocardial proliferation and regeneration—cardiac development and repair function; (3) thioredoxin1 redox regulation via DNAJB5 interaction—oxidative stress response; (4) antihypertrophic effects in heart—cardioprotective function; (5) TDP-43-mediated neurodegeneration—protein aggregation response in ALS/FTLD. The canonical Hsp40 domain architecture and strong HSPA8 interaction (0.943) indicate DNAJB5 functions as a type II J-protein co-chaperone: the J-domain (aa3-79) binds Hsp70 ATPase domain and stimulates ATP hydrolysis via the HPD triad (aa45-64), while the C-terminal substrate-binding domain (aa169-341) recognizes hydrophobic patches on unfolded client proteins and delivers them to Hsp70 for folding. High expression in tongue (203.7 nTPM) and skeletal muscle (154.1 nTPM) suggests specialized roles in striated muscle protein homeostasis—these tissues have high metabolic demand and require robust chaperone capacity for contractile protein maintenance. The zebrafish cardiac regeneration data indicates DNAJB5 supports cardiomyocyte proliferation, potentially by folding cell cycle regulators or stress response proteins during cardiac repair. The thioredoxin1 interaction suggests redox-sensitive chaperone function—DNAJB5 may sense oxidative stress and modulate Hsp70 activity accordingly. The P2RX6 interaction (0.804) is notable as this purinergic receptor mediates ATP-gated ion channel activity—DNAJB5 may regulate P2RX6 folding, trafficking, or signaling, linking protein homeostasis to purinergic signaling. The 39 ClinVar CNVs without assigned disease suggest DNAJB5 dosage alterations cause phenotypes not yet mapped to this specific gene—potential mechanisms include: (1) chaperone network imbalance affecting global protein folding; (2) specific client protein misfolding in dosage-sensitive pathways; (3) developmental defects from altered chaperone capacity during embryogenesis. DNAJB5 represents a partially characterized dark gene with clear mechanistic role as Hsp40 co-chaperone but incomplete understanding of tissue-specific functions, client protein repertoire, and disease mechanisms.
