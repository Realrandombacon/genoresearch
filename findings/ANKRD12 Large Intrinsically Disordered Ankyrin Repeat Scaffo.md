# ANKRD12: Large Intrinsically Disordered Ankyrin Repeat Scaffold Protein Recruiting Histone Deacetylases for Transcriptional Repression

**Quality Score:** 3.5/10  (E=6.7, D=0.3) [MODERATE]

## Description
ANKRD12 encodes a massive 2062 amino acid ankyrin repeat domain-containing protein (UniProt Q6UB98) located on chromosome 18p11.22. InterPro analysis reveals extensive ankyrin repeat architecture: three tandem ankyrin repeats at positions 177-282 (IPR002110, PF00023, PF12796, SM00248) forming the canonical stacked helix-turn-helix structure for protein-protein interactions, plus a large C-terminal ANKRD12-specific domain (IPR053210, PTHR24149, aa 1097-2062). Despite these structured repeats, AlphaFold predicts low global confidence (pLDDT 41.2), indicating the protein is predominantly intrinsically disordered with localized ordered ankyrin repeat modules—a architecture optimized for scaffolding multiple protein partners simultaneously. HPA classifies ANKRD12 as a cancer-related intracellular protein with ubiquitous expression across all tissues, low tissue specificity, and dual nucleoplasmic/cytosolic localization. STRING interaction network reveals connections to chromatin regulators and transcriptional machinery: KMT2E (histone methyltransferase, score 0.537), TADA3 (transcriptional adaptor/ADA3, 0.450), ARID4B (chromatin remodeler, 0.434), ZBTB14 (zinc finger transcription factor, 0.451), and RBM25 (RNA-binding protein, 0.439), plus cytoskeletal/structural partners SLIT2 (0.563), PDE4DIP (0.457), and LRRC30 (0.583). ClinVar documents 102 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number gains/losses spanning 18p11.32-11.21 and missense mutations (e.g., p.Glu1002Val), indicating strong dosage sensitivity. Functional hypothesis: ANKRD12 acts as a large disordered transcriptional co-repressor scaffold that bridges nuclear receptors with histone deacetylase complexes through its ankyrin repeat modules, recruiting HDACs to silence target genes. The disordered regions enable dynamic, multivalent interactions with p160 coactivators, TADA3/ADA3, and chromatin modifiers, while ankyrin repeats provide specific binding interfaces. Disease mechanism involves haploinsufficiency or overexpression disrupting transcriptional repression programs, contributing to developmental disorders and cancer through aberrant gene silencing.
