## Description
ECRG4 encodes Augurin (148 aa, Q9H1Z8), a small secreted peptide of the augurin family with emerging roles in cancer suppression and neuroimmune regulation. InterPro analysis reveals ECRG4-specific architecture: Augurin family domain (IPR028173, positions 1-148), ECRG4/PF15187 domain (positions 32-148), and PTHR31613 augurin family signature - representing a distinct protein family without homology to known functional domains. GO annotations are limited, reflecting incomplete functional characterization. HPA reveals striking choroid plexus enrichment (412.4 nTPM - exceptionally high), cancer-enhanced expression pattern, and immune cell enhancement indicating dual neuroendocrine-immune functions; predicted secreted protein consistent with signaling peptide role. Literature (154 papers) reveals multifaceted disease roles: 2024 studies show DNMT1-mediated ECRG4 downregulation promotes esophageal carcinoma growth via IRF3/IFN-γ/miR-29b positive feedback loop; ECRG4 mediates host response to cutaneous infection by regulating neutrophil recruitment and adhesion receptor expression; 2026 studies demonstrate ECRG4 suppresses breast cancer progression via NFIC/PTEN and SHP2/PI3K/SP1 signaling; MAZ/UHRF1/ECRG4 axis implicated in metastatic colorectal carcinoma; schizophrenia-linked role in dendritic spine nanostructure revealed in mouse models. Functional hypothesis: ECRG4/augurin functions as a secreted signaling peptide with tumor suppressor activity in multiple cancers (esophageal, breast, colorectal) and neuroimmune regulatory functions in choroid plexus (CSF production, blood-brain barrier) and neutrophil-mediated infection response; high choroid plexus expression suggests cerebrospinal fluid signaling role potentially linking metabolic state to brain function. Mechanistic proposal: Secreted augurin peptide may act through GPCR or receptor tyrosine kinase signaling (SPX interaction suggests neuropeptide-like mechanism); cancer suppression via PTEN pathway activation and PI3K/AKT inhibition; epigenetic silencing by DNMT1 and UHRF1 in tumors indicates tumor suppressor status; neutrophil regulation suggests chemokine-like activity; choroid plexus enrichment implies CSF-borne signaling potentially affecting appetite, metabolism, or neuroinflammation; schizophrenia association suggests synaptic development role. Dosage sensitivity (14 CNVs) suggests haploinsufficiency could disrupt tumor suppression or neuroimmune homeostasis. This gene represents a moderately characterized secreted peptide with established cancer suppressor function but incompletely understood receptor systems and physiological substrates.
