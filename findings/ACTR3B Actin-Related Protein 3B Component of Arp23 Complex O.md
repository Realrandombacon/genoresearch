## Description
ACTR3B (Actin Related Protein 3B) is a 418 amino acid actin-family protein that serves as an essential component of the Arp2/3 complex, a seven-subunit molecular machine that nucleates branched actin filament networks critical for cell motility, membrane trafficking, and cytoskeletal dynamics. InterPro analysis identifies canonical actin-fold architecture including nucleotide-binding domain (NBD) of Arp3 and similar proteins (cd10221, residues 7-410) for ATP binding and hydrolysis, actin family signature (IPR004000, PF00022, PTHR11937, SM00268, residues 5-413) defining the actin superfamily, actin/actin-like conserved site (IPR020902, PS01132, residues 111-123) for functional conservation, ATPase nucleotide binding domain superfamily (IPR043129, SSF53067, residues 4-155/162-414), and actin domain structural homology (G3DSA:2.30.36.70, G3DSA:3.30.420.40, G3DSA:3.90.640.10). AlphaFold structure (AF-Q9P1U1-F1) predicts very high confidence (pLDDT 93.1) across the 418 residue sequence, indicating an exceptionally well-folded actin-like protein with stable nucleotide-binding cleft suitable for ATP-dependent actin nucleation. HPA data classifies ACTR3B as an intracellular actin-binding protein involved in cytoskeletal organization, with brain-enriched expression (51.8 nTPM, tissue enhanced), detected in all tissues, cell type-enhanced distribution, and implications in neurological and reproductive disorders. STRING interactions reveal exceptionally tight coupling to the Arp2/3 complex machinery: ACTR2 (Arp2, 0.999) as the partner actin-related protein; ARPC2 (0.982), ARPC5L (0.979), ARPC5 (0.979), ARPC4 (0.977), ARPC3 (0.968), ARPC1A (0.964) as the five complex subunits; WAS (Wiskott-Aldrich syndrome protein, 0.978) and WASL (N-WASP, 0.919) as activators that stimulate Arp2/3-mediated nucleation; and ACTB (Beta-actin, 0.975) as the filament substrate. ClinVar lists 81 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number losses on 7q33-36.3 and 7q34-36.3 (multiple independent CNV events spanning 137-159 Mb), duplications, splice site variants (c.101-10T>G, c.101-76A>T, c.45-172A>G), and intronic deletions (c.101-4del), though no specific monogenic syndrome is defined, suggesting potential roles in neurodevelopmental disorder or male infertility. Literature (20 papers) establishes ACTR3B as a context-dependent regulator: lncRNA AC009022.1 enhances colorectal cancer cell proliferation, migration, and invasion by promoting ACTR3B expression via suppressing miR-497-5p; lncRNA KCNK15-AS1 inhibits pancreatic cancer progression by promoting ACTR3B expression as an RNA-binding protein cofactor; recent microarray analyses identify ACTR3B as a novel cytoskeleton protein involved in spermatogenic cells and Sertoli cells in non-obstructive azoospermia; and ACTR3B functions in actin filament formation and cellular division pathways. As a partially characterized actin regulator with emerging roles in cancer metastasis and male fertility, ACTR3B represents a critical node connecting Arp2/3 complex-mediated actin nucleation to tumor progression and reproductive biology through lncRNA-dependent expression control.
