## Description
PTCD3 (Pentatricopeptide Repeat Domain 3), also known as mitochondrial small ribosomal subunit protein mS39, is a 689-amino acid essential component of the mitochondrial ribosome (mitoribosome) small subunit (UniProt Q96EY7). Despite only 26 publications, it has emerged as a critical node linking mitochondrial translation defects to severe neurodevelopmental disorders and cancer metabolism. Structurally, PTCD3 is dominated by pentatricopeptide repeat (PPR) motifs (IPR002885, PF13812, positions 255-379) and tetratricopeptide-like helical domains (G3DSA:1.25.40.10) that form an extended RNA-binding cradle, consistent with its role in binding single-stranded mitochondrial mRNA at the ribosomal entry channel. AlphaFold predicts a confident structure (pLDDT 79.0) reflecting this stable helical repeat architecture. Functionally, PTCD3 is indispensable for mitochondrial translation initiation; loss-of-function leads to combined oxidative phosphorylation deficiency, manifesting clinically as Leigh syndrome (encephalopathy, lactic acidosis, developmental delay) with 30 pathogenic ClinVar variants documented (including nonsense p.Gln214Ter, p.Ser389Ter, p.Trp477Ter and frameshift p.Leu95fs). STRING interactions confirm exclusive association with the mitoribosome small subunit (MRPS22, MRPS31, DAP3, MRPS2, MRPS5; all scores >0.99). Beyond rare disease, PTCD3 drives oncogenic metabolic reprogramming: epigenetic activation in colorectal cancer promotes glutamine metabolism and metastasis via IGF2BP2-mediated m6A modification of SLC38A2 (ASCT2) mRNA, highlighting a dual role in energy metabolism regulation. HPA data shows ubiquitous expression with mitochondrial localization, consistent with its housekeeping role in oxidative phosphorylation across all tissues.
