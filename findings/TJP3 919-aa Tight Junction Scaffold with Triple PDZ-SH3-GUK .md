## Description
TJP3 (Tight Junction Protein 3, also known as ZO-3; UniProt O95049) is a 919 amino acid modular scaffold protein with 49 publications, representing a partially characterized component of the tight junction complex that integrates membrane organization with nuclear signaling through its triple PDZ domain architecture and intrinsically disordered regions.\n\nSTRUCTURAL ARCHITECTURE: InterPro analysis reveals a canonical MAGUK (Membrane-Associated Guanylate Kinase) family architecture optimized for scaffolding. The N-terminal region contains three tandem PDZ domains: PDZ1 (cd06727/IPR001478, residues 10-91/11-93) for binding C-terminal motifs of transmembrane proteins; PDZ2 (cd06728/IPR001478, residues 193-271/195-272) which forms domain-swapping dimers to cluster receptors; and PDZ3 (cd06729/IPR001478, residues 380-461/380-463) for additional cargo selection. A central SH3 domain (cd12028/IPR001452, residues 476-546/475-549) mediates interactions with cytoskeletal regulators and signaling enzymes. The C-terminal region contains a Guanylate Kinase-like (GUK) domain (G3DSA:3.40.50.300, residues 620-764) that serves as a protein-protein interaction module rather than an enzymatic center. The full-length protein belongs to the Tight Junction Protein ZO family (IPR005417). AlphaFold predicts medium confidence structure (global pLDDT 66.8), indicating a mix of ordered domains (PDZ/SH3/GUK) connected by long intrinsically disordered linkers—consistent with MAGUK proteins that require flexibility to bridge membrane proteins to the actin cytoskeleton and translocate to the nucleus.\n\nINTERACTION NETWORK: STRING-DB identifies high-confidence interactions with core tight junction components and polarity regulators: PATJ (0.996, Polarity-associated protein, multi-PDZ scaffold), TJP1 (0.857, ZO-1, primary tight junction scaffold), OCLN (0.851, Occludin, integral membrane tight junction protein), CLDN7 (0.803, Claudin-7, barrier-forming transmembrane protein), CLDN3 (0.721, Claudin-3), TJP2 (0.721, ZO-2, paralog), CLDN1 (0.699, Claudin-1), CGN (0.569, Cingulin, junctional adaptor), CLDN8 (0.513, Claudin-8), and MARVELD2 (0.489, Tricellulin, tricellular junction protein). This interaction profile establishes TJP3 as a core constituent of the tight junction plaque that links claudins/occludin to the actin cytoskeleton via TJP1/TJP2/PATJ complexes.\n\nEXPRESSION & LOCALIZATION: HPA shows tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in barrier tissues: intestine (40.5 nTPM), stomach (25.5 nTPM), and esophagus (24.6 nTPM). Subcellular localization includes nucleoplasm (nuclear signaling pool), plasma membrane (junctional pool), and cell junctions. Protein class includes predicted intracellular proteins and transporters. RNA distribution is detected in many tissues, consistent with ubiquitous barrier function. Single cell type specificity is cell type-enhanced (epithelial cells).\n\nDISEASE LANDSCAPE: ClinVar contains 21 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains and losses at 19p13.3 (e.g., chr19:3134687-5513059x3, chr19:3501624-5357124x1) and splice site mutations (c.1394-1G>C, c.1060+5G>A). While no specific monogenic disease is yet definitively assigned, the burden of structural variants suggests haploinsufficiency or dosage sensitivity may cause developmental anomalies or barrier defects. Recent literature establishes prominent roles in cancer: (1) TJP3 identified as prognostic marker for radiotherapy/chemoradiotherapy response in oral squamous cell carcinoma (2025, somatic mutations predict outcome); (2) TJP3 promotes T cell immunity escape and chemoresistance in breast cancer by inhibiting anoikis (2023, 10 citations, comprehensive analysis of drug sensitivity); (3) Highly-cited zebrafish study (63 citations) demonstrates Tjp3/zo-3 is critical for epidermal barrier function in embryos, with morpholino knockdown causing severe skin defects. Additional studies show TJP3 translocates to the nucleus to regulate gene expression, linking junctional status to transcriptional programs.\n\nCONSERVATION: Orthologs confirmed in mouse (Q9QXY1, 905 aa) and zebrafish, indicating strong evolutionary constraint on the PDZ-SH3-GUK architecture across vertebrates for conserved barrier function.\n\nFUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: TJP3 functions as a dynamic scaffold that organizes tight junction assembly and transduces junctional signals to the nucleus. At the membrane, TJP3 uses its three PDZ domains to cluster claudins (CLDN1/3/7/8) and occludin (OCLN) into higher-order arrays, while the SH3 domain recruits actin regulators and the GUK domain binds signaling enzymes. This creates a stable barrier preventing paracellular leakage in intestine, stomach, and esophagus. Uniquely among tight junction proteins, TJP3 (like ZO-1/ZO-2) can translocate to the nucleus via its disordered regions, where it may regulate transcription of proliferation or differentiation genes in response to junctional integrity. In cancer, TJP3 dysregulation has dual roles: (1) In oral SCC, somatic mutations alter TJP3 scaffolding, predicting radiotherapy resistance possibly through impaired DNA damage signaling at junctions; (2) In breast cancer, TJP3 overexpression suppresses anoikis (detachment-induced cell death), allowing metastatic spread and immune evasion by maintaining survival signaling despite loss of matrix attachment. The zebrafish phenotype confirms that TJP3 loss disrupts epidermal barrier formation during development, likely through failure to organize claudin networks. Therapeutic strategies targeting TJP3 could sensitize tumors to anoikis or enhance barrier repair in inflammatory bowel disease.
