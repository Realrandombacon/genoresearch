## Description
EXT2 (UniProt Q93063), also known as SOTV or SSMS, encodes a 718 aa type II transmembrane glycosyltransferase functioning as the critical elongation enzyme in heparan sulfate (HS) biosynthesis. InterPro analysis reveals a bipartite catalytic architecture essential for polymerization: an N-terminal Exostosin GT47 domain (IPR040911/PF03016, residues 101-380) responsible for adding GlcNAc residues, and a C-terminal Glycosyl transferase family 64 domain (IPR015338/PF09258, residues 456-701) responsible for adding GlcA residues, both embedded within the larger Exostosin-like family (IPR004263/PTHR48261, residues 78-714) and sharing the canonical nucleotide-diphospho-sugar transferase fold (IPR029044/SSF53448/G3DSA:3.90.550.10, residues 452-707). AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (pLDDT 87.5) indicating a rigid, well-folded enzymatic core where the two distinct catalytic domains are likely arranged to facilitate processive transfer of alternating sugar units without dissociation from the growing chain. STRING interactions define an extremely tight functional module centered on the hetero-oligomeric EXT complex: EXT1 (0.999, obligate partner forming the active heterocomplex), ALX4 (0.912, transcription factor linked to parietal foramina and HS regulation), ACCS/ACCSL (0.873/0.818, acyl-CoA synthetases potentially linking lipid metabolism to glycosylation), HS2ST1 (0.783, heparan sulfate 2-O-sulfotransferase acting downstream), NDST2/NDST1 (0.757/0.505, N-deacetylase/N-sulfotransferases modifying the chain), and TRAP1 (0.760, ER chaperone). HPA classifies EXT2 as low tissue specificity with detection in all tissues, low cancer specificity, strictly localized to the Golgi apparatus, reflecting its ubiquitous housekeeping role in generating HS proteoglycans required for extracellular matrix integrity and growth factor signaling. ClinVar lists a massive burden of 305 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including frameshifts (p.Met174fs, p.Asn637fs, p.His88fs, p.Gly225fs, p.Phe306fs), nonsense mutations (p.Trp573Ter, p.Tyr114Ter), splice site defects, and large indels, directly causing Hereditary Multiple Exostoses (HME/Osteochondroma), a skeletal disorder characterized by benign bone tumors, with some variants also linked to intellectual disability and epilepsy suggesting broader neurodevelopmental roles for HS. Functional hypothesis: EXT2 operates as the processive engine of HS chain elongation through a coordinated mechanism: (1) Complex assembly - Forms a stable hetero-oligomer with EXT1 (and potentially EXT3) in the Golgi where EXT1 may initiate chains and EXT2 drives elongation, or they function cooperatively with distinct but coupled activities; (2) Dual catalytic cycle - The GT47 domain transfers GlcNAc from UDP-GlcNAc while the GT64 domain transfers GlcA from UDP-GlcA in alternating fashion, utilizing the conserved DXD motifs in both domains to coordinate Mn2+/Mg2+ cofactors; (3) Processivity - The rigid structure (pLDDT 87.5) suggests a clamp-like mechanism holding the growing polysaccharide chain to prevent premature release; (4) Signaling modulation - By controlling HS chain length and sulfation patterns (via coupling to HS2ST/NDST enzymes), EXT2 regulates the gradient formation of morphogens like Hedgehog, Wnt, and FGF, explaining skeletal phenotypes when disrupted; (5) Tumor suppression - Loss of EXT2 leads to shortened HS chains unable to sequester growth factors, resulting in hyperactive signaling pathways driving osteochondroma formation and potential malignant transformation to chondrosarcoma; (6) Neurodevelopment - Specific variants causing epilepsy/intellectual disability suggest HS is critical for synaptic organization or neuronal migration. Conservation is absolute across metazoans (Mouse P70428, Drosophila Q9Y169, Bovine O77783) indicating ancient origin of the HS biosynthetic machinery. Dark aspects requiring investigation include: precise structural arrangement of GT47/GT64 domains relative to each other and the membrane anchor, mechanism of substrate channeling between the two active sites, stoichiometry of the EXT1/EXT2/EXT3 complex, regulatory post-translational modifications controlling activity, specific HS epitopes generated by EXT2 vs EXT1, therapeutic strategies to restore HS synthesis in HME patients, role in viral entry (HS as co-receptor), and genotype-phenotype correlations explaining variable expressivity in HME families.
