## Description
GIMAP8 (GTPase IMAP Family Member 8) is a 665-amino acid intracellular protein belonging to the GIMAP/IAN family of immunity-associated nucleotide-binding proteins, characterized by an unusual triple-domain architecture containing three tandem AIG1-type G domains (IPR006703 at positions 8-210, 245-435, 436-653; PF04548 AIG1 family at 11-204, 248-430, 439-653) with P-loop NTPase hydrolase activity (IPR027417, G3DSA:3.40.50.300), suggesting complex GTP-dependent conformational switching and potential regulatory functions beyond simple hydrolysis. STRING interactions reveal tight clustering within the GIMAP family: GIMAP6 (0.913), GIMAP4 (0.911), GIMAP7 (0.895), GIMAP5 (0.825), GIMAP1 (0.710), consistent with known roles in T-cell survival and lymphocyte homeostasis; unexpected partners include SRP54 (0.786) and SRP68 (0.554) from the signal recognition particle, hinting at potential involvement in co-translational targeting or ER-associated processes. HPA data shows tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in lymphoid tissue (38.2 nTPM), cancer-enhanced RNA patterns, group-enriched single-cell specificity, and classification as a predicted intracellular protein. ClinVar contains 69 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains and losses spanning 7q33-36.3 regions, though specific monogenic disease phenotypes remain under characterization (significance marked '?' for most); the genomic location overlaps with regions implicated in hematologic malignancies. AlphaFold structure (AF-Q8ND71-F1) predicts high confidence folding (pLDDT 81.8) consistent with a stable multi-domain GTPase. With 27 papers, GIMAP8 represents a 'gray' gene transitioning from dark: recent studies (2025) identify it as a prognostic factor in lung adenocarcinoma correlating with immune infiltration, functioning through Wnt/β-catenin pathway regulation (synergistic with oxymatarine for tumor suppression), while earlier work (2020) identified KMT2A/GIMAP8 fusions in pediatric acute undifferentiated leukemia and tumor-induced expression as an ovarian cancer marker. The mechanistic hypothesis posits that GIMAP8 functions as a lymphoid-specific GTPase switch modulating T-cell survival signals and cancer cell proliferation via β-catenin stabilization, potentially competing with or regulating other GIMAP family members in apoptotic control, making it a candidate immunotherapy target in hematologic and solid tumors.
