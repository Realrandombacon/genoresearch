## Description
SLC26A10P (Solute Carrier Family 26 Member 10 Pseudogene, also SLC26A10) is a 563 amino acid membrane protein (UniProt Q8NG04) with a well-folded transporter structure (AlphaFold pLDDT 76.6, high confidence), characteristic of the SLC26 anion transporter family despite its pseudogene annotation.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals SLC26A10P contains canonical SLC26 family domains: a SLC26A/SulP transporter domain (IPR001902/IPR011547/PF00916, residues 37-411; PTHR11814) conferring transmembrane transport (GO:0055085) and membrane localization (GO:0016020), plus a C-terminal STAS domain (IPR002645/IPR036513/PF01740/PS50801, residues 412-541; G3DSA:3.30.750.24/SSF52091, residues 406-544). The STAS domain (Sulfate Transporter and Anti-Sigma factor antagonist) plays roles in transport activity regulation and proposed general NTP binding function (cd07042, residues 412-534).\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals SLC26A10P functions within the anion transporter network: strongest interactions with ARHGEF25 (0.517, Rho guanine nucleotide exchange factor), MT-ND4 (0.506, mitochondrial NADH dehydrogenase subunit 4), LYRM4 (0.497, LYR motif-containing protein 4), SLC4A3 (0.463, anion exchanger 3), CLCNKA (0.442, chloride channel Ka), MRE11 (0.436, DNA repair protein), TMEM253 (0.427), SLC26A11 (0.418, solute carrier family 26 member 11), SLC4A9 (0.418, anion exchanger 9), and EPS8L1 (0.413). The SLC26A11, SLC4A3, SLC4A9, and CLCNKA interactions support anion transport function.\n\nHPA expression could not be resolved, consistent with pseudogene annotation suggesting low or absent transcription. However, the gene is predicted to enable inorganic anion transmembrane transporter activity and organic anion transmembrane transporter activity, involved in chloride and sulfate transmembrane transport, located in plasma membrane.\n\nClinVar documents no pathogenic variants, consistent with pseudogene status where mutations would not cause disease. However, the well-conserved structure suggests potential regulatory or functional roles.\n\nConservation is evident from mouse ortholog (Q5EBI0, 676 aa), dog ortholog (A0A8I3RXF3, 694 aa; A0A8C0MJZ7, 475 aa), and zebrafish ortholog (A0A8M1QQZ8, 690 aa), indicating the gene is functional in other species despite human pseudogene annotation.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: SLC26A10P represents either a human-specific pseudogenization event or a misannotated functional gene. The well-folded structure with intact STAS and transporter domains suggests potential residual function as a sulfate/chloride anion transporter or regulatory pseudogene producing non-coding RNA. The STAS domain typically regulates transport activity through nucleotide binding and protein-protein interactions. Interaction with SLC26A11 and other anion transporters (SLC4A3, SLC4A9, CLCNKA) suggests roles in cellular anion homeostasis, potentially in kidney, brain, or other tissues requiring chloride/sulfate balance. Conservation in mouse, dog, and zebrafish indicates ancestral function that may be partially retained in humans through alternative splicing or regulatory mechanisms. The pseudogene annotation warrants re-evaluation given the structural integrity and cross-species conservation.
