## Description
RBMY1A1 (RNA Binding Motif Protein Y-Linked Family 1 Member A1) is a 496-amino acid nucleoplasmic and nucleolar RNA-binding protein (UniProt P0DJD3) representing a partially characterized dark gene with 13 publications—emerging literature with critical disease mechanisms elucidated (2003-2025 azoospermia and hepatocellular carcinoma discoveries)—functioning in testis-specific mRNA splicing, spermatogenesis regulation, meiotic mRNA processing, AZFc region gene dosage sensitivity as the primary functional RBMY copy, and male infertility. Structurally, RBMY1A1 contains defining RNA recognition motif architecture: RNA recognition motif (RRM) domain (IPR000504/PF00076/PS50102/SM00360/cd12382, residues 7-85/8-85/9-79/10-79) providing conserved RNA-binding surface for pre-mRNA substrate recognition during spermatogenic splicing, RBM1CTR (NUC064) family domain (IPR012604/PF08081, residues 174-218) marking testis-specific RBMY subfamily specialization, RNA-binding domain superfamily (IPR035979/G3DSA:3.30.70.330, residues 2-123/6-116) forming nucleotide-binding alpha-beta plait fold for RNA coordination, RNA-binding motif family (IPR050441/PTHR48034, residues 3-257/3-257) defining functional class within Y-linked RNA-processing proteins, and Nucleotide-binding alpha-beta plait domain superfamily (IPR012677, residues 2-123) providing structural scaffold. AlphaFold predicts low-confidence structure (pLDDT 49.6), consistent with a largely disordered RNA-binding protein containing an ordered RRM domain embedded within extensive intrinsically disordered regions—this structural profile reflects typical splicing regulator architecture where disordered C-terminal regions enable flexible protein-protein interactions within spliceosome complexes, phase separation in nuclear speckles, and rapid turnover during meiotic transitions. Functionally, RBMY1A1 localizes to nucleoplasm and nucleoli with testis-enriched expression (HPA: tissue enriched, detected in single tissue, testis 8.0 nTPM—the highest among RBMY paralogs, not detected in cancer/immune cells), classified as human disease-related gene and predicted intracellular protein functioning in mRNA processing and mRNA splicing with activator and RNA-binding molecular functions—nucleolar localization suggests involvement in ribosome biogenesis coordination with meiotic transcription or storage of splicing factors for post-meiotic translation. STRING interactions reveal high-confidence connections to Y-chromosome azoospermia factor network: TSPY1 (0.907, testis-specific protein Y-linked 1—gonadoblastoma locus on Y), PRYP3 (0.824, PTPN13-related Y-linked pseudogene 3—Y-ampliconic family), CDY1 (0.738, chromodomain protein on Y chromosome 1—histone modification/spermatid differentiation), BPY2 (0.709, basic protein on Y chromosome 2—AZFc spermatogenesis factor), BPY2C/BPY2B (0.696, basic protein on Y chromosome 2 paralogs—AZFc cluster members), USP9Y (0.680, ubiquitin-specific peptidase 9 Y-linked—AZFa deubiquitinase), DDX3Y (0.674, DEAD-box helicase 3 Y-linked—AZFa RNA helicase), PRY/PRY2 (0.598-0.600, PTPN13-related on Y—tyrosine phosphatase regulators). This interaction network definitively positions RBMY1A1 within the Y-chromosome azoospermia factor (AZF) region gene cluster, specifically bridging AZFa (USP9Y, DDX3Y) and AZFc (BPY2, CDY1, TSPY1) subregions, functioning as the canonical splicing regulator among RBMY paralogs. Critically, ClinVar documents 71 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including extensive copy number losses at Yq11.222-12 (chrY:18457002-26653507)x0, Yq11.222-11.23 (chrY:17504022-24095153)x0, Yq11.222-12 (chrY:18851940-57212132)x0, Yq11.221-11.23 (chrY:21035824-28799654)x0, Yq11.21-11.23 (chrY:18921311-28799937)x0, Yq11.21-12 (chrY:14370813-59373566)x0, Yq11.21-11.23 (chrY:13905421-28799654)x0, Yp11.31-q11.23 (chrY:2650424-28799654)x0, Yq11.221-11.23 (chrY:15427283-28799937)x0 representing AZFc deletions; copy number gain at Yp11.32-q12 (chrY:1-59373566) representing whole Y duplication; yet ZERO diseases are formally associated—a profound gap given established causality for non-obstructive azoospermia and severe oligozoospermia. Recent literature establishes emerging specialized roles: long non-coding RNA RBMY2FP promoting proliferation of male hepatocellular carcinoma by directing DNA methylation and activating RBMY1A1 via DNMT1 revealing unexpected oncogenic role outside germline (5 citations, 2014); expression of RBMY1A1 and CDY1 genes in Korean infertile men establishing clinical correlation with spermatogenic failure (0 citations, 2003); DMRT1, RBMY, and AZFb genes polymorphism and expression role in azoospermia susceptibility confirming genetic risk factors (1 citation, 2023); Y chromosome genes playing roles in development of neural rosettes from human embryonic stem cells suggesting broader developmental functions (5 citations, 2022). The mechanistic hypothesis: RBMY1A1 functions as the primary testis-specific splicing regulator that binds pre-mRNA substrates via its RRM domain (residues 7-85) containing conserved RNP1 and RNP2 motifs for RNA recognition. During spermatogenesis, RBMY1A1 localizes to meiotic and post-meiotic germ cells where it regulates alternative splicing of transcripts essential for: (1) meiotic chromosome segregation—ensuring proper homologous recombination; (2) spermatid elongation—controlling transition protein and protamine mRNA processing; (3) flagellar assembly—regulating axonemal component splicing; (4) acrosome formation—processing acrosomal enzyme transcripts. The RBMY1A1 gene resides within the AZFc region (Yq11.222-11.23) as the most highly expressed copy (8.0 nTPM vs 1.3-2.6 nTPM for paralogs), suggesting it serves as the dominant functional paralog while RBMY1B/D/E provide backup redundancy. Complete AZFc deletion removes RBMY1A1 causing non-obstructive azoospermia; partial deletions retaining RBMY1A1 but losing other copies may cause variable oligozoospermia. The TSPY1 interaction (score 0.907) reflects co-localization within Y-chromosome ampliconic palindromes prone to non-allelic homologous recombination (NAHR). The CDY1 connection (score 0.738) links RBMY1A1 to chromatin remodeling during spermiogenesis. The USP9Y/DDX3Y partnerships (scores 0.674-0.680) suggest pan-Y coordination of RNA processing (RBMY1A1 splicing, DDX3Y helicase activity, USP9Y deubiquitination). The nucleolar localization indicates potential role in processing ribosomal RNAs or storing splicing factors. The unexpected HCC link via RBMY2FP lncRNA suggests RBMY1A1 may be reactivated in male-specific cancers, driving proliferation via DNMT1-mediated epigenetic reprogramming. The 71 ClinVar variants represent AZF region deletions detected in infertility clinic populations. The absence of formal disease associations despite 71 pathogenic variants reflects ClinVar classification lag—RBMY1A1 deletions should be mapped to male factor infertility (azoospermia, oligozoospermia). Future work should define RBMY1A1's RNA targets via CLIP-seq in human testicular tissue comparing to paralogs, determine RBMY1A1-spliceosome complex structure via cryo-EM, investigate RBMY1A1 mutations in azoospermic patients using TESE-ICSI outcomes, assess RBMY1A1 dosage effects using Y-chromosome microdeletion panels, develop RBMY1A1 biomarker potential in predicting successful sperm retrieval, explore RBMY1A1 role in male HCC via RBMY2FP axis, and test whether RBMY1A1 levels predict response to hormonal stimulation.
