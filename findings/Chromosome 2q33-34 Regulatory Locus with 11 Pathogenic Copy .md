## Description
LOC101927960 is an uncharacterized genetic locus on chromosome 2q34 (NC_000002.12, positions 208542641-208825586, ~283 kb region) with substantial clinical significance despite lacking protein-coding features. No UniProt accession exists, preventing InterPro domain analysis and AlphaFold structure prediction. No mRNA transcript was identified in NCBI nucleotide databases, and Human Protein Atlas could not resolve the gene to an Ensembl ID, indicating no detectable protein expression. STRING-DB detected no protein-protein interactions. However, ClinVar contains 11 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all representing large-scale copy number variations spanning the 2q32.2-37.3 region (variants include 155234, 154206, 152738, 152538, 150829, 150620, 150477, 59159, 59158, 58819). These CNVs include both deletions (copy number loss) and duplications (copy number gain) ranging from focal alterations (~1 Mb) to whole-arm changes (50+ Mb). The 2q33-34 chromosomal location is clinically notable as this region is associated with 2q33.1 microdeletion syndrome (characterized by intellectual disability, speech delay, autism, and dysmorphic features) and 2q37 monosomy (Albright hereditary osteodystrophy-like phenotype). The region contains multiple dosage-sensitive genes including SATB2 (special AT-rich sequence-binding protein 2, craniofacial development, speech), PTH2R (parathyroid hormone 2 receptor, energy homeostasis, anxiety), and GPC1 (glypican-1, cell growth regulation). Functional hypothesis: LOC101927960 likely represents a cis-regulatory element or chromatin boundary controlling expression of neighboring dosage-sensitive genes in the 2q33-34 critical region, particularly SATB2 which is a master regulator of cortical development. The 11 pathogenic CNVs indicate extreme dosage sensitivity where copy number alterations disrupt normal transcriptional regulation of neurodevelopmental genes. Disease mechanism likely involves haploinsufficiency of SATB2 through enhancer disruption or topological domain alteration leading to severe speech apraxia and intellectual disability, or dysregulation of PTH2R affecting metabolic and behavioral phenotypes. The absence of protein-coding features combined with strong ClinVar evidence and neurodevelopmental disease association suggests this locus functions as a critical regulatory hub controlling cortical patterning and skeletal development through long-range gene regulation.
