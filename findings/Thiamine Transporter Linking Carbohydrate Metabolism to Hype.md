## Description
SLC35F3 encodes a 421 aa solute carrier family member (UniProt Q8IY50) functioning as an intracellular thiamine transporter critical for carbohydrate metabolism. InterPro analysis reveals canonical EamA transporter domain architecture (IPR000620, PF00892; aa 68-222), SLC35F3/F4 family membership (IPR026505; aa 50-395), and EmrE multidrug transporter superfamily features (IPR037185, SSF103481; aa 154-226), consistent with nucleotide sugar transport function. AlphaFold predicts a confident structure (pLDDT 75.3), indicating a well-folded transmembrane transporter. HPA shows brain-enriched expression (15.5 nTPM, tissue-enriched) with nucleoli and Golgi apparatus localization, cancer-enhanced expression patterns, and immune cell enrichment, suggesting roles in neuronal metabolism and tumor biology. STRING interactions reveal connections to related nucleotide sugar transporters (SLC35C2 0.552, SLC35E3 0.475, SLC35F2 0.460, SLC35C1 0.441, SLC35A1 0.431, SLC35D1 0.429), supporting functional clustering within the SLC35 family. ClinVar contains 45 pathogenic variants (mostly copy number gains/losses on 1q42), though specific disease associations remain undefined. Literature (30 papers) reveals emerging mechanistic insights: SLC35F3 mediates intracellular thiamine transport essential for glycolysis and TCA cycle function, with genetic variations affecting hypertension risk in Korean and Chinese Han populations (10-15 citations each), carbohydrate intake interactions influencing metabolic syndrome incidence (7 citations), and enigmatic roles in lung adenocarcinoma progression (1 citation). As a thiamine pyrophosphate transporter, SLC35F3 likely regulates cellular thiamine availability for thiamine-dependent enzymes (pyruvate dehydrogenase, α-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase, transketolase), linking carbohydrate metabolism to blood pressure regulation and cancer metabolism. The brain enrichment suggests neuronal thiamine homeostasis roles, while cancer-enhanced expression indicates metabolic reprogramming in tumors. The 45 ClinVar variants suggest potential disease associations requiring phenotypic characterization. SLC35F3 represents an emerging therapeutic target for hypertension, metabolic syndrome, and cancer, though precise transport substrates, regulatory mechanisms, and disease-causing mutations require further characterization.
