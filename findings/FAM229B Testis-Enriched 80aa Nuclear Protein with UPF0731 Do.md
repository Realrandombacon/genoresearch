## Description
FAM229B encodes a small 80-amino acid nuclear protein (UniProt Q4G0N7) belonging to the UPF0731 protein family (Pfam PF14982, residues 3-80) and FAM229 conserved domain family (InterPro IPR028025, residues 1-80). The protein shows testis-specific expression with exceptionally high RNA levels (427.8 nTPM in testis per HPA), classified as tissue-enriched and cancer-enhanced. Subcellular localization includes nucleoplasm, nucleoli, and Golgi apparatus, suggesting roles in nuclear RNA processing or chromatin regulation.

STRING interaction network reveals 10 high-confidence partners, led by LRCOL1 (score 0.702), NPIPB9 (0.571), SPATA24 (0.554), MAGEE2 (0.543), and FSCB (0.518). Several partners are testis-expressed or chromatin-associated proteins, supporting a nuclear function. The interaction profile suggests FAM229B may function as a small scaffold or adaptor protein in testis-specific nuclear complexes, potentially involved in spermatogenesis or meiotic chromatin remodeling.

ClinVar documents 18 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number losses and gains spanning 6q21 region encompassing FAM229B. While no single nucleotide variants are catalogued, the CNV burden indicates haploinsufficiency or dosage sensitivity, consistent with essential function in development or reproduction.

Orthologs identified in Rattus norvegicus (B0BND4, 80 aa, 100% length conservation) and Bos taurus (Q0D252, 80 aa) demonstrate strong evolutionary conservation across mammals. The small size, conserved domain architecture, testis enrichment, and disease-associated variants collectively suggest FAM229B functions as a conserved nuclear regulatory factor required for normal spermatogenesis, with dosage sensitivity contributing to developmental phenotypes when disrupted.
