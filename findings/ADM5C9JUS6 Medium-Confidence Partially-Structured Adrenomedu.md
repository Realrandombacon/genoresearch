## Description
ADM5 (Adrenomedullin 5, also known as AM5, C19orf76) is a 153 amino acid secreted protein (UniProt C9JUS6) with medium-confidence partially-structured architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 51.0), characteristic of peptide hormone family members that function through receptor binding and G protein-coupled receptor (GPCR) signaling pathways. The protein belongs to the adrenomedullin-regulatory peptide family (IPR051665, residues 16-76; PTHR23414, residues 16-76) and is located on chromosome 19q13.33. ADM5 is predicted to be involved in adenylate cyclase-activating G protein-coupled receptor signaling pathway, positive regulation of heart rate, and regulation of urine volume, located in extracellular region. These interactions confirm ADM5 functions within the adrenomedullin peptide hormone network, with ADM2 as the highest-confidence partner (0.790) reflecting family homology and potential shared receptor usage. ADM interaction (0.517) links ADM5 to the canonical adrenomedullin peptide. MAP2K2 interaction (0.481) suggests downstream MAPK/ERK signaling pathway activation. PRR22, TTC9, RASEF, and TMEM65 interactions (0.640-0.404) indicate additional signaling or regulatory connections.\n\nHPA expression reveals ADM5 has low tissue specificity with expression detected in many tissues, consistent with peptide hormone functions that may act in paracrine or endocrine manners across multiple organ systems. Subcellular localization to extracellular region confirms its role as a secreted peptide hormone. The protein is classified as a predicted secreted protein.\n\nClinVar documents 12 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including extensive copy number gains (19q13.33-13.41 amplifications 48.9-53.1 Mb, 19q13.33-13.43 amplifications 49.6-57.6 Mb, 19p13.3-q13.43 whole chromosome arm amplifications 0.26-58.9 Mb) and losses (19q13.33 deletions 50.1-50.2 Mb). MAP2K2 interaction (0.481) suggests downstream MAPK/ERK pathway activation for cellular responses. Extracellular localization and secreted protein classification position ADM5 as a circulating or paracrine peptide hormone. Low tissue specificity with detection in many tissues indicates broad physiological roles. 19q13.33 CNVs cause ADM5 haploinsufficiency or overexpression, potentially disrupting peptide hormone signaling and causing cardiovascular, renal, or developmental phenotypes. ADM5 represents a candidate gene for 19q deletion/duplication syndrome phenotypes and may serve as a therapeutic target for cardiovascular and renal disorders.
