## Description
VWCE (Von Willebrand Factor C and EGF Domain-Containing Protein, UniProt Q96DN2) is a 955 amino acid multi-domain protein functioning as a critical lysosomal scaffold that coordinates mTORC1 signaling through KICSTOR-GATOR1 complex recruitment. With 25 literature references (partially characterized with major 2023 mechanistic breakthrough), VWCE represents an emerging regulator of nutrient sensing and cell growth control with additional roles in BMP signaling and cancer biology. The protein contains extensive modular architecture: 3 EGF-like calcium-binding domains (IPR001881, aa 142-180/181-219/220-262), 3 VWFC domains (IPR001007, aa 384-441/443-496/499-560), EGF_CA domains (cd00054, aa 142-172/181-212/220-255), laminin superfamily domains (G3DSA:2.10.25.10, aa 142-182/186-223/224-263), complement module domains (G3DSA:2.10.70.10, aa 443-476), and EGF-like aspartate/asparagine hydroxylation sites (IPR000152) indicating post-translational modification capacity. AlphaFold predicts a medium-confidence structure (pLDDT 62.7) across all 955 residues, consistent with a multi-domain scaffold protein containing both structured domains (EGF, VWFC) and flexible linker regions enabling conformational adaptability. STRING interactions reveal a functionally coherent BMP/TGF-β signaling network: FAU (ribosomal protein S30, 0.806), BMP2 (bone morphogenetic protein 2, 0.680), BMPR2 (BMP receptor type 2, 0.678), BMPR1A (0.672), BMP7 (0.672), GDF5 (growth differentiation factor 5, 0.617), VWC2 (von Willebrand factor C domain-containing protein 2, 0.601), TWSG1 (twisted gastrulation BMP signaling modulator 1, 0.519), VWF (von Willebrand factor, 0.418), and BMP6 (0.400) - this interaction cluster confirms VWCE's membership in the VWC domain protein family regulating BMP ligand-receptor interactions. HPA classifies VWCE as a predicted intracellular/secreted protein localized to nucleoplasm, vesicles, and cytosol (consistent with lysosomal localization), with tissue-enhanced RNA expression highest in liver (16.3 nTPM) and ovary (9.1 nTPM), suggesting specialized roles in hepatic metabolism and ovarian function. ClinVar documents 11 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants at chromosome 11p11.12-q13.1, including copy number gains (11p11.12-q12.2, 11q12.1-13.3, 11p11.12-q13.1, 11q12.2-13.5, 11p11.2-q12.2, 11p13-q25, 11q12.1-12.3, 11p15.5-q25), deletions, and a nonsense mutation (c.1633C>T, p.Arg545Ter) truncating the protein within VWFC domains, suggesting VWCE haploinsufficiency or dominant-negative effects may cause disease though specific syndromes remain undefined. The landmark 2023 paper (9 citations) identified VWCE as a lysosomal scaffold that modulates amino acid-dependent mTORC1 signaling by coordinating with KICSTOR (KICSTOR complex) to recruit GATOR1 (GAP activity toward Rags 1) to lysosomes, positioning VWCE as a critical node in nutrient sensing and cell growth control. Functional hypothesis: VWCE functions as a multi-domain lysosomal scaffold that: (1) recruits GATOR1 complex to lysosomal surfaces via KICSTOR interaction, enabling mTORC1 inhibition during amino acid starvation; (2) modulates BMP signaling through VWC domain-mediated ligand sequestration or receptor complex assembly (similar to VWC2, TWSG1); (3) serves as a calcium-dependent adhesion/signaling molecule via EGF-like domains. The VWFC domains (also found in VWF, COMP, BMPER) mediate protein-protein interactions critical for complex assembly. Disease mechanism: The 11 ClinVar variants suggest VWCE dosage sensitivity. Haploinsufficiency (deletions, p.Arg545Ter truncation) may impair mTORC1 regulation, leading to constitutive mTOR activation and metabolic dysregulation. Overexpression (CNV gains) could hyperactivate GATOR1 recruitment, suppressing mTORC1 and impairing cell growth. The liver enrichment suggests VWCE dysfunction may contribute to hepatic metabolic disorders, NAFLD/NASH, or hepatocellular carcinoma. The BMP interaction network suggests potential roles in skeletal development, ovarian function, and tissue repair. Cancer relevance: The 2020-2021 breast cancer studies (3-8 citations each) identify VWCE as a tumor suppressor where decreased VWCE expression correlates with poor prognosis and altered immune infiltrates, consistent with mTORC1 dysregulation driving tumor progression. Therapeutic implication: VWCE represents a potential target for mTOR-related disorders, metabolic diseases, and cancers. Small molecules modulating VWCE-KICSTOR-GATOR1 interactions could fine-tune mTORC1 activity more precisely than direct mTOR inhibitors (rapalogs). The VWC domains offer opportunities for BMP pathway modulation in skeletal disorders.
