## Description
SLU7 (SLU7 Homolog, Splicing Factor; UniProt O95391) is a 586 amino acid essential pre-mRNA splicing factor with 86 publications, representing a moderately characterized spliceosomal component with emerging oncogenic roles in cancer immune evasion, ferroptosis resistance, and alternative splicing regulation.\n\nSTRUCTURAL ARCHITECTURE: InterPro analysis reveals specialized splicing factor architecture optimized for step II catalytic activation. The N-terminal region contains a SLU7_N domain (PF30379, residues 115-140) of unknown specific function but conserved across SLU7 orthologs. The central core comprises a Prp18-interacting domain (IPR021715/PF11708, residues 162-434) that physically bridges the step I to step II transition by contacting PRPF18 during catalytic rearrangement. The full-length SLU7 family domain (IPR039974/PTHR12942, residues 26-573/26-573) encompasses the functional unit with GO annotations for pre-mRNA 3'-splice site binding (GO:0030628) and mRNA splicing via spliceosome (GO:0000398). AlphaFold predicts confident structure (global pLDDT 75.2), indicating a well-folded splicing factor with stable interaction surfaces—typical of essential spliceosomal proteins that must maintain precise geometry during catalytic transitions.\n\nINTERACTION NETWORK: STRING-DB identifies exclusively ultra-high-confidence interactions with core spliceosomal components: PRPF8 (0.999, U5 snRNP 220kDa protein, central spliceosome scaffold), CWC22 (0.999, CWC22 homolog, exon junction complex assembly), PRPF18 (0.999, pre-mRNA processing factor 18, step II catalysis), CDC40 (0.999, cell division cycle 40, spliceosome disassembly), SNRNP200 (0.998, U5 snRNP 200kDa helicase), SF3B2 (0.997, splicing factor 3b subunit 2, branch point recognition), CDC5L (0.997, cell division cycle 5-like, NineTeen Complex scaffold), PRPF3 (0.997, U4/U6 snRNP 90kDa protein), plus medium-confidence RNA helicases DHX38 (0.771) and DHX8 (0.576). This interaction profile establishes SLU7 as an integral step II spliceosome component positioned at the catalytic core alongside PRPF8, coordinating 3'-splice site selection and exon ligation through the NineTeen Complex (CDC5L-CDC40) and U5 snRNP machinery.\n\nEXPRESSION & LOCALIZATION: HPA shows ubiquitous expression detected in all tissues with low tissue and cancer specificity, consistent with fundamental requirements for constitutive splicing across all cell types. Subcellular localization includes nucleoplasm (diffuse nuclear pool) and nuclear speckles (splicing factor storage/assembly sites). Molecular function is annotated as mRNA processing and splicing. Classified as an essential protein with cell type-enhanced single cell specificity.\n\nDISEASE LANDSCAPE: ClinVar contains 19 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number losses at 5q33.2-34, 5q33.3-34, 5q33.3-35.1 encompassing SLU7, plus copy number gains at 5q33.1-35.2, 5q32-35.3, 5q33.3-35.1, plus the synonymous coding variant c.1392T>C (p.Val464=) within the Prp18-interacting domain that may affect splicing efficiency or protein stability. No monogenic disease is yet definitively assigned, but 5q CNVs cause developmental anomalies and myelodysplastic syndrome features (5q- syndrome). Recent 2024-2025 literature establishes prominent oncogenic roles: THRAP3 promotes ferroptosis resistance in acute myeloid leukemia through SLU7-mediated alternative splicing of GIT2 (G protein-coupled receptor kinase interacting ArfGAP 2), generating pro-survival isoforms (2025); SLU7 identified as pan-cancer therapeutic target with broad tumor dependencies (2025); G3BP1-SLU7 axis downregulates MHC-I expression via PI3K/Akt activation in bladder cancer, enabling immune evasion from cytotoxic T cells (2023, 29 citations); caspase-mediated cleavage of SLU7 compromises nonsense-mediated decay (NMD) activity during hepatocarcinogenesis, permitting oncogenic transcript accumulation (2024, 3 citations).\n\nCONSERVATION: Orthologs confirmed in mouse (Q8BHJ9, 585 aa), rat (Q80ZG5, 586 aa), bovine (Q3ZBE5, 586 aa), and yeast Saccharomyces cerevisiae (Q02775, 382 aa), indicating strong evolutionary constraint on the Prp18-interacting core across eukaryotes despite length variation.\n\nFUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: SLU7 functions as an essential step II splicing factor that stabilizes 3'-splice site selection during the catalytic transition from step I (branching) to step II (exon ligation). After step I completes, SLU7 binds the 3'-splice site via its Prp18-interacting domain, positioning the 3'-OH of the 5' exon for nucleophilic attack on the 3'-splice site phosphodiester bond. The PRPF8 interaction anchors SLU7 within the U5 snRNP catalytic core, while CDC5L-CDC40 recruitment links SLU7 to the NineTeen Complex that proofreads splice site geometry. In cancer, SLU7 is hijacked to drive oncogenic alternative splicing programs: (1) In AML, THRAP3 recruits SLU7 to GIT2 pre-mRNA, generating truncated isoforms that resist ferroptosis by altering lipid metabolism; (2) In bladder cancer, G3BP1-SLU7 complexes activate PI3K/Akt signaling, downregulating MHC-I antigen presentation and enabling T-cell evasion; (3) In hepatocellular carcinoma, caspase cleavage inactivates SLU7, compromising NMD surveillance and permitting accumulation of aberrant transcripts encoding oncoproteins. The c.1392T>C variant may subtly alter splicing kinetics without changing protein sequence. Therapeutic targeting of SLU7-PRPF8 or SLU7-PRPF18 interfaces could disrupt oncogenic splicing while sparing constitutive splicing.
