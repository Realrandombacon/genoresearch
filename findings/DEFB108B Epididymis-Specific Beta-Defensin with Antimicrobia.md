## Description
DEFB108B (Defensin Beta 108B, alias hBD-8) is a 73-amino acid secreted antimicrobial peptide belonging to the beta-defensin family, specifically expressed in the epididymis where it provides innate immune protection against ascending infections. With only 3 publications, this gene remains a 'very dark' member of the extensively clustered defensin locus on chromosome 11q13.4. The protein contains a canonical beta-defensin domain (IPR025933, PF13841, residues 28-56) characterized by a conserved triple-disulfide bridge motif essential for structural stability and membrane-pore formation. Unlike many defensins expressed in neutrophils or epithelial barriers, DEFB108B shows strict tissue enrichment in the epididymis (12.1 nTPM), suggesting a specialized role in sterilizing spermatozoa during maturation and preventing orchitis.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals a tight co-expression and functional network with other epididymal/testicular defensins, including DEFB106B (score 0.923), DEFB105A (0.903), DEFB107A (0.872), and DEFB121 (0.786), indicating a coordinated 'defensin cloud' strategy for broad-spectrum microbial coverage in the male reproductive tract. The AlphaFold structure predicts a compact fold (pLDDT 69.4) typical of defensins, with the structured core containing the cysteine-rich motif flanked by flexible N-terminal regions that may facilitate initial electrostatic attraction to bacterial membranes. ClinVar reports 5 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains spanning the 11q cluster, though no specific monogenic disease is yet assigned. Given the critical role of epididymal defensins in preventing infection-induced infertility, DEFB108B deficiency or dysregulation likely predisposes to chronic epididymitis, obstructive azoospermia, or increased susceptibility to sexually transmitted pathogens. The lack of somatic cancer expression further supports its restricted physiological role in reproductive immunity rather than general host defense.
