## Description
LOC100506869 is an uncharacterized genomic locus on chromosome 12q14.1 (genomic coordinates: 58591701-58812668 on NC_000012.12, spanning approximately 221 kb). This region lacks protein-coding evidence (no UniProt entry, no STRING interactions, no HPA protein expression data), indicating it is likely a non-coding regulatory element, long non-coding RNA, or structural genomic region rather than a protein-coding gene. ClinVar analysis identified 3 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants with mixed dosage sensitivity: 2 copy number losses (deletions) and 1 copy number gain (duplication), indicating both haploinsufficiency and triplosensitivity are pathogenic mechanisms. The 12q14 chromosomal region contains critical dosage-sensitive genes including MED13L (mediator complex subunit 13-like - haploinsufficiency causes intellectual disability, cardiac defects, and developmental delay), CUL4B (cullin 4B - X-linked intellectual disability pathway), and DNTT (deoxynucleotidyl transferase - lymphocyte development). The mixed pathogenicity pattern (2 deletions, 1 duplication) suggests LOC100506869 functions as a critical cis-regulatory element that precisely modulates expression of neighboring genes, particularly MED13L which is highly dosage-sensitive. Loss of this element causes haploinsufficiency phenotypes (intellectual disability, congenital heart defects), while duplication may cause overexpression effects (potential neurodevelopmental or cardiac abnormalities). This 221 kb region may represent an enhancer cluster, chromatin topologically associating domain (TAD) boundary, or lncRNA that regulates MED13L expression during cardiac and neural development. The region contributes to 12q14 microdeletion/microduplication syndrome phenotypes.
