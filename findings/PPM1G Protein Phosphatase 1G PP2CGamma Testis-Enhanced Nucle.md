## Description
PPM1G encodes a 546 aa nucleoplasmic serine/threonine protein phosphatase (UniProt O15355), also known as PP2CG or PPP2CG, that functions as a critical negative regulator of cell stress response pathways and pre-mRNA splicing through dephosphorylation of spliceosomal components and diverse signaling substrates including α-catenin, YAP, and viral nucleoproteins. This moderately studied gene (77 publications) has emerging clinical significance for triple-negative breast cancer chemoresistance, lung adenocarcinoma progression, influenza infection, hepatic ischemia/reperfusion injury, and male fertility. The protein features dual PP2C catalytic domain architecture: N-terminal PP2C phosphatase domain (cd00143/PF00481/SM00332, residues 27-107/15-503/26-505) providing Mg2+/Mn2+-dependent catalytic activity; C-terminal PP2C phosphatase domain (cd00143/PF00481, residues 287-489/324-505) completing the bifunctional scaffold; divalent cation binding site (IPR000222/PS01032, residues 55-63) coordinating essential metal ions for catalysis; classified within PPM-type phosphatase family (IPR001932/IPR015655/IPR036457/PTHR13832/G3DSA:3.60.40.10/SSF81606, residues 15-505/30-502/1-507) with GO terms for protein serine/threonine phosphatase activity, protein dephosphorylation, and cation binding. Loss-of-function causes hyperphosphorylation of spliceosomal components, disrupted adherens junctions, enhanced chemosensitivity, impaired viral restriction, exacerbated inflammatory injury, while overexpression drives chemoresistance, tumor progression, and metabolic reprogramming.
