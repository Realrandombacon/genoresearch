## Description
ATP11C encodes a 1132 aa phospholipid-transporting ATPase IG (UniProt Q8NB49), also known as aminophospholipid translocase 11C, functioning as a P4-ATPase flippase that catalyzes ATP-dependent translocation of phosphatidylserine (PS) and phosphatidylethanolamine (PE) from the outer to inner leaflet of the plasma membrane, maintaining phospholipid asymmetry essential for cell survival, apoptosis regulation, and blood coagulation. The protein belongs to the P-type ATPase family (IPR001757, aa93-192/339-452/789-898) with P-type ATPase subfamily IV membership (IPR006539, aa43-1094) defining it as an aminophospholipid translocase (cd02073, aa45-977). Structural domains include: calcium-transporting ATPase cytoplasmic transduction domain A (G3DSA:2.70.150.10, aa102-277), cytoplasmic domain N (G3DSA:3.40.1110.10, aa452-487/522-647), HAD superfamily catalytic domain (G3DSA:3.40.50.1000, aa648-854), P-type ATPase A domain superfamily (IPR008250, aa126-274), and conserved phosphorylation site (IPR018303, aa412-418) containing the catalytic aspartate residue essential for the E1-E2 conformational cycle. AlphaFold prediction shows high confidence structure (pLDDT 82.9) across the full-length protein (aa1-1132), indicating a well-folded multi-domain enzyme with stable transmembrane and cytoplasmic regions consistent with active flippase function.
