## Description
DPH3 (Diphthamide biosynthesis protein 3, also known as KTI11, DELGIP, DESR1) is an 82 amino acid cytosolic and nuclear enzyme (UniProt Q96FX2) with high-confidence well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 83.6), characteristic of the CSL zinc finger family. The protein contains a conserved DPH-type metal-binding domain (IPR007872/IPR036671, residues 4-60) and belongs to the Diphthamide biosynthesis protein 3/4-like family (IPR044248/PTHR21454, residues 3-67). Structural analysis identifies a CSL zinc finger fold (PF05207/G3DSA:3.10.660.10/SSF144217, residues 6-60), suggesting coordination of metal ions (likely Zn2+ or Fe-S clusters) essential for catalytic activity. Molecular functions include metal ion binding (GO:0046872) and protein histidyl modification to diphthamide (GO:0017183), placing DPH3 within the translation elongation and toxin susceptibility pathways.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals a tightly connected network dedicated to diphthamide biosynthesis: strongest interactions with EEF2 (0.948, Elongation factor 2, the substrate), DNAJC24 (0.890, DnaJ heat shock protein family member C24, co-chaperone), DPH5 (0.818, Diphthamide biosynthesis protein 5, methyltransferase), DPH7 (0.809, Diphthamide biosynthesis protein 7), KTI12 (0.809, Killer toxin insensitive 12 homolog), DPH6 (0.761), DPH1 (0.539), SERGEF (0.527), ELP3 (0.417, Elongator complex protein 3), and DPH2 (0.414). The extremely high confidence score with EEF2 confirms DPH3 acts directly on the target substrate. Interactions with DPH1, DPH2, DPH5, DPH6, and DPH7 indicate DPH3 is a core component of the multi-step enzymatic cascade that converts a specific histidine residue (His715 in humans) in EF-2 to diphthamide.\n\nHPA expression reveals ubiquitous detection across all tissues with low tissue specificity, consistent with an essential housekeeping role in protein synthesis. Subcellular localization is nucleoplasmic and cytosolic, reflecting the dual location of translation and potential nuclear roles in EF-2 quality control. The protein is classified as essential, underscoring the critical nature of diphthamide modification for cell viability.\n\nClinVar documents 23 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains affecting chromosome 3p (3p26.3-14.3 duplications, 3p26.3-22.3 gains, 3p25.1-24.2 amplifications). The variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity. While no specific Mendelian disease is annotated, 3p CNVs are associated with developmental delay, intellectual disability, and growth abnormalities. Given DPH3's role in EF-2 modification, dosage imbalance may disrupt diphthamide biosynthesis, altering translational fidelity or efficiency. Interestingly, diphthamide is the target of diphtheria toxin; thus, DPH3 levels may influence cellular susceptibility to bacterial toxins, although developmental phenotypes suggest a fundamental role in homeostasis independent of infection.\n\nConservation is strong across eukaryotes: human (Q96FX2, 82 aa), mouse (Q8K0W9, 82 aa), yeast (Q3E840, KTI11, 82 aa), fission yeast (Q9UT33, 79 aa), indicating an ancient and indispensable function in translation.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: DPH3 functions as a metal-dependent enzyme (likely utilizing an Fe-S cluster or Zn2+ via its CSL finger) that catalyzes an early step in the diphthamide biosynthesis pathway, potentially the formation of the carbon-carbon bond between the histidine imidazole ring and the 3-amino-3-carboxypropyl group derived from SAM. It likely forms a transient complex with EEF2 and other DPH proteins (DPH1/2/5/6/7) to ensure precise modification of His715. Loss of DPH3 results in hypomodified EF-2, which may exhibit altered translocation kinetics, reduced translational fidelity, or increased susceptibility to ribosome stalling. This disrupts global protein synthesis, triggering stress responses and developmental defects observed in 3p CNV syndromes. DPH3 represents a critical node in translational control and a candidate gene for 3p duplication-associated neurodevelopmental disorders.
