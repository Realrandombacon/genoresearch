## Description
GJB7 (Gap Junction Beta-7 Protein, also known as Connexin 30.2 or Cx30.2) is a 223 aa integral membrane protein functioning as a component of gap junction channels that facilitate direct intercellular communication. Despite being a member of the well-known connexin family, GJB7 remains a 'dark gene' with only 4 literature references, and its specific physiological roles, interaction partners, and disease associations are poorly defined compared to canonical connexins like GJB2 (Cx26) or GJA1 (Cx43). InterPro analysis confirms a canonical connexin architecture: four transmembrane helices, two extracellular loops containing conserved cysteines for docking (IPR019570, aa 134-201), an intracellular loop, and N/C-terminal cytoplasmic tails (IPR000500, aa 1-210; PF00029, aa 2-202). The conserved signature motif (PS00408, aa 157-173) is critical for channel gating and selectivity. AlphaFold predicts a confident global structure (pLDDT 83.3), indicating a stable tetraspanin-like fold capable of hexameric hemichannel assembly. HPA shows restricted expression patterns: group-enriched in fallopian tube (6.6 nTPM), pituitary gland (5.0 nTPM), and placenta (2.5 nTPM), suggesting specialized roles in reproductive physiology and neuroendocrine signaling rather than ubiquitous housekeeping functions. STRING interactions link GJB7 primarily to other connexins: GJE1 (0.752, Connexin 63), GJD3 (0.426, Connexin 45), GJC3 (0.425, Connexin 30.3), and GJA9 (0.416, Connexin 44), implying it likely forms heteromeric or heterotypic channels with these partners to modulate permeability. ClinVar lists 16 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, exclusively large copy number losses/gains on chromosome 6q, implicating GJB7 dosage sensitivity in developmental disorders, though no specific monogenic phenotype is yet assigned. The single 2020 paper directly addressing GJB7 reports somatic mutations in gastric and colorectal cancers, hinting at a potential tumor suppressor or oncogenic role depending on context, while other references are tangential (founder mutations in African populations, prognosis in head/neck cancer).
