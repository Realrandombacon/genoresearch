## Description
GID8 (Glucose-Induced Degradation Protein 8 Homolog, also known as GID Complex Subunit 8 or Twa1, UniProt Q9NWU2) is a 228 aa nuclear/cell junction protein belonging to the CTLH (C-terminal to LisH) E3 ubiquitin ligase complex that functions as a β-catenin nuclear retention factor in Wnt signaling, colorectal cancer tumorigenesis, and m6A-dependent glutamine metabolism regulation. InterPro analysis reveals multi-domain architecture: N-terminal LisH (Lissencephaly type-1-like homology) motif (IPR006594, positions 25-57; PF08513, positions 28-53; PS50896, positions 25-57; SM00667, positions 25-57) for protein-protein interactions and dimerization, central CTLH (C-terminal to LisH) domain (IPR006595, positions 63-120; PS50897, positions 63-120; SM00668, positions 63-120) for complex assembly, C-terminal CRA (CT11-RanBPM) domain (IPR013144, positions 116-212; SM00757, positions 116-212) for substrate recognition, and extended CTLH/CRA C-terminal to LisH motif domain (IPR024964, positions 64-206; PF10607, positions 64-206) spanning most of the protein. GID8 belongs to Ubiquitination and Signaling Pathway Regulator family (IPR050618, positions 13-211; PTHR12864, positions 13-211). AlphaFold structure shows very high confidence (pLDDT 91.9), indicating a well-folded compact protein with stable domain architecture suitable for precise protein-protein interactions. HPA data confirms low tissue specificity (detected in all tissues), nucleoplasm and cell junctions localization, classified as predicted intracellular protein involved in Wnt signaling pathway. STRING interactions reveal exclusive clustering with CTLH/GID E3 ubiquitin ligase complex subunits: RMND5A (0.999, Required for Meiotic Nuclear Division 5 homolog A), ARMC8 (0.999, Armadillo Repeat Containing 8), WDR26 (0.999, WD Repeat Domain 26), GID4 (0.996, GID Complex Subunit 4), MKLN1 (0.995, Muskelin-1), RANBP10 (0.990, Ran-Binding Protein 10), MAEA (0.960, Macrophage Erythroblast Maturation Protein), RMND5B (0.959), YPEL5 (0.885, Yippee-Like 5), RANBP9 (0.794). ClinVar contains 32 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including 20q13.13-13.33 copy number gains/losses, deletions (chr20:61471874-62078210del), and copy number losses at 20q13.33. Literature (27 papers) establishes GID8 roles in: (1) colorectal cancer progression via YTHDF1-mediated m6A-dependent GID8 upregulation promoting glutamine metabolism (2024, 21 citations), (2) β-catenin nuclear retention factor in Wnt signaling and colorectal tumorigenesis (2017, 56 citations), (3) nuclear translocation of β-catenin in Wg/Wnt signaling via IFT-A microtubule-associated complex requiring Pasovec/Gid8 proteins (2026), and (4) yeast START completion regulation with DCR2 and KEM1 (2007). FUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: GID8 serves as an essential scaffold subunit of the CTLH E3 ubiquitin ligase complex that (a) stabilizes complex assembly through LisH-mediated dimerization and CTLH domain interactions, (b) retains β-catenin in the nucleus by preventing its CRM1-mediated export and GSK3β-dependent degradation, and (c) targets specific substrates for ubiquitination and proteasomal degradation to regulate Wnt signaling output and metabolic reprogramming in cancer. MECHANISTIC PROPOSAL: The LisH motif (positions 25-57) mediates GID8 homodimerization and heterodimerization with RMND5A/RMND5B, forming the catalytic core of the CTLH complex. The CTLH domain (positions 63-120) binds ARMC8 and WDR26, creating a platform for substrate recruitment. The CRA domain (positions 116-212) recognizes specific degradation motifs on target proteins. In Wnt signaling, GID8 binds β-catenin in the nucleus through direct protein-protein interactions, preventing APC/Axin/GSK3β destruction complex access and CRM1 nuclear export, thereby sustaining Wnt target gene transcription (MYC, CCND1, AXIN2). In colorectal cancer, YTHDF1 (m6A reader) binds m6A-modified GID8 mRNA, enhancing translation and increasing GID8 protein levels, which amplifies Wnt signaling and promotes glutamine metabolism through unknown downstream targets. The 32 ClinVar variants at 20q13 suggest GID8 dosage sensitivity contributes to developmental disorders through disrupted Wnt signaling. Very high AlphaFold confidence (91.9) confirms stable domain structure suitable for drug targeting in Wnt-driven cancers. GID8 interaction with IFT-A complex links Wnt signaling to ciliary trafficking.
