## Description
GPR108 (UniProt Q9NPR9), also known as LUSTR2, encodes a 543 aa Golgi-resident seven-transmembrane receptor functioning as an essential host factor for adeno-associated virus (AAV) entry and a negative regulator of antiviral innate immunity. InterPro analysis reveals a specialized GPCR architecture: a GPR107/GPR108-like transmembrane family domain (IPR009637/PTHR21229, residues 22-535) defining this receptor subclass, and a GOST seven-transmembrane domain (IPR053937/PF06814, residues 259-503) forming the canonical 7TM bundle characteristic of class A GPCRs though with unique structural features distinguishing it from classical GPCRs. AlphaFold predicts a confident structure (pLDDT 75.3) consistent with a well-folded multi-pass membrane protein where the 7TM core forms a stable helical bundle suitable for potential ligand binding or conformational changes, while N-terminal and intracellular loops may mediate partner interactions. STRING interactions define a network centered on membrane trafficking and related GPCRs: SCAMP2 (0.766, secretory carrier membrane protein 2 involved in vesicle trafficking), KIAA0319L (0.659), CNPPD1 (0.636, cyclin Y-dependent kinase regulator), GPR152 (0.631), GPR137 (0.556), GPR137B (0.555), TPRA1 (0.543, transmembrane protein), GPR137C (0.529), GPR171 (0.494), and TM9SF2 (0.458, transmembrane 9 superfamily member 2 - co-identified AAV entry factor). HPA classifies GPR108 as low tissue specificity with detection in all tissues, low cancer specificity, localized to Golgi apparatus and vesicles consistent with its role in viral trafficking, involved in host-virus interaction, reflecting ubiquitous housekeeping function in membrane trafficking with specialized viral entry role. ClinVar lists 10 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including intronic variants (c.549+13C>T, c.1350+24C>T), missense mutation (p.Tyr315Phe in transmembrane region), and large copy number gains/deletions on chromosome 19p (19p13.3-13.2x3, 19p13.3-q13.43x3, 19p13.3 deletion) though no single-gene disorder is definitively assigned suggesting contribution to 19p duplication/deletion syndromes or susceptibility to viral infections. Functional hypothesis: GPR108 operates through a multi-step mechanism integrating membrane trafficking and immune regulation: (1) AAV entry mediation - Acts as essential cellular receptor/cofactor enabling AAV transduction across multiple serotypes (AAV2, AAV5, AAV6, AAV8, AAV9) by facilitating endosomal escape or Golgi-to-nucleus trafficking as demonstrated by CRISPR screens showing complete transduction block upon GPR108 knockout; (2) Golgi retention - Unlike classical cell-surface GPCRs, GPR108 resides primarily in trans-Golgi network where it may sense luminal cargo or membrane composition; (3) Antiviral immunity suppression - 2023 studies demonstrate GPR108 cooperates with E3 ubiquitin ligase Smurf1 to suppress type I interferon signaling and toll-like receptor pathways, preventing excessive inflammation during viral infection; (4) Partner coordination - TM9SF2 functions synergistically with GPR108 in AAV entry suggesting a multi-protein entry complex; (5) Vesicle trafficking - SCAMP2 interaction links GPR108 to secretory pathway regulation and cargo sorting; (6) Disease mechanisms - 2025 research shows GPR108 deficiency promotes urate-induced renal interstitial fibrosis suggesting protective role in kidney injury; 2024 genomic studies reveal GPR108 loss disrupts AAV transduction in birds explaining species-specific AAV tropism; (7) Therapeutic implications - GPR108 represents critical target for enhancing AAV gene therapy efficiency (overexpression) or blocking unwanted AAV exposure (inhibition); Smurf1-mediated immune suppression axis may be targetable in autoimmune diseases. Conservation extends to mouse (Q91WD0, 569 aa), rat (Q6P6V6, 577 aa), and cattle (Q148L1, 548 aa) indicating preserved role in mammalian viral susceptibility and immune regulation. Dark aspects requiring investigation include: endogenous ligand identity (orphan receptor status), precise structural basis of AAV interaction (direct binding vs cofactor), mechanism of Smurf1 recruitment and ubiquitination targets, role in other viral infections beyond AAV, contribution to Golgi structure/maintenance, tissue-specific interactome differences, potential G protein coupling vs arrestin-biased signaling, therapeutic strategies for modulating GPR108 in gene therapy contexts, genotype-phenotype correlations in 19p CNV syndromes, and evolutionary basis of species-specific AAV tropism (bird vs mammalian GPR108 differences).
