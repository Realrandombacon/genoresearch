## Description
SCRTR1 is a zinc finger transcription factor belonging to the scratch family, characterized by its role as a negative regulator in beta-cell proliferation during neonatal maturation. Literature identifies it as essential for glucose-induced insulin secretion development (2021), where loss of function impairs islet expansion. In oncology, SCRT1 acts upstream of TCL6 lncRNA to drive PDK1/AKT signaling pathways promoting non-small cell lung cancer metastasis and proliferation (2023). Its protein interactions include SLC6A2 (high confidence 0.81), suggesting potential roles in neurotransmitter transport or synaptic regulation alongside its transcriptional function. While HPA expression data was unavailable due to symbol resolution issues, the gene's dual role as a developmental regulator of insulin secretion and an oncogenic driver via AKT signaling suggests context-dependent functions: promoting differentiation/proliferation in beta-cells while potentially suppressing tumor growth when overexpressed or acting as a metastasis suppressor depending on cellular state. The presence of zinc finger domains (confirmed by UniProt Q9BWW7 length 348aa) supports its DNA-binding capability, likely targeting enhancers to modulate insulin-related genes.
