Recent literature (2022-2024) reveals: (1) TRIM75 as a maternal factor contributing to ZGA program in mouse early embryos, (2) structural studies showing the coiled-coil domain forms a tetramer architecture facilitating E3 ligase complex assembly (5 citations). Functional hypothesis: TRIM75 functions as a maternal E3 ubiquitin ligase deposited in oocytes that targets specific substrates for degradation during the maternal-to-zygotic transition. The RING domain recruits E2 ubiquitin-conjugating enzymes, the B-box mediates TRIM75 tetramerization (as shown structurally), and the SPRY domain recognizes embryonic transcription factors or chromatin regulators for ubiquitination. This clears maternal proteins to enable zygotic genome activation. The restricted expression pattern explains why TRIM75 was missed in standard tissue surveys—it operates in a narrow developmental window. The 16 ClinVar CNVs likely cause infertility, early embryonic loss, or developmental disorders undiagnosed due to early pregnancy loss. Interaction with ZFAND5 suggests potential coupling to autophagy for clearance of ubiquitinated maternal factors. TRIM42/TRIM36 interactions indicate TRIM75 may function in heteromeric TRIM complexes, expanding substrate specificity. The high-confidence structure (pLDDT 85.9) and conserved domain arrangement (mouse Q3UWZ0 467 aa, 95% identity) indicate strong evolutionary constraint on this ZGA mechanism.
