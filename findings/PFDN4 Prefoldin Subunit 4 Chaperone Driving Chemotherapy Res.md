# PFDN4: Prefoldin Subunit 4 Chaperone Driving Chemotherapy Resistance via CREBP1/AURKA Pathway and Extracellular Vesicle Secretion in Multiple Carcinomas

## Description
PFDN4 (Prefoldin Subunit 4) is a 134-amino acid molecular chaperone component of the heterohexameric prefoldin complex, functioning as a co-chaperone that captures unfolded substrate proteins and delivers them to the CCT/TRiC chaperonin for ATP-dependent folding. InterPro analysis confirms membership in the prefoldin beta-like family (IPR002777, residues 20-125) with characteristic coiled-coil structure (PF01920, residues 20-125) forming the signature 'jellyfish' architecture with flexible tentacles for substrate capture. Additional domain annotations include IPR009053 (Prefoldin superfamily, 7-125) and IPR016661 (Prefoldin subunit 4 family, 1-131). AlphaFold predicts high-confidence structure (pLDDT 87.4), consistent with stable coiled-coil folding required for chaperone function.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals PFDN4 operates within a tightly coordinated chaperone network with near-perfect confidence scores: PFDN6 (0.999), PFDN5 (0.999), PFDN2 (0.974), VBP1/PFDN3 (0.972), PFDN1 (0.945) representing other prefoldin subunits; and CCT4 (0.974), CCT8 (0.967), CCT7 (0.956), CCT2 (0.956), CCT5 (0.955) representing the CCT/TRiC chaperonin complex that receives unfolded substrates. This interaction profile confirms PFDN4's obligate role in the two-step chaperone cascade.\n\nHuman Protein Atlas classifies PFDN4 as a plasma protein and predicted intracellular chaperone with low tissue specificity (detected in all tissues), cell type enhanced expression, and low cancer/immune cell specificity, consistent with housekeeping chaperone function. ClinVar reports 9 pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants, all large copy number gains spanning 20q13.12-13.33, indicating dosage sensitivity though no specific monogenic syndrome is yet defined.\n\nLiterature analysis (23 papers) reveals emerging oncogenic roles: 2024 studies demonstrate PFDN4 as a prognostic marker associated with chemotherapy resistance in triple-negative breast cancer through the CREBP1/AURKA pathway, upregulation in hepatocellular carcinoma correlating with poor outcomes, regulation of extracellular vesicle secretion from prostate cancer cells via miR-26a targeting, and biomarker potential in colorectal cancer. We hypothesize that PFDN4 overexpression in carcinomas enhances folding capacity for oncoproteins (potentially including AURKA, CREBP1, or cytoskeletal clients like actin/tubulin that require CCT/TRiC), conferring survival advantages under chemotherapy-induced proteotoxic stress. The prefoldin complex may also facilitate extracellular vesicle biogenesis by ensuring proper folding of ESCRT machinery or tetraspanin cargo. PFDN4 represents a targetable vulnerability where chaperone inhibition could sensitize tumors to chemotherapy by overwhelming proteostasis capacity.
