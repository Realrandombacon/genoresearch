## Description
FAM168A (UniProt Q92567) is a 244 amino acid intrinsically disordered nuclear protein representing an understudied component of the human dark genome with emerging roles in transcriptional regulation, protein ubiquitination, and chemotherapy resistance. The protein contains a defining FAM168A/MANI domain family (IPR029247/PTHR31844) spanning the full length (residues 1-244) and a Pfam domain PF14944 annotated as 'Tongue Cancer Chemotherapy Resistant Protein 1' covering residues 1-244, directly implicating FAM168A in cancer drug resistance mechanisms. AlphaFold predicts a low-confidence structure (global pLDDT 45.7) spanning the full 1-244 residue length, indicating a predominantly intrinsically disordered protein (IDP) lacking stable tertiary structure—this is a hallmark feature of proteins functioning through dynamic protein-protein interactions rather than enzymatic catalysis, consistent with roles as transcriptional co-regulators, scaffolds, or signaling hubs. HPA classifies FAM168A as a disease-related and essential protein with nucleoplasmic subcellular localization, detected in all tissues with low tissue specificity but immune cell enhancement, suggesting fundamental nuclear function across cell types with specialized roles in hematopoietic/immune cells. The interaction profile reveals three functional modules: (1) ubiquitination machinery (WWP2, WWP1, UBASH3B) suggesting FAM168A is a substrate or regulator of protein degradation pathways, (2) transcription apparatus (POLR2B/C/D) indicating direct involvement in RNA polymerase II-mediated gene expression, (3) signaling kinases (AKT1) connecting to cell survival and growth pathways. The mechanistic model positions FAM168A as an intrinsically disordered nuclear scaffold protein functioning through: (1) WWP2/WWP1-mediated ubiquitination regulating protein stability of FAM168A itself or its binding partners, potentially targeting transcription factors or signaling proteins for proteasomal degradation, (2) direct association with RNA polymerase II complex (POLR2B/C/D) modulating transcriptional elongation or initiation at specific gene targets, (3) AKT1 pathway integration linking growth factor signaling to transcriptional outputs, (4) chemotherapy resistance through PF14944 domain-mediated mechanisms potentially involving drug efflux regulation, DNA damage response modulation via RECQL5 interaction, or anti-apoptotic signaling through AKT1. Loss-of-function through deletions or gain-of-function through duplications would disrupt ubiquitination-transcription coupling, contributing to developmental anomalies in 11q duplication/deletion syndromes and altered chemotherapy sensitivity in cancers.
