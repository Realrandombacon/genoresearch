## Description
CDC42EP4 (Cdc42 Effector Protein 4, also known as BORG4; UniProt Q9H3Q1) is a 356 amino acid intracellular scaffold protein with 18 publications, representing a partially characterized cytoskeletal regulator that bridges Rho GTPase signaling to septin filament assembly in neural and epithelial contexts.\n\nSTRUCTURAL ARCHITECTURE: InterPro analysis reveals a modular architecture optimized for GTPase binding and cytoskeletal scaffolding. The N-terminal region contains a CRIB domain (IPR000095/PF00786/PS50108/SM00285, residues 26-84/27-61/27-41) belonging to the P21-Rho-binding domain family that specifically binds active GTP-loaded CDC42 and related Rho family GTPases. A central Cdc42 effector-like domain (IPR029273/PF14957, residues 112-228) mediates downstream signaling and protein-protein interactions. The full-length protein belongs to the Cdc42 Effector Protein BORG/CEP family (IPR051296/PTHR15344, residues 1-356). GO annotations include cell shape regulation. AlphaFold predicts medium confidence structure (global pLDDT 58.2), indicating a mix of ordered domains (CRIB) connected by intrinsically disordered regions—consistent with scaffold proteins that require flexibility to bridge multiple binding partners and undergo conformational changes upon GTPase activation.\n\nINTERACTION NETWORK: STRING-DB identifies exceptionally high-confidence interactions with septin cytoskeleton components and upstream GTPases: CDC42 (0.942, Cell division control protein 42 homolog, primary upstream activator), SEPTIN7 (0.902, Septin-7, core septin filament component), SEPTIN6 (0.896, Septin-6), SEPTIN8 (0.895, Septin-8), SEPTIN3 (0.887, Septin-3, neuron-specific), SEPTIN11 (0.885, Septin-11), SEPTIN4 (0.883, Septin-4), SEPTIN9 (0.882, Septin-9), SEPTIN5 (0.878, Septin-5), and RHOQ (0.590, Rho-related GTP-binding protein RhoQ/TC10). This interaction profile establishes CDC42EP4 as a critical linker between CDC42 activation and septin filament assembly—the CRIB domain binds active CDC42 at the plasma membrane while the effector domain recruits septin hetero-oligomers to form diffusion barriers and scaffolds.\n\nEXPRESSION & LOCALIZATION: HPA shows ubiquitous expression (detected in all tissues) with low tissue specificity but immune cell enhancement in blood cells. Subcellular localization includes plasma membrane (CDC42 binding sites), actin filaments (cytoskeletal integration), and microtubules (transport tracks). Protein class includes predicted intracellular proteins. Biological process is cell shape regulation. RNA cancer specificity is low. Single cell type specificity is cell type-enhanced. The 2015/2018 literature demonstrates exclusive expression in Bergmann glia within the cerebellum—a highly restricted pattern despite ubiquitous RNA detection, suggesting post-transcriptional regulation or protein stability differences across tissues.\n\nDISEASE LANDSCAPE: ClinVar contains 13 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains and losses at 17q24.3-25.3 (e.g., chr17:70161447-81041938x3, chr17:69501527-71380722x1, chr17:62778720-81041938x3). While no specific monogenic disease is definitively assigned, the burden of structural variants suggests haploinsufficiency or dosage sensitivity may cause neurodevelopmental anomalies or cytoskeletal defects. Recent literature establishes prominent roles in cancer and neuroscience: (1) 2023 study (3 citations) demonstrates CDC42EP4 inhibits prostate cancer progression by suppressing ERK signaling pathway—overexpression reduces cell proliferation and invasion; (2) Landmark 2015 paper (28 citations) shows CDC42EP4 forms a perisynaptic glial scaffold with septins that facilitates glutamate clearance at tripartite synapses; (3) 2018 follow-up (10 citations) confirms CDC42EP4 is required for glutamatergic tripartite synapse configuration in Bergmann glia, affecting synaptic transmission and cerebellar function. The septin interaction network suggests potential roles in cytokinesis defects and cancer cell migration.\n\nCONSERVATION: Orthologs confirmed in mouse (Q9JM96, 349 aa—nearly identical length), indicating strong evolutionary constraint on the CRIB-effector architecture across mammals for conserved cytoskeletal regulation.\n\nFUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: CDC42EP4 functions as a GTPase-dependent molecular switch that recruits septin filaments to specific membrane domains through a coordinated mechanism: (1) Inactive state—CDC42EP4 cytosolic, CRIB domain autoinhibited; (2) Activation—GTP-loaded CDC42 at plasma membrane binds CRIB domain (residues 26-84), inducing conformational change that exposes septin-binding surfaces in the effector domain (residues 112-228); (3) Scaffold assembly—CDC42EP4 recruits septin hetero-oligomers (SEPTIN3/4/5/6/7/8/9/11) to form ring-like diffusion barriers at perisynaptic glial processes; (4) Functional outcome—in Bergmann glia, this scaffold positions glutamate transporters (EAAT1/EAAT2) near synaptic clefts for rapid neurotransmitter clearance, preventing excitotoxicity and shaping synaptic transmission; (5) In prostate cancer, CDC42EP4 suppresses ERK phosphorylation through unknown mechanism—possibly by sequestering upstream kinases at septin barriers or altering growth factor receptor trafficking. Disordered regions (pLDDT 58.2) enable dynamic reorganization during synapse remodeling or cell migration. ClinVar CNVs at 17q24.3-25.3 may cause haploinsufficiency leading to cerebellar dysfunction (ataxia, glutamate excitotoxicity) or cancer predisposition. Therapeutic CDC42EP4 modulation could enhance glutamate clearance in neurodegenerative disease or restore tumor suppression in prostate cancer.
