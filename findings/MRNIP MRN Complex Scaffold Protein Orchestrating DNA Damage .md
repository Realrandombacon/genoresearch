## Description
MRNIP encodes a 343 aa MRN complex-interacting protein (UniProt Q6NTE8), also known as C5orf45, that functions as a critical regulator of DNA double-strand break (DSB) repair and replication stress response. InterPro analysis reveals: (1) MRN complex-interacting protein family domain (IPR032739, aa 5-338) spanning nearly the entire protein; (2) N-terminal domain (IPR049472, PF15749, aa 9-110) likely mediating MRN complex binding; (3) Panther family classification (PTHR15863, aa 5-338). AlphaFold predicts a medium-confidence structure (pLDDT 56.2), indicating a partially disordered protein with structured N-terminal domain and flexible C-terminal region—characteristic of proteins undergoing phase separation. STRING interactions reveal unexpected connections to ribosomal machinery: FAU (0.995), RPS23 (0.980), RPS3A (0.980), RPL14 (0.980), RPS6 (0.980), RPS2 (0.980), RPS24 (0.980), RPL6 (0.980), RPL5 (0.980), RPS18 (0.980), suggesting potential coupling between DNA damage response and translation regulation. HPA has no expression data, indicating low abundance or tissue-restricted expression. ClinVar reports 19 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly 5q35.2-35.3 copy number losses/gains, though specific disease associations remain undefined. Literature (12 papers) reveals: (1) MRNIP condensates promote DNA DSB sensing and end resection (2022, 55 citations)—demonstrating phase separation-driven MRN complex recruitment; (2) MRNIP limits ssDNA gaps during replication stress by regulating PRIMPOL-mediated replication repriming (2024, 7 citations); (3) MRNIP interacts with sex body chromatin supporting meiotic progression, spermatogenesis, and male fertility in mice (2022, 8 citations); (4) Novel candidate gene for developmental language disorder via whole exome sequencing (2025, 1 citation). Mechanistically, MRNIP functions through: (1) Phase separation into biomolecular condensates at DSB sites, concentrating MRN complex (MRE11-RAD50-NBN) for efficient break sensing; (2) Recruitment of end resection machinery to initiate homologous recombination; (3) Coordination with PRIMPOL to prevent ssDNA gap accumulation during replication stress; (4) Potential ribosome interaction linking DNA damage to translational control. In disease, MRNIP dysfunction likely contributes to: genomic instability and cancer predisposition via defective DSB repair; male infertility through meiotic defects; neurodevelopmental disorders (developmental language disorder) via replication stress in neural progenitors. The disordered structure and condensate formation suggest MRNIP as a therapeutic target for enhancing DNA repair in cancer therapy or protecting against replication stress in neurodegeneration.
