## Description
RUBCNL (UniProt Q9H714) encodes a 662 aa intracellular autophagy enhancer protein, also known as PACER (Protein Associated with UVRAG as Autophagy Enhancer), functioning as a critical regulator of autophagosome maturation and cell death pathways. InterPro analysis reveals a specialized multi-domain architecture optimized for autophagy complex assembly: a central PI3K-binding domain (aa 202-411, IPR048569, PF21054) mediating interaction with class III phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase complexes; a C-terminal Rubicon Homology Domain (aa 450-651, IPR025258, PF13901, SM01175/DUF4206) enabling binding to UVRAG and BECN1; and classification within the Autophagy and Host Defense Regulator family (IPR052428, PTHR45971). AlphaFold predicts a medium-confidence structure (global pLDDT 60.6), consistent with a partially structured protein containing ordered domains embedded in intrinsically disordered regions—a hallmark of scaffold proteins that undergo conformational changes during complex assembly. STRING interactions define RUBCNL as a node in the autophagy network with high-confidence partners: PGM2 (0.788), CWC25 (0.788), C1orf109 (0.772), UVRAG (0.755), BECN1 (0.739), STX17 (0.705), and medium-confidence links to SLCO1A2 (0.698), FTSJ1 (0.655), PIK3R4 (0.653), and LIX1 (0.504), establishing its role in autophagosome-lysosome fusion and mTOR-dependent autophagy regulation. HPA expression shows tissue-enhanced distribution with lymphoid tissue enrichment (38.5 nTPM), cytosolic localization, and group-enriched expression in blood cells and certain cancers, consistent with immune cell autophagy functions. ClinVar reports 27 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains/losses on chromosome 13q (spanning 13q11-34), indicating genomic instability in this region and potential dosage sensitivity, though no specific monogenic disease has been definitively assigned. Literature (17 papers) highlights: (1) Bevacizumab Resistance—tumor-derived lactate induces H3K18 lactylation at the RUBCNL promoter, enhancing autophagy and conferring anti-VEGF therapy resistance in colorectal cancer (338 citations); (2) Cell Death Suppression—RUBCNL represses RIPK1 kinase-dependent apoptosis and necroptosis, protecting mesenchymal stem cells from inflammatory microenvironment-induced death (17 citations); (3) ALS Progression—neuronal RUBCNL overexpression accelerates disease in SOD1G93A ALS mouse models, suggesting excessive autophagy may be detrimental in neurodegeneration (5 citations); (4) Ulcerative Colitis—Syk blockade modulates neutrophil immune responses via mTOR/RUBCNL-dependent autophagy, alleviating intestinal inflammation (12 citations). Functional hypothesis: RUBCNL functions as a molecular scaffold that: (1) Autophagosome Maturation—binds UVRAG-BECN1 complexes via its Rubicon Homology Domain to promote autophagosome-lysosome fusion, opposing the inhibitory effects of Rubicon (RUBCN); (2) Epigenetic Regulation—serves as a readout of metabolic state via histone lactylation (H3K18la), linking tumor metabolism to autophagy enhancement; (3) Cell Death Gatekeeper—suppresses RIPK1-mediated necroptosis and apoptosis, protecting cells from inflammatory death while potentially enabling cancer cell survival. Mechanistic proposal: In colorectal cancer, hypoxia-induced lactate production triggers histone H3K18 lactylation at the RUBCNL locus, upregulating RUBCNL expression, which enhances autophagic flux via UVRAG-BECN1-STX17 complex assembly, clearing damaged organelles and conferring bevacizumab resistance. In inflammatory conditions, RUBCNL protects stem cells by inhibiting RIPK1 kinase activity, preventing necroptotic cell death. The combination of a partially structured scaffold architecture, specific autophagy protein interactions, lymphoid/cancer-enriched expression, chromosomal 13q CNV burden, and emerging roles in therapy resistance and cell death positions RUBCNL as a genuine dark gene with significant therapeutic potential in oncology and inflammatory disease.
