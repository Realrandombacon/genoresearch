# MPP2: MAGUK Scaffold Protein Organizing Synaptic Protein Complexes in Basolateral Amygdala and Epithelial Cell Polarity with Variants in Vogt-Koyanagi-Harada Disease

## Description
MPP2 (Membrane Palmitoylated Protein 2, also known as DLG2 or MAGUK p55 subfamily member 2, UniProt Q14168) is a 576-amino acid peripheral membrane scaffold protein belonging to the membrane-associated guanylate kinase (MAGUK) family, functioning as a critical organizer of protein complexes at specialized membrane domains including synapses and epithelial junctions. The protein architecture follows the canonical MAGUK domain organization: an N-terminal L27 domain (IPR004172, aa 11-145) mediating heterodimerization with other MAGUK family members, followed by a PDZ domain (IPR001478, cd10832, aa 163-243) recognizing C-terminal peptide motifs of client proteins, an SH3 domain (IPR001452, cd12037, aa 253-311) binding proline-rich sequences, and a C-terminal guanylate kinase-like domain (IPR008144, IPR008145, G3DSA:3.40.50.300, aa 374-561) that has lost enzymatic activity but serves as a protein interaction module. The AlphaFold predicted structure shows high confidence (pLDDT 78.9), indicating a well-folded multi-domain scaffold capable of simultaneous binding to multiple partners. STRING interaction network places MPP2 within the synaptic scaffolding and cell polarity machinery with high-confidence associations to: LIN7A (0.972), lin-7 homolog A forming the CRB complex regulating apical-basal polarity; TJAP1 (0.849), tight junction-associated protein 1 localizing to epithelial junctions; CAMK2A (0.791) and CAMK2D (0.704), calcium/calmodulin-dependent protein kinase II alpha/delta subunits critical for synaptic plasticity and long-term potentiation; GRIN2B (0.775), glutamate ionotropic receptor NMDA type subunit 2B mediating excitatory neurotransmission; MPP6 (0.715), paralogous MAGUK protein with overlapping functions; LIN7B (0.555) and LIN7C (0.554), additional lin-7 family members; BEGAIN (0.553), brain-enriched guanylate kinase-associated protein; and DLG1 (0.466), discs large homolog 1 (SAP97) another MAGUK scaffold. HPA expression profiles demonstrate tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in brain (32.8 nTPM), cancer-enhanced specificity suggesting dysregulation in tumors, detection across many tissues indicating broad functional roles, absence from immune cells, and classification as predicted intracellular protein. ClinVar records document 9 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number losses on 17q21.31 (43.6-44.8 Mb) and gains spanning 17q12-25.3 regions, plus a splice site variant c.1353+3A>C; recent trio-based whole-exome sequencing identified de novo missense mutations conferring increased risk for Vogt-Koyanagi-Harada disease (VKH), an autoimmune inflammatory disorder affecting melanocyte-containing tissues causing uveitis, hearing loss, and vitiligo. Literature analysis (181 papers) reveals emerging functional insights: MPP2 interacts with SK2 (small conductance calcium-activated potassium channel 2) in the basolateral amygdala (BLA) to rescue glutamatergic neuron excitability and facilitate extinction of conditioned fear memories, suggesting therapeutic potential for PTSD and anxiety disorders; FGF14-AS2/MPP2 axis serves as prognostic biomarker in colorectal cancer with DNA methylation levels correlating with patient survival; MPP2 forms part of the Crumbs complex maintaining apical membrane identity in polarized epithelia; and involvement in NMDA receptor clustering at postsynaptic densities through GRIN2B interaction. We hypothesize that MPP2 functions through a multi-context scaffolding mechanism: (1) In neuronal synapses, MPP2 anchors NMDA receptors (via GRIN2B PDZ-binding) and CaMKII kinases to the postsynaptic density, organizing signaling microdomains for efficient calcium-dependent plasticity; (2) In the basolateral amygdala, MPP2-SK2 interaction modulates potassium channel surface expression regulating neuronal firing patterns during fear memory extinction; (3) In epithelial cells, MPP2 heterodimerizes via L27 domains with LIN7 family proteins forming the Crumbs polarity complex at tight junctions, maintaining apical-basal asymmetry essential for barrier function; (4) The guanylate kinase-like domain, despite catalytic inactivity, provides structural rigidity and additional protein-binding surfaces for complex assembly; (5) In cancer, MPP2 overexpression may stabilize oncogenic signaling complexes or disrupt normal polarity contributing to epithelial-mesenchymal transition; (6) VKH-associated variants potentially disrupt MPP2-mediated immune tolerance in melanocytes through altered protein complex assembly. The high-confidence structure suggests drugability for targeting specific domain interactions in neurological disorders or autoimmunity.
