# LOC129388891: MPRA-Validated Silencer Element on 2q11.2 with Moderate Duplication Bias (3 Dup:1 Del)

## Description
LOC129388891 is a 200bp cis-regulatory element (CRE) located on chromosome 2q11.2 (GRCh38: 97625832-97626032), functionally validated as a transcriptional silencer through STARR-seq massively parallel reporter assays (MPRAs) in K562 erythroleukemia cells. This element lacks canonical promoter, enhancer, or CTCF-bound insulator chromatin signatures but demonstrates robust repressive activity against the super core promoter SCP1. As a non-coding regulatory locus, it has no UniProt accession, protein domains, or STRING interaction partners. Human Protein Atlas could not resolve this locus to an Ensembl ID, consistent with its non-coding regulatory classification.\n\nClinVar analysis reveals 4 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variants disrupting this region—a moderate burden with duplication bias (3 duplications:1 deletion ratio). Variants include: (1) a focal 2q11.1-11.2 deletion (~2.2Mb), (2) a large 2q11.1-13 duplication (~16Mb), (3) a 2q11.1-12.1 duplication (~8.4Mb), and (4) a complex 2p11.2-q11.2 duplication spanning the centromere (~11Mb). This 3:1 ratio suggests triplosensitivity intolerance where silencer gain causes pathological hyper-repression of target genes, though less extreme than loci with 11:1 ratios. These recurrent CNVs are associated with 2q11.2 deletion/duplication syndromes featuring developmental delay, intellectual disability, craniofacial dysmorphism, cardiac defects, and psychiatric disorders (schizophrenia susceptibility). The 2q11.2 region contains critical dosage-sensitive genes including ACOX3 (Acyl-CoA Oxidase 3, peroxisomal fatty acid oxidation), BCL2L11 (BCL2-Like 11/BIM, apoptosis regulator), and CRIM1 (Cysteine Rich Transmembrane BMP Regulator 1, neural tube development).\n\nHYPOTHESIS: LOC129388891 functions as a critical distal silencer within the 2q11.2 topologically associating domain (TAD), regulating expression of dosage-sensitive apoptotic or metabolic genes. Candidate targets include BCL2L11 (apoptosis induction, neuronal survival) or ACOX3 (peroxisomal metabolism). The silencer likely recruits co-repressor complexes (PRC2, NCoR/SMRT, HDACs) to deposit H3K27me3 marks at target promoters. The duplication bias (3:1 ratio) indicates that silencer gain causes excessive repression of BCL2L11, inhibiting apoptosis and potentially contributing to neurodevelopmental defects or cancer predisposition, while silencer loss may cause mild BCL2L11 overexpression with less severe consequences. The K562 validation suggests potential roles in hematopoietic lineage specification, possibly regulating apoptosis in erythroid progenitors. 2q11.2 synteny with BCL2L11 suggests potential cis-regulation of this critical apoptotic switch, contributing to 2q11.2 syndrome phenotypes.
