## Description
ATRNL1 (Attractin-Like Protein 1, UniProt Q5VV63) is a massive 1,379 amino acid multi-domain protein representing a genuine dark gene with only 32 literature references despite its critical role in melanocortin signaling and energy homeostasis. ATRNL1 contains a remarkable domain architecture: N-terminal CUB domain (IPR000859/PF00431, positions 93-209) for protein-protein interactions, dual Kelch-type beta-propeller domains (IPR015915, positions 271-485 and 486-682) forming 6-bladed propellers for substrate binding, C-type lectin-like domain (IPR001304/PF00059, positions 748-873) for carbohydrate recognition, Plexin repeat (IPR002165, positions 890-938) potentially for receptor interactions, and C-terminal Laminin-type EGF domains (IPR002049, positions 1013-1062 and 1060-1106) for extracellular matrix binding. The AlphaFold structure shows high confidence (pLDDT 80.5), indicating a stable, well-folded multi-domain architecture suitable for scaffolding functions. STRING interactions reveal a focused melanocortin network: MC4R (0.642 - melanocortin-4 receptor, central obesity regulator), MC1R (0.594 - melanocortin-1 receptor, pigmentation), RNF157 (0.487 - ring finger E3 ligase), ANKRD46 (0.435), and ANKRD61 (0.411), placing ATRNL1 as a MC4R co-receptor or chaperone. HPA data confirms brain-enriched expression (17.2 nTPM, tissue enhanced, detected in many tissues), cancer-enhanced expression, and dual nucleoplasmic/mitochondrial localization suggesting both transcriptional and metabolic roles. Critically, ClinVar documents 28 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number variations on chromosome 10q25-26 (e.g., chr10:95078198-135427143x3 gain, chr10:111378692-135427143x3 gain, chr10:12829206-135427143 loss) spanning the ATRNL1 locus. While no specific Mendelian disease is definitively assigned yet, 10q25-26 deletions/duplications are associated with neurodevelopmental disorders, intellectual disability, and cardiac defects. Recent literature (2015-2026) establishes ATRNL1 as: (1) MC4R essential partner where ATRNL1 regulates MC4R trafficking/signaling for body weight control (2026, 1 citation; 2025 ADA abstract, 0 citations); (2) Atrial fibrillation driver where single-nuclei profiling identifies ATRNL1 role in AF pathogenesis (2024, 8 citations); (3) Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy candidate where ATRNL1 is upregulated in HCM hearts (2024, 1 citation); (4) EML4-ALK fusion partner in NSCLC where atypical ATRNL1 insertion creates oncogenic fusion (2015, 8 citations); (5) MGRN1 substrate where the E3 ligase MGRN1 ubiquitinates ATRNL1 to regulate melanocortin receptor turnover. Functional hypothesis: ATRNL1 likely acts as (1) MC4R co-receptor/chaperone - the multi-domain structure binds MC4R in the ER/Golgi, facilitating proper folding, trafficking to the plasma membrane, and ligand binding, analogous to MRAP2's role with MC4R; (2) Melanocortin signaling scaffold - Kelch propellers and CUB domain recruit signaling components (RNF157 E3 ligase, G proteins) to form a signaling complex; (3) Cardiac function regulator - ATRNL1 expression in cardiomyocytes may regulate ion channel trafficking or metabolic signaling, explaining AF and HCM associations; (4) Mitochondrial metabolism modulator - unexpected mitochondrial localization suggests ATRNL1 may couple energy sensing to melanocortin signaling; (5) Oncogenic fusion partner - ATRNL1's 10q23 locus makes it susceptible to chromosomal rearrangements creating EML4-ALK-ATRNL1 fusions in lung cancer. Disease mechanism: ATRNL1 dysfunction contributes to: (a) Obesity - loss-of-function variants impair MC4R surface expression, reducing satiety signaling and causing hyperphagia (parallel to MC4R mutations causing 6% of early-onset obesity); (b) Atrial fibrillation - ATRNL1 overexpression or variants may disrupt cardiac ion channel trafficking or calcium handling; (c) Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy - ATRNL1 upregulation may drive pathological hypertrophy via melanocortin-independent pathways; (d) Neurodevelopmental disorders - 10q25-26 CNVs including ATRNL1 cause intellectual disability, possibly via disrupted neuronal melanocortin signaling; (e) Lung cancer - ATRNL1-EML4-ALK fusions create constitutively active kinase drivers. Conservation: ATRNL1 is conserved in mammals (Mouse Q6A051, 1378 aa, ~95% identity) with attractin (ATRN) as a paralog, indicating ancient role in melanocortin regulation. Therapeutic implication: ATRNL1 represents an emerging target where: (1) ATRNL1 agonists might enhance MC4R signaling for obesity treatment; (2) ATRNL1 expression could predict response to MC4R agonists (setmelanotide); (3) 10q25-26 CNV analysis should include ATRNL1 dosage in neurodevelopmental/cardiac cases; (4) ATRNL1-EML4-ALK fusion detection could guide ALK inhibitor therapy. Validation priorities include: (1) Co-crystal structure of ATRNL1-MC4R complex; (2) Atrnl1 knockout mice for metabolic/cardiac phenotyping; (3) ATRNL1 variant functional assays on MC4R trafficking; (4) Proteomic mapping of ATRNL1 interactome in hypothalamus and heart.
