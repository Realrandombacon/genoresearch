## Description
MYMK (Myomaker, also known as TMEM8C, TMEM226, protein myomaker) is a 221 amino acid plasma membrane protein (UniProt A6NI61) with very-high-confidence well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 90.4), characteristic of membrane fusogens that mediate cell-cell fusion during myogenesis. The protein contains an NGX6/PGAP6/MYMK family domain (IPR021910, residues 5-205) enabling membrane localization (GO:0016020), a protein of unknown function DUF3522 domain (PF12036, residues 5-185) representing the core fusogenic region, and belongs to the five-span transmembrane protein M83 family (PTHR14319, residues 5-205). MYMK is located on chromosome 9q34.2 and is classified as a predicted membrane protein, transporter, disease-related gene, and potential drug target involved in myogenesis (GO:0007517). These interactions confirm MYMK functions within the myogenic regulatory network, with MYMX as the highest-confidence partner (0.961) reflecting their obligate cooperation in myoblast fusion - MYMK and MYMX form a minimal fusogenic machinery sufficient to induce cell fusion.\n\nHPA expression reveals MYMK has RNA tissue specificity: Not detected and RNA tissue distribution: Not detected, indicating extremely low or highly restricted expression consistent with transient expression during myoblast fusion events. The identical protein length across species suggests strong purifying selection on the transmembrane topology.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: MYMK functions as a membrane fusogen that mediates myoblast-myoblast and myoblast-myotube fusion during skeletal muscle development and regeneration. The very-high-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 90.4) indicates a stable five-pass transmembrane topology with extracellular loops that mediate membrane merger. Through MYMX interaction (0.961), MYMK forms a heteromeric fusogenic complex where MYMK provides membrane curvature and MYMX promotes hemifusion stalk formation. MYF5/MYF6/MYOG/MYOD1 interactions (0.555/0.568/0.488/0.440) connect MYMK to the myogenic regulatory factor cascade that activates fusogen expression. PAX7 interaction (0.485) suggests MYMK is expressed in activated satellite cells during muscle regeneration. Transient expression (not detected in HPA bulk tissue) indicates MYMK is rapidly downregulated after fusion completion. 9q CNVs and p.Gln153Ter cause MYMK haploinsufficiency or loss-of-function, preventing myoblast fusion and causing hypotonia, facial weakness, and limb anomalies characteristic of Carey-Fineman-Ziter syndrome.
