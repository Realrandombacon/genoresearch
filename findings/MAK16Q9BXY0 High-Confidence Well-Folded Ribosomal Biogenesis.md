## Description
MAK16 (MAK16 homolog, RBM13, MAK16L) is a 300 amino acid intracellular protein localized to the nucleolus and vesicles (UniProt Q9BXY0) with high-confidence well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 74.3) containing canonical ribosomal biogenesis architecture including Mak16 protein family domain (IPR006958/PIRSF003352/PTHR23405, residues 1-299) encompassing nearly the full length, Ribosomal eL28/Mak16 domain (IPR029004/PF01778, residues 6-119) forming a ~114 aa core module (~38%) defining the structural fold shared with ribosomal protein eL28, Mak16 protein C-terminal region (PF04874, residues 139-237) forming a ~99 aa auxiliary domain (~33%), and molecular functions including RNA binding, maturation of 5.8S rRNA, maturation of large subunit (LSU) rRNA, 60S ribosomal subunit biogenesis, ubiquitous expression (detected in all tissues), low tissue/cancer/immune specificity, predicted intracellular and essential proteins classification, functioning as a high-confidence well-folded nucleolar scaffold that coordinates late-stage 60S subunit assembly through eL28-like RNA binding, C-terminal protein recruitment, and MAK16 network assembly via partnerships with EBNA1BP2, RPF1, WDR74, NOC3L, GTPBP4, FTSJ3, WDR12, NIFK, BRIX1, and RSL24D1.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals MAK16 is a conserved factor essential for the production of mature 60S ribosomal subunits. The N-terminal domain (residues 6-119) shares structural homology with ribosomal protein eL28, suggesting MAK16 may transiently occupy the eL28 binding site on pre-60S particles or act as a chaperone to facilitate eL28 incorporation. The distinct C-terminal region (residues 139-237) likely serves as a platform for recruiting other biogenesis factors or modifying enzymes. The high-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 74.3) confirms a stable two-domain architecture suitable for bridging RNA substrates and protein cofactors within the dense environment of the nucleolus.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places MAK16 at the heart of a highly connected 'ribosome biogenesis interactome' with exceptionally high confidence scores (>0.95 for top partners): strongest interactions with EBNA1BP2 (0.999, a nucleolar protein involved in rRNA processing) and RPF1 (0.998, Required for Processing of 60S subunits), indicating a direct functional partnership in pre-60S maturation. High confidence links also include WDR74 (0.997), NOC3L (0.997), and GTPBP4 (0.997), all established components of the pre-60S assembly line. The cluster of interactions with FTSJ3 (methyltransferase), WDR12 (PeBoW complex), NIFK, BRIX1, and RSL24D1 confirms MAK16's role in the sequential handover of pre-60S particles through distinct maturation states, potentially coordinating methylation, GTPase activation, and nuclear export.\n\nHPA expression shows ubiquitous detection across all tissues with low specificity, consistent with an essential housekeeping role in ribosome production required for cell growth and proliferation in every cell type. Subcellular location is confirmed as nucleoli (the site of rRNA synthesis and early assembly) and vesicles (potentially reflecting transport intermediates or cytoplasmic pre-60S particles).\n\nClinVar documents 66 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains affecting the 8p11.1-p23.3 region: CNV gains (8p21.3-11.1, 8p23.3-11.21, 8p21.2-q21.3, 8p23.3-q24.3 duplications) and one duplication event. The high variant burden indicates strong dosage sensitivity of the 8p locus. Haploinsufficiency (though less represented in ClinVar than gains) would likely cause severe ribosomopathy phenotypes due to impaired 60S production, while duplications may lead to stoichiometric imbalances in the assembly line, causing aggregation of incomplete pre-60S particles and nucleolar stress, contributing to developmental delays and congenital anomalies associated with 8p duplication syndromes.\n\nConservation is strict across eukaryotes: human (Q9BXY0, 300 aa), mouse (Q8BGS0, 296 aa), yeast (P10962, 306 aa), indicating fundamental preservation of the ribosome assembly mechanism.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: MAK16 acts as a transient assembly factor that binds pre-60S particles via its eL28-like domain, stabilizing the rRNA folding landscape near the polypeptide exit tunnel. It recruits the RPF1-EBNA1BP2 complex to facilitate correct processing of the 27S pre-rRNA into 5.8S and 25S/28S rRNAs. The C-terminal domain then engages WDR74 and NOC3L to license the particle for nuclear export. By coordinating these steps, MAK16 ensures only properly assembled subunits reach the cytoplasm. Dysregulation via 8p CNVs disrupts this precision: loss causes ribosome shortage and p53 activation (cell cycle arrest/apoptosis), while gain creates toxic 'traffic jams' in the nucleolus, driving the pathogenesis of 8p syndromes characterized by growth retardation and intellectual disability.
