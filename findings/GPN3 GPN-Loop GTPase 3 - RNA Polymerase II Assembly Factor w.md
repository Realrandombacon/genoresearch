## Description
GPN3 (GPN-loop GTPase 3, UniProt Q9UHW5) is a 284 amino acid nuclear/cytosolic protein functioning as an essential RNA polymerase II (RNAPII) assembly and nuclear import factor. With only 24 literature references (partially characterized dark gene status), GPN3 has emerging roles in cancer progression, transcription machinery biogenesis, and EGFR trafficking. The protein contains a conserved GPN-loop GTPase domain (IPR004130, IPR030228, PF03029, aa 8-254) belonging to the P-loop NTPase superfamily (IPR027417, G3DSA:3.40.50.300), characteristic of GTP-binding proteins that undergo conformational changes during nucleotide hydrolysis. AlphaFold predicts a well-folded structure (pLDDT 86.2, high confidence) across all 284 residues, indicating a stable globular GTPase fold. STRING interactions reveal high-confidence partnerships with RNA Pol II machinery: RPAP2 (0.955 - RNAPII assembly factor), POLR2F (0.913), POLR2L (0.911), POLR2D (0.908), POLR1C (0.908), POLR2B (0.903), POLR2A (0.889), POLR2C (0.723), and GPN1 (0.648 - paralogous GTPase), confirming integration into the RNAPII biogenesis pathway. HPA shows ubiquitous expression across all tissues with dual nuclear speckle and cytosolic localization, consistent with nucleocytoplasmic shuttling function. ClinVar documents 10 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number gains/losses at 12q23-24 and a 5' UTR variant (c.-21C>T), though specific GPN3-associated diseases require further characterization. Recent 2025 research demonstrates GPN3 facilitates non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) cell proliferation and migration by impeding clathrin-mediated endocytosis of EGFR, revealing an unexpected role in receptor trafficking beyond transcription. 2024 studies show GPN3 nucleocytoplasmic shuttling is regulated by serum and cell density in mammary cells. 2020-2022 work established that GPN3 (yeast Npa3) cooperates with assembly factors Rba50 and GPN1 to prevent cytoplasmic clumping of RNAPII subunits and facilitate proper enzyme assembly. 2019 research confirmed GPN3 is essential for breast cancer cell proliferation independent of malignancy degree. Functional hypothesis: GPN3 acts as a GTP-dependent molecular switch that binds nascent RNAPII subunits in the cytoplasm, using GTP hydrolysis to drive conformational changes required for subunit assembly and nuclear import through the nuclear pore complex. The 2025 EGFR trafficking discovery suggests GPN3 may have broader roles in vesicular transport, potentially linking transcription machinery biogenesis to growth factor receptor signaling in cancer. The GPN1-GPN3 paralogous pair may provide redundancy or specialized functions in RNAPII vs RNAPIII assembly.
