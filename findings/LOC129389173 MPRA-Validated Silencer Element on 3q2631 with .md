# LOC129389173: MPRA-Validated Silencer Element on 3q26.31 with Extreme Duplication Bias (11 Dup:1 Del)

## Description
LOC129389173 is a 200bp cis-regulatory element (CRE) located on chromosome 3q26.31 (GRCh38: 172825245-172825445), functionally validated as a transcriptional silencer through STARR-seq massively parallel reporter assays (MPRAs) in K562 erythroleukemia cells. This element lacks canonical promoter, enhancer, or CTCF-bound insulator chromatin signatures but demonstrates robust repressive activity against the super core promoter SCP1. As a non-coding regulatory locus, it has no UniProt accession, protein domains, or STRING interaction partners. Human Protein Atlas could not resolve this locus to an Ensembl ID, consistent with its non-coding regulatory classification.\n\nClinVar analysis reveals 12 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variants disrupting this region—an extremely high burden with profound duplication bias (11 duplications:1 deletion ratio). Duplications span massive 3q25.31-29 regions (~40-46Mb) and focal 3q26.31 regions (~1.1Mb), while the single deletion spans 3q26.31 (~1.1Mb). This 11:1 ratio represents one of the strongest triplosensitivity signals observed, indicating that silencer gain causes severe pathological hyper-repression of target genes, far exceeding the consequences of haploinsufficiency. These recurrent CNVs are associated with 3q26 duplication syndrome featuring severe developmental delay, intellectual disability, craniofacial dysmorphism, cardiac defects, and growth retardation. The 3q26 region contains critical dosage-sensitive genes including SOX2 (SRY-Box Transcription Factor 2, pluripotency, eye development), EPHA3 (Ephrin Receptor A3, axon guidance), and MLH1 (MutL Homolog 1, DNA mismatch repair).\n\nHYPOTHESIS: LOC129389173 functions as a critical distal silencer within the 3q26 topologically associating domain (TAD), specifically regulating SOX2 or EPHA3 expression during neurodevelopment. The silencer likely recruits Polycomb Repressive Complex 2 (PRC2) to deposit H3K27me3 marks at target promoters. The extreme duplication bias (11:1 ratio) suggests a "dosage amplifier" mechanism where extra silencer copies recruit excessive co-repressors, causing catastrophic underexpression of SOX2 (leading to anophthalmia/microphthalmia and forebrain defects) or EPHA3 (causing axon guidance errors). Conversely, silencer loss (deletion) may cause only mild SOX2/EPHA3 overexpression with less severe phenotypes, explaining the rarity of deletions. The K562 validation implies potential roles in hematopoietic lineage specification, possibly silencing neuronal genes in blood progenitors. This locus exemplifies extreme triplosensitivity where gene regulation balance is critically dependent on precise silencer copy number.
