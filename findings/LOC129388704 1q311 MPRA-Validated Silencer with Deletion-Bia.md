## Description
LOC129388704 is a 200bp cis-regulatory element (CRE) located on chromosome 1q31.1 (GRCh38: 192165104-192165304), functionally validated as a transcriptional silencer through STARR-seq massively parallel reporter assays (MPRAs) in K562 erythroleukemia cells. This locus lacks canonical promoter, enhancer, or CTCF-bound insulator chromatin signatures but demonstrates robust repressive activity against the super core promoter SCP1. As a non-coding regulatory sequence, it has no UniProt accession, protein domains, STRING interaction partners, or Human Protein Atlas expression data (query failed to resolve Ensembl ID).\n\nClinVar analysis reveals 5 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variants disrupting this region. The variant spectrum is predominantly composed of large-scale deletions (copy number losses), including events spanning 1q25.2-31.3 (~19.7Mb), 1q25.2-32.1 (~20.7Mb), and 1q31.1-32.1 (~10.6Mb). A single large-scale duplication (1q31.1-42.11, ~37.2Mb) is also present, yielding a deletion-to-duplication ratio of 4:1. This bias suggests that haploinsufficiency of the silencer (leading to target gene overexpression) is the primary driver of pathology, though triplication effects are also clinically significant. These CNVs are associated with 1q deletion/duplication syndromes, characterized by developmental delay, intellectual disability, growth retardation, and distinctive facial features. The 1q31 region contains critical genes such as RGS7 (Regulator of G-protein Signaling 7) and members of the Regulator of G-protein Signaling family, which are crucial for neuronal signaling and potentially hematopoietic differentiation given the K562 validation context.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: LOC129388704 functions as a critical distal silencer within the 1q31 topologically associating domain (TAD), specifically regulating genes involved in G-protein signaling cascades or erythroid maturation. Given its validation in K562 cells, it likely represses proto-oncogenes or lineage-inappropriate transcription factors in normal erythroid progenitors. The mechanism involves recruitment of co-repressor complexes (e.g., PRC2, NuRD) to deposit repressive histone marks (H3K27me3) at target promoters. Loss of this silencer (via 1q deletions) leads to aberrant overexpression of target genes (potentially RGS7 or neighbors), contributing to the neurodevelopmental defects and potential hematologic anomalies seen in 1q syndrome patients. Conversely, duplication may lead to ectopic silencing or altered chromatin topology, disrupting normal gene expression patterns. The presence of both loss and gain events highlights the dosage sensitivity of the regulated targets, requiring precise stoichiometric control for proper development.
