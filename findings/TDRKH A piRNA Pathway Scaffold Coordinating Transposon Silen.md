# TDRKH: A piRNA Pathway Scaffold Coordinating Transposon Silencing and Spermatogenesis via Tudor-KH Domain Architecture

**Date:** 2026-06-26T16:38:46.354215

**Quality Score:** 9/10 (EXCELLENT)

**Confidence:** MEDIUM

## Description
TDRKH (Tudor and KH domain-containing protein, UniProt Q9Y2W6), also known as TDRD2, is a 561-amino acid intracellular protein that serves as a critical scaffold in the piRNA (PIWI-interacting RNA) pathway essential for transposon silencing during spermatogenesis. InterPro analysis reveals a modular RNA-binding architecture: two N-terminal K-homology (KH) domains (IPR004087, IPR004088, aa 49-205) that recognize single-stranded RNA sequences, and a C-terminal Tudor domain (IPR002999, aa 303-422) that specifically binds methylated arginine residues on PIWI proteins. The AlphaFold predicted structure shows confident folding (pLDDT 74.4), supporting a stable bipartite conformation suitable for simultaneous RNA and protein interactions. STRING interaction data places TDRKH at the center of the piRNA biogenesis machinery: strong associations with PIWIL2 (0.904) and PIWIL4 (0.739), the core Argonaute-family effectors, and other Tudor-domain proteins TDRD9 (0.883), TDRD12 (0.819), and MAEL (0.758), which together form the 'pi-body' granules in germ cells. Links to the RNA helicase MOV10L1 (0.507) suggest involvement in piRNA precursor processing. HPA expression profiles show tissue enhancement in testis (20.7 nTPM) and parathyroid gland (28.3 nTPM), with subcellular localization to centrosomes and cytosol—consistent with roles in meiotic spindle organization and cytoplasmic granule assembly. ClinVar records document 18 pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number losses and gains at 1q21.3, implying that gene dosage is critical for fertility. We hypothesize that TDRKH functions as a 'molecular bridge' that recruits PIWI-piRNA complexes to target transposon transcripts via its KH domains while anchoring them to germ granules through Tudor-mediated protein interactions. This dual recognition ensures efficient transposon silencing during the vulnerable window of meiotic recombination. Disruption of TDRKH would lead to transposon reactivation, DNA damage in germ cells, and male infertility, phenotypes observed in Tdrkh knockout mice.
