## Description
FAM193A encodes a 1515-amino acid large scaffold protein containing conserved FAM193 family domains (IPR029717/PF15914/PTHR15109, residues 1-1515) with an N-terminal domain (IPR060283/PF27002, residues 292-463) and C-terminal domain (IPR031802/PF15914, residues 1458-1514), characteristic of large scaffolding proteins that organize multiprotein complexes through modular interaction interfaces. AlphaFold structure prediction (pLDDT=50.1) indicates low-to-medium confidence folding with extensive intrinsically disordered regions, consistent with large scaffold proteins that use conformational flexibility to bind multiple partners and undergo post-translational regulation. Protein interaction analysis reveals functional coherence across transcription regulation, nucleocytoplasmic transport, and cell cycle control: DCTN2 (0.756, dynactin subunit 2, dynactin complex component for dynein-mediated microtubule transport), TAF1 (0.556, TATA-box binding protein associated factor 1, RNA polymerase II transcription initiation factor), ZNF318 (0.545, zinc finger protein 318, transcriptional regulator), NOP14 (0.482, nucleolar protein 14, ribosome biogenesis), EIF4ENIF1 (0.469, eukaryotic translation initiation factor 4E nuclear import factor 1, mRNA nuclear export), SH3BP2 (0.459, SH3 domain binding protein 2, signal transduction adaptor), MDM4 (0.414, MDM4 proto-oncogene, p53 pathway negative regulator), and DCTN4 (0.407, dynactin subunit 4, dynactin complex). The DCTN2-DCTN4-TAF1 triad strongly suggests FAM193A functions as a nuclear-cytoplasmic scaffold coordinating transcription machinery (TAF1) with microtubule-based transport (DCTN2/4), analogous to scaffold proteins that spatially organize nuclear pore complexes and transcription factories. Tissue expression shows ubiquitous detection across all tissues with low tissue specificity, plasma membrane and cytosol subcellular localization, and predicted intracellular protein classification, indicating housekeeping role in fundamental cellular processes where nuclear-cytoplasmic coordination is critical. Clinical significance includes 132 pathogenic/likely pathogenic ClinVar variants, predominantly copy number gains and losses spanning 4p16.3-15.33 region, indicating strong dosage sensitivity where gene amplification or haploinsufficiency disrupts transcription-transport homeostasis and may contribute to developmental disorders and cancer predisposition. The combination of FAM193 domain architecture, large size (1515 aa), partial structural disorder, ubiquitous expression, DCTN2-TAF1-MDM4 interaction network, and extreme CNV dosage sensitivity strongly suggests FAM193A functions as a large intrinsically disordered nuclear-cytoplasmic scaffold protein that spatially organizes transcription initiation complexes (TAF1) with dynein-dynactin transport machinery (DCTN2/4) and cell cycle regulators (MDM4), potentially regulating gene expression through nucleocytoplasmic shuttling and chromatin organization.
