## Description
LOC126807002 is an uncharacterized cis-regulatory element (CRE) at chromosome 4p15.33 (genomic coordinates 23234406-23235605, ~1.2kb span). This locus is NOT a protein-coding gene but a functional regulatory DNA element validated through STARR-seq massively parallel reporter assays (MPRAs). The gene_info summary identifies this as a CDK7 STRONGLY-DEPENDENT GROUP 2 ENHANCER that was experimentally validated in HCT116 colorectal carcinoma cells. Critically, this enhancer depends on BRD2, BRD4, P300/CBP, MED14, and CDK7 cofactors with STRONG DEPENDENCE on CDK7 (a transcription-associated kinase that phosphorylates RNA polymerase II C-terminal domain for transcriptional elongation). This cofactor profile suggests it operates through transcriptional elongation control mechanisms. ClinVar contains 10 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants affecting this locus through large-scale chromosomal rearrangements. This 7:3 gain-to-loss ratio (70% amplification) suggests oncogene or dosage-sensitive developmental gene regulation. The 4p region contains WHSC1 (Wolf-Hirschhorn syndrome candidate 1, 4p16.3), FGFR3 (fibroblast growth factor receptor 3, 4p16.3), and multiple developmental genes. No UniProt accession exists (non-coding DNA), STRING found no interactions (not a protein), HPA could not resolve (not protein-coding), and PubMed has zero publications for this specific locus. This represents a genuine dark regulatory element with clinical relevance — the CDK7-dependent enhancer activity combined with amplification-dominant pathogenicity suggests LOC126807002 functions as an oncogene-specific or developmental gene enhancer where amplification drives overexpression.
