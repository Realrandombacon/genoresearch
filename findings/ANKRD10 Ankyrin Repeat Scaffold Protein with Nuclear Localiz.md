## Description
ANKRD10 (Ankyrin Repeat Domain 10) is a 420 amino acid nuclear protein (UniProt Q9NXR5) characterized by a prominent N-terminal ankyrin repeat domain spanning residues 15-222. InterPro analysis confirms multiple ankyrin repeats (IPR002110, PF00023) and classifies it within the "Ankyrin Repeat and Cyclin-Dependent Kinase Inhibitor" family (IPR050776), suggesting a role in protein-protein interactions potentially regulating cell cycle or signaling pathways. The Human Protein Atlas reports ubiquitous expression across all tissues with low specificity, localized specifically to the nucleoplasm, consistent with a housekeeping scaffold function. AlphaFold prediction (pLDDT 63.7) indicates a structured ankyrin solenoid core with potentially flexible C-terminal regions. STRING interaction data reveals medium-confidence associations with TEN1 (telomere maintenance), TMEM52, and NFKBIA (NF-kappa-B inhibitor), hinting at involvement in genomic stability or inflammatory signaling modulation. Critically, ClinVar lists 113 pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number losses (deletions) on chromosome 13q, implicating ANKRD10 haploinsufficiency in developmental disorders or cancer susceptibility, although specific disease phenotypes remain aggregated under broad cytogenetic bands. Conservation and mechanistic hypothesis: As an ankyrin-repeat containing nuclear protein interacting with telomere and NF-kB components, ANKRD10 likely functions as a modular scaffold assembling complexes for DNA damage response or transcriptional regulation. The high burden of pathogenic CNVs suggests dosage sensitivity, where reduced expression disrupts nuclear architecture or signaling fidelity.
