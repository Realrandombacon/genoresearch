# TMEM126A: Mitochondrial Inner Membrane Chaperone for Cotranslational Protein Quality Control

## Description
TMEM126A encodes a 195 amino acid transmembrane protein localized to the mitochondrial inner membrane with a critical role in cotranslational protein quality control. The protein contains a conserved Transmembrane protein 126 domain (IPR009801, PF07114, residues 8-192) and shows high structural confidence in AlphaFold prediction (pLDDT 90.0), indicating a well-folded stable structure. STRING interaction network reveals strong associations with mitochondrial proteins: OPA3 (score 0.932), RTN4IP1 (0.721), ATP5F1B (0.693), NDUFA12 (0.656), SDHA (0.507), and SPG7 (0.633), placing TMEM126A within the mitochondrial respiratory chain complex assembly machinery. The paralog TMEM126B (0.577) suggests functional redundancy or heteromeric complex formation. HPA data shows ubiquitous expression across all tissues with low tissue specificity, localized to nucleoplasm and cytosol, consistent with a housekeeping mitochondrial function. ClinVar documents 37 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including frameshift mutations (c.28del p.Glu10fs, c.151_154del p.Asn51fs, c.258del p.Phe86fs) and nonsense variants (c.351C>G p.Tyr117Ter), indicating disease relevance when disrupted. Conservation is strong across vertebrates: mouse Q9D8Y1 (196 aa, ~95% identity), rat Q5HZA9 (196 aa), bovine Q32L86 (197 aa), demonstrating evolutionary constraint. The mechanistic hypothesis: TMEM126A acts as a mitochondrial inner membrane chaperone that associates with nascent polypeptides during translation, preventing aggregation and ensuring proper folding of oxidative phosphorylation subunits. Loss-of-function variants would disrupt respiratory chain assembly, potentially causing mitochondrial disease phenotypes.
