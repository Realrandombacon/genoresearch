## Description
FXYD7 encodes FXYD domain-containing ion transport regulator 7 (FXYD7/CHIF-like protein), a small 80 aa single-pass transmembrane protein (UniProt P58549) belonging to the FXYD family of ion transport regulators that functions as a tissue-specific modulator of Na+/K+-ATPase (sodium-potassium pump) activity critical for neuronal membrane potential maintenance, ion homeostasis, and excitability. InterPro reveals characteristic FXYD family architecture: FXYD domain-containing ion transport regulator 7 signature (cd20325/IPR047284, residues 12-62) defining subfamily membership within the broader FXYD regulatory protein superfamily, ion-transport regulator FXYD family classification (IPR000272/PF02038/PTHR14132, residues 13-60/8-77) with associated GO molecular functions including ion channel regulator activity (GO:0099106) and regulation of monoatomic ion transport (GO:0043269), FXYD motif conserved site (IPR047297/PS01310, residues 16-29) containing the invariant PFXYD signature sequence essential for Na+/K+-ATPase binding and regulatory function, single helix bin structural classification (G3DSA:1.20.5.780, residues 10-75) indicating a transmembrane alpha-helix structure typical of small regulatory subunits that associate with larger pump complexes. AlphaFold predicts high confidence structure (pLDDT 70.6) indicating a well-folded compact transmembrane protein with stable alpha-helical conformation suitable for integration into the Na+/K+-ATPase holoenzyme and precise positioning of regulatory residues at the pump-regulator interface. STRING reveals strong functional clustering with Na+/K+-ATPase subunits and FXYD family paralogs: ATP1B1 (0.897, sodium/potassium-transporting ATPase subunit beta-1 - core pump component), FXYD4 (0.829, phospholemman-like protein paralog regulating cardiac/skeletal muscle pumps), FXYD3 (0.774, MAT-8 paralog in epithelial tissues), FXYD5 (0.682, dysadherin paralog implicated in cancer metastasis), SYP (0.626, synaptophysin synaptic vesicle protein suggesting neuronal localization), ATP1A3 (0.620, sodium/potassium-transporting ATPase subunit alpha-3 neuron-specific isoform), ATP1A2 (0.599, alpha-2 isoform in glial cells), GFAP (0.588, glial fibrillary acidic protein astrocyte marker), ATP1A4 (0.562, alpha-4 isoform), ATP1B2 (0.553, beta-2 isoform). HPA shows brain-enriched expression pattern (tissue enriched specificity) with top expression in brain tissue at 322.5 nTPM indicating specialized neuronal/glial function, detected in many tissues reflecting broader physiological roles, classified as predicted membrane protein and transporter with group-enriched cancer specificity and immune cell-enhanced pattern suggesting potential roles in tumor ion homeostasis and immune cell activation. ClinVar lists 17 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants all consisting of large copy number variations affecting chromosome 19q region: copy number gains (19q11-13.2 chr19:28271106-43255204, 19q11-13.2 chr19:28271146-41508851, 19p13.11-q13.2 chr19:19546923-41313229, 19q11-13.33 chr19:28271106-49213832), copy number losses (19q11-13.13 chr19:28271107-38637350, 19q13.11-13.12 chr19:35223021-36895699, 19q13.11-13.12 chr19:35043556-36316644, 19q13.11-13.12 chr19:35111811-37744992), and deletions though no specific monogenic disease phenotype is currently definitively assigned to FXYD7 alone suggesting contribution to 19q deletion/duplication syndromes through haploinsufficiency or dosage effects disrupting neuronal ion homeostasis and membrane excitability. Functional mechanism: FXYD7 operates through Na+/K+-ATPase regulatory subunit model: (1) Complex assembly - FXYD7 transmembrane helix associates with alpha-beta heterodimeric Na+/K+-ATPase core complex through hydrophobic interactions at the membrane interface; (2) Kinetic modulation - FXYD7 binding alters pump affinity for sodium and potassium ions, Vmax of ATP hydrolysis, and voltage-dependence of pump activity; (3) Tissue specificity - brain-enriched expression (322.5 nTPM) suggests FXYD7 fine-tunes pump properties for neuronal requirements distinct from cardiac (FXYD1/phospholamban), skeletal muscle (FXYD4), or epithelial (FXYD3/MAT-8) contexts; (4) Isoform selectivity - STRING interactions with ATP1A3 (neuronal alpha-3) and ATP1A2 (glial alpha-2) suggest preferential regulation of brain-specific pump isoforms; (5) Synaptic function - SYP/synaptophysin interaction indicates potential localization to synaptic vesicles or presynaptic terminals where rapid ion flux restoration is critical; (6) Astrocyte role - GFAP association suggests FXYD7 involvement in astrocytic potassium buffering and glutamate clearance mechanisms. Disease mechanism: FXYD7 CNVs likely cause disease through haploinsufficiency reducing Na+/K+-ATPase regulatory capacity in neurons leading to altered membrane potentials, impaired action potential propagation, disrupted neurotransmitter reuptake, and neuronal hyperexcitability or hypoexcitability depending on direction of kinetic shift; dosage effects from duplications may cause opposite phenotypes through pump over-regulation; brain-enriched expression predicts neurological phenotypes including epilepsy, developmental delay, movement disorders, or cognitive impairment consistent with 19q syndromes; FXYD family member interactions suggest potential compensatory mechanisms through FXYD3/4/5 upregulation though tissue specificity may limit compensation in brain; recent literature reveals FXYD proteins as dynamic regulators responding to phosphorylation by PKA/PKC, redox state through cysteine modifications, and lipid environment suggesting FXYD7 may integrate multiple signaling inputs to tune pump activity; ATP1A3 mutations cause alternating hemiplegia of childhood and rapid-onset dystonia-parkinsonism establishing Na+/K+-ATPase dysfunction as causative for severe neurological disease supporting FXYD7 contribution to similar phenotypes through regulatory rather than catalytic disruption. Despite mechanistic understanding of FXYD family Na+/K+-ATPase regulation and brain-enriched expression, complete tissue-specific interactomes beyond STRING predictions especially neuronal versus glial contexts, regulatory post-translational modifications controlling FXYD7 stability versus pump affinity, specific kinetic parameters altered by FXYD7 binding (Km for Na+/K+, Vmax, voltage-dependence), genotype-phenotype correlations for 19q CNV syndromes attributable to FXYD7 versus neighboring genes, isoform-specific functions from alternative splicing, structural basis for FXYD7-alpha subunit interface and comparison with other FXYD paralogs, therapeutic strategies for pump modulation in neurological disorders, and role in synaptic transmission versus astrocytic buffering remain incompletely characterized - representing genuine dark aspects of this critical ion homeostasis regulator bridging membrane biophysics with neuronal physiology and neurodevelopment.
