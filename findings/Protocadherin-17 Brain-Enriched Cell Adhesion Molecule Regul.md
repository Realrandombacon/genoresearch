## Description
PCDH17 encodes a 1159 amino acid non-clustered protocadherin (UniProt O14917) functioning as a calcium-dependent cell adhesion molecule critical for neural circuit formation and vascular integrity. The protein contains six extracellular cadherin repeats (EC1-EC6) mediating homophilic binding, a transmembrane domain, and a unique cytoplasmic tail distinct from classical cadherins. InterPro analysis confirms multiple cadherin domains (IPR002126, PF00028, IPR050174) spanning residues 15-875, with GO terms for calcium ion binding and homophilic cell-cell adhesion. HPA data shows tissue-enhanced expression highest in lymphoid tissue (23.6 nTPM), brain (14.7 nTPM), and lung (13.0 nTPM), with subcellular localization to plasma membrane and vesicles. STRING interactions reveal connections to other protocadherins (PCDH12 score 0.682, PCDHGC5 0.437) and FCGR1A (0.735), suggesting roles in immune-neural crosstalk or clustered adhesion complexes. Recent literature (128 papers) highlights specific functions: (1) PCDH17 restricts dendritic spine morphogenesis via ROCK2-dependent actin cytoskeleton control, modulating emotional behavior; (2) PCDH17 induces colorectal cancer metastasis by disrupting vascular endothelial barriers; (3) Circulating PCDH17 DNA methylation correlates with rheumatoid arthritis. ClinVar lists 34 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number losses/gains at 13q21.1, though specific monogenic diseases are not yet fully delineated. AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 67.7) indicates medium confidence with ordered extracellular domains and potentially flexible intracellular regions. Conservation is implied by presence in zebrafish (C4P342, 1137 aa). FUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: PCDH17 acts as a selective homophilic adhesion receptor that stabilizes specific synaptic connections in the brain and maintains endothelial barrier function; dysregulation leads to neurodevelopmental defects (via spine morphology errors) or enhanced tumor metastasis (via vascular leakage). MECHANISTIC PROPOSAL: Upon homophilic engagement, PCDH17 recruits ROCK2 to the cytoplasmic tail, locally activating RhoA signaling to remodel actin filaments, thereby controlling spine size/density and endothelial junction tension.
