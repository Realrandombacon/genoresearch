# GUK1: A Highly Conserved P-Loop Nucleotide Kinase Catalyzing GMP Phosphorylation and Serving as a Hub for Purine Metabolism

**Quality Score:** 10/10 (EXCELLENT)

## Description
GUK1 (Guanylate Kinase 1, UniProt Q16774) is a compact 197-amino acid enzyme that catalyzes the ATP-dependent phosphorylation of guanosine monophosphate (GMP) to guanosine diphosphate (GDP), a critical step in the purine nucleotide salvage pathway and the maintenance of cellular GTP pools. InterPro analysis reveals a canonical P-loop nucleotide kinase fold: a central GMPK domain (IPR008144, PF00625, cd00071, aa 4-186) containing the signature P-loop motif (IPR027417, aa 1-195) for ATP binding and a conserved catalytic site (IPR020590, aa 39-56) featuring the essential arginine finger and aspartate residues for phosphate transfer. The AlphaFold predicted structure shows exceptional confidence (pLDDT 93.9), reflecting a rigid, well-folded catalytic core typical of housekeeping enzymes with high evolutionary constraint. STRING interaction data places GUK1 within a tight purine metabolism network with very high confidence associations to GMPS (0.978), which synthesizes GMP from XMP; APRT (0.971), involved in adenine salvage; NME2 (0.946) and AK9 (0.945), other nucleoside diphosphate kinases; ITPA (0.944), which hydrolyzes non-canonical purine nucleotides; and HPRT1 (0.871), the primary salvage enzyme converting hypoxanthine/guanine to IMP/GMP. HPA expression profiles confirm ubiquitous presence ('Detected in all') with significant enhancement in brain tissue (463.7 nTPM), consistent with the high energy and signaling demands of neurons; subcellular localization includes the Golgi apparatus and plasma membrane, suggesting potential roles beyond cytosolic metabolism, possibly in local GTP supply for vesicle trafficking or G-protein signaling. ClinVar records document 44 pathogenic variants including copy number gains/losses at 1q42.13, nonsense mutations (p.Gln47Ter), splice defects (c.61+1G>T), and missense changes (p.Gly32Arg), linked to mitochondrial disease phenotypes (MTDPS21). We hypothesize that GUK1 functions as the gatekeeper for GDP production: by efficiently converting GMP to GDP, it prevents GMP accumulation and ensures a steady supply of substrate for nucleoside diphosphate kinases (NMEs) to generate GTP. The high expression in brain suggests particular vulnerability to GUK1 deficiency, where impaired GTP recycling could disrupt microtubule dynamics, synaptic vesicle cycling, or Ras-family GTPase signaling, contributing to the neurological manifestations of MTDPS21. The structural rigidity implies that most pathogenic variants likely destabilize the fold or directly disrupt the active site, leading to complete loss of function.
