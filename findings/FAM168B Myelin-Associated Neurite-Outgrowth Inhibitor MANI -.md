Conservation across vertebrates: Mus musculus (194 aa, Q80XQ8), Rattus norvegicus (194 aa, D4AEP3) indicates strong functional constraint. Functional hypothesis: FAM168B functions as intracellular regulator of axonal regeneration and neuronal plasticity via RTN4R-dependent signaling. Mechanistic proposal: (1) FAM168B interacts with RTN4R/Nogo receptor complex at growth cone membrane, modulating RhoA/ROCK pathway activation that collapses growth cones and inhibits axonal extension; (2) WSB2 interaction suggests regulation via ubiquitin-proteasome degradation - FAM168B may be degraded upon axonal injury to permit regeneration; (3) ALKBH5 interaction links to epitranscriptomic regulation (m6A demethylation) coordinating local translation in growth cones; (4) ubiquitous expression suggests housekeeping role in membrane trafficking or cytoskeletal dynamics beyond neuronal contexts; (5) PF14944 domain homology to tongue cancer chemotherapy resistance protein suggests potential role in drug efflux or DNA damage response. Pathogenicity mechanism: haploinsufficiency (deletions) reduces FAM168B levels impairing RTN4R signaling fidelity, potentially causing aberrant axonal guidance during neurodevelopment; triplosensitivity (duplications) causes excessive inhibition of neurite outgrowth contributing to intellectual disability. 2q21.1 focal deletions directly disrupt FAM168B locus, predicting specific neurological phenotypes.
