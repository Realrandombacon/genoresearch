## Description
CUEDC1 (CUE Domain-Containing Protein 1) is a 386 amino acid intracellular protein featuring a canonical CUE (Coupling of Ubiquitin Conjugation to ER Degradation) domain that mediates ubiquitin binding and protein-protein interactions in ubiquitin-dependent signaling pathways. InterPro analysis identifies a conserved CUE domain (IPR003892, PF02845, PS51140, SM00546, residues 46-89) with ubiquitin binding capability (GO:0043130), classified within the UBA-like superfamily (IPR009060, SSF46934, residues 41-93) and the CUEDC1-specific family (IPR040192, IPR040195, PTHR13467, residues 36-385). AlphaFold structure (AF-Q9NWM3-F1) predicts medium confidence (pLDDT 61.7) across the 386 residue sequence, suggesting a partially structured protein with an ordered CUE domain core connected to flexible regulatory regions, consistent with proteins that undergo conformational changes during ubiquitin recognition. HPA data classifies CUEDC1 as a predicted intracellular protein with low tissue specificity (detected in all tissues), immune cell-enriched expression, and subcellular localization to plasma membrane, microtubules, cytokinetic bridge, mitotic spindle, and primary cilium, implicating roles in cell division and ciliary signaling. STRING interactions reveal functional connections to CUEDC2 (0.530), its paralog sharing the CUE domain architecture; CDR2L (0.524), a cerebellar degeneration-related antigen; MRPS23 (0.481), a mitochondrial ribosomal protein; TOM1 (0.477), involved in endosomal trafficking; SMURF2 (0.452), an E3 ubiquitin ligase regulating TGF-β signaling; and ARRDC3 (0.450), an arrestin-domain protein mediating ubiquitin-dependent cargo sorting. ClinVar lists 16 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number losses and gains on chromosome 17q22, deletions spanning 17q12-22, though no specific monogenic syndrome is yet defined. Literature (28 papers) establishes CUEDC1 as an oncogenic driver in multiple cancers: promotes hepatocellular carcinoma growth, migration, and epithelial-mesenchymal transition via TGF-β/Smad signaling through TβRI regulation; enhances ER-positive breast cancer progression by reprogramming glycolytic metabolism via the CUEDC1/CACNG4/PI3K axis; suppresses JAK1/STAT3 signaling in esophageal cancer; and interacts with Smad2/3 to modulate TGF-β receptor stability through ubiquitin-mediated degradation. As a dark gene with emerging therapeutic relevance, CUEDC1 represents a critical ubiquitin-binding adaptor bridging protein degradation machinery to oncogenic signaling pathways, with potential as a biomarker and therapeutic target in TGF-β-driven malignancies.
