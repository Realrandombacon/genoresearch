## Description
OR2W1 (Olfactory Receptor Family 2 Subfamily W Member 1, alias hs6M1-15) is a 320 amino acid G protein-coupled receptor (UniProt Q9Y3N9) with a well-folded 7-transmembrane architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 88.6, high confidence), functioning as a class A rhodopsin-like GPCR that detects specific odorant molecules in olfactory sensory neurons, initiating Gαolf-mediated cAMP signaling cascades that trigger neuronal depolarization and smell perception.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals OR2W1 contains canonical olfactory receptor domains: olfactory receptor family (IPR000725/PF13853, residues 32-305), GPCR rhodopsin-like family (IPR000276/PR00237, residues 26-50, 59-80, 104-126), 7-transmembrane domain (IPR017452, residues 41-290), and olfactory receptor subfamily 2W (cd15434, residues 25-301). The rhodopsin 7-helix transmembrane superfamily (G3DSA:1.20.1070.10, residues 21-319) confirms class A GPCR architecture. GO annotations include olfactory receptor activity (GO:0004984), G protein-coupled receptor activity (GO:0004930), G protein-coupled receptor signaling pathway (GO:0007186), and membrane localization (GO:0016020). The high-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 88.6) confirms a stable 7TM fold optimized for odorant binding and G protein coupling.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places OR2W1 within the olfactory signaling cascade: interactions with GNAL (0.685, G protein subunit alpha olfactory, activates adenylate cyclase), GNB1 (0.656, G protein subunit beta 1), PRKACA/PRKACB/PRKACG (0.650, cAMP-dependent protein kinase catalytic subunits), GNG7/GNG13 (0.650/0.566, G protein subunit gamma), ARRB2 (0.650, beta-arrestin 2, receptor desensitization), and GRK2/GRK3 (0.569/0.547, G protein-coupled receptor kinases). The GNAL-GNB1-GNG7 cluster confirms canonical olfactory G protein coupling. ARRB2-GRK2-GRK3 partnerships indicate receptor desensitization mechanisms. PRKACA/B/G interactions link OR2W1 to cAMP-dependent phosphorylation cascades.\n\nHPA expression: No data available in Human Protein Atlas database, typical for olfactory receptors which are expressed in olfactory epithelium not sampled in standard HPA tissues. Olfactory receptors are typically expressed in subsets of olfactory sensory neurons in the nasal epithelium.\n\nClinVar documents 5 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all large chromosome 6 copy number gains and one deletion (6p25.3-q27, 6p25.3-12.3, 6p25.2-21.33). The CNV burden indicates dosage sensitivity, with large deletions/duplications potentially causing anosmia or developmental disorders through disrupted olfactory receptor clusters.\n\nConservation is strong across mammals: human (Q9Y3N9, 320 aa), yak (A0A8B9XX91, 317 aa), indicating evolutionary conservation of olfactory functions.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: OR2W1 functions as a canonical olfactory GPCR that binds specific volatile odorant molecules in the cilia of olfactory sensory neurons. Upon odorant binding, the 7TM receptor undergoes conformational change activating Gαolf (GNAL, 0.685), which stimulates adenylate cyclase III to produce cAMP. cAMP opens cyclic nucleotide-gated (CNG) channels, causing Ca2+ influx and neuronal depolarization. The Gβγ subunits (GNB1 0.656, GNG7 0.650) may regulate downstream effectors. ARRB2 (0.650) and GRK2/GRK3 (0.569/0.547) mediate receptor phosphorylation and internalization for signal termination. PRKACA/B/G (0.650) phosphorylate targets for signal amplification. The well-folded structure (pLDDT 88.6) enables precise odorant recognition. OR2W1 represents a typical olfactory receptor contributing to combinatorial odor coding in the mammalian olfactory system.
