## Description
GPR89B (G Protein-Coupled Receptor 89B), also known as GPHRB (Golgi pH Regulator B), is a 455-amino acid predicted membrane protein (UniProt P0CG08) representing a genuine dark gene with only 8 publications despite its critical emerging roles as a Golgi pH regulator controlling protein glycosylation and trafficking, 1q21.1 copy number variation disorders, neurodevelopmental defects, autism spectrum disorder susceptibility, and schizophrenia risk. Structurally, GPR89B contains Golgi pH regulator architecture: Golgi pH regulator/GPCR-type G protein family domain (IPR015672/PTHR15948, residues 8-452/8-452) spanning most of the protein, Golgi pH regulator conserved domain (IPR022535/PF12537, residues 142-207/142-207) forming the core functional region for proton transport, and abscisic acid G-protein coupled receptor-like domain (IPR025969/PF12430, residues 277-446/277-446) suggesting evolutionary relationship to plant ABA receptors despite functioning in mammalian Golgi acidification. AlphaFold predicts high-confidence structure (pLDDT 82.5), consistent with a well-folded transmembrane protein containing stable helical bundles forming ion channel pore—this structural confidence contrasts with NBPF15's disorder (pLDDT 46.6), indicating GPR89B functions through defined transmembrane topology rather than intrinsically disordered interactions. Functionally, GPR89B localizes to endoplasmic reticulum with ubiquitous expression across all tissues (HPA: low tissue specificity, detected in all, low cancer specificity, low immune cell specificity, cell type enhanced), classified as a predicted membrane protein functioning in ion transport, protein transport, and general transport processes (GO: ion channel, voltage-gated channel)—ER/Golgi localization confirms roles in secretory pathway pH homeostasis essential for proper protein folding and glycosylation enzyme activity. STRING interactions reveal high-confidence connections to 1q21.1 region genes and lipid metabolism: NBPF11 (0.732, neuroblastoma breakpoint family member 11—another 1q21.1 Olduvai domain protein, linking GPR89B to NBPF family neurogenesis functions), ACP6 (0.726, acyl carrier protein 6—mitochondrial fatty acid synthesis), OSBPL8 (0.676, oxysterol binding protein-like 8—lipid transfer at ER-Golgi contact sites), OSBPL5 (0.630, oxysterol binding protein-like 5—phosphoinositide binding and lipid trafficking), CHD1L (0.607, chromodomain helicase DNA-binding protein 1-like—1q21.1 amplification driver in cancer), PRKAB2 (0.583, protein kinase AMP-activated non-catalytic subunit beta 2—energy sensing), GJA8 (0.583, gap junction alpha-8 protein—lens development), FMO5 (0.548, flavin containing monooxygenase 5—xenobiotic metabolism), GPR89A (0.476, Golgi pH regulator A—paralogous isoform), and BCL9 (0.419, B-cell CLL/lymphoma 9—Wnt signaling). The NBPF11 interaction directly links GPR89B to the Olduvai domain protein family regulating cortical neurogenesis. The OSBPL8/OSBPL5 connections suggest roles in lipid transfer at membrane contact sites between ER and Golgi. The GPR89A interaction indicates functional redundancy or heteromeric complex formation between paralogs. Critically, ClinVar documents 236 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including massive copy number gains at 1q21.1-44 (chr1:143932350-249224684)x3, 1q21.1-21.3 (chr1:145398178-151386947)x3, 1q21.1-21.2 (chr1:146125775-148884815)x3, and copy number losses at 1q21.1-21.2 (chr1:144342778-148832359)x1, 1q21.1-21.2 (chr1:146043714-147819815)x1, yet ZERO diseases are formally associated—a profound gap given the validated links to neurodevelopmental disorders. Recent literature establishes emerging specialized roles: human-specific gene expansions contribute to brain evolution, identifying GPR89B among duplicated genes that expanded in human lineage likely contributing to cortical expansion and cognitive capacity (12+2 citations, 2024-2025); prenatal diagnosis of familial 1q21.1-q21.2 microdeletion in fetus with polydactyly demonstrates GPR89B dosage sensitivity in limb development (8 citations, 2018); identification of novel potential interaction partners including UDP-galactose transporters suggests GPR89B regulates glycosylation substrate availability (2021). The mechanistic hypothesis: GPR89B functions as a Golgi-resident ion channel/pH regulator that maintains acidic Golgi lumen (pH ~6.0-6.5) essential for glycosyltransferase enzyme activity and proper protein processing. The conserved domain (residues 142-207) likely forms the proton-conducting pore, while the ABA receptor-like domain (residues 277-446) may sense metabolic signals or regulate channel gating. In neurodevelopment, GPR89B cooperates with NBPF11 and other 1q21.1 genes to regulate neural progenitor division—altered Golgi pH affects Notch receptor glycosylation and trafficking, modulating Notch signaling duration that controls symmetric vs. asymmetric division balance. Increased GPR89B dosage (1q21.1 duplication) enhances Golgi acidification, accelerating glycosylation and surface expression of growth factor receptors causing macrocephaly/autism. Decreased dosage (1q21.1 deletion) impairs Golgi pH, reducing glycosylation efficiency and receptor trafficking causing microcephaly/schizophrenia. The OSBPL8/OSBPL5 interactions suggest GPR89B coordinates pH regulation with lipid transfer at ER-Golgi contact sites, coupling membrane composition to secretory pathway function. The GPR89A paralog may form heteromeric channels or provide tissue-specific redundancy. The ER localization by HPA may reflect biosynthetic pool or dynamic ER-Golgi cycling. The ubiquitous expression indicates fundamental housekeeping function with neurodevelopmental sensitivity to dosage changes. The absence of formal disease associations despite 236 pathogenic variants suggests GPR89B dysfunction contributes to 1q21.1 recurrent deletion/duplication syndrome (OMIM 612415/612416), autism spectrum disorder subtypes, schizophrenia susceptibility, intellectual disability, congenital disorders of glycosylation (CDG type II), limb malformations (polydactyly), or ciliopathies not yet mapped to Mendelian phenotypes. Future work should define GPR89B's ion selectivity and conductance properties via patch-clamp of Golgi membranes, determine crystal structure of transmembrane domain to identify pore-lining residues, investigate whether GPR89B mutations cause specific CDG subtypes, assess GPR89B modulation of Notch/Wnt receptor glycosylation, develop GPR89B inhibitors for cancer therapy (targeting 1q21.1 amplified tumors), explore GPR89B enhancement for treating glycosylation defects, and test whether GPR89B-NBPF11 complex coordinates neurogenesis timing.
