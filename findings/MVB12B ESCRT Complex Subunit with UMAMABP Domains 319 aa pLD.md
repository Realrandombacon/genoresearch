## Description
MVB12B (Multivesicular Body Subunit 12B, UniProt Q9H7P6) is a 319 amino acid ESCRT (Endosomal Sorting Complex Required for Transport) complex component representing a partially characterized dark gene with only 24 literature references despite critical roles in multivesicular body biogenesis, innate immune signaling, and ciliary function. MVB12B contains a MABP (Myeloid Differentiation Primary Response 88-Associated Bank1 Protein) domain (IPR023341, PF10240, positions 47-193) and a UMA (Ubiquitin-Mediated Autophagy) domain (IPR023340, positions 254-303), both implicated in ubiquitin recognition and membrane trafficking. The AlphaFold structure shows high confidence (pLDDT 76.3), indicating a well-folded protein with stable tertiary structure suitable for protein-protein interactions within the ESCRT machinery. STRING interactions reveal MVB12B functions as an integral ESCRT-I complex component with exceptionally high-confidence partners including VPS28 (0.999), TSG101 (0.984), UBAP1 (0.966), VPS36 (0.957), SNF8 (0.954), VPS25 (0.943), and VPS37A-D (0.911-0.943) - all core ESCRT subunits involved in ubiquitinated cargo sorting into intraluminal vesicles. HPA data shows brain-enriched expression (110.8 nTPM), immune cell enhancement, and striking subcellular localization to vesicles, primary cilium, primary cilium tip, and primary cilium transition zone - suggesting roles in ciliary signaling and vesicle trafficking. Critically, ClinVar documents 37 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly copy number losses and gains spanning 9q33.3-34.11 and 9p24.3-q34.3, indicating dosage sensitivity and clinical relevance. Recent literature (2019-2025) establishes MVB12B as: (1) Innate immune signaling mediator where intracellular bacteria (Listeria monocytogenes) engage a STING-TBK1-MVB12B pathway enabling paracrine cGAS-STING signaling (2019, 118 citations - landmark paper demonstrating MVB12B phosphorylation by TBK1 promotes type I interferon production and bystander cell activation); (2) Bladder cancer suppressor where MVB12B overexpression represses proliferation and migration in bladder urothelial carcinoma (2025, tumor suppressor function); (3) Melanoma prognostic marker where MVB12B variants in endosome-related pathway predict cutaneous melanoma-specific survival (2020, 4 citations); (4) Osteosarcoma regulator where lncRNA NRON inhibits osteosarcoma cell proliferation/invasion by regulating MVB12B (2020). Functional hypothesis: MVB12B likely acts as (1) ESCRT-I complex stabilizer/scaffold - the MABP and UMA domains bind ubiquitinated cargo proteins and recruit ESCRT-I components (TSG101-VPS28-VPS37) to endosomal membranes, facilitating intraluminal vesicle formation and cargo sorting into multivesicular bodies for lysosomal degradation or exosome release; (2) STING pathway amplifier - the 2019 landmark study shows TBK1 phosphorylates MVB12B during intracellular bacterial infection, promoting MVB12B association with STING-containing vesicles and enhancing type I interferon production in a paracrine manner, suggesting MVB12B bridges endosomal trafficking with innate immune signaling; (3) Ciliary trafficking regulator - the primary cilium tip/transition zone localization suggests MVB12B mediates vesicle delivery to cilia, potentially regulating ciliary membrane composition, receptor trafficking (e.g., GPCRs, growth factor receptors), and ciliary signaling pathways (Hedgehog, Wnt, PDGFRα); (4) Tumor suppressor via ESCRT function - MVB12B downregulation in bladder cancer and osteosarcoma suggests loss of ESCRT-mediated degradation of oncogenic receptors (EGFR, MET, PDGFR) contributes to tumorigenesis, while melanoma survival association indicates germline variants affect endosomal sorting efficiency. Disease mechanism: The 37 ClinVar CNVs (9q33-34 deletions/duplications) suggest MVB12B dosage alteration causes: (a) ESCRT complex instability, impairing multivesicular body formation and cargo sorting, leading to accumulation of ubiquitinated proteins and defective lysosomal degradation; (b) Impaired STING-TBK1 signaling, reducing type I interferon production and compromising antibacterial immunity; (c) Ciliary dysfunction, disrupting ciliary signaling pathways critical for development (Hedgehog) and tissue homeostasis; (d) Oncogenic receptor accumulation, promoting proliferation and migration in bladder cancer and osteosarcoma. The 9q33-34 region overlaps known disease loci, and MVB12B haploinsufficiency may contribute to neurodevelopmental phenotypes (given brain-enriched expression) and immunodeficiency. Therapeutic implication: MVB12B represents a druggable node where: (1) Enhancing MVB12B expression/function could restore ESCRT activity in cancers with MVB12B downregulation; (2) MVB12B-TBK1 interaction could be targeted to modulate innate immune responses in autoinflammatory diseases; (3) MVB12B-dependent exosome biogenesis could be inhibited to block tumor-derived exosome-mediated immunosuppression; (4) Ciliary MVB12B function could be exploited for ciliopathy treatment. Validation priorities include: (1) Defining MVB12B ubiquitin-binding specificity via MABP/UMA domain structural studies; (2) Mapping MVB12B phosphosites targeted by TBK1 and functional consequences; (3) Determining MVB12B-dependent cargo proteins via proteomics; (4) Elucidating ciliary MVB12B roles in Hedgehog/Wnt signaling; (5) Correlating 9q33-34 CNV breakpoints with MVB12B dosage and clinical phenotypes; (6) Testing MVB12B restoration in bladder cancer and osteosarcoma models.
