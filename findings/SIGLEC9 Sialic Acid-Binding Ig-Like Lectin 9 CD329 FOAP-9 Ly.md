## Description
SIGLEC9 encodes a 463 aa type I transmembrane receptor protein (UniProt Q9Y336), also known as CD329 or FOAP-9, that functions as an inhibitory immune checkpoint receptor expressed on myeloid cells, tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs), and lymphoid tissues recognizing sialylated glycans to suppress immune responses and promote tumor immune evasion. This well-studied gene (57 publications) has significant clinical implications for cancer immunotherapy and prognosis. The protein features characteristic Siglec architecture: N-terminal V-set immunoglobulin domain (IPR013106/cd05719, residues 26-137/23-140) mediating sialic acid binding with conserved arginine residue in FXXR motif; two C2-set immunoglobulin domains (IPR003599/IPR007110/IPR013151, residues 149-233/146-229 and 257-338/236-336) providing structural scaffold; Siglec family signature (IPR051036, residues 2-460) defining the sialic acid-binding lectin class; C-terminal cytoplasmic domain containing immunoreceptor tyrosine-based inhibitory motifs (ITIMs) for inhibitory signaling; classified within immunoglobulin superfamily with GO terms for sialic acid binding, cell adhesion, and negative regulation of inflammatory response. STRING interactions reveal SIGLEC9 operates within high-confidence glycan recognition and immune regulation networks: MUC1 (0.967)/MUC16 (0.885), mucin glycoproteins presenting sialylated ligands on tumor cells; AOC3 (0.794), amine oxidase copper containing 3 in leukocyte trafficking; KLK13 (0.784)/KLK14 (0.588), kallikrein-related peptidases in extracellular matrix remodeling; NCAM1 (0.752), neural cell adhesion molecule 1 with polysialic acid modifications; PTPN11 (0.742), SHP-2 phosphatase recruited to ITIMs for inhibitory signaling; FCGR3B (0.676)/FCGR3A (0.518), Fc gamma receptors in myeloid cells; SIGLEC7 (0.494), paralogous sialic acid-binding lectin. HPA confirms tissue-enhanced expression with lymphoid tissue enrichment (19.9 nTPM), group-enriched blood cell expression, detected in many tissues, low cancer specificity, predicted membrane protein with cell adhesion biological process. ClinVar lists 14 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including intronic variant (c.1016-11T>C) and extensive copy number gains spanning chromosome 19q regions (19q13.33-13.43 49-57Mb x3, 19q13.33-13.41 49-53Mb, 19q13.33-13.43 48-57Mb x3, 19q13.32-13.42 47-54Mb, 19q13.31-13.42 44-53Mb x3, 19p13.3-q13.43 0.26-58Mb x3, 19q13.33-13.43 50-59Mb x3, 19q13.33-13.43 50-58Mb x3), demonstrating strong dosage sensitivity though specific monogenic diseases remain undefined. AlphaFold structure prediction yields high confidence (global pLDDT 73.4), revealing well-folded tandem immunoglobulin domains characteristic of Siglec family receptors with properly positioned sialic acid binding pocket. Mechanistically, SIGLEC9 operates through integrated modes: (1) Immune checkpoint function—2024 study (11 citations) demonstrates Siglec9+ tumor-associated macrophages predict prognosis and therapeutic vulnerability in colon cancer through immunosuppressive signaling; (2) Radioresistance induction—2023 study (28 citations) shows LINC01004-SPI1 axis activates SIGLEC9 in TAMs inducing radioresistance and immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment in esophageal squamous cell carcinoma; (3) Cervical cancer progression—2025 study shows SIGLEC9 highly expressed in cervical cancer correlating with poor prognosis, macrophage/T-cell infiltration, and immunosuppression; (4) Colorectal cancer biomarker—2024 studies (4+ citations) associate SIGLEC9 expression with cytokine profiles, tumor grading, KRAS/NRAS/BRAF/PIK3CA/AKT mutations, and MSI status; (5) Melanoma immune infiltration—2024 study (2 citations) identifies prognostic implications of SIGLEC9 in skin cutaneous melanoma; (6) Sialic acid recognition—V-set Ig domain binds α2-3 and α2-6 linked sialic acids on tumor cell glycans including MUC1/MUC16; (7) ITIM-mediated inhibition—cytoplasmic ITIMs recruit SHP-1/SHP-2 phosphatases (PTPN6/PTPN11) suppressing activation signals; (8) Paralog redundancy—SIGLEC7 represents closely related paralog with overlapping ligand specificity. Loss-of-function causes enhanced immune activation, reduced tumor immunosuppression, improved radiotherapy response, altered myeloid cell function, and potential autoimmune susceptibility.
