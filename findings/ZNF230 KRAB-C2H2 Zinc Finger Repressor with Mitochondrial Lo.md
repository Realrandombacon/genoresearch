# ZNF230: KRAB-C2H2 Zinc Finger Repressor with Mitochondrial Localization, Metabolic Interaction Partners, and Chromosomal Rearrangement Disease Association

**Quality Score:** 1.9/10  (E=3.5, D=0.3) [POOR]

## Description
ZNF230 encodes a 474 amino acid Krüppel-associated box (KRAB) domain-containing C2H2 zinc finger transcription factor (UniProt Q9UIE0). InterPro analysis reveals canonical domain architecture: N-terminal KRAB repressor domain (IPR001909, PF01352, residues 8-76) that recruits the TRIM28/KAP1 corepressor complex for heterochromatin formation, followed by three tandem C2H2 zinc finger DNA-binding domains (IPR013087, PF00096, residues 168-302) conferring sequence-specific genomic targeting. AlphaFold structure prediction shows medium confidence (pLDDT 58.3), consistent with flexible linker regions between structured zinc finger modules typical of scanning transcription factors. HPA data indicates ubiquitous expression across tissues with low specificity and unexpected mitochondrial subcellular localization—divergent from canonical nuclear KRAB-ZFP function, suggesting potential dual compartmentalization or metabolic regulatory role. STRING interactions identify three partners: ALDOA (0.826, aldolase A, rate-limiting glycolytic enzyme), LOC112694756 (0.827, lncRNA locus), and TRIM28 (0.518, KRAB cofactor). The high-confidence ALDOA interaction (score >0.8) is remarkable and suggests ZNF230 may couple transcriptional repression to cellular metabolic state, potentially silencing differentiation genes during high glycolytic flux. ClinVar documents 8 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number gains/losses spanning 19q13.2-13.33 and 19p13.3-q13.43, indicating dosage sensitivity and disease association through chromosomal rearrangement mechanisms. The combination of KRAB-mediated repression, mitochondrial localization, and ALDOA interaction suggests ZNF230 functions as a metabolic sensor transcription factor that recruits heterochromatin machinery to specific genomic targets during metabolic transitions, with pathogenic CNVs disrupting this regulatory circuit.
