## Description
UQCR10 is a core subunit of mitochondrial complex III (cytochrome bc1 complex), responsible for facilitating electron transfer from ubiquinone to cytochrome c via the Qo site. As an iron-sulfur protein, it plays a critical role in oxidative phosphorylation and ATP production. While its fundamental biochemical function is well-established in biochemistry, UQCR10 mutations are associated with severe mitochondrial disorders including Leigh syndrome (Leigh's disease) and encephalomyopathy, indicating potential pathogenic variants exist despite limited recent high-impact literature availability via current tools. Its role as a structural scaffold for the Rieske iron-sulfur protein is critical; dysfunction leads to impaired respiratory chain efficiency and increased ROS production. Given its essential housekeeping function in energy metabolism and known disease associations with mitochondrial DNA/nuclear gene interactions, UQCR10 does not meet the criteria of being an uncharacterized 'dark gene' requiring novel mechanistic discovery.
