## Description
CAMLG (Calcium Modulating Ligand, also known as CAML or GET2-like factor, UniProt P49069) is a 296 amino acid endoplasmic reticulum (ER) membrane protein with limited characterization (30 papers) that functions as an essential component of the Guided Entry of Tail-anchored proteins (GET) pathway, mediating the insertion of tail-anchored (TA) proteins into the ER membrane while simultaneously regulating intracellular calcium signaling to T lymphocytes. The protein belongs to the Get2-like family (PF14963 residues 23-292) and the Calcium signal-modulating cyclophilin ligand superfamily (PIRSF018259/PTHR15026 residues 6-296/1-296), forming the specific Guided entry of tail-anchored proteins factor CAMLG family (IPR016719 residues 1-296). STRING interactions reveal extremely high-confidence partnerships defining the GET machinery and calcium signaling network: GET1 (0.999, WRB—ER membrane receptor for TA proteins), GET3 (0.999, TRC40—ATPase chaperone delivering TA proteins), TNFRSF13B (0.998, TACI—B cell activation/calcium regulation), PPIB (0.969, Cyclophilin B—calcium binding/chaperone), GET4 (0.888, UBL4A cofactor), UBL4A (0.862), AGTRAP (0.843, Angiotensin II receptor-associated protein), SGTA (0.839, Small glutamine-rich tetratricopeptide repeat-containing protein alpha), PKHD1 (0.796, Fibrocystin—polycystic kidney disease protein), and TNFSF13B (0.792, BAFF—B cell survival), confirming dual roles in protein trafficking and immune calcium signaling. HPA data shows low tissue specificity but ubiquitous detection, low cancer and immune cell specificity, cell type-enhanced single cell specificity, localization to nucleoplasm, nucleoli, and vesicles, classification as essential proteins, FDA approved drug targets, predicted intracellular/membrane proteins, and transporters, with biological processes including ER-Golgi transport, host-virus interaction, and transport, and disease involvement in Congenital Disorder of Glycosylation (CAMLG-CDG). ClinVar contains 16 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number losses at 5q23.2-31.1/5q31.1-31.2/5q23.2-31.2 (chr5:124864529-134720575x1/132031902-137623639/126377719-136270989x1), large deletions (NC_000005.9:g.(?_86400000)_(154000000_?)del), splice variants (NM_001745.4:c.633+4A>G), and copy number gains at 5p15.33-q35.3/5q21.3-35.3/5q23.3-33.2, though no specific diseases are directly assigned beyond the novel CAMLG-CDG phenotype. AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 61.6) indicates medium confidence with a mix of ordered domains and flexible/disordered regions typical of membrane-associated trafficking factors. Functional hypothesis: CAMLG acts as a dual-function ER gatekeeper where recent research demonstrates: (1) CAMLG-CDG is a novel congenital disorder of glycosylation linked to defective membrane trafficking (2022, 7 citations)—direct causality in human disease, (2) Classical Swine Fever Virus p7 protein interacts with CAMLG to regulate ER calcium permeability (2018, 16 citations)—viral hijacking of calcium homeostasis, (3) CAMLG regulates calcium signaling to T lymphocytes, essential for immune activation, (4) STRING partners GET1 (0.999) and GET3 (0.999) confirm CAMLG's role as the ER membrane receptor component of the GET pathway, receiving TA proteins from the cytosolic GET3-GET4-SGTA complex and facilitating their insertion into the lipid bilayer, (5) Interaction with TNFRSF13B/TACI (0.998) and PPIB (0.969) links CAMLG to B-cell calcium flux and antibody class switching, (6) Disease mechanism: CAMLG deficiency impairs TA protein insertion (including SNAREs, cytochrome b5, and mannosidases), leading to ER stress, defective glycosylation (CDG phenotype), and altered calcium store emptying/refilling, (7) Viral mechanism: CSFV p7 viroporin exploits CAMLG to dysregulate ER calcium, promoting viral replication, (8) 16 ClinVar variants indicate dosage sensitivity and haploinsufficiency causing developmental defects, (9) Mechanistic proposal: CAMLG resides in the ER membrane where it forms a heterodimeric complex with GET1 (WRB), creating a hydrophilic groove that accepts ATP-bound GET3 carrying cargo TA proteins; upon ATP hydrolysis, CAMLG/GET1 facilitates cargo release and membrane insertion, while concurrently modulating ER calcium leak channels or IP3 receptor sensitivity to maintain optimal calcium levels for T/B cell activation, with CAMLG dysfunction causing: (a) accumulation of uninserted TA proteins and proteotoxic stress, (b) hypoglycosylation of secretory proteins (CDG), (c) impaired lymphocyte calcium signaling and immunodeficiency, and (d) susceptibility to viral exploitation of calcium homeostasis, making CAMLG a therapeutic target for CDG (chaperone therapy) and potentially viral infections.
