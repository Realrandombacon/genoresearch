## Description
SLC7A7 (Q9UM01, 511 aa), also known as y+LAT-1 (Y+L Amino Acid Transporter 1) or LAT3, is the light subunit of a sodium-independent cationic amino acid transporter belonging to solute carrier family 7 (amino acid transporter light chain family). InterPro identifies complete amino acid permease architecture: amino acid/polyamine transporter I family (IPR002293, PF13520, G3DSA:1.20.1740.10, aa 37-436), cellular amino acid transporter superfamily (IPR050598, PTHR11785, aa 28-491), and amino acid transporter family (PIRSF006060, aa 28-452). AlphaFold predicts high-confidence structure (pLDDT 83.8, residues 1-511) consistent with 12-14 transmembrane helix topology typical of SLC7 family transporters. HPA shows kidney-enriched expression (253.4 nTPM) with intestine (113.9 nTPM); immune cell enhanced; classified as FDA-approved drug target, metabolic protein, and disease-related gene involved in amino-acid transport with osteoporosis association. STRING reveals 10 interactions forming amino acid transport network: SLC43A2/LAT4 (0.671), SLC6A14/ATB0,+ (0.668), SLC6A19/B0AT1 (0.605), SLC1A4/ASCT1 (0.521), SLC3A1/rBAT (0.515), SLC1A1/EAAT3 (0.515), SLC3A2/4F2hc (0.462), SLC38A2/SNAT2 (0.455), SLC15A1/PEPT1 (0.433), and SLC7A8/LAT2 (0.419). ClinVar contains 232 pathogenic variants including nonsense mutations (p.Gly306Ter, p.Trp121Ter), frameshifts (p.Thr244fs, p.Phe214fs, p.Pro154fs, p.Asn41fs, p.Ile371fs, p.Ala448fs, p.Gly453fs, p.Val204fs), causing lysinuric protein intolerance (LPI). Functional hypothesis: SLC7A7/y+LAT-1 functions as the light chain subunit of a heterodimeric amino acid transporter that exchanges cationic amino acids (L-arginine, L-lysine, L-ornithine) and large neutral amino acids across basolateral membranes of epithelial cells. Requires obligatory heterodimerization with heavy chain SLC3A2/4F2hc (0.462 interaction) via conserved disulfide bond for plasma membrane trafficking and function. The transporter operates via facilitated exchange mechanism (antiport) independent of sodium gradients. Kidney enrichment (253.4 nTPM) reflects critical role in renal reabsorption of dibasic amino acids from proximal tubule. Intestinal expression (113.9 nTPM) supports dietary amino acid absorption. Pathogenic variants disrupt transporter folding, membrane trafficking, or substrate binding, causing LPI characterized by impaired intestinal absorption and renal reabsorption of cationic amino acids, leading to hyperammonemia, growth retardation, pulmonary alveolar proteinosis, and osteoporosis. SLC6A14 (0.668) and SLC43A2 (0.671) interactions suggest functional compensation by other amino acid transporters. Conservation across vertebrates (mouse 510 aa, rat 512 aa) confirms essential metabolic role. NOTE: This gene is well-characterized as causative for LPI with extensive clinical literature.
