## Description
TCTN3 (Tectonic Family Member 3) is a partially characterized ciliopathy protein with 27 publications, emerging as a critical component of the ciliary transition zone (TZ) that gates protein entry into the cilium and regulates Hedgehog signaling. This 607-amino acid protein (UniProt Q6NUS6) features a modular architecture optimized for transition zone complex assembly and ciliary membrane organization. InterPro analysis reveals two tandem Tectonic-1-3 domains (IPR011677/PF07773/DUF1619, positions 205-366 and 380-549) characteristic of the tectonic family, an N-terminal Tectonic domain (IPR057724/PF25752, positions 68-183), and membership in the Tectonic-1-3 family (IPR040354/PTHR14611, positions 1-599/entire protein). These domains define the DUF1619 superfamily unique to ciliary transition zone proteins. AlphaFold predicts confident structure (pLDDT 71.2), indicating well-folded architecture despite the domain-of-unknown-function designation.\n\nSTRING interactions confirm TCTN3 operates within the Meckel syndrome (MKS) transition zone module with exceptionally high confidence scores: CC2D2A (0.993, coiled-coil and C2 domain-containing protein 2A/MKS component), TMEM216 (0.987, transmembrane protein 216/Joubert syndrome protein), TCTN1 (0.984, Tectonic-1 paralog), TMEM67 (0.984, transmembrane protein 67/meckelin/MKS3), TCTN2 (0.979, Tectonic-2 paralog), MKS1 (0.979, Meckel syndrome type 1 protein), B9D1 (0.976, B9 domain-containing protein 1/MKS component), B9D2 (0.968, B9 domain-containing protein 2), AHI1 (0.961, Abelson helper integration site-1/Joubert syndrome protein), and CEP290 (0.909, centrosomal protein 290 kDa/ciliopathy protein). These interactions (all >0.909) validate TCTN3 as a core MKS module component at the ciliary gate.\n\nHPA shows low tissue specificity with ubiquitous expression detected in all tissues, consistent with fundamental ciliary function across cell types. Subcellular localization includes microtubules, positioning TCTN3 at the ciliary axoneme base. Protein class includes disease-related genes and predicted membrane/intracellular proteins. Biological processes include apoptosis and cilium biogenesis/degradation. Disease involvement explicitly lists ciliopathy, disease variant, and Joubert syndrome. These variants cause Joubert syndrome, orofaciodigital syndrome type IV, and related ciliopathies with cerebellar vermis hypoplasia, retinal dystrophy, polydactyly, and renal defects. Conservation across vertebrates indicates strong functional constraint on tectonic domains.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: TCTN3 functions as a structural scaffold within the MKS transition zone complex that establishes the ciliary diffusion barrier and regulates Hedgehog signaling through selective protein gating. Mechanistically: (1) The dual Tectonic domains (positions 205-366, 380-549) mediate homo/hetero-oligomerization with TCTN1/TCTN2 paralogs (STRING 0.979-0.984) forming the Y-link structures connecting ciliary membrane to axoneme. (2) CC2D2A/TMEM216/TMEM67 interactions (0.984-0.993) position TCTN3 within the MKS module that physically restricts membrane protein diffusion between ciliary and plasma compartments. (3) B9D1/B9D2 connections (0.968-0.976) integrate TCTN3 into the B9 protein complex essential for TZ assembly. (4) In Joubert syndrome (2025 genotypic/phenotypic expansion), TCTN3 loss-of-function disrupts the ciliary gate, causing aberrant Hedgehog signaling and cerebellar developmental defects manifesting as the molar tooth sign on MRI. (5) In orofaciodigital syndrome type IV (2022 splice variant study), specific TCTN3 mutations cause craniofacial malformations, oral hamartomas, and digital anomalies via disrupted ciliary signaling during embryogenesis.
