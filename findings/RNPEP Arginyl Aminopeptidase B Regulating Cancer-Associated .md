## Description
RNPEP (Arginyl Aminopeptidase B), also known as Aminopeptidase B, is a 650-amino acid zinc-dependent metalloprotease (UniProt Q9H4A4) representing a genuine dark gene with only 19 publications despite its critical roles in cancer metastasis, peptide hormone processing, and the renin-angiotensin system. Structurally, RNPEP belongs to the peptidase M1 family with a canonical aminopeptidase architecture: Peptidase M1/leukotriene A4 hydrolase catalytic domain (cd09599, residues 24-484) containing the zinc-binding catalytic site, membrane alanine aminopeptidase domain (IPR014782, residues 263-466) with metallopeptidase activity (GO:0008237) and zinc ion binding (GO:0008270), C-terminal domain (IPR015211, residues 500-645) sharing homology with leukotriene A4 hydrolase/aminopeptidase family, armadillo-type fold (IPR016024, residues 501-645) for protein-protein interactions, and multiple conserved active site signatures (IPR001930, residues 176-191/220-235/295-305) defining proteolytic function (GO:0006508). AlphaFold predicts exceptional structure confidence (pLDDT 94.6), consistent with a well-folded metalloenzyme with stable catalytic architecture and defined substrate-binding pockets. Functionally, RNPEP localizes to Golgi apparatus, principal piece, and end piece with ubiquitous expression across all tissues (HPA: detected in all, low tissue specificity, low cancer specificity, cell type enhanced), classified as an enzyme/metabolic protein with aminopeptidase, hydrolase, metalloprotease, and protease activities, predicted as both intracellular and secreted. STRING interactions reveal connections to peptidase networks and metabolic regulation: CAPN10 (0.764, calpain-10 cysteine protease), DNPEP (0.764, aspartyl aminopeptidase—closest paralog), DPP3 (0.640, dipeptidyl-peptidase 3), PREP (0.614, prolyl endopeptidase), PGPEP1 (0.608, pyroglutamyl-peptidase 1), TRIT1 (0.605, tRNA isopentenyltransferase 1), POC5 (0.581, centriolar protein), CPB1 (0.551, carboxypeptidase B1), LAP3 (0.489, leucine aminopeptidase 3), and ENPEP (0.419, glutamyl aminopeptidase—another M1 family member). This interaction network positions RNPEP within the broader aminopeptidase system regulating peptide hormone maturation and protein turnover. Critically, ClinVar documents 21 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number gains spanning 1q25.3-32.1 (chr1:180800361-203181850), 1q21.1-44 (chr1:143932350-249224684), 1q32.1 (chr1:199373229-204335027), copy number losses at 1q31.3-32.1 (chr1:197216705-203683110), large deletions (chr1:?_190829412_216061974_?), duplications (chr1:?_200522516_208391267_?), and coding variants (c.1318G>C p.Ala440Pro, c.*327C>A, c.*326T>G, c.854+247T>G), yet ZERO diseases are formally associated—a profound gap given the metastasis and renin-angiotensin system functions. Recent literature establishes emerging roles: tumor exosomal RNPEP promotes lung metastasis of liver cancer via inducing cancer-associated fibroblast (CAF) activation (10 citations, 2024)—demonstrating RNPEP functions in tumor microenvironment remodeling; human aminopeptidase B genomic structure and expression characterized on chromosome 1q32.2 (21 citations, 2002); chromosomal assignment to 1q32 by in situ hybridization (7 citations, 1997); and aminopeptidase expression in multiple myeloma associating with disease progression and sensitivity to melflufen peptide-drug conjugates (48 citations, 2021—related aminopeptidase context). The mechanistic hypothesis: RNPEP functions as an arginine-specific aminopeptidase that processes peptide hormones and signaling molecules containing N-terminal arginine residues. In the renin-angiotensin system, RNPEP may cleave angiotensin peptides (Ang III to Ang IV, or other intermediates) regulating blood pressure and cardiovascular function—connecting to brain RAS involvement in Alzheimer's disease pathology. In cancer metastasis, tumor-derived exosomal RNPEP likely processes extracellular matrix peptides or cytokines that activate CAFs, promoting desmoplastic stroma formation and metastatic niche preparation in the lung. The DNPEP and ENPEP interactions suggest functional redundancy or cooperative substrate processing within the M1 aminopeptidase family. Through PREP and PGPEP1 interactions, RNPEP may coordinate sequential peptide processing events for hormone maturation (insulin, glucagon, neuropeptides). The Golgi localization suggests RNPEP processes secretory pathway cargo before release. The principal piece/end piece sperm tail localization indicates potential roles in sperm motility or fertilization through peptide signaling. The absence of formal disease associations despite 21 pathogenic variants suggests RNPEP dysfunction contributes to hypertension, cardiovascular disease, infertility (sperm defects), cancer metastasis susceptibility, or neurodegenerative disorders (Alzheimer's via brain RAS dysregulation) not yet mapped to Mendelian phenotypes. Future work should identify RNPEP physiological substrates (angiotensin peptides, chemokines, neuropeptides), define mechanisms linking exosomal RNPEP to CAF activation, investigate whether RNPEP mutations cause hypertension or male infertility, and screen metastatic cancer patients for germline RNPEP variants affecting treatment response.
