## Description
RGPD2 (RANBP2 like and GRIP domain containing 2, also known as RANBP2L2, RGP2, ranBP2-like 2) is a 1756 amino acid nuclear membrane protein (UniProt P0DJD1) with medium-confidence partially-structured architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 65.3), characteristic of large nucleoporin-associated proteins that function through multiple structured domains connected by flexible linkers. The protein contains multiple Ran binding domains (IPR000156, residues 1029-1165 and 1326-1462) enabling intracellular transport (GO:0046907), a GRIP domain (IPR000237, residues 1693-1743) for Golgi targeting, Tetratricopeptide repeat domains (IPR019734/IPR013105, residues 59-92) enabling protein binding (GO:0005515), PH-like domains (IPR011993, residues 1014-1165 and 1310-1469), and a GCC2 Rab binding domain (IPR032023, residues 16-190). RGPD2 is located on chromosome 2p11.2 and is classified as a predicted intracellular protein with subcellular localization to nuclear membrane and vesicles, functioning in NLS-bearing protein import into nucleus and as part of the nuclear pore complex.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals an extremely high-confidence network of nuclear pore complex components and Ran GTPase cycle regulators: RANGAP1 (0.998, Ran GTPase activating protein 1, Ran-GAP regulation), NUP153 (0.982, Nuclear pore complex protein Nup153, nuclear basket), NUP88 (0.977, Nuclear pore complex protein Nup88, cytoplasmic filaments), NUP62 (0.947, Nuclear pore complex protein Nup62, central channel), PPIA (0.884, Peptidyl-prolyl cis-trans isomerase A, protein folding), NUP98 (0.606, Nuclear pore complex protein Nup98, FG-repeat nucleoporin), UBE2I (0.589, SUMO-conjugating enzyme Ubc9, SUMOylation), NUP214 (0.525, Nuclear pore complex protein Nup214, cytoplasmic filaments), SUMO1 (0.514, Small ubiquitin-like modifier 1, protein modification), and BICD2 (0.435, Bicaudal D cargo adaptor 2, vesicle transport). These interactions confirm RGPD2 functions within the nuclear pore complex and Ran GTPase cycle, with RANGAP1 as the highest-confidence partner (0.998) reflecting RGPD2's role as a RANBP2 paralog in Ran-GAP regulation and nucleocytoplasmic transport.\n\nHPA expression reveals RGPD2 has tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in testis (13.6 nTPM), detected in many tissues, consistent with roles in nuclear transport and potential spermatogenesis functions. This testis enrichment (13.6 nTPM) indicates RGPD2 may have specialized functions in germ cell nuclear dynamics. Low cancer specificity suggests housekeeping roles in nuclear transport. Immune cell enriched blood cell specificity suggests potential roles in immune cell activation. Subcellular localization to nuclear membrane and vesicles confirms RGPD2 functions at the nuclear envelope and potentially in vesicular trafficking. The protein is classified as a predicted intracellular protein with nucleocytoplasmic transport and SUMOylation molecular functions.\n\nClinVar documents 11 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including extensive copy number losses (2p11.2, 2p13.1-11.2, 2p25.1-q13, 2p25.3-q37.3 deletions spanning various coordinates) and gains (2p11.2-q11.2, 2p25.3-q37.3 amplifications), and single allele deletions. The variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity with both haploinsufficiency and overexpression effects. While no specific Mendelian disease is annotated, 2p11.2 CNVs are associated with developmental delay, intellectual disability, and growth abnormalities. Given RGPD2's nuclear pore localization and RANGAP1/NUP interactions, loss-of-function may cause nuclear transport defects, impaired protein import, or disrupted SUMOylation leading to developmental disorders.\n\nConservation is present across vertebrates: human (P0DJD1, 1756 aa), with orthologs in fish (Nothobranchius kadleci A0A1A8E5W5, 1822 aa; Nothobranchius kuhntae A0A1A8JJ05, 2040 aa; Iconisemion striatum A0A1A7Y538, 1742 aa), indicating the RANBP2-like architecture with multiple Ran-binding and GRIP domains is conserved in vertebrates with similar protein length, suggesting strong purifying selection on nuclear pore function.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: RGPD2 functions as a RANBP2 paralog that regulates nucleocytoplasmic transport through Ran GTPase cycle modulation and nuclear pore complex assembly. The medium-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 65.3) indicates a large multi-domain protein with structured Ran-binding domains (residues 1029-1165, 1326-1462) connected by flexible linkers, consistent with nucleoporin architecture. Through RANGAP1 interaction (0.998), RGPD2 may regulate Ran-GAP activity for Ran-GTP/GDP cycling essential for nuclear import/export. NUP153, NUP88, and NUP62 interactions (0.982/0.977/0.947) link RGPD2 to nuclear basket, cytoplasmic filaments, and central channel components for nuclear pore assembly. UBE2I and SUMO1 interactions (0.589/0.514) suggest RGPD2 participates in SUMOylation of nuclear pore components, similar to RANBP2's E3 SUMO ligase activity. PPIA interaction (0.884) connects RGPD2 to protein folding at the nuclear envelope. NUP98 and NUP214 interactions (0.606/0.525) link RGPD2 to FG-repeat nucleoporins for transport receptor docking. BICD2 interaction (0.435) suggests vesicle trafficking roles. GRIP domain (residues 1693-1743) may target RGPD2 to Golgi or nuclear membrane. Testis-enriched expression (13.6 nTPM) indicates specialized roles in germ cell nuclear dynamics. 2p11.2 CNVs cause RGPD2 haploinsufficiency or overexpression, potentially disrupting nuclear transport and causing developmental abnormalities. RGPD2 represents a candidate gene for nuclear transport disorders and may serve as a therapeutic target for nucleocytoplasmic transport defects.
