## Description
RAB34 (RAB34, member RAS oncogene family, also known as NARR, OFD20, RAB39, RAH) is a 259 amino acid intracellular protein (UniProt Q9BZG1, isoform NARR P0DI83 is 198 aa) with high-confidence well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 86.1), characteristic of small GTPases that function as molecular switches cycling between GTP-bound active and GDP-bound inactive states. The protein contains a Rab GTPase domain (cd04108, residues 53-220) specific to Rab34 and Rab36 family members, belongs to the small GTPase superfamily (IPR001806/IPR005225/IPR027417/IPR050227/PF00071/PS51419/PS51420/PS51421/PTHR47977/SM00173, residues 41-259/45-258/52-208/53-228) that enables GTPase activity (GO:0003924) and GTP binding (GO:0005525), contains a P-loop containing nucleotide triphosphate hydrolase domain (G3DSA:3.40.50.300/IPR027417, residues 51-233), and is classified as a predicted intracellular protein involved in cilium biogenesis/degradation (GO:0044782), protein transport (GO:0015031), and transport biological processes. Ciliary/centrosomal/basal body localization suggests RAB34 regulates ciliary vesicle trafficking and ciliogenesis. Missense variants (p.Arg211His, p.Glu218Lys, p.Gly202Val, p.Ile85Thr) in the GTPase domain likely impair GTP binding/hydrolysis or effector interactions, causing ciliopathy phenotypes. RAB34 represents a candidate gene for oral-facial-digital syndrome and related ciliopathies through disrupted lysosomal positioning and ciliary vesicle transport.
