## Description
FAM180A (alias UNQ1940, family with sequence similarity 180 member A) encodes a 173 amino acid well-folded secreted protein (AlphaFold pLDDT 86.6) containing a FAM180 family domain (IPR029170/PF15173, aa 28-163) and belonging to the PTHR34034 family (aa 3-170). HPA shows tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in blood vessel (35.4 nTPM), gallbladder (15.0 nTPM), and heart muscle (14.9 nTPM), detected in many tissues distribution, vesicles subcellular localization, low cancer specificity, not detected in immune cells, and group-enriched single cell pattern. Protein class is predicted secreted proteins. ClinVar contains 33 pathogenic variants predominantly consisting of 7q31.32-36.1, 7q32.3-36.1, and 7q33-36.3 copy number gains/losses associated with chromosomal duplication/deletion syndromes. STRING interactions reveal network with extracellular matrix, mitochondrial transport, and DNA repair regulators: SLC25A29 (solute carrier family 25 member 29, mitochondrial S-adenosylmethionine transporter, 0.517), ANKS1A (ankyrin repeat and sterile alpha motif domain containing 1A, receptor tyrosine kinase signaling, 0.514), COL28A1 (collagen type XXVIII alpha 1 chain, extracellular matrix organization, 0.510), KLHL35 (kelch like family member 35, ubiquitin ligase substrate recognition, 0.485), GLT6D1 (glycosyltransferase family 6 domain containing 1, protein glycosylation, 0.479), TCP11 (tetratricopeptide repeat protein 11, sperm-egg fusion, 0.473), GCC2 (GRT1 Golgi retention protein 2, vesicle tethering, 0.452), PI15 (peptidase inhibitor 15, extracellular protease inhibition, 0.448), C17orf67 (chromosome 17 ORF 67, 0.437), and FANCE (Fanconi anemia complementation group E, DNA interstrand crosslink repair, 0.409). Conservation across vertebrates (mouse Q8BR21, zebrafish A0A8M1RNC5) indicates functional constraint. The FAM180 domain architecture combined with high-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 86.6) and secreted/vesicle localization strongly suggests FAM180A functions as an extracellular matrix-associated vesicle cargo protein involved in tissue organization and cell-cell communication. COL28A1 interaction (0.510) places FAM180A in the extracellular matrix network—COL28A1 is a transmembrane collagen regulating basement membrane organization and cell adhesion, suggesting FAM180A may function as a secreted ECM scaffold or modulator of collagen assembly. SLC25A29 interaction (0.517) is particularly intriguing—this mitochondrial SAM transporter links FAM180A to cellular methylation capacity, potentially coordinating extracellular signaling with epigenetic regulation through SAM availability. GCC2 association (0.452) indicates role in Golgi vesicle tethering and cargo sorting, consistent with secreted protein trafficking through the secretory pathway. FANCE interaction (0.409) connects FAM180A to the Fanconi anemia DNA repair complex, suggesting potential nuclear function or coordination between extracellular signaling and genome stability. Blood vessel enrichment (35.4 nTPM) indicates specialized function in vascular tissue, potentially regulating angiogenesis, vascular basement membrane organization, or endothelial cell-extracellular matrix interactions. Heart muscle expression (14.9 nTPM) suggests potential role in cardiac tissue organization or cardiomyocyte-ECM communication. Vesicle subcellular localization indicates packaging into secretory vesicles for extracellular release, potentially functioning as a paracrine signaling factor or ECM structural component. High pLDDT score (86.6) indicates stable folded structure, consistent with secreted proteins requiring defined tertiary structure for extracellular stability and receptor binding. Group-enriched expression pattern suggests coordinated upregulation in specific cell types, potentially reflecting tissue-specific ECM remodeling demands. Pathogenic 7q CNVs likely cause dosage effects disrupting ECM organization, vesicle trafficking, and tissue morphogenesis, potentially contributing to 7q deletion/duplication syndrome (7q11.23 duplication syndrome, Williams-Beuren syndrome region) phenotypes including cardiovascular defects, connective tissue abnormalities, and developmental delays through impaired extracellular matrix assembly and cell-cell communication.
