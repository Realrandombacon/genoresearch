# ZNF3: KRAB-Zinc Finger Transcriptional Repressor with Disease-Associated Variants

**Date:** 2026-03-20T17:57:22.180343

**Quality Score:** 3.45/10  (E=5.1, D=1.8) [WEAK]

## Description
ZNF3 encodes a 446-amino acid Krüppel-associated box (KRAB) C2H2 zinc finger protein localized to the nucleoplasm. The protein contains a canonical KRAB domain (residues 50-123, IPR001909, PF01352) that recruits the corepressor TRIM28/KAP1 to establish heterochromatin via histone methyltransferase activity, and three C2H2 zinc finger DNA-binding domains (residues 200-283, IPR013087, PF00096) that recognize specific GC-rich promoter sequences. HPA data shows ubiquitous expression across all tissues with low tissue specificity (detected in all), consistent with a general transcriptional regulatory role. STRING interactions reveal high-confidence associations with TRIM28 (0.526), ZNF711 (0.575), and ZNF44 (0.454), supporting its function within the KRAB-ZFP silencing complex. Additional partners include ATP1B2 (0.770) and NOX4 (0.770), suggesting potential links to ion transport and oxidative stress pathways. ClinVar documents 23 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number losses and deletions across 7q22.1, implicating ZNF3 dosage sensitivity in human disease, though specific phenotypes remain uncharacterized. The KRAB domain mediates heterodimerization with other zinc finger proteins, while the C2H2 arrays provide sequence-specific DNA recognition. ZNF3 likely functions as a sequence-specific transcriptional repressor that recruits chromatin remodeling complexes to silence target genes involved in cell differentiation and proliferation.
