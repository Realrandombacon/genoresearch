# Cerebellar Degeneration-Related Protein 2 (CDR2/Yo): Paraneoplastic Antigen and Nucleoplasmic Regulator of Cell Cycle Progression via MYC and WEE1 Interactions

## Description
CDR2 encodes a 454 aa nucleoplasmic protein (UniProt Q01850), also known as Yo antigen or CDR62, that belongs to the centrocortin family (PTHR19232) and is best characterized as the target autoantigen in paraneoplastic cerebellar degeneration (PCD) associated with ovarian and breast cancers. The protein contains a conserved CDR2 family domain (IPR026079, residues 1-450) and a distinctive C-terminal domain (IPR060423, PF27644, residues 417-450) of unknown molecular function. STRING interactions place CDR2 in a network regulating cell proliferation and cell cycle control: CDR1 (0.999), its paralog also implicated in paraneoplastic syndromes; MYC (0.872), the master transcriptional regulator of cell growth; CDR2L (0.797), a CDR2-like protein; BRSK1 (0.778) and WEE1 (0.719), kinases controlling G2/M checkpoint and mitotic entry; KTN1 (0.704), a kinetochore protein; and DUSP5 (0.671), a MAPK phosphatase. HPA confirms ubiquitous expression across all tissues with exclusive nucleoplasmic localization and cell type-enhanced specificity, consistent with roles in transcriptional regulation or chromatin-associated processes. ClinVar lists 76 pathogenic variants including extensive copy number losses/gains spanning chromosome 16p12.2, deletions, and splice variants (c.507-7del), demonstrating extreme dosage sensitivity and linking CDR2 disruption to developmental disorders and cancer susceptibility. AlphaFold structure prediction yields high confidence (global pLDDT 74.1), revealing a well-folded architecture suitable for stable protein-protein interactions. Mechanistically, CDR2 functions through multiple integrated modes: (1) As a paraneoplastic antigen, CDR2 is aberrantly expressed in tumor cells (particularly ovarian adenocarcinomas), triggering autoantibody production that cross-reacts with cerebellar Purkinje neurons, causing PCD; (2) Interaction with MYC suggests CDR2 may regulate MYC stability, localization, or transcriptional activity, potentially acting as a tumor suppressor or oncogenic cofactor; (3) Association with WEE1 and BRSK1 indicates involvement in cell cycle checkpoint control, possibly modulating G2/M transition during stress responses; (4) Nucleoplasmic localization and KTN1 interaction suggest roles in kinetochore assembly or chromosome segregation fidelity; (5) The C-terminal domain may mediate specific protein-protein interactions or post-translational modifications critical for function. Disruption of CDR2 would cause defective cell cycle regulation, genomic instability, and potentially altered neuronal survival—phenotypes consistent with both the neurodegeneration observed in PCD and the developmental disorders linked to 16p12.2 deletions.
