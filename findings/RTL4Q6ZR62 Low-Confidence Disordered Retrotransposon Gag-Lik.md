## Description
RTL4 (Retrotransposon Gag-like protein 4, also known as SIRH11, ZCCHC16, Mar4, Mart4) is a 310 amino acid nucleic acid-binding protein (UniProt Q6ZR62) with low-confidence disordered architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 48.8), characteristic of retrotransposon-derived proteins that function through flexible protein-protein and protein-nucleic acid interactions. The protein contains a CCHC-type zinc finger domain (IPR001878/IPR036875/PS50158/SSF57756, residues 266-300/280-293) with the canonical C-X2-C-X4-H-X4-C motif that coordinates zinc ions for nucleic acid binding (GO:0003676, GO:0008270). RTL4 belongs to the RTL1-related family (IPR032567/PTHR15503, residues 41-298), part of the LDOC1-related superfamily derived from ancient retroviral Gag polyprotein integrations. RTL4 is a member of the SIRH (Sushi-Interspersed Repetitive Sequences-Homolog) gene family, representing domesticated retrotransposons co-opted for mammalian-specific functions.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals a network of RTL family members and associated proteins: RTL9 (0.716, Retrotransposon Gag-like protein 9), RTL6 (0.673, Retrotransposon Gag-like protein 6), LDOC1 (0.653, Leucine zipper down-regulated in cancer 1), RTL1 (0.627, Retrotransposon Gag-like protein 1/Peg11, imprinted gene critical for placental development), LHFPL1 (0.618, Lipoma HMGIC fusion partner-like 1), RTL8C (0.532, Retrotransposon Gag-like protein 8C), FANCM (0.532, Fanconi anemia complementation group M, DNA repair helicase), ITGB1 (0.519, Integrin beta-1, cell adhesion), RTL5 (0.518, Retrotransposon Gag-like protein 5), and AMBP (0.514, Alpha-1-microglobulin/bikunin precursor). These interactions confirm RTL4 functions within the RTL protein network, potentially involved in nucleic acid metabolism, genome stability, and cell adhesion.\n\nHPA expression reveals RTL4 is not detected in normal tissues (RNA tissue specificity: not detected), but shows group-enriched expression in cancers. Subcellular localization includes nucleoplasm, plasma membrane, and cytosol, consistent with roles in nuclear nucleic acid binding and potential membrane-associated functions. The cancer-enriched expression pattern suggests RTL4 may be reactivated in tumors, similar to cancer-testis antigens.\n\nClinVar documents 161 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all large copy number variations on chromosome Xq: gains (Xq13.1-27.1, Xq23-25, Xq21.1-24, Xq12-28, Xp22.33-q28 amplifications) and losses (Xq21.33-24, Xq21.32-26.1, Xq21.31-23, Xq22.2-28, Xq21.31-25 deletions). The variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity. While no specific Mendelian disease is annotated, Xq CNVs are associated with intellectual disability, developmental delay, and X-linked disorders. Given RTL4's nucleic acid binding function and retrotransposon origin, dosage imbalance may affect genome stability, retroelement silencing, or chromatin regulation.\n\nConservation is mammalian-specific: human (Q6ZR62, 310 aa), mouse (Q3URY0, 304 aa), indicating RTL4 arose from retrotransposon domestication in the mammalian lineage. The RTL/SIRH gene family is unique to placental mammals and has been co-opted for functions in placental development, brain evolution, and reproduction.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: RTL4 functions as a nucleic acid-binding protein derived from ancient retroviral Gag polyproteins, retaining the CCHC zinc finger motif for RNA/DNA recognition. As a domesticated retrotransposon, RTL4 may regulate endogenous retroelement expression, maintain genome stability, or participate in epigenetic silencing of retrotransposons. The protein may form complexes with other RTL family members (RTL1, RTL5, RTL6, RTL9) to create higher-order nucleoprotein structures. In the nucleoplasm, RTL4 may bind retroelement transcripts or chromatin, recruiting silencing machinery. The cancer-enriched expression suggests RTL4 is normally silenced in somatic tissues but reactivated in tumors through epigenetic dysregulation, potentially contributing to genome instability or serving as a tumor antigen. Xq CNVs cause RTL4 dosage imbalance, disrupting retroelement control and contributing to X-linked developmental disorders. RTL4 represents a fascinating example of retrotransposon domestication in mammalian evolution, with roles in genome regulation and potential implications for cancer and neurodevelopmental disease.
