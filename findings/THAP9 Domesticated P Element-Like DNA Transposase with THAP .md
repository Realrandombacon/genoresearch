## Description
THAP9 encodes a 903 aa domesticated DNA transposase (UniProt Q9H5L6) derived from ancestral P element-like transposons, now repurposed for human genome regulation. The protein contains an N-terminal THAP-type zinc finger domain (IPR006612/PF05485, aa1-95) for sequence-specific DNA binding, followed by a THAP9-like helix-turn-helix domain (IPR021896/PF12017, aa174-245), and a catalytic core with P transposase-like RNase H domains (IPR048365-67/PF21787-89, aa252-638) plus a C-terminal domain (IPR055035, aa708-852). AlphaFold prediction shows confident structure (pLDDT 73.5), indicating a well-folded transposase architecture. HPA shows ubiquitous expression across all tissues with cell type-enhanced specificity and mitochondrial localization, suggesting broad regulatory roles. STRING interactions reveal functional clustering with other domesticated transposases and THAP family members: PGBD5 (0.802), HARBI1 (0.592), GIN1 (0.590), ZBED8 (0.586), THAP12 (0.585), THAP8 (0.581), THAP10 (0.570), PGBD2 (0.563), THAP11 (0.545). ClinVar contains 23 pathogenic variants (primarily 4q copy number losses/gains), though specific disease associations remain undefined. Literature (47 papers) demonstrates THAP9 retains transposase activity in vitro, regulates oligodendrocyte differentiation as a human-specific factor, and functions through THAP9-AS1 lncRNA in cancer progression. THAP9 likely functions as a domesticated transposase that lost mobility but retained DNA-binding and catalytic capabilities for endogenous genome regulation, potentially mediating DNA rearrangements, transcriptional control, or chromatin remodeling through THAP domain-mediated sequence recognition and RNase H-like catalytic activity. The mitochondrial localization suggests possible roles in mitochondrial DNA maintenance or retrograde signaling.
