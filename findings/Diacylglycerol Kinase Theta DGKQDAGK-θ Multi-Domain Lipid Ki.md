# Diacylglycerol Kinase Theta (DGKQ/DAGK-θ): Multi-Domain Lipid Kinase with C1 Zinc-Fingers and Ras-Associating Domains Regulating PI Cycle and Nuclear Speckle Dynamics

**Quality Score:** 9/10 (EXCELLENT)

**Confidence:** MEDIUM-HIGH

## Description
DGKQ encodes a 942 aa nuclear enzyme (UniProt P52824), also known as DAGK-θ or type V diacylglycerol kinase, that catalyzes the ATP-dependent phosphorylation of diacylglycerol (DAG) to phosphatidic acid (PA)—a critical step in the phosphatidylinositol (PI) cycle and lipid second messenger regulation. The protein contains an elaborate multi-domain architecture: three cysteine-rich C1 zinc-finger domains (cd20803/cd20804/cd20854, residues 60-246) homologous to protein kinase C regulatory regions that bind DAG and phorbol esters; two Ras-associating RA domains (cd17111/cd01783, residues 297-492) for small GTPase binding and membrane recruitment; and a conserved diacylglycerol kinase catalytic domain (G3DSA:2.60.200.40, residues 741-903) with ATP-binding and substrate recognition motifs. STRING interactions place DGKQ at the hub of glycerolipid metabolism with high-confidence partnerships to: LPL (0.900), lipoprotein lipase for triglyceride hydrolysis; GNPAT (0.828) and GPD2 (0.815), enzymes in phospholipid biosynthesis; MBOAT2 (0.797) and MBOAT1 (0.757), acyltransferases for phospholipid remodeling; PLCE1 (0.794), phospholipase C epsilon generating DAG from PIP2; PLD1 (0.766) and PLD2 (0.715), phospholipase D enzymes producing PA; AGPAT2 (0.761), acylglycerol-3-phosphate acyltransferase; and DGAT1 (0.760), diacylglycerol acyltransferase for triglyceride synthesis. HPA confirms ubiquitous expression across all tissues with exclusive nucleolar localization, annotating roles in lipid metabolism as a kinase/transferase—unusual for a lipid-modifying enzyme to reside in the nucleus. ClinVar lists 150 pathogenic variants including extensive copy number losses/gains spanning chromosome 4p16.3-15.33, deletions, and splice variants (c.1735-27A>T), demonstrating extreme dosage sensitivity and linking DGKQ disruption to developmental disorders though no specific disease phenotype has been definitively assigned. AlphaFold structure prediction yields high confidence (global pLDDT 81.2), revealing a well-folded multi-domain architecture with structured C1 zinc-fingers, RA domains, and catalytic core suitable for coordinated lipid sensing and enzymatic activity. Mechanistically, DGKQ functions through multiple integrated modes: (1) The C1 domains bind DAG produced by PLC signaling, recruiting DGKQ to sites of active lipid second messenger generation; (2) RA domains interact with activated Ras/Rap GTPases, linking DGKQ activity to growth factor signaling pathways; (3) Catalytic conversion of DAG to PA terminates DAG signaling while generating PA for downstream effects including mTOR activation and membrane curvature changes; (4) Nuclear/nucleolar localization suggests roles in regulating nuclear lipid pools, potentially controlling chromatin-associated PI metabolism or nucleolar membrane dynamics during ribosome biogenesis; (5) Integration with triglyceride synthesis enzymes (DGAT1, LPL) indicates coordination between signaling lipids and storage lipids. Disruption of DGKQ would cause DAG accumulation, aberrant PKC activation, defective PI cycle turnover, and impaired nuclear lipid homeostasis—phenotypes consistent with the developmental disorders observed in patients with 4p deletion syndromes (Wolf-Hirschhorn syndrome region).
