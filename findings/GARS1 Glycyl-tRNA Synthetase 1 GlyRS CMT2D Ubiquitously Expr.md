## Description
GARS1 encodes a 739 aa cytoplasmic enzyme (UniProt P41250), also known as GlyRS, that functions as a class II aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase catalyzing the ATP-dependent ligation of glycine to its cognate tRNA(Gly) while participating in multi-synthetase complexes and exhibiting non-canonical signaling functions in neurodegeneration. This well-studied gene (35 publications) has critical clinical significance for Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease type 2D (CMT2D), distal hereditary motor neuropathy type V (dHMN-V), severe early-onset spinal muscular atrophy (SMA), and autoimmune myopathies. The protein features conserved aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase architecture: GlyRS-like core domain (cd00774, residues 124-470) containing the catalytic site for glycine activation; GlyRS anticodon-binding domain (cd00858, residues 588-711) recognizing tRNA(Gly) anticodon loop; GlyRS RNA-binding domain (cd00935, residues 64-114) mediating tRNA interaction; WHEP-TRS domain (IPR000738, residues 63-122) characteristic of vertebrate aaRSs enabling protein-protein interactions and non-canonical functions; Class II aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase domain (IPR002314, residues 295-369) defining the catalytic fold; Glycyl-tRNA synthetase family signature (IPR002315, residues 121-708); classified within S15/NS1 RNA-binding superfamily (G3DSA:1.10.287.10, residues 66-110) and anticodon-binding domain superfamily (G3DSA:3.40.50.800, residues 613-730) with GO terms for aminoacyl-tRNA ligase activity, ATP binding, and protein biosynthesis. STRING interactions reveal GARS1 operates within extremely high-confidence multi-synthetase complex networks: HARS1 (0.980)/HARS2 (0.969), histidyl-tRNA synthetases; GPHN (0.962), gephyrin scaffolding protein linking to inhibitory neurotransmission; YARS1 (0.898), tyrosyl-tRNA synthetase; AARS1 (0.881), alanyl-tRNA synthetase; SARS1 (0.840)/NARS1 (0.833)/IARS1 (0.828)/MARS1 (0.827), serine/asparagine/isoleucine/methionyl-tRNA synthetases; EPRS1 (0.822), glutamyl-prolyl-tRNA synthetase. HPA confirms low tissue specificity (detected in all tissues), low cancer and immune cell specificity, cytosol subcellular localization, classified as disease-related enzyme/essential metabolic protein with involvement in Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease, neuropathy, and neurodegeneration. ClinVar lists 165 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including extensive missense mutations throughout catalytic core (c.67C>T p.Arg23Trp, c.148G>T p.Ala50Ser, c.176G>A p.Gly59Glu, c.815T>G p.Leu272Arg, c.1239C>G p.Ile413Met, c.2017G>A p.Asp673Asn, c.2034C>G p.Asn678Lys, c.2215G>A p.Glu739Lys), intronic variant (c.1699+93A>T), and large-scale copy number loss (7p14.3-12.3 29-47Mb x1), demonstrating extreme mutational burden causing dominant peripheral neuropathies through gain-of-function mechanisms rather than haploinsufficiency. AlphaFold structure prediction yields high confidence (global pLDDT 86.1), revealing a well-folded class II synthetase structure with properly positioned catalytic and tRNA-binding domains. Mechanistically, GARS1 operates through integrated modes: (1) Canonical translation—charges tRNA(Gly) enabling protein synthesis; (2) CMT2D/dHMN pathogenesis—2025 study (5 citations) demonstrates glycyl-tRNA sequestration as unifying mechanism where mutant GARS1 sequesters tRNA(Gly) causing translational defects in peripheral neurons; (3) Non-canonical signaling—WHEP-TRS domain mediates interactions with neuropilin receptors, VEGF-A signaling, and angiogenic pathways independent of catalytic activity; (4) SMA association—2025 case report links GARS1 variants to severe early-onset spinal muscular atrophy expanding phenotypic spectrum; (5) Cancer biomarker—2024 pan-cancer analysis (2 citations) identifies GARS1 as prognostic and immunological biomarker in bladder cancer and other malignancies; (6) Autoimmune target—gene summary confirms GARS1 is autoantigen in polymyositis/dermatomyositis; (7) Multi-synthetase complex—associates with other aaRSs (HARS1, YARS1, EPRS1) forming cytoplasmic complex for efficient translation. Loss-of-function causes defective glycine-tRNA charging, impaired protein synthesis, while dominant mutations cause toxic gain-of-function through tRNA sequestration, aberrant signaling, and peripheral axon degeneration.
