# CORO1B (Coronin-1B, CORONIN-2): WD40 Repeat-Containing Actin-Binding Protein Coordinating Lamellipodia Dynamics and Cell Motility via Branched Actin Network Turnthrough Regulation Through Cortactin Recruitment and ADF/Cofilin Pathway Modulation with Breast Cancer Metastasis Association

**Confidence:** MEDIUM

## Description
CORO1B encodes a 489 aa cytosolic/plasma membrane protein (UniProt Q9BR76), also known as CORONIN-2, that functions as a critical regulator of actin cytoskeleton dynamics controlling cell migration, lamellipodia formation, and metastatic potential through WD40 repeat-mediated protein interactions and branched actin network turnover coordination. The protein features a characteristic coronin architecture: an N-terminal domain of unknown function DUF1899 (IPR015048/PF08953, residues 5-70) potentially involved in membrane association or regulatory interactions; a central beta-propeller core formed by seven WD40 repeats (IPR001680/PF00400/PS00678/PS50082, residues 68-387) creating a versatile protein interaction scaffold; and a C-terminal extension completing the coronin family signature (IPR015505, residues 4-484). The YVTN repeat-like/YWTD beta-propeller superfamily (G3DSA:2.130.10.10/IPR015943/IPR036322, residues 3-352) provides structural stability for partner binding. STRING interactions confirm CORO1B operates within actin cytoskeleton remodeling networks: CORO1C (0.917), paralogous family member with overlapping/compensatory functions in autophagosome formation; CORO6 (0.787), another coronin family member suggesting cooperative regulation; ACTR2 (0.780), ARP2/3 complex subunit essential for branched actin nucleation; CTTN (0.763), cortactin stabilizing Arp2/3-mediated branches and recruiting CORO1B to leading edge; JAG2 (0.755), Jagged2 Notch ligand linking to developmental signaling; ACTG1 (0.681), gamma-actin cytoskeletal component; CFL1 (0.644) and CFL2 (0.507), cofilin family severing aged ADP-actin filaments; CORO1A (0.435), immune cell-specific paralog; and HCLS1 (0.409), hematopoietic lineage cell-specific protein. HPA confirms ubiquitous expression (detected in all tissues) with low tissue specificity, plasma membrane and cytosol localization consistent with actin cortex function, classified as actin-binding protein with immune cell enhancement in blood. ClinVar lists 12 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants predominantly copy number alterations including gains (11q12.1-13.3 spanning 57-69Mb, 11q12.2-13.5 spanning 60-76Mb, 11p13-q25 spanning 33-135Mb, 11q13.1-13.2 spanning 65-68Mb, 11p15.5-q25 spanning 0.2-135Mb x2) and losses (11q13.1-13.2 spanning 66-68Mb, 11q13.2-13.3 spanning 67-69Mb), demonstrating dosage sensitivity though specific monogenic diseases remain undefined. AlphaFold structure prediction yields high confidence (global pLDDT 89.4), revealing well-folded seven-bladed beta-propeller characteristic of WD40 proteins providing stable interaction platform. Mechanistically, CORO1B operates through integrated modes: (1) Lamellipodia dynamics—2022 study demonstrates CORO1B/CORO1C cooperatively regulate branched actin turnover by tuning ADF/cofilin activity at leading edge; (2) Cortactin recruitment—binds cortactin (CTTN) targeting to Arp2/3-nucleated branches stabilizing protrusive structures; (3) Cell motility control—coordinates actin filament disassembly enabling persistent migration essential for wound healing and cancer invasion; (4) Breast cancer metastasis—2025 study identifies CORO1B as biomarker for lymph node metastasis through bulk/single-cell sequencing integration; (5) HNSCC aggressiveness—2016 study links CORO1B amplification/expression to aggressive chromosome 11q13-amplified head and neck squamous cell carcinoma subset; (6) Autophagy coordination—paralog CORO1C promotes autophagosome formation suggesting CORO1B may similarly link cytoskeleton to vesicle trafficking. Loss-of-function causes defective lamellipodia stability, impaired cell migration, disrupted actin turnover leading to aberrant cytoskeletal organization, reduced metastatic potential in cancer contexts, and developmental defects reflected in CNV-associated phenotypes.
