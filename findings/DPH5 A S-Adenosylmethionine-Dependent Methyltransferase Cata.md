# DPH5: A S-Adenosylmethionine-Dependent Methyltransferase Catalyzing the Final Step of Diphthamide Biosynthesis on Elongation Factor 2

## Description
DPH5 (Diphthamide Biosynthesis 5, UniProt Q9H2P9), also known as diphthine methyl ester synthase, is a 285-amino acid cytosolic enzyme that catalyzes the terminal methylation step in diphthamide biosynthesis—the conversion of diphthine to diphthine methyl ester using S-adenosylmethionine (SAM) as methyl donor. Diphthamide is a unique post-translationally modified histidine residue (2-[3-carboxyamido-3-(trimethylammonio)propyl]histidine) found exclusively on eukaryotic elongation factor 2 (EEF2), and is the target of diphtheria toxin and Pseudomonas exotoxin A which ADP-ribosylate this residue to halt protein synthesis. InterPro analysis reveals a conserved tetrapyrrole methylase fold (IPR000878, IPR035996, PF00590, aa 1-239) with two subdomains: an N-terminal domain (IPR014777, G3DSA:3.40.1010.10, aa 1-111) and a C-terminal domain (IPR014776, G3DSA:3.30.950.10, aa 112-274) that together form the SAM-binding pocket and substrate recognition site; DPH5 belongs to the diphthine synthase family (IPR004551, MF_01084, aa 1-277). The AlphaFold predicted structure exhibits exceptional confidence (pLDDT 92.1), revealing a well-ordered α/β fold characteristic of Class I methyltransferases with a central β-sheet flanked by α-helices creating the active site cleft. STRING interaction data places DPH5 at the terminus of the seven-step diphthamide pathway with high confidence associations to: EEF2 (0.948), the substrate bearing the modified histidine; DPH3 (0.857), an iron-sulfur cluster protein required for early steps; DNAJC24 (0.818), a J-domain chaperone facilitating complex assembly; and other biosynthetic enzymes DPH1/DPH2/DPH6/DPH7 (0.492-0.599); RTCA (0.763) and PIGM (0.709) represent additional metabolic connections. HPA expression profiles confirm ubiquitous detection across all tissues with nucleolar and Golgi apparatus localization—consistent with roles in ribosome biogenesis and protein trafficking; DPH5 is classified as essential for translation fidelity and is associated with intellectual disability when mutated. ClinVar records document 15 pathogenic variants including splice defects (c.260+1G>T), missense mutations in critical regions (p.Asn110Ser in N-domain, p.Ala223Val in C-domain, p.His260Arg near active site), nonsense mutations (p.Arg207Ter causing truncation), small deletions (c.635-6del), and large copy number losses/gains spanning 1p21.1-p22.1 (8-27 Mb regions). We hypothesize that DPH5 functions as the pathway terminus enzyme: after six prior steps generate diphthine from a conserved histidine on EEF2 (involving DPH1-DPH4, DPH6, DPH7), DPH5 binds SAM and diphthine-modified EEF2; the methyltransferase active site positions the SAM methyl group for nucleophilic attack by the diphthine carboxylate; this produces diphthine methyl ester, which is subsequently amidated by DPH6/DPH7 to form mature diphthamide; the nucleolar localization suggests DPH5 may modify newly synthesized EEF2 during ribosome assembly; loss of DPH5 causes incomplete diphthamide modification, impairing translation elongation fidelity and contributing to neurodevelopmental disorders; the intellectual disability phenotype reflects the brain's sensitivity to translational defects; diphtheria toxin resistance in DPH5-deficient cells confirms the essential role of this modification step.
