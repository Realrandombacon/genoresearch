## Description
LOC375196 (Gene ID: 375196, UniProt Q8N2V9) is a 240 amino acid protein located on chromosome 2p22.1 (NC_000002.12, positions 38960343-38959286). This represents a poorly characterized locus with conflicting molecular evidence—while UniProt confirms a protein entry exists, multiple databases lack functional data. InterPro domain analysis reveals a zinc-containing alcohol dehydrogenase signature (IPR002328/PS00059, positions 71-85) with associated GO terms for zinc ion binding (GO:0008270) and oxidoreductase activity (GO:0016491), suggesting potential enzymatic function in alcohol/aldehyde metabolism. However, AlphaFold structure prediction (AF-Q8N2V9-F1) yields very low confidence model (global pLDDT 32.8), indicating the protein is predominantly intrinsically disordered rather than adopting a stable globular fold typical of functional alcohol dehydrogenases. This structural disorder contradicts the expected well-folded catalytic domain architecture of canonical ADH enzymes, raising questions about enzymatic functionality. STRING database returned no protein interactions (no protein found for LOC375196 in species 9606), confirming absence of interaction network data despite UniProt entry existence. Human Protein Atlas could not resolve LOC375196 to expression data (ENSG00000269210 not in HPA database), suggesting either very low/absent expression across surveyed tissues or technical detection limitations. ClinVar lists 3 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all large copy number gains or losses spanning extensive 2p regions (multi-megabase CNVs from ~23Mb-55Mb affecting many genes); variant types include duplications NC_000002.12:g.(?_236816)_(45983232_?)dup and deletions spanning 2p22-p21 regions. No specific monogenic disease is attributed solely to LOC375196. Conservation data is limited without confirmed orthologs in standard databases. Functional hypothesis: LOC375196 may represent (1) a pseudogene remnant retaining ADH-like sequence motifs but lacking proper folding capacity, (2) a highly divergent ADH family member that has evolved alternative non-enzymatic functions through intrinsic disorder, (3) a primate-specific gene with extremely restricted spatiotemporal expression, or (4) an intrinsically disordered protein that uses its ADH-like motif for protein-protein interactions rather than catalysis. The combination of ADH domain signature with extremely low structural confidence (pLDDT 32.8) and absent expression data strongly suggests this protein does not function as a canonical alcohol dehydrogenase enzyme. Disease mechanism: Given absence of specific LOC375196 point mutations in ClinVar (only large 2p CNVs), this gene is unlikely to cause monogenic disease when disrupted alone. Clinical associations with 2p CNVs likely reflect haploinsufficiency of neighboring well-characterized genes. Priority experiments include deep RNA-seq across diverse tissues/cell types to detect transcripts, ribosome profiling to confirm translation, mass spectrometry to detect protein product and assess post-translational modifications, NMR spectroscopy to characterize disordered state dynamics, and comparative genomics to identify orthologs and assess evolutionary constraint on the ADH-like motif.
