Recent literature establishes emerging specialized roles: circFN1 promotes acute myeloid leukemia cell proliferation and invasion while restraining apoptosis via miR-1294/ARHGEF10L axis demonstrating ceRNA regulatory mechanism (2 citations, 2024); ARHGEF10L effect on tumor growth in gastric cancer investigated using quantitative MRI parameters in nude mouse model establishing in vivo oncogenic function (2 citations, 2022); ARHGEF10L promotes cervical tumorigenesis via RhoA-mediated signaling revealing GTPase-dependent transformation mechanism (6 citations, 2021); ARHGEF10L contributes to liver tumorigenesis through RhoA-ROCK1 signaling and epithelial-mesenchymal transition establishing metastasis-promoting pathway (19 citations, 2019). The mechanistic hypothesis: ARHGEF10L functions as a membrane-associated RhoGEF that activates RHOA/CDC42 GTPases through its DH-PH tandem domain module. The DH domain (residues 306-521) catalyzes GDP-to-GTP exchange on RHOA/CDC42 switch I/II regions, converting them to active signaling states. The adjacent PH domain (residues 525-753) binds phosphatidylinositol phosphates (PIP2/PIP3) at Golgi and vesicle membranes, recruiting ARHGEF10L to specific subcellular compartments where Rho signaling initiates. The C-terminal WD40 repeats (residues 822-1131) serve as protein interaction platform recruiting regulators (ESRP2, RBM28) or effectors (MYO1B). In acute myeloid leukemia, circFN1 sponges miR-1294 preventing ARHGEF10L mRNA degradation, increasing ARHGEF10L protein levels and enhancing RHOA activation driving proliferation/invasion. In gastric cancer, ARHGEF10L overexpression promotes tumor growth detectable by MRI volumetric changes. In cervical cancer, ARHGEF10L-RHOA axis drives transformation through downstream ROCK/LIMK/cofilin cytoskeletal remodeling. In liver cancer, ARHGEF10L activates RhoA-ROCK1 signaling inducing EMT transcription factors (SNAIL, TWIST, ZEB1) promoting metastasis. The 1p36 CNV dosage changes alter ARHGEF10L expression: deletion reduces Rho signaling potentially causing developmental defects or immune dysfunction; duplication increases Rho signaling promoting cancer progression and metastasis. The blood cell enrichment suggests ARHGEF10L regulates hematopoietic cell migration, adhesion, or differentiation. The absence of formal disease associations despite 20 pathogenic variants suggests ARHGEF10L dysfunction contributes to 1p36 deletion syndrome (OMIM #607872), hematologic malignancies (AML, MDS), solid tumor susceptibility, or congenital disorders of cell migration not yet mapped to Mendelian phenotypes. Future work should define ARHGEF10L's GEF specificity and kinetics via nucleotide exchange assays with RHOA/RAC1/CDC42, determine crystal structure of ARHGEF10L DH-PH tandem to identify autoinhibitory interfaces, investigate ARHGEF10L mutations in 1p36 deletion syndrome patients with cancer predisposition, assess ARHGEF10L role in hematopoietic stem cell homing and engraftment, develop ARHGEF10L inhibitors for blocking Rho-driven metastasis, explore ARHGEF10L biomarker potential in AML minimal residual disease monitoring, and test whether ARHGEF10L levels predict response to ROCK inhibitors in liver cancer.
