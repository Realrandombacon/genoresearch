## Description
AASDHPPT (L-Aminoadipate-Semialdehyde Dehydrogenase-Phosphopantetheinyl Transferase) is a unique 309 aa bifunctional enzyme that couples the lysine degradation pathway with the activation of mitochondrial carrier proteins via phosphopantetheinylation, playing a critical role in cellular energy metabolism and redox balance. InterPro analysis reveals a complex domain architecture characteristic of phosphopantetheinyl transferases (PPTases): it contains a canonical PPTase catalytic domain (IPR008278/PF01648, aa 125-241) responsible for transferring the 4'-phosphopantetheine moiety from CoA to serine residues on target proteins, an N-terminal PPTase subdomain (IPR055066/PF22624, aa 25-122), and belongs to the broader PPTase superfamily (IPR037143/IPR050559, aa 14-282) with GO annotations for magnesium ion binding (GO:0000287) and holo-[acyl-carrier-protein] synthase activity (GO:0008897). The G3DSA:3.90.470.20 superfamily (aa 17-290) indicates a classic alpha-beta fold typical of nucleotidyltransferases. AlphaFold predicts a very high-confidence structure (pLDDT 90.3), confirming a rigid, well-folded globular enzyme capable of precise catalysis. STRING interactions place AASDHPPT at the intersection of fatty acid synthesis and mitochondrial metabolism with ultra-high confidence partners: FASN (0.987, Fatty Acid Synthase), COASY (0.913, Coenzyme A Synthase), MCAT (0.880, Malonyl-CoA-ACP Transacylase), and mitochondrial complex components TMEM126A (0.831) and TMEM126B (0.851), as well as DLD (0.845, Dihydrolipoamide Dehydrogenase). These interactions strongly suggest AASDHPPT provides the essential phosphopantetheine cofactor for mitochondrial acyl carrier proteins (mtACP) required for lipoic acid synthesis and fatty acid oxidation. HPA shows ubiquitous expression (detected in all tissues) with low tissue and cancer specificity, consistent with a housekeeping metabolic enzyme, and classification as an intracellular transferase. ClinVar contains 19 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number losses and gains on chromosome 11q (11q22.1-23.3), implicating AASDHPPT haploinsufficiency in 11q deletion syndrome phenotypes, though no specific monogenic disorder is yet definitively attributed solely to AASDHPPT point mutations. Literature (7 papers) establishes AASDHPPT as: essential for mitochondrial oxidative metabolism via phosphopantetheinylation of mitochondrial carriers (2024-2025); a risk locus identified in genome-wide association studies for hospitalized influenza severity (2025); and a gene under selective pressure in chicken evolution (2016).
