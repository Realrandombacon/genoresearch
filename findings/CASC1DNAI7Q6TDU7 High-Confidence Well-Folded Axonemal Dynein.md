## Description
CASC1 (Cancer Susceptibility Candidate 1), also known as DNAI7 (Dynein axonemal intermediate chain 7) or IC97, is a 716 amino acid intracellular protein localized to the cilium, axoneme, and cytoplasmic microtubules (UniProt Q6TDU7) with high-confidence well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 84.8). The protein features a canonical dynein intermediate chain architecture comprising an N-terminal domain (IPR031826/PF15927, residues 24-224) forming a ~201 aa module (~28%) likely involved in light chain binding or dimerization, and a distinctive C-terminal domain (IPR022110/PF12366, residues 461-628) forming a ~168 aa module (~23%) unique to the IC97 subfamily, all embedded within the Dynein-f intermediate chain IC97/Dnai7-like family (IPR023247/PTHR20929, residues 4-716). Molecular functions include beta-tubulin and microtubule binding, enabling its role as a critical structural and regulatory component of the axonemal dynein complex responsible for ciliary beating. HPA expression reveals a tissue-enhanced pattern with exceptional levels in testis (21.4 nTPM), choroid plexus (13.5 nTPM), and fallopian tube (13.2 nTPM), tissues rich in motile cilia or flagella, consistent with its function in generating mechanical force. Subcellular location in vesicles and cytosol reflects its assembly pathway before ciliary transport.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places CASC1/DNAI7 at the heart of the axonemal dynein machinery: strongest interactions with WDR78 (0.795, a WD-repeat protein essential for dynein arm assembly), DNAH2 (0.782, Dynein heavy chain 2), and DNAL1 (0.782, Dynein light chain 1). High confidence links extend to other heavy chains (DNAH1, DNAH7, DNAH5) and intermediate chains (DNAI2), as well as coiled-coil domain proteins like CCDC114 and CFAP70. This dense network confirms its role as a central scaffold linking heavy chain motor domains to light chains and the microtubule doublet, essential for the coordinated sliding of microtubules that drives ciliary motion.\n\nDespite its critical role in ciliary function, ClinVar reports zero pathogenic variants for CASC1/DNAI7. This absence may indicate extreme evolutionary constraint where null mutations are embryonic lethal, or that mutations cause subtle phenotypes (like reduced fertility or mild respiratory issues) not yet captured in clinical databases. Alternatively, redundancy with other intermediate chains might buffer against loss-of-function.\n\nConservation is strong across vertebrates: human (Q6TDU7, 716 aa), mouse (Q6TDU8, 730 aa), zebrafish (A6H8T2, 731 aa), indicating strict preservation of the IC97 structure.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: CASC1/DNAI7 acts as the 'IC97' intermediate chain of outer dynein arms in motile cilia. Its N-terminal domain anchors light chains (like DNAL1) which modulate motor activity, while the C-terminal domain mediates specific attachment to the A-tubule of the microtubule doublet or interacts with docking complexes (like WDR78). By bridging the heavy chain motor to the microtubule track, it ensures efficient conversion of ATP hydrolysis into mechanical sliding. In testis, it is vital for sperm flagellar motility; in the respiratory tract and brain ventricles (choroid plexus), it drives mucociliary clearance and CSF flow. The lack of ClinVar variants suggests that complete loss is incompatible with life or causes severe ciliopathy phenotypes (e.g., Primary Ciliary Dyskinesia) that are currently under-diagnosed genetically.
