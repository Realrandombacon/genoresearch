## Description
HEMK2 (UniProt Q9Y5N5), also known as N6-adenine-specific DNA methyltransferase 1 or HemK methyltransferase 2, encodes a 214 aa nuclear/cytosolic enzyme belonging to the evolutionarily conserved PrmC/HEMK family of S-adenosyl-L-methionine (SAM)-dependent methyltransferases, functioning as a critical regulator of translation termination, chromatin dynamics, and RNA stability through its unique ability to methylate both protein glutamine and lysine residues. InterPro analysis reveals a compact, single-domain architecture: a canonical AdoMet-dependent methyltransferase fold (aa 50-175, cd02440) with a PrmC-related catalytic core (aa 22-209, IPR004557, IPR052190) capable of dual-substrate specificity; classification within the Mtq2-like release factor methyltransferase family (IPR052190, PTHR45875) links it functionally to yeast Mtq2 in translation termination fidelity. AlphaFold predicts a very high-confidence structure (global pLDDT 94.5), indicating a well-folded, stable globular domain with precise geometry for SAM binding and substrate recognition, consistent with dedicated enzymatic regulators of post-translational modification. STRING interactions define HEMK2 as a hub in the methylation and chromatin networks: TRMT112 (0.999, methyltransferase activator subunit), and multiple histone H4 variants (H4C1-H4C16, scores 0.875-0.900), suggesting roles in coordinating protein methylation with chromatin remodeling. HPA expression shows low tissue specificity with detection in many tissues, classified as a chromatin regulator and methyltransferase localized to nucleoplasm and centrosome, consistent with roles in both transcriptional regulation and cell division. ClinVar reports 22 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including copy number gains on chromosome 21q11.2-22.3 (spanning the HEMK2 locus) and splice site mutations (c.134+2T>G, c.221+1G>T), indicating genomic instability at this region potentially linked to developmental disorders and cancer susceptibility, though no specific monogenic disease has been definitively assigned to HEMK2 alone. Literature (20 papers) highlights: (1) Dual Substrate Specificity—HEMK2 methylates eRF1 at glutamine Q185 (eRF1-Q185me1) for translation termination fidelity AND histone H4 at lysine K12 (H4K12me1) for chromatin regulation, a rare example of a methyltransferase targeting both amino acid types; (2) TRMT112 Dependence—Requires binding to TRMT112 for stability and catalytic activity, forming a heterodimeric complex analogous to other MT-A70 family members; (3) RNA Stability—Functions in Drosophila oogenesis for sufficient protein synthesis and RNA stability through eRF1 methylation; (4) Cancer Target—Identified as a target for NTMT1 bisubstrate inhibitors, suggesting therapeutic potential in cancers with HEMK2 overexpression. Functional hypothesis: HEMK2 functions as a: (1) Translation Termination Regulator—methylates eRF1-Q185 to enhance stop codon recognition and prevent readthrough errors, ensuring proteome integrity; (2) Histone Modifier—methylates H4K12 to modulate chromatin accessibility and transcriptional activation of cell cycle genes; (3) Centrosome Function Coordinator—localizes to centrosomes where it may methylate centrosomal proteins to regulate spindle assembly and chromosome segregation. Mechanistic proposal: HEMK2 forms a stable heterodimer with TRMT112 (STRING 0.999), which stabilizes the catalytic domain and positions SAM for methyl transfer. In the cytoplasm, the complex methylates eRF1-Q185, enhancing its affinity for stop codons and promoting efficient translation termination. In the nucleus, HEMK2 methylates H4K12 at promoters of proliferation genes, recruiting chromatin readers that facilitate transcriptional elongation. The very high-confidence structure (pLDDT 94.5) supports a rigid catalytic mechanism with precise substrate positioning. Chromosome 21q CNVs disrupt HEMK2 dosage, potentially altering both translation fidelity and chromatin states, contributing to the pleiotropic effects of 21q duplication syndromes. The combination of a well-folded catalytic domain, dual-substrate specificity (Gln/Lys), obligate TRMT112 partnership, centrosomal localization, and clinical variant burden positions HEMK2 as a genuine dark gene where intrinsic methyltransferase function bridges translation control and epigenetic regulation.
