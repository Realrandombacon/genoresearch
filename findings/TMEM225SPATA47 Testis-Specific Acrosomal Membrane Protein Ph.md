## Description
TMEM225 (also known as SPATA47, PMP22CD, or PPP1R154) encodes a 225 amino acid transmembrane protein (UniProt Q6GV28) that functions as a protein phosphatase 1 (PP1) regulator during spermatogenesis. InterPro analysis identifies a conserved TMEM225 family domain (IPR033542, IPR057351, PF25452) spanning residues 1-164, representing a novel structural fold unique to this protein family. The GO annotation indicates negative regulation of phosphatase activity, consistent with its alias PPP1R154 (protein phosphatase 1 regulatory subunit 154). AlphaFold prediction shows high confidence (pLDDT 76.0), indicating a well-folded transmembrane structure.\n\nHPA expression demonstrates striking testis specificity (20.5 nTPM) with tissue-enriched classification and detection in a single tissue type only. Gene ontology annotations predict localization to inner and outer acrosomal membranes—the specialized vesicle structure covering the sperm head that contains enzymes essential for egg penetration during fertilization. This places TMEM225 in the acrosome reaction pathway critical for male fertility.\n\nSTRING interaction network reveals connections to spermatogenesis-associated proteins: TMEM225B (paralog, 0.653), SPATS1 (spermatogenesis-associated serine-rich protein 1, 0.618), SPATA3 (spermatogenesis-associated protein 3, 0.599), plus DHRS12 (dehydrogenase/reductase, 0.648) and FSCN3 (fascin actin-bundling protein 3, 0.433—testis-specific isoform). This network forms a functional module for sperm maturation and acrosome biogenesis.\n\nClinVar contains 53 pathogenic copy number variants at 11q24.1-25, predominantly large deletions encompassing the Jacobsen syndrome critical region. TMEM225 haploinsufficiency may contribute to male infertility phenotypes through disrupted PP1 regulation during spermiogenesis, impairing acrosome formation and sperm-egg recognition.
