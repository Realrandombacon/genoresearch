# AP4M1: A Mu Homology Domain-Containing Cargo Recognition Subunit of the AP-4 Complex Essential for Autophagy and Neuronal Protein Sorting

**Date:** 2026-06-26T17:07:28.511032

**Quality Score:** 10/10 (EXCELLENT)

**Confidence:** HIGH

## Description
AP4M1 (Adaptor Protein Complex 4 Subunit Mu-1, UniProt O00189) is a 453-amino acid cargo recognition subunit of the heterotetrameric AP-4 vesicle coat complex that mediates protein sorting from the trans-Golgi network (TGN) to endosomal-lysosomal compartments. InterPro analysis reveals a canonical adaptor complex mu subunit architecture: an N-terminal Longin-like domain (IPR011012, G3DSA:3.30.450.60, aa 1-141) that mediates interactions with other AP-4 subunits; a central mu/sigma subunit domain (IPR022775, aa 42-130); and a large C-terminal mu homology domain (MHD, IPR028565, G3DSA:2.60.40.1170, aa 175-453) containing the conserved tyrosine-based motif recognition site (IPR018240, aa 270-284) that binds dileucine and YXXΦ sorting signals on cargo proteins. The AlphaFold predicted structure shows high confidence (pLDDT 85.4), confirming a stable fold optimized for cargo binding and complex assembly. STRING interaction data places AP4M1 within the vesicle trafficking network with very high confidence associations to other AP-4 complex subunits AP4E1 (0.927), AP4B1 (0.915), and AP4S1 (0.914); related adaptor complexes AP2B1 (0.979), AP1S1 (0.923), and AP5Z1/AP5S1 (0.958-0.960); and the extracellular matrix protein SPP1 (0.946), potentially a cargo or regulator. HPA expression profiles confirm ubiquitous detection across tissues with disease involvement in hereditary spastic paraplegia and neurodegeneration, consistent with essential housekeeping trafficking functions. ClinVar records document 133 pathogenic variants including frameshifts (p.Thr125fs, p.Phe6fs, p.Ser301fs), nonsense mutations (p.Tyr119Ter, p.Trp304Ter), splice defects (c.974+1G>A), and missense changes (p.Leu298Pro), causing autosomal recessive hereditary spastic paraplegia type 50 (SPG50)—a complex neurodegenerative disorder characterized by progressive lower limb spasticity, intellectual disability, and microcephaly. We hypothesize that AP4M1 functions as the primary cargo selector for the AP-4 complex: its mu homology domain recognizes tyrosine-based sorting motifs on transmembrane cargoes (including autophagy receptors like ATG9A and neurotransmitter receptors); upon cargo binding, AP4M1 nucleates assembly of the full AP-4 tetramer with AP4E1, AP4B1, and AP4S1; this recruits clathrin-independent vesicle formation at the TGN for delivery to peripheral endosomes; in neurons, this pathway is critical for maintaining axonal homeostasis by clearing autophagic vesicles and recycling synaptic receptors; loss of AP4M1 disrupts cargo sorting, causing accumulation of undigested autophagosomes in axons, impaired receptor trafficking, and progressive neurodegeneration characteristic of SPG50.
