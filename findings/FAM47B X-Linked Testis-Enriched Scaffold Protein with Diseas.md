## Description
FAM47B encodes a 645 amino acid intracellular scaffold protein (UniProt Q8NA70) belonging to the FAM47 family (InterPro IPR032743, Pfam PF14642). The protein contains three conserved FAM47 domains spanning residues 1-252, 283-339, and 344-436, suggesting a multi-domain architecture optimized for protein-protein interactions. Expression profiling reveals testis-specific enrichment (8.8 nTPM) with no detectable expression in somatic tissues or cancer cell lines, indicating a specialized role in spermatogenesis or germ cell maintenance. Protein interaction network analysis identifies 10 medium-confidence partners (STRING scores 0.475-0.609) including SATL1 (spermatogenesis-associated), TMEM47 and TMEM191B (transmembrane scaffolds), CFAP47 (cilia/flagella assembly), and MAP3K15 (stress-activated kinase signaling). This interaction profile suggests FAM47B functions as a cytoplasmic scaffold organizing germ cell-specific signaling complexes, potentially linking cytoskeletal dynamics to kinase cascades during spermatid differentiation. Clinical significance is evidenced by 148 pathogenic/likely pathogenic ClinVar variants, predominantly X-chromosome copy number gains and losses spanning the FAM47B locus (Xp22.33-Xp11.4 region). The variant burden and X-linkage suggest haploinsufficiency or dosage sensitivity mechanisms may disrupt male fertility pathways. Conservation analysis is pending, but the FAM47 domain architecture is conserved across vertebrates (Panther PTHR47415). AlphaFold structure prediction was unavailable due to server error, but domain organization suggests an extended conformation typical of scaffold proteins. Mechanistic hypothesis: FAM47B serves as a testis-specific adaptor protein that nucleates multi-protein complexes required for spermatid maturation, with disruption causing male infertility through defective germ cell cytoskeletal organization.
