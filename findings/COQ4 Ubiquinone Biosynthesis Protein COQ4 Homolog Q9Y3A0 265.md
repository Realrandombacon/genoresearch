## Description
COQ4 (Coenzyme Q4; UniProt Q9Y3A0, 265 aa) is a PARTIALLY CHARACTERIZED mitochondrial protein with 128 publications representing a BORDERLINE DARK GENE with critical roles in CoQ10 biosynthesis but unresolved molecular mechanism. While recent 2024-2025 studies established COQ4 biallelic variants causing childhood-onset pure hereditary spastic paraplegia [2024], newborn presentations with diverse phenotypes [2024, 4 cites], iPSC models for CoQ4-associated deficiency [2025], and genotype-phenotype correlations in hereditary spastic paraplegia [2025], critical mechanistic gaps persist: (1) Unlike other COQ enzymes, COQ4 lacks clear catalytic activity and may function as an organizing/scaffolding factor for the multi-subunit CoQ synthome complex, yet the structural basis for complex assembly remains uncharacterized despite very high-confidence STRING interactions with all major CoQ biosynthetic enzymes (COQ6 score 0.991, COQ7 0.987, COQ9 0.947, COQ8B/COQ8A 0.927, PDSS1 0.916, COQ2 0.912, COQ5 0.893, COQ3 0.890); (2) HPA data reveals ubiquitous expression across all tissues with mitochondrial inner membrane localization [HPA], biological process of ubiquinone biosynthesis (GO:0006744), and disease associations including neurodegeneration and primary mitochondrial disease [HPA], yet tissue-specific vulnerability patterns (CNS > muscle > kidney) remain unexplained; (3) The presence of 120 ClinVar pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants on chr9q33.3-34.11 including large copy number losses (chr9:126778711-128563751x1), splice variants (c.532+5G>A), frameshifts (p.Ser137fs, p.Leu177fs), and missense mutations (p.His41Arg, p.Arg160Gln, p.Thr144Asn) with variable clinical phenotypes ranging from early epileptic encephalopathy to adult-onset ataxia-spasticity spectrum requires systematic genotype-phenotype mapping [ClinVar]; (4) AlphaFold structure shows high confidence pLDDT: 88.1 across residues 1-265, indicating a well-folded protein with conserved eukaryotic COQ4 architecture (IPR007715 residues 33-262, IPR027540 residues 30-257, PF05019 residues 38-258), consistent with stable mitochondrial inner membrane protein potentially containing protein-protein interaction surfaces for complex assembly; (5) The molecular function annotation lists lyase activity, but no specific biochemical reaction has been assigned to COQ4, supporting the hypothesis that it functions as a complex organizer rather than catalytic enzyme; (6) The phenotypic heterogeneity (encephalopathy, spastic paraplegia, ataxia, cardiomyopathy) despite ubiquitous expression suggests context-dependent requirements for CoQ10 levels or tissue-specific interacting partners remain undefined. This gene represents an under-explored node connecting mitochondrial respiratory chain function, metabolite channeling, and neurodegeneration with therapeutic implications for CoQ10 supplementation strategies.
