## Description
RBM11 (UniProt P57052) encodes a 281 aa RNA-binding protein functioning as a tissue-specific splicing regulator with emerging roles in epididymal differentiation, cancer progression, and nuclear RNA processing. InterPro analysis reveals a canonical RNA recognition motif (RRM) domain (aa 9-87, IPR000504, PF00076, SM00360, cd12593, IPR034501) characteristic of vertebrate RBM11, enabling sequence-specific RNA binding and splicing regulation; classification within the nuclear exosome targeting complex subfamily (IPR052285, PTHR13798, positions 6-202) suggests roles in RNA degradation and quality control; CATH-Gene3D homologous superfamilies confirm nucleotide-binding alpha-beta plait domain architecture (G3DSA:3.30.70.330, IPR012677) and RNA-binding domain superfamily folds (IPR035979, SSF54928). AlphaFold predicts a medium-confidence structure (pLDDT 61.9), consistent with a partially structured protein containing an ordered RRM domain and intrinsically disordered regions suitable for dynamic RNA-protein interactions and splicing complex assembly. STRING interactions define RBM11 as a central node in the splicing regulatory network with high-confidence partners: ZCCHC8 (0.805), a zinc finger protein involved in nuclear exosome complex assembly; FAU (0.651), a ubiquitin-like protein; RBM4 (0.623) and RBM10 (0.593), related RNA-binding motif proteins regulating alternative splicing; RSRC1 (0.616), an arginine/serine-rich splicing factor; MTREX (0.611), a nuclear exosome targeting factor; TRA2B (0.599), a transformer-2 protein homolog; KHDRBS1 (0.592), a KH domain splicing regulator; RBM25 (0.590) and RBM7 (0.539), additional splicing factors. HPA expression shows striking tissue-enriched expression in epididymis (42.7 nTPM), with nucleoplasm subcellular localization, immune cell-enhanced expression in blood cells, and cell type-enriched specificity, suggesting specialized roles in male reproductive tract differentiation and immune cell RNA processing. ClinVar reports 27 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large chromosome 21q11.2-22.3 copy number gains/losses, though no single monogenic disease is yet definitively assigned to RBM11 point mutations, suggesting dosage sensitivity in development and cancer. Literature (only 19 papers) reveals emerging functional characterization: (1) Bladder cancer—RBM11 drives malignant progression by regulating the GNPDA1-PKM2 metabolic axis, linking splicing regulation to cancer metabolism; (2) Ovarian cancer—RBM11 serves as a prognostic biomarker promoting tumor progression; (3) Tissue-specific splicing—RBM11 is a novel splicing regulator with tissue-restricted expression patterns; (4) Apoptotic cell-derived extracellular vesicles—RBM11 transfer via extracellular vesicles promotes glioblastoma malignancy through intercellular splicing factor exchange. Functional hypothesis: RBM11 functions through: (1) Alternative Splicing Regulation—RRM domain binds specific RNA sequences to regulate exon inclusion/exclusion in tissue-specific manner; (2) Epididymal Differentiation—high epididymal expression suggests roles in sperm maturation and male fertility via splicing of reproductive genes; (3) Cancer Metabolism—GNPDA1-PKM2 axis regulation links splicing to glycolytic reprogramming in bladder and ovarian cancers; (4) Nuclear Exosome Targeting—classification within nuclear exosome complex suggests roles in RNA surveillance and degradation of aberrant transcripts; (5) Therapeutic Target—splicing factor dependency in cancers makes RBM11 a potential target for splicing-modulating therapies. The combination of conserved RRM domain, epididymis-specific expression, extensive pathogenic CNV burden, and minimal literature (confirming genuine dark gene status despite functional importance) positions RBM11 as a high-priority target for male reproductive disorders and cancer therapeutics.
