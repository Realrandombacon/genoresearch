## Description
TMC5 (Q6UXY8) encodes a 1006 amino acid transmembrane channel-like protein 5 belonging to the TMC family (PTHR23302, positions 184-978) that functions as a calcium-permeable ion channel potentially coupling membrane mechanics to cellular signaling in gastrointestinal tissues and cancer progression. InterPro analysis reveals a conserved channel architecture: a TMC domain (IPR012496/PF07810, positions 724-830) forming the putative pore-forming region characteristic of transmembrane channel-like proteins; and a transmembrane channel-like protein family domain (IPR038900, positions 184-978) containing multiple predicted transmembrane helices enabling membrane integration and ion conductance. AlphaFold predicts a partially structured protein (pLDDT 62.9, medium confidence) consistent with multi-pass transmembrane topology where transmembrane helices achieve confident folding while cytoplasmic loops exhibit conformational flexibility required for channel gating and regulatory protein interactions. STRING interactions demonstrate partnerships with membrane proteins and signaling regulators: KCNH8 (0.508), potassium voltage-gated channel subfamily H member 8 suggesting coordinated ion flux regulation; TMEM221 (0.473) and TMEM211 (0.424), transmembrane proteins 221 and 211 of unknown function potentially forming channel complexes; ADAMTS12 (0.472), a disintegrin and metalloproteinase with thrombospondin motifs 12 involved in extracellular matrix remodeling; CMC1 (0.454), coiled-coil motif containing 1; RNF175 (0.435), ring finger protein 175 E3 ubiquitin ligase potentially regulating TMC5 turnover; CCP110 (0.434), centriolar coiled-coil protein 110 involved in ciliogenesis; ANKUB1 (0.429), ankyrin repeat and ubiquitin domain containing 1; LRRC71 (0.423), leucine rich repeat containing 71; WSCD1 (0.422), WSC domain containing 1 involved in cell surface sensing. HPA shows tissue-enhanced expression with intestine as top expressing tissue (50.3 nTPM) followed by stomach (43.5 nTPM), detected in many tissues, dual nucleoplasm and plasma membrane localization consistent with channel trafficking between compartments, classified under predicted membrane proteins and transporters biological category. ClinVar contains 18 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number gains spanning chromosome 16p regions (chr16:15368750-28342902 duplications, chr16:4380767-30445350 amplifications, chr16:2959279-30190593 gains, chr16:5805001-34230001 duplications, chr16:18238275-30177240 gains, chr16:85880-22442007 duplications, chr16:85881-90155062 amplifications) and splice site variants (c.2932-22T>G, c.2931+5G>A affecting intron-exon boundaries near the TMC domain), though specific monogenic disease associations remain undefined in ClinVar database. Literature (35 papers) demonstrates TMC5 functions as an oncogene in multiple cancer types: RBM15 promotes colon adenocarcinoma progression through m6A modification of TMC5 mRNA enhancing its stability and translation; TMC5 promotes lung adenocarcinoma cell proliferation, migration and invasion by regulating epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT); TMC5 serves as a marker of tumor-associated telocytes in hepatocellular carcinoma correlating with poor patient outcomes; comprehensive bioinformatics analysis shows TMC5 highly expressed in human cancers correlating with prognosis and immune cell infiltration patterns; emerging evidence suggests TMC5-CIB complex couples membrane mechanics to calcium influx during sperm development. The mechanistic hypothesis: TMC5 functions as a calcium-permeable mechanosensitive ion channel where the TMC domain (positions 724-830) forms the ion conduction pore while surrounding transmembrane helices (positions 184-978) create the membrane-embedded scaffold enabling force transmission from membrane tension to channel gating; medium-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 62.9) indicates flexible cytoplasmic domains allowing conformational changes during mechanotransduction; intestinal enrichment (50.3 nTPM) reflects specialized role in gut epithelial mechanosensation detecting luminal distension and peristaltic forces triggering calcium signaling cascades regulating secretion, absorption, and barrier function; nucleoplasm localization suggests potential non-canonical nuclear functions possibly related to transcriptional regulation or RNA processing; oncogenic activity in colon, lung, and liver cancers operates through EMT promotion where TMC5-mediated calcium influx activates calcineurin-NFAT or CaMKII-CREB pathways inducing transcriptional programs driving mesenchymal transition, invasion, and metastasis; RBM15-mediated m6A modification enhances TMC5 mRNA stability creating positive feedback amplifying oncogenic signaling; interaction with KCNH8 (score 0.508) suggests coordinated potassium-calcium flux regulation affecting membrane potential and excitability; ADAMTS12 partnership indicates TMC5 may coordinate ion signaling with extracellular matrix remodeling during cancer invasion; copy number gains on 16p cause TMC5 overexpression hyperactivating calcium signaling promoting uncontrolled proliferation and metastatic behavior; centrosomal protein CCP110 interaction hints at potential ciliary localization where TMC5 could function in primary cilium mechanosensation analogous to TMC1/TMC2 in hair cell stereocilia.
