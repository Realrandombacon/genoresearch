## Description
FAM90A9 encodes a 464 amino acid primate-specific protein (UniProt A6NNJ1) belonging to the FAM90A gene family that originated from multiple duplications and rearrangements on chromosome 8p23.1. InterPro analysis identifies the protein within the FAM90 family (IPR039213, positions 2-462) and reveals a CCHC-type zinc knuckle domain (IPR041670, PF15288, positions 41-82), a compact zinc-binding motif typically involved in single-stranded nucleic acid recognition.

HPA subcellular localization places FAM90A9 in the nucleoplasm and nucleoli, consistent with roles in ribosome biogenesis or nucleolar stress response. However, RNA expression is reported as "not detected" across tissues and cancer samples, suggesting highly restricted or condition-specific expression (possibly testis-specific, stress-induced, or developmental stage-limited).

AlphaFold structure prediction yields a low global pLDDT score of 49.6, indicating substantial intrinsic disorder. This disordered architecture is characteristic of nucleolar proteins that function through multivalent protein-protein or protein-RNA interactions rather than structured enzymatic activity.

ClinVar contains 38 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly copy number losses and gains spanning 8p23.1, implicating FAM90A9 dosage sensitivity in disease. BLAST analysis confirms primate-specificity with no significant orthologs in non-primate species.

HYPOTHESIS: FAM90A9 functions as a primate-specific nucleolar scaffold protein that uses its zinc knuckle domain to bind nascent ribosomal RNA or nucleolar non-coding RNAs during ribosome assembly. The intrinsically disordered regions enable multivalent interactions with nucleolar proteins (fibrillarin, nucleophosmin, nucleolin), potentially modulating ribosome biogenesis rates in response to cellular stress. Copy number variants disrupt this regulatory function, contributing to 8p23.1 microdeletion/duplication syndrome phenotypes through altered nucleolar function and proteotoxic stress.
