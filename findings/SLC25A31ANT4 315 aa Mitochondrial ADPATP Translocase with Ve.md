## Description
SLC25A31 (Q9H0C2, 315 aa), also known as ANT4 (Adenine Nucleotide Translocase 4), is a specialized member of the mitochondrial carrier family (SLC25) responsible for exchanging cytosolic ADP for matrix ATP. InterPro identifies a canonical triple-repeat architecture characteristic of mitochondrial carriers: three homologous domains forming the transport pore (IPR018108, PF00153, PS50920, aa 18-113, 123-216, 219-309), belonging to the eukaryotic ADP/ATP carrier family (IPR002113, aa 16-307) and the broader mitochondrial carrier superfamily (IPR002067, IPR023395, G3DSA:1.50.40.10, aa 16-308). AlphaFold predicts a very high-confidence structure (pLDDT 90.2, residues 1-315), indicating an extremely stable, well-folded six-transmembrane helix bundle typical of solute carriers, with a deeply buried substrate-binding cavity. HPA shows strict testis-enriched expression (44.2 nTPM, detected in single tissue); localized to mitochondria; classified as a transporter involved in antiport, differentiation, and spermatogenesis. STRING reveals 10 extremely high-confidence interactions defining the mitochondrial permeability transition pore (mPTP) and energy exchange network: VDAC1 (0.961), VDAC2 (0.959), VDAC3 (0.955), CYCS (0.950, cytochrome c), PPID (0.948, cyclophilin D), TSPO (0.935, translocator protein), SLC25A4 (0.903, ANT1), PRKCE (0.903, PKC epsilon), SLC25A5 (0.903, ANT2), and PPIF (0.807). The perfect scores with VDACs and cyclophilins confirm its integration into the mPTP complex regulating apoptosis and metabolic flux. ClinVar contains 27 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number losses (chr4:125166237-142987037, chr4:114872547-138005267) and gains (chr4:123399154-190957473, chr4:101203509-190957473) affecting the 4q28 region, suggesting dosage sensitivity; although no specific monogenic disease is explicitly named, the testis-specific expression and anti-apoptotic phenotype strongly implicate it in male infertility and spermatogenic failure. Functional hypothesis: SLC25A31/ANT4 functions as the primary ADP/ATP exchanger in developing germ cells, specifically tailored to support the unique bioenergetic demands of spermatogenesis. Unlike ubiquitous isoforms (ANT1/2), ANT4 may possess distinct kinetic properties or regulatory mechanisms (e.g., resistance to inhibition by bongkrekic acid) optimized for the glycolytic-to-oxidative metabolic shift during sperm maturation. Its interaction with VDACs and cyclophilin D positions it as a critical gatekeeper of the mPTP, preventing premature apoptosis in meiotic cells and ensuring survival until mature sperm formation. Disruption via CNVs likely leads to azoospermia or severe oligozoospermia due to energetic collapse and increased apoptotic susceptibility in the testis. Conservation across vertebrates (mouse 320 aa, bovine 323 aa) confirms essential reproductive role. NOTE: This gene represents a 'dark' but mechanistically clear driver of male fertility, potentially underlying undiagnosed cases of non-obstructive azoospermia.
