Functional hypothesis: ZNF253 functions as a sequence-specific transcriptional repressor that binds genomic DNA through its C2H2 zinc finger array recognizing specific nucleotide motifs, then recruits the TRIM28/KAP1 co-repressor complex via its KRAB domain to establish repressive chromatin states at target loci. This places ZNF253 within the large KRAB-ZFP family (~400 human genes) involved in retrotransposon silencing, developmental gene regulation, and epigenetic control. Disease mechanism likely involves haploinsufficiency or triplosensitivity where altered ZNF253 dosage disrupts transcriptional programs, potentially affecting neurodevelopment given 19p13.11 microdeletion/duplication syndrome associations. Priority experiments include ChIP-seq to identify genomic binding sites, CRISPR knockout to determine phenotypic consequences, and motif analysis to define DNA recognition specificity.
