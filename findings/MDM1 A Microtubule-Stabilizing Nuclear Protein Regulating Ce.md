## Description
MDM1 (Mitochondrial deafness modifier 1, UniProt Q8TC05) is a 714 aa nuclear protein that functions as a microtubule-binding regulator of centriole duplication. Despite its misleading name suggesting mitochondrial involvement, MDM1 localizes to the nucleus and cytoplasm where it binds and stabilizes microtubules, negatively regulating centriole replication (GO:0046600). Structurally, AlphaFold predicts a medium-confidence fold (pLDDT=54.1) with mixed ordered/disordered regions, consistent with the PF15501 MDM1 family domain (IPR029136, aa 9-548) that mediates microtubule interactions. Functionally, STRING interactions reveal connections to sorting nexins (SNX14 0.696, SNX13 0.620, SNX25 0.614, SNX19 0.545) involved in endosomal trafficking, MDM2 (0.669) linking to p53 regulation, ACSL3 (0.570) for lipid metabolism, and PRIMPOL (0.536) for DNA damage tolerance. This interaction network suggests MDM1 coordinates microtubule dynamics with membrane trafficking and genome stability. ClinVar identifies 10 pathogenic copy number variants (deletions/duplications on chr12), though no specific disease is yet annotated, implying haploinsufficiency may cause developmental phenotypes. Literature (80 papers) reveals emerging roles in colorectal cancer chemoradiotherapy sensitivity, Th1 cell fate specification via CTCF-binding, neurodevelopmental disorders with TLK1, and retinal degeneration through intraflagellar transport defects. The gene represents an underexplored node linking cytoskeletal regulation to cell cycle control, where its microtubule-stabilizing activity likely serves as a checkpoint for proper centriole number and ciliary function.
