## Description
DIPK1C (Divergent Protein Kinase Domain 1C, also known as FAM69C) encodes a 419-amino acid intracellular kinase (UniProt Q0P6D2) representing a genuinely dark gene with only 1 publication on Semantic Scholar despite strong brain-specific expression and significant clinical variant burden. Structurally, DIPK1C contains a bipartite domain architecture: an N-terminal cysteine-rich endoplasmic reticulum domain (IPR029244/SM01299/PF14875, residues 15-175/48-166) likely mediating membrane anchoring or protein-protein interactions within the secretory pathway; and a C-terminal divergent protein kinase-like domain (IPR011009/SSF56112, residues 190-308) belonging to the FAM69 kinase family (IPR022049/PF12260, residues 187-384). Unlike canonical kinases, the FAM69 family possesses unique active site configurations suggesting altered substrate specificity or regulatory mechanisms. AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (pLDDT 86.4 across all 419 residues), indicating a stable, well-folded enzyme with rigid catalytic core and structured N-terminal domain. Functionally, Human Protein Atlas data reveals DIPK1C is 'Tissue enhanced' with striking brain specificity (36.3 nTPM in brain, 15.3 nTPM in retina), detected in some tissues but highly enriched in neuronal compartments. It shows 'Group enriched' cancer specificity and is not detected in immune cells, pointing to a specialized role in neural development or maintenance rather than general housekeeping or immunity. STRING interactions reveal a high-confidence partner: DIPK2B (score 0.724), a paralogous kinase suggesting functional redundancy or heterocomplex formation; and medium-confidence partners including IPCEF1 (0.493), CHST10 (0.472), KLHL30 (0.465), TRMU (0.434), ZNF286A (0.429), CADM2 (0.422), LONRF2 (0.416), and TBC1D8 (0.407), linking DIPK1C to cell adhesion, ubiquitination, tRNA modification, and vesicle trafficking pathways. ClinVar documents 153 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large-scale copy number gains and losses spanning chromosome 18q11.1-q23 (e.g., chr18:20966775-80255845 x3, chr18:62873887-80255845 x1, chr18:53564430-74587425 x1), implicating DIPK1C dosage sensitivity in 18q deletion/duplication syndromes characterized by developmental delay, intellectual disability, and neurological deficits. Recent literature: Only 1 paper (2019) mentions DIPK1C as part of a biomarker panel for SMAC mimetic response in pediatric B-ALL, but provides no mechanistic insight—confirming its 'dark' status. The mechanistic hypothesis: DIPK1C functions as a serine/threonine kinase localized to the endoplasmic reticulum (via its N-terminal cysteine-rich domain) where it phosphorylates specific substrates involved in neuronal protein folding, quality control, or synaptic vesicle trafficking. The interaction with DIPK2B suggests they may form homo/heterodimers or act redundantly on shared substrates. Partners like CADM2 (cell adhesion molecule) and TBC1D8 (RabGAP) hint at roles in synapse formation or membrane dynamics. The high brain expression (36.3 nTPM) and retinal expression (15.3 nTPM) align with phenotypes of 18q syndromes (neurodevelopmental delay, vision issues). The 153 ClinVar CNVs suggest haploinsufficiency (deletion) causes loss of critical kinase activity leading to neurodevelopmental defects, while duplication may cause toxic gain-of-function or dysregulated signaling. The confident AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 86.4) enables rational design of kinase inhibitors or activators to modulate DIPK1C activity for treating 18q syndrome or neurodegenerative conditions.
