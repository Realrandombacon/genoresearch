# FAM90A8: Intrinsically Disordered Nuclear Protein with Zinc Knuckle Domain Potentially Regulating Chromatin or RNA Processing

## Description
FAM90A8 (Gene ID: 348796, UniProt A6NJQ4) is a 464 amino acid protein of the FAM90 family located on chromosome 8p23.1 (NC_000008.11). InterPro domain analysis reveals membership in the FAM90 protein family (IPR039213/PTHR16035, positions 2-462) with a single CCHC-type zinc knuckle domain (IPR041670/PF15288, positions 41-82). Zinc knuckles are small zinc-binding motifs typically involved in RNA or DNA recognition, commonly found in retroviral nucleocapsid proteins and cellular RNA-binding proteins. AlphaFold structure prediction (AF-A6NJQ4-F1) yields a low-confidence model (global pLDDT 49.4), indicating FAM90A8 is predominantly intrinsically disordered with only the zinc knuckle region potentially structured. This disordered architecture is characteristic of proteins functioning through dynamic protein-protein or protein-nucleic acid interactions rather than enzymatic catalysis. Human Protein Atlas classifies FAM90A8 as a predicted intracellular protein localized to the nucleoplasm and nucleoli; notably, RNA expression was not detected across tissues, cancers, blood cells, or single-cell types, suggesting either highly restricted expression (possibly testis-specific like many FAM90 paralogs), low abundance, or post-transcriptional regulation. STRING database returned no interaction partners, consistent with poor characterization of the FAM90 family. ClinVar lists 38 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all large copy number losses or gains spanning 8p23.3-23.1 (multi-megabase regions affecting many genes including DEF6, SOX7, CSMD1); no specific monogenic disease is yet attributed solely to FAM90A8, but 8p23.1 deletion syndrome causes developmental delay, congenital heart defects, and behavioral abnormalities. The FAM90 family (A1-A19) is primate-specific and expanded through segmental duplications, with most members showing testis-enriched expression and unknown functions. Conservation is limited to primates (chimpanzee, gorilla, gibbon orthologs expected) with no clear mouse ortholog, explaining the absence of conservation data. Functional hypothesis: FAM90A8 functions as an intrinsically disordered nuclear scaffold protein that binds RNA or DNA via its zinc knuckle domain, potentially regulating chromatin organization, transcription, or RNA processing in a cell-type specific manner (possibly germ cells or neurons). The nucleolar localization hint suggests potential roles in ribosome biogenesis or rRNA processing. As a primate-specific gene, FAM90A8 may contribute to human-specific traits. Disease mechanism: While no monogenic phenotype exists, FAM90A8 haploinsufficiency within 8p23.1 deletions may contribute additively to neurodevelopmental phenotypes. The zinc knuckle disruption would impair nucleic acid binding. Priority experiments include RT-PCR across diverse tissues (especially testis), immunofluorescence for subcellular localization validation, RNA/DNA binding assays for the zinc knuckle domain, and CRISPR knockout in pluripotent stem cells to assess differentiation effects.
