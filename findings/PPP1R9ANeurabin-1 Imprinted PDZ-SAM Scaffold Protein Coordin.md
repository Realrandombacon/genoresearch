## Description
PPP1R9A (1098 aa, UniProt Q9ULJ8), also known as Neurabin-1, is a maternally imprinted scaffolding protein that bridges protein phosphatase 1 (PP1) catalytic subunits with the actin cytoskeleton to regulate dendritic spine morphogenesis and synaptic plasticity. The protein architecture features two key protein-protein interaction domains: an N-terminal PDZ domain (IPR001478/PF00595/PF17817, aa434-592) for membrane receptor anchoring and a C-terminal SAM domain (IPR001660/PF07647, aa982-1051) for oligomerization and higher-order complex assembly, connected by an extended neurabin-family specific region (IPR043446, aa1-922) containing F-actin binding motifs. HPA confirms tissue-enhanced expression with retina enrichment (17.2 nTPM), plasma membrane localization, and explicit roles in neurogenesis and differentiation pathways. Mechanistically, PPP1R9A forms the core of a PP1 holoenzyme regulatory complex through ultra-high-confidence interactions with all three PP1 catalytic subunits: PPP1CC (STRING 0.909), PPP1CA (0.769), and PPP1CB (0.689), positioning it as a master regulator of serine/threonine dephosphorylation at postsynaptic densities. Additional interactions with RPS6KB2/p70S6K (0.760) link PPP1R9A to mTOR signaling and protein synthesis control, while connections to imprinted genes SGCE/sarcoglycan-epsilon (0.626), NDN/necdin (0.621), and PEG10 (0.598) embed it within the 7q21 imprinted cluster governing fetal development. AlphaFold prediction (pLDDT 59.6) reveals a partially disordered architecture with structured PDZ and SAM domains separated by flexible linker regions, characteristic of dynamic scaffolds that undergo conformational changes during synaptic activity. Clinical significance is demonstrated by 21 pathogenic/likely pathogenic ClinVar variants including large copy number losses at 7q21.3 (chr7:94813388-96264152), deletions spanning 7q11.23-21.3, and splice site mutations (c.1528+5G>C), though no specific monogenic syndrome has been definitively established. Conservation across mammals (rat O35867 1095 aa) indicates essential function maintained over ~90 million years. Literature (40 papers) establishes PPP1R9A in neuropsychiatric disease: altered prefrontal cortical expression in schizophrenia and bipolar disorder correlates with dendritic spine loss; genomic imprinting in skeletal muscle and extra-embryonic tissues suggests parent-of-origin effects on development; polymorphisms associate with restless legs syndrome; deregulated expression in prostate cancer and head/neck squamous cell carcinoma implicates imprinted gene network disruption in tumorigenesis. The functional hypothesis posits that PPP1R9A acts as a 'synaptic phosphatase organizer': the PDZ domain anchors PPP1R9A to NMDA/AMPA receptor complexes at postsynaptic densities while F-actin binding regions tether the scaffold to cytoskeletal structures; upon synaptic activation, conformational changes expose PP1-binding sites enabling targeted dephosphorylation of synaptic substrates (e.g., DARPP-32, inhibitor-1) that control spine morphology and AMPA receptor trafficking; maternal imprinting ensures monoallelic expression in developing brain, creating dosage sensitivity where haploinsufficiency disrupts synaptic plasticity contributing to schizophrenia/bipolar pathogenesis; cancer-associated derepression of the imprinted cluster causes biallelic overexpression driving proliferation through PP1-mediated cell cycle checkpoint override.
