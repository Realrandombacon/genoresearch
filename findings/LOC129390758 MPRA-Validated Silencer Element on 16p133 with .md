## Description
LOC129390758 is a 200bp cis-regulatory element (CRE) located on chromosome 16p13.3 (GRCh38: 5176438-5176638), functionally validated as a transcriptional silencer through STARR-seq massively parallel reporter assays (MPRAs) in K562 erythroleukemia cells. This element lacks canonical promoter, enhancer, or CTCF-bound insulator chromatin signatures but demonstrates robust repressive activity against the super core promoter SCP1. As a non-coding regulatory locus, it has no UniProt accession, protein domains, or STRING interaction partners. Human Protein Atlas could not resolve this locus to an Ensembl ID, consistent with its non-coding regulatory classification.\n\nClinVar analysis reveals 6 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variants disrupting this region—a high burden with extreme duplication bias (5 duplications:1 deletion ratio). Duplications span large 16p13.3-13.11 regions (~10-29Mb) while the single deletion spans 16p13.3 (~4.3Mb). This 5:1 ratio strongly suggests triplosensitivity intolerance where silencer gain causes hyper-repression of target genes, leading to more severe or frequent phenotypes than haploinsufficiency. These recurrent CNVs are associated with 16p13.3 deletion/duplication syndromes featuring developmental delay, intellectual disability, craniofacial dysmorphism, cardiac defects, and renal anomalies. The 16p13.3 region is gene-dense, containing critical dosage-sensitive genes such as CREBBP (CREB Binding Protein, histone acetyltransferase, Rubinstein-Taybi syndrome), PKD1 (Polycystic Kidney Disease 1, calcium signaling), and TSC2 (Tuberous Sclerosis Complex 2, mTOR pathway regulation).\n\nHYPOTHESIS: LOC129390758 functions as a critical distal silencer within the 16p13.3 topologically associating domain (TAD), regulating expression of dosage-sensitive developmental and tumor suppressor genes. Candidate targets include CREBBP (chromatin remodeling, neural development) or TSC2 (mTOR inhibition, cell growth control). The silencer likely recruits co-repressor complexes (PRC2, NCoR/SMRT, HDACs) to deposit H3K27me3 marks at target promoters. The extreme duplication bias (5:1 ratio) indicates that silencer gain (duplication) causes excessive recruitment of repressors, leading to pathological underexpression of CREBBP or TSC2—phenocopying Rubinstein-Taybi syndrome or tuberous sclerosis. Conversely, silencer loss (deletion) may cause mild derepression with less severe consequences. The K562 validation suggests potential roles in hematopoietic lineage specification. 16p13.3 synteny with CREBBP suggests potential cis-regulation of this critical epigenetic modifier, contributing to 16p13.3 duplication syndrome with severe neurodevelopmental phenotypes.
