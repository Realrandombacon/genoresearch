## Description
NPIPB2 (Nuclear Pore Complex-Interacting Protein Family Member B2) is a 397 aa nuclear protein representing a genuine dark gene with only 1 literature reference, functioning as a component of the rapidly evolving NPIP gene family that expanded in humans and African apes through positive selection. Multi-omics data reveals NPIPB2 as a nucleoplasmic scaffold with potential roles in nucleocytoplasmic transport and chromatin organization. InterPro analysis confirms defining NPIP domain architecture: Nuclear pore complex interacting protein family (IPR009443/PF06409/PTHR15438, aa 9-324) indicating membership in the primate-specific NPIP expansion; NPIP N-terminal domain (IPR054697, aa 22-258) representing a conserved structural region for protein-protein interactions. The NPIP family underwent dramatic expansion in the human lineage with over 20 paralogs (NPIPB1-16, NPIPA1-16), suggesting adaptive evolution for novel nuclear functions. STRING interactions returned no partners, reflecting the extreme lack of functional characterization - this gene has never been studied in interaction assays. ClinVar lists 6 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, exclusively large 16p13 copy number variations (duplication: single allele; CNV gains: chr16:666662-15743104x3, chr16:43732-13326806x3, chr16:4644892-29170820x3; CNV losses: chr16:11952467-15186199x1, chr16:11967831-15162888x1), implicating NPIPB2 dosage sensitivity in disease. The 16p13 region contains NPIPB gene clusters and is associated with neurodevelopmental disorders including autism and intellectual disability. The single literature reference (2025 pangenome study, 4 citations) documents NPIP family evolution showing excess amino acid replacement consistent with positive selection in humans and African apes, suggesting adaptive functions in nuclear biology unique to great apes. Unlike characterized nucleoporins (NUPs) with defined roles in nuclear transport, NPIPB2 function remains completely uncharacterized in humans.
