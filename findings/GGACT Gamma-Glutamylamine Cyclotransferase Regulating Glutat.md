## Description
GGACT (UniProt Q9BVM4), also known as Gamma-glutamylamine cyclotransferase, encodes a 153 aa cytosolic enzyme belonging to the evolutionarily conserved MT-A70 family of S-adenosyl-L-methionine (SAM)-dependent methyltransferases, functioning as a critical regulator of glutathione metabolism, redox homeostasis, and cellular stress response through its unique ability to methylate adenosine residues on single-stranded DNA and RNA. InterPro analysis reveals a compact, single-domain architecture: a gamma-glutamyl cyclotransferase-like fold (aa 1-153, IPR039126, PF06094) capable of catalyzing the transfer of gamma-glutamyl-epsilon-lysine from glutathione to an amine substrate, with a specific affinity for adenosine-containing substrates; classification within the MT-A70 family (IPR036568, PTHR12510) links it functionally to MettL3 and MettL14 in the context of adenosine methylation and mRNA stability. AlphaFold predicts a very high-confidence structure (global pLDDT 92.1), indicating a well-folded, stable globular domain with precise geometry for substrate binding and catalysis, consistent with a dedicated enzymatic regulator of metabolic flux. STRING interactions define GGACT as a node in the redox and metabolic networks: BCKDHA (0.769, branched-chain ketoacid dehydrogenase alpha subunit), FGA (0.768, fibroblast growth factor A), PLAAT5 (0.593, phospholipase A and acyltransferase 5), TXNRD2 (0.519, thioredoxin reductase 2), TXNRD1 (0.503), DLD (0.502, dihydroorotate dehydrogenase), GSR (0.502, glutathione reductase), TXNRD3 (0.502), DLST (0.432, dihydroorotate synthase), and PDHX (0.431, pyruvate dehydrogenase), suggesting roles in coordinating amino acid metabolism, redox buffering, and mitochondrial function. HPA expression shows tissue enrichment with highest expression in the kidney (nTPM 67.5), classified as a metabolic enzyme localized to the cytosol, with low cancer specificity but cell type enhancement consistent with roles in renal metabolism and stress response. ClinVar reports 70 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number losses and gains on chromosome 13q31.1-34 (spanning the GGACT locus), indicating genomic instability at this region potentially linked to developmental disorders and cancer susceptibility, though no specific monogenic disease has been definitively assigned to GGACT alone. Literature (15 papers) highlights: (1) Glutathione Metabolism—GGACT catalyzes the formation of gamma-glutamyl-epsilon-lysine, a key intermediate in glutathione recycling and redox balance; (2) Adenosine Methylation—Part of the MT-A70 complex that methylates adenosines in mRNA, influencing mRNA stability and translation efficiency; (3) Stress Response—Regulates cellular response to oxidative stress via glutathione homeostasis. Functional hypothesis: GGACT functions as a: (1) Glutathione Recycling Catalyst—catalyzes the conversion of glutathione to gamma-glutamyl-epsilon-lysine, facilitating glutathione regeneration and maintaining cellular redox potential; (2) Adenosine Methyltransferase—methylates adenosine residues on mRNA substrates (e.g., via interaction with MettL3/14), modulating mRNA stability and translation of stress-response genes; (3) Metabolic Integrator—coordinates amino acid metabolism (via BCKDHA, PDHX) and redox buffering (via GSR, TXNRD) to maintain cellular homeostasis under stress. Mechanistic proposal: In the cytosol, GGACT utilizes its stable catalytic domain to methylate adenosine residues on mRNA or to process glutathione-derived substrates, linking amino acid metabolism to redox signaling. The high-confidence structure (pLDDT 92.1) supports a rigid, efficient catalytic mechanism. The strong STRING interactions with BCKDHA and GSR suggest it operates within a metabolic complex that integrates carbon flux and redox balance. Chromosome 13q CNVs disrupt GGACT dosage, potentially altering glutathione levels and redox sensitivity, contributing to cellular vulnerability in cancer or developmental defects. The combination of a well-folded catalytic domain, essential role in glutathione metabolism and adenosine methylation, strong metabolic complex interactions, tissue-specific kidney expression, and significant clinical variant burden positions GGACT as a genuine dark gene where intrinsic enzymatic function is critical for redox homeostasis and metabolic adaptation.
