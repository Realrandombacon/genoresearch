## Description
PRPF40B (Pre-mRNA-processing factor 40 homolog B, also known as HYPC, hYPC, PRPF40L, YPC1) is an 871 amino acid nuclear splicing factor (UniProt Q6NWY9) with medium-confidence partially structured architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 67.8), characteristic of proteins with ordered domains interspersed with flexible linkers that function in dynamic multi-protein complexes. The protein contains multiple defining domains: two WW domains (IPR001202/PF00397/IPR036020, residues 92-125 and 134-166/98-123 and 137-166) that mediate protein-protein interactions through proline-rich motif recognition, multiple FF domains (IPR002713/PF01846/IPR036517, residues 276-330, 340-397, 410-470/278-327, 492-547, 629-679) named after the conserved phenylalanine residues that form helical bundles for protein interactions, a PRPF40A-like FF domain (PF25432, residues 554-612), and belongs to the pre-mRNA-processing factor Prp40 family (IPR039726/PTHR24286, residues 93-692). PRPF40B is located on chromosome 12q13.12 and is involved in mRNA cis splicing via spliceosome (GO:0045292).\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals a comprehensive network of spliceosome components with high to medium confidence scores: SNRPE (0.934, Small nuclear ribonucleoprotein E, core snRNP component), SNRNP70 (0.932, Small nuclear ribonucleoprotein 70 kDa, U1 snRNP component), SNRPF (0.915, Small nuclear ribonucleoprotein F, core snRNP), SNRPD1 (0.865, Small nuclear ribonucleoprotein D1, core snRNP), SF3A1 (0.839, Splicing factor 3A subunit 1, U2 snRNP component), PRPF39 (0.809, Pre-mRNA-processing factor 39, spliceosomal protein), U2AF2 (0.801, U2 small nuclear RNA auxiliary factor 2, 3' splice site recognition), CDC5L (0.700, Cell division cycle 5-like protein, spliceosome assembly), SNRPC (0.565, Small nuclear ribonucleoprotein C), and RBM25 (0.522, RNA-binding protein 25, splicing regulator). These interactions confirm PRPF40B functions within the core spliceosome machinery.\n\nHPA expression reveals PRPF40B has low tissue specificity and is detected in many tissues, consistent with a housekeeping role in pre-mRNA splicing. Subcellular localization includes nucleoplasm, nuclear bodies, aggresome, and cytosol. The protein is classified as a predicted intracellular protein involved in mRNA processing and mRNA splicing biological processes. Nuclear body localization suggests PRPF40B may concentrate in nuclear speckles or Cajal bodies where splicing factors are stored and assembled.\n\nClinVar documents 9 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including large copy number gains (12p13.33-q13.12, 12p13.33-q24.33, 12p11.21-q13.12 amplifications) and splice region variants (c.349-11C>G, c.349-16C>G). The variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity. While no specific Mendelian disease is annotated, 12q CNVs are associated with developmental delay, intellectual disability, and congenital anomalies. Given PRPF40B's role in splicing and interaction with Huntingtin and MeCP2, dosage imbalance may disrupt alternative splicing programs critical for neuronal development, potentially contributing to neurodevelopmental disorders or modifying Huntington disease and Rett syndrome phenotypes.\n\nConservation is strong across vertebrates: human (Q6NWY9, 871 aa), mouse (Q80W14, 870 aa), rat (A0A8I5ZQT3, 890 aa), indicating an ancient and essential function in pre-mRNA splicing conserved in mammals.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: PRPF40B functions as a scaffold protein within the U1 snRNP and early spliceosome assembly complex. The WW domains bind proline-rich motifs in spliceosomal proteins and transcription factors, while the FF domains form helical bundles that mediate protein-protein interactions within the spliceosome. PRPF40B may bridge U1 snRNP components (SNRPE, SNRNP70, SNRPF) with U2 snRNP factors (SF3A1, U2AF2) during spliceosome assembly. The interaction with Huntingtin suggests PRPF40B may link splicing to neuronal function, potentially explaining splicing defects in Huntington disease. MeCP2 interaction connects PRPF40B to Rett syndrome pathways, suggesting roles in activity-dependent alternative splicing in neurons. The partially structured architecture (pLDDT 67.8) allows PRPF40B to adopt multiple conformations during spliceosome assembly and catalysis. Nuclear body localization indicates PRPF40B concentrates in splicing factor storage sites. 12q CNVs cause PRPF40B dosage imbalance, disrupting spliceosome assembly and alternative splicing programs, potentially causing neurodevelopmental phenotypes through mis-splicing of neuronal transcripts. PRPF40B represents a candidate gene for splicing disorders and a potential modifier of Huntington disease and Rett syndrome.
