## Description
BLTP3A (UniProt Q6BDS2), or bridge-like lipid transfer protein family member 3A, encodes a large 1440-amino acid intracellular protein belonging to the emerging BLTP family (IPR026728/PF24917/PTHR22774, aa 1-1436). These rod-shaped proteins are hypothesized to mediate non-vesicular lipid transfer at intracellular membrane contact sites via a bridge-like mechanism, spanning opposing membranes to facilitate lipid exchange. AlphaFold predicts a medium-confidence structure (global pLDDT: 66.2), consistent with a partially ordered protein containing both structured lipid-transfer domains and flexible linker regions typical of membrane-tethering factors.\n\nSTRING interaction data is unavailable for BLTP3A, reflecting its dark gene status and limited experimental characterization. HPA data shows ubiquitous expression with immune cell enhancement and localization to nucleoplasm, nuclear membrane, and cytosol—distribution patterns compatible with roles in both nuclear signaling and cytoplasmic membrane trafficking.\n\nWith only 7 papers, BLTP3A is a genuine dark gene with breakthrough functional insights emerging in 2024-2025. Recent studies establish BLTP3A as: (1) an effector of CASM (LC3-associated endocytosis/phagocytosis), linking it to innate immune signaling and pathogen clearance; (2) associated with late endocytic pathway membranes, positioning it at the convergence of endosomal sorting and autophagy; (3) a regulator of anti-tumor immunity in high-grade serous ovarian cancer (HGSOC), where dysfunctional BLTP3A enhances T cell infiltration and tumor suppression via autophagic modulation. ClinVar lists 10 pathogenic variants including chromosome 6p CNVs and a missense variant (p.Ile1362Val), suggesting dosage sensitivity and functional constraint. We hypothesize BLTP3A functions as a membrane-tethering lipid transfer protein that bridges late endosomes with autophagosomal membranes, regulating lipid composition and CASM-dependent immune signaling—a mechanism co-opted by ovarian cancers to evade immune surveillance.
