# PTCHD3: Partially Characterized Sperm Membrane Protein with Sterol-Sensing Domain and Clinical CNV Burden

**Date:** 2026-06-29T09:51:14.883371

**Quality Score:** 7/10 (GOOD)

**Confidence:** MEDIUM

## Description
PTCHD3 encodes a 954 amino acid patched domain-containing protein located at chromosome 10p12.1, with documented localization to endoplasmic reticulum and sperm midpiece. InterPro analysis reveals canonical patched family architecture including IPR000731 (Sterol-sensing-like domain, residues 383-540), IPR003392 (Patched SSD domain, residues 392-540), PF02460, and multiple transmembrane domains (G3DSA:1.20.1640.10 at residues 337-547 and 755-953), consistent with the 12-transmembrane topology of patched-related transporters. The AlphaFold predicted structure (Q3KNS1) shows high confidence folding (pLDDT=81.6), indicating a stable multi-pass membrane protein. STRING found no interaction partners, though literature indicates PTCHD3 localizes to sperm surface in mouse, rat, and human. Mouse knockout studies demonstrate Ptchd3 is non-essential for fertility despite germ-cell specific expression. Critically, ClinVar documents 15 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants—predominantly copy number losses and gains affecting 10p12.1 (e.g., accession 4682768: chr10:27659942-29401622 deletion; accession 443260: chr10:100026-50961640 duplication)—suggesting dosage sensitivity. Recent human studies link PTCHD3 DNA methylation changes to neurobehavioral deficits from lead exposure in children, and a novel mutation was identified in basal cell nevus syndrome-associated odontogenic keratocysts. With 25 publications but limited mechanistic characterization beyond localization and association studies, PTCHD3 represents a partially characterized gene warranting investigation into its sterol-sensing function in sperm membrane dynamics and potential role as a Hedgehog pathway modulator analogous to PTCH1.
