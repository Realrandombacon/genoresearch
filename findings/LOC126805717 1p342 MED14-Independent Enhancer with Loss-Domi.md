## Description
LOC126805717 is an uncharacterized cis-regulatory element (CRE) at chromosome 1p34.2 (genomic coordinates 41006884-41008083, ~1.2kb span). This locus is NOT a protein-coding gene but a functional regulatory DNA element validated through STARR-seq massively parallel reporter assays (MPRAs). The gene_info summary identifies this as a MED14-INDEPENDENT GROUP 3 ENHANCER that was experimentally validated in HCT116 colorectal carcinoma cells. Critically, this enhancer depends on BRD2, BRD4, P300/CBP, and CDK7 cofactors but shows LIMITED OR NO DEPENDENCE on the MED14 core Mediator complex subunit. This cofactor profile distinguishes it from canonical Mediator-dependent enhancers and suggests it operates through BET-dependent chromatin remodeling mechanisms that bypass core Mediator recruitment. ClinVar contains 6 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants affecting this locus through large-scale chromosomal rearrangements. STRIKINGLY, this locus shows LOSS-DOMINANT pathogenicity: 4 copy number LOSSES (chr1:40834404-43123071x1, chr1:40462415-44668040x1, chr1:38108665-42327551x1, chr1:40693289-44514104x1), 1 copy number GAIN (chr1:24381206-41401517x3), and 1 inversion (single allele). This 4:1 loss-to-gain ratio (67% deletions) strongly suggests haploinsufficient gene regulation rather than oncogene amplification. The 1p34 region contains metabolic and developmental genes including PEX19 (peroxisomal biogenesis factor 19), ATP1A1 (sodium/potassium ATPase alpha subunit), and multiple other genes involved in cellular metabolism. No UniProt accession exists (non-coding DNA), STRING found no interactions (not a protein), HPA could not resolve (not protein-coding), and PubMed has zero publications for this specific locus. This represents a genuine dark regulatory element with clinical relevance — the MED14-independent enhancer activity combined with loss-dominant pathogenicity suggests LOC126805717 functions as a haploinsufficient enhancer controlling 1p34 metabolic gene expression, where deletion causes disease through insufficient gene dosage.
