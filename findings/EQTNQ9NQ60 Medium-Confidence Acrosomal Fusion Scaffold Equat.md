## Description
EQTN (Equatorin, also known as SPACA8, AFAF, C9orf11) is a 294 amino acid membrane protein (UniProt Q9NQ60) predicted to function in acrosomal vesicle exocytosis and sperm-egg plasma membrane fusion. The protein belongs to the Equatorin family (IPR029282, residues 1-292), characterized by an Acrosome formation-associated factor domain (PF15339, residues 53-250), and is conserved across vertebrates (Human, Mouse, Rat).\n\nStructural analysis via AlphaFold (pLDDT 58.9) suggests EQTN is partially structured, likely containing ordered domains coupled with intrinsically disordered regions (IDRs), which is common for proteins involved in dynamic membrane remodeling and protein-protein interaction networks. This disordered nature may facilitate flexible scaffolding at the plasma membrane.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals a network centered on cell cycle and fusion machinery: strongest interactions with PCNA (0.658, Polymerase gamma catalytic subunit A, DNA repair/replication), PAN3 (0.574, Pan-1, DNA repair), MOB3B (0.554, Mitotic cell cycle protein), FAM170B (0.496, Endosomal sorting complex), APMAP (0.485, Acrosome formation), IFNK (0.471, IFN-kappa), TEX101 (0.464, Testis expressed 101), IZUMO1 (0.462, Sperm-egg fusion protein), and SPAM1 (0.454, Sperm-associated membrane protein 1), EXOSC10 (0.435, Exosome complex). The presence of PCNA, PAN3, and MOB3B links EQTN to DNA repair and cell cycle progression, while IZUMO1 and APMAP directly connect it to fertilization. This suggests EQTN acts as a bridge between the acrosome reaction, DNA integrity, and membrane fusion.\n\nHPA expression data indicates EQTN is tissue-enriched in the testis (35.3 nTPM), detected in a single cell type (likely spermatogenic cells), with no cancer specificity. Subcellular localization is predicted at the plasma membrane and actin filaments, consistent with its role in sperm-egg fusion.\n\nClinVar documents 73 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains on chromosome 9p (9p24.3-q21.13 duplications). The high variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity. While no specific Mendelian disease is annotated, 9p duplications are associated with developmental delay, intellectual disability, and autism spectrum disorders. Given EQTN's role in fertilization and potential links to DNA repair (PCNA, PAN3), dosage imbalance may disrupt spermatogenesis, cause infertility, or contribute to developmental phenotypes via impaired cell cycle regulation or membrane dynamics.\n\nConservation is strong across mammals (Human, Mouse, Rat), indicating essential function. GO terms include endocytosis, fusion of sperm to egg plasma membrane, and acrosomal vesicle exocytosis.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: EQTN functions as a scaffold at the sperm plasma membrane, coordinating the acrosome reaction with DNA repair machinery (PCNA, PAN3) and fusion proteins (IZUMO1). Its IDR-rich nature allows dynamic assembly during fertilization. Dysregulation via 9p CNVs disrupts this coordination, leading to defective sperm-egg fusion, DNA damage accumulation, and potential developmental consequences.
