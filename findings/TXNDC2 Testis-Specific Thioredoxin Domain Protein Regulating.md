## Description
TXNDC2 encodes a 553 aa thioredoxin domain-containing protein (UniProt Q86VQ3) localized to intracellular compartments, functioning as a critical regulator of spermatogenesis, oxidative stress defense, and male fertility. InterPro analysis confirms its canonical thioredoxin architecture: Thioredoxin domain (IPR013766/PF00085, aa 429-553) with redox-active cysteine residues, thioredoxin-like superfamily fold (IPR036249/SSF52833, aa 417-551), and glutaredoxin homologous superfamily (G3DSA:3.40.30.10, aa 430-553). AlphaFold predicts medium-confidence structure (pLDDT 52.2), indicating a partially disordered protein with an ordered C-terminal thioredoxin domain connected by flexible N-terminal regions typical of redox regulatory proteins. STRING interactions reveal a specialized redox network: MSRA (0.602, methionine sulfoxide reductase A, primary partner), PRDX4 (0.562, peroxiredoxin-4), VPS53 (0.537, vacuolar protein sorting), TSSK3 (0.525, testis-specific serine kinase 3), NME9 (0.522, nucleoside diphosphate kinase), UBE2I (0.519, SUMO-conjugating enzyme), TXNRD3 (0.512, thioredoxin reductase 3), and PRDX1 (0.426). HPA expression shows extreme testis specificity (98.0 nTPM) with roles in spermatogenesis and differentiation, consistent with male fertility functions. ClinVar reports 55 pathogenic variants, predominantly large 18p11.32-11.21 copy number gains (x6) and losses (x5), suggesting dosage sensitivity; specific intronic SNVs (c.-92-19T>G) may affect transcription or splicing. Literature (12 papers) confirms emerging roles: (1) Sperm Retrieval Prediction - TXNDC2 joint molecular marker with PRM1/PRM2 accurately predicts sperm retrieval success in azoospermia (7 citations); (2) Testis Pathology - TXNDC2 deficiency associated with azoospermia phenotype and testicular pathology; (3) DLBCL - testis-specific thioredoxins (TXNDC2/3/6) expressed in testicular and systemic diffuse large B-cell lymphoma correlating with clinical presentation (3 citations); (4) Oxidative Stress - 8-OHdG, Trx-1, Prx6 connected to TXNDC2/3/6 expression in seminoma; (5) Age-Related Oxidative Damage - functional deletion of Txndc2/Txndc3 increases spermatozoa susceptibility to age-related oxidative stress. Functional hypothesis: TXNDC2 functions through: (1) Redox Regulation - thioredoxin domain reducing disulfide bonds in client proteins to maintain redox homeostasis in developing sperm; (2) MSRA/PRDX4 Cooperation - coordinating methionine repair and peroxide detoxification pathways; (3) Spermatogenesis - essential for sperm maturation and motility via oxidative stress protection; (4) TSSK3 Interaction - testis-specific kinase signaling for sperm differentiation. Disease implications include Male Infertility, Azoospermia, Testicular Cancer (Seminoma), and DLBCL. Despite clinical variant burden and clear testis-specific function, the precise client protein repertoire and full redox signaling cascade remain partially unexplored.
