## Description
MYO16 (UniProt Q9Y6X6) encodes a massive 1858 amino acid unconventional myosin that uniquely combines a class XVI motor domain (residues 398-1146, IPR001609) with extensive N-terminal ankyrin repeats (IPR002110, residues 17-324) and a C-terminal tail functioning as a protein phosphatase 1 (PP1) regulatory subunit (alias PPP1R107). Unlike conventional myosins, MYO16's motor domain contains atypical insertions suggesting distinct mechanochemical properties or regulation. InterPro analysis confirms the presence of the P-loop NTPase fold (IPR027417) within the motor head, essential for ATP hydrolysis and actin translocation. STRING interactions reveal connections to PP1 catalytic subunits (PPP1CA, score 0.465), NYAP family proteins (NYAP1 0.486, NYAP2 0.430), and cytoskeletal organizers like ANLN (0.432) and TRIM67 (0.500), implicating MYO16 in complexes regulating actin dynamics and signal transduction. HPA data shows tissue-enhanced expression primarily in the brain (2.6 nTPM) and pituitary gland (2.4 nTPM), with subcellular localization to vesicles, consistent with a role in intracellular trafficking or membrane remodeling. ClinVar lists 74 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number losses/gains spanning 13q31-q34, suggesting that MYO16 dosage is critical for development, potentially contributing to neurodevelopmental disorders or schizophrenia susceptibility as hinted by literature. Recent studies (72 papers) highlight: (1) The MYO16-AS1 lncRNA axis drives proliferation in ovarian and bladder cancers; (2) MYO16 regulates cell cycle progression via PP1 targeting; (3) Potential involvement in neuronal morphogenesis. AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 62.3) predicts a medium-confidence model with structured motor and ankyrin domains but significant flexibility in the long C-terminal tail, typical for scaffold/regulatory proteins. FUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: MYO16 acts as a dual-function molecule: its motor domain generates force on actin filaments to transport vesicles or remodel the cortex during cell division/migration, while its C-terminus recruits PP1 to specific substrates (e.g., cyclins, cytoskeletal regulators) to control dephosphorylation events timing cell cycle entry or neurite extension. MECHANISTIC PROPOSAL: Upon activation, MYO16 binds actin via its motor domain to position the holoenzyme at specific cortical sites or vesicles; the ankyrin repeats dock cargo or signaling partners, while the C-tail binds PPP1CA, locally suppressing phosphorylation of targets like Rb or cofilin to promote G1/S transition or actin polymerization.
