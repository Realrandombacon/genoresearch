# RFT1: Essential ER Membrane Flippase for N-Glycosylation Precursor Translocation

**Date:** 2026-04-24T10:32:05.302222

**Confidence:** HIGH

## Description
RFT1 (RFT1 homolog) is a 541-amino acid essential membrane protein located in the endoplasmic reticulum (ER). It functions as a critical 'flippase' responsible for translocating the lipid-linked oligosaccharide precursor Man(5)GlcNAc(2)-PP-dolichol from the cytosolic leaflet to the luminal leaflet of the ER membrane. This step is indispensable for the subsequent completion of N-glycosylation, a process vital for the folding and stability of ~70% of human proteins. The AlphaFold model (AF-Q96AA3-F1) predicts a highly stable multi-pass transmembrane structure with a global pLDDT of 90.4, consistent with its role as a structured transporter. InterPro analysis confirms the presence of the RFT1 family domain (IPR007594, residues 2-108) and the Pfam Rft protein family (PF04506), categorizing it within the ER multispans.\n\nFunctionally, RFT1 operates in a tight complex with early glycosylation enzymes. STRING interaction data reveals strong associations with ALG11 (score 0.954), PMM2 (0.781), and ALG1 (0.739), placing it at the heart of the dolichol-linked oligosaccharide biosynthetic pathway (GO:0006488). Disruption of RFT1 leads to Congenital Disorders of Glycosylation (CDG), specifically CDG type P, characterized by severe developmental delays, skeletal abnormalities, and neurological deficits. ClinVar lists 44 pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants, including nonsense mutations (e.g., p.Trp102Ter, p.Arg77Ter) and splice site defects, underscoring its dosage sensitivity and non-redundant function. HPA data indicates ubiquitous expression across all tissues with low tissue specificity, reflecting its housekeeping role in basic cellular physiology, though it shows enhanced expression in specific cell types. Despite its essential nature, mechanistic details of its transport cycle and regulation remain partially obscure, fitting the profile of a functionally critical but mechanistically 'dark' gene.
