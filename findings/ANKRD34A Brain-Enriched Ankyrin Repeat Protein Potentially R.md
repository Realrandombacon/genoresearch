# ANKRD34A: Brain-Enriched Ankyrin Repeat Protein Potentially Regulating Transcription and Cell Adhesion via Scaffolding

**Quality Score:** 3.4/10  (E=5.8, D=1.0) [WEAK]

## Description
ANKRD34A (Gene ID: 84277, UniProt Q69YU3) is a 496 amino acid intracellular protein located on chromosome 1q21.3 (NC_000001.11). InterPro domain analysis reveals a canonical array of ankyrin repeats (IPR002110/PF12796/SM00248) spanning residues 4-167, forming a structured solenoid domain typical for protein-protein interactions. The protein belongs to the specific ANKRD34A/B/C family (IPR042637), suggesting functional specialization within this clade. AlphaFold structure prediction (AF-Q69YU3-F1) yields a medium-confidence model (global pLDDT 58.2), consistent with a structured N-terminal ankyrin domain connected to a flexible, potentially intrinsically disordered C-terminal region, a architecture often seen in scaffolding proteins that require conformational plasticity. Human Protein Atlas classifies ANKRD34A as a predicted intracellular protein localized to the cytosol, with tissue-enhanced expression highest in the brain (17.7 nTPM); it is detected in many tissues but shows cell-type enhanced specificity at the single-cell level. STRING database identifies 10 interaction partners suggesting diverse roles: POLR3GL (score 0.584, RNA polymerase III subunit) and POLR3C (0.454) link ANKRD34A to transcriptional machinery, specifically Pol III-mediated synthesis of tRNAs and small RNAs; LIX1L (0.555, centriole-associated protein) suggests a role in centrosome/cilia biology; ADGRB2 (0.463, adhesion GPCR) implies involvement in cell adhesion or signaling; while ACP6 (0.461), MANEAL (0.453), HS3ST6 (0.449), ZNF669 (0.448), NUDT17 (0.447), and PNMA6A (0.431) hint at connections to metabolism, glycosylation, and neuronal function. ClinVar lists 137 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains and losses spanning 1q21.1-q21.3 (multi-megabase regions affecting many genes including the NBPF family); no specific monogenic disease is yet attributed solely to ANKRD34A, but dosage sensitivity of the 1q21.1 region is strongly linked to neurodevelopmental disorders, autism, schizophrenia, and macro/microcephaly. Conservation is evident across mammals (mouse Q7TST1, rat Q5BJT1, chimp A0A6D2XIM6). Functional hypothesis: ANKRD34A acts as a cytosolic scaffolding protein that bridges transcriptional regulation (via Pol III interaction) with cytoskeletal or adhesion dynamics (via LIX1L/ADGRB2). In the brain, it may regulate the production of non-coding RNAs essential for synaptic plasticity or neuronal development. The disordered C-terminus may serve as a hub for dynamic, context-dependent interactions. Disease mechanism: Haploinsufficiency or overexpression due to 1q21.1 CNVs disrupts the stoichiometry of these complexes, leading to aberrant transcription of small RNAs or defective cell adhesion/migration during neurodevelopment, contributing to the 1q21.1 deletion/duplication syndrome phenotype. Priority experiments include co-IP validation of POLR3GL/LIX1L interactions, subcellular localization studies relative to centrosomes, and CRISPR knockout in neuronal progenitors to assess effects on differentiation and ciliogenesis.
