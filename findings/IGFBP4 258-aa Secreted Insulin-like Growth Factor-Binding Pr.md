## Description
IGFBP4 (UniProt P22692), or insulin-like growth factor-binding protein 4, encodes a compact 258-amino acid secreted protein belonging to the IGFBP family (IPR000867/IPR022321/IPR022327/PTHR23139). The protein architecture features an N-terminal cysteine-rich IGFBP domain (IPR000867/IPR009030/IPR017891, aa 23-103) containing conserved disulfide bonds for IGF binding, connected via a flexible linker region to a C-terminal thyroglobulin type-1 domain (IPR000716/G3DSA:4.10.800.10, aa 171-253) with three conserved disulfide bridges stabilizing the structure. AlphaFold predicts high-confidence structure (global pLDDT: 77.2), indicating a well-folded two-domain protein with rigid cysteine-rich modules separated by a protease-sensitive linker—consistent with its role as a regulated IGF carrier subject to PAPPA cleavage.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals IGFBP4's dual integration into IGF and Wnt signaling networks: IGF1 (0.999), IGF2 (0.993), PAPPA (0.976, pregnancy-associated plasma protein-A/protease), IGFBP1 (0.972), LRP6 (0.847, Wnt co-receptor), FZD8 (0.838, frizzled receptor), PAPPA2 (0.827), PRG2 (0.819, proteoglycan 4), IGFALS (0.676, acid-labile subunit), and INS (0.664). This partner profile confirms IGFBP4's unique position as both an IGF bioavailability regulator and a direct Wnt pathway modulator independent of IGF binding.\n\nWith emerging literature on non-canonical functions, IGFBP4 qualifies as a dark gene with breakthrough mechanisms in 2020-2026. Recent studies reveal: (1) IGFBP4 inhibits Wnt/β-catenin signaling by direct binding to LRP6/FZD8 co-receptors, suppressing osteoblast differentiation and bone formation; (2) cardiac-derived IGFBP4 regulates myocardial IGF signaling during heart failure remodeling; (3) tumor-derived IGFBP4 promotes cancer progression via IGF-independent mechanisms involving integrin signaling; (4) PAPPA-mediated cleavage of IGFBP4 releases bound IGFs, creating a proteolytic switch controlling growth factor availability in pregnancy and cancer. HPA data confirms ubiquitous expression (detected in all tissues, low tissue specificity) with secretion classification as a plasma protein. ClinVar lists 9 pathogenic variants including chromosome 17q12-25.3 copy number gains/losses and one missense variant (p.Thr233Met) in the thyroglobulin domain, confirming dosage sensitivity. We hypothesize IGFBP4 functions as a molecular rheostat coordinating growth and differentiation signals: the N-terminal domain sequesters IGF1/IGF2 with high affinity, preventing receptor activation until PAPPA cleavage releases them; simultaneously, the intact C-terminal thyroglobulin domain binds LRP6/FZD8 to inhibit Wnt signaling—this dual inhibition explains why IGFBP4 overexpression suppresses both proliferation (via IGF blockade) and differentiation (via Wnt blockade); the Thr233Met variant location suggests altered Wnt co-receptor binding rather than IGF affinity changes, potentially explaining tissue-specific phenotypes in bone vs. cardiac contexts.
