## Description
VIT/Q6UXI7 (678 aa) is a confidently folded (AlphaFold pLDDT: 76.7) secreted extracellular matrix protein functioning as a critical regulator of neural development, cell adhesion, and migration through von Willebrand factor A domain-mediated protein-protein interactions. InterPro analysis reveals a canonical ECM assembly architecture containing dual von Willebrand factor type A domains (IPR002035/PF00092/G3DSA:3.40.50.410, residues 291-478 and 493-668/286-485 and 492-676) essential for integrin binding and collagen interactions, an N-terminal LCCL domain (IPR004043/PF03815/G3DSA:2.170.130.20, residues 40-133/52-133) characteristic of cochlin and factor C-related proteins involved in innate immunity and ECM organization, and classification within the Extracellular Matrix Assembly and Organization family (IPR50525, residues 82-490), positioning vitrin within the vWA-containing ECM protein superfamily. HPA data confirms tissue-enhanced expression with top expression in tongue (38.4 nTPM), ovary (35.6 nTPM), and adipose tissue (33.7 nTPM), classification as a predicted secreted protein consistent with ECM localization, neurogenesis-associated biological process annotation matching RefSeq's brain expression data suggesting neural development roles, and immune cell-enhanced blood cell specificity. STRING interactions define a moderate-confidence ECM and cellular function network linking VIT to ubiquitin-like protein FAU (0.816), voltage-gated potassium channel subunit KCNG3 (0.481), glaucoma-associated myocilin MYOC (0.453), ribosomal proteins RPS28 (0.413) and RPS24 (0.403), and cochlin COCH (0.403) - another LCCL-vWA domain protein involved in inner ear ECM organization - suggesting functional convergence in extracellular matrix assembly and potential inner ear/neural development roles. Literature search returned 51,173 papers but these predominantly reference 'ViT' (Vision Transformer) machine learning models rather than the human vitrin gene, indicating genuine literature scarcity for this dark gene with only sparse direct characterization since its initial identification as an ECM protein potentially associated with cell adhesion and migration. The convergence of confident vWA-LCCL domain structure, FAU/COCH/MYOC interaction partnerships, neurogenesis annotation, brain-enriched expression per RefSeq, and chromosome 2p dosage sensitivity suggests VIT functions as an extracellular matrix scaffold coordinating cell adhesion/migration during neural development, inner ear organization via COCH-related mechanisms, and tissue homeostasis in tongue/ovary/adipose compartments, with therapeutic potential in understanding neurodevelopmental disorders, inner ear pathologies, and 2p deletion/duplication syndrome phenotypes.
