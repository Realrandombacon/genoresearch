# RAB27A-Binding Effector with Dual C2 Domains Regulating Exocytosis and Cancer Metastasis

## Description
SYTL2 (Synaptotagmin-like 2, also known as Slp2-a) is a 934 amino acid effector protein that bridges small GTPases to the exocytic machinery. InterPro analysis reveals a modular architecture: an N-terminal Rab-binding domain (RBD, IPR010911, residues 1-57) specific for GTP-bound RAB27A/B, followed by a central FYVE-type zinc finger (IPR041282, PF02318, residues 6-51) potentially involved in phosphoinositide binding, and two C-terminal C2 domains (IPR000008, PF00168) at residues 628-755 (C2A) and 768-897 (C2B). The C2B domain is part of the synaptotagmin-like specific superfamily (IPR043567). AlphaFold structure (AF-Q9HCH5-F1) predicts a medium-confidence fold (global pLDDT 57.7), reflecting the structured RBD and C2 domains connected by a long, likely disordered linker region typical of tethering factors. HPA data shows ubiquitous expression with enrichment in stomach (118.9 nTPM) and intestine (68.5 nTPM), localizing to the Golgi apparatus, plasma membrane, and nucleoplasm. STRING interactions confirm its role as a dedicated RAB27A effector (score 0.998), with strong links to RAB27B (0.882), the motor adapter MYO5A (0.784), and the melanophilin MLPH (0.581), placing it in the same functional network as melanosome and granule transport. Additional partners include syntaxins STX3 (0.658) and STXBP2 (0.648), suggesting direct coupling to SNARE-mediated fusion. ClinVar lists 27 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, primarily large copy number losses on chromosome 11q, though specific monogenic phenotypes remain undefined. With only 41 publications, SYTL2 is undercharacterized relative to its paralogs. Mechanistically, SYTL2 likely acts as a molecular clamp that captures RAB27A-positive secretory vesicles via its RBD and tethers them to the plasma membrane via C2 domain-phospholipid interactions, facilitating metastasis in prostate cancer by enhancing FSCN1-mediated invadopodia formation.
