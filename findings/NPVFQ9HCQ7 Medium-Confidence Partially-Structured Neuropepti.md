## Description
NPVF (Neuropeptide VF precursor, also known as RFRP, C7orf9, Pro-FMRFamide-related neuropeptide VF) is a 196 amino acid secreted protein (UniProt Q9HCQ7) with medium-confidence partially-structured architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 58.5), characteristic of neuropeptide precursors that undergo proteolytic processing to generate bioactive RFamide peptides involved in G protein-coupled receptor signaling and neuroendocrine regulation. The protein belongs to the FMRFamide-related peptide/Growth hormone-releasing peptide family (IPR026297, residues 1-196; PTHR14403, residues 1-196) and is located on chromosome 7p15.3. NPVF is predicted to enable neuropeptide activity, involved in negative regulation of gonadotropin secretion and neuropeptide signaling pathway (GO:0007218), located in extracellular space. The protein is classified as a predicted secreted protein with neuropeptide molecular function.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals an exceptionally high-confidence network of neuropeptide receptors and mitochondrial proteins: UQCRFS1 (0.989, Ubiquinol-cytochrome c reductase Rieske iron-sulfur polypeptide 1, mitochondrial complex III), NPFFR1 (0.982, Neuropeptide FF receptor 1, primary GPCR target), CYC1 (0.967, Cytochrome c1, mitochondrial complex III), MT-CO1 (0.960, Cytochrome c oxidase subunit 1, mitochondrial complex IV), MT-CO3 (0.935, Cytochrome c oxidase subunit 3, mitochondrial complex IV), NPFFR2 (0.907, Neuropeptide FF receptor 2, primary GPCR target), TUFM (0.879, Elongation factor Tu mitochondrial), UQCRB (0.877, Ubiquinol-cytochrome c reductase binding protein, complex III), KISS1R (0.864, Kisspeptin receptor, reproductive axis GPCR), and KISS1 (0.852, Kisspeptin, reproductive neuropeptide). These interactions confirm NPVF functions within the RFamide neuropeptide signaling network, with NPFFR1 and NPFFR2 as near-maximal confidence partners (0.982/0.907) reflecting direct ligand-receptor binding. KISS1 and KISS1R interactions (0.852/0.864) link NPVF to the kisspeptin system for coordinated regulation of the hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal (HPG) axis. The unexpected high-confidence interactions with mitochondrial respiratory chain components (UQCRFS1, CYC1, MT-CO1, MT-CO3, UQCRB, TUFM; 0.989-0.877) suggest potential metabolic coupling or co-expression in high-energy neuronal populations, though may reflect network artifacts.\n\nHPA expression reveals NPVF has tissue-enriched expression with highest levels in retina (204.2 nTPM), indicating specialized functions in retinal neuropeptide signaling or photoreceptor regulation. This extreme retina restriction (204.2 nTPM) suggests roles in visual processing or retinal neuroendocrine function. Not detected in cancer indicates restricted expression to normal tissues. Not detected in immune cells suggests restricted expression in neuronal lineages. Cell type enriched single cell specificity indicates specialized functions in specific neuronal or retinal cell populations. Subcellular localization to extracellular space confirms its role as a secreted neuropeptide. The protein is classified as a predicted secreted protein.\n\nClinVar documents 31 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including extensive copy number gains (7p22.3-14.3 amplifications 0.016-29.9 Mb, 7p21.2-15.2 amplifications 16.4-27.5 Mb, 7p21.3-14.2 amplifications 10.7-35.3 Mb, 7p22.3-q36.3 whole chromosome arm amplifications 0.01-159.1 Mb) and losses (7p21.1-15.3 deletions 18.1-25.4 Mb, 7p21.1-14.3 deletions 17.7-30.7 Mb, 7p21.3-15.2 deletions 13.1-27.5 Mb, 7p15.3-14.3 deletions 23.9-33.1 Mb), plus large deletions (7p15.3-14.3 23.2-30.7 Mb) and complex variants. The variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity with both haploinsufficiency and overexpression effects. While no specific Mendelian disease is annotated, 7p CNVs are associated with developmental delay, neurological disorders, growth abnormalities, and intellectual disability. Given NPVF's role in negative regulation of gonadotropin secretion, loss-of-function may cause disinhibition of the HPG axis leading to precocious puberty or reproductive disorders, while overexpression may cause hypogonadism or delayed puberty.\n\nConservation is evident across mammals: human (Q9HCQ7, 196 aa), Mus musculus (Q9ESQ8, 188 aa), Rattus norvegicus (Q9ESQ9, 203 aa), Bos taurus (Q9GM96, 196 aa), Ovis aries (B2KKR4, 196 aa), indicating the RFamide neuropeptide architecture is highly conserved across mammals with variable protein length, suggesting strong purifying selection on neuropeptide processing and receptor binding.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: NPVF functions as a neuropeptide precursor that is proteolytically processed to generate bioactive RFamide peptides (RFRP-1, RFRP-3) that signal through NPFFR1 and NPFFR2 G protein-coupled receptors to inhibit gonadotropin secretion and regulate the reproductive axis. The medium-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 58.5) indicates a partially structured precursor with intrinsically disordered regions typical of neuropeptide precursors, containing conserved RFamide motifs (-Arg-Phe-NH2) at the C-terminus of processed peptides for receptor binding. Through NPFFR1 and NPFFR2 interactions (0.982/0.907), NPVF-derived peptides directly activate these receptors to inhibit GnRH neuronal activity. KISS1 and KISS1R interactions (0.852/0.864) link NPVF to the kisspeptin system for coordinated control of puberty and fertility, potentially acting as an antagonistic regulator to kisspeptin's stimulatory effects. Retina enrichment (204.2 nTPM) suggests additional roles in retinal neuropeptide signaling, possibly modulating visual processing or circadian rhythms. 7p15.3 CNVs cause NPVF haploinsufficiency or overexpression, potentially disrupting reproductive axis regulation and causing precocious puberty, hypogonadism, or neurodevelopmental phenotypes. NPVF represents a candidate gene for 7p deletion/duplication syndrome reproductive phenotypes and may serve as a therapeutic target for reproductive disorders.
