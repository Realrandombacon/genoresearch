# FAXDC2: Sterol Biosynthesis Regulator with Fatty Acid Hydroxylase Domain

## Description
FAXDC2 (fatty acid hydroxylase domain containing 2) encodes a 333 amino acid cytoplasmic protein (UniProt Q96IV6) with a conserved fatty acid hydroxylase domain (IPR006694, PF04116, residues 176-299) and sterol desaturase-related family signature (IPR050307, residues 58-304). The protein shows very high structural confidence in AlphaFold prediction (pLDDT 92.2), indicating a well-folded enzymatic structure.

STRING interaction analysis reveals FAXDC2 functions within the cholesterol/sterol biosynthesis pathway, with high-confidence interactions to CYP51A1 (0.972), SQLE (0.949), FDFT1 (0.885), LSS (0.876), SC5D (0.869), and NSDHL (0.790) — all canonical enzymes in sterol metabolism. This interaction network strongly suggests FAXDC2 acts as a regulatory or auxiliary factor in sterol biosynthesis, potentially modulating C-4 methylsterol oxidase activity as predicted by Gene Ontology.

HPA expression data shows FAXDC2 is tissue-enhanced with ubiquitous detection across all tissues, with peak expression in parathyroid gland (286.7 nTPM). This expression pattern suggests housekeeping metabolic function with potential tissue-specific regulation. ClinVar contains 11 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly copy number gains and losses on chromosome 5q33.1-35.2, indicating genomic dosage sensitivity and clinical relevance.

The combination of fatty acid hydroxylase domain architecture, sterol pathway protein interactions, and high structural confidence supports a mechanistic hypothesis: FAXDC2 functions as a sterol biosynthesis regulator that may catalyze oxidative modifications of sterol intermediates or serve as a scaffold protein recruiting pathway enzymes into functional complexes. Its parathyroid-predominant expression may link sterol metabolism to calcium homeostasis regulation.
