## Description
USP34 (Ubiquitin Specific Peptidase 34) is a partially characterized dark gene with 113 publications, representing one of the largest deubiquitinating enzymes (DUBs) in the human proteome and a critical regulator of protein stability in cancer and development. UniProt accession Q70CQ2 identifies it as a massive 3546-amino acid intracellular hydrolase. InterPro scan reveals a complex domain architecture: a central catalytic USP domain (Peptidase C19, IPR001394/PF00443, residues 1894-2236) containing the essential cysteine/histidine/asparagine catalytic triad for cleaving ubiquitin chains, flanked by extensive Armadillo (ARM) repeat regions (IPR016024, residues 2550-3321) that likely serve as a scaffold for protein-protein interactions and substrate recognition. Additional domains include a DUF3517 region (IPR021905, 2359-2464) of unknown function but conserved in USPs, and multiple N-terminal ARM-like repeats (IPR056850) suggesting a role in assembling large multiprotein complexes. This architecture classifies USP34 as a 'mega-DUB' capable of processing specific polyubiquitin substrates within signaling hubs.\n\nAlphaFold does not have a predicted structure for the full-length protein, likely due to its extreme size (>3500 aa) and potential flexibility, which exceeds standard modeling limits. (4) Disease mechanism—29 pathogenic variants likely disrupt catalytic activity or substrate binding, leading to unstable oncoproteins (if loss-of-function in cancer context) or accumulation of toxic aggregates (if gain-of-function or haploinsufficiency in neurodevelopment); (5) Structural modularity—the lack of a full AlphaFold model hints at a flexible, multi-domain structure that undergoes conformational changes upon substrate binding; (6) Therapeutic potential—small molecule inhibitors targeting the USP34 catalytic pocket could destabilize Pin1/c-Myc in glioblastoma and HCC;
