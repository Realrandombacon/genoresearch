## Description
TTC6 (tetratricopeptide repeat domain 6, aliases C14orf25, NCRNA00291, Gene ID: 319089) is a 520-amino acid protein encoded on chromosome 14q21.1 (37595628-37842716) that represents a dark gene with only 13 publications, including a pivotal 2023 study (2 citations) directly demonstrating its role in stabilizing the sperm flagellum annulus to ensure rapid and directed sperm motion. This recent functional characterization confirms TTC6 as a critical but understudied component of male fertility machinery. UniProt entry Q86TZ1 exists for the 520-aa human protein. InterPro scan reveals extensive tetratricopeptide repeat (TPR) architecture: IPR019734 (TPR repeat, positions 57-90, 101-138, 139-172, 383-415, 450-483), PF00515 (TPR, multiple positions), PF13181/PF13432 (TPR variants), and IPR011990 (TPR-like helical superfamily, positions 54-519, covering 90% of the protein). Notably, it also contains IPR050498/PTHR44858 (Photosystem I assembly protein Ycf3 homology, positions 101-518), suggesting evolutionary conservation of TPR-mediated protein complex assembly mechanisms from bacteria to humans. The TPR domains form stacked helix-turn-helix motifs that create scaffolds for protein-protein interactions, essential for assembling multi-protein complexes like the flagellar annulus. AlphaFold structure prediction shows VERY HIGH confidence (global pLDDT 91.8), indicating an exceptionally well-folded protein with stable TPR solenoid structures ideal for scaffolding functions. STRING database returned no interactions, likely reflecting the gene's dark status and specialized localization in sperm flagella not captured in standard interaction databases. HPA expression reveals TTC6 has tissue-enhanced specificity with highest expression in prostate (6.1 nTPM) and testis (4.6 nTPM), with group-enriched cancer specificity and detection in some tissues. Subcellular localization is predicted at centrosome and basal body—critical sites for cilia and flagella nucleation. The testis/prostate enrichment combined with basal body localization strongly supports roles in spermatogenesis and ciliary function. ClinVar contains 30 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all copy number variations spanning 14q11.2-q23.1 regions, including large deletions and duplications. While no specific monogenic disease is attributed solely to TTC6, 14q deletions are associated with developmental delays, intellectual disability, and potentially male infertility. The 2023 study explicitly links TTC6 loss to defective sperm motility due to annulus instability, positioning TTC6 as a candidate gene for asthenozoospermia (reduced sperm motility) and ciliopathies. TTC6's TPR domain architecture, Ycf3 homology, centrosome/basal body localization, testis expression, and flagellar annulus function suggest it acts as: (1) a structural scaffold protein organizing the annulus—a ring-like structure separating sperm midpiece and principal piece—by bridging septin filaments and mitochondrial sheath components, (2) a chaperone facilitating assembly of axonemal or peri-axonemal complexes via TPR-mediated protein interactions, (3) a ciliopathy-associated factor where mutations disrupt basal body docking or ciliary beating in respiratory epithelia or renal tubules (given TPR roles in other ciliopathies), or (4) a prostate-specific regulator of secretory vesicle trafficking or cilia-dependent signaling. The Ycf3 homology is intriguing: Ycf3 in plants assembles Photosystem I; TTC6 may similarly assemble large macromolecular complexes in sperm flagella. The high-confidence structure suggests rigid TPR solenoids suitable for precise spatial organization of binding partners. This represents a dark gene (13 papers, 1 key functional study) with strong mechanistic predictions based on TPR/Ycf3 domain architecture, basal body localization, testis/prostate expression, annulus stabilization function, and clinical CNVs, warranting experimental validation of binding partners (septins, mitochondrial proteins), structural analysis of annulus defects in TTC6-knockout models, and screening of asthenozoospermia patients for TTC6 mutations.
