# TESK2: Dual-Specificity Actin-Regulating Kinase Mediating Cytoskeletal Dynamics Through Cofilin Pathway Integration, Testis-Enriched Expression with Cancer Enhancement, and Uncharacterized Substrate Specificity in Epi-cblC Metabolic Disorder

**Quality Score:** 9/10 (EXCELLENT)

**Confidence:** MEDIUM

## Description
TESK2 (Testis Associated Actin Remodelling Kinase 2, also known as Dual specificity testis-specific protein kinase 2, UniProt Q96S53) is a 571-amino acid serine/threonine/tyrosine dual-specificity protein kinase belonging to the LIMK/TESK family of actin-regulating kinases. The protein architecture features a conserved N-terminal catalytic kinase domain (IPR000719/PF00069 aa 58-313) with ATP binding site (IPR017441 aa 64-87) and active site motif (IPR008266 aa 172-184), serine-threonine/tyrosine-protein kinase catalytic domain (IPR001245 aa 62-308), complete Actin-regulating Serine/Threonine Kinase family membership (IPR050940 aa 1-517), and Protein kinase-like domain superfamily (IPR011009 aa 57-318) with GO term annotations for ATP binding (GO:0005524), protein kinase activity (GO:0004672), and protein phosphorylation (GO:0006468). The AlphaFold predicted structure shows medium confidence (pLDDT 65.8), indicating a partially structured protein with an ordered kinase domain core and potentially flexible C-terminal regulatory regions—a feature consistent with kinase regulation through conformational changes and substrate accessibility modulation. STRING interaction network reveals moderate-confidence associations to cytoskeletal and signaling regulators: TEX36 (0.572, testis-expressed protein of unknown function), YWHAB (0.526, 14-3-3 beta scaffold protein mediating phospho-dependent protein interactions), ADGRL1/ADGRL2/ADGRL3 (0.514 each, adhesion G protein-coupled receptors L1-3 involved in synapse formation), SSH3 (0.491, slingshot phosphatase 3 regulating cofilin activity), PDXP (0.480, pyridoxal phosphatase), TOE1 (0.476, target of EGR1 phosphatase), SSH1 (0.455, slingshot phosphatase 1 direct cofilin regulator), and YWHAQ (0.448, 14-3-3 theta scaffold protein). HPA expression profiles demonstrate low tissue specificity with detection in all tissues but cell type-enhanced expression pattern, cancer-enhanced expression indicating upregulation in tumors suggesting potential oncogenic role, nucleoplasm subcellular localization consistent with nuclear functions in transcription-coupled cytoskeletal regulation or chromatin remodeling, classification as enzyme/plasma protein/predicted intracellular protein, and molecular functions of kinase/serine-threonine-protein kinase/transferase/tyrosine-protein kinase. ClinVar records document 7 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including intronic variant c.222+257A>G, copy number gains spanning 1p34.1-22.2 (chr1:44475302-89585894), 1p35.1-33 (chr1:33285582-47891811), and 1p36.33-q44 (chr1:849467-249224684), copy number loss at 1p34.1-32.3 (chr1:45303358-52157856), and single allele inversion, though no specific monogenic syndrome has been definitively assigned; notably, TESK2 promoter epimutations co-occur with MMACHC promoter hypermethylation in epi-cblC inherited disorder of intracellular vitamin B12 metabolism causing hematological, neurological, and cardiometabolic outcomes, suggesting TESK2 dysregulation contributes to this metabolic phenotype. Literature analysis (21 papers, confirming partially characterized status) reveals: 2022 studies identified TESK2/MMACHC promoter epimutations in epi-cblC disorder, 2013 study demonstrated phenytoin-induced TESK2 downregulation in rat testis, and 1999 original characterization identified TESK2 as novel LIMK/TESK family member predominantly expressed in testis. We hypothesize that TESK2 functions through a dual-specificity kinase mechanism integrating cofilin-mediated actin dynamics with nuclear signaling: (1) The conserved kinase domain (aa 58-313) phosphorylates both serine/threonine and tyrosine residues on substrate proteins, distinguishing TESK2 from LIMKs which primarily target serine/threonine; (2) Medium-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 65.8) suggests ordered catalytic core with flexible regulatory regions enabling substrate selection plasticity; (3) SSH1/SSH3 interactions (0.455-0.491) place TESK2 within the cofilin regulatory cycle where TESK2 likely phosphorylates cofilin at Ser3 (like LIMK1/2) to inhibit actin depolymerization while slingshot phosphatases reverse this modification; (4) YWHAB/YWHAQ 14-3-3 associations (0.448-0.526) suggest phospho-dependent scaffolding regulating TESK2 subcellular localization and substrate access through 14-3-3 binding to phosphorylated motifs; (5) ADGRL1-3 interactions (0.514) implicate TESK2 in adhesion GPCR signaling pathways potentially linking extracellular cues to cytoskeletal reorganization during cell migration and synapse formation; (6) TEX36 association (0.572, highest confidence) suggests testis-specific function potentially in spermatogenesis where dynamic actin remodeling is critical for acrosome formation and sperm maturation; (7) Cancer-enhanced expression indicates oncogenic potential through cytoskeletal dysregulation promoting invasion and metastasis similar to LIMK1/2 overexpression in tumors; (8) Nucleoplasm localization suggests non-canonical nuclear functions possibly in transcription-coupled actin polymerization or chromatin remodeling through nuclear actin regulation; (9) Epi-cblC association through promoter hypermethylation suggests TESK2 expression reduction contributes to neurological phenotypes via impaired neuronal cytoskeletal dynamics; (10) Phenytoin sensitivity indicates anticonvulsant drugs may modulate TESK2 expression potentially affecting testicular function as side effect; (11) Dual-specificity activity implies broader substrate repertoire than LIMKs potentially including novel cytoskeletal regulators beyond cofilin; (12) Clinical CNV burden without defined syndrome suggests haploinsufficiency or dosage sensitivity mechanisms affecting cytoskeletal integrity in multiple tissues. Therapeutic strategies could target TESK2 kinase activity in cancer metastasis through selective inhibitors, while antisense oligonucleotides correcting TESK2 promoter methylation might ameliorate epi-cblC neurological symptoms.
