## Description
STK38 (UniProt Q15208), also known as NDR1 (Nuclear Dbf2-Related kinase 1), encodes a 465-amino acid AGC family serine/threonine kinase containing an N-terminal Mob-binding domain (cd21782, aa 11-85) and a C-terminal catalytic kinase domain (cd05628/IPR000719, aa 87-462) with conserved AGC kinase C-terminal extension (IPR000961, aa 383-455). The active site motif (IPR008271, aa 208-220) and ATP binding site (IPR017441, aa 95-118) confirm enzymatic capacity for protein phosphorylation. AlphaFold predicts a well-folded bilobal kinase structure (pLDDT: 84.2), consistent with stable catalytic architecture.\n\nSTRING interactions define STK38's position in the Hippo/NDR pathway: strongest links to Mob co-activators MOB1A (0.974), MOB2 (0.971), and MOB1B (0.919), with connections to cytoskeletal regulators FRYL (0.874), MICAL1 (0.744), paralogue STK38L (0.776), calcium sensor S100B (0.722), and phosphatase CDC14A (0.702). HPA shows ubiquitous expression with cytosolic localization and DNA damage response annotation.\n\nWith 95 papers, STK38 is a transitional dark gene—established as a cell cycle/apoptosis regulator but with emerging 2025 mechanistic insights: (1) SCRN1-STK38-GPX4 axis conferring ferroptosis resistance in hepatocellular carcinoma; (2) autophagy-dependent MIST1-UFM1-STK38 pathway regulating YAP1 during paligenosis/regeneration; (3) oncogenic Ras-driven cell migration via MerTK activation; (4) bidirectional regulation with rabies virus M protein coordinating apoptosis. ClinVar contains 9 pathogenic variants (6p21.31 CNVs). This positions STK38 as a multifunctional kinase hub bridging cell cycle control to ferroptosis, tissue regeneration, and viral pathogenesis.
