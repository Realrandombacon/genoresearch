# LOC129390765: MPRA-Validated Silencer Element on 16p13.3 with Moderate Burden of 6 Recurrent Pathogenic CNVs

**Date:** 2026-03-20T20:27:04.108297

**Quality Score:** 2.4/10  (E=3.8, D=1.0) [WEAK]

## Description
LOC129390765 is a 200bp cis-regulatory element (CRE) located on chromosome 16p13.3 (GRCh38: 8071712-8071912), functionally validated as a transcriptional silencer through STARR-seq massively parallel reporter assays (MPRAs) in K562 erythroleukemia cells. This element lacks canonical promoter, enhancer, or CTCF-bound insulator chromatin signatures but demonstrates robust repressive activity against the super core promoter SCP1. As a non-coding regulatory locus, it has no UniProt accession, protein domains, or STRING interaction partners. Human Protein Atlas could not resolve this locus to an Ensembl ID, consistent with its non-coding regulatory classification.\n\nClinVar analysis reveals 6 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variants disrupting this region—a moderate burden among characterized silencer elements. Variants include 5 duplications (16p13.3-13.11, 16p13.3-13.12, 16p13.3-11.2, 16p13.3-13.13 spanning ~1.5-28Mb) and 1 deletion (16p13.3-13.2 spanning ~2.5Mb). These recurrent CNVs are associated with 16p13.3 deletion/duplication syndromes featuring developmental delay, intellectual disability, autism spectrum disorder, craniofacial dysmorphism, skeletal anomalies, and congenital heart defects. The 16p13.3 region is gene-dense, containing critical dosage-sensitive genes such as MYH11 (smooth muscle myosin), ABCC6 (pseudoxanthoma elasticum), and NDE1 (neurodevelopment).\n\nHYPOTHESIS: LOC129390765 functions as a critical distal silencer within the 16p13.3 topologically associating domain (TAD), regulating expression of dosage-sensitive neurodevelopmental and structural genes. Candidate targets include NDE1 (critical for neuronal migration and mitotic spindle orientation, haploinsufficiency causes microhydranencephaly), MYH11 (vascular smooth muscle function), or ABCC6 (mineralization regulator). The silencer recruits co-repressor complexes (PRC2, NCoR/SMRT, HDACs) to deposit H3K27me3 marks at target promoters. The moderate CNV burden (6 variants, skewed toward duplications 5:1) suggests duplication intolerance, where silencer gain leads to co-repressor titration or ectopic repression of developmental genes. The 16p13.3 location near NDE1 explains the neurodevelopmental phenotypes (autism, intellectual disability) in carriers. Deletions may cause derepression of targets, while duplications cause silencer-mediated silencing of adjacent genes or TAD boundary disruption. K562 erythroleukemia validation indicates potential hematopoietic regulatory roles.
