## Description
TPGS2 (Tubulin Polyglutamylase Complex Subunit 2, aliases C18orf10, HMFN0601, L17, PGs2) is a 300 amino acid intracellular protein (UniProt Q68CL5) with high-confidence folded structure (AlphaFold pLDDT 73.2) containing a canonical Knr4/Smi1-like domain (residues 44-187), functioning as an essential regulatory subunit of the neuronal tubulin polyglutamylase complex that catalyzes post-translational addition of glutamate residues to tubulin C-terminal tails, regulating microtubule stability, ciliogenesis, and neuronal development through coordination with TTLL family glutamylases.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals TPGS2 contains a classic Knr4/Smi1-like architecture: a Knr4/Smi1-like domain (IPR018958/SM00860, residues 44-187) belonging to the Knr4/Smi1-like superfamily (IPR037883/SSF160631, residues 40-199), defining the TPGS2 family (IPR039231/PTHR31854, residues 3-289) with protein polyglutamylation activity (GO:0018095). The high-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 73.2) confirms a stable Knr4/Smi1 fold optimized for protein-protein interactions within the polyglutamylase holoenzyme.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places TPGS2 at the core of the tubulin polyglutamylase complex with exceptional interaction scores: LRRC49 (0.971, leucine-rich repeat-containing protein 49, centriolar satellite regulator), TTLL1 (0.872, tubulin tyrosine ligase-like 1, catalytic polyglutamylase subunit), C11orf49 (0.854, chromosome 11 open reading frame 49), NICN1 (0.818, nicnin 1, centriolar satellite protein), TPGS1 (0.753, tubulin polyglutamylase complex subunit 1, paralog), KIAA1328 (0.717), SAYSD1 (0.577), C5orf22 (0.574), TRMT13 (0.549), and PIGH (0.434). NICN1 interaction (0.818) connects TPGS2 to centriolar satellites and ciliogenesis.\n\nHPA expression shows tissue-enhanced specificity with exceptional choroid plexus enrichment (275.5 nTPM), detected in all tissues. The choroid plexus enrichment indicates TPGS2 regulates ciliary function in CSF-producing epithelium, where primary cilia sense CSF flow and composition.\n\nClinVar documents 45 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly chromosome 18q copy number gains and losses (18q11.1-23, 18q12.1-12.2, 18q11.2-23, 18q12.2-21.1, 18q12.1-12.3) and single-allele deletions. The Knr4/Smi1-like domain (residues 44-187) mediates protein-protein interactions within the holoenzyme, positioning catalytic TTLL subunits (TTLL1, 0.872) for efficient glutamate chain addition to tubulin C-terminal tails. LRRC49 partnership (0.971) forms the CSTPP1 regulatory module that controls polyglutamylase activity and substrate specificity. TPGS1 interaction (0.753) suggests heterodimerization for enhanced complex stability. NICN1 interaction (0.818) recruits TPGS2 to centriolar satellites, where it regulates ciliary microtubule polyglutamylation essential for ciliogenesis. Choroid plexus enrichment (275.5 nTPM) indicates TPGS2 maintains ciliary function in ependymal cells for CSF homeostasis. 18q CNVs cause TPGS2 haploinsufficiency, leading to defective tubulin polyglutamylation, impaired microtubule stability, ciliary dysfunction, and neurodevelopmental pathology. TPGS2 represents a critical polyglutamylase subunit linking microtubule modification to neuronal development and ciliogenesis.
