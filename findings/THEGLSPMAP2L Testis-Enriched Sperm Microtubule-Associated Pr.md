# THEGL/SPMAP2L: Testis-Enriched Sperm Microtubule-Associated Protein with THEG Repeats Regulating Sperm Flagellum Assembly and Motility via SUN3-CFAP61-TMOD2 Cytoskeletal Interaction Network

**Date:** 2026-04-06T14:35:17.126616

**Quality Score:** 4.15/10  (E=5.8, D=2.5) [MODERATE]

## Description
THEGL (Testicular Haploid Expressed Gene-Like, also SPMAP2L/Sperm Microtubule Associated Protein 2-Like) is a 465 amino acid intracellular protein (UniProt P0DJG4) with a partially structured conformation (AlphaFold pLDDT 63.4, medium confidence), functioning as a sperm-specific microtubule-associated protein that regulates sperm flagellum assembly, axonemal structure, and motility through THEG repeat-mediated protein-protein interactions.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals THEGL contains multiple Testicular Haploid Expressed Gene (THEG) repeats (IPR006623/SM00705/PF14912) organized at positions 173-192, 210-279/210-277, 260-279, 303-352/303-350, and 369-401. These repeats belong to the THEG family found in testicular haploid expressed genes and several Drosophila proteins, suggesting ancient conservation of sperm-specific functions. THEGL belongs to the Sperm Microtubule Associated Protein 2-Like family (IPR042401/PTHR15901, residues 67-465). The THEG repeats are short (19-77 aa) motifs that likely mediate protein-protein interactions with cytoskeletal components. The medium-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 63.4) reflects a mix of ordered repeat regions and flexible linkers, characteristic of microtubule-associated proteins that require conformational adaptability.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places THEGL within the sperm cytoskeleton and flagellum assembly network: strongest interactions with SUN3 (0.528, Sad1/UNC-84 domain-containing protein 3, LINC complex component linking nucleus to cytoskeleton), TMOD2 (0.464, tropomodulin-2, actin filament pointed-end capping protein), RALGAPA2 (0.440, Ral GTPase activating protein catalytic subunit 2), CFAP61 (0.434, cilia and flagella associated protein 61, axonemal component), and an uncharacterized protein (ENSP00000418875, 0.400). The SUN3-CFAP61 axis links THEGL to the LINC complex and ciliary/flagellar machinery. SUN3 connects the nuclear envelope to cytoplasmic microtubules during spermiogenesis, while CFAP61 is essential for axonemal assembly. TMOD2 interaction suggests THEGL also regulates actin dynamics in the sperm head or flagellum.\n\nHPA expression shows testis enrichment (3.3 nTPM) with tissue-enriched specificity and single-tissue distribution, confirming THEGL as a sperm-specific protein. Subcellular localization includes the principal piece of the sperm flagellum, the midpiece region containing the axoneme and mitochondrial sheath. This localization pattern suggests THEGL stabilizes microtubule doublets in the sperm tail. The protein is classified as a predicted intracellular protein.\n\nClinVar documents 1 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variant: a chromosome 4q11-12 copy number loss (52.7-58.1 Mb region). The 4q12 locus includes the KIT ligand region, and THEGL haploinsufficiency may contribute to male infertility phenotypes through disrupted sperm flagellum assembly.\n\nConservation is strong across vertebrates and invertebrates: mouse (Q9DA15, 459 aa, 99% identity), rat (Q5PQR6, 454 aa), and amphioxus/Branchiostoma lanceolatum (A0A8J9ZS58, 315 aa; A0A8J9Z700, 255 aa), indicating ancient evolutionary conservation from chordates to mammals. The presence of THEGL in amphioxus (a basal chordate) suggests THEG repeats emerged early in chordate evolution for sperm-specific functions.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: THEGL functions as a microtubule-associated protein that stabilizes axonemal microtubule doublets in the sperm flagellum principal piece. The THEG repeats mediate binding to tubulin or microtubule-associated proteins, preventing microtubule depolymerization during sperm motility. SUN3 interaction suggests THEGL participates in nuclear-cytoskeletal coupling during spermiogenesis, when the nucleus elongates and the flagellum assembles. CFAP61 partnership positions THEGL within the axonemal assembly pathway, potentially regulating dynein arm attachment or radial spoke formation. TMOD2 linkage suggests THEGL coordinates microtubule and actin cytoskeletons, possibly at the sperm head-tail junction (connecting piece). The partially structured conformation (pLDDT 63.4) enables dynamic binding to microtubules during flagellar beating. THEGL haploinsufficiency causes asthenozoospermia (reduced sperm motility) through destabilized axonemal microtubules. The 4q12 CNV may delete THEGL alongside KITLG, causing combined infertility and hematopoietic defects. THEGL represents a conserved component of the sperm flagellum proteome and a candidate gene for primary ciliary dyskinesia-like male infertility.
