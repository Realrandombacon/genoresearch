## Description
ENTREP2 encodes Endosomal Transmembrane Epsin Interactor 2 (UniProt O60320), a 539 aa membrane-associated protein of unknown molecular function with striking brain-enriched expression and significant clinical relevance. InterPro analysis reveals a multi-domain architecture: an N-terminal CD20-like transmembrane domain (IPR007237, PF04103, aa 34-199) characteristic of tetraspanning membrane proteins involved in ion transport and membrane trafficking, membership in the Endosomal transmembrane epsin interactor family (IPR030431, PTHR17615, aa 1-539), and a conserved C-terminal domain (IPR060553, PF27697, aa 483-536) unique to the ENTREP1/2/3 paralogs. AlphaFold predicts a medium-confidence structure (pLDDT 59.0), indicating a partially folded protein with substantial intrinsically disordered regions—consistent with proteins that function through dynamic protein-protein interactions rather than rigid enzymatic catalysis. HPA confirms brain-enriched expression (23.2 nTPM in brain, tissue-enriched classification) with subcellular localization to nucleoplasm, vesicles, and aggresomes, suggesting roles in endosomal trafficking and protein quality control. The cancer-enriched RNA pattern implies potential dysregulation in neoplastic contexts. ClinVar reports 65 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all large copy number gains/losses spanning 15q11.2-13.2/13.3/14, encompassing ENTREP2 within the Prader-Willi/Angelman syndrome critical region—though specific phenotypes attributable to ENTREP2 alone remain undefined. Literature is extremely sparse (only 2 papers), with one reporting a familial 15q13.1q13.2 microdeletion encompassing APBA2, ENTREP2, NSMCE3, and TJP1 without apparent phenotypic abnormality across three generations, suggesting potential redundancy or incomplete penetrance. Functional hypothesis: ENTREP2 functions through: (1) Endosomal Trafficking Regulation—the CD20-like transmembrane domain and epsin-interactor classification suggest it anchors to endosomal membranes, potentially recruiting epsin family adaptors (EPN1/2/3) for clathrin-mediated vesicle formation; (2) Neuronal Protein Homeostasis—brain enrichment and aggresome localization imply a role in clearing misfolded proteins via the endolysosomal pathway, particularly relevant in neurodegenerative contexts; (3) Dosage-Sensitive Neurodevelopment—15q11.2 CNVs cause well-established neurodevelopmental disorders (Prader-Willi, Angelman, 15q13.3 microdeletion syndrome with epilepsy/ID/autism); ENTREP2 may contribute to these phenotypes through haploinsufficiency or triplosensitivity, potentially affecting synaptic vesicle recycling or receptor trafficking; (4) Paralogue Compensation—the existence of ENTREP1 and ENTREP3 may explain the lack of phenotype in some deletion carriers, with functional redundancy masking individual gene contributions; (5) Cancer Association—the cancer-enriched expression pattern suggests ENTREP2 may be upregulated in specific tumor types, potentially supporting enhanced endosomal trafficking required for growth factor receptor signaling (EGFR, MET) in malignancy. The combination of brain-specific expression, endosomal localization, 15q dosage sensitivity, and minimal literature establishes ENTREP2 as a genuine dark gene with high potential for neurodevelopmental disease mechanisms.
