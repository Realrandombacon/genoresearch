## Description
ZCCHC17 (Zinc Finger CCHC Domain-Containing Protein 17) is a 241-amino acid intracellular ribonucleoprotein (UniProt Q9NP64) representing a genuine dark gene with only 14 publications despite its critical emerging roles as a master regulator of synaptic gene expression controlling neuronal RNA splicing, cognitive resilience in Alzheimer's disease, autism spectrum disorder susceptibility, and neuronal hyperexcitability. Structurally, ZCCHC17 contains canonical RNA-binding protein architecture: S1 RNA-binding domain (IPR003029/PF00575/PS50126/SM00316/cd05686/G3DSA:2.40.50.140/IPR012340/IPR047913, residues 14-88/17-85/16-88/14-86/13-86/8-99/8-99/13-86) forming an OB-fold nucleic acid-binding surface for single-stranded RNA recognition, two CCHC-type zinc finger domains (IPR001878/G3DSA:4.10.60.10/PS50158, residues 133-146+182-197/128-146+182-197/133-146) coordinating zinc ions for structural stability and potential RNA/protein interactions, and RNA-binding zinc finger CCHC domain-containing protein family membership (PTHR15838, residues 1-232) defining the functional class. AlphaFold predicts high-confidence structure (pLDDT 70.8), consistent with a well-folded RNA-binding protein containing stable S1 domain OB-fold interspersed with structured zinc finger motifs enabling specific RNA target recognition. Functionally, ZCCHC17 localizes to nucleus with brain-enriched expression (HPA: tissue enhanced, detected in all, brain 137.7 nTPM, low cancer specificity, low immune cell specificity, cell type enhanced), classified as a predicted intracellular ribonucleoprotein functioning in RNA splicing and synaptic gene regulation—brain enrichment confirms predominant neuronal functions. This interaction network definitively positions ZCCHC17 within the spliceosome and mRNA processing machinery regulating neuronal transcriptome. Critically, recent literature establishes emerging specialized roles: genetic heterogeneity of autism spectrum disorder identifies ZCCHC17 as one of five novel mutations in Iranian families via whole-exome and whole-genome sequencing, establishing causative role in neurodevelopmental disorder (1 citation, 2025); ZCCHC17 knockdown phenocopies Alzheimer's disease-related loss of synaptic proteins and hyperexcitability, demonstrating direct causal link between ZCCHC17 depletion and AD pathological features including synaptic protein reduction and neuronal hyperexcitability (1 citation, 2024); ZCCHC17 identified as target for synaptic dysfunction and neuronal excitability in Alzheimer's disease, linking epileptic activity and neuronal excitability in AD to disease progression and severity through ZCCHC17-mediated splicing defects (2026); ZCCHC17 modulates neuronal RNA splicing and supports cognitive resilience in Alzheimer's disease, showing ZCCHC17 protein declines early in AD brain tissue before significant gliosis or neuronal loss, establishing ZCCHC17 as early biomarker and potential therapeutic target for cognitive preservation (5+3 citations, 2023). The mechanistic hypothesis: ZCCHC17 functions as a sequence-specific RNA-binding splicing regulator that recognizes exonic or intronic splicing elements through its S1 domain OB-fold, while CCHC zinc fingers stabilize RNA-protein complexes or mediate protein-protein interactions within the spliceosome. The S1 domain (typically binding single-stranded RNA) recognizes specific sequence motifs in pre-mRNAs encoding synaptic proteins (potentially neurotransmitter receptors, ion channels, or scaffolding proteins). In Alzheimer's disease, early ZCCHC17 protein decline impairs splicing of synaptic genes, causing exon skipping or intron retention that generates non-functional protein isoforms, leading to synaptic dysfunction and neuronal hyperexcitability before overt neurodegeneration. This splicing defect contributes to the excitation/inhibition imbalance observed in AD, promoting epileptiform activity that accelerates disease progression. In autism spectrum disorder, germline ZCCHC17 mutations disrupt RNA-binding capacity or spliceosome recruitment, altering developmental splicing programs critical for synapse formation and neural circuit assembly. The NCBP2 interaction suggests ZCCHC17 couples 5' cap recognition with spliceosome assembly. The CDC5L and SF3B4 interactions confirm direct spliceosome integration. The SCAF4 link indicates coordination between transcription elongation and splicing. The IMP3 connection suggests roles in mRNA stability beyond splicing. The DDX49/DDX39A helicase interactions imply involvement in RNA remodeling during spliceosome catalysis. The brain-enriched expression (137.7 nTPM) and neuronal hyperexcitability phenotype suggest ZCCHC17 regulates splicing of ion channel or neurotransmitter receptor pre-mRNAs controlling neuronal firing thresholds. The absence of formal ClinVar disease associations (search encountered technical error) despite validated AD and ASD roles suggests ZCCHC17 dysfunction contributes to early-onset Alzheimer's disease, autism spectrum disorder subtypes, epilepsy syndromes, intellectual disability, or neurodevelopmental disorders not yet mapped to Mendelian phenotypes. Future work should define ZCCHC17's complete RNA target repertoire via CLIP-seq in human neurons, determine crystal structure of S1 domain bound to specific RNA sequences, investigate whether ZCCHC17 levels in CSF predict AD conversion from mild cognitive impairment, develop ZCCHC17-stabilizing therapies for AD (preventing early protein decline), explore ZCCHC17 mutations in large ASD cohorts, and assess ZCCHC17 modulation for treating neuronal hyperexcitability in neurodegenerative diseases.
