# ASTE1: Shieldin-Associated ssDNA Endonuclease Blocking DNA End Resection to Promote NHEJ

## Description
ASTE1 (Q2TB18, 679 aa) is a well-folded (AlphaFold pLDDT: 85.3) single-strand DNA endonuclease essential for non-homologous end joining (NHEJ) by antagonizing DNA end resection. InterPro analysis reveals a complex architecture featuring an N-terminal XPG nuclease domain (IPR006085, PF00752, residues 1-97) embedded in a 5'-nuclease superfamily (G3DSA:3.40.50.1010), a PIN-like domain (IPR029060, SSF88723, residues 1-207), and a unique C-terminal Asteroid domain (IPR060554, PF27698, residues 462-617), defining it as the founding member of the Asteroid family (IPR026832, PTHR15665). HPA data confirms ubiquitous expression with ER localization, consistent with roles in genome maintenance. STRING interactions link ASTE1 to TAF1B (0.806), USF3 (0.543), and critically, HELQ (0.436), a helicase involved in DNA repair, suggesting coordination in replication stress responses. Literature (27 papers) establishes ASTE1 as a core component of the shieldin complex (alongside SHLD1/2/3 and REV7), where it acts as the effector nuclease that cleaves 3' overhangs to prevent excessive resection, thereby promoting NHEJ over homologous recombination (HR). This function is vital for immunoglobulin class switch recombination and confers resistance to PARP inhibitors in BRCA1-deficient cancers. ClinVar documents 12 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including truncating mutations (e.g., p.Glu290Ter) and large CNVs on chromosome 3q, implicating ASTE1 haploinsufficiency in genomic instability disorders, though specific monogenic phenotypes remain undefined. The convergence of XPG/PIN nuclease domains, shieldin complex membership, and PARPi resistance mechanisms positions ASTE1 as a critical determinant of DNA repair pathway choice, with therapeutic potential in synthetic lethality strategies for cancer.
