# TTC23L: Testis-Enriched TPR Scaffold Protein Potentially Regulating Spermatogenesis and Ciliary Function via IQCE Interaction

## Description
TTC23L (Tetratricopeptide Repeat Protein 23-Like) is a 361-amino acid intracellular scaffold protein with genuine 'dark gene' status (only 2 publications) despite having strong clinical variant data and a well-defined structural architecture. The protein is dominated by tetratricopeptide repeat (TPR) motifs that form a canonical TPR-like helical domain superfamily (IPR011990, G3DSA:1.25.40.10, residues 164-361) within the TTC23/TTC23L-specific family (IPR042621, PTHR14485, residues 1-359). TPR domains are established protein-protein interaction modules that typically assemble multi-protein complexes, suggesting TTC23L functions as a molecular scaffold. AlphaFold predicts a highly stable, well-folded structure (global pLDDT 84.1), consistent with the repetitive helical bundle architecture characteristic of TPR proteins.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals a network centered on ciliary and cytoskeletal regulators, with the highest confidence partner being IQCE (IQ domain-containing protein E, score 0.761), a cilia-associated protein implicated in Hedgehog signaling and polydactyly syndromes. Additional partners include EFCAB7 (EF-hand calcium-binding domain 7, 0.608), RAI14 (retinoic acid-induced 14, 0.559), MIA2 (melanoma inhibitory activity 2, 0.514), and SPINK14 (0.506). The IQCE interaction is particularly notable as both proteins localize to cilia/flagella structures, suggesting a functional complex.\n\nHuman Protein Atlas data shows testis-enriched expression (25.2 nTPM) with 'tissue enriched' specificity and 'detected in some' tissue distribution, strongly implicating TTC23L in spermatogenesis or sperm flagella function. This aligns with the ciliary interaction network, as sperm flagella are modified cilia. ClinVar reports 25 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains and losses spanning chromosome 5p (the gene's locus), though no specific Mendelian disease is yet assigned. The dosage sensitivity implied by these CNVs, combined with testis-specific expression, suggests TTC23L deficiency may cause male infertility or ciliopathy-related disorders.\n\nWe hypothesize that TTC23L functions as a TPR scaffold that recruits IQCE and other ciliary proteins to the sperm flagella axoneme or basal body, potentially regulating intraflagellar transport or signal transduction during spermatogenesis. The high structural confidence (pLDDT 84.1) suggests evolutionarily conserved folding critical for complex assembly. Dosage imbalance from 5p CNVs may disrupt ciliary architecture or function, contributing to infertility or ciliopathy phenotypes that remain clinically unrecognized.
