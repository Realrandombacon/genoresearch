# ZNF746: KRAB Domain C2H2 Zinc Finger Transcriptional Repressor Linking Parkinson's Disease Pathway to Mitochondrial Quality Control via Parkin-PINK1 Axis

## Description
ZNF746 (Gene ID: 84667, UniProt Q6NUN9) is a 644 amino acid Krüppel-associated box (KRAB) domain-containing C2H2 zinc finger protein located on chromosome 7q36.1 (NC_000007.14). This gene encodes a classical two-module transcriptional repressor architecture: N-terminal KRAB repression domain coupled to C-terminal DNA-binding zinc finger arrays. InterPro domain analysis reveals canonical KRAB-ZFP organization: KRAB-A box domain (cd07765, positions 106-135, ~30 residues), KRAB box domain (IPR001909/PF01352, positions 96-167, ~71 residues forming transcriptional repression module), KRAB superfamily (IPR036051/G3DSA:6.10.140.140, positions 106-151), and multiple C2H2-type zinc finger domains (IPR013087/PF00096/PS00028/PS50157 at positions 453-483, 510-537, 538-565 and beyond—spanning C-terminal ~200 residues with 3+ tandem zinc fingers). The KRAB domain recruits KAP1/TRIM28 corepressor complexes that recruit heterochromatin machinery (HP1, SETDB1 histone methyltransferase, DNMT1 DNA methyltransferase) for transcriptional silencing. C2H2 zinc fingers recognize specific DNA sequences through modular base contacts, with each finger typically recognizing 3-4 bp. STRING interaction network identifies 10 medium-to-high confidence partners revealing unexpected Parkinson's disease connections: PRKN (0.847 high confidence, parkin E3 ubiquitin-protein ligase, mutated in autosomal recessive juvenile Parkinson's disease), ZNF398 (0.774 high confidence, zinc finger protein 398, transcriptional regulator), PPARGC1A (0.681 medium confidence, peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor gamma coactivator 1-alpha, master regulator of mitochondrial biogenesis), TRIM28 (0.607 medium confidence, tripartite motif-containing protein 28/KAP1, essential KRAB-ZFP coreceptor), PINK1 (0.589 medium confidence, PTEN-induced putative kinase 1, mitochondrial serine/threonine kinase mutated in Parkinson's disease), NPM1 (0.583 medium confidence, nucleophosmin 1, nucleolar shuttle protein), AIMP2 (0.537 medium confidence, aminoacyl tRNA synthetase complex-interacting multifunctional protein 2, pro-apoptotic factor), PPP1R9B (0.493 medium confidence, protein phosphatase 1 regulatory subunit 9B, neuronal differentiation regulator), NRF1 (0.482 medium confidence, nuclear respiratory factor 1, mitochondrial transcription activator), and ZNF212 (0.435 medium confidence, another C2H2 zinc finger protein). The PRKN-PINK1 axis connection (both high-confidence partners) is remarkable—these are the two most well-established genes causing autosomal recessive Parkinson's disease, functioning in mitochondrial quality control through mitophagy. Human Protein Atlas classifies ZNF746 (ENSG00000181220) as a predicted intracellular protein and transcription factor/repressor with nucleoplasm and cytosol subcellular localization. RNA expression shows low tissue specificity with detection in ALL tissues (ubiquitous housekeeping expression), low cancer specificity, immune cell enhanced blood cell specificity, and cell type enriched single cell specificity—indicating fundamental transcriptional regulatory function with immune system specialization. Classified under transcription and transcription regulation biological processes with DNA-binding and repressor molecular functions. AlphaFold structure prediction (AF-Q6NUN9-F1) yields MEDIUM-LOW confidence model (global pLDDT 51.3), indicating a partially structured protein with mixed ordered and disordered regions across all 644 residues—the KRAB domain likely forms an α-helical bundle while zinc fingers form compact ββα folds stabilized by Zn²⁺ coordination, but extensive linker regions show disorder typical of KRAB-ZFPs. Medium-low confidence reflects challenging prediction of large proteins with flexible linkers between structured domains. ClinVar lists 66 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large chromosomal copy number variations spanning chromosome 7q regions (chr7:137521595-159119707x1 deletion, chr7:142941022-156102711x1 deletion, chr7:142961178-155604817x1 deletion, chr7:147773827-159119707x1 deletion, chr7:122190535-149944340x1 deletion, chr7:142491993-159119707x3 duplication, chr7:141690279-159119707x3 duplication, chr7:137456457-159119707x3 duplication, chr7:148896264-150963866x1 deletion); one synonymous SNV (NM_001394198.1:c.300G>A/p.Pro100=) of uncertain significance; no specific monogenic disease is attributed solely to ZNF746, suggesting dosage sensitivity within larger 7q chromosomal syndromes affecting neurodevelopment. Conservation is evidenced by orthologs across vertebrates: Mus musculus Znf746 (Q3U133, 652 aa), Bos taurus ZNF746 (Q3B7M4, 630 aa)—indicating functional importance despite sequence divergence in zinc finger count. Functional hypothesis: ZNF746 functions as a sequence-specific transcriptional repressor regulating mitochondrial quality control genes and Parkinson's disease pathway components through epigenetic silencing. The N-terminal KRAB domain (residues 96-167) recruits TRIM28/KAP1 corepressor complex (confirmed by 0.607 interaction), which recruits SETDB1 (H3K9me3 methyltransferase), DNMT1 (DNA methyltransferase), HP1 heterochromatin proteins, and NuRD complex for chromatin compaction and transcriptional silencing. The C-terminal zinc finger array (residues ~434-584+) recognizes specific DNA sequences through combinatorial zinc finger code—each finger contributes 3-4 bp specificity, enabling recognition of 10-15+ bp target sites. High-confidence PRKN interaction (0.847) suggests direct physical association with parkin E3 ligase, potentially linking transcriptional repression to ubiquitination machinery. PINK1 interaction (0.589) extends this to mitochondrial kinase signaling cascade. PPARGC1A interaction (0.681) connects ZNF746 to PGC-1α-mediated mitochondrial biogenesis program—critical for neuronal survival and dopaminergic neuron maintenance. NRF1 interaction (0.482) further supports mitochondrial transcription regulation. NPM1 interaction (0.583) suggests nucleolar shuttling and potential ribosome biogenesis coupling. AIMP2 interaction (0.537) links to apoptosis regulation—AIMP2 is released from tRNA synthetase complex upon DNA damage to induce cell death. PPP1R9B interaction (0.493) suggests roles in neuronal differentiation and neurite outgrowth. ZNF398 interaction (0.774) indicates cooperative regulation with other zinc finger transcription factors. Mechanistically, ZNF746 may function as: (1) Transcriptional repressor of mitochondrial quality control genes, fine-tuning mitophagy threshold in neurons; (2) Bridge between nuclear transcription and cytoplasmic ubiquitination via PRKN binding; (3) Regulator of PGC-1α/NRF1 mitochondrial biogenesis program through direct promoter binding; (4) Stress-responsive factor coupling DNA damage (via AIMP2) to transcriptional reprogramming. Immune cell enhanced expression suggests additional roles in inflammatory gene regulation. Disease mechanism: ZNF746 haploinsufficiency or overexpression from chromosome 7q36 CNVs may contribute to 7q deletion/duplication syndrome phenotypes including intellectual disability, growth abnormalities, and neurological defects. The 66 ClinVar variants suggest strong dosage sensitivity; 7q36 deletions cause holoprosencephaly and developmental delay. ZNF746 may contribute through disrupted mitochondrial quality control (accelerating neuronal degeneration), aberrant transcriptional repression (altering neurodevelopmental programs), or impaired stress response (compromising DNA damage repair). Parkinson's disease connection via PRKN-PINK1 axis is particularly compelling—ZNF746 may modify penetrance or age-of-onset in parkin/PINK1 mutation carriers. Priority experiments include ChIP-seq to identify genomic binding sites and PRKN/PINK1/PPARGC1A colocalization, co-IP validation of PRKN, PINK1, TRIM28, PPARGC1A interactions across mitochondrial stress conditions, reporter assays testing repression activity on mitochondrial gene promoters, CRISPR knockout to assess mitochondrial morphology, mitophagy flux, and neuronal survival phenotypes, structural analysis leveraging AlphaFold model (pLDDT 51.3) to map DNA-binding and protein interaction interfaces, investigation of ZNF746 role in dopaminergic neuron differentiation using iPSC models, and analysis of ZNF746 expression in Parkinson's disease patient brains and 7q syndrome patient cells.
