## Description
HERC3 encodes a 1050-amino acid probable E3 ubiquitin-protein ligase HERC3 (UniProt Q15034), also known as HECT and RLD domain containing E3 ubiquitin protein ligase 3, a well-folded cytosolic enzyme functioning in protein ubiquitination and ER-associated degradation (ERAD) through HECT catalytic domain and RCC1 repeat architecture. The protein contains a canonical HECT domain (IPR000569, positions 702-1050; cd00078 HECTc, positions 702-1048) characteristic of HECT-type E3 ubiquitin ligases that form thioester intermediate with ubiquitin before substrate transfer, classified within the HECT E3 ligase catalytic domain superfamily (IPR035983, positions 687-1043; G3DSA:2.130.10.30/3.30.2160.10/3.30.2410.10/3.90.1750.10 multiple HECT domains) with RCC1 repeats (IPR000408, positions 1-52/53-102/103-158; IPR009091, positions 1-419; G3DSA:2.130.10.30, positions 1-122/123-419) providing protein-protein interaction surfaces for substrate recognition, and Ubiquitin ligases and GTPase regulators family (IPR051709, positions 130-1045). AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (pLDDT 89.1, residues 1-1050) indicating stable folding with well-defined HECT catalytic domain and RCC1 repeat bundle suitable for E2 binding and substrate recruitment. STRING interaction analysis reveals HERC3 forms a ubiquitin-proteasome network with high-confidence partners: UBQLN1 (0.853, ubiquilin-1), FAM13A (0.578, family with sequence similarity 13 member A), UBE2L3 (0.549, ubiquitin-conjugating enzyme E2 L3), BLCAP (0.520, bladder cancer-associated protein), CHRAC1 (0.514, chromatin accessibility complex protein 1), UBE2E2 (0.513, ubiquitin-conjugating enzyme E2 E2), TRAPPC9 (0.494, trafficking protein particle complex 9), UBQLN2 (0.492, ubiquilin-2), SH3RF1 (0.482, SH3 domain containing ring finger 1), NAP1L5 (0.449, nucleosome assembly protein 1 like 5), confirming HERC3's role in ubiquitin-dependent protein degradation and proteostasis. HPA data shows tissue-enhanced expression with retina as top tissue (7.9 nTPM), cytosol subcellular localization consistent with ERAD and cytosolic protein quality control, annotated for predicted intracellular proteins class with cell type enriched single cell specificity. ClinVar contains 25 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number losses (4q21.21-22.3, 4q21.23-22.3, 4p12-q35.2, 4q21.21-23, 4q21.23-22.1, 4q21.21-22.1, 4q21.21-22.2), copy number gains (4q21.23-22.1, 4q13.3-22.1), and splice site variant (c.463+2T>G), though no specific Mendelian disorder is named, suggesting HERC3 dosage sensitivity affects protein homeostasis and neurodegeneration across multiple organ systems. With 51 publications, HERC3 has emerging roles in ERAD, cancer, and neurodegeneration: HERC3 facilitates ERAD of select membrane proteins by recognizing membrane-spanning domains independent of glycosylation status (2024, 8 cit), HERC3 deficiency leads to accumulation of subretinal microglia and retinal neurodegeneration modeling age-related macular degeneration (2024, 6 cit), HERC3 promotes YAP/TAZ stability and tumorigenesis independently of its ubiquitin ligase activity through protein-protein interactions (2023, 22 cit - highly cited), HERC3 deficiency causes NCOA1 accumulation leading to MMP transactivation and extracellular matrix degradation in intervertebral disc degeneration (2023, 10 cit). The functional hypothesis: HERC3 serves as a HECT-type E3 ubiquitin ligase where the C-terminal HECT domain (positions 702-1050) catalyzes ubiquitin transfer from E2 conjugating enzymes (UBE2L3/UBE2E2) to substrate proteins through canonical E2~Ub thioester intermediate formation and substrate lysine ubiquitination; N-terminal RCC1 repeats (positions 1-158, three repeats) form a beta-propeller structure providing protein-protein interaction surface for substrate recognition, particularly membrane protein transmembrane domains as revealed by 2024 ERAD study; high structural confidence (pLDDT 89.1) indicates stable architecture suitable for sustained E3 ligase activity; UBQLN1/UBQLN2 interactions suggest HERC3 functions with ubiquilin adaptors that shuttle ubiquitinated substrates to the proteasome, linking HERC3 activity to proteasomal degradation; UBE2L3/UBE2E2 interactions confirm specific E2 enzyme partnerships for ubiquitin chain formation; TRAPPC9 interaction links HERC3 to vesicle trafficking, potentially coordinating ERAD with membrane traffic; FAM13A interaction is notable as FAM13A is a COP9 signalosome-interacting protein, suggesting HERC3 may coordinate with CSN complex for cullin-RING ligase regulation; BLCAP (bladder cancer protein) interaction suggests tumor suppressor regulation; CHRAC1 interaction links HERC3 to chromatin remodeling, indicating potential nuclear functions beyond cytosolic ERAD; retina-enriched expression (7.9 nTPM) explains retinal neurodegeneration phenotype where HERC3 deficiency causes subretinal microglia accumulation, suggesting HERC3 maintains retinal proteostasis and prevents inflammatory microglial activation; YAP/TAZ stabilization independent of ligase activity reveals non-canonical scaffolding function where HERC3 protects Hippo pathway effectors from degradation through protein-protein interactions rather than ubiquitination, promoting tumorigenesis; NCOA1 accumulation in deficiency states indicates HERC3 normally ubiquitinates and degrades this transcriptional coactivator, and loss leads to MMP overexpression and ECM degradation in disc degeneration; membrane protein ERAD function reveals unique substrate recognition mechanism where HERC3 recognizes transmembrane domains directly rather than misfolded luminal domains, providing quality control for membrane protein biogenesis; 25 pathogenic CNVs without named disorder suggests HERC3 dosage affects global protein homeostasis, causing neurodegeneration, cancer susceptibility, and tissue degeneration through altered ubiquitination capacity; cytosol localization confirms primary site for cytosolic and ER-associated protein quality control; cell type enriched specificity suggests HERC3 function is critical in specific cell populations, potentially retinal pigment epithelium or photoreceptors.
