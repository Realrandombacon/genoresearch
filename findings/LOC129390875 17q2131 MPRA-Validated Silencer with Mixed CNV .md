## Description
LOC129390875 is a 200bp cis-regulatory element (CRE) located on chromosome 17q21.31 (GRCh38: 44235146-44235346), functionally validated as a transcriptional silencer through STARR-seq massively parallel reporter assays (MPRAs) in K562 erythroleukemia cells. This locus lacks canonical promoter, enhancer, or CTCF-bound insulator chromatin signatures but demonstrates robust repressive activity against the super core promoter SCP1. As a non-coding regulatory sequence, it has no UniProt accession, protein domains, STRING interaction partners, or Human Protein Atlas expression data (query failed to resolve Ensembl ID).\n\nClinVar analysis reveals 5 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variants disrupting this region. The variant spectrum shows a mix of large-scale deletions (copy number losses) and duplications (copy number gains). Deletions include events spanning the critical 17q21.31 region (e.g., 43.9-44.8Mb, ~920kb; 43.6-44.8Mb, ~1.2Mb), which overlaps with the KANSL1 gene locus responsible for Koolen-de Vries syndrome. Duplications involve massive arm-level gains (e.g., 36.4-75.0Mb, ~38.6Mb; 39.2-45.6Mb, ~6.4Mb; 36.4-68.2Mb, ~31.7Mb), often encompassing the entire 17q21-q25 region. The ratio of duplications to deletions is 3:2, suggesting that both haploinsufficiency (loss of silencing) and triplication (hyper-silencing) are pathogenic mechanisms. These CNVs are associated with 17q21.31 microdeletion/duplication syndromes, characterized by intellectual disability, hypotonia, friendly demeanor (deletion), or developmental delay and distinct facial features (duplication). The locus resides near critical genes such as KANSL1 and MAPT (Microtubule-Associated Protein Tau), implying potential regulatory roles.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: LOC129390875 functions as a critical distal silencer within the 17q21.31 topologically associating domain (TAD), specifically regulating dosage-sensitive neurodevelopmental genes like KANSL1 or MAPT. Given its validation in K562 cells, it likely acts as a general repression module. The mechanism involves recruitment of co-repressor complexes (e.g., PRC2, NuRD) to maintain target genes in a silenced state during specific developmental windows. Loss of this silencer (via 17q21.31 deletions) leads to aberrant overexpression of targets like KANSL1, contributing to the cognitive deficits and congenital anomalies of Koolen-de Vries syndrome. Conversely, duplication may lead to excessive recruitment of repressive machinery, causing profound downregulation of essential drivers, resulting in the distinct phenotype of 17q21.31 duplication syndrome. The mixed CNV burden highlights the exquisite dosage sensitivity of the regulated targets, requiring precise stoichiometric control for proper brain development.
