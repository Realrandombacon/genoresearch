## Description
PSMG4 (Proteasome assembly chaperone 4, also known as PAC4, Pba4, C6orf86) is a 123 amino acid cytosolic chaperone (UniProt Q5JS54) with high-confidence well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 87.0), characteristic of the Pba4 family of proteasome assembly factors. The protein belongs to the Proteasome assembly chaperone 4 family (IPR032157/PTHR33559, residues 7-123) with a conserved Pba4 domain (PF16093, residues 26-97). Molecular functions include chaperone activity (GO:0003757) and proteasome assembly (GO:0043248), placing PSMG4 within the ubiquitin-proteasome system and protein quality control pathways.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals a network exclusively composed of proteasome subunits and assembly factors: strongest interactions with PSMA1 (0.822, Proteasome subunit alpha type-1, 20S core particle), PSMB9 (0.770, Proteasome subunit beta type-9, immunoproteasome), POMP (0.705, Proteasome maturation protein, assembly chaperone), PSMA5 (0.696, Proteasome subunit alpha type-5), PAAF1 (0.633, Proteasome activator PA28 family), PSMG2 (0.601, Proteasome assembly chaperone 2), PSMG1 (0.576, Proteasome assembly chaperone 1), PSMD4 (0.558, Proteasome 26S subunit non-ATPase 4), CA10 (0.558, Carbonic anhydrase 10), and PSMG3 (0.422, Proteasome assembly chaperone 3). This profile confirms PSMG4 functions as a dedicated proteasome assembly factor, specifically interacting with alpha-ring subunits (PSMA1, PSMA5) and other chaperones (PSMG1, PSMG2, PSMG3, POMP) during 20S core particle biogenesis.\n\nHPA expression reveals ubiquitous detection across all tissues with low tissue specificity, consistent with an essential housekeeping role in proteostasis. Subcellular localization is nucleoplasmic and mitochondrial, suggesting PSMG4 may facilitate proteasome assembly in multiple compartments. The protein is classified as essential, reflecting the critical nature of proteasome function for cell viability. Molecular function is explicitly annotated as chaperone.\n\nClinVar documents 53 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains and losses affecting chromosome 6p (6p25.3-25.1 deletions, 6p25.3-22.3 duplications, 6p25.3-25.2 losses). The high variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity. While no specific Mendelian disease is annotated, 6p CNVs are associated with developmental delay, hearing loss, eye anomalies, and intellectual disability. Given PSMG4's role in proteasome assembly, dosage imbalance may cause proteotoxic stress, impaired protein degradation, and accumulation of damaged proteins, triggering cellular stress responses and developmental defects.\n\nConservation is strong across eukaryotes: human (Q5JS54, 123 aa), mouse (P0C7N9, 123 aa), yeast (Q12245, POC4, 148 aa), indicating ancient and essential function in proteasome biogenesis.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: PSMG4 functions as a dedicated chaperone that binds nascent proteasome alpha-ring subunits (PSMA1, PSMA5) during 20S core particle assembly. It likely forms a heterodimer with PSMG3 (Pba3) to stabilize alpha-ring intermediates, preventing premature subunit aggregation or degradation. Interaction with POMP suggests coordination with the proteasome maturation pathway, while PSMG1/PSMG2 interactions indicate handoff to downstream assembly factors. Loss of PSMG4 causes defective alpha-ring formation, reduced 20S proteasome levels, and impaired protein degradation, leading to proteotoxic stress and activation of unfolded protein responses. This mechanism parallels known proteasome assembly disorders and explains the developmental phenotypes associated with 6p CNVs. PSMG4 represents an essential proteostasis gene and a potential contributor to 6p deletion/duplication syndromes.
