## Description
MID1IP1 (Midline 1 Interacting Protein 1) is a 183-amino acid intracellular protein with 32 publications confirming its partially characterized dark gene status. The protein belongs to the Spot 14 family (IPR009786, positions 1-180) with thyroid hormone-inducible hepatic protein Spot 14 domain (PF07084, positions 2-178), lipogenesis and microtubule stabilization superfamily architecture (G3DSA:6.10.140.1610 and IPR053719, positions 1-178), and PTHR14315 family membership (positions 1-180). AlphaFold predicts a partially structured protein (pLDDT 67.6, medium confidence), consistent with potential intrinsically disordered regions common in regulatory proteins. HPA reveals high expression in brain (154.5 nTPM top tissue) and skeletal muscle (140.7 nTPM), tissue-enhanced RNA specificity, detected-in-all distribution pattern, low cancer specificity, immune cell-enhanced blood cell specificity, with annotated functions in lipid biosynthesis and lipid metabolism. ClinVar documents 141 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants (all X chromosome Xp22.33-11.4 copy number gains/losses and single nucleotide variants with uncertain clinical significance), indicating extreme dosage sensitivity consistent with X-chromosome location. STRING interactions reveal MID1IP1 functions within lipogenic and transcriptional regulatory networks with high confidence connections to THRSP (0.784) - thyroid hormone-inducible hepatic protein/Spot 14 paralog, MID1 (0.708) - midline 1 E3 ubiquitin ligase (name-sake interaction partner), medium confidence interactions with ACACA (0.620) - acetyl-CoA carboxylase 1 rate-limiting lipogenic enzyme, plus lower confidence connections to ZBTB8A (0.479) - zinc finger and BTB domain containing 8A transcriptional repressor, GINM1 (0.405), and TATDN2 (0.402). Recent studies (2020-2025) established MID1IP1 as a glucose-responsive gene regulated by carbohydrate response element binding protein (ChREBP), with critical roles in liver cancer growth via c-Myc colocalization and regulation of ribosomal proteins L5/L11 and CNOT2, colorectal cancer progression through CNOT2-MID1IP1-p53 apoptotic axis, ferroptosis induction upon MID1IP1 inhibition with concurrent c-Myc suppression, and lipid metabolism regulation via AMPK/ACC phosphorylation (shikimic acid and icariside E4 hypolipogenic effects). We hypothesize MID1IP1 functions as a Spot 14 family lipogenic regulator that bridges glucose sensing (ChREBP signaling) with lipid biosynthesis (ACACA regulation) and transcriptional control (c-Myc, CNOT2 complex), potentially through MID1-mediated ubiquitination pathways. The Spot 14 domain (positions 1-180) likely mediates protein-protein interactions within lipogenic complexes. High brain expression (154.5 nTPM) suggests neuronal lipid metabolism roles. X-chromosome location with 141 ClinVar variants indicates dosage sensitivity potentially contributing to X-linked metabolic disorders. The THRSP paralog connection (0.784) suggests functional redundancy within Spot 14 subfamily. MID1IP1 represents an uncharacterized X-linked lipogenic regulator linking glucose metabolism, lipid biosynthesis, and cancer progression through c-Myc/CNOT2 signaling.
