## Description
NOL4L (UniProt Q96MY1) encodes a 436 aa nucleoplasmic protein belonging to the Nucleolar protein 4 family, functioning as a novel oncogenic driver in multiple cancers through PI3K/AKT pathway activation and serving as a hub for circRNA/miRNA regulatory networks. InterPro analysis reveals a specialized domain architecture: a central NOL4 family domain (aa 15-374, IPR039788, PTHR12449) classified within the Death domain-containing protein superfamily, suggesting potential roles in protein-protein interactions and cell death regulation; a helical domain (aa 185-279, IPR056549, PF23079) forming a structured core within an otherwise flexible scaffold. AlphaFold predicts a medium-confidence structure (global pLDDT 61.2), consistent with a partially structured protein containing ordered helical domains embedded in intrinsically disordered regions—a hallmark of nuclear scaffold proteins that undergo conformational changes during complex assembly and transcriptional regulation. STRING interactions define NOL4L as a node connecting transcriptional regulation and cell cycle control: CTBP1 (0.821, high confidence), TEX9 (0.738), SKA3 (0.597, spindle apparatus), PAX5 (0.577, B-cell transcription factor), CTBP2 (0.483), KIF3B (0.476, kinesin motor), RUNX1 (0.468, hematopoietic transcription factor), COMMD7 (0.462), and NOL4 (0.406, paralog), indicating roles in transcriptional repression (CTBP interaction), mitotic progression (SKA3, KIF3B), and hematopoietic development (PAX5, RUNX1). HPA expression shows tissue-enhanced distribution with retina enrichment (45.9 nTPM), nucleoplasmic localization, and ubiquitous detection across tissues, consistent with a nuclear regulatory protein; low cancer specificity suggests constitutive expression rather than tumor-specific upregulation. ClinVar reports 14 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number losses and gains on chromosome 20q11.21-13.12 (spanning the NOL4L locus), indicating genomic instability at this region potentially linked to cancer susceptibility and developmental disorders, though no specific monogenic disease has been definitively assigned. Literature (16 papers) highlights: (1) Neuroblastoma Progression—multiple circRNAs (circPDE5A, circ_0132817) and lncRNAs (DUXAP8, CASC11) regulate NOL4L via miRNA sponging (miR-362-5p, miR-432-5p, miR-29), promoting cell proliferation, migration, invasion, and glycolysis; (2) Ovarian Cancer—NOL4L enhances PI3K/AKT pathway activation, driving cell proliferation and metastasis; (3) Acute Myeloid Leukemia—initially identified in AML, suggesting hematopoietic roles; (4) Wnt/β-catenin Activation—DUXAP8/miR-29/NOL4L axis activates Wnt signaling in neuroblastoma. Functional hypothesis: NOL4L functions as a: (1) Nuclear Scaffold Protein—bridges transcriptional repressors (CTBP1/2) with cell cycle regulators (SKA3, KIF3B) and lineage-specific transcription factors (PAX5, RUNX1) to coordinate gene expression programs; (2) Oncogenic Hub—integrates multiple non-coding RNA regulatory inputs (circRNAs, lncRNAs, miRNAs) to amplify PI3K/AKT and Wnt/β-catenin signaling in cancers; (3) Retinal Function Protein—high retina expression suggests specialized roles in retinal development or function, potentially linked to 20q11 genomic disorders affecting vision. Mechanistic proposal: In neuroblastoma and ovarian cancer, oncogenic circRNAs and lncRNAs sequester tumor-suppressive miRNAs (miR-362-5p, miR-432-5p, miR-29) that normally repress NOL4L, leading to NOL4L overexpression. Elevated NOL4L binds CTBP1/2 transcriptional corepressors and recruits them to specific genomic loci, while simultaneously activating PI3K/AKT signaling through an unknown intermediate, promoting cell cycle progression (via SKA3/kinesin interactions), metabolic reprogramming (glycolysis enhancement), and metastatic behavior. The partially structured architecture enables dynamic interactions with multiple partners, functioning as a molecular hub that integrates diverse regulatory inputs. Chromosome 20q11 CNVs may alter NOL4L dosage, contributing to genomic instability phenotypes. The combination of a death domain-containing scaffold architecture, CTBP/transcription factor interactions, retina-enriched expression, cancer-associated copy number variations, and integration into multiple oncogenic non-coding RNA networks positions NOL4L as a genuine dark gene requiring mechanistic dissection of its nuclear functions and therapeutic potential in neuroblastoma and ovarian cancer.
