## Description
ZNF664 (Gene ID: 144348, UniProt Q8N3J9) is a 261 amino acid C2H2-type zinc finger transcription factor located on chromosome 12q24.31 (NC_000012.12, positions 123973214-124015426, ~42kb region). InterPro domain analysis reveals extensive zinc finger architecture: three canonical C2H2-type zinc finger domains (IPR013087, positions 3-30, 31-58, 59-86), membership in the C2H2 zinc finger superfamily (IPR036236, positions 2-96, 95-152, 151-249), Krüppel C2H2-type zinc-finger family (PTHR24394, positions 3-147), and classic zinc finger homologous superfamily (G3DSA:3.30.160.60) with PROSITE signatures (PS00028, PS50157) confirming functional zinc-coordinating residues. This multi-finger architecture is characteristic of sequence-specific DNA-binding transcription factors that recognize extended DNA motifs through combinatorial finger arrangements. AlphaFold structure prediction (AF-Q8N3J9-F1) shows very high confidence (global pLDDT 91.5), indicating a well-folded stable structure with properly formed zinc finger folds suitable for DNA recognition. Human Protein Atlas classifies ZNF664 as a transcription factor with DNA-binding molecular function, localized to nucleoplasm, involved in transcription regulation. Expression shows low tissue specificity with detection in all surveyed tissues, but group-enriched single cell type specificity suggesting specialized roles in particular cell populations despite ubiquitous presence. STRING database identifies 10 protein-protein interactions revealing connections to transcriptional regulation and diverse cellular processes: DNAH10 (score 0.702, dynein axonemal heavy chain 10, ciliary motor protein - highest confidence interaction), RFLNA (0.543, filamin-A repeat-containing protein, cytoskeletal organizer), TRIM28 (0.518, tripartite motif containing 28/KAP1, transcriptional co-repressor and heterochromatin regulator), GALNT2 (0.466, polypeptide N-acetylgalactosaminyltransferase 2, O-glycosylation enzyme), SORCS1 (0.459, sortilin-related VPS10 domain containing receptor 1, trafficking receptor), TRIB1 (0.448, tribbles pseudokinase 1, signaling scaffold), NT5DC2 (0.448, 5'-nucleotidase domain containing 2), OR56B1 (0.447, olfactory receptor 56B1), FOXN3 (0.446, forkhead box N3, transcription factor), and CCDC92 (0.427, coiled-coil domain containing 92). The TRIM28 interaction is particularly significant as TRIM28/KAP1 is a master transcriptional co-repressor that recruits SETDB1 histone methyltransferase and CHD3 chromatin remodelers to silence endogenous retroviruses and regulate developmental genes. ClinVar contains 22 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variants affecting chromosome 12q24 regions spanning ZNF664 locus, including large duplications (12q23.1-24.33 chr12:99532287-133777902x3, 12q24.21-24.33 chr12:116422123-133777902x3, 12q24.22-24.33 chr12:117533207-133777902x3) and deletions (12q24.31-24.32 chr12:123878845-126829341, 12q24.31-24.32 chr12:122169403-129084163x1), though clinical significance remains uncertain for most entries. Conservation is evidenced by orthologs in mouse (Q4VA44, 261 aa) and orangutan (Q5RAM9, 261 aa) with identical protein length, indicating strong evolutionary constraint. Functional hypothesis: ZNF664 functions as a sequence-specific DNA-binding transcriptional repressor that recruits the TRIM28/KAP1 co-repressor complex to silence target genes through heterochromatin formation. The three C2H2 zinc fingers likely recognize a specific DNA motif (typically 9-10bp for triple-finger arrays), positioning ZNF664 as a targeted gene silencer rather than broad-spectrum repressor. Interaction with DNAH10 suggests potential coupling between transcriptional regulation and ciliary function, possibly coordinating gene expression programs during ciliogenesis or cilia-dependent signaling. The FOXN3 interaction indicates potential cross-regulation within the zinc finger transcription factor network. Disease mechanism: Dosage sensitivity from chromosome 12q24 copy number variations disrupts ZNF664 expression, potentially causing transcriptional dysregulation of target genes involved in development, ciliary function, or heterochromatin maintenance. The 12q24 region is associated with multiple developmental disorders, and ZNF664 dysfunction may contribute through misregulation of TRIM28-targeted loci including endogenous retroviruses and imprinted genes. Priority experiments include identification of DNA binding motifs via SELEX or ChIP-seq, mapping of genomic binding sites, characterization of TRIM28-dependent repression activity, assessment of ciliary phenotypes upon ZNF664 depletion, determination of crystal structure of zinc finger-DNA complexes, and investigation of developmental roles in knockout models.
