## Description
LINS1 (Lines Homolog 1, UniProt Q8NG48) is a 757 aa nuclear protein belonging to the Protein Lines family (IPR024875/PTHR16057, positions 7-751) that functions as an essential regulator of the Wingless/Wnt signaling pathway and neurodevelopment. The molecular architecture consists of an N-terminal Lines domain (IPR032794/PF14694, positions 193-542) for protein-protein interactions and transcriptional regulation, followed by a C-terminal Lines domain (IPR029415/PF14695, positions 713-747) for complex assembly, forming a multi-domain scaffold for Wnt pathway modulation. Literature (24 papers) establishes LINS1 roles in: (1) domain-specific phenotypes in LINS1-related disorder with Chinese family Q92X variant and literature review (2024), (2) novel missense homozygous variant in LINS1 in two distinct Iranian families with consanguineous marriage causing intellectual disability (2026), (3) expansion of clinical and molecular spectrum of LINS1-associated disease (2026), (4) novel nonsense homozygous mutation of LINS1 in two sisters with intellectual disability, schizophrenia, and anxiety (2021, 7 citations), (5) novel LINS1 truncating mutation in autosomal recessive nonsyndromic intellectual disability (2020, 6 citations). Functional hypothesis: LINS1 functions as a nuclear scaffold protein that modulates Wnt/beta-catenin signaling through interaction with transcriptional co-regulators and spliceosome components, regulating neurodevelopmental gene expression programs. The mechanistic model proposes: (1) N-terminal Lines domain binds transcriptional co-activators or repressors for Wnt target gene regulation, (2) C-terminal Lines domain mediates complex assembly with PLRG1-PRPF19-BCAS2 spliceosome components for alternative splicing of neurodevelopmental transcripts, (3) LINS1-POLR2B interaction modulates RNA polymerase II transcription elongation at Wnt-responsive promoters, (4) LINS1 mitochondrial protein interactions (MT-CO1, MT-CYB) suggest role in metabolic regulation of neuronal development, (5) LINS1-NPAT interaction links Wnt signaling to cell cycle progression in neural progenitors. In autosomal recessive intellectual disability, homozygous loss-of-function variants disrupt Wnt signaling and spliceosome assembly, causing aberrant neurodevelopmental gene expression and synaptic dysfunction. Domain-specific phenotypes correlate with variant position: N-terminal variants cause severe intellectual disability with schizophrenia, C-terminal variants cause milder phenotypes. LINS1 represents an understudied Wnt pathway regulator linking transcriptional control, splicing, and neurodevelopment to intellectual disability and psychiatric disorders.
