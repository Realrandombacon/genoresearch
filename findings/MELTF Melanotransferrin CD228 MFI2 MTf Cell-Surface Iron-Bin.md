## Description
MELTF encodes a 738 aa cell-surface glycoprotein (UniProt P08582), also known as CD228 or MFI2 (Melanoma-associated antigen recognized by T-cells), that functions as an atypical transferrin family member with iron-binding capacity regulating cancer progression, cell death pathways, and extracellular proteolysis through a dual-lobed periplasmic binding protein architecture. The protein features a canonical transferrin structure: two homologous lobes formed by transferrin-like domains (IPR001156/PF00405/G3DSA:3.40.190.10, residues 23-355 and 367-704) creating the characteristic bilobal architecture; conserved iron-binding sites (IPR018195/PS00205/PS00206/PS00207, residues 107-116/210-226/252-282/451-460/556-572) coordinating Fe3+ through tyrosine, histidine, aspartate residues and carbonate anion bridge; classified within transferrin family (IPR016357/PIRSF002549/PR00422, residues 7-711) localized to extracellular region. STRING interactions reveal MELTF operates within iron transport and fibrinolysis networks: PLG (0.880), plasminogen linking to extracellular matrix degradation and cell invasion; TFRC (0.792), transferrin receptor suggesting shared iron uptake pathway or competitive binding; HPGDS (0.658), hematopoietic prostaglandin D synthase connecting to inflammatory signaling; LRP2 (0.589), low-density lipoprotein receptor-related protein 2 (megalin) involved in endocytic clearance; SRPRB (0.560), signal recognition particle receptor beta subunit; FGA (0.534), fibrinogen alpha chain in coagulation cascade; FAM20C (0.506), Golgi casein kinase phosphorylating secretory proteins; WDR53 (0.466), WD repeat protein; VTN (0.465), vitronectin in cell adhesion; and PIGZ (0.464), GPI transamidase component. HPA confirms tissue-enhanced expression with salivary gland enrichment (67.1 nTPM) and kidney expression (37.8 nTPM), detected in many tissues, cancer-enhanced expression pattern, classified as CD marker/plasma protein involved in ion/iron transport with cell type enhanced specificity. ClinVar lists 95 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number losses (3q29 spanning 195.9-197.6Mb x1, 3q29 194.8-197.8Mb x3, deletions 196.0-197.5Mb) and gains (3q22.1-29 spanning 132.5-197.8Mb x3), splice site mutations (c.1751-6C>G), intronic variants (c.712+1591C>G), missense changes (c.2088C>G p.Asp696Glu), and lncRNA MELTF-AS1 variants (n.89C>T), demonstrating strong dosage sensitivity though specific monogenic diseases remain undefined. AlphaFold structure prediction yields high confidence (global pLDDT 89.4), revealing well-folded bilobal transferrin structure with properly positioned iron coordination residues in both N-lobe and C-lobe. Mechanistically, MELTF operates through integrated modes: (1) Iron transport—binds Fe3+ through conserved coordination sites though physiological importance remains debated compared to serum transferrin; (2) Melanoma suppression—2024 study demonstrates MELTF expression attenuates malignant melanoma progression in A375-Luc2 murine metastasis model and human patients, acting as tumor suppressor contrary to initial oncogene classification; (3) Lung adenocarcinoma promotion—2023 study reveals E2F1/MELTF axis fosters LUAD progression by regulating Notch signaling pathway, demonstrating context-dependent oncogenic function; (4) Phase separation regulation—2022 study shows SP1-activated lncRNA MELTF-AS1 drives YBX1 phase separation activating ANXA8 in NSCLC tumorigenesis via copy number amplification; (5) Cell death modulation—2022 study suggests MELTF regulates ferroptosis, pyroptosis, and autophagy in platelet-rich plasma-mediated endometrial epithelium regeneration; (6) Plasminogen interaction—high-confidence PLG binding suggests role in fibrinolysis and extracellular matrix remodeling facilitating cell migration; (7) GPI-anchor attachment—predicted glycosylphosphatidylinositol modification anchors protein to cell surface enabling receptor-like signaling. Loss-of-function causes disrupted iron homeostasis at cell surface, altered melanoma metastatic potential, dysregulated Notch signaling in lung cancer, impaired endometrial regeneration via cell death pathway dysregulation, and defective extracellular matrix remodeling.
