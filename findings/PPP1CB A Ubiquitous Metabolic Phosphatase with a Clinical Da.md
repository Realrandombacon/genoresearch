## Description
PPP1CB (Protein phosphatase 1 catalytic subunit beta) is a core component of the PP1 holoenzyme, functioning as a Serine/Threonine-specific protein phosphatase with a very high-confidence (pLDDT 91.6) stable 3D structure. It exhibits ubiquitous expression ('Detected in all' tissues) and is localized to the nucleoplasm and cytosol, playing critical roles in glycogen metabolism, cell cycle regulation, and biological rhythms. While well-studied (178 papers) and known to interact with key regulatory subunits (PPP1R12A, PPP1R15A) and catalytic partners (PPP1CC), it presents a significant Clinical Data Gap: ClinVar lists 55 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants (including copy number gains/losses and missense mutations), yet **zero specific genetic diseases** are cataloged. This contrasts with its defined roles in cancer and metabolism. The lack of cataloged syndromes likely stems from its 'hub' nature; by dephosphorylating hundreds of diverse substrates simultaneously via multiple regulatory complexes, PPP1CB may induce broad, systemic metabolic and signaling dysregulation rather than discrete, Mendelian phenotypes, obscuring the specific clinical presentation.
