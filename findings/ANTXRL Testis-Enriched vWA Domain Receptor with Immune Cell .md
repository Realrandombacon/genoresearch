## Description
ANTXRL (Anthrax toxin receptor-like, UniProt A6NF34) is a 631-amino acid type I transmembrane protein located on chromosome 10q11.22-q11.23. InterPro analysis reveals a bipartite extracellular domain architecture: an N-terminal von Willebrand factor type A domain (IPR002035/PF00092/SM00327, aa 74-251) mediating protein-protein or protein-ligand interactions through its characteristic Rossmann-fold βαββαβ topology, followed by an anthrax toxin receptor extracellular domain (IPR008400/PF05587, aa 249-348) defining membership in the anthrax toxin receptor-like family (PTHR16059, aa 8-500). The vWA domain confers GO annotations for signaling receptor activity and membrane localization. AlphaFold structure prediction achieves medium confidence (pLDDT 63.1), indicating a partially structured protein with ordered vWA and receptor domains connected by flexible linkers, consistent with receptors requiring conformational flexibility for ligand binding and signal transduction. Human Protein Atlas reveals testis-enriched expression (12.0 nTPM) with tissue-enriched RNA specificity detected in single tissue type, plus immune cell enhancement in blood cells, suggesting dual roles in reproductive biology and immune surveillance. STRING interaction network reveals 10 partners spanning diverse functional categories: FAM25C/FAM25A (0.480-0.542, family with sequence similarity 25), AGAP6/AGAP9 (0.448-0.509, ArfGAP with GTPase and ANK repeat domains), ZNF488 (0.505, zinc finger transcription factor), SYT15 (0.488, synaptotagmin calcium sensor), MOSPD1 (0.472, motile sperm domain-containing protein), ZFAND4 (0.467, zinc finger AN1-type domain), and GRAMD1C (0.464, GRAM domain-containing lipid transfer protein). This interactome suggests ANTXRL functions at the intersection of vesicular trafficking, transcriptional regulation, and membrane dynamics. ClinVar contains 20 pathogenic variants including multiple copy-number losses and gains across 10q11.21-q21.3, deletions, duplications, and point mutations (c.1878C>T p.Pro626=, c.797-3C>A splice variant), demonstrating extreme dosage sensitivity where genomic rearrangements disrupting ANTXRL cause disease phenotypes. Conservation across mammals (mouse Q8BVM2, 641 aa; macaque Q4R7B7, 557 aa) and birds indicates ancient evolutionary origin. ANTXRL likely functions as a cell surface receptor utilizing its vWA domain for ligand recognition and its anthrax receptor-like domain for signal transduction, potentially regulating spermatogenesis, immune cell activation, or vesicular trafficking through interactions with ArfGAPs and membrane-associated partners.
