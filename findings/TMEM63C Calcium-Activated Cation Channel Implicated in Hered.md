## Description
TMEM63C (Transmembrane Protein 63C, UniProt Q9P1W3) is an 806 amino acid osmosensitive cation channel belonging to the CSC1/OSCA1 family of calcium-permeable stress-gated channels. The protein contains three major structural domains: an N-terminal transmembrane domain (aa 42-203, IPR032880/PF13967), a cytosolic domain (aa 220-398, IPR027815/PF14703), and a 7-transmembrane region (aa 409-663, IPR003864/PF02714) characteristic of calcium-dependent channels. The full-length protein spans the CSC1/OSCA1-like family (IPR045122, aa 36-720) with GO annotation for calcium-activated cation channel activity (GO:0005227). AlphaFold predicts a well-folded structure with global pLDDT of 74.3, indicating confident structural modeling across most of the protein.\n\nHPA RNA-seq shows enriched expression in brain (53.9 nTPM) and pituitary gland (39.9 nTPM), with subcellular localization to centrosomes. This neural enrichment aligns with disease phenotype: ClinVar documents 23 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants causing Hereditary Spastic Paraplegia 87 (SPG87), a neurodegenerative disorder characterized by progressive lower limb spasticity. Variants include frameshift mutations (c.1216del/p.Trp406fs, c.1572dup/p.Tyr525fs, c.1641_1656delinsGGC/p.Asn547fs) and nonsense mutations (c.585C>G/p.Tyr195Ter) that likely cause loss-of-function through truncation.\n\nSTRING interaction network reveals high-confidence partners: INCENP (0.955, chromosomal passenger complex), KCNJ6 (0.844, G-protein activated inward rectifier potassium channel), and AURKB (0.796, Aurora kinase B). These interactions suggest TMEM63C may coordinate ion transport with cell division machinery at centrosomes, potentially linking calcium signaling to mitotic regulation in neural progenitors. Medium-confidence partners STRIP1, STRN (striatins), and SNX15 suggest connections to cytoskeletal organization and endosomal trafficking.\n\nConservation analysis shows orthologs in mouse (Q8CBX0, 802 aa) and zebrafish (X1WEM4, 825 aa), indicating strong evolutionary preservation of channel function across vertebrates. The mechanosensitive/osmosensitive nature of the CSC1/OSCA1 family suggests TMEM63C responds to membrane tension changes, potentially serving as a cellular volume regulator in neurons where osmotic stress occurs during excitotoxicity or ischemia.
