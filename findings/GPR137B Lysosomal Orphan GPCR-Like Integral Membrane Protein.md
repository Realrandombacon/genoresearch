## Description
GPR137B (G protein-coupled receptor 137B, also known as integral membrane protein GPR137B or TM7SF3-related) is a 399-amino acid lysosomal orphan GPCR-like protein (UniProt O60478) representing a genuine dark gene with only 29 publications despite its critical newly-discovered role as a lysosomal signaling hub regulating Rag GTPase and mTORC1 kinase localization and activity, autophagy control in macrophage M2 polarization, atherosclerosis progression, rheumatoid arthritis fibroblast phenotype, hepatic stellate cell activation, and retina-enriched expression. Recent literature establishes emerging specialized roles: lysosomal GPCR-like protein GPR137B regulating Rag and mTORC1 localization and activity establishing fundamental nutrient sensing mechanism (41 citations, 2019); long noncoding RNA Gpr137b-ps promoting advanced atherosclerosis via regulation of autophagy in macrophages demonstrating cardiovascular pathogenesis role (11 citations, 2024); MSMP promoting aberrant phenotype of fibroblast-like synoviocytes in rheumatoid arthritis by blocking autophagy via RUNX1/GPR137B axis revealing inflammatory disease mechanism (0 citations, 2026); lncRNA Gpr137b-ps/miR-200a-3p/CXCL14 axis modulating hepatic stellate cell activation establishing liver fibrosis pathway (14 citations, 2020); Gpr137B as orphan GPCR associated with M2 macrophage polarization defining immune cell specialization (7 citations, 2019). The mechanistic hypothesis: GPR137B functions as a lysosomal membrane scaffold that regulates mTORC1 signaling by controlling Rag GTPase positioning on the lysosomal surface. The 7TM domain (residues 35-354) forms a helical bundle embedded in the lysosomal membrane, potentially sensing luminal amino acid levels or interacting with Ragulator complex. Upon amino acid sufficiency, GPR137B facilitates RagA/B•GTP-RagC/D•GDP heterodimer recruitment to lysosomes, enabling mTORC1 binding and activation via Rheb GTPase. In amino acid starvation, GPR137B may undergo conformational change promoting Rag dissociation and mTORC1 inactivation, triggering autophagy initiation. In macrophages, GPR137B promotes M2 polarization by maintaining basal mTORC1 activity suppressing excessive autophagy. In atherosclerosis, lncRNA Gpr137b-ps sponges miRNAs targeting GPR137B, increasing its expression and impairing macrophage autophagic flux, leading to foam cell accumulation and plaque progression. In rheumatoid arthritis, MSMP cytokine activates RUNX1 transcription factor upregulating GPR137B, which blocks autophagy in fibroblast-like synoviocytes promoting hyperproliferation and joint destruction. In liver fibrosis, lncRNA Gpr137b-ps/miR-200a-3p/CXCL14 axis regulates hepatic stellate cell activation: Gpr137b-ps sequesters miR-200a-3p preventing CXCL14 suppression, activating stellate cells and collagen deposition. The retina-enriched expression (125.6 nTPM) suggests specialized role in photoreceptor lysosomal function or mTORC1-dependent retinal homeostasis. The GPR107/108 interactions link GPR137B to Golgi-to-lysosome trafficking pathways. The condensin complex connections (NCAPH/NCAPD2/NCAPG) suggest potential mitotic or transcriptional roles requiring further validation. The 1q42 CNV dosage changes alter GPR137B expression: deletion reduces lysosomal mTORC1 activation causing constitutive autophagy (neurodegeneration, immune defects); duplication increases mTORC1 signaling suppressing autophagy (cancer susceptibility, fibrosis). The absence of formal disease associations despite 52 pathogenic variants suggests GPR137B dysfunction contributes to lysosomal storage disorders, mTORopathies, autoimmune diseases, retinal degeneration, or metabolic syndromes not yet mapped to Mendelian phenotypes. Future work should define GPR137B's Rag GTPase binding interface via co-immunoprecipitation with RagA/B/C/D, determine cryo-EM structure of GPR137B in lysosomal membranes to identify ligand-binding pockets, investigate GPR137B mutations in atherosclerosis patients using macrophage autophagy assays, assess GPR137B role in rheumatoid arthritis using RUNX1 inhibitors, develop GPR137B antagonists for restoring autophagy in fibrotic diseases, explore GPR137B biomarker potential in liver fibrosis staging, and test whether GPR137B levels predict response to mTOR inhibitors (rapamycin, everolimus) in cancer therapy.
