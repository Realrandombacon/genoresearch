## Description
SLC10A7 (Q0GE19, 340 aa) is a critical Golgi-resident sodium/solute symporter that functions as a master regulator of protein O-GalNAc glycosylation and intracellular calcium homeostasis within the secretory pathway. InterPro identifies a canonical SLC10 architecture featuring a Sodium/Solute Symporter superfamily domain (IPR038770, G3DSA:1.20.1530.20, aa 1-323) and a specific SBF-like CPA transporter family domain (DUF4137, PF13593, aa 10-323), classifying it as a Putative Sodium Bile Acid Cotransporter (IPR016833) despite its actual role in glycosylation precursor transport. AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (global pLDDT 85.1, residues 1-340), revealing a compact bundle of transmembrane helices typical of the NSS fold, optimized for coupling sodium gradients to substrate uptake in the acidic Golgi environment. HPA classifies it as a predicted membrane protein involved in ion transport and symport, with low tissue specificity but detected in all tissues; crucially, it is linked to Amelogenesis Imperfecta, Dwarfism, and Skeletal Dysplasia. STRING reveals 10 interactions defining its functional network: SLC10A5 (0.793), SLC10A6 (0.738), TMEM165 (0.562, Golgi Ca2+/H+ exchanger), SLC35F5 (0.485), TGDS (0.453, dTDP-glucose 4,6-dehydratase), SLC35D1 (0.450, UDP-GlcNAc/UDP-Glc transporter), and other SLC10 family members (SLC10A4, A3, A2, A1). The strong link to TMEM165 and SLC35D1 confirms its role in maintaining the Golgi luminal environment necessary for glycosyltransferase activity. ClinVar contains 32 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including frameshifts (e.g., c.285del p.Leu95fs), nonsense mutations (c.184G>T p.Glu62Ter), and large copy number gains/losses on chromosome 4q; these cause Short Stature, Amelogenesis Imperfecta, and Skeletal Dysplasia with Scoliosis (SSASKS), also known as SLC10A7-CDG. Functional hypothesis: SLC10A7 acts as a sodium-dependent transporter importing specific substrates (potentially nucleotide sugars or calcium-regulating ions) into the Golgi lumen. By maintaining optimal luminal conditions, it ensures the proper function of GalNAc-transferases (ppGalNAc-Ts) responsible for initiating mucin-type O-glycosylation. Loss of SLC10A7 disrupts this process, leading to hypoglycosylation of secreted proteins and extracellular matrix components, which manifests as defective enamel formation (amelogenesis imperfecta) and impaired bone growth (dwarfism/scoliosis). Additionally, recent studies suggest it modulates Golgi calcium levels, interacting functionally with TMEM165 to prevent calcium overload that could inhibit glycosyltransferases. Conservation across vertebrates confirms its essential evolutionary role in secretory pathway fidelity. NOTE: This gene represents a 'dark' but mechanistically defined cause of congenital disorders of glycosylation.
