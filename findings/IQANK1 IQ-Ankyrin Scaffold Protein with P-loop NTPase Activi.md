# IQANK1: IQ-Ankyrin Scaffold Protein with P-loop NTPase Activity in Nuclear-Cytoplasmic Regulation

**Date:** 2026-03-19T12:59:17.736704

**Quality Score:** 2.88/10  (E=4.65, D=1.1) [WEAK]

## Description
IQANK1 (Gene ID: 642574, UniProt A8MXQ7) encodes a 560 amino acid multidomain scaffold protein combining an N-terminal IQ calmodulin-binding motif (PS50096, aa 62-91), central ankyrin repeat scaffold (IPR002110/PF00023, aa 191-275), and C-terminal P-loop NTP hydrolase domain (IPR027417, aa 411-549). AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 79.6) confirms a well-folded, stable conformation. The protein belongs to Panther family PTHR24171 (Multifunctional E3 Ligases and Regulators, aa 98-374), suggesting ubiquitination regulatory capacity. HPA shows tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in esophagus (39.7 nTPM) and skin (27.0 nTPM), detected across many tissues with low cancer specificity. STRING interactions reveal connections to spectrin cytoskeleton (SPTBN1/SPTBN4/SPTBN5, scores 0.473), transcriptional regulators (ZNF280A 0.601, ZMYND10 0.447, RIPPLY3 0.529), membrane transporter MFSD4A (0.571), and chaperone DNAJC12 (0.455). ClinVar contains 25 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly copy number gains/losses spanning 8q21.2-q24.3, though no specific Mendelian disease is assigned. Conservation is evident with mouse ortholog Iqank1 (540 aa, UniProt Q3TYL0). Functional hypothesis: IQANK1 operates as a calcium-regulated scaffold coordinating nuclear-cytoplasmic trafficking and protein stability. The IQ motif binds calmodulin in response to calcium fluxes, triggering conformational changes that modulate ankyrin-mediated protein-protein interactions. The P-loop domain likely hydrolyzes ATP/GTP to drive nucleocytoplasmic transport or chromatin remodeling. Interaction with spectrins suggests membrane-cytoskeleton anchoring, while ZNF/ZMYND partners implicate transcriptional regulation. The E3 ligase family association implies ubiquitin-dependent protein turnover control. Disease-associated CNVs may disrupt dosage-sensitive regulatory functions in epithelial tissues (esophagus/skin), potentially contributing to developmental or oncogenic phenotypes through dysregulated protein stability and nuclear transport.
