# TICAM2: Endosomal TIR Domain Adapter Bridging Toll-Like Receptors to TRAF6-IRF3 Signaling in Innate Immunity with Roles in Sepsis-Induced Monocyte Exhaustion and Neutrophil Dysfunction

**Date:** 2026-06-14T18:37:44.258132

**Quality Score:** 9/10 (EXCELLENT)

**Confidence:** MEDIUM

## Description
TICAM2 (Q86XR7) encodes a 235 amino acid TIR domain-containing adapter molecule that functions as a critical signaling hub in endosomal Toll-like receptor (TLR) pathways, specifically bridging TLR3 and TLR4 activation to downstream TRAF6-IRF3 transcriptional programs controlling type I interferon production and inflammatory cytokine expression. InterPro analysis reveals a canonical TIR domain architecture (IPR000157/PF13676, positions 73-229) embedded within the TIR domain superfamily (IPR035897, positions 70-227) and TICAM1/2-specific family (IPR046946, positions 27-233), with the TIR domain mediating homo- and hetero-typic interactions through BB loop interfaces essential for signalosome assembly. AlphaFold predicts a well-folded structure (pLDDT 74.4, high confidence) consistent with stable TIR domain folding required for precise protein-protein interaction geometry. STRING interactions demonstrate exclusive partnerships with innate immunity components: TRAF6 (0.984), the primary E3 ubiquitin ligase activating NF-κB; TLR4 (0.947), the LPS receptor; TICAM1/TRIF (0.908), the related adapter for TLR3; IRF3 (0.897), the interferon regulatory factor; SARM1 (0.882), a negative regulator of TIR signaling; TLR3 (0.856), the dsRNA receptor; TIRAP/Mal (0.774), the MyD88 adapter for TLR2/4; TLR9 (0.655), the CpG DNA receptor; RIPK1 (0.521), regulating apoptosis/necroptosis; and TLR2 (0.482), placing TICAM2 at the center of endosomal TLR signal transduction. HPA shows ubiquitous expression across tissues with low specificity, classified under immunity/inflammatory response/innate immunity biological processes with intracellular localization. ClinVar contains 25 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number losses spanning chromosome 5q15-23.2 regions (e.g., chr5:93828571-123711334 deletions, chr5:112557391-128106299 deletions) and duplications (chr5:89949118-129317455 gains), though specific monogenic disease associations remain undefined. Literature (67 papers) demonstrates TICAM2 ablation facilitates monocyte exhaustion recovery after sepsis, mediates neutrophil exhaustion in severe systemic inflammatory syndrome, and participates in STING-TICAM2-IRF3-IDO1 signalosome during Toxoplasma gondii infection via the tryptophan-kynurenine pathway. The mechanistic hypothesis: TICAM2 functions as an endosomal adapter recruited to activated TLR3/TLR4 complexes through TIR-TIR homotypic interactions, nucleating assembly of a TRAF6-IRF3 signalosome that drives type I interferon production distinct from MyD88-dependent pathways; during sepsis, sustained TICAM2 signaling contributes to monocyte and neutrophil exhaustion phenotypes characterized by impaired cytokine production and bacterial clearance; the TICAM2-IRF3 axis couples with IDO1 induction to suppress anti-parasitic immunity during Toxoplasma infection through β-catenin-dependent mechanisms; copy number losses on 5q may cause haploinsufficiency leading to immunodeficiency phenotypes with impaired antiviral defense, while duplications may predispose to autoimmune/inflammatory conditions through excessive interferon production; SARM1 interaction suggests negative feedback regulation preventing excessive inflammation; the high-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 74.4) indicates the TIR domain adopts the canonical five-stranded β-sheet fold enabling BB loop-mediated oligomerization essential for signal propagation.
