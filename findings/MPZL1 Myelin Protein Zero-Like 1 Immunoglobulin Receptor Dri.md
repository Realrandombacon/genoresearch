# MPZL1: Myelin Protein Zero-Like 1 Immunoglobulin Receptor Driving Cancer Progression via TGF-β1 and HGF/MET Signaling with Unmapped Clinical Phenotypes

## Description
MPZL1 (Myelin Protein Zero-Like 1) is a 269-amino acid type I transmembrane protein (UniProt O95297) representing a genuine dark gene with only 55 publications despite its critical emerging roles in cancer metastasis and immune modulation. Structurally, MPZL1 contains a canonical immunoglobulin V-set domain (IPR013106/PF07686/SM00406, residues 42-155) characteristic of cell adhesion molecules and receptors, embedded within the broader Myelin P0-related family (IPR000920/PTHR13869, residues 17-266). The Ig-like fold (G3DSA:2.60.40.10/SSF48726) suggests a role in homophilic or heterophilic protein-protein interactions at the cell surface. AlphaFold predicts high-confidence structure (pLDDT 76.4), consistent with a well-folded extracellular Ig domain connected to a transmembrane helix and short cytoplasmic tail, typical of signaling receptors that undergo conformational changes upon ligand binding. Functionally, MPZL1 localizes to the plasma membrane and vesicles with ubiquitous expression across all tissues (HPA: detected in all, low tissue specificity) but shows immune cell-enriched patterns in blood, suggesting dual roles in neural/epithelial adhesion and immune regulation. STRING interactions reveal strong connections to myelin components and signaling regulators: PTPN11 (0.954, SHP2 phosphatase—critical for RAS/MAPK signaling), RNMT (0.901, RNA guanine-N7 methyltransferase), GJB1 (0.864, Connexin 32 gap junction protein), MBP (0.859, Myelin Basic Protein), SIRPA (0.822, Signal Regulatory Protein alpha immune checkpoint), MAG (0.821, Myelin-Associated Glycoprotein), PRX (0.634, Periaxin), PMP22 (0.521, Peripheral Myelin Protein 22), GDAP1 (0.507, Ganglioside-induced differentiation protein), and EGR2 (0.486, Early Growth Response 2 transcription factor). Critically, ClinVar documents 21 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number gains/losses spanning 1q21-25 (multiple entries affecting megabase regions) and intronic/synonymous SNVs (c.473-4C>G, c.282A>G p.Gln94=), yet ZERO diseases are formally associated—a remarkable gap given the cancer and neuropathy links. Recent literature establishes MPZL1 as a potent oncogenic driver: repurposing passenger amplifications identifies MPZL1 as a target for solid cancers (2025); promotes lung adenocarcinoma progression by enhancing proliferation, invasion, migration, and suppressing immune function via TGF-β1 signaling (7 citations, 2023); suppresses cancer stem-like properties in lung cancer through β-catenin/TCF4 modulation (4 citations, 2023); acts as an HGF/MET signaling amplifier promoting glioblastoma migration and invasion (5 citations, 2023); and serves as a target for novel concabody immunotherapies enhancing antibody-induced cell death in B-cell malignancies (2026). The mechanistic hypothesis: MPZL1 functions as a context-dependent immunoglobulin superfamily receptor that modulates TGF-β1 and HGF/MET signaling cascades to regulate epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT), cell motility, and immune evasion. In cancer, MPZL1 overexpression activates downstream effectors (SHP2/PTPN11, β-catenin) to drive metastasis and suppress anti-tumor immunity. The strong interaction with myelin proteins (MBP, MAG, PMP22) suggests potential roles in peripheral nerve integrity, where loss-of-function could contribute to Charcot-Marie-Tooth-like neuropathies not yet mapped to MPZL1. The 1q21 copy number variant syndromes may include MPZL1 dosage effects contributing to neurodevelopmental or autoimmune phenotypes. Future work should identify the specific ligand(s) binding the MPZL1 Ig domain, define phosphorylation sites in the cytoplasmic tail, and screen patients with 1q21 CNVs or unexplained neuropathies for MPZL1 mutations.
