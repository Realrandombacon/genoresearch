## Description
OR2A5 (Olfactory receptor family 2 subfamily A member 5, UniProt Q96R48) is a 311-amino acid integral membrane protein located on chromosome 7q35 within the olfactory receptor gene cluster. InterPro analysis reveals canonical class A G protein-coupled receptor (GPCR) architecture: olfactory receptor domain (IPR000725/PF13853, aa 30-306) embedded within rhodopsin-like 7-transmembrane helix bundle (IPR000276/IPR017452/G3DSA:1.20.1070.10, aa 21-311/25-49/58-79/104-126) forming the characteristic serpentine fold with extracellular N-terminus and intracellular C-terminus. GO annotations confirm olfactory receptor activity (GO:0004984), G protein-coupled receptor signaling pathway (GO:0007186), and membrane localization (GO:0016020). AlphaFold structure prediction achieves high confidence (pLDDT 88.4), indicating a well-folded stable 7TM structure optimal for odorant binding and G protein activation. Human Protein Atlas shows not detected expression in bulk tissues — consistent with highly restricted expression in olfactory sensory neurons of the nasal epithelium where individual ORs exhibit monoallelic, neuron-specific expression patterns below detection thresholds of bulk RNA-seq. STRING interaction network reveals 10 partners representing the canonical olfactory signaling cascade: GNAL (0.788, Gαolf subunit — highest confidence interaction confirming direct G protein coupling), ARRB2/ARRB1 (0.683 each, arrestins mediating receptor desensitization and internalization), GNG7 (0.678, Gβγ subunit), PRKACA/PRKACB (0.650/0.649, cAMP-dependent protein kinase catalytic subunits), GRK2/GRK3 (0.569/0.547, G protein-coupled receptor kinases), GNG13 (0.566, Gγ subunit), and GNB1 (0.503, Gβ1 subunit). This interactome confirms OR2A5 functions within the canonical cAMP-mediated olfactory transduction pathway identical to other OR family members. ClinVar contains 53 pathogenic variants consisting predominantly of large copy-number gains and losses across 7q33-q36.3, demonstrating dosage sensitivity where genomic rearrangements disrupting chromosome 7q cause disease phenotypes potentially affecting olfactory function and neurodevelopment. Conservation across mammals (rat D4AEF3, 310 aa; dog A0A8C0RET1, 310 aa) indicates ancient evolutionary origin tied to fundamental chemosensory mechanisms. OR2A5 likely functions as an odorant-activated GPCR, using its 7TM bundle to bind specific volatile ligands in the olfactory epithelium while activating Gαolf to stimulate adenylyl cyclase, increase cAMP, open cyclic nucleotide-gated channels, and depolarize olfactory sensory neurons — initiating action potentials that transmit smell information to the olfactory bulb, with potential extra-nasal functions in non-olfactory tissues where ectopic OR expression regulates processes like sperm chemotaxis, wound healing, and cancer metastasis.
