## Description
LRP12 encodes an 859 aa low-density lipoprotein receptor-related protein (UniProt Q9Y561) functioning as a transmembrane scaffold in endocytosis, NOTCH signaling modulation, and repeat expansion-associated neurodegeneration. The protein contains multiple CUB domains (IPR000859/PF00431, aa47-159/259-372) for protein-protein interactions, three LDL receptor class A repeats (IPR002172/PF00057, aa165-202/214-256/374-412) for ligand binding, and belongs to the LDL receptor-like superfamily (IPR036055). AlphaFold prediction shows medium confidence (pLDDT 62.5), indicating a partially structured transmembrane receptor with flexible linker regions. HPA shows ubiquitous expression (detected in all tissues) with immune cell enrichment, localizing to nucleoli fibrillar center, plasma membrane, and mitochondria—consistent with roles in endocytosis and nuclear signaling. STRING interactions reveal connections to NOTCH2NLC (0.722, high confidence), NOTCH2NLA (0.609), extracellular matrix proteins HSPG2/perlecan (0.577) and AGRN/agrin (0.575), endocytic adaptor GIPC1 (0.529), paralog LRP11 (0.490), and lipoprotein carriers APOE (0.477)/APOB (0.463). ClinVar contains 24 pathogenic variants including CGG repeat expansions, splice site mutations, and 8q copy number gains/losses. Literature (92 papers) demonstrates LRP12's emerging role in oculopharyngodistal myopathy (OPDM) and ALS28 through CGG repeat expansions in the 5'UTR causing RNA toxicity and nuclear inclusions, NOTCH2NLC interaction modulating neurogenesis, cancer metastasis via β-catenin/PI3K/AKT activation in triple-negative breast cancer and gastric cancer, and M2 macrophage-mediated immunosuppression. LRP12 likely functions as an endocytic receptor coordinating ligand internalization (lipoproteins, ECM proteins) with NOTCH pathway regulation through NOTCH2NLC sequestration. CGG repeat expansions cause pathogenic RNA foci formation disrupting nucleolar function and mitochondrial homeostasis, explaining the neurodegenerative phenotype. The dual localization (plasma membrane + nucleoli) suggests LRP12 undergoes regulated intramembrane proteolysis releasing an intracellular domain for nuclear signaling, analogous to NOTCH and LDLR family members.
