## Description
FILIP1 (UniProt Q7Z7B0) encodes a 1213 aa intracellular protein functioning as a critical regulator of actin cytoskeleton dynamics and neuronal migration by promoting the proteasomal degradation of filamin A (FLNA) and linking myofibrils to microtubules. InterPro analysis reveals a specialized domain architecture: an N-terminal cortactin-binding protein-2 domain (IPR019131/PF09727, residues 74-255) mediating interactions with the actin nucleation promoting factor cortactin, and a large C-terminal region belonging to the cortactin-binding and actin dynamics regulator family (IPR050719/PTHR23166, residues 376-1187) essential for cytoskeletal remodeling. AlphaFold predicts a medium confidence structure (pLDDT 66.6) consistent with a hybrid architecture featuring structured domains interspersed with extensive intrinsically disordered regions, typical of scaffold proteins that undergo conformational changes upon binding partners or post-translational modifications. STRING interactions define a high-confidence network centered on cytoskeletal organization: FLNA (0.980, primary substrate for degradation), RHOD (0.711, Rho GTPase regulating actin), WHAMM (0.611, actin nucleation on Golgi), STRIP2 (0.591, striatin-interacting phosphatase complex), SENP6 (0.590, SUMO protease), ERVW-1 (0.568, syncytin-1), FLNC (0.524, filamin C muscle isoform), KLHL42 (0.507, E3 ligase adaptor), TMEM30A (0.496, lipid flippase subunit), and STRN3 (0.495, striatin-3). HPA classifies FILIP1 as tissue-enhanced with highest expression in heart muscle (50.3 nTPM), tongue (45.6 nTPM), and skeletal muscle (33.8 nTPM), localized to plasma membrane and actin filaments, involved in disease processes, reflecting its dual role in cardiac/skeletal muscle integrity and cortical neuron migration. ClinVar lists 20 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number losses (6q14.1 chr6:75295879-75362949x0, 6q12-14.1 chr6:66523005-81798980x1, 6q12-14.1 chr6:64954687-79581678) and truncating mutations (p.Glu347Ter, p.Arg232Ter, p.Arg57Ter, p.Arg889Ter, p.Glu155Ter) alongside missense variants (p.Pro1133Leu); recent 2023 literature confirms bi-allelic variants cause a novel congenital myopathy syndrome featuring dysmorphism and neurological defects, while paternal uniparental disomy 6 (UPD6) leads to phenotypic blending. Functional hypothesis: FILIP1 operates through a multi-step mechanism: (1) Complex assembly - N-terminal domain binds cortactin/actin networks while C-terminal region recruits FLNA; (2) Substrate targeting - Direct interaction with FLNA (and FLNC in muscle) marks these large actin-crosslinking proteins for ubiquitin-proteasome degradation, regulating their steady-state levels; (3) Cytoskeletal coupling - Links actin filaments to microtubules via interactions with WHAMM and RHOD, facilitating coordinated cytoskeletal remodeling during cell migration and muscle contraction; (4) Mechanical stress response - Recent 2024 studies show protein phosphatase-1 regulates FILIP1-FLNC binding under mechanical stress in skeletal muscle, suggesting a feedback loop where tension modulates degradation rates; (5) Neuronal migration - In developing cortex, controlled FLNA turnover by FILIP1 is essential for growth cone dynamics and neuronal positioning; loss leads to reduced dendritic spine length and altered excitatory signaling (mouse models); (6) Disease mechanism - Haploinsufficiency or biallelic loss causes accumulation of toxic FLNA aggregates or failure to remodel cytoskeleton, leading to muscle weakness (myopathy), cardiac defects, and neurodevelopmental delays; specific truncating mutations likely cause complete loss of function, while missense variants may disrupt specific interfaces (e.g., p.Pro1133Leu in C-terminal regulatory region). Conservation extends to mouse (Q9CS72, 1214 aa) and rat (Q8K4T4, 1212 aa) indicating preserved role in mammalian cytoskeletal dynamics. Dark aspects requiring investigation include: precise E3 ubiquitin ligase recruited by FILIP1 for FLNA degradation (potential link to KLHL42 or other Cullin complexes), structural basis of cortactin-binding domain interaction with actin patches, role in cardiac hypertrophy or heart failure given high heart expression, potential involvement in cancer metastasis via cytoskeletal remodeling, crosstalk between SUMOylation (SENP6 interaction) and ubiquitination pathways, impact of mechanical force on FILIP1 conformation and activity, and therapeutic strategies to modulate FILIP1 activity in myopathies or neuronal migration disorders.
