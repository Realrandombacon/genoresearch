## Description
MICAL2 (Microtubule Associated Monooxygenase, Calponin and LIM Domain Containing 2) is a partially characterized redox enzyme with 89 publications, emerging as a critical regulator of cytoskeletal dynamics through direct oxidation of actin filaments, driving endosomal fission, axon guidance, and cancer metastasis. This massive 1957-amino acid multi-domain protein (UniProt O94851) features a unique modular architecture: an N-terminal FAD-binding monooxygenase domain (IPR002938/IPR036188, positions 2-494) responsible for catalytic activity, a central calponin homology (CH) domain (IPR001715/G3DSA:1.10.418.10, positions 516-622) for actin binding, a C-terminal LIM zinc finger domain (IPR001781/G3DSA:2.10.110.10, positions 1000-1062) for protein-protein interactions, a ProQ/FinO RNA-binding domain (IPR016103, positions 1627-1721), and a bMERB dimerization domain (IPR022735, positions 1796-1945). This network positions MICAL2 as a downstream effector of Semaphorin-Plexin signaling that couples receptor activation to rapid actin disassembly via localized ROS production.\n\nHPA shows tissue-enhanced expression specifically in the brain (138.2 nTPM), with additional detection in immune cells. Subcellular localization includes nucleoplasm, plasma membrane, and cytosol, consistent with roles in both nuclear signaling and peripheral cytoskeletal dynamics. Molecular functions include actin-binding, monooxygenase, and oxidoreductase activities. ClinVar contains 18 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including missense mutations (p.Asn1549Ser, p.Ala388Val), splice deletions, and large copy number gains on chromosome 11p15.5-14.2, though specific monogenic diseases remain uncharacterized. Conservation across mouse (1951 aa) and rat (1948 aa) indicates strong functional constraint.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: MICAL2 functions as a redox-dependent 'molecular scalpel' that selectively severs F-actin filaments to drive membrane remodeling events essential for cell migration and neuronal development. Upon Semaphorin binding to Plexin receptors, MICAL2 is recruited to the plasma membrane where its monooxygenase domain generates local reactive oxygen species (ROS) that directly oxidize specific methionine residues on actin, causing filament disassembly. The CH domain anchors MICAL2 to actin bundles, while the LIM domain recruits signaling partners like NEDD9 to amplify migratory signals. Recent studies link MICAL2 to endosomal fission, suggesting it may sever actin coats surrounding endosomes to facilitate cargo sorting. In cancer (pancreatic, ovarian), MICAL2 overexpression drives metastasis by enhancing invadopodia formation and EMT through sustained cytoskeletal turnover. The ProQ/FinO domain suggests potential RNA-binding capabilities, possibly coordinating cytoskeletal changes with translational control of migration-related transcripts. Pathogenic variants disrupting the catalytic core or LIM domain may cause neurodevelopmental defects or immune dysfunction due to impaired axon guidance or leukocyte trafficking.
