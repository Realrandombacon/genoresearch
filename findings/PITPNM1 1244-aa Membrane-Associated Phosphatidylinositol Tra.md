## Description
PITPNM1 (Phosphatidylinositol Transfer Protein Membrane-Associated 1; UniProt O00562) is a large 1244 amino acid lipid transfer protein with only 16 publications, classifying it as a genuine dark gene despite essential roles in phosphoinositide trafficking, ER-plasma membrane contact site formation, and cancer progression.\n\nSTRUCTURAL ARCHITECTURE: InterPro analysis reveals a complex multi-domain architecture specialized for lipid binding and transfer. The N-terminal region contains a lipid-binding SRPBCC domain (cd08889, residues 1-257) characteristic of Class IIA phosphatidylinositol transfer proteins (PITPs), embedded within a START-like superfamily fold (IPR023393/G3DSA:3.30.530.20, residues 1-267) that forms the hydrophobic pocket for phosphatidylinositol (PI) extraction from membranes. A central DDHD domain (IPR004177/PF02862, residues 686-880) confers metal ion binding capacity (GO:0046872) and may regulate enzymatic activity or membrane association. The C-terminal region contains a HAD (haloacid dehalogenase) superfamily domain (IPR023214/G3DSA:3.40.50.1000, residues 1016-1125) and an LNS2/PITP domain (IPR031315, residues 1023-1154), suggesting potential phosphatase activity or regulatory phosphorylation sites. The protein belongs to the PITP family (IPR001666/PF02121, residues 1-1225/1-249). AlphaFold predicts medium confidence structure (global pLDDT 69.9), indicating a protein with well-folded catalytic domains connected by flexible/linker regions, consistent with dynamic membrane tethering functions.\n\nINTERACTION NETWORK: STRING-DB identifies exclusively high-confidence interactions with ER-resident lipid transfer proteins and signaling scaffolds: VAPB (0.948, vesicle-associated membrane protein-associated protein B, ER anchor for lipid transfer), PTK2B (0.944, proline-rich tyrosine kinase 2 beta, focal adhesion kinase), VAPA (0.901, VAMP-associated protein A), OCM2 (0.866, oncomodulin-2, calcium-binding), OCM (0.826, oncomodulin), SLC26A5 (0.775, prestin, motor protein), RHOA (0.739, Ras homolog family member A, small GTPase), PDE6B (0.696, phosphodiesterase 6B), CERT1 (0.695, ceramide transporter 1), and OSBP (0.642, oxysterol-binding protein). This interaction profile establishes PITPNM1 as a core component of ER-plasma membrane contact sites, where it cooperates with VAP proteins to extract PI from the ER and deliver it to the plasma membrane for conversion to PIP2/PIP3, while simultaneously regulating RhoA signaling and focal adhesion dynamics via PTK2B.\n\nEXPRESSION & LOCALIZATION: HPA shows ubiquitous expression detected in all tissues with low tissue and cancer specificity, consistent with fundamental requirements for phosphoinositide homeostasis in all cell types. Subcellular localization includes vesicles and cytosol, reflecting its shuttling role between ER and plasma membrane. Biological processes include protein transport and general transport. Classified as a predicted intracellular protein and transporter with cell type-enhanced single cell specificity.\n\nDISEASE LANDSCAPE: ClinVar contains 16 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number gains at 11q12.1-13.3, 11q12.2-13.5, and 11p13-q25 encompassing PITPNM1, plus coding/splice variants such as c.2317+1G>A (splice donor mutation) and c.3343-18T>G (intronic). No monogenic disease is yet definitively assigned, but 11q CNVs cause developmental anomalies. Recent literature establishes oncogenic roles: PITPNM1 attenuation inhibits breast cancer progression (2021), likely by disrupting PIP3-Akt signaling required for invasion. In osteoarthritis, miR-490-5p inhibition promotes chondrogenesis via the PITPNM1/PI3K/AKT axis (2020), suggesting PITPNM1 drives cartilage degeneration through excessive PI3K activation. Despite expression in developing hair cells, Pitpnm1 knockout mice show no hearing deficits (2013), indicating functional redundancy with PITPNM2.\n\nCONSERVATION: Orthologs confirmed in mouse (O35954, 1243 aa) and rat (Q5U2N3, 1242 aa), indicating strong evolutionary constraint on the SRPBCC-DDHD-HAD modular architecture across mammals.\n\nFUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: PITPNM1 functions as a non-vesicular lipid transfer protein that extracts phosphatidylinositol (PI) from the ER membrane via its N-terminal SRPBCC/START domain and delivers it to the plasma membrane at ER-PM contact sites anchored by VAPB/VAPA. At the plasma membrane, PI is phosphorylated by PI4K and PIP5K to generate PIP2, which serves as a substrate for PLC (calcium signaling) and PI3K (Akt survival signaling). The DDHD domain may coordinate metal ions essential for lipid binding or regulate interaction with RhoA to couple lipid transfer to cytoskeletal remodeling. The C-terminal HAD domain could provide regulatory phosphatase activity to reset the transfer cycle or dephosphorylate specific substrates. In breast cancer, PITPNM1 overexpression amplifies PIP3-Akt signaling, driving proliferation and metastasis. In osteoarthritis, elevated PITPNM1 activates PI3K-AKT in chondrocytes, promoting catabolic gene expression and apoptosis. The 16 ClinVar variants warrant investigation for neurodevelopmental disorders involving defective phosphoinositide signaling, potentially overlapping with disorders of ER-PM contact site dysfunction.
