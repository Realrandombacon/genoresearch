## Description
TMEM272 (Gene ID: 440839, UniProt A0A1B0GTI8) is a 187 amino acid predicted transmembrane protein located on chromosome 13q12.11 (NC_000013.11). InterPro domain analysis identifies TMEM272 as a member of the specific Transmembrane protein 272 family (IPR040350/PTHR33444, positions 8-183), related to the SI:DKEY-19B23.12 protein family, suggesting evolutionary conservation of this small membrane protein across vertebrates. No classical catalytic domains or well-characterized structural motifs (e.g., zinc fingers, LRRs) are present, indicating a potentially novel fold or a specialized regulatory role. AlphaFold structure prediction (AF-A0A1B0GTI8-F1) yields a high-confidence model (global pLDDT 82.6), depicting a compact, well-folded structure likely containing one or more transmembrane helices consistent with its classification as a membrane protein. Human Protein Atlas classifies TMEM272 as a predicted intracellular and membrane protein. Expression is highly tissue-enriched in the brain (13.8 nTPM), with detection limited to some tissues; notably, it is immune cell-enriched at the single-cell level, suggesting specific roles in neuronal and immune contexts. STRING database reveals 5 interaction partners of moderate confidence: ASB9 (score 0.524, ankyrin repeat and SOCS box-containing 9 - E3 ubiquitin ligase substrate recognizer), BAIAP2L2 (0.520, BAR/IMD domain adapter involved in membrane curvature), NTM (0.439, neurotrimin - GPI-anchored neural adhesion molecule), C7 (0.420, complement component 7), and BCO1 (0.415, beta-carotene oxygenase 1). The association with ASB9 suggests potential regulation via ubiquitination/proteasomal degradation, while links to BAIAP2L2 and NTM imply involvement in membrane dynamics, cytoskeletal organization, or neuronal adhesion/synapse formation. The connection to C7 hints at a possible, albeit unexpected, role in complement regulation or immune surveillance within the CNS. ClinVar lists 30 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains and losses spanning 13q11-q34 (multi-megabase regions affecting many genes), plus one missense variant (c.216G>T, p.Leu72Phe) of uncertain significance within the conserved region. No specific monogenic disease is yet attributed solely to TMEM272, but dosage sensitivity of the 13q region is linked to developmental delays, intellectual disability, and retinoblastoma (RB1 locus adjacent). Conservation is implied by the PanTHer family classification. Functional hypothesis: TMEM272 functions as a small transmembrane regulator of membrane trafficking or signal transduction in neurons and immune cells. It may act as a co-factor for ASB9-mediated ubiquitination of specific substrates involved in synaptic plasticity or immune receptor turnover. Interaction with BAIAP2L2 suggests a role in shaping membrane curvature during vesicle formation or filopodia extension. Disease mechanism: Haploinsufficiency or overexpression due to 13q CNVs disrupts neuronal development or immune homeostasis. The p.Leu72Phe variant may alter protein stability or membrane insertion. Priority experiments include subcellular localization studies (confocal microscopy), co-IP/MS to validate ASB9/BAIAP2L2 interactions and identify substrates, CRISPR knockout in neuronal/immune cell lines to assess effects on morphology and signaling, and investigation of TMEM272's role in complement regulation.
