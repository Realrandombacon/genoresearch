## Description
NPIPB4 (Nuclear pore complex-interacting protein family member B4, UniProt C9JG80) is a 1138-amino acid intracellular protein that functions as a component of the nuclear pore complex (NPC) interaction network. InterPro analysis reveals defining NPIP family architecture: N-terminal NPIP domain (IPR009443/PF06409/PTHR15438, aa 2-335/41-243) characteristic of nuclear pore complex-interacting proteins, plus an internal MII repeat domain (IPR048893/PF20885, aa 622-788) found in NPIPB13-like proteins suggesting protein-protein interaction capability, and an N-terminal NPIP domain (IPR054697, aa 41-243) — these domains collectively place NPIPB4 within the NPIP family (NPIPB1-16) that localize to the nuclear envelope and interact with nucleoporins. AlphaFold structure prediction achieves very low confidence (pLDDT 39.9), indicating a predominantly intrinsically disordered protein (IDP) with minimal stable secondary structure — this architecture is characteristic of nucleoporin-associated proteins that function through flexible protein-protein interactions rather than enzymatic catalysis, enabling conformational sampling and binding to multiple NPC components during nucleocytoplasmic transport cycles. Human Protein Atlas contains no expression data for NPIPB4 (ENSG00000283855 not in database), limiting tissue distribution insights, though NPIP family members typically show ubiquitous or testis-enriched expression patterns. STRING interaction network reveals 5 partners with moderate confidence scores: SIAE (0.512, sialate O-acetylesterase — lysosomal enzyme, potential trafficking substrate), DEPDC4 (0.448, DEP domain-containing protein 4 — mTOR signaling regulator), KRTAP4-8 and KRTAP5-7 (0.447/0.420, keratin-associated proteins — cytoskeletal components), and MTRNR2L3 (0.445, mitochondrial-derived peptide humanin-like 3 — stress response). The limited interactome suggests NPIPB4 may function primarily through direct NPC binding rather than stable cytoplasmic complexes. ClinVar contains 54 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants consisting predominantly of copy number variations (deletions and duplications) spanning chromosome 16p12.2 region (16p12.2, 16p13.11-12.1, 16p12.2-11.2) — these CNVs encompass multiple genes including the NPIPB gene cluster and are associated with developmental disorders, intellectual disability, and craniofacial anomalies, though no specific disease is attributed solely to NPIPB4. Conservation across the NPIP family indicates primate-specific expansion with 16 human paralogs (NPIPB1-16) clustered on chromosome 16p11.2-p12.2, suggesting recent evolutionary adaptation of nuclear transport regulation. NPIPB4 likely functions as an intrinsically disordered scaffold protein that binds nucleoporins through its NPIP domains, facilitating cargo receptor docking, translocation, or release at the nuclear pore complex — the MII repeats potentially mediating homo/hetero-oligomerization with other NPIPB family members, with substantial CNV burden causing developmental phenotypes through combined disruption of nucleocytoplasmic transport, gene regulation, and multiple co-deleted genes essential for neurodevelopment.
