## Description
SYT15 (Synaptotagmin-15, also known as CHR10SYT, sytXV) is a 421 amino acid membrane protein (UniProt Q9BQS2) with high-confidence well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 76.3), characteristic of the Synaptotagmin family of Ca2+ sensors and vesicle docking factors. The protein belongs to the Synaptotagmin family (PTHR10024, residues 6-415) and contains two tandem C2 domains: a C2A domain (IPR000008/PF00168, residues 147-265) and a C2 domain (IPR035892/SSF49562, residues 278-400), along with specific Synaptotagmin 15/17 C2A domain signatures (cd08390, cd08409, G3DSA:2.60.40.150). The presence of GPRIN2 and NPY4R suggests SYT15 functions in neuroendocrine or paracrine signaling, potentially regulating vesicle release in response to neuropeptide or GPCR signaling. SNAP25 interaction confirms its role in SNARE-mediated membrane fusion, likely in non-neuronal secretory cells given the gene summary.\n\nHPA expression reveals ubiquitous detection across many tissues with low tissue specificity, consistent with a housekeeping role in the secretory pathway. Subcellular localization is predicted at the Golgi apparatus, which is the primary site of protein sorting and vesicle formation for the secretory pathway. The Golgi localization directly supports the predicted membrane trafficking function. Protein class includes predicted intracellular and membrane proteins.\n\nClinVar documents 51 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains and losses affecting chromosome 10q (10q11.22-11.23 duplications, 10q11.22-11.23 deletions, 10p14-q26.3 gains). The high variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity. While no specific Mendelian disease is annotated, 10q CNVs are associated with developmental delay, autism spectrum disorders, and intellectual disability. Given SYT15's role in vesicle docking and secretion, dosage imbalance may disrupt neuroendocrine signaling, impair synaptic plasticity, or cause developmental defects in tissues reliant on precise secretory dynamics.\n\nConservation is strong across vertebrates: human (Q9BQS2, 421 aa), mouse (Q8C6N3, 418 aa), rat (P59926, 422 aa), indicating essential function in membrane trafficking.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: SYT15 functions as a Golgi-resident Ca2+ sensor that regulates vesicle docking and fusion in the secretory pathway. Upon Ca2+ influx, the tandem C2 domains bind phospholipids (e.g., PIP2) and recruit vesicles to the Golgi or plasma membrane, facilitating SNARE-mediated fusion (via SNAP25). Interaction with GPRIN2 and NPY4R suggests SYT15 acts as a downstream effector in neuroendocrine signaling, potentially coupling GPCR activation to vesicle release. Loss of SYT15 causes defective vesicle trafficking, impaired secretion, and disrupted signaling, leading to developmental phenotypes associated with 10q CNVs. SYT15 represents a key regulator of non-neuronal secretory dynamics and a potential contributor to neurodevelopmental disorders.
