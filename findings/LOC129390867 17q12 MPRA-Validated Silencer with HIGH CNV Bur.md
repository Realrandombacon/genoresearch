## Description
LOC129390867 is a 200bp cis-regulatory element (CRE) located on chromosome 17q12 (GRCh38: 37458891-37459091), functionally validated as a transcriptional silencer through STARR-seq massively parallel reporter assays (MPRAs) in K562 erythroleukemia cells. This locus lacks canonical promoter, enhancer, or CTCF-bound insulator chromatin signatures but demonstrates repressive activity against the super core promoter SCP1. As a non-coding regulatory sequence, it has no UniProt accession, protein domains, STRING interaction partners, or Human Protein Atlas expression data (not applicable for non-coding locus).\n\nClinVar analysis reveals 53 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants disrupting this region, representing HIGH CNV burden (compared to FAM53A: 146 variants, RNF115: 134 variants, LOC101928389: 82 variants, ZNF500: 30 variants, LOC129389193: 19 variants, LOC129389757: 16 variants, ZNF716: 9 variants). This is the third-highest burden observed in this research series. Variant spectrum includes multiple copy number gains (duplications spanning 17q12, ~1.5-2 Mb genomic spans - variants 4796413, 4796236, 4755384, 4755371, 4755334, 4526729), multiple copy number losses (deletions spanning 17q12, ~1.5-2 Mb - variants 4796300, 4796182, 4081873, 3775095), and single allele deletions. The variant distribution shows BALANCED/BIPHASIC dosage sensitivity with substantial representation of both gains (~50-55%) and losses (~45-50%), indicating that both increased and decreased silencer activity cause pathology. This balanced pattern mirrors FAM53A and RNF115, contrasting with extreme duplication-biased elements (LOC129389193: 100% duplications) and extreme deletion-biased elements.\n\nThe genomic context places LOC129390867 within the 17q12 region, a well-established genomic disorder locus associated with 17q12 deletion syndrome (characterized by renal cysts, maturity-onset diabetes of the kidney (MODY5), pancreatic atrophy, genital malformations, and neurodevelopmental abnormalities) and 17q12 duplication syndrome (intellectual disability, autism spectrum disorder, schizophrenia). The 17q12 region contains critical genes including HNF1B (hepatocyte nuclear factor 1-beta, POU domain transcription factor, renal/pancreatic development, MODY5), LHX1 (lim homeobox 1, kidney/urogenital development), PIGW (phosphatidylinositol glycan anchor biosynthesis), AATF (apoptosis antagonizing transcription factor), and ACACA (acetyl-CoA carboxylase alpha). HNF1B haploinsufficiency causes renal cysts and diabetes syndrome (RCAD). The 17q12 region is critical for renal development, pancreatic function, and neurodevelopment.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: LOC129390867 functions as a distal silencer within the 17q12 topologically associating domain (TAD), potentially regulating HNF1B, LHX1, or PIGW expression through long-range chromatin interactions. The balanced ~50:50 duplication/deletion ratio suggests that both LOSS-of-silencing (haploinsufficiency, 50% activity) AND GAIN-of-silencing (triplication, 150% activity) are equally pathogenic. This indicates the target gene(s) require PRECISE silencer-mediated repression - deviations in either direction cause disease. The mechanism likely involves: (1) silencer-mediated repression of HNF1B maintaining appropriate expression levels in renal tubular epithelium and pancreatic beta cells; (2) recruitment of co-repressor complexes (possibly Polycomb repressive complexes, HDACs, or DNA methyltransferases) to maintain basal HNF1B expression; (3) chromatin boundary function preventing ectopic enhancer activation of HNF1B or neighboring genes; or (4) allele-specific silencing in imprinted 17q12 subdomains. In K562 cells (erythroleukemia), this silencer may regulate hematopoietic expression of 17q12 genes. The 17q12 location within the HNF1B critical region raises the possibility that LOC129390867 maintains appropriate HNF1B expression levels. HNF1B loss causes renal cysts, pancreatic atrophy, and MODY5 diabetes. HNF1B overexpression may cause developmental defects through ectopic target gene activation. The HIGH 53 variant burden indicates significant mutational target size with balanced dosage sensitivity. 17q12 deletions cause RCAD syndrome through HNF1B haploinsufficiency. 17q12 duplications cause neurodevelopmental phenotypes. LOC129390867 dosage alterations may contribute to 17q12 phenotypes through: (1) HNF1B dysregulation causing renal/pancreatic defects; (2) LHX1 dysregulation causing urogenital anomalies; or (3) PIGW/AATF dysregulation affecting GPI anchor biosynthesis or apoptosis regulation. The balanced CNV ratio suggests LOC129390867 functions as a critical rheostat maintaining precise HNF1B expression levels.
