## Description
TMEM59L (UniProt Q9UK28) encodes a 342 amino acid type-I membrane glycoprotein predominantly expressed in the central nervous system. InterPro analysis identifies a brain-specific membrane-anchored protein domain (PF12280, residues 79-284) and membership in the uncharacterized TMEM59 family (IPR022065), suggesting a conserved structural scaffold for neuronal membrane anchoring. The protein features an N-terminal signal peptide and a single transmembrane helix, positioning the C-terminal domain intracellularly. STRING interaction profiling reveals a network enriched for mitochondrial and cytosolic chaperones, including STIP1 (Hsp70/Hsp90 organizing protein, score 0.632), DNAJA4 (0.554), GRPEL2 (0.534), and TRAP1 (0.481), alongside the mTOR pathway inhibitor TSC1 (0.490). This interactome implies a role in protein folding, mitochondrial stress response, or regulation of neuronal growth signaling. Human Protein Atlas data confirms strong tissue enrichment in the brain (342.8 nTPM) and cancer enrichment, consistent with a specialized neuronal function potentially co-opted in malignancy. ClinVar records 7 pathogenic copy number gains spanning 19p13, indicating dosage sensitivity where gene duplication may disrupt neuronal homeostasis or mTOR signaling. The mechanistic hypothesis posits TMEM59L as a membrane-anchored scaffold that recruits chaperone complexes to regulate the stability or activity of neuronal signaling proteins, with dysregulation leading to neurodevelopmental or oncogenic phenotypes.
