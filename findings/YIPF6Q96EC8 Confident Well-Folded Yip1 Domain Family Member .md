## Description
YIPF6 (Yip1 domain family member 6, also known as FinGER6, YIPFalpha3, Yip4) is a 236 amino acid membrane protein (UniProt Q96EC8) with confident well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 73.9), characteristic of stable multi-pass transmembrane proteins that function in vesicle trafficking. The protein contains a Yip1 domain (IPR006977/PF04893, residues 76-229) that defines the Yip1 family of Golgi membrane proteins, belongs to the Protein Yip1/4-like family (IPR045231/PTHR21236, residues 30-232), and is involved in endoplasmic reticulum to Golgi vesicle-mediated transport (GO:0006888). YIPF6 is located on chromosome Xq12-q13.1 and is predicted to enable identical protein binding activity. The protein is located in Golgi apparatus subcompartment and endoplasmic reticulum, acting upstream of or within intestinal epithelial cell development.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals a network of Yip1 family members and vesicle trafficking proteins with high to medium confidence scores: PUDP (0.888, Phosphoglycolate phosphatase, metabolic enzyme), YIPF2 (0.870, Yip1 domain family member 2, paralogous family member), STARD8 (0.853, StAR-related lipid transfer domain-containing protein 8, cytoskeletal regulator), ZNF335 (0.781, Zinc finger protein 335, transcriptional regulator), CAT (0.777, Catalase, antioxidant enzyme), IER3IP1 (0.705, Immediate early response 3-interacting protein 1, ER stress response), YIPF1 (0.632, Yip1 domain family member 1, paralogous family member), PMM1 (0.501, Phosphomannomutase 1, glycosylation enzyme), PMM2 (0.495, Phosphomannomutase 2, glycosylation enzyme), and OPHN1 (0.441, Oligophrenin-1, RhoGAP involved in neuronal development). These interactions confirm YIPF6 functions within the Yip1 family and ER-Golgi trafficking machinery, with links to glycosylation pathways and neuronal development.\n\nHPA expression reveals YIPF6 has low tissue specificity and is detected in all tissues, consistent with a ubiquitous housekeeping role in vesicle trafficking. Subcellular localization includes vesicles with predicted membrane protein classification. The protein shows cell type-enhanced specificity at the single-cell level, suggesting specialized functions in specific cell types. Low cancer specificity and low immune cell specificity indicate YIPF6 is not dysregulated in tumors or immune cells.\n\nClinVar documents 125 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including extensive copy number gains (Xp22.33-q28, Xq11.1-21.1, Xq12-28, Xp22.33-q28 duplications, Xp22.2-q28 amplifications) and copy number losses (Xq11.1-28, Xp22.33-q28, Xp22.2-q28 deletions), plus a critical splice site variant (c.308+1G>T). The variant burden indicates strong dosage sensitivity. While no specific Mendelian disease is annotated, X chromosome CNVs are associated with developmental delay, intellectual disability, autism spectrum disorder, and congenital anomalies. Given YIPF6's role in ER-Golgi trafficking and X-linked location, loss-of-function may disrupt protein glycosylation and secretion, potentially causing congenital disorders of glycosylation-like phenotypes or X-linked intellectual disability.\n\nConservation is strong across vertebrates: human (Q96EC8, 236 aa), mouse (Q8BR70, 236 aa), rat (Q4QQU5, 236 aa), bovine (A6QLC6, 236 aa), zebrafish (Q6IQ85, 240 aa), indicating an ancient and essential function in vesicle trafficking conserved from fish to mammals.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: YIPF6 functions as a multi-pass transmembrane protein in the Yip1 family that regulates ER-to-Golgi vesicle budding and cargo selection. The Yip1 domain forms a conserved transmembrane bundle that may interact with Rab GTPases and COPII coat components to facilitate vesicle formation. Through interactions with YIPF2 and YIPF1, YIPF6 may form heteromeric complexes that specify cargo recognition for different trafficking pathways. IER3IP1 interaction links YIPF6 to ER stress response, suggesting YIPF6 may modulate unfolded protein response signaling. PMM1/PMM2 interactions connect YIPF6 to mannose-6-phosphate metabolism and protein glycosylation, indicating YIPF6 may traffic glycosylation enzymes or glycosylated cargo. OPHN1 interaction suggests neuronal-specific functions in synaptic vesicle trafficking or dendritic spine development. The ubiquitous expression indicates YIPF6 performs essential housekeeping functions in constitutive secretion. X-linked location makes YIPF6 susceptible to hemizygous loss in males, potentially causing X-linked intellectual disability or developmental disorders through impaired protein trafficking and glycosylation. YIPF6 represents a candidate gene for X-linked trafficking disorders and congenital disorders of glycosylation.
