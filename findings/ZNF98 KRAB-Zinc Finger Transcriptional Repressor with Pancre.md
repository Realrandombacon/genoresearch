# ZNF98: KRAB-Zinc Finger Transcriptional Repressor with Pancreas-Enhanced Expression and 19p12 Dosage Sensitivity

**Date:** 2026-03-28T03:09:11.568441

**Quality Score:** 4.95/10  (E=6.3, D=3.6) [MODERATE]

## Description
ZNF98 (Gene ID: 148198, UniProt A6NK75, alias ZNF739) is a 572 amino acid Krüppel-associated box (KRAB) domain-containing zinc finger protein located on chromosome 19p12 (NC_000019.10, positions 22391018-22422345, ~31kb region). InterPro domain analysis reveals canonical KRAB-ZNF architecture: N-terminal KRAB repression domain (IPR001909/PF01352, positions 13-84; cd07765 KRAB-A box, positions 13-51) that recruits transcriptional co-repressors, followed by multiple C2H2-type zinc finger DNA-binding domains (IPR013087/PF00096, positions 182-209, 210-237, 238-265, plus additional fingers at 162-219, 219-275, 274-331 per IPR036236) arranged in tandem arrays conferring sequence-specific DNA recognition. The KRAB domain superfamily (IPR036051, positions 10-68) mediates heterochromatin formation through recruitment of TRIM28/KAP1 and associated histone methyltransferases establishing repressive H3K9me3 marks at target loci. AlphaFold structure prediction (AF-A6NK75-F1) shows high global confidence (pLDDT 72.9), indicating a well-folded stable protein with structured zinc finger domains - this contrasts with many KRAB-ZNFs that show lower confidence due to flexible linkers, suggesting ZNF98 has more rigid domain organization. Human Protein Atlas classifies ZNF98 as a predicted intracellular transcription factor with tissue-enhanced RNA expression, highest in pancreas (35.0 nTPM), detected in many tissues, cancer-enhanced expression pattern suggesting upregulation in malignant cells, and cell type-enhanced specificity. Notably not detected in immune cells, distinguishing it from immune-related KRAB-ZNFs. STRING database identifies 7 protein-protein interaction partners: ANKRD20A4 (score 0.475, ankyrin repeat domain 20A4, highest confidence), ANKRD20A2 (0.475, related ankyrin protein), TMEM14B (0.471, transmembrane protein 14B), FAM83F (0.447, family with sequence similarity 83 member F), RNF166 (0.420, ring finger protein 166 - E3 ubiquitin ligase), DTWD1 (0.412, DTW domain containing 1), and DHRS13 (0.404, dehydrogenase/reductase 13). The ANKRD20 interactions suggest potential roles in chromatin organization or nuclear scaffold association, while RNF166 connection indicates possible ubiquitination-mediated regulation. ClinVar contains 10 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants affecting ZNF98: 2 single nucleotide variants (c.31-168G>T, c.254-18T>A - likely intronic/splice region variants) and 8 copy number gains spanning 19p12-q13 regions (chr19:19546923-41313229x3 ~21.8Mb, chr19:260912-58956888 ~58.7Mb whole arm, chr19:15133594-24193591x3 ~9.1Mb, chr19:21591877-22802732x3 ~1.2Mb minimal region, chr19:11227942-44626354x3 ~33.4Mb, chr19:13974677-27839676x3 ~13.9Mb, chr19:17176767-34924150x3 ~17.7Mb), indicating triplosensitivity. No specific diseases are associated with ZNF98 alone, suggesting phenotypes result from combined effects of multiple genes in duplicated regions. Conservation: ZNF98 orthologs identified in primates (Papio anubis, Pan troglodytes) and surprisingly in crustaceans (Portunus trituberculatus) and spiders (Araneus ventricosus), suggesting ancient evolutionary origin despite typical rapid evolution of KRAB-ZNF genes. Functional hypothesis: ZNF98 functions as a sequence-specific transcriptional repressor binding genomic DNA through its C2H2 zinc finger array and recruiting TRIM28/KAP1 via the KRAB domain to establish heterochromatin at target loci. Pancreas-enhanced expression (35.0 nTPM) suggests specific roles in pancreatic development, acinar/ductal cell differentiation, or endocrine function. Cancer-enhanced expression indicates potential oncogenic function through silencing tumor suppressor genes or differentiation programs. Disease mechanism: ZNF98 duplication leads to overexpression and excessive transcriptional repression of target genes, disrupting normal developmental gene regulatory networks contributing to 19p12 duplication syndrome phenotypes including developmental delay, intellectual disability, and congenital anomalies. Priority experiments include ChIP-seq for genome-wide DNA binding targets, CRISPR knockout in pancreatic cell lines, zinc finger DNA-binding specificity determination via SELEX, investigation of ANKRD20 and RNF166 interaction mechanisms, and analysis of ZNF98 expression patterns across pancreatic cancer subtypes.
