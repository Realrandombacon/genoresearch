## Description
LHFPL5 (LHFPL Tetraspan Subfamily Member 5) is a 219-amino acid predicted membrane protein belonging to the lipoma HMGIC fusion partner-like protein family (IPR019372, residues 5-212; PF10242, residues 25-202; PTHR12489, residues 5-212) that functions as an essential component of the mechanoelectrical transduction (MET) channel complex in cochlear hair cells. InterPro analysis reveals a tetraspan-like topology with four transmembrane domains characteristic of the LHFPL family, though specific transmembrane predictions require additional analysis. AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (pLDDT 89.2), indicating a stable, well-folded membrane protein with defined topology suitable for integration into the hair cell stereocilia membrane.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places LHFPL5 in an exceptionally tight functional cluster centered on hair cell mechanotransduction with near-perfect confidence scores: TMC1 (0.997, transmembrane channel-like protein 1, the pore-forming subunit of the MET channel), TMIE (0.989, transmembrane inner ear protein, essential for MET channel gating), CIB2 (0.872, calcium and integrin-binding protein 2, regulates MET channel adaptation), GGT7 (0.789, gamma-glutamyltransferase 7), TMC2 (0.767, transmembrane channel-like protein 2, paralog of TMC1), MYO7A (0.581, myosin VIIA, motor protein for tip link tension), CDH23 (0.485, cadherin-23, upper tip link component), COL11A2 (0.462, collagen type XI alpha 2), PCDH15 (0.434, protocadherin-15, lower tip link component), and USH1C (0.419, usherin type IC, harmonin scaffold protein). This interaction network definitively establishes LHFPL5 as a core component of the hair cell MET channel complex, physically linking the tip link (PCDH15-CDH23) to the channel pore (TMC1-TMIE-LHFPL5).\n\nHuman Protein Atlas classifies LHFPL5 as a tissue-enhanced transporter with highest expression in bone marrow (17.2 nTPM) and epididymis (11.6 nTPM), surprisingly not in inner ear tissues (which may reflect low RNA-seq coverage of cochlea). It is cancer-enhanced and immune cell-enhanced, with subcellular localization to vesicles. The protein class includes disease-related genes, potential drug targets, predicted membrane proteins, and transporters. Disease involvement explicitly lists deafness, disease variant, and non-syndromic deafness.\n\nClinVar reports 53 pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants causing autosomal recessive non-syndromic hearing loss (DFNB67), including nonsense mutations (p.Lys12Ter, p.Trp132Ter), missense mutations (p.Pro47Ser, p.Glu217Lys, p.Pro90Leu, p.Leu170Gln, p.Tyr18Cys), frameshift deletions (p.Ser74fs, p.Ser75fs), and splice site variants (c.650-2A>G). These variants disrupt LHFPL5's role in MET channel assembly or function, causing profound hearing loss without other syndromic features.\n\nLiterature analysis (69 papers) reveals LHFPL5 is well-studied for its hearing function—recent 2024-2025 papers characterize it as a key element in force transmission from the tip link to the hair cell MET channel, required for maximal channel activation, and mutated in DFNB67 hearing loss patients. However, its expression in bone marrow (17.2 nTPM), epididymis (11.6 nTPM), and immune cells suggests potential non-auditory functions that remain unexplored.\n\nWe hypothesize that LHFPL5 has dual roles: (1) Primary auditory function—as a tetraspan scaffold protein, LHFPL5 stabilizes the TMC1-TMIE pore complex and transmits mechanical force from PCDH15 tip links to gate the MET channel, essential for sound transduction; (2) Unexplored non-auditory functions—bone marrow expression (17.2 nTPM, highest tissue) suggests potential roles in hematopoietic cell mechanosensing or ion transport; epididymal expression (11.6 nTPM) suggests possible sperm maturation or motility functions; immune cell enhancement suggests potential roles in immune cell activation or migration. The cancer-enhanced classification indicates LHFPL5 may be dysregulated in tumors, potentially affecting cell mechanotransduction pathways relevant to metastasis. LHFPL5 represents a validated deafness gene (DFNB67), a potential biomarker for bone marrow disorders, and a candidate target for hearing restoration therapies via MET channel modulation.
