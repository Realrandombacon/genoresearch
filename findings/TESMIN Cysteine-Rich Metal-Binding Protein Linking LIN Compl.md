# TESMIN: Cysteine-Rich Metal-Binding Protein Linking LIN Complex Cell Cycle Regulation to Spermatogenesis and Cancer Progression

**Date:** 2026-05-01T20:31:52.202436

**Quality Score:** 6/10 (GOOD)

**Confidence:** MEDIUM

## Description
TESMIN (Testis expressed metallothionein like protein, also known as MTL5) encodes a 508 amino acid cysteine-rich nuclear protein (UniProt Q9Y4I5) that combines metallothionein-like metal-binding capacity with cell cycle regulatory functions. InterPro analysis reveals a distinctive domain architecture: a Lin-54 family domain (IPR028307, PTHR12446, residues 171-501) that connects TESMIN to the MuvB/LIN complex for cell cycle gene regulation, a CRC domain (IPR005172, PS51634, residues 292-405) of unknown function, and two Tesmin/TSO1-like CXC domains (IPR033467, SM01114, residues 305-331 and 365-406; PF03638, residues 368-403) that are cysteine-rich motifs characteristic of metal-binding proteins. The CXC domains contain conserved cysteine residues that likely coordinate zinc or other heavy metals, analogous to metallothioneins.\n\nAlphaFold prediction shows low-medium global confidence (pLDDT 53.8) across all 508 aa, indicating substantial intrinsic disorder with some structured regions. This flexibility is consistent with a protein that undergoes conformational changes upon metal binding or protein partner interactions, and may facilitate interactions with multiple partners in the LIN complex and chromatin machinery.\n\nSTRING interactions reveal TESMIN operates at the intersection of metal homeostasis and cell cycle control: MT1E (0.870, high confidence) is metallothionein-1E, a canonical heavy metal-binding protein—this strong interaction confirms TESMIN's metallothionein-like function; LIN9 (0.746), LIN52 (0.682), and LIN37 (0.665) are core components of the MuvB/LIN complex that regulates G1/S and G2/M cell cycle gene expression; NAF1 (0.583) is involved in snoRNP assembly; RBBP4 (0.581) and RBBP7 (0.493) are histone-binding chromatin remodeling factors (RbAp48/46); TMEM147 (0.486), TRAPPC5 (0.482), and TRAPPC2L (0.476) are trafficking proteins. The LIN complex interactions position TESMIN as a cell cycle regulator, while MT1E interaction confirms metal-binding capacity.\n\nHPA expression shows TESMIN is testis-enriched (49.1 nTPM) with tissue-enriched RNA specificity, consistent with its name and role in spermatogenesis. Subcellular localization includes nucleoplasm, vesicles, and cytosol. Biological processes include differentiation, meiosis, and spermatogenesis. Cancer specificity is cancer-enhanced, indicating upregulation in tumors. Not detected in immune cells.\n\nClinVar reports 12 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly 11q13.3 and 11q12.1-13.3 copy number losses and gains, plus intronic variants (e.g., c.1334+107C>T). While no specific monogenic disease is definitively assigned, the 11q13 region is amplified in multiple cancers. The CNV sensitivity suggests TESMIN dosage affects cell proliferation.\n\nLiterature (43 papers) reveals dual roles in reproduction and cancer: TESMIN promotes lung adenocarcinoma proliferation, migration and invasion via PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling (2025); serves as a prognostic biomarker associated with immunosuppression and activated cell cycle in lung adenocarcinoma (2024, 3 citations); has clinical significance in hepatocellular carcinoma (2024); and TESMIN-associated proteins were identified by BioID in testicular germ cells (2022, 8 citations). TESMIN plays vital roles in spermatogenesis and oogenesis, and recent research unveils involvement in malignancy.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: TESMIN functions as a metal-dependent cell cycle regulator that bridges metal homeostasis with LIN complex-mediated transcriptional control during germ cell development and cancer progression. The cysteine-rich CXC domains bind zinc or other metals, and metal binding may induce conformational changes (explaining low AlphaFold confidence) that regulate TESMIN's interaction with LIN9/LIN52/LIN37 components of the MuvB complex. In testis, TESMIN likely regulates meiotic cell cycle genes through the LIN complex, ensuring proper spermatogenesis. The MT1E interaction suggests TESMIN may buffer metal ions during rapid cell division or respond to metal stress. In cancer, TESMIN overexpression drives proliferation by enhancing LIN complex activity at cell cycle promoters (e.g., cyclins, CDKs) via PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling. The RBBP4/7 interactions suggest chromatin remodeling involvement—TESMIN may recruit histone modifiers to cell cycle genes. The cancer-enhanced expression and 11q13 amplification indicate TESMIN is an oncogene in multiple tumor types. Therapeutic targeting of TESMIN's metal-binding domains or LIN complex interactions could disrupt cancer cell cycle progression while sparing normal tissues.
