## Description
LOC129389849 is a 200bp cis-regulatory element (CRE) located on chromosome 7q31.31 (GRCh38: 118684381-118684581), functionally validated as a transcriptional silencer through STARR-seq massively parallel reporter assays (MPRAs) in K562 erythroleukemia cells. This locus lacks canonical promoter, enhancer, or CTCF-bound insulator chromatin signatures but demonstrates robust repressive activity against the super core promoter SCP1. As a non-coding regulatory sequence, it has no UniProt accession, protein domains, STRING interaction partners, or Human Protein Atlas expression data (query failed to resolve Ensembl ID).\n\nClinVar analysis reveals 7 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variants disrupting this region. The variant spectrum includes both large-scale deletions (e.g., 7q31.1-31.33, ~11Mb; 7q22.1-31.31, ~19Mb; whole arm 7p-q deletion) and duplications (7q31.2-36.3, ~44Mb; 7q21.3-36.3, ~61Mb). The presence of both loss and gain events suggests dosage sensitivity where silencer disruption alters the expression of critical target genes. These CNVs are associated with 7q deletion/duplication syndromes, characterized by developmental delay, intellectual disability, and distinctive facial features. The 7q31 region is a known hotspot for genomic instability and contains several critical genes, including MET (hepatocyte growth factor receptor), FOXP2 (language development), and IMMP2L. The location of LOC129389849 within this cluster implies it may regulate one or more of these dosage-sensitive loci.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: LOC129389849 functions as a critical distal silencer within the 7q31 topologically associating domain (TAD), specifically regulating genes involved in neurodevelopment and hematopoiesis. Given its validation in K562 cells, it likely represses proto-oncogenes or lineage-inappropriate transcription factors in erythroid progenitors. The mechanism involves recruitment of co-repressor complexes (e.g., PRC2, NuRD) to deposit repressive histone marks (H3K27me3) at target promoters. Loss of this silencer (via 7q deletions) leads to aberrant overexpression of target genes (potentially MET or nearby regulators), contributing to the developmental defects and potential leukemogenic predisposition seen in 7q syndrome patients. Conversely, duplications may lead to ectopic silencing or altered chromatin topology, disrupting normal gene expression patterns. The balanced presence of deletions and duplications in ClinVar highlights the precise stoichiometric requirement for this regulatory element in maintaining cellular homeostasis.
