## Description
ANKRD36 (Ankyrin repeat domain-containing protein 36A, UniProt A6QL64) is a 1915 amino acid scaffolding protein encoded on chromosome 2q11.2. InterPro analysis reveals a bipartite domain architecture: an N-terminal ankyrin repeat array (IPR002110, PF12796, 3 copies spanning residues 31-228) that mediates protein-protein interactions, and a C-terminal CCDC144C-like coiled-coil domain (IPR039497, PF14915, residues 1498-1853) that likely drives oligomerization or structural assembly. The ankyrin repeats form a curved solenoid structure ideal for binding diverse protein partners, while the extensive coiled-coil region (~355 aa) suggests multimerization or cytoskeletal anchoring capability. High-confidence partners include its paralog ANKRD36B (0.983), indicating functional redundancy or heteromeric complex formation. Key interactors include cell cycle inhibitors CDKN2C (p18-INK4c, 0.480) and CDKN2D (p19-INK4d, 0.499), which inhibit CDK4/6 to arrest G1 progression. Additional links to TMEM269 (0.652, lysophylipid transporter), ASB5/12 (0.447/0.443, ankyrin-box ubiquitin ligase substrate receptors), and FAM178B (0.460) suggest roles in membrane trafficking and protein turnover. The gene summary predicts ion channel inhibitor activity, consistent with ankyrin proteins like ankyrin-G that anchor ion channels to the cytoskeleton.\n\nClinVar records 36 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, comprising both copy number losses (2q11.1-11.2 deletions spanning ~2-3 Mb) and coding sequence variants (missense p.Asp1014Tyr, splice site c.1162+6T>C, c.3090+22T>A, and intronic variants). This mixed variant spectrum indicates both haploinsufficiency (via deletions) and dominant-negative or gain-of-function mechanisms (via coding mutations) contribute to disease. These variants are associated with 2q11.2 deletion syndromes, characterized by developmental delay, intellectual disability, and dysmorphic features, though no specific disease name is established in ClinVar.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: ANKRD36 functions as a multi-domain scaffolding protein that anchors cell cycle inhibitors (CDKN2C/D) and potentially ion channels to subcellular structures via its ankyrin repeat array. The C-terminal coiled-coil domain mediates homooligomerization with ANKRD36A or heteromerization with ANKRD36B/C, creating higher-order scaffolds that concentrate regulatory complexes at specific cellular compartments (e.g., nuclear envelope, cytoskeletal junctions, or plasma membrane). By recruiting CDKN2C/D to specific loci, ANKRD36 may enforce localized cell cycle arrest in differentiated tissues or during stress responses. The ion channel inhibitor prediction suggests it may also anchor potassium or sodium channels (e.g., KCNQ family) to ankyrin-repeat binding partners, regulating excitability in neural or cardiac tissues. Haploinsufficiency (via 2q11.2 deletions) disrupts this scaffolding function, leading to mislocalization of cell cycle regulators and ion channels, causing developmental defects and potential arrhythmia susceptibility. Coding variants may exert dominant-negative effects by incorporating into scaffolds but failing to recruit proper partners. The paralog network (ANKRD36B/C) suggests partial functional redundancy, explaining why some individuals tolerate heterozygous loss.
