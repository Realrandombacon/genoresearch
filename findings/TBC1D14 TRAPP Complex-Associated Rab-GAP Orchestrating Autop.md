# TBC1D14: TRAPP Complex-Associated Rab-GAP Orchestrating Autophagosome Biogenesis via ATG9 Trafficking and Suppressing Cancer Metastasis through DDX31 Ubiquitination

## Description
TBC1D14 (TBC1 Domain Family Member 14) is a partially characterized Rab GTPase-activating protein with 22 publications, emerging as a critical regulator of autophagy initiation and cancer metastasis suppression. This 693-amino acid cytosolic/vesicular protein (UniProt Q9P2M4) features a defining C-terminal Rab-GAP TBC domain (IPR000195/PF00566, positions 398-634) composed of two structural lobes (G3DSA:1.10.10.750, positions 345-417; G3DSA:1.10.472.80, positions 536-687) that catalyze GTP hydrolysis on specific Rab substrates. AlphaFold predicts medium-confidence structure (pLDDT 65.4), reflecting a structured catalytic core connected by flexible linkers to an N-terminal region of unknown function.\n\nSTRING interactions reveal TBC1D14 operates as a dedicated effector of the TRAPP (Transport Protein Particle) complex and autophagy machinery: ULK1 (0.884, serine/threonine kinase initiating autophagy), TRAPPC12 (0.849), TRAPPC13 (0.837), TRAPPC2L (0.827), TRAPPC11 (0.822), TRAPPC3 (0.807), TRAPPC5 (0.790), TRAPPC6B (0.746), TRAPPC8 (0.495), and RAB11A (0.484). This dense network of TRAPP subunits confirms TBC1D14's role as a bridge between the TRAPP tethering complex and Rab GTPase regulation, specifically controlling the trafficking of ATG9 vesicles essential for autophagosome membrane expansion.\n\nHPA shows low tissue specificity with ubiquitous expression across all tissues, but cancer-enriched expression patterns suggest dysregulation in tumors. Subcellular localization includes nucleoplasm, Golgi apparatus, and vesicles, consistent with roles in membrane trafficking and autophagy. Biological processes center on autophagy and GTPase activation. ClinVar contains 89 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number losses (deletions) and gains (duplications) on chromosome 4p16.3-15.33, though specific monogenic diseases remain uncharacterized, suggesting haploinsufficiency or dosage sensitivity may underlie developmental disorders. Conservation across mammals indicates functional constraint.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: TBC1D14 functions as a spatiotemporal brake on autophagy by regulating the recycling of ATG9, the only transmembrane core autophagy protein. Upon starvation, the TRAPP complex recruits TBC1D14 to specific membrane compartments where its GAP activity inactivates Rab11A (and potentially other Rabs like Rab8/Rab10), switching ATG9 vesicles from a recycling mode to a fusion-competent state for autophagosome formation. Recent studies reveal a non-canonical tumor suppressor role in head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC), where TBC1D14 inhibits lymph node metastasis via two distinct mechanisms: (1) downregulating macrophage erythroblast attacher (MREA) to suppress autophagy-dependent invasion, and (2) mediating ubiquitination and degradation of DDX31 (a nucleolar helicase), thereby inhibiting ribosome biogenesis and reducing metastatic potential. The high burden of ClinVar CNVs suggests that genomic instability at 4p16 may disrupt this delicate balance, potentially contributing to neurodevelopmental phenotypes or cancer susceptibility via autophagy dysregulation.
