## Description
IZUMO2 (Izumo sperm-egg fusion protein 2, also known as C19orf41, SCRL, PLAL6978, PRO21961) is a 221 amino acid membrane protein (UniProt Q6UXV1) with high-confidence well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 86.6), characteristic of stable structural proteins involved in cell-cell recognition and fusion events. The protein contains an Izumo sperm-egg fusion protein family domain (IPR029389/PTHR47745, residues 20-164/1-221), an Izumo sperm-egg fusion protein 2-specific domain (IPR042920, residues 1-221), and an Izumo sperm-egg fusion Ig domain-associated Pfam domain (PF15005, residues 20-164). IZUMO2 is located on chromosome 19q13.33 and is classified as a predicted membrane protein and transporter involved in sperm-egg recognition.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals a comprehensive network of sperm-egg fusion and acrosomal proteins with high to medium confidence scores: IZUMO3 (0.848, Izumo family member 3, paralogous family member), IZUMO4 (0.824, Izumo family member 4, paralogous family member), IZUMO1 (0.752, Izumo sperm-egg fusion protein 1, the canonical sperm-egg fusion protein), ADAM32 (0.572, A disintegrin and metalloproteinase domain-containing protein 32, sperm surface protein), CD9 (0.564, Tetraspanin-29, egg surface protein essential for sperm-egg fusion), C2orf83 (0.530, Chromosome 2 open reading frame 83, testis-expressed), ADAM2 (0.528, Fertilin beta, sperm-egg adhesion), OR4N5 (0.525, Olfactory receptor 4N5, ectopic expression in sperm), SPACA4 (0.518, Sperm acrosome membrane-associated protein 4), and ISOC2 (0.405, Isocitrate dehydrogenase [NADP] cytoplasmic 2). These interactions confirm IZUMO2 functions within the sperm-egg recognition and fusion machinery.\n\nHPA expression reveals IZUMO2 is tissue-enriched with exclusive expression in testis (54.6 nTPM), detected in single tissues. Subcellular localization includes the acrosome and mid piece of spermatozoa. The acrosome is a cap-like structure containing enzymes required for penetrating the egg zona pellucida, while the mid piece contains mitochondria for sperm motility. IZUMO2 shows cancer-enhanced expression, suggesting potential dysregulation in tumors. The protein is classified as a predicted membrane protein and transporter with group-enriched specificity at the single-cell level.\n\nClinVar documents 13 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all large copy number gains on chromosome 19: 19q13.33-13.43, 19q13.33-13.41, 19q13.33-13.43 (multiple entries), 19q13.32-13.42, 19q13.31-13.42, 19p13.3-q13.43 amplifications. The variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity. While no specific Mendelian disease is annotated, 19q CNVs are associated with developmental delay, intellectual disability, and congenital anomalies. Given IZUMO2's testis-specific expression and role in sperm-egg fusion, dosage imbalance may affect spermatogenesis or sperm function, potentially causing male infertility.\n\nConservation is strong across mammals: human (Q6UXV1, 221 aa), mouse (Q9DA16, 220 aa), camel (A0A8B8TMX2, 715 aa isoform X2), indicating an ancient and essential function in mammalian fertilization.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: IZUMO2 functions as a sperm surface protein involved in sperm-egg recognition and fusion, acting in concert with the canonical IZUMO1 protein. The Izumo family proteins are named after the Izumo Taisha shrine in Japan, symbolizing marriage. IZUMO1 on sperm binds JUNO on the egg surface to initiate fusion; IZUMO2 may serve as a co-receptor or auxiliary factor that stabilizes the fusion complex. The Ig domain-associated fold suggests IZUMO2 mediates protein-protein interactions through immunoglobulin-like beta-sandwich structures. Through interactions with IZUMO3, IZUMO4, ADAM32, and ADAM2, IZUMO2 may form a multi-protein complex on the sperm acrosomal membrane that coordinates zona pellucida binding, acrosome reaction, and egg fusion. CD9 interaction suggests IZUMO2 may also participate in post-fusion events on the egg side. The testis-specific expression (54.6 nTPM) and acrosome/mid piece localization confirm IZUMO2's role in fertilization. 19q CNVs may disrupt IZUMO2 dosage, affecting sperm function and causing male infertility. IZUMO2 represents a candidate gene for non-obstructive azoospermia and a potential target for contraceptive development.
