# HIBCH: Mitochondrial 3-Hydroxyisobutyryl-CoA Hydrolase Linking Valine Catabolism to Leigh Syndrome, Protein Lysine Methacrylation Defects, and Neurodegeneration via Toxic Metabolite Accumulation

## Description
HIBCH (3-Hydroxyisobutyryl-CoA Hydrolase) is a 386 amino acid mitochondrial hydrolase that catalyzes the hydrolysis of 3-hydroxyisobutyryl-CoA to 3-hydroxyisobutyrate in the valine catabolic pathway, serving as a critical checkpoint preventing accumulation of toxic methacrylyl-CoA intermediates that cause protein lysine methacrylation defects. InterPro analysis identifies canonical crotonase-like domain architecture (cd06558, residues 38-222) within the ClpP/crotonase-like superfamily (IPR029045, SSF52096, residues 36-362), enoyl-CoA hydratase/isomerase HIBYL-CoA-H type family (IPR032259, PTHR43176, residues 31-379) with 3-hydroxyisobutyryl-CoA hydrolase activity (GO:0003860), and enoyl-CoA hydratase/isomerase domain (IPR045004, PF16113, residues 47-376). AlphaFold structure (AF-Q6NVY1-F1) predicts very high confidence (pLDDT 93.9) across the 386 residue sequence, indicating an exceptionally well-folded enzyme with stable tertiary structure suitable for CoA-thioester hydrolysis catalysis. HPA data classifies HIBCH as a disease-related metabolic enzyme and potential drug target involved in branched-chain amino acid catabolism, with mitochondrial localization, low tissue specificity (detected in all tissues), and cell type-enhanced expression. STRING interactions reveal tight functional coupling to valine catabolism and mitochondrial translation: ECHS1 (Enoyl-CoA hydratase short chain 1, 0.984), its partner enzyme in the valine degradation pathway; DAP3 (Death-associated protein 3, 0.965) and mitochondrial ribosomal proteins MRPS15 (0.964), MRPS10 (0.954), MRPS9 (0.548), MRPS35 (0.496), MRPL46 (0.494), MRPS5 (0.443) reflecting mitochondrial proteostasis connections; EHHADH (Peroxisomal bifunctional enzyme, 0.432) for fatty acid oxidation; and HIBADH (3-hydroxyisobutyrate dehydrogenase, 0.410), the downstream enzyme in valine catabolism. ClinVar lists 88 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including frameshift duplications/deletions (p.Ile84fs, p.Gly44fs, p.Phe165fs), large copy number losses on chromosome 2q31.3-33.1, deletions spanning 2q, splice variants (c.220-35A>G, c.751-24T>G), and missense mutations (p.Cys163Tyr, p.Gly179Asp), causing autosomal recessive HIBCH deficiency presenting as Leigh syndrome with basal ganglia lesions, developmental regression, hypotonia, seizures, poor feeding, and lethal neonatal acidosis. Literature (93 papers) establishes HIBCH deficiency as a treatable inborn error of metabolism where loss of HIBCH or ECHS1 causes abnormal mitochondrial morphology, respiratory defects, and ectopic protein lysine methacrylation due to methacrylyl-CoA accumulation; recent work identifies HIBCH as a mitochondrial dynamics-related biomarker in Alzheimer's disease; high-dose methylprednisolone has been used casuistically for acute encephalopathy during metabolic crisis; and multi-omics investigations reveal novel variants causing lethal neonatal presentations. As a well-studied metabolic gene with emerging mechanistic insights into toxic metabolite-mediated protein modification, HIBCH represents a critical node connecting valine catabolism to mitochondrial function, neurodevelopment, and neurodegeneration through prevention of aberrant lysine methacrylation.
