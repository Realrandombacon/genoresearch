HPA reveals highly restricted expression with testis enrichment (nTPM 2.2) and absence in immune cells and cancers, suggesting a specialized role in spermatogenesis or blood-testis barrier function. The 8p23.1 region is a known hotspot for genomic disorders including congenital heart defects and developmental delay, suggesting SLC35G5 haploinsufficiency may contribute to these phenotypes. With only 2 literature papers, SLC35G5 remains a genuine dark gene. Mechanistically, the EamA/EmrE fold suggests SLC35G5 functions as a H+-driven antiporter exporting toxic metabolites, xenobiotics, or signaling lipids from germ cells to maintain spermatogenic integrity. The testis-specific expression pattern parallels other SLC35 family members involved in nucleotide sugar transport for glycosylation, but SLC35G5's EmrE topology indicates small molecule transport rather than sugar translocation. The 115 ClinVar variants imply SLC35G5 disruption causes male infertility, developmental disorders via 8p23.1 deletion syndrome, or both. XKR6 interaction suggests a role in phosphatidylserine exposure during germ cell apoptosis, while DEFB134 linkage indicates immune privilege maintenance in the testis. SLC35G5 represents an understudied node connecting transporter biology, reproductive health, and genomic disorder pathogenesis.
