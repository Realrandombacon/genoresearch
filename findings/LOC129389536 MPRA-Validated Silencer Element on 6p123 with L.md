# LOC129389536: MPRA-Validated Silencer Element on 6p12.3 with Low Burden of 2 Recurrent Pathogenic CNVs

## Description
LOC129389536 is a 200bp cis-regulatory element (CRE) located on chromosome 6p12.3 (GRCh38: 49914801-49915001), functionally validated as a transcriptional silencer through STARR-seq massively parallel reporter assays (MPRAs) in K562 erythroleukemia cells. This element lacks canonical promoter, enhancer, or CTCF-bound insulator chromatin signatures but demonstrates robust repressive activity against the super core promoter SCP1. As a non-coding regulatory locus, it has no UniProt accession, protein domains, or STRING interaction partners. Human Protein Atlas could not resolve this locus to an Ensembl ID, consistent with its non-coding regulatory classification.\n\nClinVar analysis reveals 2 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variants disrupting this region—a low burden compared to other characterized silencers. Both variants are deletions (copy number losses): one spanning 6p12.3 (~4.2Mb) and another spanning 6p21.1-12.1 (~8.5Mb). These recurrent CNVs are associated with 6p deletion syndromes featuring developmental delay, intellectual disability, craniofacial dysmorphism, skeletal anomalies, and congenital heart defects. The 6p12.3 region is gene-dense, containing critical dosage-sensitive genes such as FOXC1 (Forkhead Box C1, mutations cause Axenfeld-Rieger syndrome and glaucoma), IRF4 (Interferon Regulatory Factor 4), and multiple immune-related genes within the MHC class III region vicinity.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: LOC129389536 functions as a critical distal silencer within the 6p12.3 topologically associating domain (TAD), regulating expression of dosage-sensitive developmental and immune genes. Candidate targets include FOXC1 (transcription factor essential for eye and craniofacial development) or IRF4 (lymphocyte differentiation regulator). The silencer likely recruits co-repressor complexes (PRC2, NCoR/SMRT, HDACs) to deposit H3K27me3 marks at target promoters. The low CNV burden (2 variants, both deletions 2:0) suggests haploinsufficiency intolerance where silencer loss causes derepression of developmental genes. The exclusive deletion pattern (no duplications) indicates that loss of repression is more pathogenic than silencer gain, possibly because FOXC1 overexpression causes severe ocular/craniofacial phenotypes. The K562 validation suggests potential roles in hematopoietic/immune lineage specification, consistent with 6p\'s enrichment for immune-related genes.
