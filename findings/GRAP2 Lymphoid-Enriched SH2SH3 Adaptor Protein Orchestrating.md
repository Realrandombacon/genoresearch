## Description
GRAP2 encodes a 330 aa GRB2-related adaptor protein 2 (UniProt O75791), functioning as a critical signaling adaptor in T-cell receptor (TCR) and immune cell activation pathways. The protein contains a central SH2 domain (IPR000980, PF00017, aa56-149) for phosphotyrosine binding, flanked by N-terminal (aa1-56) and C-terminal (aa271-330) SH3 domains (IPR001452, PF00018) that bind proline-rich motifs—this SH3-SH2-SH3 architecture is characteristic of GRB2-family adaptors enabling scaffold function. AlphaFold prediction shows high confidence structure (pLDDT 70.2), indicating a well-folded adaptor with stable domain organization suitable for protein-protein interactions. HPA shows tissue-enriched expression with highest levels in lymphoid tissue (171.9 nTPM), localized to nucleoplasm, plasma membrane, and cytosol—consistent with dual roles in membrane-proximal signaling and nuclear functions. STRING interactions reveal GRAP2 forms the core TCR signaling scaffold: LAT (0.999, linker for activation of T-cells—highest confidence, direct binding partner), GRB2 (0.993, paralogous adaptor), SHC1 (0.992, upstream adaptor), VAV1 (0.992, RhoGEF activating Rac/Cdc42), FYB1 (0.980, FYN-binding protein), ITK (0.979, IL-2-inducible T-cell kinase), LCP2 (0.843, SLP-76 adaptor), ZAP70 (0.628, Syk-family kinase), PLCG1 (0.578, phospholipase C-gamma1), CD28 (0.502, T-cell co-stimulatory receptor). ClinVar contains 10 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants—all copy number variations on 22q11.1-13.33 including deletions (22q13.1-13.2) and duplications (22q11.1-13.33), though no specific disease is yet assigned. Literature (55 papers) documents GRAP2 roles in: (1) cervical cancer as novel immune-related prognostic biomarker correlating with immune infiltration; (2) lung adenocarcinoma prognosis and immune infiltration correlation; (3) breast cancer diagnosis via cfDNA methylation patterns (RANBP3/LCP2/GRAP2); (4) rheumatoid arthritis where GRAP2 (cyclin D1-binding protein 1) negatively regulates CREB-binding protein (CBP), inhibiting fibroblast-like synoviocyte growth; (5) T-cell activation and leukocyte signal transduction. GRAP2 functions as a TCR signaling adaptor—the SH2 domain (aa56-149) binds phosphorylated LAT tyrosines following TCR engagement, while SH3 domains (aa1-56, aa271-330) recruit proline-rich effectors like VAV1, ITK, and PLCG1. The ultra-high confidence LAT interaction (0.999) places GRAP2 at the membrane-proximal signaling hub where LAT serves as the central TCR signalosome scaffold. GRB2 (0.993) and SHC1 (0.992) connections suggest functional redundancy or cooperative signaling with the canonical GRB2-SHC1 pathway. VAV1 (0.992) and ITK (0.979) interactions position GRAP2 in actin cytoskeleton remodeling and calcium flux regulation—critical for immunological synapse formation. FYB1 (0.980) links GRAP2 to FYN kinase signaling. Lymphoid tissue enrichment (171.9 nTPM) confirms immune-specific function, while nucleoplasmic localization suggests potential transcriptional co-regulation roles. The CBP negative regulation in rheumatoid arthritis reveals a nuclear function—GRAP2 may shuttle between membrane and nucleus, inhibiting CBP-mediated transcription of proliferation genes in synoviocytes. Cancer prognostic value (cervical, lung, breast) reflects GRAP2's role in tumor immune microenvironment modulation—altered GRAP2 expression may affect T-cell infiltration and anti-tumor immunity. The 10 ClinVar CNVs on 22q suggest GRAP2 dosage sensitivity; 22q13 deletions/duplications may cause immune dysregulation, developmental abnormalities, or cancer predisposition through GRAP2 haploinsufficiency or overexpression. The SH3-SH2-SH3 architecture enables GRAP2 to bridge phosphorylated membrane receptors (via SH2) with cytoplasmic effectors (via SH3), assembling multi-protein signaling complexes that amplify TCR signals and coordinate downstream responses including calcium mobilization, cytoskeletal reorganization, and transcriptional activation.
