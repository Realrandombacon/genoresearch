## Description
MARCHF7 encodes a 704 amino acid membrane-associated RING-CH-type E3 ubiquitin ligase (UniProt Q9H992), a post-translational regulator with emerging roles in autophagy, peroxisome homeostasis, and antiviral defense. With only 7 publications, MARCHF7 remains a genuine dark gene despite critical functions in cellular quality control pathways.\n\nSTRUCTURAL FEATURES: AlphaFold predicts low-medium confidence structure (pLDDT 50.1) across all 704 residues, consistent with a membrane protein containing an ordered catalytic RING domain embedded in extensive intrinsically disordered regions typical of E3 ligases requiring conformational flexibility for substrate recognition. While no specific monogenic disease is yet annotated, the variant burden and autophagy/pexophagy roles suggest potential contributions to neurodegenerative disorders or metabolic diseases where protein/organelle quality control is compromised.\n\nFUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: MARCHF7 functions as a selective E3 ubiquitin ligase coordinating multiple quality control pathways: (1) Autophagy regulation—recent literature (2023, 6 citations) demonstrates MARCHF7 mediates K48-linked ubiquitination of ATG14, controlling its solubility and autophagosome formation. This positions MARCHF7 as a brake on excessive autophagy. (2) Pexophagy control—recent literature (2025, 2 citations) identifies a novel TBK1-MARCHF7-PXMP4-NBR1 axis where MARCHF7 ubiquitinates PXMP4 (peroxisomal membrane protein) to recruit NBR1 for selective peroxisome degradation during PEX1 depletion. (3) Antiviral defense—recent literature (2025, 7 citations) shows SARS-CoV-2 nsp16 (methyltransferase) is regulated by MARCHF7 and UBR5, suggesting MARCHF7 targets viral proteins for proteasomal degradation. (4) Inflammasome modulation—STRING interaction with NLRP3 and literature on autophagic NLRP3 degradation suggest MARCHF7 limits inflammatory responses. Mechanistically, the C-terminal RING-CH domain (residues 544-614) recruits E2~Ub conjugates while N-terminal regions confer substrate specificity. The low-confidence AlphaFold structure reflects intrinsic disorder enabling recognition of diverse substrates. Loss-of-function would cause autophagy dysregulation, peroxisome accumulation, and impaired viral clearance; gain-of-function could drive excessive protein degradation.
