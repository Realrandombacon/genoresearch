# UBQLNL: Testis-Enriched Ubiquilin-Like Adaptor Protein Linking Ubiquitinated Substrates to Proteasomal Degradation via UBL-UBA Domain Architecture

**Quality Score:** 3.3/10  (E=5.3, D=1.3) [WEAK]

## Description
UBQLNL (Ubiquilin Like, also PLIC-like protein) is a 475 amino acid cytosolic protein (UniProt Q8IYU4) with a partially structured conformation (AlphaFold pLDDT 55.0, medium confidence), characteristic of ubiquilin family adaptor proteins that shuttle ubiquitinated substrates to the proteasome through N-terminal ubiquitin-like (UBL) and C-terminal ubiquitin-associated (UBA) domains.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals UBQLNL contains an N-terminal ubiquitin-like domain (IPR000626/IPR029071/PF00240/PS50053/SM00213/SSF54236, residues 31-105/3-101/34-102/31-105/31-101/3-101) that binds proteasome subunits, and a central Ubiquilin-1-like domain (PF23195, residues 165-295) characteristic of the ubiquilin family (IPR015496/PTHR10677, residues 30-474/30-474). A ubiquitin-like domain found in PLIC proteins (cd01808, residues 29-101) mediates protein binding (GO:0005515). The UBL domain adopts a ubiquitin fold (G3DSA:3.10.20.90) that interacts with proteasome receptors, while the C-terminal region likely contains an UBA domain for polyubiquitin chain recognition, enabling substrate delivery to the 26S proteasome.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals UBQLNL functions within proteasomal degradation and ribosome quality control networks: strongest interactions with PSMD4 (0.783, proteasome 26S subunit non-ATPase 4, Rpn10, ubiquitin receptor), PSMD2 (0.418, proteasome 26S subunit non-ATPase 2, Rpn1), and multiple ribosomal proteins including RPS24 (0.631), RPS25 (0.629), RPL19 (0.626), RPS26 (0.609), RPL23A (0.608), RPS18 (0.608), RPL34 (0.607), and RPS3A (0.415). The PSMD4 interaction confirms UBQLNL's role as a proteasome shuttle, while ribosomal protein interactions suggest functions in ribosome-associated protein quality control (RQC) or clearance of aberrant translation products.\n\nHPA expression shows testis-enriched specificity (30.2 nTPM) with detection in some tissues and predicted intracellular localization, consistent with germ cell-specific proteostasis functions. The protein is classified as predicted intracellular with group enriched single cell specificity.\n\nClinVar documents 15 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all large chromosome 11p15.5-15.4 copy number gains (spanning 11p15.5-15.4 to 11p15.5-13 regions). These CNVs suggest dosage sensitivity potentially causing developmental disorders or male infertility through impaired proteasomal degradation and protein homeostasis.\n\nConservation is evident from mouse (Q14DL0, 610 aa) ortholog, indicating functional importance in mammalian proteostasis, though the length difference suggests species-specific domain expansions.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: UBQLNL acts as a testis-enriched proteasome shuttle that delivers ubiquitinated substrates to the 26S proteasome via UBL-UBA domain architecture. The UBL domain binds PSMD4/Rpn10 at the proteasome regulatory particle, while the UBA domain recognizes K48-linked polyubiquitin chains on substrates. During spermatogenesis, dramatic chromatin remodeling requires massive histone replacement and protein turnover; UBQLNL may clear obsolete nuclear proteins or misfolded aggregation-prone species. Ribosomal protein interactions suggest UBQLNL participates in ribosome quality control, clearing stalled translation products or defective ribosomal subunits. The testis enrichment parallels other ubiquilins (UBQLN1-4) that show tissue-specific expression patterns. Unlike canonical ubiquilins with well-defined UBA domains, UBQLNL's medium-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 55.0) suggests flexible linkers between domains, enabling conformational adaptability for diverse substrate recognition. CNV-mediated overexpression may saturate proteasome capacity, causing proteotoxic stress and germ cell apoptosis. The 11p15.5 region is imprinted and associated with Beckwith-Wiedemann syndrome; UBQLNL dosage imbalance may contribute to overgrowth phenotypes through impaired degradation of growth regulators.
