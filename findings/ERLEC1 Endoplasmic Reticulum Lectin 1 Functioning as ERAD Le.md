## Description
ERLEC1 (Endoplasmic reticulum lectin 1, Q96DZ1), also known as OS-9-related protein or XTP3-B, encodes a 483 aa lectin chaperone functioning as a critical component of the endoplasmic reticulum-associated degradation (ERAD) pathway that specifically recognizes misfolded N-glycosylated proteins via its dual mannose-6-phosphate receptor homology (MRH) domains, delivering substrates to the SEL1L-HRD1 ubiquitin ligase complex for retrotranslocation and proteasomal degradation, with emerging roles in bone metabolism, osteoblast collagen synthesis, and craniofacial development linked to Class III malocclusion. Structural architecture reveals a well-folded organization with high AlphaFold confidence (pLDDT 72.9): two distinct MRH domains (IPR044865/PS51914/G3DSA:2.70.130.10/SSF50911) positioned at residues 111-246 and 342-469 forming the canonical beta-sandwich fold characteristic of mannose-6-phosphate receptors but adapted for recognizing high-mannose N-glycans on misfolded glycoproteins, Protein OS9-like domain (IPR012913/PF07915) spanning residues 112-199 and 343-421 corresponding to the core lectin modules, Glucosidase II beta subunit-like family (PF07915) indicating evolutionary relationship to glycan-processing enzymes, OS-9-related family (IPR045149/PTHR15414, residues 18-341) defining the specific functional lineage distinct from OS9, GO terms endoplasmic reticulum unfolded protein response (GO:0030968) and ERAD pathway (GO:0036503) confirming central role in proteostasis; the dual MRH domain arrangement suggests bivalent glycan binding capability enhancing affinity for specific mannose truncation patterns indicative of misfolding.
