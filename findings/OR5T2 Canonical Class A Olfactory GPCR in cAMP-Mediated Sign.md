## Description
OR5T2 (Olfactory receptor 5T2, UniProt Q8NGG2) is a 359-amino acid G protein-coupled receptor located on chromosome 11p11.12 within the large olfactory receptor gene cluster. InterPro analysis confirms this as a canonical class A rhodopsin-like GPCR containing the olfactory receptor family domain (IPR000725/PF13853, aa 70-345), seven transmembrane helices characteristic of rhodopsin-like receptors (IPR017452, aa 80-329; G3DSA:1.20.1070.10, aa 59-354), and membership in the olfactory receptor subfamily 5T (PTHR48018, aa 42-343). The protein contains conserved GPCR signature motifs (PS00237, aa 149-165; PS50262, aa 80-329) critical for G protein coupling and odorant binding, with PRINTS fingerprints (PR00237 GPCRRHODOPSN at aa 65-89/143-165/311-337; PR00245 OLFACTORYR at aa 131-142/168-180/215-231). AlphaFold structure prediction achieves high confidence (pLDDT 80.6), indicating a well-folded stable 7-transmembrane bundle architecture typical of functional olfactory receptors ready for ligand binding and signal transduction. Human Protein Atlas shows no detection in standard tissue panels, which is expected for olfactory receptors expressed exclusively in olfactory sensory neurons of the nasal epithelium not profiled in HPA. STRING interaction network reveals 10 partners comprising the canonical olfactory signal transduction cascade: GNAL (0.686, olfactory-specific Gα subunit), GNB1 (0.682, Gβ subunit), ARRB1/ARRB2 (0.679, β-arrestins mediating desensitization), GNG7 (0.678, Gγ subunit), PRKACA/PRKACB (0.650, cAMP-dependent protein kinase catalytic subunits), GRK2 (0.569), GNG13 (0.566, Gγ subunit), and GRK3 (0.547, G protein-coupled receptor kinases). This interactome confirms OR5T2 functions through the canonical cAMP-mediated olfactory transduction pathway. ClinVar contains 8 pathogenic variants including massive copy-number gains across 11p11.12-q12.2, 11p11.12-q13.1, 11p11.2-q12.2, 11p13-q25, 11p15.5-q25, plus one deletion, demonstrating dosage sensitivity where genomic rearrangements disrupting the olfactory receptor cluster cause disease phenotypes. Conservation across mammals indicates ancient evolutionary origin tied to chemosensory function. OR5T2 likely functions as a volatile odorant receptor detecting specific molecular features, activating GNAL to stimulate adenylate cyclase III, increasing cAMP, opening cyclic nucleotide-gated channels, and depolarizing olfactory sensory neurons.
