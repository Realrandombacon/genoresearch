## Description
ZNF618 (Zinc Finger Protein 618, alias FP13169/NEDD10, UniProt Q5T7W0) is a 954 amino acid nuclear protein containing multiple C2H2-type zinc finger domains (IPR013087/PS00028/PS50157, residues 147-174, 188-211, 256-279) characteristic of sequence-specific DNA-binding transcription factors, plus a Hermes transposase DNA-binding domain (IPR018473/PF10683, residues 460-520) indicating evolutionary relationship to hAT transposase superfamily. Additional domains include Ribonuclease H-like superfamily (IPR012337, residues 561-952) and membership in zinc finger/BTB domain-containing protein family (PTHR24383, residues 49-842). HPA expression demonstrates ubiquitous detection across all tissues with low tissue specificity and nucleoplasmic subcellular localization, classified as transcription factor involved in DNA-binding and transcription regulation. ClinVar contains 29 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number gains and losses spanning 9q31.2-34.3 regions, indicating dosage sensitivity though no specific disease phenotype is yet defined. Gene location at 9q32 within pericentric heterochromatin suggests potential role in heterochromatin maintenance or centromere function. Mechanistic hypothesis: ZNF618 functions as a chromatin-associated transcriptional coregulator that bridges sequence-specific DNA recognition (via C2H2 zinc fingers) with chromatin remodeling machinery (via BRD3, CHD2, NUTM1 interactions). The Hermes transposase domain suggests evolutionary origin from transposable elements, potentially repurposed for endogenous genomic regulation of repetitive elements or heterochromatin organization. Casein kinase interactions indicate dynamic phosphorylation control of DNA-binding affinity or protein stability. Ubiquitous expression supports fundamental role in maintaining chromatin architecture and transcriptional homeostasis across cell types. Dosage sensitivity from ClinVar CNVs suggests precise ZNF618 levels critical for proper heterochromatin function, with potential implications for genomic stability and centromeric integrity.
