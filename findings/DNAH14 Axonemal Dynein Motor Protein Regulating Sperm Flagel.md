## Description
DNAH14 (Dynein axonemal heavy chain 14, UniProt Q0VDD8) is a 3507 aa axonemal dynein motor protein with multiple AAA+ ATPase domains and characteristic dynein structural features. InterPro analysis identifies dynein heavy chain AAA module D4 (IPR024317, positions 2007-2243), coiled coil stalk domain (IPR024743, positions 2258-2488), and dynein heavy chain family signatures (IPR026983, positions 1017-2011) with P-loop NTPase motifs (G3DSA:3.40.50.300) at multiple positions for ATP hydrolysis-driven conformational changes. AlphaFold structure shows medium confidence (pLDDT 64.6), consistent with large motor proteins having flexible linker regions between structured AAA+ domains. HPA data reveals testis-enhanced expression (18.5 nTPM) with subcellular localization to Golgi apparatus, centrosome, and basal body — all critical for ciliogenesis and flagella assembly. STRING interactions confirm functional clustering with other axonemal dyneins: DNAH1 (0.957), DNAH12 (0.808), DNAH6 (0.538), DNAH5 (0.536), DNAH3 (0.525), plus ciliary assembly factors DNAI1 (0.691), CCDC114 (0.657), and centrosomal protein CETN2 (0.702). ClinVar contains 246 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants. Literature (30 papers) reveals DNAH14 deficiency impairs sperm motility by reducing flagellar beat amplitude, disrupts sperm annulus positioning compromising offspring postnatal development, and causes neurodevelopmental disorder with predominant ataxia when biallelic truncating variants occur. FUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: DNAH14 is an outer dynein arm motor protein that generates sliding force between microtubule doublets in sperm flagella and motile cilia, with specialized roles in regulating beat amplitude rather than frequency. MECHANISTIC PROPOSAL: DNAH14 hydrolyzes ATP via its AAA+ domains to produce conformational changes in the coiled coil stalk, generating ~8nm power strokes that slide adjacent microtubule doublets. The testis-specific expression and basal body localization suggest DNAH14 incorporates into axonemes during spermatogenesis. Reduced beat amplitude in DNAH14 deficiency indicates it may be an outer dynein arm component controlling stroke magnitude. The neurodevelopmental phenotype (ataxia) suggests DNAH14 also functions in neuronal cilia, possibly in cerebellar Purkinje cells where ciliary signaling coordinates motor coordination. The 246 ClinVar variants indicate DNAH14 mutations cause male infertility and ciliopathy-related neurodevelopmental disorders.
