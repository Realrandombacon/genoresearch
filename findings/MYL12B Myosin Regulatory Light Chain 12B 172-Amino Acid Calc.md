## Description
MYL12B (Myosin Regulatory Light Chain 12B, Ensembl ENSG00000118680) encodes a 172-amino acid calcium-binding regulatory protein (UniProt O14950, ~20 kDa) representing a partially characterized dark gene with only 29 publications despite its fundamental role in actomyosin cytoskeleton regulation, biomarker potential in sepsis-associated acute kidney injury (SA-AKI), and extensive clinical variant data. Structurally, MYL12B contains canonical EF-hand calcium-binding domains defining its regulatory function: dual EF-hand domain pairs (InterPro IPR002048/EF-hand domain, residues 29-77 and 98-133; IPR011992/EF-hand domain pair, residues 26-165; PF13499/EF-hand domain pair, residues 33-76; PF08976/EF-hand domain, residues 102-160; G3DSA:1.10.238.10/EF-hand homologous superfamily, residues 10-95 and 96-164), with conserved calcium-binding sites (IPR018247/EF-Hand 1 calcium-binding site, residues 42-54; PS00018/EF-hand calcium-binding domain, residues 42-54; PS50222/EF-hand profile, residues 29-64 and 98-133; SM00054/EF-hand calcium binding motif, residues 33-61 and 102-130); belongs to myosin regulatory light chain family (IPR050403/Myosin regulatory light chain, residues 1-170; PTHR23049/Myosin Regulatory Light Chain, residues 1-170); GO annotation includes calcium ion binding (GO:0005509). AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (global pLDDT 83.5, modeled residues 1-172), indicating a well-folded protein with stable EF-hand helix-loop-helix motifs characteristic of calcium-binding regulatory proteins. Strong vertebrate conservation is evident from orthologs: Mus musculus (172 aa, Q3THE2), Rattus norvegicus (172 aa, P18666)—maintaining identical length across mammals, suggesting critical functional constraints in actomyosin regulation. Functionally, Human Protein Atlas data reveals 'Low tissue specificity' with expression 'Detected in all' tissues, 'Low cancer specificity', 'Low immune cell specificity', and 'Cell type enhanced' single cell type specificity—consistent with a housekeeping role in cytoskeletal dynamics across cell types; protein class includes 'Plasma proteins', 'Predicted intracellular proteins'; molecular function annotations include 'Motor protein', 'Muscle protein', 'Myosin'. The mechanistic hypothesis: MYL12B functions as a calcium-sensitive regulatory switch in the actomyosin cytoskeleton that: (1) In normal physiology—binds to myosin II heavy chains (MYH9, MYH10, MYH11) via its EF-hand domains in calcium-dependent manner; phosphorylation by MYLK3 at Ser19 activates myosin ATPase activity promoting actin filament sliding for cell contraction, migration, and cytokinesis; dephosphorylation by PPP1R12A-targeted myosin phosphatase returns myosin to inactive state; ubiquitous expression reflects fundamental role in all cell types requiring motility or shape changes; (2) In disease—phosphorylated MYL12B released into plasma during septic AKI serves as biomarker of endothelial/epithelial barrier disruption and cytoskeletal damage; elevated MYL12B in JIA reflects inflammatory cytoskeletal remodeling in synovial cells; chromosome 18p deletions causing MYL12B haploinsufficiency may impair neural progenitor migration and cortical development contributing to 18p deletion syndrome phenotypes; altered MYL12B regulation may contribute to metastatic cell invasion via enhanced motility; (3) The high-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 83.5) with well-defined EF-hand motifs suggests precise calcium coordination geometry enabling rapid conformational switching upon calcium binding; (4) The 132 ClinVar CNVs suggest MYL12B is dosage-sensitive, consistent with roles in cytoskeletal dynamics requiring precise stoichiometry with myosin heavy chains; (5) Connection to LDLR/APOE/Aβ clearance suggests MYL12B may regulate endocytic trafficking via actomyosin-dependent vesicle transport, linking cytoskeletal function to lipid metabolism and neurodegeneration.
