## Description
KATNA1 encodes a 491 aa ATPase (UniProt O75449), also known as katanin p60, that functions as the catalytic subunit of the katanin heterodimeric complex responsible for ATP-dependent microtubule severing—a critical process for mitotic spindle organization, neuronal morphogenesis, and spermatogenesis. The protein features a sophisticated AAA+ ATPase architecture: an N-terminal domain (NTD, residues 5-72) for regulatory interactions; a central AAA+ ATPase domain (IPR003593/IPR003959/IPR003960, residues 241-383) containing the conserved Walker A/B motifs for ATP binding and hydrolysis; a P-loop NTP hydrolase fold (G3DSA:3.40.50.300, residues 203-381/468-482); and a C-terminal Spastin/Vps4-like domain (IPR015415, residues 451-489) mediating substrate engagement, embedded within the katanin p60 family scaffold (IPR028596). STRING interactions place KATNA1 at the absolute core of microtubule dynamics with near-perfect confidence partnerships: KATNB1 (0.999), the p80 regulatory subunit that targets katanin to centrosomes; KATNBL1 (0.954), an alternative regulatory subunit; LZTS2 (0.830), a microtubule stabilizer and tumor suppressor; ASPM (0.772), essential for mitotic spindle orientation in neural progenitors; GPD2 (0.739), linking metabolism to cytoskeletal dynamics; KATNAL1 (0.683), a paralogous catalytic subunit; NDEL1 (0.625), a dynein regulator; PSMD6 (0.620), a proteasome subunit; CUL3 (0.609), an E3 ubiquitin ligase scaffolding protein; and AURKA (0.424), Aurora kinase A regulating mitotic entry. HPA confirms ubiquitous expression across all tissues with subcellular localization to nucleoplasm and centriolar satellites, annotated for cell cycle, cell division, and mitosis functions—consistent with roles in spindle assembly and centrosome dynamics. ClinVar lists 24 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including extensive copy number losses/gains spanning chromosome 6q24.2-q27, deletions, duplications, and splice site mutations (c.163-140G>T, c.163-15C>A, c.320+3A>T), demonstrating dosage sensitivity and linking KATNA1 disruption to developmental disorders and reproductive defects. AlphaFold structure prediction yields high confidence (global pLDDT 75.0), revealing a well-folded AAA+ ATPase architecture optimized for hexameric ring assembly and microtubule engagement. Mechanistically, KATNA1 functions through multiple integrated modes: (1) As a microtubule-severing enzyme, KATNA1 forms hexameric rings that bind microtubule lattices and use ATP hydrolysis to generate mechanical force, extracting tubulin dimers and creating new microtubule ends for rapid cytoskeletal remodeling; (2) Partnership with KATNB1 targets the complex to centrosomes and spindle poles, enabling focused severing activity during mitosis; (3) Recent studies (2025-2026) reveal cooperative function with KATNAL1 during mammalian meiosis and spermiogenesis, explaining male fertility defects upon disruption; (4) Interaction with CRMP3 and EEF1B2 (2025-2026 literature) modulates neurite outgrowth in hippocampal neurons, indicating neurodevelopmental roles; (5) A deep intronic pentanucleotide AAAAT repeat expansion causes recurrent triploidy of maternal origin (2026), revealing unexpected roles in oocyte meiotic fidelity; (6) ASPM and LZTS2 interactions suggest coordination with spindle orientation pathways critical for cortical development. Disruption of KATNA1 would cause defective microtubule dynamics, aberrant mitotic spindle formation, impaired neuronal migration, male infertility, and meiotic errors—phenotypes consistent with the 6q25 deletion syndromes and reproductive defects observed in patients.
