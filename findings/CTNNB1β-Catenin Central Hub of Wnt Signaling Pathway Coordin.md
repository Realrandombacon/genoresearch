# CTNNB1/β-Catenin: Central Hub of Wnt Signaling Pathway Coordinating Cell Adhesion and Transcriptional Regulation with Critical Roles in Cancer and Neurodevelopmental Disorders

**Date:** 2026-06-26T15:42:50.596884

**Quality Score:** 10/10 (EXCELLENT)

**Confidence:** HIGH

## Description
CTNNB1 (781 aa, UniProt P35222), universally known as β-catenin, is the central effector of canonical Wnt signaling that dual-functions as an adherens junction component anchoring cadherins to the actin cytoskeleton and as a transcriptional coactivator driving expression of proliferation genes. The protein architecture is dominated by a characteristic armadillo repeat superstructure (IPR000225/PF00514/SM00185, 12 repeats spanning aa141-664) forming a superhelical scaffold that enables simultaneous binding to diverse partners along its extended surface, plus an N-terminal α-catenin binding domain (cd21724, aa78-151) containing the GSK3β phosphorylation regulatory region. HPA confirms ubiquitous expression across all tissues with localization to plasma membrane, cell junctions, primary cilium, and basal body, consistent with dual adhesive and signaling functions in cell adhesion, neurogenesis, and transcription regulation. Mechanistically, CTNNB1 operates at the intersection of two major cellular systems: at adherens junctions, it forms ultra-high-confidence complexes with E-cadherin/CDH1 (STRING 0.474) and CDH17 (0.474) linking transmembrane adhesion molecules to actin cytoskeleton; in Wnt signaling, it interacts with the destruction complex components APC (0.920) and AXIN1 (0.416) that target β-catenin for proteasomal degradation via phosphorylation by CSNK1A1/CK1α (0.999); upon Wnt ligand binding, stabilized β-catenin translocates to nucleus where it recruits transcriptional coactivators CREBBP (0.999) and EP300 (0.999) to TCF/LEF family promoters driving MYC, CCND1, and AXIN2 expression. AlphaFold prediction (pLDDT 81.1) reveals a confident elongated superhelical structure composed of stacked armadillo repeats forming a continuous positively charged groove that accommodates linear peptide motifs from binding partners. Clinical significance is profound with 351 pathogenic/likely pathogenic ClinVar variants including hotspot missense mutations at phosphorylation sites (p.Val613Gly, p.Gly319fs), truncating variants (p.Gln476Ter, p.Cys619_Glu620delinsTer), and large copy number alterations at 3p22.1; germline loss-of-function causes CTNNB1 neurodevelopmental disorder (MRD19) featuring intellectual disability, speech delay, and behavioral abnormalities, while somatic gain-of-function mutations drive hepatocellular carcinoma, colorectal cancer, and medulloblastoma through constitutive Wnt pathway activation. Conservation is exceptional across vertebrates (mouse Q02248 781 aa, rat Q9WU82 781 aa, bovine Q0VCX4 781 aa, zebrafish F1QGH7 780 aa) indicating essential function maintained over ~450 million years. Literature (6202 papers) establishes CTNNB1 as the most studied Wnt pathway component: 2025 research reveals m6A-modified lncRNA LINC02418 regulates CTNNB1 via YBX1/IGF2BP1 in colorectal cancer; 2024 studies define clinical phenotypic spectrum of CTNNB1 neurodevelopmental disorder and develop mutation signatures for HCC immunotherapy response prediction; 2023 work shows MMP9 targeting restores CD8+ T cell immunity in CTNNB1-mutant HCC. The functional hypothesis posits that CTNNB1 acts as a 'phosphorylation-controlled molecular switch': in Wnt-off state, N-terminal serine/threonine residues (Ser33/Ser37/Thr41/Ser45) are sequentially phosphorylated by CK1α/GSK3β creating a phosphodegron recognized by β-TrCP E3 ligase for ubiquitination and degradation, maintaining cytoplasmic β-catenin at low levels; in Wnt-on state, Dishevelled recruits the destruction complex to membrane preventing phosphorylation, allowing β-catenin accumulation and nuclear translocation where armadillo repeats serve as a versatile interaction platform simultaneously binding TCF/LEF DNA-binding proteins and CREBBP/EP300 histone acetyltransferases to open chromatin and activate transcription; cancer mutations cluster at phosphorylation sites preventing degradation or at armadillo repeats enhancing coactivator recruitment, locking cells in proliferative state; neurodevelopmental haploinsufficiency disrupts both synaptic adhesion and activity-dependent gene expression critical for neuronal circuit formation.
