## Description
CAMKMT (Calmodulin-Lysine N-Methyltransferase, UniProt Q7Z624) is a 323 aa S-adenosyl-L-methionine-dependent methyltransferase belonging to the CAMKMT family that catalyzes trimethylation of calmodulin at specific lysine residues to regulate calcium signaling and inflammatory responses. The molecular architecture includes a conserved AdoMet-dependent methyltransferase domain (cd02440, positions 150-259) within the VP39-like structural fold (G3DSA:3.40.50.150, positions 60-323) and lysine methyltransferase family domain (IPR019410/PF10294, positions 125-276). Literature (21 papers) establishes CAMKMT roles in: (1) CAMKMT knockdown delaying myopia progression by reducing calcium ion-mediated ER stress-induced apoptosis (2026), (2) novel CAMKMT exon3-ALK exon20 fusion variant identified in primary pulmonary mucinous adenocarcinoma (2019, 3 citations), (3) CAMKMT polymorphisms affecting growth traits in Ujumqin sheep (2016, 18 citations), (4) anti-inflammatory activity through suppressing caspase-11 non-canonical inflammasome activation (2023, 9 citations), (5) CAMKMT deficiency delineating phenotype with congenital myasthenic syndrome-22 in contiguous gene syndrome with PREPL (2017, 23 citations). HPA confirms ubiquitous expression across all tissues with low tissue specificity and nucleoplasm/nucleoli localization, consistent with regulatory role in calcium signaling and transcription. STRING interactions reveal methyltransferase and contiguous gene network: PREPL (0.976) - prolyl endopeptidase-like protein at same 2p21 locus, deleted in contiguous gene syndrome; PPM1B (0.953) - protein phosphatase regulating calcium signaling; SLC3A1 (0.935) - amino acid transporter at same locus; EHMT2 (0.861), DOT1L (0.861), SUV39H1 (0.687), PRDM2 (0.454) - lysine methyltransferases; PLOD1/2/3 (0.650-0.655) - procollagen-lysine hydroxylases. ClinVar contains 20 pathogenic variants including large deletions at 2p21-25, copy number losses/gains, nonsense (p.Tyr99Ter), splice (c.377-6T>G), and promoter variants, with disease associations including congenital myasthenic syndrome-22 in contiguous gene deletions with PREPL. AlphaFold shows high-confidence structure (pLDDT 84.8) indicating well-folded methyltransferase core with stable catalytic domain, typical of active SAM-dependent enzymes. Conservation: mouse Camkmt (Q3U2J5) is 323 aa, rat (B0K012) is 323 aa, Xenopus (Q6GQ33) is 318 aa - highly conserved across vertebrates. Functional hypothesis: CAMKMT specifically trimethylates calmodulin at lysine residues (likely K115 or K148 based on structural homology) to modulate calcium-dependent signaling cascades. Trimethylated calmodulin exhibits altered affinity for target enzymes including calcineurin, CaMKII, and nitric oxide synthase, thereby regulating inflammatory responses, neuronal excitability, and ER stress pathways. The anti-inflammatory mechanism involves suppression of caspase-11 inflammasome activation, potentially through calmodulin-dependent regulation of calcium flux. In myopia, CAMKMT-mediated calmodulin methylation influences ER stress-induced apoptosis in retinal cells. The contiguous gene syndrome with PREPL explains congenital myasthenic features when both genes are deleted. Pathogenic variants disrupting the methyltransferase domain impair calmodulin modification, leading to dysregulated calcium signaling and inflammatory hyperactivation. CAMKMT represents an understudied epigenetic-like regulator of calcium signaling with emerging roles in inflammation, myopia, and neuromuscular disorders.
