# IGFBP5: A Dual-Function IGF-Binding Protein and Extracellular Matrix Scaffold Regulating Tissue Repair and Fibrosis

**Date:** 2026-06-26T16:30:31.807805

**Quality Score:** 9/10 (EXCELLENT)

**Confidence:** MEDIUM

## Description
IGFBP5 (Insulin-like growth factor-binding protein 5, UniProt P24593) is a 272-amino acid secreted protein that serves as a critical modulator of the IGF axis and an independent extracellular matrix (ECM) component. InterPro analysis reveals a conserved bipartite domain architecture: an N-terminal cysteine-rich domain (IPR000867, IPR009030, aa 23-103) responsible for high-affinity binding to IGF1 and IGF2, and a C-terminal thyroglobulin type-1 domain (IPR000716, G3DSA:4.10.800.10, aa 179-267) that mediates interactions with ECM proteins and cell surface receptors. The AlphaFold predicted structure shows confident folding (pLDDT 76.1), supporting a stable globular conformation suitable for ternary complex formation. STRING interaction data places IGFBP5 at the center of the IGF regulatory network, with near-perfect associations to IGF1 (0.999), IGF2 (0.998), and the acid-labile subunit IGFALS (0.995), which together form the long-circulating ternary complex. Crucially, IGFBP5 also interacts strongly with proteases PAPPA and PAPPA2 (0.977, 0.867) that cleave it to release bioactive IGFs, and ECM components FHL2 (0.819), FN1 (0.812), and THBS1 (0.805), indicating a structural role independent of IGF transport. HPA expression profiles show extreme tissue enhancement in the cervix (1401.0 nTPM), with significant presence in vesicles and plasma, suggesting roles in reproductive tissue remodeling and systemic growth regulation. ClinVar records list 24 pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains at 2q35, implicating dosage sensitivity in developmental disorders. We hypothesize that IGFBP5 acts as a 'molecular switch' in tissue repair: in its intact form, it sequesters IGFs to prevent over-proliferation, while proteolytic cleavage by PAPPA releases IGFs to stimulate regeneration. Simultaneously, its C-terminal domain anchors it to the ECM via fibronectin and thrombospondin, providing a scaffold for cell migration. Dysregulation of this balance likely drives fibrotic diseases (e.g., pulmonary fibrosis) where excessive IGFBP5 accumulation inhibits repair or promotes pathological scarring.
