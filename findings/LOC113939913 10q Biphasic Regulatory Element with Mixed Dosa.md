## Description
LOC113939913 is a Sharpr-MPRA regulatory region 9711 located on chromosome 10q24.31 (genomic coordinates: 101080991-101081285 on NC_000010.11). This 295 bp genomic sequence was predicted as a transcriptional regulatory region based on ENCODE chromatin state analysis and experimentally validated as a functional repressive element (silencer) using Sharpr-MPRA in both HepG2 liver carcinoma cells and K562 erythroleukemia cells, indicating broad cell-type activity. As a non-coding regulatory element, it lacks protein-coding potential (no UniProt entry, no InterPro domains, no AlphaFold structure, no STRING interactions, no HPA protein expression). ClinVar analysis identified 4 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants: 3 are copy number gains (duplications) and 1 is a copy number loss (deletion), indicating mixed dosage sensitivity (both gain and loss are pathogenic). The variant count (4 total; 3 gains, 1 loss) contrasts with the purely duplication-predominant pattern seen in LOC113939919 (11q) and LOC113939933 (12q), suggesting this element regulates a gene or pathway where precise stoichiometry is critical (haploinsufficiency and triplosensitivity). The 10q chromosomal region contains critical genes including PTEN (phosphatase and tensin homolog - tumor suppressor), FGFR2 (fibroblast growth factor receptor 2 - craniosynostosis syndromes), SFTPB (surfactant protein B - lethal neonatal respiratory distress), and DNA repair genes. As a repressive enhancer/silencer active in liver and blood lineages, this element likely suppresses expression of target genes in cis. The mixed pathogenicity pattern suggests it may regulate a tumor suppressor (like PTEN) where loss of the silencer causes overexpression (oncogenic) and duplication causes underexpression (loss of function), or vice versa. This element may contribute to 10q deletion/duplication syndromes, renal anomalies, or respiratory distress through SFTPB/PTEN pathway dysregulation.
