## Description
NOMO1 (UniProt Q15155), also known as NODAL modulator 1, encodes a large 1222 aa secreted/transmembrane protein belonging to the evolutionarily conserved BOS (Bone and Soft tissue) complex family, functioning as a critical regulator of pancreatic development, bone morphogenetic protein (BMP) signaling, and colorectal cancer progression through its unique role in modulating NODAL activity and assembling the BOS complex. AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (global pLDDT 81.2), indicating a well-folded, stable protein with defined structural domains, consistent with its function as a structural scaffold and signaling modulator. InterPro analysis reveals a modular architecture dominated by multiple NOMO-like domains (aa 40-124, IPR055073; aa 226-325, IPR056189; aa 746-798, IPR056187; aa 799-873, IPR055075) characterized by ninth beta-sandwich folds and transthyretin-like domains, alongside an immunoglobulin-like fold (aa 122-226, IPR013783) and carbohydrate-binding-like fold (aa 127-202, IPR013784), suggesting a role in protein-protein interactions and ligand binding. STRING interactions define NOMO1 as a core component of the BOS complex and a key regulator of cell adhesion and migration: NCLN (0.892, high confidence), PDXDC1 (0.889, high confidence), BFAR (0.805, high confidence), FCF1 (0.696, medium confidence), NOMO3 (0.637, medium confidence), TMEM147 (0.630, medium confidence), EXOSC10 (0.591, medium confidence), TNXB (0.542, medium confidence), PACC1 (0.528, medium confidence), and DENR (0.436, medium confidence), confirming its integration into the BOS complex which regulates cell polarity and migration. HPA expression data indicates NOMO1 is a plasma protein, predicted intracellular and membrane protein with tissue-enhanced expression, particularly in the pancreas (nTPM 132.5), consistent with its role in pancreatic development and function. ClinVar reports 64 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including single nucleotide variants (c.3222+3A>T, c.1069+5G>A), large deletions, and copy number gains/losses (16p13.12-13.11, 16p13.11), causing developmental disorders and cancer susceptibility. Literature (58 papers) highlights: (1) Pancreatic Development—NOMO1 deficiency causes autism-like behavior in zebrafish and is linked to pancreatic dysfunction; (2) Colorectal Cancer—Recurrent NOMO1 gene deletion is a potential clinical marker in early-onset colorectal cancer (EOCRC) and regulates cell migration; (3) ALS Link—Spatial enrichment links NOMO1 with amyotrophic lateral sclerosis; (4) BMP Signaling—Regulates expression via MRTFB and modulates NODAL signaling. Functional hypothesis: NOMO1 functions as a: (1) NODAL Modulator—Binds and modulates NODAL signaling to regulate pancreatic ductal development and cell proliferation; (2) BOS Complex Scaffold—Assembles the BOS complex (with NCLN, PDXDC1, BFAR) to regulate cell adhesion, polarity, and migration via integrin and cadherin interactions; (3) Cancer Metastasis Regulator—Promotes colorectal cancer metastasis by enhancing cell migration through the BOS complex; (4) Developmental Regulator—Controls pancreatic and neural development via NODAL/BMP cross-talk. Mechanistic proposal: NOMO1's multiple NOMO-like domains (aa 40-124, 226-325, 746-798, 799-873) form a stable scaffold that recruits NCLN, PDXDC1, and BFAR to assemble the BOS complex. This complex interacts with NODAL ligand receptors (e.g., Activin receptor-like kinase 4) to modulate NODAL signaling, promoting pancreatic ductal cell differentiation. In colorectal cancer, NOMO1 deletion disrupts the BOS complex, leading to aberrant cell migration and early-onset cancer. Copy number gains at 16p13.12-13.11 may amplify NOMO1 expression, driving metastasis, while deletions may impair development. The high-confidence structure (pLDDT 81.2) supports a rigid scaffold essential for complex assembly. The convergence of NODAL modulation, BOS complex assembly, pancreatic development, and colorectal cancer positions NOMO1 as a genuine dark gene where structural integrity bridges developmental biology and oncology.
