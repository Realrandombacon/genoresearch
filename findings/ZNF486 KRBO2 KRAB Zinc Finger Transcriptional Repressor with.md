## Description
ZNF486 (Gene ID: 90649, UniProt Q96H40, alias KRBO2) is a 463 amino acid Krüppel-associated box (KRAB) domain-containing zinc finger protein located on chromosome 19p12 (NC_000019.10, positions 20167213-20200487, ~33kb genomic region). This gene encodes a canonical KRAB-zinc finger transcription factor architecture with clear molecular function predictions but incompletely characterized biological roles. InterPro domain analysis reveals N-terminal KRAB box domain (IPR001909/PF01352, positions 13-84; cd07765 KRAB-A box, positions 15-52) functioning as transcriptional repression module, followed by three C2H2-type zinc finger DNA-binding domains (IPR013087/PF00096, positions 154-181, 210-237, 238-265) enabling sequence-specific DNA recognition. GO annotations confirm regulation of DNA-templated transcription (GO:0006355) and RNA polymerase II cis-regulatory region binding. STRING interaction network identifies 7 medium-confidence protein partners: ZNF626 (0.630, another KRAB-ZFP suggesting heterodimerization or co-regulation), TRIM28 (0.533, also known as KAP1/TIF1β—the universal KRAB domain co-repressor that recruits SETDB1 histone methyltransferase and HP1 for heterochromatin formation), USP49 (0.458, deubiquitinase), PCDHGB5 (0.418, protocadherin), ZNF93 (0.408, another KRAB-ZFP), LGALS16 (0.404, galectin), and ANKRD18A (0.403, ankyrin repeat protein). The TRIM28 interaction is particularly significant as it places ZNF486 within the canonical KRAB-ZFP silencing machinery. Human Protein Atlas classifies ZNF486 (ENSG00000256229) as a predicted intracellular protein with thyroid gland-enriched expression (32.4 nTPM top tissue), tissue-enhanced RNA specificity, cancer-enhanced expression pattern, and immune cell-enhanced blood cell specificity. AlphaFold structure prediction (AF-Q96H40-F1) yields medium-confidence model (global pLDDT 68.2), consistent with KRAB-ZFP architecture where zinc finger domains form ordered structures while linker regions remain flexible. ClinVar lists 9 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains spanning chromosome 19p-q regions (e.g., chr19:260912-58956888x3, chr19:11227942-44626354x3); no specific monogenic disease is attributed solely to ZNF486 point mutations, suggesting dosage sensitivity rather than loss-of-function pathology. Conservation evidenced by primate orthologs (Pan paniscus A0A2R9BAC9, 463 aa; Pongo abelii A0A663DHS3, 463 aa) with identical lengths indicating strong evolutionary constraint. Functional hypothesis: ZNF486 functions as a sequence-specific transcriptional repressor that recognizes target DNA motifs via its three C2H2 zinc fingers and recruits TRIM28/KAP1 co-repressor complex through its KRAB domain, leading to H3K9me3 deposition and heterochromatin formation at target loci. Thyroid-enriched expression suggests potential role in thyroid hormone-responsive gene regulation or thyroid development. Cancer-enhanced expression pattern indicates possible oncogenic function through silencing of tumor suppressor genes. Disease mechanism: ZNF486 haploinsufficiency or overexpression may contribute to chromosome 19p12 deletion/duplication syndromes, though specific phenotype remains undefined. The 9 ClinVar CNVs suggest dosage sensitivity; triplication may cause aberrant silencing of target genes while deletion may cause derepression. TRIM28 interaction suggests ZNF486 operates within epigenetic silencing pathway; disruption may alter chromatin states at developmental genes. Priority experiments include ChIP-seq to identify DNA binding motifs and genomic targets, co-IP validation of TRIM28 interaction, CRISPR knockout in thyroid cell lines to assess transcriptional consequences, investigation of ZNF486 role in thyroid differentiation, and assessment of ZNF486 expression levels in thyroid cancers versus normal tissue.
