# ANKRD39: Ankyrin Repeat Scaffold Protein in E3 Ligase Complexes

## Description
ANKRD39 (Q53RE8) encodes a 183-amino acid ankyrin repeat-containing protein localized to the nucleoplasm and vesicles. The protein contains 3 canonical ankyrin repeat domains (IPR002110, PF12796, positions 30-161) that form a curved solenoid structure for protein-protein interactions. Sequence analysis reveals 27.3% charged residues and 36.1% hydrophobic content, consistent with a structured scaffold protein.

BLAST analysis demonstrates strong evolutionary conservation: 91.8% identity with Bos taurus ortholog and 90.2% with Mus musculus, indicating purifying selection across 90+ million years. Distant homologs exist in Dictyostelium (43.8% identity), suggesting an ancient eukaryotic origin.

STRING interaction network identifies 10 partners with medium confidence (0.41-0.67): ANKRD36B (0.671), FAHD2B (0.622), UIMC1/RAP80 (0.591), BARD1 (0.581), UBE2S (0.580), and CDC27/ANAPC4 (0.576). These interactions cluster into two functional modules: (1) DNA damage response via UIMC1-BARD1-UBE2S (BRCA1-A complex, E3 ubiquitin ligase activity) and (2) cell cycle regulation via ANAPC4-CDC27 (anaphase-promoting complex).

HPA data shows ubiquitous expression across all tissues with nucleoplasmic localization, consistent with a housekeeping regulatory role. ClinVar documents 31 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly copy number losses and gains at 2q11.1-11.2, implicating ANKRD39 dosage sensitivity in disease etiology.

FUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: ANKRD39 functions as an ankyrin repeat scaffold that bridges E3 ubiquitin ligase complexes to cell cycle machinery. The ankyrin repeats mediate transient protein interactions while the C-terminal region may recruit specific substrates. Through interaction with BARD1-UBE2S and ANAPC4-CDC27, ANKD39 likely coordinates ubiquitin-dependent proteolysis during G2/M transition and DNA damage checkpoint recovery. Copy number variants disrupt stoichiometric balance of these complexes, leading to genomic instability.
