# ELAPOR2/KIAA1324L: Multi-Domain Endolysosomal Lectin with TNF Receptor-Like Folds Regulating Autophagy

**Date:** 2026-04-06T12:39:07.703072

**Quality Score:** 3.25/10  (E=4.9, D=1.6) [WEAK]

## Description
ELAPOR2 (Endosome-Lysosome Associated Apoptosis and Autophagy Regulator Family Member 2, also KIAA1324L or EIG121L) is a large 1029 amino acid protein (UniProt A8MWY0) with a well-folded multi-domain architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 76.1, high confidence). The protein contains an elaborate domain organization characteristic of endolysosomal trafficking regulators: multiple TNF receptor-like cysteine-rich domains (G3DSA:2.10.50.10, IPR009030 at residues 284-343, 389-436, 607-672), a mannose-6-phosphate receptor homology (MRH) domain (IPR044865/IPR056607, residues 672-877), two galactose-binding lectin domains (IPR056608/IPR056609, residues 133-285 and 438-601), and a C-terminal ELAPOR-specific domain (IPR056606, residues 882-917). The protein belongs to the ELAPOR1/2 family (IPR039181, residues 34-1028).\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals modest but specific connections to endolysosomal and membrane trafficking proteins: OR5M8 (0.575), zinc finger proteins ZNF804B (0.508) and ZNF287 (0.422), tyrosylprotein sulfotransferase TPST1 (0.417), TMEM243 (0.415), ALS2CL (0.407, an ALS2-associated endolysosomal protein), and SIGLECL1 (0.405, a sialic acid-binding lectin). This interaction pattern suggests roles in glycoprotein sorting and membrane receptor trafficking.\n\nHPA expression shows lung-enriched pattern (20.6 nTPM) with tissue-enhanced specificity and endoplasmic reticulum localization, consistent with involvement in secretory pathway glycoprotein processing. The protein is classified as a predicted membrane protein.\n\nClinVar shows 0 pathogenic variants, suggesting either functional redundancy with ELAPOR1 or that loss-of-function phenotypes have not yet been captured in clinical databases. Conservation is evident from Xenopus ortholog (Q6DDW2, 995 aa) and amphioxus orthologs (~1023 aa), indicating ancient evolutionary origin in deuterostomes.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: ELAPOR2 acts as a multi-domain lectin scaffold in the endosome-lysosome system, where its MRH domain recognizes mannose-6-phosphate tags on lysosomal enzymes while TNF receptor-like domains mediate protein-protein interactions with trafficking machinery. The galactose-binding domains may recognize glycosylated cargo or membrane receptors. ELAPOR2 likely regulates autophagy flux by controlling lysosomal enzyme sorting or receptor-mediated endocytosis, with dysfunction potentially contributing to neurodegenerative diseases given ALS2CL interaction.
