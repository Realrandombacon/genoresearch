# ABCF2: A Cytosolic ABCF Family ATPase Regulating Ribosome Biogenesis and Translation Initiation via ABCE1-Dependent Mechanisms

**Date:** 2026-06-26T17:23:47.014059

**Quality Score:** 9/10 (EXCELLENT)

**Confidence:** HIGH

## Description
ABCF2 (ATP-Binding Cassette Subfamily F Member 2, UniProt Q9UG63), also known as ABC28 or HUSSY-18, is a 623-amino acid cytosolic ATPase that functions as a ribosome-associated translation regulator rather than a membrane transporter despite belonging to the ABC superfamily. Unlike canonical ABC transporters, ABCF subfamily members lack transmembrane domains and instead operate in the cytosol to modulate ribosome function and translation initiation. InterPro analysis reveals a bipartite ATPase architecture characteristic of ABCF proteins: two tandem ABC transporter ATP-binding domains (IPR003439, PF00005, cd03221, aa 86-325 and aa 396-613) separated by an ABC-transporter extension domain (IPR032781, PF12848, aa 296-368); the protein contains AAA+ ATPase domains (IPR003593, aa 110-303 and aa 422-590) and belongs to the ABCF protein family (IPR050611); conserved P-loop NTP hydrolase folds (G3DSA:3.40.50.300, IPR027417) span both nucleotide-binding domains. The AlphaFold predicted structure shows high confidence folding (pLDDT 83.3), consistent with a stable two-lobed ATPase optimized for conformational changes during ATP hydrolysis cycles. STRING interaction data places ABCF2 within the translation machinery network with high confidence associations to: ABCE1 (0.921), the ribosome-splitting factor essential for translation termination and ribosome recycling; multiple ribosomal proteins including MRPL20 (0.816), MRPL27 (0.808), RPL35 (0.806), RPL23A (0.805), RPS18 (0.801), RPL11 (0.798), MRPL21 (0.795), and RPS3 (0.793); and ABCB1 (0.812), a canonical membrane transporter representing evolutionary relationship. HPA expression profiles confirm ubiquitous detection across all tissues with cytosolic localization and low tissue specificity, consistent with housekeeping roles in fundamental cellular processes; classified as a transporter though functioning in translation regulation. ClinVar records document 84 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number losses (7q33-36.3, 7q34-36.3, 7q35-36.3 spanning 5-22 Mb regions), copy number gains, splice site mutations (c.722+5G>C, c.1228-16C>G), missense variants (p.Asn560Ser in C-terminal ATPase domain), and 3' UTR variants (c.*34C>G), though specific disease phenotypes remain undefined. We hypothesize that ABCF2 functions as a translation initiation/elongation regulator: upon ribosome assembly or stress conditions, ABCF2 binds to the 60S ribosomal subunit via interactions with ribosomal proteins; ATP binding induces conformational changes in the twin ABC domains that transmit mechanical force to the ribosome; this regulates subunit joining, tRNA accommodation, or translocation during elongation; coordination with ABCE1 suggests ABCF2 may participate in ribosome recycling following translation termination or in quality control pathways that dissociate stalled ribosomes; the ubiquitous expression pattern reflects essential roles in protein synthesis across all cell types; loss of ABCF2 would impair translation efficiency, cause ribosome stalling, and trigger integrated stress response; cancer associations (hepatocellular carcinoma, renal cell carcinoma) suggest ABCF2 dysregulation contributes to malignant progression through altered proteome composition; the 84 ClinVar variants indicate dosage sensitivity and functional constraint.
