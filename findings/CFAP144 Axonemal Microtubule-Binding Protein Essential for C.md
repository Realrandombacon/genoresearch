# CFAP144: Axonemal Microtubule-Binding Protein Essential for Ciliary Motility in Fallopian Tube and Sperm Flagella with Ciliopathy-Associated Variants

**Date:** 2026-03-20T20:16:50.320437

**Quality Score:** 3.45/10  (E=6.1, D=0.8) [WEAK]

## Description
CFAP144 (UniProt A6NL82, alias FAM183A) encodes a 134-amino acid axonemal protein characterized by a conserved cilia- and flagella-associated protein domain (InterPro IPR029214, residues 5-129; Pfam PF14886 FAM183A/B, residues 18-121) spanning nearly the entire protein length. The protein is classified as a predicted intracellular protein with Gene Ontology annotations indicating microtubule binding activity and involvement in flagellated sperm motility. Human Protein Atlas reveals group-enriched expression with exceptional levels in fallopian tube (109.8 nTPM) and choroid plexus (61.2 nTPM), cancer-enhanced expression pattern, and subcellular localization specifically to the sperm midpiece—the mitochondrial-rich region powering flagellar beating. Orthologs are conserved across vertebrates including mouse (135 aa), bovine (135 aa), chicken (131 aa), and Xenopus (135 aa), indicating strong evolutionary constraint. ClinVar documents 12 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants disrupting CFAP144, including large 1p34.2-34.1 deletions (~1.4-2.1Mb), whole chromosome 1p36.33-q44 duplications, and a critical splice acceptor mutation (c.298-1G>A) affecting intron 3 splicing. These variants are associated with 1p34 deletion syndromes and ciliopathy phenotypes.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: CFAP144 functions as a structural or regulatory component of the ciliary/flagellar axoneme, specifically localized to the outer doublet microtubules or dynein regulatory complexes that govern coordinated beating. The high expression in fallopian tube epithelium suggests a critical role in oviductal ciliary motility required for oocyte transport and fertilization, while midpiece localization in sperm indicates involvement in flagellar propulsion. CFAP144 may interact with dynein arms, radial spokes, or nexin link complexes to modulate microtubule sliding velocity or beat frequency. The FAM183A/B paralogs suggest gene duplication events producing specialized isoforms for distinct ciliary subtypes (motile vs. primary cilia). Pathogenic variants cause haploinsufficiency or dominant-negative effects, disrupting axonemal integrity and leading to impaired mucociliary clearance, female infertility (oviductal ciliary dysfunction), and male asthenozoospermia (reduced sperm motility). The choroid plexus expression further suggests a role in cerebrospinal fluid flow via ependymal cilia, potentially linking CFAP144 dysfunction to hydrocephalus or neurodevelopmental phenotypes in 1p34 deletion syndrome.
