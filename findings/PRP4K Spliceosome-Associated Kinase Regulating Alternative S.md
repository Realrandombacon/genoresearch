## Description
PRP4K (Pre-mRNA Processing Factor 4 Kinase, also known as PRPF4B) is a partially characterized serine/threonine kinase with only 24 publications, recently implicated (2024-2025) in alternative splicing regulation, YAP/Hippo signaling modulation, autophagy via CHMP4B/vps32 splicing circuit, and antimicrobial peptide transcription in innate immunity. This 1007-amino acid nuclear protein (UniProt Q13523) contains a C-terminal protein kinase domain (PF00069/IPR000719, positions 688-1003) with conserved serine/threonine kinase active site (PS00108, positions 811-823), PRP4 catalytic domain (IPR044092, positions 686-1003), and extensive N-terminal regulatory regions. AlphaFold predicts medium-confidence structure (pLDDT 60.2), indicating a structured kinase domain with intrinsically disordered N-terminal regions typical of spliceosome-associated regulatory proteins.\n\nSTRING interactions reveal PRP4K operates at the core of the spliceosome machinery: PRPF6 (0.973), PRPF31 (0.965), PRPF4 (0.959), PRPF8 (0.957), EFTUD2 (0.956), SART1 (0.948), SNRNP200 (0.940), DDX23 (0.937) - all essential spliceosomal components. This positions PRP4K as a regulatory kinase that phosphorylates spliceosome proteins to modulate alternative splicing decisions. Lower-confidence partners RBM25 (0.419) and PNN (0.413) link to RNA binding and cell adhesion pathways.\n\nHPA shows ubiquitous expression with specific localization to nuclear speckles - the subnuclear compartments where pre-mRNA splicing factors concentrate. ClinVar contains 48 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including missense mutations (p.Ile860Val in kinase domain) and copy number variations on 6p25.3-25.1, though specific diseases remain uncharacterized. Conservation across mouse (1007 aa), rat (1007 aa), and bovine (1008 aa) indicates strong functional constraint.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: PRP4K is a spliceosome-associated regulatory kinase that phosphorylates core spliceosomal proteins (PRPF6, PRPF4, PRPF8) to control alternative splicing decisions during cellular stress, differentiation, and immune responses. The disordered N-terminal region may serve as a scaffold for recruiting specific splicing substrates or regulatory complexes. Recent evidence linking PRP4K to YAP signaling suggests it may phosphorylate Hippo pathway components or regulate YAP target gene splicing. The CHMP4B/vps32 splicing circuit connection indicates PRP4K coordinates autophagy through alternative splicing of ESCRT-III components. Pathogenic variants disrupting kinase activity may cause widespread splicing defects, potentially contributing to developmental disorders or cancers with aberrant splice isoform expression.
