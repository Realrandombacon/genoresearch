# UTP18: WD40-Repeat Scaffold of the 90S Pre-Ribosome with 13 ClinVar Variants, Nucleolar Localization, and p21-Mediated Colorectal Cancer Progression

## Description
UTP18 (U3 Small Nucleolar RNA-Associated Protein 18; UniProt Q9Y5J1) is a 556 amino acid essential nucleolar scaffold protein with only 23 publications, classifying it as a genuine dark gene despite critical roles in ribosome biogenesis and emerging functions in colorectal cancer progression.\n\nSTRUCTURAL ARCHITECTURE: InterPro analysis reveals a classic WD40-repeat architecture: the C-terminal region (residues 230-551) forms a beta-propeller structure composed of multiple WD40 repeats (IPR001680/PF00400 at residues 239-279, 284-324, 329-369, 385-410), belonging to the WD40/YVTN repeat-like superfamily (IPR015943/IPR036322). The protein is a canonical member of the UTP18 family (IPR045161/PTHR18359, residues 16-553), optimized for protein-protein interactions within large ribonucleoprotein complexes. AlphaFold predicts high confidence structure (global pLDDT 78.1), consistent with a rigid beta-propeller core typical of stable scaffolding proteins.\n\nINTERACTION NETWORK: STRING-DB identifies ultra-high confidence interactions (all scores 0.999) exclusively with components of the 90S pre-ribosome (small subunit processome): UTP15, WDR75, UTP6, KRR1, BYSL, PWP2, UTP3, WDR3, MPHOSPH10, and NOL6. This exclusive interaction profile confirms UTP18's role as an integral, non-exchangeable structural component of the early ribosomal assembly machinery, specifically involved in 18S rRNA processing.\n\nEXPRESSION & LOCALIZATION: HPA shows ubiquitous expression detected in all tissues with low tissue specificity, consistent with the fundamental requirement for ribosome biogenesis in all dividing cells. Subcellular localization is strictly restricted to the nucleolus and nucleolar rim, with additional presence in the nucleoplasm and nuclear membrane. Classified as an essential protein involved in rRNA processing.\n\nDISEASE LANDSCAPE: ClinVar contains 13 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including large copy number gains/losses spanning 17q12-25 and specific intronic/snV changes. No monogenic disease is yet definitively assigned, but 17q deletions are associated with developmental anomalies. Recent 2023 literature establishes a critical oncogenic role: UTP18 drives adenoma-to-carcinoma progression in colorectal cancer by destabilizing p21 (CDKN1A) mRNA, thereby removing a key cell cycle checkpoint and promoting uncontrolled proliferation. This links ribosome biogenesis stress directly to tumor suppression pathways.\n\nCONSERVATION: Orthologs confirmed in mouse (Q5SSI6, 552 aa), yeast (P40362, 594 aa), and Drosophila (Q9V7P1, 506 aa), indicating extreme evolutionary conservation of the WD40 scaffold across eukaryotes.\n\nFUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: UTP18 functions as a rigid WD40 beta-propeller scaffold that stabilizes the assembly of the 90S pre-ribosome, facilitating the correct folding and processing of 18S rRNA. By bridging multiple processome components (UTP15, WDR75, etc.), it ensures the fidelity of small ribosomal subunit production. The discovery of its role in p21 mRNA instability suggests a secondary, perhaps regulatory function where UTP18 levels or activity modulate cell cycle checkpoints independently of its ribosomal role, or that ribosome biogenesis stress signals through UTP18 to control p21 stability. The 13 ClinVar variants warrant investigation for haploinsufficiency effects on development and cancer susceptibility.
