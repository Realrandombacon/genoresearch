## Description
TARS2 (Threonyl-tRNA Synthetase 2, Mitochondrial) is a 718 amino acid class II aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase that catalyzes the attachment of threonine to its cognate tRNA in mitochondria, serving as an essential component of the mitochondrial translation machinery required for oxidative phosphorylation complex assembly. InterPro analysis identifies canonical threonyl-tRNA synthetase domains including ThrRS catalytic core (cd00771, residues 300-613), anticodon-binding domain (cd00860, residues 613-704), TGS regulatory domain (IPR004095, cd01667, residues 55-126), class II aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase signature (IPR002314, residues 399-602) with nucleotide/ATP binding capability (GO:0000166, GO:0005524), and threonine-tRNA ligase activity (IPR002320, GO:0004829, GO:0006435). AlphaFold structure (AF-Q9BW92-F1) predicts very high confidence (pLDDT 91.1) across the full 718 residue sequence, indicating a well-folded enzyme with stable tertiary structure suitable for tRNA recognition and aminoacylation catalysis. HPA data classifies TARS2 as a disease-related enzyme and potential drug target involved in protein biosynthesis, with low tissue specificity (detected in all tissues), localized to nucleoplasm and cytosol, and implicated in primary mitochondrial disease. STRING interactions reveal tight functional clustering with other aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases: LARS2 (Leucyl-tRNA synthetase 2, mitochondrial, 0.982), LARS1 (0.976), AARS1 (Alanyl-tRNA synthetase, 0.924), VARS1 (Valyl-tRNA synthetase, 0.910), EPRS1 (Glutamyl-Prolyl-tRNA synthetase, 0.905), TARS1 (cytoplasmic paralog, 0.905), TARS3 (0.896), IARS2 (Isoleucyl-tRNA synthetase 2, mitochondrial, 0.890), NARS1 (Asparaginyl-tRNA synthetase, 0.837), and AARS2 (Alanyl-tRNA synthetase 2, mitochondrial, 0.791), reflecting coordinated regulation of mitochondrial and cytoplasmic translation. ClinVar lists 64 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including missense mutations (p.Arg346His, p.His367Tyr, p.Ala629Thr), nonsense (p.Tyr332Ter), splice variants (c.387+3A>C), and large copy number gains/losses on chromosome 1q21, causing Combined Oxidative Phosphorylation Deficiency 21 (COXPD21, MIM #615918), a rare autosomal recessive mitochondrial encephalomyopathy featuring early-onset lactic acidosis, hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, neurological regression, and liver dysfunction; recent work identifies homozygous TARS2 variants as novel causes of syndromic neonatal diabetes with neurological features, and a Chinese-specific founder mutation (c.470C>G) in three unrelated families with mitochondrial encephalomyopathy. Literature (46 papers) establishes TARS2 as critical for mitochondrial protein synthesis fidelity, with multi-omics analyses revealing it as a key regulator of dilated cardiomyopathy through impaired respiratory chain assembly; chronic kidney disease manifests in TARS2-related mitochondrial disease; and clinical characterization demonstrates variable phenotypes ranging from lethal infantile presentations to milder adult-onset myopathy. As a dark gene with established disease causality but incompletely characterized tissue-specific vulnerability mechanisms, TARS2 represents a critical node connecting mitochondrial translation fidelity to energy metabolism, with therapeutic potential for mitochondrial diseases through tRNA supplementation or small molecule chaperones.
