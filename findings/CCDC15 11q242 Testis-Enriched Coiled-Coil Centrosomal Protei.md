## Description
CCDC15 (Coiled-Coil Domain Containing 15, UniProt Q0P6D6, Gene ID: 80071) is a 951 amino acid protein encoded on chromosome 11q24.2 (GRCh38: 124954201-125041488). InterPro analysis identifies a family-specific coiled-coil domain spanning nearly the entire protein (IPR037693, residues 1-950) and a distinct C-terminal domain (IPR060026, PF28517, residues 831-949). The extensive coiled-coil architecture suggests a structural scaffolding role, likely mediating oligomerization or interaction with other centrosomal components. The protein belongs to the PTHR14817 family, conserved across vertebrates.\n\nSTRING interaction network reveals 10 partners (scores 0.405-0.571), including several uncharacterized coiled-coil proteins and potential ciliary/centrosomal associates: PRR5-ARHGAP8 (0.571, Proline Rich 5 - ARHGAP8 readthrough, cytoskeletal regulation), RELL2 (0.528, RELL-like 2), CCDC102B (0.486) and CCDC106 (0.453) (other coiled-coil domain proteins), SPATC1 (0.446, Spermatogenesis Associated Coiled-Coil 1), CCDC154 (0.446), DNASE1L2 (0.436), IFI30 (0.426), SCML2 (0.414, Sex Comb On Midleg-Like 2, Polycomb repression), and SH2D4A (0.405). The presence of SPATC1 and the cluster of CCDC proteins strongly supports a role in spermatogenesis and centrosome/cilium assembly. The link to SCML2 hints at potential epigenetic regulation during germ cell development.\n\nHuman Protein Atlas classifies CCDC15 as "tissue enhanced" with primary expression in the testis (9.8 nTPM), detected in some tissues. Subcellular localization includes the plasma membrane and cytosol, consistent with its known association with centrioles and centrosomes. Biological process annotations confirm involvement in "cilium biogenesis/degradation." This restricted expression profile underscores its specialized function in male germ cell development, specifically in the formation of the sperm flagellum (a modified cilium).\n\nClinVar analysis reveals a high burden of 60 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large-scale copy number alterations spanning 11q23-25. Variants include massive deletions (11q24.1-25, ~11-12Mb; 11q23.3-25, ~18Mb) and duplications (11q23.3-25, ~18Mb; 11p13-q25, ~102Mb). The high frequency of CNVs reflects the structural instability of the 11q terminal region and the dosage sensitivity of genes within this locus (e.g., FLI1, ETS1). While no specific disease is uniquely attributed to CCDC15 monogenic mutations in ClinVar, its location within the 11q deletion syndrome (Jacobsen syndrome) critical region suggests that haploinsufficiency of CCDC15 contributes to the syndromic features, particularly potential male infertility or ciliary defects often under-diagnosed in broad syndromic presentations.\n\nAlphaFold structure prediction unavailable (500 server error).\n\nHYPOTHESIS: CCDC15 functions as a structural scaffold protein essential for centriole elongation and cilium assembly, specifically during spermatogenesis. The extensive coiled-coil domain (residues 1-950) likely forms a rigid rod or flexible tether that stabilizes the growing microtubule array of the centriole or axoneme. Interaction with SPATC1 suggests a cooperative mechanism in building the sperm flagellum. The high CNV burden (60 variants) indicates that CCDC15 resides in a genomic hotspot where large structural variants are frequent; while Jacobsen syndrome phenotypes (developmental delay, cardiac defects, thrombocytopenia) are driven by neighboring genes like FLI1, CCDC15 loss may specifically contribute to reproductive phenotypes (azoospermia, asthenozoospermia) in male patients with 11q deletions. The testis-enriched expression (9.8 nTPM) confirms its specialized role in gametogenesis, potentially acting as a ruler or spacer protein determining centriole length.
