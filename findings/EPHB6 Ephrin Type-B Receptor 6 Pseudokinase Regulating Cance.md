## Description
EPHB6 (Ephrin Type-B Receptor 6, UniProt O15197) is a 1021 aa transmembrane receptor tyrosine pseudokinase belonging to the Eph receptor family that lacks catalytic kinase activity but retains critical signaling functions through protein-protein interactions. FUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: EPHB6 serves as a kinase-dead scaffold receptor that transduces ephrin signals through heterodimerization with kinase-active Eph receptors (trans-phosphorylation) and recruitment of signaling adaptors (ABL1, RASA1), regulating cell invasion, autophagy, and receptor tyrosine kinase crosstalk. MECHANISTIC PROPOSAL: The ligand-binding domain binds ephrin-B ligands (EFNB1/2/3) inducing receptor clustering. Unlike kinase-active Eph receptors, EPHB6 cannot autophosphorylate but serves as a substrate for trans-phosphorylation by partner receptors or recruits ABL1 for downstream signaling. The SAM domain mediates receptor oligomerization and protein-protein interactions. In cancer, EPHB6 downregulation promotes metastasis by releasing invasion brakes; ephrinB1 trans-clustering restrains invasion. CBX7 binding links EPHB6 to polycomb-mediated transcriptional repression in cervical cancer. The EPHB6-TFEB axis promotes autophagy flux, explaining oat beta-glucan's anti-colitis effects. EGFR crosstalk suggests EPHB6 modulates growth factor signaling networks. The 51 ClinVar variants indicate EPHB6 dosage sensitivity may contribute to developmental or cancer predisposition phenotypes. Pseudokinase status makes EPHB6 a unique signaling node dependent on partner receptors for signal transduction.
