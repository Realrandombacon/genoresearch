## Description
PCDHGB7 (Protocadherin Gamma Subfamily B, 7) is a 929-amino acid single-pass transmembrane protein belonging to the clustered protocadherin family, functioning in calcium-dependent homophilic cell-cell adhesion with emerging roles as a cancer epigenetic biomarker. InterPro analysis reveals a characteristic multi-domain cadherin architecture: six extracellular cadherin domains (IPR002126, positions 56-347; PF00028 at 141-232, 247-337, 355-443), N-terminal cadherin domain (IPR013164, 33-112), C-terminal catenin-binding domain (IPR031904, 808-929), and cytoplasmic C-terminal domain (IPR032455, 687-768), consistent with classical cadherin-mediated adhesion mechanisms requiring calcium ion binding (GO:0005509). STRING interactions identify clustering with other protocadherin family members: PCDHGA4 (0.678), PCDHA9 (0.646), PCDHGB2 (0.555), PCDHGA11 (0.518), plus MTNR1A (0.477, melatonin receptor), suggesting coordinated expression in neuronal circuit assembly and potential neuroendocrine connections. HPA data shows cancer-enhanced RNA expression patterns with detection in many tissues, low tissue specificity, and multi-compartment localization (plasma membrane, nucleoplasm, vesicles, cytosol), classified as a predicted membrane protein involved in cell adhesion. ClinVar contains 23 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including nonsense mutations (p.Gln243Ter, p.Gln457Ter, p.Arg415Ter, p.Lys714Ter), frameshift deletions (p.Gln65fs, p.Lys184fs, p.Leu228fs, p.Phe108fs), and missense variants (p.Ala488Val), though specific monogenic disease phenotypes remain under characterization (significance marked '?' for most); the variant burden suggests potential neurodevelopmental relevance given the protocadherin family's role in brain development. AlphaFold structure (AF-Q9Y5F8-F1) predicts confident folding (pLDDT 75.1) across the full-length protein. With only 24 papers, PCDHGB7 represents a 'gray' gene transitioning from dark: while clustered protocadherins are known for neuronal self-avoidance and synaptic specificity, PCDHGB7's specific physiological functions remain largely unexplored despite emerging evidence as a universal cancer-only marker (UCOM) with promoter methylation changes in non-small cell lung cancer, triple-negative breast cancer (via XRCC5/MYC ribosome biogenesis suppression), urothelial carcinoma (urine-based detection), and cervical cancer triage strategies—suggesting dual roles in neural development and tumor suppression through epigenetic regulation.
