# TMEM9B: Lysosomal Membrane Scaffold Potentiating NF-κB Signaling via Endolysosomal Trafficking

## Description
TMEM9B encodes a 198 amino acid lysosomal membrane protein (UniProt Q9NQ34) belonging to the TMEM9/TMEM9B family (IPR008853, PF05434, residues 55-197). The protein is predicted to be a transmembrane scaffold localized to early endosome and lysosomal membranes (GO:0005765). HPA data shows ubiquitous expression across all tissues with low tissue specificity but cell type-enhanced expression patterns, consistent with a housekeeping endolysosomal function. STRING interactions reveal moderate-confidence associations with DPY19L4 (0.507), SLC45A1 (0.479), CCNQ (0.475), and CLCN5 (0.402)—partners implicated in glycosylation, solute transport, and endosomal chloride transport. ClinVar documents 13 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly copy number gains on chromosome 11p15.4, suggesting dosage sensitivity. Gene ontology annotation indicates TMEM9B positively regulates canonical NF-κB signal transduction, likely through endolysosomal modulation of IκB degradation or TLR signaling platforms. The TMEM9 domain architecture (PTHR13064) is conserved across vertebrates, with paralog TMEM9 sharing structural homology. TMEM9B may function as a membrane adaptor recruiting NF-κB pathway components (e.g., IKK complex, ubiquitin machinery) to lysosomal surfaces, facilitating signalosome assembly during inflammatory responses. Disruption of TMEM9B copy number could alter lysosomal pH or trafficking kinetics, impairing NF-κB activation and immune surveillance.
