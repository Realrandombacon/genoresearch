## Description
ZNF169 (Zinc Finger Protein 169) is a 603-amino acid KRAB domain-containing C2H2 zinc finger transcription factor encoded on chromosome 9q22.32 (UniProt Q14929). InterPro analysis reveals a canonical KRAB repressor domain (residues 14-85, PF01352; IPR001909; IPR036051) that mediates transcriptional silencing through recruitment of the KAP1/TRIM28 corepressor complex and associated histone methyltransferases for H3K9me3 deposition. The protein contains multiple C2H2-type zinc finger motifs (IPR013087, PF00096, PS00028) spanning residues 234-439, with at least 3 canonical fingers adopting the classic beta-beta-alpha fold (G3DSA:3.30.160.60) for sequence-specific DNA binding in the major groove. Human Protein Atlas confirms ubiquitous expression across all tissues with low tissue specificity, dual nuclear speckle and cytosolic localization, and classification as a DNA-binding transcription factor involved in RNA polymerase II transcriptional regulation. ClinVar documents 33 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variants encompassing ZNF169, including large-scale deletions (CNV losses) and duplications (CNV gains) on chromosome 9 (9q22.31-31.2, 9p24.3-q34.3 regions), indicating strong dosage sensitivity. The KRAB-ZNF architecture strongly suggests ZNF169 functions as a sequence-specific transcriptional repressor that recruits the TRIM28-SETDB1 complex to silence target genes through heterochromatin formation. Given ubiquitous expression, nuclear speckle localization (sites of active transcription/splicing), and extensive clinical CNV burden, ZNF169 likely regulates fundamental cellular processes such as cell cycle control, differentiation, or stress response, with haploinsufficiency or overexpression contributing to developmental phenotypes through dysregulation of downstream target gene networks. STRING and AlphaFold queries failed technically, but domain architecture predicts chromatin-binding and transcriptional regulatory function.
