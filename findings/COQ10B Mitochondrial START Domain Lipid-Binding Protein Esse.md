## Description
COQ10B/Q9H8M1 (238 aa) is a confidently folded (AlphaFold pLDDT: 78.4) mitochondrial START domain protein functioning as a critical lipid-binding cofactor in coenzyme Q (ubiquinone) biosynthesis and respiratory chain electron transport. InterPro analysis reveals a canonical START lipid-binding architecture containing the Coenzyme Q-binding protein COQ10p family domain (cd07813/IPR044996/PTHR12901, residues 78-216/59-231/59-231) with the characteristic START-like domain superfamily fold (IPR023393/G3DSA:3.30.530.20/SSF55961, residues 75-221/75-221/78-221) and Polyketide cyclase/dehydrase and lipid transport domain (PF03364/IPR005031, residues 85-213/85-213) that enables ubiquinone binding (GO:0048039) and cellular respiration functions (GO:0045333), classifying it within the COQ10 lipid carrier family distinct from but homologous to COQ10A. HPA data confirms ubiquitous expression across all tissues with low tissue specificity, cytosolic localization (though UniProt annotates mitochondrial), classification as a predicted intracellular protein, and cell type-enhanced expression pattern consistent with housekeeping mitochondrial respiratory functions. STRING interactions define an exceptionally coherent CoQ biosynthesis complex network linking COQ10B exclusively to all major CoQ pathway enzymes: COQ2 (0.739), COQ8B (0.726), COQ8A (0.726), COQ3 (0.722), COQ4 (0.714), COQ6 (0.703), COQ9 (0.672), COQ5 (0.653), COQ7 (0.629), and PDSS1 (0.626), positioning it as an integral scaffold/cofactor within the multi-enzyme CoQ synthome complex. Literature (23 papers) establishes emerging roles in oncology and lipid metabolism: 2026 multi-omics analysis identifies COQ10B as driving tumor progression and immune escape in esophageal squamous cell carcinoma; 2022 studies demonstrate COQ10B knockdown modulates cell proliferation, invasion, migration, and apoptosis in ESCC (later retracted but findings corroborated); 2019 landmark biochemical characterization establishes human COQ10A and COQ10B as distinct lipid-binding START domain proteins required for coenzyme Q function, demonstrating direct ubiquinone binding capacity essential for respiratory electron transport. ClinVar documents 38 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants predominantly consisting of large chromosome 2q CNVs (2q31.3-33.1 losses, 2q32.3-33.3 losses, 2q32.3-34 losses, 2q32.1-36.1 gains, 2q32.2-34 losses) implicating COQ10B dosage sensitivity in developmental disorders though specific monogenic phenotypes remain undefined due to contiguous gene effects, though CoQ deficiency syndromes typically present with encephalomyopathy, nephrotic syndrome, and cerebellar ataxia. The convergence of confident START domain lipid-binding structure, exclusive CoQ biosynthesis enzyme partnerships, ubiquitous expression, ESCC oncogenic driver function, and chromosome 2q dosage sensitivity suggests COQ10B functions as a mitochondrial ubiquinone carrier/scaffold coordinating CoQ biosynthesis complex assembly, respiratory chain electron transport efficiency, and metabolic reprogramming in cancer, with therapeutic potential in CoQ deficiency syndromes, ESCC targeting, and mitochondrial disease stratification.
