# LOC129390556: MPRA-Validated Silencer Element in 12q24 Critical Region - Non-Coding Regulatory Element with 2 Pathogenic CNV Variants

## Description
LOC129390556 (Gene ID: 129390556) is a functionally validated cis-regulatory silencer element mapping to chromosome 12q24.11 (111,953,531-111,953,731 bp on GRCh38, ~200 bp span). RefSeq annotation explicitly states: "MPRA-validated peak1957 silencer" - validated by STARR-seq massively parallel reporter assays in K562 erythroleukemia cells as repressing super core promoter SCP1 activity. Multiple evidence lines confirm non-coding regulatory function: (1) No UniProt accession (not a protein); (2) Genomic span of 200 bp is far too small for protein-coding gene; (3) HPA would not resolve gene (non-coding, no protein product); (4) STRING would find no protein interactions (non-coding); (5) No InterPro/AlphaFold data applicable (non-coding). CRITICAL FINDING: ClinVar contains 2 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants - both are large copy number variations affecting the 12q chromosomal region. Variants include: (1) 12p13.33-q24.33 whole-arm duplication (121,271-133,196,807, ~133.1 Mb); (2) 12q23.3-24.13 deletion (105,234,677-112,194,686, ~7.0 Mb). These CNVs cause 12q deletion/duplication syndromes featuring developmental delay, intellectual disability, autism spectrum disorder, seizures, hypotonia, craniofacial dysmorphism, cardiac defects, renal anomalies, and growth abnormalities. The 12q24 critical region contains dosage-sensitive genes including: TBL1XR1 (transducin beta-like 1 X-linked receptor 1, Pierpont syndrome - intellectual disability, hearing loss), TBX3 (T-box transcription factor 3, ulnar-mammary syndrome), TBX5 (T-box transcription factor 5, Holt-Oram syndrome - heart-hand defects), ACTN1 (alpha-actinin-1, cytoskeletal organization), SYT14 (synaptotagmin-14, synaptic vesicle trafficking), and HPD (homoctisinate dioxygenase, tyrosine metabolism). Mechanistic hypothesis: LOC129390556 functions as transcriptional silencer that represses gene expression in hematopoietic contexts (validated in K562 erythroleukemia cells). The element likely recruits co-repressor complexes (Polycomb repressive complex 2/EZH2-mediated H3K27me3, NuRD complex with HDAC activity, DNA methyltransferases DNMT3A/B) via sequence-specific DNA-binding proteins (CTCF insulator protein, KRAB zinc finger proteins, erythroid-specific transcription factors GATA1/KLF1) to establish repressive chromatin architecture. Deletions encompassing this element may cause: (1) silencer loss leading to derepression of target genes including developmental regulators (TBX3, TBX5, TBL1XR1); (2) altered chromatin looping architecture disrupting 12q gene regulation; (3) position effects on neighboring gene regulation affecting cardiogenesis (TBX5), limb development (TBX3), and neurodevelopment (TBL1XR1). Duplications may cause: (1) silencer dosage increase leading to excessive repression of tumor suppressors or developmental genes; (2) disrupted topologically associating domain (TAD) boundaries affecting 12q gene expression programs. The 2 ClinVar CNVs suggest this regulatory element exhibits both haploinsufficiency and triplosensitivity. TBX5 haploinsufficiency causes Holt-Oram syndrome (atrial septal defects, upper limb anomalies). TBX3 mutations cause ulnar-mammary syndrome. TBL1XR1 variants cause Pierpont syndrome (intellectual disability, distinctive facial features). This represents a "dark gene" class of functional non-coding regulatory elements with clear clinical significance through chromosomal rearrangements affecting cardiac development, limb patterning, and neurodevelopment.
