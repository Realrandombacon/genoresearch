# SLC9A5 (NHE5): A Brain-Enriched Sodium/Proton Exchanger Regulating Synaptic pH and Tumor Motility via ACOX1 and Arrestin Interactions

## Description
SLC9A5 (UniProt Q14940), also known as NHE5, encodes an 896 amino acid sodium/proton exchanger localized to recycling endosomes, focal adhesions, and neuron spines. Unlike ubiquitous NHE isoforms, SLC9A5 is highly enriched in the brain, where it regulates intracellular pH critical for synaptic plasticity and vesicle trafficking. InterPro analysis confirms canonical CPA1 family domains (IPR004709, IPR006153, PF00999) spanning the transmembrane core (aa 54-455), with specific motifs for isoform 5 regulation (IPR018410). AlphaFold structure (AF-Q14940-F1) predicts a medium-confidence fold (global pLDDT 64.1), reflecting a structured transmembrane transporter coupled with large, potentially disordered cytosolic regulatory tails involved in protein-protein interactions. Literature search (14 papers) reveals emerging roles in cancer biology, where SLC9A5 promotes tumor growth and cell motility via ACOX1-mediated peroxisomal fatty acid oxidation, and in dental enamel formation regulated by TGF-β. ClinVar lists 27 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains and losses on chromosome 16q, though no specific monogenic disease is yet annotated, suggesting contributions to complex neurodevelopmental or renal disorders (gene implicated in end-stage renal disease). STRING interactions identify high-confidence partners including SLC9B2 (0.936), ARRB1 (0.661), and CHP1 (0.576), placing SLC9A5 within a network of pH regulators and arrestin-mediated signaling complexes. Functional hypothesis: SLC9A5 functions as a specialized neuronal and endosomal pH regulator that couples sodium flux to vesicle recycling and synaptic strength; its interaction with arrestins (ARRB1) and calmodulin-like proteins (CHP1) allows dynamic response to cellular stress, while dysregulation drives metastatic potential in cancers through metabolic reprogramming.
