# CDH16/KSP-Cadherin: Kidney-Specific Cadherin Regulating Renal Tubule Architecture and Serving as Diagnostic Biomarker for Renal Cell Carcinoma and Thyroid Cancer

## Description
CDH16 (UniProt O75309), also known as KSP-cadherin (kidney-specific cadherin), encodes an 829-amino acid type I transmembrane glycoprotein containing six tandem cadherin repeats (IPR002126/PF00028, aa 21-555) within the cadherin superfamily (IPR015919/G3DSA:2.60.40.60). Conserved features include calcium-binding motifs (GO:0005509) at each EC domain interface, the cadherin domain signature (PS00232, aa 437-447), and the conserved site (IPR020894) essential for homophilic cell-cell adhesion (GO:0007156). AlphaFold predicts a well-folded extracellular region (pLDDT: 81.8), consistent with stable adhesive architecture.\n\nSTRING interactions confirm CDH16's renal identity: strongest links to uromodulin UMOD (0.527), paired box transcription factors PAX2 (0.521)/PAX8 (0.485), myo-inositol oxygenase MIOX (0.510), cytokeratin KRT7 (0.489), alpha-methylacyl-CoA racemase AMACR (0.480), transcription factor TFE3 (0.474), hepatocyte nuclear factor HNF1B (0.449), and aquaporins AQP1 (0.432)/AQP2 (0.424)—all established renal tubule markers. HPA confirms extreme kidney specificity (252.2 nTPM, tissue enriched).\n\nWith 66 papers, CDH16 is a transitional dark gene—characterized as a developmental renal cadherin but with emerging 2022-2025 diagnostic utility: reduced CDH16 expression predicts lymph node metastasis in Middle Eastern papillary thyroid cancer and poor prognosis in clear cell renal cell carcinoma. ClinVar contains 28 pathogenic variants (16q21-22.1 CNVs). This positions CDH16 as a critical regulator of renal tubule morphogenesis through calcium-dependent homophilic adhesion, with untapped potential as a tissue-of-origin biomarker in metastatic carcinomas.
