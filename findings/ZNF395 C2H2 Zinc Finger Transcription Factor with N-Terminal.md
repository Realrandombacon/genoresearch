## Description
ZNF395 encodes a 513 amino acid zinc finger protein (UniProt Q9H8N7) featuring a canonical C2H2-type zinc finger DNA-binding domain (aa 280-310, IPR013087, PS00028) that enables sequence-specific DNA recognition and transcriptional activation. The N-terminus contains a ZNF395/ZNF704-specific domain (aa 7-109, IPR031940, PF15997/DUF4772) of unknown function but conserved across CR1/CR2 motif-containing transcription regulators (IPR052253, PTHR13006). The C-terminus harbors a c-clamp domain (aa 483-513, SM01366, PF27556) characteristic of zinc finger protein 704 family members, potentially mediating protein-protein interactions or nucleic acid binding. HPA data shows ubiquitous expression across all tissues with cancer-enriched RNA profiles, localized to nucleoplasm and cytosol, consistent with its role as a DNA-binding transcription activator for RNA polymerase II. ClinVar documents 76 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including missense mutations (e.g., c.1234G>T p.Ala412Ser) and large copy number gains/losses spanning 8p21.1, indicating dosage sensitivity and potential tumor suppressor or oncogene function. The domain architecture suggests ZNF395 functions as a sequence-specific transcription factor that binds GC-rich promoter/enhancer elements via its C2H2 zinc finger, recruits co-activators through its N-terminal DUF4772 domain, and may dimerize or interact with chromatin remodelers via the C-terminal c-clamp. Cancer-enriched expression and high variant burden implicate ZNF395 in oncogenic transcriptional programs, potentially regulating cell proliferation or differentiation genes.
