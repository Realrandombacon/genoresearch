## Description
KCNK4 (Potassium Two Pore Domain Channel Subfamily K Member 4, also known as TRAAK-related channel) is a 393 amino acid background potassium channel that establishes resting membrane potential and regulates neuronal excitability through constitutive potassium leak conductance. InterPro analysis identifies canonical two-pore domain potassium channel architecture including K2P family signature (IPR003280, PTHR11003, residues 7-263) with potassium channel activity (GO:0005267) and potassium ion transmembrane transport (GO:0071805), TRAAK-specific motifs (IPR008074, PR01691, residues 71-86/143-161/175-190), dual potassium channel domains (IPR013099, PF07885, residues 86-142/180-258) forming the characteristic four-transmembrane two-pore structure, and voltage-gated potassium channel superfamily homology (SSF81324, G3DSA:1.10.287.70). AlphaFold structure (AF-Q9NYG8-F1) predicts high confidence (pLDDT 78.7) across the 393 residue sequence, indicating a well-folded ion channel with stable transmembrane topology suitable for potassium selectivity and gating. HPA data classifies KCNK4 as a disease-related membrane protein and potential drug target involved in ion transport, with brain-enriched expression (13.3 nTPM), tissue-enriched RNA specificity, cell type-enhanced distribution, and implications in epilepsy and intellectual disability. STRING interactions reveal functional clustering with related K2P channels: KCNK9 (TASK3, 0.713), KCNK18 (TRESK, 0.681), KCNK2 (TREK1, 0.665), KCNK3 (TASK1, 0.643), KCNK12 (THIK2, 0.639), KCNK13 (THIK1, 0.630), KCNK10 (TREK2, 0.604), KCNK7 (TWIK2, 0.599); strong connection to KRT76 (Keratin 76, 0.881) suggesting epithelial roles; and PIEZO1 (0.547), a mechanosensitive channel, indicating potential mechanotransduction coupling. ClinVar lists 56 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including missense mutations (p.Lys389Arg, p.Arg369Cys, p.Gly98Arg, p.Leu243Arg, p.Glu64Val, p.Arg117His, p.Ser19Cys), nonsense (p.Ser171Ter, p.Trp197Ter), and frameshift insertion (p.Arg375fs), causing autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder with epilepsy, febrile seizures plus (EFS+), intellectual disability, hypertrichosis, and gingival fibromatosis syndrome with variable phenotypic expressivity. Literature (51 papers) establishes KCNK4 as critical for neuronal resting potential maintenance with de novo variants causing recognizable epilepsy syndromes; recurrent variants produce dominant pedigrees with hypertrichosis and gingival overgrowth; lncRNA SLC25A21-AS1 regulates KCNK4 expression affecting ovarian cancer chemosensitivity; and expanding phenotypic spectrum includes rolandic epilepsy. As a well-studied gene with emerging genotype-phenotype correlations, KCNK4 represents a critical node connecting background potassium conductance to neuronal excitability, with therapeutic potential for epilepsy through channel modulation.
