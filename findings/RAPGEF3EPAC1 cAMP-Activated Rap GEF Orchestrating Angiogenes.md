# RAPGEF3/EPAC1: cAMP-Activated Rap GEF Orchestrating Angiogenesis, Macrophage Reprogramming, and Cancer Metastasis via PKA-AKAP Scaffolding and RAP1-MAPK Signaling

## Description
RAPGEF3 (Rap Guanine Nucleotide Exchange Factor 3, also known as EPAC1, O95398) encodes a 923 aa multidomain signaling protein functioning as a direct effector of cyclic AMP (cAMP) that activates Rap1/2 GTPases independent of Protein Kinase A (PKA). Structural architecture reveals a sophisticated autoinhibited organization: N-terminal DEP domain (IPR000591/cd04437, residues 111-186/100-223) mediating membrane localization and protein interactions, central cyclic nucleotide-binding domain/CNB (IPR000595/cd00038, residues 245-364/247-356) serving as the cAMP sensor that relieves autoinhibition, RasGEF N-terminal domain (IPR000651), REM domain (cd06224, residues 392-511) stabilizing the catalytic core, and C-terminal RasGEF catalytic domain (cd00155/G3DSA:1.10.840.10, residues 658-890/650-923) catalyzing GDP-GTP exchange on Rap substrates. AlphaFold prediction (pLDDT 77.5) confirms a well-folded multi-domain structure consistent with regulated GEF activity. STRING interactions define a precise cAMP signaling module with adenylate cyclases ADCY5 (0.981)/ADCY1 (0.595), PKA catalytic subunits PRKACG (0.973)/PRKACB (0.968)/PRKACA (0.644), A-kinase anchoring proteins AKAP1 (0.957)/AKAP6 (0.707), and direct substrates RAP1A (0.939)/RAP1B (0.912), plus MAPK7 (0.569). HPA localizes RAPGEF3 to cytosol and endoplasmic reticulum with cancer-enhanced expression pattern, classified as guanine-nucleotide releasing factor involved in angiogenesis. ClinVar documents 11 pathogenic variants including 12p13-q24 CNVs and splice site mutations (c.1924-186G>T). Literature (69 papers) highlights critical roles in osteoarthritis synovitis under mechanical loading (3x citations, 2025), basal-subtype lung squamous cell carcinoma vulnerability (2025), ciprofloxacin-induced betaglycan upregulation suppressing metastasis (2025), TXNIP-mediated oxidative stress regulation in melanoma (2025), and integrin activation in angiogenesis. Mechanistic hypothesis: In resting state, DEP/CNB domains occlude the RasGEF catalytic site; cAMP binding to CNB induces conformational change exposing the catalytic domain; RAPGEF3 then activates Rap1/2, triggering integrin-mediated cell adhesion, endothelial barrier formation, and MAPK7/ERK signaling; scaffolding by AKAPs localizes RAPGEF3 near PKA and ADCY for signal fidelity; in cancer, RAPGEF3 drives metastasis via Rap1-dependent integrin activation and EMT, while therapeutic targeting (e.g., ciprofloxacin, ESI-09) blocks this axis.
