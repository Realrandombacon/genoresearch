## Description
TUFT1 encodes tuftelin-1 (WHSF), a 390 aa acidic intracellular protein (UniProt Q9NNX1) belonging to the tuftelin/GRINL1A/MYZAP/CCD68 family that serves as critical scaffold for dental enamel biomineralization with emerging oncological functions. InterPro reveals characteristic coiled-coil architecture: tuftelin/GRINL1A/MYZAP/CCD68 family domain (IPR051375/PTHR23171, residues 55-389) defining membership in this protein family, tuftelin-like coiled-coil domain (IPR064049/PF30463, residues 173-351) mediating homodimerization and heterodimerization essential for matrix assembly. AlphaFold predicts high confidence structure (pLDDT 75.8) consistent with well-folded coiled-coil protein forming extended helical bundles typical of structural scaffold proteins requiring mechanical stability during enamel crystallite growth. STRING reveals functional clustering with enamel matrix proteins and processing enzymes: TFIP11 (0.981, tuftelin-interacting protein-11 direct binding partner), ENAM (0.981, enamelin major enamel matrix structural protein), AMELX (0.908, amelogenin predominant enamel protein ~90% of matrix), RABGAP1 (0.751, Rab GTPase activating protein regulating vesicle trafficking), DSPP (0.750, dentin sialophosphoprotein dentin mineralization), ZYX (0.734, zyxin cytoskeletal adaptor linking to actin), DHX15 (0.693, DEAH-box helicase-15 RNA metabolism), AMBN (0.673, ameloblastin enamel matrix protein), KLK4 (0.667, kallikrein-4 serine protease cleaving enamel proteins during maturation), MMP20 (0.447, matrix metalloproteinase-20/enamelysin processing pro-enamel proteins). HPA shows tissue-enhanced expression with skin predominance (105.2 nTPM highest in dataset), detected in all tissues, immune cell-enhanced pattern in blood cells, vesicular subcellular localization consistent with secretory pathway trafficking in ameloblasts, classified as predicted intracellular/secreted protein involved in biomineralization with palmoplantar keratoderma disease association. ClinVar lists 18 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including extensive copy number gains spanning 1q21.1-25.3/1q21.1-23.1/1q12-23.1 affecting gene dosage, copy number losses at 1q21.3, splice donor mutation (c.60+1G>A) disrupting exon 1 splicing, and intronic variants (c.1008+63A>T, c.136-16A>T) though specific monogenic phenotypes recently defined as woolly hair and skin fragility syndrome (2024 report of bi-allelic TUFT1 variants in two siblings). Functional mechanism: TUFT1 operates through multi-compartment coordination of enamel biomineralization: (1) secretory stage - tuftelin synthesized in ameloblasts trafficked via vesicles to Tomes process where coiled-coil domain mediates self-assembly into supramolecular scaffold organizing hydroxyapatite crystal nucleation sites, (2) matrix interaction - direct binding to TFIP11 creating heterodimeric complexes that recruit amelogenin (AMELX), enamelin (ENAM), and ameloblastin (AMBN) into organized matrix, (3) maturation stage - KLK4 and MMP20 proteases cleave tuftelin and partner proteins enabling crystal growth and enamel hardening, (4) non-dental functions - RABGAP1 interaction suggests vesicle trafficking regulation in other secretory cell types, ZYX linkage indicates cytoskeletal organization roles explaining skin fragility phenotype, DHX15 partnership implies unexpected RNA metabolism functions. Recent 2025 literature reveals expanded disease relevance beyond enamel: cervical cancer progression through EGFR signaling modulation and ATF1 transcription factor interaction regulating proliferation/migration/invasion/stemness, centrosome amplification suppression preventing mitotic spindle multipolarity and chromosomal instability in solid tumors, triple-negative breast cancer biomarker potential with Rac1-GTP pathway correlation, molar incisor hypomineralization (MIH) genetic susceptibility marker. Despite mechanistic understanding of enamel matrix assembly, complete interactome beyond identified partners especially tissue-specific complexes in non-dental contexts, regulatory phosphorylation/acetylation sites controlling coiled-coil assembly kinetics, precise contribution of 1q CNVs to ectodermal versus oncological phenotypes, genotype-phenotype correlations for splice variants affecting coiled-coil domain integrity versus N-terminal interactions, cell-type specific expression patterns across ameloblast differentiation stages, evolutionary conservation distinguishing tuftelin from GRINL1A/MYZAP paralogs, therapeutic potential for tuftelin-derived peptides enhancing remineralization versus small molecule inhibitors blocking cancer-promoting interactions remain incompletely characterized - representing genuine dark aspects of this structurally defined scaffold protein bridging developmental biology with oncology.
