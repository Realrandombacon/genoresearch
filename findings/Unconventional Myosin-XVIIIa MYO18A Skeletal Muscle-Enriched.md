## Description
MYO18A (UniProt Q92614), also known as MAJN, MYSPDZ, SP-R210, or TIAF1, encodes a massive 2054 aa unconventional myosin functioning as a molecular motor linking the Golgi apparatus to the actin cytoskeleton and assembling lamellar actomyosin bundles essential for cell migration and tissue architecture. InterPro analysis reveals a complex multi-domain architecture characteristic of class XVIII myosins but with unique features: an N-terminal PDZ domain (IPR001478/cd06747/G3DSA:2.30.42.10, residues 215-317) mediating protein-protein interactions and scaffolding functions, a central class XVIII myosin motor domain (cd01386/G3DSA:3.40.850.10/G3DSA:1.20.120.720, residues 408-1185) containing the ATPase and actin-binding sites for force generation, an IQ motif (IPR000048, residues 1187-1209, GO:0005515) for light chain binding and regulation, multiple CATH superfamily domains including Vasodilator-stimulated phosphoprotein-like region (G3DSA:1.20.5.1160, residues 1701-1816), and a large C-terminal extension distinguishing it from conventional myosins. AlphaFold predicts a confident structure (pLDDT 70.7) consistent with a well-folded multi-domain motor protein where the motor domain forms a stable core while extended regions provide flexibility for cargo binding and conformational changes during the mechanochemical cycle. STRING interactions define a network centered on cytoskeletal dynamics and Golgi trafficking: MYL6 (0.892, myosin light chain 6), MYL12B (0.871, regulatory light chain), FGFR1OP2 (0.863, FGF receptor oncogene partner), TIAF1 (0.863, TGFB1-induced anti-apoptotic factor - alternative isoform), TRIP11 (0.858, Golgi microtubule binding protein), FGFR1OP (0.847), MYL12A (0.838), MYL1 (0.833), MYL3 (0.798), and GOLPH3 (0.566, Golgi phosphoprotein 3 - key cargo linker). HPA classifies MYO18A as an essential protein with tissue-enhanced expression showing highest levels in skeletal muscle (381.4 nTPM) and tongue (115.5 nTPM), detected in all tissues, low cancer specificity, localized as a predicted intracellular motor protein, reflecting dual roles in muscle contraction/maintenance and ubiquitous cell migration functions. ClinVar lists 12 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including missense mutations (p.Asn417Ser in motor domain, p.Arg1630Trp in C-terminal region), synonymous variant (p.Ala1174=), splice site mutation (c.4230+2T>G), and large copy number gains (17p11.2-q11.2, 17q11.1-11.2x4, 17p11.2-q21.2, 17p13.3-q25.3) plus one translocation t(13;17)(q12.2;q11.2) though no single-gene disorder is definitively assigned suggesting contribution to 17q duplication syndromes or myopathic phenotypes. Functional hypothesis: MYO18A operates through a unique mechanism divergent from conventional myosins: (1) Golgi tethering - PDZ domain interacts with GOLPH3 and TRIP11 anchoring the Golgi to actin filaments maintaining Golgi structure and enabling vesicle budding for secretion; (2) Motor activity - ATPase cycle in motor domain generates force along actin filaments though class XVIII myosins may have reduced motility compared to conventional myosins; (3) Light chain regulation - IQ motifs bind calmodulin-like light chains (MYL6/MYL12A/MYL12B) modulating motor activity and cargo affinity; (4) Actomyosin bundle assembly - Forms lamellar actomyosin structures at cell periphery enabling cell migration and membrane protrusion; (5) Cancer stem cell regulation - 2025 studies show GOLPH3/MYO18A complex controls trans-Golgi network (dTGN) formation in lung cancer stem cells where golgicide A induces pyroptosis by disrupting this complex; (6) Renal epithelial architecture - 2025 research demonstrates MYO18A maintains thick ascending limb cellular structure and traffics UMOD (uromodulin) and NKCC2 transporters critical for electrolyte homeostasis; (7) Disease mechanism - Motor domain mutations (p.Asn417Ser) likely impair ATPase activity causing myopathy; C-terminal truncations disrupt cargo binding affecting Golgi positioning; CNV gains cause overexpression enhancing cell migration contributing to cancer metastasis; splice site mutations cause exon skipping and loss of functional domains. Conservation extends to rat (D3ZFD0, 2044 aa) indicating preserved role in mammalian cytoskeletal dynamics. Dark aspects requiring investigation include: precise step size and force generation characteristics compared to conventional myosins, structural basis of PDZ domain partner specificity beyond GOLPH3, role in sarcomere organization given high skeletal muscle expression (class XVIII myosins as specialist sarcomeric proteins per 2024 review), contribution to Golgi fragmentation in neurodegeneration, potential involvement in cytokinesis via actomyosin ring formation, therapeutic targeting of GOLPH3-MYO18A interface in cancer stem cells, genotype-phenotype correlations explaining clinical variability in 17q CNV syndromes, and crosstalk between MYO18A and other myosins (MYH9/MYH10) in renal epithelial cells.
