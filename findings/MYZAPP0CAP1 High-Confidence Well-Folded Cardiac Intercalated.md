## Description
MYZAP (Myocardial zonula adherens protein, also known as MYOZAP, Gup) is a 466 amino acid intracellular scaffold protein (UniProt P0CAP1) with high-confidence well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 77.2), characteristic of the Tuftelin/GRINL1A/MYZAP/CCD68 family (IPR051375/PTHR23171, residues 25-464). Despite lacking canonical structural domains like SH3 or PDZ, MYZAP forms a stable globular structure that localizes specifically to intercalated discs in cardiomyocytes, the specialized junctions connecting heart muscle cells. Molecular functions include activation of Rho-dependent serum-response factor (SRF) signaling, linking mechanical stress at cell-cell contacts to transcriptional reprogramming. The protein acts as a critical adaptor, translating cytoskeletal tension into gene expression changes required for cardiac hypertrophy and maintenance.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals a network centered on transcriptional machinery and intercalated disc components: strongest interactions with POLR2M (0.933, RNA polymerase II subunit M, readthrough partner), STRN (0.570, Striatin, scaffolding in signaling complexes), PLEC (0.565, Plectin, cytoskeletal linker), RND1 (0.527, Rho family GTPase, cytoskeleton dynamics), MED26 (0.479, Mediator complex subunit 26, transcription), DSP (0.475, Desmoplakin, desmosome anchor), TJP1 (0.457, Tight junction protein 1/ZO-1), CTNNB1 (0.437, Beta-catenin, adherens junction), TCP11L1 (0.436), and PKP2 (0.414, Plakophilin-2, desmosome). This profile confirms MYZAP's dual role: coupling to the transcriptional apparatus (POLR2M, MED26) via its readthrough relationship, and anchoring to the mechanical junctional complex (DSP, PKP2, PLEC) to sense contractile force.\n\nHPA expression reveals extreme tissue enrichment in heart muscle (551.2 nTPM), with low detection in other tissues, consistent with its specialized cardiac function. Cancer-enriched and immune cell-enhanced patterns suggest potential roles in tumor progression or inflammation, but the primary physiological role is cardiac. Disease involvement is explicitly noted as 'Cardiomyopathy, Disease variant' in HPA.\n\nClinVar documents 22 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including frameshifts (c.1128dup, c.779del), nonsense mutations (c.349C>T, c.1150A>T, c.388C>T), splice site defects (c.933+1G>A), and large copy number losses/gains on chromosome 15q (15q21.3-22.31 deletions, 15q21.1-22.2 duplications). The presence of truncating mutations and CNVs directly links MYZAP loss-of-function to human disease, specifically dilated or arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy. Disruption of MYZAP likely impairs the mechanical integrity of intercalated discs and the adaptive transcriptional response to stress, leading to heart failure.\n\nConservation is strong across vertebrates: human (P0CAP1, 466 aa), mouse (Q3UIJ9, 466 aa), rat (Q5EB94, 466 aa), indicating evolutionary pressure to maintain the cardiac-specific adaptor function.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: MYZAP functions as a mechanotransducer at the cardiac intercalated disc. Under conditions of increased wall stress or stretch, MYZAP recruits or stabilizes RhoA/RND1 signaling complexes at the junction, activating the SRF pathway to upregulate cytoskeletal and contractile genes. Its interaction with POLR2M (via readthrough transcription) may facilitate rapid recruitment of RNA polymerase II to target promoters, ensuring swift transcriptional adaptation. Simultaneously, binding to desmosomal (DSP, PKP2) and cytolinker (PLEC) proteins anchors MYZAP to the force-bearing skeleton, allowing it to sense tension directly. Loss of MYZAP uncouples mechanical stress from transcriptional output, preventing compensatory hypertrophy and leading to progressive dilation and systolic dysfunction. Truncating mutations likely cause haploinsufficiency or dominant-negative effects, disrupting junctional stability and promoting arrhythmias. MYZAP is a confirmed cardiomyopathy gene and a potential target for therapies aiming to enhance cardiac resilience.
