## Description
CEACAM21 (Carcinoembryonic Antigen-Related Cell Adhesion Molecule 21) is a 293 amino acid type I transmembrane glycoprotein (UniProt Q3KPI0) with a well-folded extracellular architecture containing two immunoglobulin-like domains (AlphaFold pLDDT 85.2, high confidence), functioning as an immune regulatory receptor that modulates T cell activation and immune synapse formation through homophilic/heterophilic adhesion and recruitment of Src-family kinase signaling cascades.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals CEACAM21 contains canonical CEACAM domains: an N-terminal Immunoglobulin V-set domain (IPR013106/PF07686/cd05774, residues 40-141) responsible for ligand binding specificity, followed by an Immunoglobulin C2-type domain (IPR003598/cd05740, residues 146-232) providing structural stability. The protein belongs to the carcinoembryonic antigen-related cell adhesion family (IPR050831) and the broader immunoglobulin superfamily (G3DSA:2.60.40.10). The high-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 85.2) confirms a stable V-C2 tandem Ig fold typical of CEACAM receptors, optimized for extracellular interactions.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places CEACAM21 within the immune regulation network: strongest interactions with CEACAM19 (0.652, closely related family member, likely heterophilic partner), SYK (0.613, spleen tyrosine kinase, signal transduction), VAV1 (0.524, guanine nucleotide exchange factor, cytoskeletal rearrangement), CDON (0.509, cell adhesion molecule), EFHB (0.467, calcium sensor), ERV3-1/ERVFRD-1 (0.459/0.450, endogenous retroviral envelope proteins), RYK (0.454, receptor tyrosine kinase), CD79A (0.432, B-cell receptor component), and CD55 (0.405, complement decay-accelerating factor). The CEACAM19 partnership suggests functional redundancy or heterodimerization within the CEACAM cluster on chromosome 19q. SYK and VAV1 interactions indicate CEACAM21 triggers downstream signaling cascades leading to actin remodeling and transcriptional activation upon ligand engagement.\n\nHPA expression data is unavailable, consistent with many immune-specific genes not captured in standard tissue panels; however, gene summaries predict membrane localization and involvement in T cell activation. The chromosomal location (19q13.2) within the CEACAM gene cluster suggests co-regulation with other immune modifiers.\n\nClinVar documents 5 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all large chromosome 19 copy number gains (19q11-13.33, 19p13.3-q13.43, 19p13.2-q13.31, 19q13.12-13.2). The CNV burden indicates dosage sensitivity, where overexpression may disrupt immune tolerance or contribute to autoimmune pathology through aberrant CEACAM clustering.\n\nConservation is evident in primates (Pongo abelii Q3KPI0 homolog) and mammals, indicating evolutionary conservation of immune functions.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: CEACAM21 functions as a cell surface adhesion receptor that regulates immune cell interactions. The N-terminal IgV domain mediates specific ligand recognition, potentially binding CEACAM19 or other family members in cis or trans. Upon engagement, the cytoplasmic tail (though short) likely recruits ITAM-containing adaptors or directly interacts with SYK (0.613) to initiate phosphorylation cascades. VAV1 recruitment (0.524) links CEACAM21 to Rho GTPase activation and cytoskeletal reorganization essential for immune synapse stability. The well-folded structure (pLDDT 85.2) ensures precise spatial orientation for dimerization. Clustered genomic organization with CEACAM19 suggests coordinated expression during immune responses. Overexpression via 19q amplification may lead to hyperactive T cell signaling or impaired inhibitory checkpoints. CEACAM21 represents a critical immune modulator linking cell adhesion, kinase signaling, and T cell effector function.
