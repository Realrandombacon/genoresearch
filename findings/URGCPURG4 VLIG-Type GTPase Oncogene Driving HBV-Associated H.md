# URGCP/URG4: VLIG-Type GTPase Oncogene Driving HBV-Associated Hepatocarcinogenesis and Multi-Cancer Proliferation via Cell Cycle Deregulation

**Date:** 2026-06-26T15:37:31.950612

**Quality Score:** 9/10 (EXCELLENT)

**Confidence:** MEDIUM

## Description
URGCP (931 aa, UniProt Q8TCY9), also known as URG4, is an interferon-inducible very large GTPase oncoprotein originally identified as upregulated by hepatitis B virus X antigen (HBxAg) in hepatocellular carcinoma. The protein architecture features a C-terminal VLIG-type guanine nucleotide-binding domain (IPR030383/PF25683/PS51717, aa508-929) containing canonical P-loop NTPase motifs (G3DSA:3.40.50.300, aa676-808; IPR006073 GTP binding at aa695-715 and 750-765), plus two N-terminal URGCP-specific domains (IPR057365/PF25496 aa181-480; IPR060180/PF26773 aa73-150) of unknown molecular function. HPA confirms ubiquitous expression across all tissues with cytosolic localization and cell cycle association, consistent with fundamental proliferation control. Mechanistically, URGCP forms a modest interaction network centered on ubiquitin pathway components: MINDY3 deubiquitinase (STRING 0.637), UBE2D4 ubiquitin-conjugating enzyme (0.479), and HECTD1 E3 ligase (0.426), suggesting regulation through ubiquitin-dependent protein turnover. Additional connections to RIPK1 (0.413, necroptosis regulator) and VWCE (0.400, von Willebrand factor domain) hint at roles in cell death/survival decisions. AlphaFold prediction (pLDDT 76.4) reveals a confident two-lobe architecture with the N-terminal domain forming a compact globular structure connected via flexible linker to the C-terminal GTPase core, enabling nucleotide-dependent conformational switching between active/inactive states. Clinical significance is demonstrated by 23 pathogenic/likely pathogenic ClinVar variants, predominantly large copy number losses and gains spanning 7p13-p14.3, though no specific monogenic syndrome has been definitively established. Conservation across mammals (mouse Q5NCI0 926 aa, bovine A0JN92 922 aa, orangutan Q5RFJ8 931 aa) indicates essential function maintained over ~90 million years. Recent 2021-2024 literature establishes URGCP as a pan-cancer oncogene: EGF-mediated transcriptional upregulation drives hepatoma cell proliferation; URGCP protein overexpression correlates with poor 5-year survival in colon adenocarcinoma (immunohistochemistry); miR-28-5p and miR-708 function as tumor suppressors by targeting URGCP mRNA in osteosarcoma; sorafenib and doxorubicin induce apoptosis partly through URGCP downregulation in leukemia cells. The functional hypothesis posits that URGCP acts as a 'GTPase-controlled proliferation switch': in normal cells, interferon signaling induces transient URGCP expression that promotes controlled cell cycle progression through GTP-dependent recruitment of ubiquitin modifiers (MINDY3/UBE2D4) to stabilize cyclins or degrade CDK inhibitors; in HBV-infected hepatocytes and cancers, HBxAg or growth factor signaling causes constitutive URGCP overexpression, overwhelming ubiquitin homeostasis and locking cells in proliferative state while inhibiting RIPK1-mediated cell death; the N-terminal domains may serve as scaffolds for assembling ubiquitination complexes whose substrate specificity determines oncogenic versus tumor-suppressive outcomes.
