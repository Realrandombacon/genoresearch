## Description
DNAI3 (Dynein Axonemal Intermediate Chain 3) is an 891-amino acid ciliary protein (UniProt Q8IWG1) that represents a genuine dark gene with only 6 publications despite critical roles in axonemal dynein assembly and ciliary/flagellar motility. Structurally, DNAI3 contains multiple WD40 repeats (IPR001680/SM00320, residues 387-426, 467-524, 661-700) forming beta-propeller structures for protein-protein interactions, a dynein axonemal intermediate chain 3 N-terminal domain (IPR060631/PF27785, residues 40-129), a WD40 domain specific to DNAI3 (PF28639, residues 320-792), and belongs to the dynein axonemal intermediate chain family (IPR050687/PTHR12442, residues 28-829). The protein contains YVTN repeat-like domains (G3DSA:2.130.10.10, residues 313-431, 462-569, 626-850) characteristic of quinoprotein amine dehydrogenase superfamily adapted for dynein scaffolding. AlphaFold predicts high-confidence structure (pLDDT 77.0), indicating a stable fold consistent with its role as a structural component of the axonemal dynein complex. Functionally, DNAI3 localizes to specialized ciliary structures including perinuclear theca, calyx, connecting piece, and mid piece—regions critical for sperm flagella assembly and function. Expression is group-enriched with highest levels in choroid plexus (40.0 nTPM, HPA), adrenal gland (20.6 nTPM), testis (17.6 nTPM), and fallopian tube (13.6 nTPM), reflecting roles in cerebrospinal fluid production, reproductive tract ciliary motility, and spermatogenesis. STRING interactions confirm integration within the axonemal dynein machinery through strong associations with dynein heavy chains DNAH1 (0.888), DNAH2 (0.877), DNAH17 (0.871), DNAH8 (0.866), DNAH7 (0.846), DNAH5 (0.761), DNAH3 (0.750), plus regulatory partners WDR78 (0.948, dynein assembly factor), CASC1 (0.844, cancer susceptibility candidate), and NME8 (0.574, nucleoside diphosphate kinase linked to ciliary function). Critically, ClinVar documents 18 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number losses spanning 1p31-22 and deletions, yet ZERO diseases are formally associated—a significant gap given the established role of dynein intermediate chains in primary ciliary dyskinesia (PCD). The mechanistic hypothesis: DNAI3 functions as an intermediate chain within the outer dynein arm complex of motile cilia and flagella, serving as a molecular bridge between the catalytic heavy chain ATPases and the radial spoke/central pair apparatus that regulates ciliary beat frequency and waveform. The WD40 repeats mediate protein-protein interactions with dynein heavy chains and assembly factors (WDR78), while the N-terminal domain may anchor the complex to the microtubule doublet. In choroid plexus, DNAI3-dependent ciliary motility drives cerebrospinal fluid circulation; dysfunction could contribute to hydrocephalus or impaired waste clearance. In reproductive tissues, DNAI3 is essential for sperm flagellar motility (male infertility) and oviductal ciliary transport (female infertility/ectopic pregnancy risk). The absence of formal disease nomenclature despite pathogenic variants suggests phenotypic heterogeneity, incomplete penetrance, compensation by other intermediate chains (DNAI1, DNAI2 cause PCD), or requirement for biallelic mutations not yet identified. Future work should identify DNAI3-specific binding partners within the dynein regulatory complex and screen PCD patients for DNAI3 mutations.
