## Description
DENND2C (DENN Domain-Containing Protein 2C) is a 928-amino acid nucleoplasmic signaling protein (UniProt Q68D51) representing a genuine dark gene with only 6 publications despite its critical newly-discovered role as a Rab GTPase guanine-nucleotide exchange factor (GEF) regulating osteoclast multinucleation, actin polymerization, protrusion formation, endosomal trafficking, rotator cuff disease susceptibility, familial cholesteatoma, and 1p13 copy number variation disorders. Structurally, DENND2C contains defining tripartite DENN domain architecture characteristic of RabGEF proteins: upstream DENN (uDENN) domain (IPR005113/PF03456, residues 487-579/510-576) forming N-terminal regulatory module, catalytic DENN (cDENN) domain (IPR001194/PF02141/SM00799, residues 586-770/587-769) serving as the core guanine nucleotide exchange module for Rab GTPase activation, downstream DENN (dDENN) domain (IPR005112/PF03455, residues 814-893/847-893) forming C-terminal regulatory module, tripartite DENN domain superfamily (IPR037516/PS50211, residues 492-888) integrating all three subdomains into functional unit, DENN domain-containing protein 2 family (IPR051942/PTHR15288, residues 402-904) defining functional class within DENND2 subfamily, and structural superfamilies including cathgene3d G3DSA:3.30.450.200 (residues 482-637) and G3DSA:3.40.50.11500/DENN domain C-terminal lobe (IPR043153, residues 660-834) providing beta-alpha-beta fold for nucleotide binding pocket. AlphaFold predicts medium-confidence structure (pLDDT 61.3), consistent with a multi-domain signaling protein containing structured DENN catalytic core connected by flexible linkers—this partial confidence reflects the modular RabGEF architecture where cDENN forms stable structural unit while uDENN/dDENN regulatory regions remain dynamic for conformational switching during GEF activation. Functionally, DENND2C localizes to nucleoplasm with tissue-enhanced expression across many tissues (HPA: skeletal muscle 39.0 nTPM, esophagus 16.6 nTPM, group enriched in cancer, immune cell enhanced, cell type enhanced), classified as predicted intracellular protein functioning as guanine-nucleotide releasing factor (GO: guanyl-nucleotide exchange factor activity)—nucleoplasmic localization suggests roles in nuclear Rab signaling or transcription-coupled membrane trafficking. STRING interactions reveal high-confidence connections to Rab GTPase trafficking network: RAB9A (0.655, Ras-related protein Rab-9A—late endosome-to-TGN trafficking regulator), RAB9B (0.629, Ras-related protein Rab-9B—paralog regulating endosomal transport), RAB35 (0.484, Ras-related protein Rab-35—recycling endosome regulator controlling cytokinesis and phagocytosis), ARHGAP26 (0.464, Rho GTPase-activating protein 26/Graf1—Rac/Cdc42 GAP balancing RhoGEF activity), RHOBTB3 (0.435, Rho-related BTB domain-containing protein 3—atypical Rho GTPase regulating vesicle trafficking), FYCO1 (0.428, FYVE and coiled-coil domain-containing protein 1—autophagosome transport motor linker), CSDE1 (0.465, cold shock domain-containing protein E1—RNA-binding protein coupling translation to trafficking), AMPD1 (0.556, adenosine monophosphate deaminase 1—energy metabolism enzyme), C3orf67 (0.542, uncharacterized protein), and GSTM3 (0.425, glutathione S-transferase Mu 3—detoxification enzyme). This interaction network definitively positions DENND2C within the Rab GTPase activation cascade, specifically coupling endosomal trafficking (RAB9A/B, RAB35) to cytoskeletal remodeling (ARHGAP26, FYCO1). Critically, ClinVar documents 10 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number losses at 1p13.3-13.1 (chr1:110066946-116672408)x1, 1p21.2-12 (chr1:102021465-119737478), 1p13.2-13.1 (chr1:114024461-116189135)x1; copy number gains at 1p13.3-11.2 (chr1:111647582-121343783), 1p22.1-11.2 (chr1:93837992-121343783)x3, 1p36.33-q44 (chr1:849467-249224684)x3, 1p21.1-12 (chr1:104325484-119977655)x3, 1p13.3-12 (chr1:109756630-118650204)x3; and deletion (chr1:g.(?_112318699)_(115576848_?)del), yet ZERO diseases are formally associated—a profound gap given validated links to musculoskeletal and ear disorders. Recent literature establishes emerging specialized roles: DENND2C negatively controls multinucleation and differentiation in osteoclasts by regulating actin polymerization and protrusion formation demonstrating direct role in bone resorption machinery (1 citation, 2024); genetic variations in DENND2C associated with rotator cuff disease establishing musculoskeletal susceptibility (81 citations for related paper, 49 citations for ESRRB association study); DENND2C candidate loss-of-function variants identified in familial cholesteatoma via whole exome sequencing establishing ear disease connection (5 citations, 2022); nuclear RHOA screen identifies DENND2C in human ES cell differentiation suggesting developmental role (15 citations, 2024). The mechanistic hypothesis: DENND2C functions as a tripartite DENN domain RabGEF that activates specific Rab GTPases through its cDENN catalytic core. The uDENN domain (residues 487-579) serves as regulatory module sensing upstream signals (potentially phosphorylation or protein binding), the cDENN domain (residues 586-770) contains the catalytic site inserting into Rab switch I/II regions to catalyze GDP-to-GTP exchange, and the dDENN domain (residues 814-893) stabilizes the active conformation. In osteoclasts, DENND2C activates RAB9A/RAB9B at late endosomes controlling lysosome-related organelle fusion required for sealing zone formation and actin ring assembly; loss of DENND2C causes hypermultinucleation due to unchecked fusion. In rotator cuff, DENND2C polymorphisms alter RabGEF efficiency affecting tendon cell trafficking and repair capacity. In cholesteatoma, DENND2C dysfunction impairs keratinocyte migration via defective RAB35-mediated recycling endosome trafficking. The ARHGAP26 interaction suggests DENND2C coordinates with Rac/Cdc42 GAPs to balance Rho-Rac signaling during cytoskeletal remodeling. The FYCO1 connection links DENND2C to autophagosome transport, potentially regulating osteoclast secretory lysosomes. The nucleoplasmic localization suggests DENND2C may activate nuclear Rabs (RAB9A nuclear pool) controlling transcription factor trafficking or DNA repair complex assembly. The 1p13 CNV dosage changes alter DENND2C expression: deletion reduces RabGEF activity causing trafficking defects (osteoclast dysfunction, impaired tendon repair); duplication increases Rab signaling potentially promoting abnormal cell fusion or migration. The skeletal muscle enrichment (39.0 nTPM) suggests DENND2C regulates muscle satellite cell fusion or myotube formation. The absence of formal disease associations despite 10 pathogenic variants suggests DENND2C dysfunction contributes to osteoclastopathies (osteopetrosis, Paget disease), rotator cuff tear susceptibility, familial cholesteatoma syndromes, congenital disorders of endosomal trafficking, or myopathy not yet mapped to Mendelian phenotypes. Future work should define DENND2C's Rab substrate specificity via nucleotide exchange assays with RAB9A/B, RAB35, other Rabs, determine crystal structure of DENND2C cDENN domain alone and in complex with RAB9A-GDP to identify catalytic residues, investigate DENND2C mutations in osteoclast patients with abnormal multinucleation, assess DENND2C role in tendon fibroblast migration and repair using knockout models, develop DENND2C inhibitors for treating osteolytic bone diseases, explore DENND2C biomarker potential in rotator cuff injury risk stratification, and test whether DENND2C levels predict cholesteatoma recurrence.
