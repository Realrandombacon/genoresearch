## Description
SLC5A9 (UniProt Q2M3M2, 681 aa), also known as Sodium/Glucose Cotransporter 4 (SGLT4), is a partially characterized solute carrier with only 25 literature papers despite its fundamental role in sugar transport. The seminal 2005 study (203 citations) established SLC5A9 as an essential Na+-dependent transporter for mannose, 1,5-anhydro-D-glucitol (1,5-AG), and fructose, distinguishing it from other SGLT family members. InterPro reveals canonical sodium/solute symporter architecture (IPR001734, PF00474, aa 34-510) with the conserved signature motif (PS00456, aa 183-208) critical for sodium coupling, and G3DSA:1.20.1730.10 superfamily domains (aa 61-579) forming the characteristic LeuT-fold with 14 transmembrane helices typical of the SSS family. AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (global pLDDT 82.9), indicating a stable transporter capable of alternating access mechanisms for substrate translocation. HPA shows tissue-enriched expression with highest levels in intestine (40.9 nTPM), consistent with its role in dietary sugar absorption; subcellular localization includes actin filaments and cytosol, suggesting potential cytoskeletal interactions for membrane trafficking. STRING interactions reveal medium-confidence partners including SLC2A2/GLUT2 (0.619, facilitated glucose transporter), SLC2A5/GLUT5 (0.580, fructose transporter), SLC2A7/GLUT7 (0.539), SLC2A11/GLUT11 (0.534), and SLC2A8/GLUT8 (0.464), indicating functional coordination with facilitative glucose transporters for comprehensive sugar handling. Additional partners include FAM71E1 (0.533), WDR25 (0.492), TRABD2B (0.471), and ANKRD50 (0.469), suggesting regulatory complexes. ClinVar contains 9 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains/losses spanning 1p34.1-32.3 and 1p36.33-q44 regions, though specific SLC5A9 point mutations remain of uncertain significance (?), including c.542C>T (p.Ala181Val) and c.1838-56A>G, suggesting potential dosage sensitivity rather than monogenic pathology. Conservation is strong across vertebrates: mouse Q8VDT1 (685 aa), zebrafish A8WHP3 (657 aa), chimpanzee isoforms (681-706 aa), indicating evolutionary constraint on sugar transport function. Functional hypothesis: SLC5A9 functions as a low-affinity, high-capacity Na+-coupled symporter for mannose, 1,5-AG, and fructose in intestinal epithelium, operating via the rock-and-roll alternating access mechanism characteristic of the SSS family. Its intestinal enrichment (40.9 nTPM) suggests a primary role in absorbing dietary mannose (from glycoprotein digestion) and 1,5-AG (a dietary polyol used as a glycemic marker), complementing SGLT1 (high-affinity glucose/galactose) and SGLT2 (renal glucose reabsorption). The interaction network with GLUT2, GLUT5, GLUT7, and GLUT11 suggests coordinated basolateral efflux following apical SGLT4-mediated uptake, forming a complete transcellular sugar transport pathway. The 1,5-AG transport function is clinically significant: 1,5-AG serum levels inversely correlate with glycemic excursions in diabetes, and SLC5A9 polymorphisms may influence 1,5-AG homeostasis and diabetes biomarker interpretation. The actin filament localization suggests regulation via cytoskeletal dynamics during postprandial membrane trafficking. Priority research: (1) kinetic characterization of mannose/1,5-AG/fructose transport (Km, Vmax), (2) association studies linking SLC5A9 variants to 1,5-AG levels and diabetes phenotypes, (3) investigation of SGLT4-GLUT5 functional coupling in intestinal fructose handling, (4) structural analysis of substrate-binding pocket for drug design, (5) role in fructose malabsorption syndromes.
