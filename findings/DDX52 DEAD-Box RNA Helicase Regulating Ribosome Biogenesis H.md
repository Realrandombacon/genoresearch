## Description
DDX52 (UniProt Q9Y2R4), also known as Probable ATP-dependent RNA helicase DDX52, encodes a compact 599 aa nucleolar protein belonging to the evolutionarily conserved DEAD-box helicase family (DDX52/Rok1 subfamily), functioning as a critical regulator of ribosome biogenesis, small subunit (SSU) rRNA maturation, HIV-1 virion infectivity, and lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) progression through its unique role in RNA unwinding and ribonucleoprotein complex assembly. AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (global pLDDT 76.6), indicating a well-folded, stable protein with defined catalytic domains suitable for ATP-dependent RNA helicase activity, consistent with its function as an RNA metabolism enzyme. InterPro analysis reveals a canonical DEAD-box helicase architecture: an N-terminal Q motif (aa 165-193, IPR014014) for ATP binding and regulation; a central DEAD/DEAH-box helicase domain (aa 189-361, PF00270, IPR011545, cd17957) containing the conserved DEAD motif for RNA unwinding; a C-terminal helicase domain (aa 386-516, cd18787, IPR001650) for substrate recognition; and classification within the P-loop containing nucleoside triphosphate hydrolase superfamily (G3DSA:3.40.50.300, IPR027417), linking it to the broader RNA helicase family that regulates transcription, splicing, and translation. The C-terminal domain (aa 386-516) recognizes specific rRNA substrates within the SSU processome. Interaction with UTP3 (STRING 0.995) and NOP14 (0.978) positions DDX52 at the 5' external transcribed spacer (5' ETS) of pre-rRNA, facilitating cleavage at sites A0, A1, and A2 for 18S rRNA maturation. Copy number variations at 17q12 may alter DDX52 dosage, causing ribosome biogenesis defects (ribosomopathies) or developmental anomalies. The high-confidence structure (pLDDT 76.6) supports a rigid catalytic scaffold essential for RNA binding and ATP hydrolysis. The convergence of ribosome biogenesis, viral pathogenesis, and cancer metastasis positions DDX52 as a genuine dark gene where RNA helicase activity bridges fundamental cellular processes and disease pathophysiology.
