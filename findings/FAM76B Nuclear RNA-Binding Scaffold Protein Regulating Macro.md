# FAM76B: Nuclear RNA-Binding Scaffold Protein Regulating Macrophage Activation and mRNA Stability

## Description
FAM76B encodes a 339 amino acid nuclear protein (UniProt Q5HYJ3) with a conserved FAM76 domain (IPR032017, PF16046, residues 3-332) that confers RNA-binding activity. AlphaFold predicts a well-folded structure with high confidence (pLDDT 76.1), indicating stable tertiary structure suitable for molecular interactions. The protein localizes to nuclear specks and nucleoli fibrillar centers, consistent with roles in mRNA processing and stabilization. Human Protein Atlas data shows highest expression in bone marrow (37.3 nTPM) with tissue-enhanced expression pattern detected across all tissues, suggesting housekeeping function with hematopoietic enrichment. ClinVar documents 15 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly chromosomal 11q copy number variants (deletions and duplications), indicating dosage sensitivity and clinical relevance. Orthologs exist in mouse (Q80XP8, 339 aa, 100% length conservation) and zebrafish (Q6PBM7, 328 aa, 97% length conservation), demonstrating strong evolutionary preservation across 450+ million years. Functional hypothesis: FAM76B acts as a nuclear RNA-binding scaffold that stabilizes specific mRNA transcripts and negatively regulates macrophage activation, potentially through sequestration of inflammatory transcripts in nuclear specks. The negative regulation of protein export from nucleus suggests FAM76B may retain target mRNAs or transcription factors in the nucleus, preventing translation of pro-inflammatory proteins. Mechanistic proposal: FAM76B binds AU-rich elements or stem-loop structures in 3′UTRs of inflammatory cytokine mRNAs (e.g., TNF, IL6), recruiting them to nuclear specks for storage or degradation, thereby dampening macrophage activation responses. Dosage sensitivity (ClinVar CNVs) suggests precise expression levels are critical for immune homeostasis.
