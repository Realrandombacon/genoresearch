# TMEM216: Ciliary Transition Zone Scaffold Protein in Meckel-Joubert Ciliopathy Complex

## Description
TMEM216 (transmembrane protein 216) encodes a 145-amino acid tetraspanin-like membrane protein (UniProt Q9P0N5) with high-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 89.1), indicating a well-folded transmembrane architecture. InterPro identifies it as part of the transmembrane-17 uncharacterized protein family (IPR019184, residues 8-144) with a predicted membrane domain (PF09799, residues 22-128). HPA classifies TMEM216 as a disease-related gene involved in cilium biogenesis/degradation, with low tissue specificity (detected in all tissues) and immune cell enhancement. STRING analysis reveals 10 high-confidence interaction partners forming the ciliopathy disease module: TMEM67 (0.998), CC2D2A (0.997), TCTN1 (0.995), CEP290 (0.990), TCTN3 (0.988), MKS1 (0.987), B9D1 (0.976), B9D2 (0.968), AHI1 (0.961), and TMEM231 (0.880). These partners collectively define the Meckel-Gruber and Joubert syndrome protein complex localized to the ciliary transition zone. ClinVar documents 82 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including frameshift deletions (c.395_398del p.Leu132fs, c.135del p.Gly46fs, c.246_247del p.Cys83fs), splice variants (c.230-1G>C), and missense mutations (p.Arg73Ser), confirming strong genotype-phenotype correlation. Evolutionary conservation is strong: mouse ortholog Q9CQC4 (141 aa, 97% identity) and zebrafish E7EYQ9 (160 aa, 85% identity). Mechanistically, TMEM216 functions as a transmembrane scaffold that anchors the MKS-JBTS ciliopathy complex at the ciliary diffusion barrier, regulating selective protein trafficking into the cilium. Loss-of-function variants disrupt transition zone integrity, causing aberrant Hedgehog and Wnt signaling underlying renal cysts, cerebellar vermis hypoplasia, and retinal degeneration in Meckel-Gruber and Joubert syndromes.
