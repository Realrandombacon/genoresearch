## Description
LOC102724465 is an uncharacterized genomic locus on chromosome 15q25.3 (genomic coordinates: 87703854-87324168 on NC_000015.10, spanning approximately 379 kb). This region lacks protein-coding evidence (no UniProt entry, no STRING interactions, no HPA protein expression data), indicating it is likely a non-coding regulatory element, long non-coding RNA, or structural genomic region rather than a protein-coding gene. ClinVar analysis identified 7 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all copy number gains (duplications) ranging from large chromosomal duplications spanning 15q25.2-26.3 (coordinates 82859676-101920998). The exclusive duplication pathogenicity pattern (7 gains, 0 losses) indicates strong triplosensitivity where increased copy number causes disease through gene dosage effects. The 15q25.3 chromosomal region contains critical genes including CHRNA3, CHRNA5, and CHRNB4 (nicotinic acetylcholine receptor subunits alpha-3, alpha-5, and beta-4) which are associated with nicotine addiction, lung cancer susceptibility, and autosomal dominant nocturnal frontal lobe epilepsy. Additional genes in this region include IREB2 (iron regulatory protein 2), PSMA4 (proteasome subunit alpha-4), and LOC102724465 itself may represent a regulatory element controlling expression of these neighboring genes. Duplications of this region likely cause overexpression of nicotinic receptor subunits or other dosage-sensitive genes, leading to neurological phenotypes (epilepsy, addiction susceptibility) or developmental abnormalities. The lack of protein-coding potential combined with strong CNV pathogenicity suggests LOC102724465 functions as a cis-regulatory element (enhancer, silencer, or chromatin boundary) that modulates expression of adjacent genes in the 15q25 cluster. This region may contribute to 15q duplication syndrome phenotypes including developmental delay, seizures, and behavioral abnormalities.
