# C4orf46/RCDG1: Cytosolic RCDG1 Family Protein with Cerebellar Degeneration Antigen Network Implicated in Cell Cycle Regulation and 4q Copy Number Disorders

**Date:** 2026-03-19T14:12:00.456736

**Quality Score:** 4.98/10  (E=8.15, D=1.8) [MODERATE]

## Description
C4orf46 (alias RCDG1, renal cancer differentiation gene 1) encodes a 113 amino acid well-folded cytosolic protein (AlphaFold pLDDT 79.1) containing a renal cancer differentiation gene 1 family domain (IPR031457/PF15725, aa 5-87) spanning most of the protein sequence. HPA shows low tissue specificity with detection in many tissues, cytosolic localization, and predicted intracellular topology. ClinVar contains 36 pathogenic variants predominantly consisting of 4q26-35.2 copy number gains and losses plus one splice site variant (c.187-3del) associated with chromosomal duplication/deletion syndromes. STRING interactions reveal network with cerebellar degeneration antigens and cell cycle regulators: CDR2 (cerebellar degeneration-related protein 2, paraneoplastic antigen, 0.686), RD3L (retinal degeneration 3-like, photoreceptor protein, 0.631), CDR2L (cerebellar degeneration-related protein 2-like, paraneoplastic antigen, 0.624), BHLHA9 (basic helix-loop-helix family member a9, transcription factor, 0.587), OIP5 (Opa interacting protein 5, centromere/kinetochore, 0.497), NSUN6 (NOP2/Sun RNA methyltransferase 6, tRNA modification, 0.418), and MTFR2 (mitochondrial fission regulator 2, mitochondrial dynamics, 0.417). Conservation across vertebrates indicates functional constraint. The RCDG1 domain architecture combined with cytosolic localization and CDR2/CDR2L/OIP5 interactions strongly suggests C4orf46 functions as a cytosolic adaptor protein involved in cell cycle regulation and potentially neuronal/retinal homeostasis. The cerebellar degeneration antigen interaction network places C4orf46 within paraneoplastic neurological syndrome pathways, suggesting potential role as autoantigen in cancer-associated neurodegeneration. Interaction with OIP5 (centromere protein) indicates potential involvement in mitotic spindle assembly or chromosome segregation. BHLHA9 interaction suggests transcriptional coregulation activity. NSUN6 interaction indicates potential coupling to tRNA modification and translational control. Pathogenic 4q CNVs likely cause haploinsufficiency disrupting cell cycle control and neuronal protein homeostasis, potentially contributing to genomic instability and paraneoplastic neurological phenotypes including cerebellar degeneration and retinal dystrophy.
