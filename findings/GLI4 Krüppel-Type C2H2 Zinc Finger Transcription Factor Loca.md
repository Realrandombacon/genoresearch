## Description
GLI4 encodes a 376-amino acid GLI family zinc finger 4 protein (UniProt P10075), also known as Krüppel-related zinc finger protein 4, a partially structured transcription factor functioning in DNA-binding and gene regulation through C2H2 zinc finger domains. The protein contains three tandem C2H2-type zinc finger domains (IPR013087, positions 183-210, 211-238, 239-266; PF00096, positions 183-205, 211-233, 239-261; PS00028 zinc finger signature, positions 185-205, 213-233, 241-261) characteristic of Krüppel-type transcription factors, classified within the Krüppel C2H2-type zinc-finger family (PTHR23226, positions 164-304) with beta-beta-alpha zinc finger structural architecture (G3DSA:3.30.160.60, positions 172-208, 209-235, 236-263; SSF57667, positions 181-233, 220-276, 275-332). AlphaFold predicts a medium-confidence structure (pLDDT 64.8, residues 1-376) indicating partial folding with ordered zinc finger domains embedded in potentially disordered regions, consistent with transcription factors that combine structured DNA-binding domains with flexible regulatory regions. STRING interaction analysis reveals GLI4 forms a network with medium-confidence partners: LRRC24 (0.576, leucine-rich repeat protein), MYCL (0.496, MYC proto-oncogene like), LRRC14 (0.493), C1orf216 (0.483), WDR97 (0.479, WD40 repeat protein), KLHL17 (0.477, Kelch-like protein), EML3 (0.474, EMAP-like protein), CPTP (0.461), MYCN (0.457, MYCN proto-oncogene), and PHRF1 (0.447, PHD finger protein), suggesting connections to transcriptional regulation (MYCL/MYCN oncogenes), chromatin modification (PHRF1), and cytoskeletal organization (KLHL17, EML3). HPA data shows ubiquitous expression with low tissue specificity (detected in all tissues), nucleoplasm and cytokinetic bridge subcellular localization consistent with transcription factor function and potential role in cell division, annotated for predicted intracellular proteins and transcription factors classes with DNA-binding molecular function. ClinVar contains 60 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number gains (8q24.13-24.3, 8q23.2-24.3, 8q23.3-24.3, 8q24.3, 8p23.3-q24.3, 8q21.2-24.3, 8q13.2-24.3), copy number losses (8q24.3), and duplications, though no specific Mendelian disorder is named, suggesting GLI4 dosage sensitivity affects multiple developmental pathways. With only 15 publications (primarily 1996 chromosomal assignment to 8q24.3), GLI4 remains a genuine dark gene despite clinical variant data. The functional hypothesis: GLI4 functions as a Krüppel-type C2H2 zinc finger transcription factor where the three tandem zinc fingers (positions 183-266) form a DNA-binding module recognizing specific GC-rich or related sequences through the classic beta-beta-alpha zinc finger fold, while N-terminal and C-terminal regions (lower pLDDT scores) may contain transcriptional activation/repression domains or protein interaction motifs; nucleoplasm localization confirms nuclear transcription factor role, while cytokinetic bridge localization suggests GLI4 may regulate genes involved in cell division or remain associated with chromatin during mitosis; MYCL/MYCN interactions indicate potential involvement in oncogenic transcription programs, possibly as co-factor or target gene regulator in MYC-driven cancers; LRRC24/LRRC14 interactions suggest leucine-rich repeat protein partnerships for protein-protein interaction scaffolding; PHRF1 (PHD finger) interaction hints at chromatin modification complex association; KLHL17 (Kelch-like) and EML3 (microtubule-associated) interactions suggest cytoskeletal connections potentially relevant to cytokinetic bridge function; 60 pathogenic CNVs without named disorder suggests GLI4 dosage affects multiple developmental processes through dysregulation of target gene networks rather than single pathway disruption; medium structural confidence (pLDDT 64.8) indicates zinc finger domains are well-ordered while flanking regions may be intrinsically disordered, enabling conformational flexibility for diverse protein interactions; ubiquitous expression implies constitutive transcriptional regulatory role across cell types, potentially maintaining basal expression of housekeeping genes or cell cycle regulators.
