## Description
ING5 encodes a 240 aa nuclear tumor suppressor protein (UniProt Q8WYH8) that functions as a critical chromatin regulator and transcriptional coactivator within multi-subunit histone acetyltransferase (HAT) complexes, integrating histone mark recognition through its C-terminal PHD finger with N-terminal histone-binding capacity to coordinate epigenetic regulation of cell cycle progression, apoptosis induction, and potentially immune cell differentiation. This genuine dark gene has substantial clinical significance with 105 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants though specific monogenic diseases await characterization beyond chromosomal instability syndromes involving 2q37.3 deletions/duplications. The protein features specialized chromatin reader architecture enabling coordinated histone recognition and HAT complex recruitment: N-terminal histone-binding domain (IPR024610/PF12998/G3DSA:6.10.140.1740, residues 6-107/2-105) mediating direct histone tail interactions and nucleosome positioning; C-terminal PHD-type zinc finger (IPR001965/IPR011011/IPR013083/IPR019786/IPR019787/G3DSA:3.30.40.10/cd15685, residues 187-235/188-233/179-236/189-232/186-235/163-238) recognizing unmethylated or methylated histone H3 tails (typically H3K4me3) for chromatin targeting; classified within inhibitor of growth family (IPR028651, residues 2-238) with GO terms for chromatin regulator, transcription activator, transcription regulation functioning as epigenetic reader. STRING interactions reveal ING5 operates within extremely high-confidence HAT complex networks: MEAF6 (0.997), MYST/Esa1-associated factor 6 essential scaffold component of MOZ/MORF complexes; BRD1 (0.991), bromodomain-containing protein 1 core subunit of HAT complexes; KAT6B (0.986)/KAT6A (0.449), lysine acetyltransferases 6A/6B (MOZ/MORF) catalytic subunits; BRPF3 (0.964)/BRPF1 (0.947), bromodomain and PHD finger-containing proteins 1/3 serving as HAT complex scaffolds; KAT7 (0.688), lysine acetyltransferase 7 (HBO1); TP53 (0.551), tumor protein p53 linking chromatin regulation to cell cycle checkpoint control; JADE1 (0.482)/JADE2 (0.418), jade family PHD finger proteins related to ING family. HPA confirms ubiquitous expression with low tissue specificity, detected in all tissues, low cancer specificity, immune cell enhanced blood cell pattern consistent with hematopoietic regulatory function, cell type enhanced classification, nuclear localization consistent with chromatin regulator function, classified as predicted intracellular protein involved in transcription/transcription regulation functioning as activator/chromatin regulator. ClinVar lists 105 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants dominated by large-scale copy number losses (2q37.3 chr2:241644934-242783384 x1, 2q37.2-37.3 chr2:236478472-243048854 x1, 2q37.1-37.3 chr2:235267074-242782258 x1, 2q37.2-37.3 chr2:236057846-242783384 x1, 2q37.3 chr2:239671606-242782258 x1) and gains (2q33.3-37.3 chr2:206965837-242783384 x3, 2q37.2-37.3 chr2:236127397-242783384 x3, 2q35-37.3 chr2:216815496-242782258 x3) plus single allele deletions and large terminal deletions (NC_000002.11:g.(?_237481970)_(242801596_?)del), demonstrating strong dosage sensitivity with haploinsufficiency and triplosensitivity potentially causing developmental defects and cancer predisposition. AlphaFold structure prediction yields high confidence (global pLDDT 78.6), revealing a well-folded bi-domain architecture with structured N-terminal histone-binding region connected to ordered C-terminal PHD zinc finger suitable for chromatin engagement. Mechanistically, ING5 operates through integrated modes: (1) HAT complex recruitment—ultra-high confidence MEAF6/BRD1/KAT6B interactions position ING5 within MOZ/MORF histone acetyltransferase complexes catalyzing H3K14/H4K8 acetylation for transcriptional activation; (2) Histone mark reading—PHD finger recognizes specific histone modifications (likely H3K4me3) targeting ING5 to active promoters/enhancers; (3) Dual histone engagement—N-terminal domain provides secondary histone contact stabilizing nucleosome binding; (4) Tumor suppression—TP53 interaction (0.551) links ING5 to p53-dependent cell cycle arrest and apoptosis pathways, explaining tumor suppressor classification; (5) Transcriptional coactivation—p300/CBP interaction (from gene summary) suggests ING5 recruits additional HAT activity for robust gene activation; (6) Immune regulation—immune cell enhanced expression indicates potential role in lymphoid/myeloid differentiation programs; (7) Epigenetic memory—PHD finger-mediated histone recognition may maintain transcriptional states through cell divisions. Loss-of-function causes defective HAT complex assembly, impaired histone acetylation, dysregulated cell cycle control, failed apoptosis induction, chromosomal instability from 2q37.3 dosage alterations, and potential cancer predisposition or developmental disorders.
