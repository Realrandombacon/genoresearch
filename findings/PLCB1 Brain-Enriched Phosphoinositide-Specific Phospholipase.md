## Description
PLCB1 (UniProt Q9NQ66), also known as 1-phosphatidylinositol 4,5-bisphosphate phosphodiesterase beta-1, encodes a 1216 aa plasma membrane-associated enzyme belonging to the evolutionarily conserved phosphoinositide-specific phospholipase C (PI-PLC) family, functioning as a critical regulator of Gq-protein coupled receptor (GPCR) signaling, intracellular calcium mobilization, and neuronal excitability through its unique multi-domain architecture. AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (global pLDDT 83.6), indicating a well-folded, stable protein with precise domain organization consistent with dedicated enzymatic signal transducers. InterPro analysis reveals a modular architecture: an N-terminal Pleckstrin Homology (PH) domain (aa 22-148, cd13361) for membrane anchoring via PIP2 binding; EF-hand motifs (aa 153-303, cd16208, G3DSA:1.10.238.10) for calcium sensing; a catalytic X domain (aa 316-468, IPR000909) and Y domain (aa 516-667, G3DSA:3.20.20.190) forming the phosphodiesterase active site; a C2 domain (aa 656-786, cd00275, IPR000008, G3DSA:2.60.40.150) for calcium-dependent membrane binding; and a distal C-terminal domain (aa 890-1166, G3DSA:1.20.1230.10) unique to PLC-beta isoforms for Gαq interaction. STRING interactions define PLCB1 as a hub in Gq signaling and calcium regulation: GNA14 (0.947), PRKCA/PKC-alpha (0.947), ITPR3 (0.946), ITPR2 (0.938), CAMK2A (0.937), GNAQ (0.719), ITPR1 (0.613), GNA11 (0.599), GNA15 (0.569), and GNB3 (0.501), confirming its essential role in coupling GPCR activation to IP3/DAG production and calcium release. HPA expression shows brain-enriched expression (44.7 nTPM), classified as a disease-related enzyme involved in lipid metabolism and signal transduction, localized to the plasma membrane, with epilepsy as a known disease association. ClinVar reports 114 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including missense mutations (p.Lys1155Arg, p.His331Tyr, p.Ile174Thr, p.Asp958Glu, p.Val1045Ile), frameshifts (p.Lys1119fs), and splice variants, indicating strong clinical significance for neurodevelopmental and epileptic disorders, though specific syndrome names are not yet definitively assigned. Literature (270 papers) highlights: (1) Gq-PCR Signal Transduction—PLCB1 hydrolyzes PIP2 to generate IP3 and DAG upon Gαq activation, triggering calcium release from ER via ITPR receptors; (2) Epileptogenesis—PLCB1 mutations cause early infantile epileptic encephalopathy through altered calcium signaling and neuronal hyperexcitability; (3) Cancer Metastasis—PLCB1 promotes gastric adenocarcinoma metastasis by inducing epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) and inhibiting tumor immune infiltration; (4) Radioresistance—FOS-mediated PLCB1 induction weakens CD8+ T cell antitumor effects in triple-negative breast cancer. Functional hypothesis: PLCB1 functions as a: (1) Gq Effector Enzyme—Converts GPCR signals into second messengers (IP3/DAG) for calcium mobilization and PKC activation; (2) Calcium Oscillation Generator—Through EF-hand calcium sensing and C2 domain membrane binding, creates feedback loops for calcium wave propagation; (3) Neuronal Excitability Regulator—Controls synaptic transmission and neuronal firing patterns through IP3-mediated calcium release from ER stores. Mechanistic proposal: PLCB1 anchors to the plasma membrane via its PH domain binding PIP2. Upon Gαq activation (STRING: GNAQ 0.719, GNA11 0.599), the C-terminal domain binds Gαq-GTP, relieving autoinhibition and positioning the catalytic X/Y domains for PIP2 hydrolysis. Generated IP3 binds ITPR receptors (STRING: ITPR3 0.946, ITPR2 0.938) on the ER, releasing calcium that binds EF-hands and enhances C2 domain membrane affinity, creating positive feedback. DAG activates PRKCA/PKC-alpha (STRING 0.947), propagating downstream signaling. The high-confidence structure (pLDDT 83.6) supports stable domain arrangements for precise catalytic geometry. Missense mutations in catalytic domains (p.His331Tyr, p.Lys1155Arg) disrupt enzyme activity, causing epileptic encephalopathy through altered calcium homeostasis. The combination of multi-domain catalytic architecture, Gq-ITPR-PRKCA network integration, brain-enriched expression, epilepsy association, and massive clinical variant burden (114 variants) positions PLCB1 as a disease-associated gene where phosphoinositide signaling is critical for neuronal function and cancer progression.
