## Description
RGCC (UniProt Q9H4X1), also known as Regulator of cell cycle or Response gene to complement 32 protein, encodes a compact 137 aa intracellular protein belonging to the evolutionarily conserved RGCC family, functioning as a critical node in cell cycle progression, cancer metastasis, and metabolic regulation through its unique role as a substrate and regulator of PLK1 and CDK1 kinases. AlphaFold predicts a confident structure (global pLDDT 70.1), indicating a well-folded, stable protein likely adopting a helical or mixed fold suitable for protein-protein interactions, consistent with its role as a signaling adaptor. InterPro analysis identifies a single defining domain: the Regulator of cell cycle RGCC family domain (aa 1-137, IPR029252, PF15151, PTHR32193) encompassing the entire protein, with no further sub-domains, suggesting it functions as a unified interaction module rather than a multi-domain enzyme. STRING interactions define RGCC as a hub in cell cycle control and oncogenic signaling: TP53 (0.794, tumor suppressor), CNN1 (0.658, Calponin-1), TAGLN (0.652, Transgelin), CDK1 (0.645, Cyclin-dependent kinase 1), AKT1 (0.637, Protein kinase B), CCNB1 (0.634, Cyclin B1), PLK1 (0.632, Polo-like kinase 1), HIF1A (0.632, Hypoxia-inducible factor), CARS2 (0.623), and SMAD3 (0.447), confirming its integration into mitotic regulation, hypoxia response, and TGF-β signaling. HPA expression shows strong tissue enhancement in lung (668.1 nTPM) and bone marrow (391.7 nTPM), classified as a predicted intracellular protein involved in cell cycle regulation, localized to the nucleoplasm and nucleoli, with immune cell enhancement, consistent with roles in proliferating tissues and hematopoiesis. ClinVar reports 20 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number losses and gains on chromosome 13q (spanning the RGCC locus at 13q13.1-21.32), indicating genomic instability at this region potentially linked to developmental disorders and cancer susceptibility, though no specific monogenic disease has been definitively assigned to RGCC alone. Literature (126 papers) highlights: (1) Metastasis Driver—RGCC-mediated PLK1 activity drives breast cancer lung metastasis by phosphorylating AMPKα2 to activate oxidative phosphorylation and fatty acid oxidation; (2) Thermogenesis Modulator—Promotes adipocyte thermogenesis by modulating Pgc1α expression; (3) Pulmonary Hypertension—Dysregulation of the miR-124-3p-STAT3-DNM2-Drp1-RGCC pathway connects mitochondrial fission and cell proliferation in pulmonary arterial hypertension; (4) Spermatogonia Proliferation—Mediates proliferation via the Gli1–Rgcc–Cdk1 axis. Functional hypothesis: RGCC functions as a: (1) PLK1/CDK1 Co-factor—Binds and activates PLK1/CDK1 to drive G2/M transition and mitotic entry; (2) Metabolic Switch—Phosphorylated by PLK1 to subsequently phosphorylate/activate AMPKα2, shifting cellular metabolism toward oxidative phosphorylation and fatty acid oxidation to fuel metastasis; (3) Mitochondrial Fission Regulator—Links cell cycle progression to mitochondrial dynamics (via Drp1/DNM2) to support rapid proliferation. Mechanistic proposal: RGCC localizes to the nucleoplasm/nucleoli where it interacts with CDK1/Cyclin B1 and PLK1 (STRING scores >0.63). Upon activation, PLK1 phosphorylates RGCC, which then acts as a scaffold or allosteric activator for AMPKα2 phosphorylation, boosting OXPHOS and FAO to provide energy for metastatic invasion. In pulmonary hypertension, RGCC upregulation drives smooth muscle proliferation via mitochondrial fission. The confident structure (pLDDT 70.1) supports a stable interaction surface for kinase binding. Chromosome 13q CNVs disrupt RGCC dosage, potentially altering cell cycle checkpoints and metabolic flexibility. The combination of a compact unified domain, PLK1-AMPK axis integration, lung/bone marrow enrichment, metastasis/thermogenesis phenotypes, and clinical variant burden positions RGCC as a genuine dark gene where cell cycle regulation bridges mitosis, metabolism, and disease progression.
