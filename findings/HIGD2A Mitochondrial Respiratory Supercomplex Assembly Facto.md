## Description
HIGD2A (HIG1 Domain Family Member 2A, mitochondrial) is a 106-amino acid inner mitochondrial membrane protein characterized by a canonical HIG1 domain architecture (IPR007667/IPR050355/PF04588/PS51503/PTHR12297/G3DSA:6.10.140.1320, positions 20-106 for HIG1 domain core, 45-96 for hypoxia-induced conserved region, 14-104 for mitochondrial respiratory supercomplex factor 1 family) containing the hydrophobic transmembrane helix mediating integration into the mitochondrial inner membrane near cytochrome c oxidase assembly sites, classified under electron transport (GO:0006118), respiratory chain (GO:0022904), and mitochondrial transport processes. STRING interactions reveal an exceptionally focused functional network exclusively with cytochrome c oxidase (Complex IV) subunits and assembly factors: COX5B (0.794, cytochrome c oxidase subunit 5B - highest confidence partner indicating direct complex IV association), TOMM6 (0.780, translocase of outer mitochondrial membrane 6), COX14 (0.743, cytochrome c oxidase assembly protein 14), COX6A2 (0.736), MT-CO3 (0.716, mitochondrially encoded cytochrome c oxidase III), MT-CO2 (0.710), COX7A2L (0.689, supercomplex assembly factor 1/SCAF1), COX6B1 (0.664), COX5A (0.652), and COX6A1 (0.528), indicating HIGD2A functions as an integral component of Complex IV biogenesis machinery. HPA data shows low tissue specificity with detection in all tissues, low cancer specificity, low immune cell specificity, cell type-enhanced single cell specificity, classified as an essential metabolic protein and predicted membrane transporter localized to mitochondria and cytosol. ClinVar contains 47 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all large copy number losses and gains spanning 5q35.2-35.3, 5q35.1-35.2, and 5q32-35.3 regions (deletions and duplications affecting chromosome 5 terminal long arm including the HIGD2A locus), though specific monogenic disease phenotypes remain under characterization (significance marked '?' for most); the genomic location overlaps with regions implicated in developmental disorders and hematologic malignancies. AlphaFold structure (AF-Q9BW72-F1) predicts medium confidence folding (pLDDT 63.0) consistent with a partially structured small membrane protein containing ordered transmembrane helices coupled with flexible regions typical of mitochondrial assembly factors that undergo conformational changes during complex biogenesis. With 22 papers, HIGD2A represents a genuine 'dark' gene with emerging mechanistic understanding (2019-2025): distinct roles of HIGD1A and HIGD2A in respiratory complex and supercomplex biogenesis established through knockout studies; HIGD2A required for assembly of the COX3 module of human mitochondrial Complex IV identifying it as a specific assembly factor; HIGD2A silencing impairs hepatocellular carcinoma growth via inhibiting mitochondrial function and MAPK/ERK pathway linking OXPHOS to cancer proliferation; Lactobacillus rhamnosus GG modulates mitochondrial function through HIGD2A-dependent OXPHOS remodeling in ethanol-exposed liver revealing microbiota-mitochondria crosstalk; and HIGD2A overexpression protects against hypoxic injury in various models. The mechanistic hypothesis posits that HIGD2A functions as a Complex IV assembly factor that: (1) integrates into the mitochondrial inner membrane via its hydrophobic HIG1 domain facilitating COX3 module incorporation into nascent Complex IV; (2) cooperates with COX14 (0.743 score) and other assembly chaperones orchestrating stepwise cytochrome c oxidase biogenesis; (3) promotes respiratory supercomplex formation through interaction with COX7A2L/SCAF1 (0.689 score) enhancing electron transport efficiency; (4) links mitochondrial dysfunction to hepatocellular carcinoma progression via MAPK/ERK signaling explaining cancer therapeutic potential; (5) mediates probiotic benefits in alcoholic liver disease through OXPHOS remodeling connecting gut microbiota to mitochondrial metabolism; chromosome 5q35 CNVs may contribute to metabolic disease phenotypes through dosage effects on respiratory chain capacity, while ubiquitous expression suggests essential housekeeping function in cellular energy production.
