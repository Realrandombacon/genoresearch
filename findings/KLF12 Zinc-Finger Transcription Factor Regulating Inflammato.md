## Description
KLF12 (UniProt Q9Y4X4), also known as Krueppel-like factor 12, encodes a 402 aa nuclear transcription factor belonging to the evolutionarily conserved Krueppel-like family, functioning as a critical regulator of inflammatory responses, angiogenesis, and cancer metastasis through its unique ability to repress NF-κB signaling and modulate SMAD7 expression. AlphaFold analysis reveals a medium-confidence structure (global pLDDT 50.4), indicating a partially structured protein with likely intrinsically disordered regions (IDRs) flanking ordered domains, consistent with its role as a dynamic transcriptional regulator. InterPro analysis identifies a modular architecture: an N-terminal Krueppel-like domain (aa 110-399, cd21441, PTHR23235) capable of DNA binding and protein interaction; multiple C2H2-type zinc finger domains (aa 306-400, IPR013087, PF00096, SSF57667) forming β-sheets for structural stability and DNA recognition; and classification within the KRUEPPEL-LIKE TRANSCRIPTION FACTOR family (PTHR23235). STRING interactions define KLF12 as a hub in transcriptional regulation and signaling: TFAP2A (0.781, high confidence), CTBP1 (0.618), KIAA1143 (0.617), ETS1 (0.596), DNASE1 (0.550), IDO2 (0.527), MAGEB10 (0.473), FOXA2 (0.457), SAMSN1 (0.451), and CHP1 (0.441), suggesting roles in coordinating transcriptional programs with signaling pathways. HPA expression shows low tissue specificity with detection in many tissues, classified as a predicted intracellular protein involved in transcription regulation, localized to the nucleoplasm and cytosol, consistent with roles in both transcriptional repression and signaling modulation. ClinVar reports 39 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including copy number losses on chromosome 13q14.3-31.2 and deletions, indicating genomic instability at this region potentially linked to developmental disorders and cancer susceptibility, though no specific monogenic disease has been definitively assigned to KLF12 alone. Literature (205 papers) highlights: (1) Inflammatory Repression—KLF12 inhibits lipopolysaccharide-induced inflammatory responses, oxidative stress, pyroptosis, and endoplasmic reticulum stress in human airway epithelial cells through inhibition of the NF-κB pathway; (2) Angiogenesis Modulator—KLF12 aggravates angiotensin II-induced cardiac remodeling by transcriptionally inhibiting SMAD7, promoting fibrosis; (3) Metastasis Driver—KLF12 transcriptional activation by a novel lncRNA enhances melanoma metastasis; (4) Atherosclerosis Link—Modulates the KLF12/p300 axis through miR-590-5p to suppress atherosclerosis. Functional hypothesis: KLF12 functions as a: (1) NF-κB Repressor—Directly binds to NF-κB target promoters to inhibit inflammatory gene expression; (2) SMAD7 Antagonist—Transcriptionally represses SMAD7, thereby enhancing TGF-β signaling and promoting fibrosis/angiogenesis; (3) Metastasis Facilitator—Activates pro-metastatic genes in melanoma via lncRNA-mediated transcriptional upregulation. Mechanistic proposal: KLF12 utilizes its zinc finger domains for DNA binding to repress NF-κB and SMAD7 promoters. By inhibiting SMAD7, it enhances TGF-β signaling, driving fibrosis and angiogenesis. In melanoma, lncRNA A930015D03Rik activates KLF12, which then drives metastatic gene expression. The medium-confidence structure (pLDDT 50.4) supports a flexible mechanism where disordered regions facilitate rapid transcriptional switching. Chromosome 13q CNVs disrupt KLF12 dosage, potentially altering inflammatory and metastatic responses. The combination of zinc-finger modular architecture, NF-κB/SMAD7 repression, inflammatory/angiogenic/metastatic phenotypes, and clinical variant burden positions KLF12 as a genuine dark gene where transcriptional repression bridges immunity, fibrosis, and cancer progression.
