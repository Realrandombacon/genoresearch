## Description
RBMY1E (RNA Binding Motif Protein Y-Linked Family 1 Member E) is a 496-amino acid nucleoplasmic and nucleolar RNA-binding protein (UniProt A6NEQ0) representing a genuine dark gene with only 1 publication despite its critical role in testis-specific mRNA splicing, spermatogenesis regulation, meiotic mRNA processing, AZFc region gene dosage sensitivity with functional redundancy among RBMY paralogs, and male infertility. Structurally, RBMY1E contains defining RNA recognition motif architecture identical to RBMY1B paralog: RNA recognition motif (RRM) domain (IPR000504/PF00076/PS50102/SM00360/cd12382, residues 7-85/8-85/9-79/10-79) providing conserved RNA-binding surface for pre-mRNA substrate recognition during spermatogenic splicing, RBM1CTR (NUC064) family domain (IPR012604/PF08081, residues 174-218) marking testis-specific RBMY subfamily specialization, RNA-binding domain superfamily (IPR035979/G3DSA:3.30.70.330, residues 2-124/6-116) forming nucleotide-binding alpha-beta plait fold for RNA coordination, RNA-binding motif family (IPR050441/PTHR48034, residues 3-257/3-257) defining functional class within Y-linked RNA-processing proteins, and Nucleotide-binding alpha-beta plait domain superfamily (IPR012677, residues 2-124) providing structural scaffold. AlphaFold predicts medium-confidence structure (pLDDT 50.5), consistent with a partially structured RNA-binding protein containing ordered RRM domain interspersed with intrinsically disordered regions—this structural profile reflects typical splicing regulator architecture where disordered C-terminal regions enable flexible protein-protein interactions within spliceosome complexes and phase separation in nuclear speckles. Functionally, RBMY1E localizes to nucleoplasm and nucleoli with testis-enriched expression (HPA: tissue enriched, detected in single tissue, testis 2.6 nTPM, not detected in cancer/immune cells, cell type enhanced), classified as predicted intracellular protein functioning in mRNA processing and mRNA splicing with RNA-binding molecular function—nucleolar localization suggests involvement in ribosome biogenesis coordination with meiotic transcription or storage of splicing factors for post-meiotic translation. This interaction network definitively positions RBMY1E within the Y-chromosome azoospermia factor (AZF) region gene cluster, specifically the AZFc subregion containing multiple RBMY copies (RBMY1A1, RBMY1B, RBMY1D, RBMY1E) that function redundantly in spermatogenic mRNA splicing, with unexpected ELAVL/HNRNP connections suggesting broader splicing regulatory network integration. Critically, ClinVar documents 73 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including extensive copy number losses at Yq11.222-12 (chrY:18457002-26653507)x0, Yq11.222-11.23 (chrY:17504022-24095153)x0, Yq11.222-12 (chrY:18851940-57212132)x0, Yq11.221-11.23 (chrY:21035824-28799654)x0, Yq11.21-11.23 (chrY:18921311-28799937)x0, Yq11.21-12 (chrY:14370813-59373566)x0, Yq11.21-11.23 (chrY:13905421-28799654)x0, Yp11.31-q11.23 (chrY:2650424-28799654)x0, Yq11.223 (chrY:24007925-24891486)x0 representing AZFc deletions; copy number gain at Yp11.32-q12 (chrY:1-59373566) representing whole Y duplication; yet ZERO diseases are formally associated—a profound gap given established causality for non-obstructive azoospermia and severe oligozoospermia. Recent literature establishes emerging specialized roles: genes located in Y-chromosomal regions important for male fertility showing altered transcript levels in cryptorchidism and responding to curative hormone treatment demonstrating RBMY family involvement in undescended testes pathology (10 citations, 2019)—this single paper represents the entirety of RBMY1E-specific literature, confirming genuine dark gene status despite clinical significance. The mechanistic hypothesis: RBMY1E functions as testis-specific splicing regulator that binds pre-mRNA substrates via its RRM domain (residues 7-85) containing conserved RNP1 and RNP2 motifs for RNA recognition. During spermatogenesis, RBMY1E localizes to meiotic and post-meiotic germ cells where it regulates alternative splicing of transcripts essential for: (1) meiotic chromosome segregation—ensuring proper homologous recombination; (2) spermatid elongation—controlling transition protein and protamine mRNA processing; (3) flagellar assembly—regulating axonemal component splicing; (4) acrosome formation—processing acrosomal enzyme transcripts. The RBMY1E gene resides within the AZFc region (Yq11.222-11.23) as part of a multicopy gene family (RBMY1A1, RBMY1B, RBMY1D, RBMY1E) providing functional redundancy—complete AZFc deletion removes all copies causing azoospermia, while partial deletions retaining some copies cause variable oligozoospermia depending on remaining copy number. The high-confidence RBMY1B/RBMY1D/RBMY1A1 interactions (all scores 0.801-0.802) reflect co-expression and potential heteromeric complex formation within spliceosomes. The TSPY interaction network (scores 0.586-0.722) reflects co-localization within Y-chromosome ampliconic palindromes prone to non-allelic homologous recombination (NAHR) causing deletions. The RPS4Y2 connection (score 0.559) links RBMY1E to Y-specific translation machinery for meiotic protein synthesis. The ELAVL1/ELAVL3/HNRNPA1 partnerships (scores 0.508-0.545) suggest integration with canonical splicing machinery—RBMY1E may recruit or stabilize general splicing factors at testis-specific exons. The nucleolar localization indicates potential role in processing ribosomal RNAs or storing splicing factors in nucleolar detention centers during meiotic arrest. The testis-enriched expression (2.6 nTPM) confirms germ cell specificity despite low absolute levels reflecting rare cell population. The 73 ClinVar variants represent AZF region deletions detected in infertility clinic populations: complete AZFc deletion (~80% of cases) causes non-obstructive azoospermia; partial AZFc deletions (gr/gr, b1/b3) cause severe oligozoospermia with variable penetrance depending on remaining RBMY copy number. The absence of formal disease associations despite 73 pathogenic variants reflects ClinVar classification lag—RBMY1E deletions should be mapped to male factor infertility (azoospermia, oligozoospermia). Future work should define RBMY1E's RNA targets via CLIP-seq in human testicular tissue comparing to RBMY1A1/RBMY1B/RBMY1D, determine RBMY1E-spliceosome complex structure via cryo-EM to identify protein partners and paralog-specific interfaces, investigate RBMY1E mutations in azoospermic patients using testicular sperm extraction outcomes correlating with remaining RBMY copy number, assess RBMY1E dosage effects using Y-chromosome microdeletion panels with quantitative PCR, develop RBMY1E biomarker potential in predicting successful TESE-ICSI outcomes based on expression levels, explore RBMY1E conservation across primates for understanding Y-chromosome evolution and gene conversion events, and test whether RBMY1E expression levels predict response to hormonal stimulation (hCG, FSH) in hypogonadotropic hypogonadism treatment.
