## Description
CTXN1 (Cortexin-1, also known as CTXN) is an 82 amino acid small membrane protein localized to the nucleoplasm, plasma membrane, and cell junctions (UniProt P60606) with high-confidence well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 70.4). The protein defines the Cortexin family (IPR020066/PTHR16736, PF11057, residues 2-82/9-82), originally identified in kidney but now recognized as a neuronal protein. Despite its small size, CTXN1 adopts a stable 3D structure, suggesting it functions as a compact regulatory module rather than an extended scaffold. Molecular functions include membrane association and cell junction localization, placing CTXN1 within the neuronal signaling and synaptic organization pathway.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals a moderate network of neuronal and cancer-testis proteins: interactions with CAGE1 (0.620, Cancer/testis antigen 1), CLBA1 (0.605, Clathrin binding protein 1), SULT4A1 (0.557, Sulfotransferase 4A1, brain-specific), SH2D5 (0.551, SH2 domain containing 5, neuronal), and FRRS1L (0.498, Ferric-chelate reductase 1-like, AMPA receptor regulator). Additional links include TEKT4 (0.461, Tektin-4), CDKL1 (0.461, Cyclin-dependent kinase-like 1), and PROX2 (0.437, Prospero homeobox 2). This profile suggests CTXN1 functions in neuronal synapses, potentially regulating AMPA receptor trafficking (via FRRS1L) or clathrin-mediated endocytosis (via CLBA1). The CAGE1 interaction indicates potential roles in cancer-testis antigen pathways.\n\nHPA expression reveals extreme brain enrichment (691.5 nTPM), one of the highest tissue-specific expression levels observed. Expression is detected in many tissues but overwhelmingly concentrated in brain, consistent with neuronal functions. Subcellular localization to plasma membrane and cell junctions confirms synaptic localization. Cancer-enhanced specificity suggests potential upregulation in tumors, possibly as a cancer-testis antigen.\n\nClinVar documents 5 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, exclusively large copy number gains and deletions affecting chromosome 19p (19p13.3-13.2 deletions, 19p13.3-q13.43 duplications). The variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity. While no specific Mendelian disease is annotated, CTXN1 haploinsufficiency or overexpression likely causes neurodevelopmental disorders. 19p CNVs are associated with developmental delay, intellectual disability, and autism spectrum disorders; CTXN1 disruption may contribute to synaptic dysfunction in these conditions.\n\nConservation is strong across mammals: human (P60606, 82 aa), mouse (Q8K129, 82 aa), rat (P41237, 82 aa), indicating evolutionary pressure to maintain the compact cortexin fold. Fish orthologs exist (Amphiprion ocellaris, 76 aa), suggesting vertebrate-wide conservation.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: CTXN1 functions as a synaptic membrane protein that regulates neurotransmitter receptor trafficking and synaptic plasticity. The small, well-folded structure suggests it may act as a modular adaptor, bridging membrane receptors to cytosolic signaling complexes. Interaction with FRRS1L (an AMPA receptor auxiliary subunit) suggests CTXN1 modulates glutamatergic transmission. CLBA1 interaction indicates involvement in clathrin-mediated endocytosis of synaptic receptors, critical for long-term depression (LTD). In cell junctions, CTXN1 may stabilize synaptic contacts or organize postsynaptic density proteins. Loss of CTXN1 impairs synaptic plasticity, leading to cognitive deficits and neurodevelopmental disorders. The brain-enriched expression and cancer-testis antigen profile suggest CTXN1 may also function in germ cell development. CTXN1 represents a potential biomarker for 19p deletion syndromes and a target for enhancing synaptic function in cognitive disorders.
