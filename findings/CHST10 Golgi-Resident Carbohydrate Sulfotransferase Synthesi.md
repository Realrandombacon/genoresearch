## Description
CHST10 (Carbohydrate Sulfotransferase 10) is a 356-amino acid type II transmembrane sulfotransferase (UniProt O43529) that represents a genuine dark gene with only 23 publications despite clear enzymatic function and substantial clinical variant burden. Structurally, CHST10 contains a canonical sulfotransferase domain (IPR005331/PF03567, residues 112-349) and belongs to the carbohydrate sulfotransferase 8-10 family (IPR018011/PTHR12137, residues 49-355), placing it within the sulfotransferase superfamily that transfers sulfate groups to carbohydrate acceptors. AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (pLDDT 86.1) consistent with a well-folded enzymatic fold typical of sulfotransferases. Functionally, CHST10 localizes to the cytosol/Golgi apparatus (HPA: predicted membrane protein, cytosol) with ubiquitous expression across all tissues and cancer-enhanced expression patterns. The enzyme's primary characterized activity is transfer of sulfate to glucuronic acid residues to form the HNK-1 (Human Natural Killer-1) carbohydrate epitope (3-sulfated glucuronyl beta1-3 galactose) on glycoproteins and glycolipids—a modification critical for neural development, cell adhesion, and immune recognition. STRING interactions reveal exceptionally strong association with B3GAT1 (beta-1,3-glucuronyltransferase 1, score 0.979), the enzyme that creates the glucuronyl acceptor substrate for CHST10, confirming their functional coupling in HNK-1 biosynthesis. Additional partners include SEMA4C (0.792, a semaphorin carrying HNK-1 epitopes), CNGA3 (0.671), CA6 (0.664), and other sulfotransferases (SULT1C3: 0.609, SULT1C4: 0.598, SULT1B1: 0.558), suggesting coordinated regulation of sulfation pathways. Despite 18 ClinVar pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants (including missense p.Arg178Gln, splice variants, and large copy number gains/losses at 2q11.1-11.2), no specific Mendelian disease has been definitively associated—mirroring the pattern seen in other glycosylation defects where phenotypes may be subtle, tissue-specific, or require environmental triggers. Recent literature links CHST10 to mating-induced oviductal responses in rats (HNK-1 glycoprotein synthesis for sperm-oviduct interactions, 2025), steroid hormone regulation in mice (2012), osteoporotic bone mesenchymal stem cell dysfunction (2024), and colorectal cancer epigenetic regulation (2018). The mechanistic hypothesis: CHST10 functions as the terminal enzyme in HNK-1 epitope biosynthesis, acting sequentially after B3GAT1 to sulfate glucuronyl residues on neural cell adhesion molecules (NCAM), laminin, and proteoglycans—modulating cell-cell interactions, axonal guidance, and synaptic plasticity. Its cancer-enhanced expression suggests aberrant HNK-1 synthesis may promote metastasis through altered cell adhesion. The absence of defined disease despite variant burden implies either functional redundancy with related sulfotransferases (CHST8, CHST9), compensation by alternative glycosylation pathways, or phenotypes manifesting only under specific physiological stresses (reproduction, neural injury, aging).
