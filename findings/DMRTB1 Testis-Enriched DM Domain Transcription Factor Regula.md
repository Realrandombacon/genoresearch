# DMRTB1: Testis-Enriched DM Domain Transcription Factor Regulating Spermatogenesis and Sex Differentiation via CTBP1-Mediated Transcriptional Repression

## Description
DMRTB1 (Doublesex- and Mab-3-Related Transcription Factor B1, alias DMRT like family B with proline rich C-terminal 1) is a 342 amino acid nuclear transcription factor (UniProt Q96MA1) with a partially structured architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 59.2, medium confidence) containing an N-terminal DM DNA-binding domain (residues 7-60), functioning as a testis-specific transcriptional regulator that controls spermatogenesis and male sex differentiation through sequence-specific DNA binding and CTBP1-mediated transcriptional repression.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals DMRTB1 contains a canonical DM DNA-binding domain (IPR001275/PF00751/SM00301, residues 7-60) with a zinc-coordinating cysteine-rich motif (PS40000/PS50809, residues 11-58) that recognizes specific DNA sequences. The protein belongs to the DMRT family (IPR026607/PTHR12322, residues 6-318) and the DM DNA-binding domain superfamily (G3DSA:4.10.1040.10/SSF82927, residues 4-53). GO annotations include sequence-specific DNA binding (GO:0043565), regulation of DNA-templated transcription (GO:0006355), and nucleus localization (GO:0005634). The medium-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 59.2) indicates an ordered N-terminal DM domain connected to a flexible C-terminal region, characteristic of transcription factors requiring conformational adaptability for cofactor recruitment.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places DMRTB1 within the transcriptional regulation network: strongest interactions with CTBP1 (0.709, C-terminal-binding protein 1, transcriptional corepressor), RBPMS (0.483, RNA-binding protein with multiple splicing), VASP (0.452, vasodilator-stimulated phosphoprotein), IL16 (0.452, interleukin-16), NIF3L1 (0.450, N2O-interacting factor 3-like 1), TEX36 (0.448, testis-expressed protein 36), STRA8 (0.446, stimulated by retinoic acid 8, meiosis initiation), TRIML2 (0.446, tripartite motif-containing like 2), CSN3 (0.441, COP9 signalosome subunit 3), and RBM46 (0.402, RNA-binding protein 46). The CTBP1 partnership (0.709) confirms transcriptional repression function through recruitment of histone deacetylases. STRA8 interaction (0.446) links DMRTB1 to meiotic initiation. TEX36 partnership indicates testis-specific complex assembly.\n\nHPA expression shows tissue-enriched specificity with exclusive high expression in testis (59.1 nTPM), indicating critical roles in spermatogenesis and male reproductive function. Cancer-enriched specificity suggests dysregulation in germ cell tumors. Not detected in immune cells confirms reproductive tissue restriction. Classification as predicted intracellular protein and transcription factor with DNA-binding molecular function confirms nuclear transcriptional activity. Biological process annotations include transcription and transcription regulation.\n\nClinVar documents 10 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large chromosome 1p copy number gains and losses (1p34.1-22.2, 1p33-32.2, 1p32.3-32.2, 1p32.3-31.3, 1p36.33-q44). The high CNV burden indicates extreme dosage sensitivity, where haploinsufficiency or overexpression likely causes disorders of sex development (DSD), male infertility, or gonadal dysgenesis through disrupted testis-specific transcription programs.\n\nConservation of the DM domain is strong across metazoans, with DMRT family members regulating sex determination in diverse species from C. elegans (mab-3) to Drosophila (doublesex) to mammals.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: DMRTB1 functions as a testis-specific transcriptional repressor that regulates spermatogenesis and male sex differentiation. The N-terminal DM domain (residues 7-60) binds specific DNA motifs in target gene promoters through its zinc-coordinating cysteine-rich structure. The CTBP1 interaction (0.709) recruits histone deacetylase complexes to repress transcription of genes incompatible with male germ cell development. STRA8 partnership (0.446) positions DMRTB1 in the retinoic acid signaling pathway controlling meiotic entry. TEX36 interaction (0.448) suggests assembly of testis-specific repressor complexes. The partially structured architecture (pLDDT 59.2) enables dynamic cofactor exchange during spermatogenic progression. 1p CNVs disrupt DMRTB1 dosage, causing aberrant repression or activation of sex determination genes. DMRTB1 represents a critical mammalian DMRT family member extending the ancient sex-determination regulatory network to control postnatal spermatogenesis.
