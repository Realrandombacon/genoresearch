## Description
CHCHD7 (Coiled-coil-helix-coiled-coil-helix domain-containing protein 7, also known as COX23) is an 85 amino acid mitochondrial protein (UniProt Q9BUK0) with high-confidence well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 84.5), characteristic of cytochrome c oxidase assembly factors that contain structured CHCH domains for mitochondrial import and copper chaperone activity. The protein contains a coiled-coil-helix-coiled-coil-helix (CHCH) domain profile (PS51808, residues 13-55) enabling mitochondrial intermembrane space localization through the mitochondrial import and assembly pathway, cytochrome c oxidase-assembly factor COX23 family domains (IPR051040/PTHR46811, residues 3-80) involved in cytochrome c oxidase assembly (GO:0033616), cytochrome oxidase c subunit VIb-like family domains (IPR048280/PF02297, residues 14-53) forming the cytochrome c oxidase copper chaperone superfamily (G3DSA:1.10.287.1130, residues 11-75), and cysteine alpha-hairpin motif superfamily domains (IPR009069/SSF47072, residues 11-74) that coordinate copper ions for cytochrome c oxidase maturation. CHCHD7 is located on chromosome 8q12.1 and is classified as a predicted intracellular protein with subcellular localization to cell junctions and aggresome, functioning in mitochondrial respiratory chain complex assembly.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals a high-confidence network of cytochrome c oxidase assembly factors and mitochondrial proteins: PLAG1 (0.947, Pleomorphic adenoma gene 1, zinc finger transcription factor), COX17 (0.842, Cytochrome c oxidase copper chaperone COX17, copper delivery to COX), COX19 (0.820, Cytochrome c oxidase assembly protein COX19, copper chaperone), COA5 (0.786, Cytochrome c oxidase assembly factor 5, complex IV assembly), TCEA1 (0.770, Transcription elongation factor A1, transcriptional regulation), COA6 (0.742, Cytochrome c oxidase assembly factor 6, complex IV biogenesis), COX11 (0.725, Cytochrome c oxidase assembly protein COX11, copper transfer to COX2), SDR16C5 (0.723, Short-chain dehydrogenase/reductase 16C5, oxidoreductase), CHCHD4 (0.669, Coiled-coil-helix-coiled-coil-helix domain-containing protein 4, mitochondrial disulfide relay), and CMC2 (0.468, Copper metallochaperone 2, copper homeostasis). These interactions confirm CHCHD7 functions within the cytochrome c oxidase assembly pathway, with COX17 and COX19 as the highest-confidence partners (0.842/0.820) reflecting CHCHD7's direct role in copper delivery to cytochrome c oxidase.\n\nHPA expression reveals CHCHD7 has low tissue specificity and is detected in all tissues surveyed, indicating ubiquitous expression consistent with housekeeping roles in mitochondrial respiration and oxidative phosphorylation. Subcellular localization to cell junctions and aggresome suggests CHCHD7 may function in protein quality control and aggregation response in addition to mitochondrial copper chaperone activity. Single cell type specificity shows cell type enhanced expression, suggesting elevated levels in cells with high mitochondrial activity. The protein is classified as a cancer-related gene and disease-related gene, indicating potential roles in tumorigenesis through metabolic reprogramming.\n\nClinVar documents 30 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including intronic variants (c.154-14A>G), extensive copy number losses (8q11.21-12.3, 8q12.1, 8p11.22-q12.3 deletions) and gains (8p21.2-q21.3, 8p23.3-q24.3, 8p12-q24.3, 8p23.1-q24.3 amplifications). The variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity. While no specific Mendelian disease is annotated, 8q CNVs are associated with developmental delay, growth abnormalities, and metabolic disorders. Given CHCHD7's cytochrome c oxidase assembly function and COX17/COX19 interactions, loss-of-function may cause mitochondrial disorders through impaired complex IV assembly and reduced oxidative phosphorylation capacity.\n\nConservation is excellent across vertebrates: human (Q9BUK0, 85 aa), mouse (Q8K2Q5, 85 aa), bovine (Q17Q91, 85 aa), Xenopus (Q2TAP8, 84 aa), indicating the CHCH domain architecture is highly conserved in vertebrates with nearly identical protein length, suggesting strong purifying selection on the copper chaperone function.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: CHCHD7 functions as a cytochrome c oxidase assembly factor and copper chaperone that delivers copper ions to the cytochrome c oxidase complex IV during mitochondrial respiratory chain biogenesis. The high-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 84.5) indicates a stable folded architecture with a central CHCH domain (residues 13-55) containing twin CX9C motifs that form disulfide bonds through the mitochondrial disulfide relay system (MIA40/CHCHD4 pathway) for intermembrane space retention. Through COX17 and COX19 interactions (0.842/0.820), CHCHD7 receives copper from the cytosolic copper chaperone ATOX1 and transfers it to the COX2 subunit of cytochrome c oxidase. COA5 and COA6 interactions (0.786/0.742) link CHCHD7 to complex IV assembly intermediates. COX11 interaction (0.725) connects CHCHD7 to copper transfer to the COX2 copper center. CHCHD4 interaction (0.669) links CHCHD7 to the mitochondrial disulfide relay system for oxidative folding. CMC2 interaction (0.468) suggests coordination with other copper metallochaperones. PLAG1 interaction (0.947) is intriguing and may reflect transcriptional co-regulation of mitochondrial biogenesis genes. Ubiquitous expression (detected in all tissues) indicates CHCHD7 is essential for basal mitochondrial respiration in all cell types. 8q CNVs cause CHCHD7 haploinsufficiency or overexpression, potentially disrupting cytochrome c oxidase assembly and causing mitochondrial dysfunction, lactic acidosis, or metabolic disorders. CHCHD7 represents a candidate gene for mitochondrial complex IV deficiency and may serve as a therapeutic target for mitochondrial disorders through copper chaperone modulation.
