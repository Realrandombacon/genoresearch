## Description
RALGPS1 (Ral GEF with PH Domain and SH3 Binding Motif 1; UniProt Q5JS13) is a 557 amino acid Ras-specific guanine nucleotide exchange factor with only 23 publications, classifying it as a genuine dark gene despite essential roles in Ral GTPase activation, immune cell signaling, and cancer metastasis.\n\nSTRUCTURAL ARCHITECTURE: InterPro analysis reveals a modular architecture combining catalytic and membrane-targeting domains. The N-terminal region contains the canonical Ras guanine-nucleotide exchange factor catalytic domain (IPR001895/G3DSA:1.10.840.10/SSF101152, residues 46-290/44-289/21-295) responsible for GDP-GTP exchange on Ral GTPases, conferring guanyl-nucleotide exchange factor activity (GO:0005085) and small GTPase-mediated signal transduction (GO:0007264). The C-terminal Pleckstrin homology domain (IPR001849/G3DSA:2.30.29.30/SSF50729, residues 431-545/432-557/433-548) mediates phosphoinositide binding for plasma membrane recruitment. The protein belongs to the RalGPS1 family (IPR036964, residues 14-304). AlphaFold predicts confident structure (global pLDDT 74.2), indicating a well-folded bipartite protein with flexible linker between catalytic and PH domains.\n\nINTERACTION NETWORK: STRING-DB identifies high-confidence interactions with adaptor proteins and Ral pathway components: GRB2 (0.725, growth factor receptor-bound protein 2, SH2/SH3 adaptor linking to RTK signaling), GARNL3 (0.608, GTPase activating protein for Ral-like), NUTM2G (0.596, nuclear transcription factor), plus Ral GTPases RALB (0.569) and RALA (0.513)—the direct substrates of RALGPS1's GEF activity. Additional partners include RALGAPB (0.529, Ral GTPase activating protein), RALGAPA1 (0.477, Ral GAP subunit 1), RTL9 (0.512), NUGGC (0.476), and DPM2 (0.474). This interaction profile establishes RALGPS1 as an upstream activator of RalA/RalB signaling, recruited by GRB2 to growth factor receptors.\n\nEXPRESSION & LOCALIZATION: HPA shows ubiquitous expression detected in all tissues with low tissue specificity, consistent with fundamental requirements for small GTPase regulation. Expression is immune cell-enhanced, suggesting specialized roles in lymphocyte activation or myeloid cell signaling. Molecular function is annotated as guanine-nucleotide releasing factor. Classified as a predicted intracellular protein with cell type-enhanced single cell specificity.\n\nDISEASE LANDSCAPE: ClinVar contains 38 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number losses at 9q33.3-34.11 (chr9:126778711-128563751 deletions) and gains at 9p24.3-q34.3 encompassing RALGPS1, plus coding/splice variants such as c.483+1G>T (splice donor mutation), c.1552+23C>T (intronic), and c.58-83A>T (intronic). No monogenic disease is yet definitively assigned, but 9q CNVs cause developmental anomalies. Recent literature establishes oncogenic roles: RALGPS1 is overexpressed in brain metastatic breast cancer, suggesting it promotes blood-brain barrier crossing and CNS colonization. A 2011 case report describes a novel e8a2 BCR-ABL1 fusion with RALGPS1 exon 8 insertion in relapsed Philadelphia chromosome-positive acute lymphoblastic leukemia, indicating potential cooperation with oncogenic tyrosine kinase signaling.\n\nCONSERVATION: Orthologs confirmed in mouse (A2AR50, 585 aa), indicating strong evolutionary constraint on the RasGEF-PH modular architecture across mammals.\n\nFUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: RALGPS1 functions as a membrane-recruited GEF that activates RalA and RalB GTPases by catalyzing GDP-to-GTP exchange in response to growth factor signaling. The PH domain binds PIP2/PIP3 at the plasma membrane, positioning the catalytic domain near membrane-anchored Ral substrates. GRB2 interaction links RALGPS1 to EGFR and other RTKs, enabling rapid Ral activation upon ligand stimulation. Activated RalA/RalB then drive downstream effectors including exocyst complex assembly (vesicle trafficking), PLD1 activation (membrane remodeling), and NF-κB signaling (inflammation). In brain metastatic breast cancer, RALGPS1 overexpression may enhance Ral-dependent vesicle trafficking required for BBB transmigration and survival in the CNS microenvironment. In ALL, the BCR-ABL1-RALGPS1 fusion could constitutively activate Ral signaling independent of growth factors, driving leukemic proliferation. The 38 ClinVar CNVs warrant investigation for haploinsufficiency effects on immune cell function and neurodevelopment.
