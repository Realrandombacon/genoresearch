## Description
SMR3B (Submaxillary gland androgen-regulated protein 3B, also known as P-B, PBII, PROL3) is a 79 amino acid secreted salivary protein (UniProt P02814) with high-confidence folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 71.3), characteristic of proline-rich salivary proteins that function in oral lubrication, enamel pellicle formation, and antimicrobial defense. The protein contains a Submaxillary gland androgen-regulated protein-like family domain (IPR026288, residues 1-79) enabling endopeptidase inhibitor activity and cellular response to lipopolysaccharide, belongs to the Proline-rich submaxillary gland androgen-regulated family (PF15621, residues 1-78), contains a Prokaryotic membrane lipoprotein lipid attachment site profile (PS51257, residues 1-16), and is classified as an SMR1-related family member (PTHR14179, residues 1-79). SMR3B is located on chromosome 4q13.3 and is classified as an essential protein and predicted secreted protein with subcellular localization to extracellular exosome, functioning in negative regulation of peptidase activity and regulation of sensory perception of pain.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals a network of salivary gland secreted proteins and oral defense molecules: PRB1 (0.626, Proline-rich protein BstNI subfamily 1, salivary pellicle formation), HTN1 (0.608, Histatin-1, antifungal salivary protein), LCN1 (0.602, Lipocalin-1, tear/saliva transport protein), UBTD2 (0.593, Ubiquitin domain-containing protein 2, protein modification), SMCO1 (0.584, Single-pass membrane and coiled-coil domain-containing protein 1, membrane organization), PRB4 (0.559, Proline-rich protein 4, salivary function), MUC7 (0.481, Mucin-7, salivary mucin for lubrication), STATH (0.439, Statherin, calcium-binding salivary protein), PRB2 (0.418, Proline-rich protein 2, salivary pellicle), and PIP (0.406, Prolactin-inducible protein, immune regulation). These interactions confirm SMR3B functions within the salivary proteome, with PRB1 and HTN1 as the highest-confidence partners (0.626/0.608) reflecting coordinated roles in oral lubrication and antimicrobial defense.\n\nHPA expression reveals SMR3B has tissue-enriched expression with extraordinarily high levels in salivary gland (204,297.4 nTPM), detected in many tissues, consistent with specialized roles as a major salivary component. This extreme salivary enrichment (204,297.4 nTPM) indicates SMR3B is one of the most abundantly expressed salivary proteins, critical for oral homeostasis. Cancer-enriched RNA specificity suggests potential roles in tumor microenvironment or ectopic expression in malignancies. Subcellular localization to extracellular exosome suggests SMR3B may function in intercellular communication via exosomal transport. The protein is classified as an essential protein with endopeptidase inhibitor and peptidase regulator molecular functions.\n\nClinVar documents 22 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including extensive copy number losses (4q13.2-21.1, 4q13.1-13.3, 4q13.2-13.3, 4q12-13.3, 4q12-21.1 deletions) and gains (4p12-q35.2, 4q13.1-13.3, 4q12-31.21 amplifications), and large deletions (chr4:67833055_82716065del). The variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity. While no specific Mendelian disease is annotated, 4q CNVs are associated with developmental delay, craniofacial abnormalities, and growth disorders. Given SMR3B's salivary gland localization and PRB1/HTN1 interactions, loss-of-function may cause oral health disorders through impaired salivary lubrication, enamel protection, or antimicrobial defense.\n\nConservation is present across primates: human (P02814, 79 aa), Theropithecus gelada (A0A8D2FJA9, 78 aa), Papio anubis (A0A8I5N2C7, 79 aa), indicating the proline-rich salivary protein architecture is conserved in primates with nearly identical protein length.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: SMR3B functions as a major salivary proline-rich protein that contributes to oral lubrication, acquired enamel pellicle formation, and antimicrobial defense through protease inhibition and bacterial aggregation. The high-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 71.3) indicates a stable folded architecture despite small size (79 aa). Through PRB1 and PRB4 interactions (0.626/0.559), SMR3B cooperates with other proline-rich proteins to form the salivary pellicle that protects tooth enamel from acid erosion. HTN1 interaction (0.608) links SMR3B to antifungal defense against Candida albicans. LCN1 interaction (0.602) suggests roles in hydrophobic molecule transport in saliva. MUC7 interaction (0.481) connects SMR3B to mucin-mediated lubrication and bacterial clearance. STATH interaction (0.439) links SMR3B to calcium homeostasis and enamel remineralization. PIP interaction (0.406) suggests immune modulation in the oral cavity. Extreme salivary gland expression (204,297.4 nTPM) positions SMR3B as one of the most abundant salivary proteins. 4q CNVs cause SMR3B haploinsufficiency or overexpression, potentially disrupting salivary composition and causing xerostomia, dental caries susceptibility, or oral candidiasis. SMR3B represents a candidate gene for salivary gland disorders and may serve as a therapeutic target for oral health through salivary protein modulation.
