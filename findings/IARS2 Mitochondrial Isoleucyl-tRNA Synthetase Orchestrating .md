## Description
IARS2 (Isoleucyl-tRNA Synthetase 2, Mitochondrial) is a 1012 amino acid essential enzyme that catalyzes the attachment of isoleucine to its cognate tRNA (tRNA-Ile) within the mitochondrial matrix, serving as a critical gatekeeper for mitochondrial protein synthesis, oxidative phosphorylation (OXPHOS) assembly, and cellular energy homeostasis through high-fidelity translational elongation. InterPro analysis identifies characteristic class Ia aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase architecture including N-terminal catalytic domain (IPR002300, G3DSA:3.40.50.620, residues 99-710) with conserved HIGH motif (IPR001412, residues 116-127) performing isoleucine-tRNA ligase activity (GO:0004822) and ATP binding (GO:0005524), editing domain (IPR009008, G3DSA:3.90.740.10, residues 258-468) for hydrolytic proofreading of mischarged tRNAs ensuring translational fidelity, anticodon-binding domain (cd07960, G3DSA:1.10.730.20, residues 711-890) for specific tRNA-Ile recognition, CP2 domain-like region (G3DSA:1.10.10.830, residues 535-585) contributing to structural stability, and full-length isoleucine-tRNA ligase family signature (IPR002301, residues 83-872). AlphaFold structure (AF-Q9NSE4-F1) predicts high confidence (pLDDT 89.4) across the 1012 residue sequence, indicating a robust multi-domain enzyme with distinct catalytic, editing, and recognition modules suitable for precise mitochondrial translation. HPA data classifies IARS2 as an essential disease-related enzyme involved in protein biosynthesis with aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase and ligase molecular functions, ubiquitous expression (detected in all tissues), low tissue/cancer specificity, and exclusive mitochondrial localization reflecting its role in organellar proteome maintenance; disease associations include cataract, deafness, neuropathy, and severe mitochondrial disorders. STRING interactions reveal exceptionally tight coupling to the mitochondrial and cytosolic aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase network: EPRS1 (Glutamyl-Prolyl-tRNA Synthetase 1, 0.999) as a multifunctional synthetase; LARS1 (Leucyl-tRNA Synthetase 1, 0.996) and LARS2 (0.982) as cytosolic and mitochondrial leucyl counterparts; PARS2 (Prolyl-tRNA Synthetase 2, 0.990) and RARS2 (Arginyl-tRNA Synthetase 2, 0.987) as mitochondrial partners; QARS1 (0.981), KARS1 (0.954), MARS2 (0.932), MARS1 (0.924), and RARS1 (0.917) completing the synthetase interactome essential for global translation. ClinVar lists 117 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including nonsense mutations (c.890G>A p.Trp297Ter, c.2758C>T p.Gln920Ter, c.330C>A p.Cys110Ter), splice site defects (c.1066+1G>T, c.860-1G>A), frameshift duplications (c.1729dup p.Glu577fs), in-frame deletions (c.343CCT[1] p.Pro116del), and large copy number losses/gains on 1q41 and 1q21.1-44, causing a spectrum of mitochondrial diseases including Leigh syndrome (combined OXPHOS deficiency), West syndrome (infantile spasms with electrolyte disorders), sideroblastic anemia with hypoparathyroidism, premature ovarian insufficiency, and sensorineural deafness. Literature (52 papers) establishes IARS2 as a critical mitochondrial translation factor: compound heterozygous variants manifest as West syndrome and electrolyte disturbances expanding the neurodevelopmental phenotype; mutations lead to Leigh syndrome with combined oxidative phosphorylation deficiency confirming bioenergetic failure mechanism; rare cases present as sideroblastic anemia and hypoparathyroidism highlighting tissue-specific vulnerability; and IARS2-related disease represents an emerging category of mitochondrial translational defects with variable penetrance affecting high-energy tissues (brain, bone marrow, ovary, ear). As a well-characterized essential mitochondrial enzyme with defined monogenic disease spectrum, IARS2 represents a critical node connecting amino acid activation to mitochondrial proteostasis, OXPHOS complex assembly, and systemic energy metabolism through fidelity-controlled tRNA charging disrupted in severe pediatric mitochondrial syndromes.
