# RNF207: 634 aa Cardiac-Specific E3 Ubiquitin Ligase (pLDDT 75.2) Regulating QT Interval via KCNQ1/KCNH2 Degradation and TAB1-Mediated Hypertrophy

## Description
RNF207 (Q6ZRF8, 634 aa) is a critical cardiac-enriched E3 ubiquitin ligase that functions as a master regulator of cardiac repolarization and pathological hypertrophy. InterPro identifies a canonical TRIM-like architecture despite lacking the B-box/coiled-coil of classical TRIMs: an N-terminal C3HC4 RING finger domain (IPR001841, IPR018957, cd16558, aa 22-64) providing catalytic ubiquitin transfer activity, a B-box type 1 zinc finger (IPR000315, cd19814, aa 93-150) facilitating protein-protein interactions, a PML-like coiled-coil region (IPR021978, aa 213-323) enabling oligomerization, and a C-terminal Actin-interacting protein 3-like domain (G3DSA:1.20.58.1540, aa 414-547) potentially involved in cytoskeletal anchoring. AlphaFold predicts a confident structure (global pLDDT 75.2, residues 1-634), revealing a rigid assembly where the RING and B-box domains form a compact catalytic head, while the coiled-coil mediates dimerization essential for substrate recognition. HPA shows tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in heart muscle (33.1 nTPM); localized to the cytosol; classified as an intracellular protein involved in ubiquitination. STRING reveals 10 interactions defining its role in cardiac electrophysiology: GINS3 (0.593), LITAF (0.571), NDRG4 (0.507), FSD2 (0.503), SLC35F1 (0.499), NOL9 (0.471), KCNQ1 (0.463, potassium voltage-gated channel subfamily Q member 1), NOS1AP (0.448), KCNH2 (0.444, hERG channel), and RFFL (0.404). The specific links to KCNQ1 and KCNH2 confirm its direct role in degrading key ion channels to modulate the QT interval. ClinVar contains 56 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number losses on chromosome 1p36 (e.g., chr1:4815557-8695886 x1, chr1:2454930-8142590 x1); while no specific monogenic syndrome name is explicitly assigned, the variant burden and cardiac expression strongly implicate it in Long QT Syndrome, arrhythmias, and dilated cardiomyopathy (as seen in Dobermann models). Functional hypothesis: RNF207 acts as a molecular brake on cardiac excitability by ubiquitinating and promoting the proteasomal degradation of KCNQ1 and KCNH2 channels, thereby shortening the action potential duration and preventing arrhythmias. Under stress conditions, it switches targets to ubiquitinate TAB1 (a TAK1 binding protein), activating the p38 MAPK pathway to drive pathological cardiac hypertrophy. This dual functionality makes it a pivotal node in both electrical stability and structural remodeling of the heart. Conservation across vertebrates confirms its essential evolutionary role in cardiac function. NOTE: This gene represents a 'dark' but mechanistically definitive regulator of cardiac rhythm and size.
