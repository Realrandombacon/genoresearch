## Description
APLF (Aprataxin and PNKP-like factor) is a 511 amino acid multifunctional DNA repair protein that acts as both a histone chaperone and scaffold factor in non-homologous end joining (NHEJ). The protein contains an N-terminal forkhead-associated (FHA) domain (PF17913, positions 17-64) that recognizes phosphorylated partners, and two tandem PBZ domains (PF10283, positions 376-401 and 418-442) that bind poly(ADP-ribose) chains generated by PARP1 at DNA break sites. AlphaFold prediction shows medium structural confidence (pLDDT 61.5), indicating a partially disordered architecture typical of DNA repair scaffolds that require conformational flexibility. HPA data reveals ubiquitous expression with low tissue specificity, nucleoplasmic localization, and immune cell enhancement consistent with DNA damage response functions. STRING interaction network identifies 10 high-confidence partners in DNA repair pathways: core NHEJ factors XRCC4 (0.999), XRCC5/Ku80 (0.999), XRCC6/Ku70 (0.992), DNA ligases LIG3 (0.969) and LIG4 (0.935), plus PARP1 (0.919) for damage sensing, aprataxin APTX (0.992) for end processing, chromatin regulator CHFR (0.979), and histone variant MACROH2A1 (0.908). ClinVar reports 9 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variants though specific disease phenotypes remain undefined. Literature (178 papers) establishes roles in: cisplatin resistance via interstrand crosslink repair and replication fork protection; breast cancer metastasis through nuclear localization; centrosome homeostasis in embryonic stem cells; and speciation through chromosomal fission events in zokors. APLF likely functions as a molecular bridge connecting PARP1-mediated damage detection to NHEJ machinery assembly, while simultaneously remodeling nucleosomes via histone chaperone activity to allow repair factor access to damaged chromatin.
