## Description
SMCO4 (Single-pass membrane protein with coiled-coil domains 4, C11orf75, FN5) is a compact 59 amino acid single-pass membrane protein (UniProt Q9NRQ5) with high-confidence well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 74.4) containing canonical membrane-associated architecture including Domain of Unknown Function 4519 (DUF4519) (IPR027960/PF15012, residues 1-59/1-55) forming a ~55-59 aa core module (~93-100%) defining SMCO4 classification with potential extracellular or cytosolic signaling activity, Single-pass membrane and coiled-coil domain-containing protein 4 family (PTHR34644, residues 1-59) encompassing the full length, and molecular functions including cell adhesion, axon guidance, membrane anchoring, tissue-enhanced expression (salivary gland 184.0 nTPM, detected in all tissues), low cancer/immune specificity, predicted membrane proteins classification, functioning as a small high-confidence well-folded single-pass membrane anchor that regulates cell-cell or cell-matrix interactions through DUF4519-mediated extracellular recognition or cytosolic signaling, oligomerization, and SMCO4 network assembly via partnerships with VSTM5, NEO1, C11orf54, ZNF195, PDZD11, FN1, DRAXIN, NTN1, BNIP3L, and NUDT6.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals SMCO4 is defined entirely by the DUF4519 domain, a conserved motif of unknown function found in single-pass membrane proteins. The fact that the entire 59 aa protein constitutes this domain suggests it is a minimal functional unit, likely forming a compact extracellular or cytosolic module attached to the membrane via a single transmembrane helix. The high-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 74.4) confirms a stable fold despite the small size, indicating strong evolutionary constraint and a specific functional role rather than a disordered linker.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places SMCO4 within a network enriched for cell adhesion and axon guidance factors: strongest interactions with VSTM5 (0.564, V-set and transmembrane domain containing 5, an immunoglobulin superfamily member) and NEO1 (0.519, Neogenin, a receptor for axon guidance cues). High confidence links also include FN1 (0.435, Fibronectin 1, a major extracellular matrix protein), DRAXIN (0.427, Dorsal inhibitory axon guidance protein), and NTN1 (0.413, Netrin 1, a classic axon guidance molecule). This cluster strongly suggests SMCO4 functions in neural development or tissue morphogenesis, potentially as a co-receptor or modulator of guidance signaling. Additional partners like C11orf54 (chromosome 11 neighbor), ZNF195 (zinc finger transcription factor), PDZD11 (PDZ domain protein), BNIP3L (apoptosis regulator), and NUDT6 (antisense regulator) suggest broader roles in transcriptional regulation and cell survival.\n\nHPA expression shows a tissue-enhanced pattern with exceptional levels in the salivary gland (184.0 nTPM), while being detected in all tissues. The salivary gland enrichment is intriguing and may reflect a role in epithelial organization or secretory vesicle trafficking. The ubiquitous detection suggests a fundamental role in membrane integrity or signaling across cell types.\n\nClinVar documents 15 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number losses and gains affecting the 11q13.4-q25 region: CNV losses (11q14.1-22.1, 11q14.1-22.3, 11q14.1-21 deletions) and CNV gains (11p13-q25, 11q13.4-23.3, 11p15.5-q25 duplications). The moderate variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity of the 11q locus. Haploinsufficiency likely disrupts axon guidance or cell adhesion leading to neurodevelopmental defects, while duplications may cause aberrant signaling or sequestration of guidance receptors, contributing to phenotypes associated with 11q deletion/duplication syndromes (Jacobsen syndrome features, developmental delay, craniofacial anomalies).\n\nConservation is strict across vertebrates: human (Q9NRQ5, 59 aa), mouse (Q9JIS3, 59 aa), cow (Q32PD9, 59 aa), xenopus (Q6GP51, 59 aa), zebra finch (B5FXC9, 59 aa), indicating strong evolutionary pressure to maintain the exact sequence and length.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: SMCO4 functions as a single-pass membrane co-receptor that modulates axon guidance signaling by interacting with NEO1 (Neogenin) and presenting extracellular cues like Netrin (NTN1) or Fibronectin (FN1). The compact DUF4519 domain likely forms a specific binding interface for these ligands or for VSTM5, creating a multi-protein guidance complex. In salivary gland, it may regulate epithelial cell polarity or acinar organization. Dysregulation via 11q CNVs disrupts this precise signaling: loss leads to misguided axons or defective adhesion (neurodevelopmental delay, craniofacial defects), while gain may cause hyper-sensitivity to guidance cues or dominant-negative interference, driving the pathogenesis of 11q syndromes.
