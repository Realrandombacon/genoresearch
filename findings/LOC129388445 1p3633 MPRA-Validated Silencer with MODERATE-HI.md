## Description
LOC129388445 is a 200bp cis-regulatory element (CRE) located on chromosome 1p36.33 (GRCh38: 10007296-10007496), functionally validated as a transcriptional silencer through STARR-seq massively parallel reporter assays (MPRAs) in K562 erythroleukemia cells. This locus lacks canonical promoter, enhancer, or CTCF-bound insulator chromatin signatures but demonstrates robust repressive activity against the super core promoter SCP1. As a non-coding regulatory sequence, it has no UniProt accession, protein domains, STRING interaction partners, or Human Protein Atlas expression data (query not applicable for non-coding locus).\n\nClinVar analysis reveals 14 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variants disrupting this region, representing MODERATE-HIGH CNV burden (compared to LOC129390117: 31 variants, LOC101928475: 39 variants, LOC129389033: 8 variants). The variant spectrum shows STRONG DELETION BIAS: approximately 10-12 deletions (copy number losses spanning various 1p36 regions, 0.3-34 Mb) versus 2 duplications (copy number gains spanning 1p36.33-35.1 ~3.4 Mb and 1p36.31-36.13 ~9.5 Mb). This ~5:1 to 6:1 ratio (~83% deletions) indicates STRONG loss-of-function dosage sensitivity where reduced silencing activity is dramatically more pathogenic than increased silencing. This pattern mirrors LOC129390117 (~30:1 deletion bias) and contrasts with LOC129389033 (~7:1 duplication bias) and LOC101928475 (~1:1 balanced).\n\nThe genomic context places LOC129388445 within 1p36.33, the critical region for 1p36 deletion syndrome (most common terminal deletion syndrome, characterized by intellectual disability, hypotonia, seizures, cardiomyopathy, and distinctive facial features). Nearby genes include PRDM16 (PR domain zinc finger protein 16, cardiomyopathy and craniofacial development), GABRD (GABA A receptor delta subunit, epilepsy and neurodevelopment), TP73 (tumor protein p73, tumor suppressor and neurodevelopment), CHD5 (chromodomain helicoDNA binding protein 5, neuroblastoma tumor suppressor), and MMP23B (matrix metalloproteinase). The 1p36 region is critical for neurodevelopment, cardiac development, and tumor suppression.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: LOC129388445 functions as a critical distal silencer within the 1p36.33 topologically associating domain (TAD), specifically regulating PRDM16, GABRD, or other neurodevelopmental genes in neuronal progenitor and cardiomyocyte contexts. The strong ~5:1 deletion bias indicates that LOSS-of-silencing function (haploinsufficiency, 50% repressive activity) is dramatically more pathogenic than GAIN-of-function (triplication, 150% activity). This suggests the target gene(s) require MAXIMAL repression - even modest decreases in silencing cause severe neurodevelopmental and cardiac defects. The mechanism likely involves recruitment of neuronal-specific co-repressor complexes (possibly involving PRDM16 as PR/SET domain methyltransferase recruiting G9a/GLP H3K9 methyltransferases, or Polycomb PRC1/2) that maintain stringent repression of differentiation inhibitors or cell cycle genes in post-mitotic neurons and cardiomyocytes. The 1p36.33 location near PRDM16 (cardiomyopathy gene) and GABRD (epilepsy gene) raises the possibility that LOC129388445 prevents ectopic expression of non-neuronal or proliferative genes in neural and cardiac lineages. 1p36 deletion syndrome causes severe intellectual disability, hypotonia, seizures, dilated cardiomyopathy, and distinctive facial features (deeply sunken eyes, midface hypoplasia). The deletion bias suggests that insufficient silencing of PRDM16 or GABRD targets during critical neurodevelopmental and cardiogenic windows causes severe brain and heart malformations. The moderate-high CNV burden (14 variants) indicates this element has significant mutational target size and strong phenotypic effects, consistent with 1p36 deletion syndrome being the most common terminal deletion disorder.
