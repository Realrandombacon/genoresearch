# ZNF517: KRAB-Zinc Finger Transcriptional Repressor with Ubiquitous Expression

**Date:** 2026-03-23T12:04:49.140785

**Quality Score:** 2.4/10  (E=4.5, D=0.3) [WEAK]

## Description
ZNF517 encodes a 492-amino acid C2H2 zinc finger transcription factor containing a canonical N-terminal KRAB repressor domain (PF01352, residues 14-54) and three tandem C2H2 zinc finger DNA-binding motifs (PF00096, residues 181-255). This domain architecture places ZNF517 within the largest family of human transcription factors, the KRAB-ZNF proteins, which function as sequence-specific transcriptional repressors. InterPro analysis confirms the KRAB domain superfamily (IPR001909, IPR036051) with GO annotation for regulation of DNA-templated transcription. HPA data shows ubiquitous expression across all tissues with nucleoplasmic subcellular localization, consistent with a housekeeping transcriptional regulator role. STRING interactions reveal 8 medium-confidence partners, most notably TRIM28/KAP1 (score 0.676), the canonical KRAB domain co-repressor that recruits heterochromatin machinery via HP1 and histone methyltransferases. Additional partners include CRIPAK (0.595), COMMD5 (0.540), and other zinc finger proteins (ZNF385B, ZNF133, ZNF292, TSHZ3), suggesting ZNF517 operates within multi-protein repression complexes. ClinVar documents 54 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly chromosome 8q copy number gains encompassing the ZNF517 locus, indicating strong dosage sensitivity and potential disease mechanisms through gene amplification. The combination of KRAB-mediated repression, TRIM28 interaction, and ubiquitous expression supports a hypothesis that ZNF517 functions as a broad-spectrum transcriptional silencer, potentially maintaining heterochromatin states at repetitive elements or regulating developmental gene networks. Disruption through copy number variation may cause disease via haploinsufficiency or dominant-negative effects on chromatin remodeling.
