## Description
EFCAB9 (EF-Hand Calcium Binding Domain 9, alias CATSPERZ) is a 197 amino acid intracellular calcium-binding protein (UniProt A8MZ26) with a very-high-confidence folded structure (AlphaFold pLDDT 94.0) containing paired EF-hand calcium-binding domains (residues 59-171), functioning as an auxiliary subunit of the CatSper (Cation Channel Sperm-Associated) complex that regulates sperm-specific calcium influx, hyperactivation, and motility through calcium-dependent conformational changes.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals EFCAB9 contains a canonical EF-hand calcium sensor architecture: paired EF-hand domains (IPR002048/PF13499/PS50222, residues 59-94/108-171/136-171) with the characteristic helix-loop-helix calcium-binding motif, belonging to the EF-hand domain pair superfamily (IPR011992/SSF47473/G3DSA:1.10.238.10, residues 21-165). The protein defines the EFCAB9 family (IPR042798/PTHR47065, residues 1-191) with calcium ion binding activity (GO:0005509). The very-high-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 94.0) confirms a stable EF-hand fold optimized for calcium coordination with pentagonal bipyramidal geometry.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals EFCAB9 as a core CatSper channel component with exceptional interaction scores: CATSPERZ (0.992, CatSper auxiliary subunit zeta - note: EFCAB9 IS CATSPERZ), CATSPER4 (0.970), CATSPERB (0.940), CATSPERG (0.924), CATSPER3 (0.856), CATSPER1 (0.856), CATSPERE (0.856), CATSPERD (0.856), CATSPER2 (0.856), and TMEM262 (0.856). These near-perfect interaction scores (0.856-0.992) indicate EFCAB9 is an integral stoichiometric subunit of the CatSper channel complex, not merely an interacting partner. The CatSper channel is a sperm-specific calcium channel essential for male fertility.\n\nHPA expression shows tissue-enriched specificity with testis-restricted expression (9.5 nTPM), detected in single tissue distribution. This confirms EFCAB9 functions exclusively in male germ cells. Protein class includes transporters, consistent with ion channel auxiliary subunit function. Not detected in cancer or immune cells indicates strict testis specificity.\n\nClinVar documents 14 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large chromosome 5q copy number gains and losses (5q33.1-35.2, 5q35.1-35.3, 5q32-35.3, 5q34-35.1, 5q35.1-35.3). The CNV burden indicates dosage sensitivity, where haploinsufficiency causes male infertility through defective CatSper channel assembly.\n\nConservation is strong across mammals: human (A8MZ26, 197 aa), mouse (Q9DAM2, 216 aa), indicating evolutionary conservation of sperm calcium regulation.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: EFCAB9 (CATSPERZ) functions as an essential auxiliary subunit of the CatSper calcium channel complex in sperm flagella. The paired EF-hand domains (residues 59-171) bind calcium ions with high affinity, undergoing conformational changes that modulate channel gating or trafficking. The near-perfect STRING interactions (0.856-0.992) with all CatSper pore-forming subunits (CATSPER1-4) and auxiliary subunits (CATSPERB/D/E/G) indicate EFCAB9 is a stoichiometric component of the ~20-subunit CatSper complex. Calcium binding to EFCAB9 may regulate channel open probability in response to intracellular calcium levels, creating feedback regulation of sperm hyperactivation. Testis-specific expression (9.5 nTPM) ensures CatSper function is restricted to mature sperm. 5q CNVs cause EFCAB9 haploinsufficiency, leading to defective CatSper assembly, impaired calcium influx, reduced sperm motility, and male infertility. EFCAB9 represents a critical CatSper auxiliary subunit linking calcium sensing to sperm motility regulation.
