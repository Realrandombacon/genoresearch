## Description
PRSS8 (UniProt Q16651) encodes prostasin, a 343 aa glycosylphosphatidylinositol (GPI)-anchored serine protease of the chymotrypsin family (peptidase S1) that functions as a key regulator of epithelial sodium transport and barrier function. InterPro analysis reveals a canonical trypsin-like serine protease domain architecture: Tryp_SPc domain (cd00190, residues 45-284), trypsin-like serine protease superfamily (G3DSA:2.40.10.10, residues 48-282), serine protease trypsin domain (IPR001254, residues 44-286, GO:0004252/GO:0006508), peptidase S1A chymotrypsin family (IPR001314, residues 71-243), PA clan peptidase S1 superfamily (IPR009003, residues 19-285), and critical active site motifs including the histidine active site (IPR018114/PS00134, residues 81-86) and serine active site (IPR033116/PS00135, residues 232-243) forming the catalytic triad essential for proteolytic activity. AlphaFold predicts a high confidence structure (pLDDT 83.8) consistent with a well-folded two-beta-barrel trypsin fold typical of secreted/membrane-associated serine proteases, though the GPI-anchor attachment site and pro-peptide regions may exhibit flexibility. STRING interactions define a specific functional network centered on sodium channel regulation and protease inhibition: SPINT1 (0.899, high confidence inhibitor HAI-1), FURIN (0.779, pro-protein convertase for activation), SCNN1A (0.729, ENaC alpha subunit direct substrate), RAB25 (0.614, vesicle trafficking), NEDD4L (0.609, E3 ligase for ENaC degradation), CLDN7 (0.586, tight junction protein), NEDD4 (0.579), ST14 (0.541, matriptase partner in protease cascade), SCNN1G (0.473, ENaC gamma subunit), and SPINT2 (0.420, inhibitor HAI-2). HPA classifies PRSS8 as tissue-enhanced with highest expression in salivary gland (231.3 nTPM), detected in many tissues including prostate (consistent with seminal fluid presence), kidney, and colon, localized as a predicted secreted and membrane protein, involved in hydrolase/protease activity, reflecting roles in fluid secretion, electrolyte balance, and epithelial integrity. ClinVar lists 15 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants predominantly consisting of large copy number losses (16p11.2 chr16:30907349-31334236x1, 30943854-31171177, 30851860-31156762x1, 30952806-31177641x1, 28826162-29043901x1) and gains (16p13.3-11.2 chr16:5805001-34230001, 16p13.3-q24.3 chr16:85881-90155062x3) plus one synonymous variant (c.78G>A p.Ser26=) though no single-gene disorder is definitively assigned suggesting contribution to 16p11.2 deletion/duplication syndromes which often feature obesity, developmental delay, and renal anomalies potentially linked to disrupted sodium handling. Functional hypothesis: PRSS8 operates through a tightly regulated proteolytic cascade: (1) Biosynthesis - Synthesized as inactive zymogen requiring furin-mediated cleavage for activation; (2) Membrane anchoring - Attached to outer leaflet of plasma membrane via GPI anchor concentrating activity at apical surface of epithelia; (3) Substrate cleavage - Directly cleaves and activates ENaC (SCNN1A/SCNN1G) increasing sodium reabsorption critical for blood pressure control and fluid balance; (4) Inhibitor control - Rapidly inhibited by SPINT1 (HAI-1) and SPINT2 (HAI-2) preventing excessive proteolysis and maintaining homeostasis; (5) Protease cascade - Functions in concert with ST14 (matriptase) where matriptase activates prostasin which then activates ENaC, forming a linear activation pathway essential for epidermal barrier formation and renal sodium conservation; (6) Disease mechanism - Loss-of-function leads to reduced ENaC activity causing salt-wasting, hypotension, and potentially skin barrier defects; gain-of-function causes hypertension via sodium retention; 16p11.2 CNVs disrupt this balance contributing to syndrome phenotypes including renal abnormalities and metabolic issues. Conservation extends to mouse (Q9ESD1, 342 aa) and rat (Q9ES87, 342 aa) indicating preserved role in mammalian epithelial physiology. Dark aspects requiring investigation include: precise structural basis of GPI-anchor interaction with lipid rafts, tissue-specific differences in activation efficiency (salivary vs renal vs colonic), role in cancer progression (prostate, ovarian) beyond simple expression changes, crosstalk between prostasin and other membrane proteases in viral entry (e.g., influenza, SARS-CoV-2), therapeutic potential of targeting the prostasin-ENaC axis in hypertension or cystic fibrosis, impact of specific 16p11.2 breakpoints on prostasin dosage versus neighboring genes, and potential non-proteolytic signaling functions of the cytoplasmic tail or shed ectodomain.
