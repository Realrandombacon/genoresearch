## Description
TANK encodes a 425 amino acid TRAF family member-associated NF-kappa-B activator (UniProt Q92844), serving as a critical scaffold protein that simultaneously regulates canonical NF-κB and type I interferon pathways. Despite extensive functional characterization, disease associations remain incompletely defined.\n\nSTRUCTURAL FEATURES: AlphaFold predicts medium confidence structure (pLDDT 59.5) across all 425 residues, consistent with a scaffold protein containing ordered domains connected by flexible linkers. InterPro analysis reveals modular architecture: N-terminal TRAF-binding region (IPR039669, PTHR15249, residues 1-425), central TBK1/IKKε binding domain (IPR024581, PF12845, TBD domain, residues 133-186) for kinase recruitment, and C-terminal zinc finger UBZ1-type domain (IPR041641, PS51905, residues 393-420) for ubiquitin chain recognition. This domain organization enables simultaneous binding to TRAF proteins and activated kinases.\n\nINTERACTION NETWORK: STRING-DB identifies 10 interaction partners with exceptionally high confidence (0.977-0.999), revealing exclusive association with NF-κB and interferon signaling complexes. TRAF adaptors: TRAF3 (0.999), TRAF6 (0.986), TRAF1 (0.979). Kinases: TBK1 (0.999), IKBKE/IKKε (0.999). NF-κB regulators: IKBKG/NEMO (0.999), TRADD (0.998). Receptors/ligands: TNF (0.996), TNFRSF1A (0.977). Scaffold homolog: TBKBP1 (0.996). This network confirms TANK's role as a hub connecting TRAF proteins to downstream kinase activation.\n\nEXPRESSION & LOCALIZATION: HPA confirms ubiquitous expression detected in all tissues with low tissue specificity and cell type-enhanced patterns. Subcellular localization includes nucleoli and cytosol, enabling regulation of both nuclear NF-κB transcription and cytoplasmic signaling cascades. Biological process annotations include DNA damage response and host-virus interaction.\n\nCLINICAL SIGNIFICANCE: ClinVar reports 23 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number losses (2q24.1-24.3, 2q24.2-31.1, 2q24.2-24.3) and gains (2p25.3-q37.3). While no specific monogenic diseases are yet annotated, the variant burden and functional importance suggest potential roles in immunodeficiency or autoinflammatory conditions.\n\nFUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: TANK functions as a bifunctional scaffold with dual regulatory roles: (1) NF-κB activation—TANK binds TRAF2/TRAF6 through its N-terminal region, recruiting IKK complex (IKBKG) to activate canonical NF-κB signaling downstream of TNF receptor and IL-1 receptor. (2) Interferon regulation—TANK binds TBK1/IKKε via its TBD domain (residues 133-186), facilitating IRF3/7 phosphorylation and type I interferon production during viral infection. (3) Ubiquitin sensing—C-terminal UBZ1 zinc finger (residues 393-420) recognizes K63-linked ubiquitin chains on TRAF proteins, stabilizing signaling complexes. (4) Negative regulation—TANK can also sequester TBK1 in inactive states, preventing spontaneous interferon activation. The medium-confidence AlphaFold structure suggests intrinsic flexibility critical for conformational switching between active/inactive states. Loss-of-function would impair both antibacterial (NF-κB) and antiviral (interferon) immunity; dysregulation could drive autoinflammation or immunodeficiency.
