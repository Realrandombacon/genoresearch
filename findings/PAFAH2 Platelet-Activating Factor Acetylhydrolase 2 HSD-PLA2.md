## Description
PAFAH2 encodes a 392 aa cytoplasmic enzyme (UniProt Q99487), also known as HSD-PLA2, that functions as a critical guardian against ferroptotic cell death and lipid peroxidation stress through hydrolysis of oxidized phospholipids and platelet-activating factor (PAF). The protein features a canonical alpha/beta hydrolase architecture: a core alpha/beta hydrolase fold (IPR029058/G3DSA:3.40.50.1820/SSF53474, residues 2-390) providing the catalytic scaffold; a specific platelet-activating factor acetylhydrolase domain (IPR016715/PF03403/PIRSF018169/PTHR10272, residues 1-387) defining substrate specificity for sn-2 acetyl group removal from PAF (1-O-alkyl-2-acetyl-sn-glyceryl-3-phosphorylcholine) and broader activity toward oxidized phosphatidylcholines; classified within eukaryotic PAF acetylhydrolase-like family with GO term 1-alkyl-2-acetylglycerophosphocholine esterase activity. STRING interactions reveal PAFAH2 operates within dense lipid metabolism and remodeling networks: PAFAH1B1 (0.983), PAFAH1B2 (0.944), PAFAH1B3 (0.940), forming the heterotrimeric cytosolic PAF-AH complex (LIS1 complex) involved in microtubule dynamics and neuronal migration; CEPT1 (0.923), choline/ethanolaminephosphotransferase 1 in phospholipid synthesis; PLA2G7 (0.831), plasma PAF acetylhydrolase (Lp-PLA2) representing secreted paralog; LPCAT2 (0.762), LPCAT4 (0.753), LPCAT1 (0.728), lysophosphatidylcholine acyltransferases driving Lands cycle remodeling; CHPT1 (0.690), cholinephosphotransferase 1; and PLA2G6 (0.670), calcium-independent phospholipase A2beta linked to neurodegeneration. HPA confirms tissue-enhanced expression with lymphoid tissue enrichment (45.4 nTPM), detected in all tissues, low cancer and immune cell specificity, classified as enzyme/metabolic protein/hydrolase/transferase involved in lipid degradation and metabolism. ClinVar lists 9 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including missense mutations affecting catalytic or structural residues (c.551A>G p.Gln184Arg, c.563G>A p.Arg188Gln, c.1084G>A p.Asp362Asn), large copy number gains (1p36.33-35.1 spanning 0.09-34Mb x3, 1p36.33-q44 spanning 0.8-249Mb x2/x3, 1p36.11-34.2 spanning 24-41Mb x3), and deletions/duplications, demonstrating dosage sensitivity though specific monogenic diseases remain undefined. AlphaFold structure prediction yields very high confidence (global pLDDT 91.1), revealing a well-folded alpha/beta hydrolase structure with properly positioned catalytic triad characteristic of active lipases. Mechanistically, PAFAH2 operates through integrated modes: (1) Ferroptosis surveillance—2024/2025 studies demonstrate PAFAH2 suppresses synchronized ferroptosis by hydrolyzing oxidized phospholipids (e.g., oxPC-aa) that accumulate during lipid peroxidation, preventing membrane rupture and cell death; (2) Acute kidney injury (AKI) protection—2024 study shows PAFAH2 ameliorates AKI by limiting ferroptotic tubular cell death via removal of toxic oxidized lipids; (3) Cancer resistance—2025 study reveals PAFAH2 mediates ferroptosis surveillance and lipid remodeling to promote resistance in KEAP1-mutant cancers, suggesting therapeutic target for restoring sensitivity; (4) Sepsis-induced vascular leakage—2026 study indicates PAFAH2 deficiency drives vascular leakage by promoting ECHS1-mediated suppression of protein crotonylation and mitochondrial dysfunction in endothelial cells; (5) Lipid remodeling—hydrolyzes sn-2 short-chain or oxidized acyl groups from phospholipids, facilitating repair of peroxidized membranes and maintaining membrane integrity; (6) Neuronal migration—interaction with PAFAH1B subunits suggests potential role in lissencephaly pathways though distinct from catalytic lipid function. Loss-of-function causes accumulation of oxidized phospholipids, uncontrolled ferroptosis, exacerbated acute kidney injury, increased sensitivity to oxidative stress in KEAP1-mutant contexts, and sepsis-associated vascular dysfunction.
