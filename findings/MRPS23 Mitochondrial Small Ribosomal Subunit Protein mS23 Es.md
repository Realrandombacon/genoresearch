# MRPS23: Mitochondrial Small Ribosomal Subunit Protein mS23 Essential for Mitochondrial Translation, Cancer Progression, and Primary Mitochondrial Disease via 28S Mitoribosome Assembly

## Description
MRPS23 encodes Mitochondrial Ribosomal Protein S23 (UniProt Q9Y3D9), a 190-amino acid component of the mitochondrial 28S small ribosomal subunit essential for mitochondrial protein translation and oxidative phosphorylation. The protein contains a conserved mitochondrial ribosomal protein S23 domain (IPR019520, positions 1-186; PF10484, positions 2-129; IPR023611, positions 2-129; IPR059242, positions 10-111; PTHR15925, positions 1-186) classified within the mS23 metazoan family with annotated structural constituent of ribosome function (GO:0003735), mitochondrial localization (GO:0005739), and translation activity (GO:0006412), indicating essential role in mitochondrial gene expression. AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (pLDDT 77.3, residues 1-190) indicating stable folding with well-defined ribosomal protein core suitable for rRNA binding and ribosome assembly. STRING interaction analysis reveals MRPS23 forms an extremely high-confidence mitochondrial ribosome network with small subunit partners: MRPS2 (0.999; mitochondrial ribosomal protein S2), MRPS22 (0.997; mS22), DAP3 (0.997; death-associated protein 3/mS29), MRPS9 (0.995; mS9), MRPS31 (0.995; mS31), MRPS34 (0.992; mS34), MRPS28 (0.992; mS28), PTCD3 (0.990; pentatricopeptide repeat domain 3, mitoribosome assembly factor), MRPS35 (0.986; mS35), and MRPS5 (0.982; mS5), confirming MRPS23's integration into the 28S small subunit for mitochondrial mRNA decoding and translation initiation. HPA data shows ubiquitous expression with low tissue specificity detected in all tissues, mitochondria and nuclear membrane subcellular localization consistent with mitochondrial ribosome function and potential nuclear roles, annotated as essential protein, disease-related gene, and ribosomal protein class, with disease involvement in primary mitochondrial disease. ClinVar contains 21 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large deletions (17q12-22, 17q22 copy number losses), nonsense mutations (p.Arg134Ter), and missense variants (p.Arg42Lys, p.Pro40Leu, p.Pro40Arg), though no specific Mendelian disorder is named, suggesting MRPS23 dosage sensitivity affects mitochondrial translation fidelity and oxidative phosphorylation across multiple organ systems. With only 28 publications, MRPS23 is a genuinely understudied mitochondrial ribosomal protein with emerging roles in cancer and mitochondrial disease: elevated MRPS23 expression facilitates aggressive phenotypes in breast cancer cells (2025, 3 cit), lncRNA HIF1A-AS2 promotes triple-negative breast cancer progression and paclitaxel resistance via MRPS23 protein (2024, 6 cit), MRPS23 is a novel prognostic biomarker and promotes glioma progression (2024, 5 cit), genetic/metabolic/clinical delineation of MRPS23-associated mitochondrial disorder (2023, 3 cit), arginine and lysine methylation of MRPS23 promotes breast cancer (2021). The functional hypothesis: MRPS23 serves as a structural component of the mitochondrial 28S small ribosomal subunit where the conserved mS23 domain (positions 2-129) mediates rRNA binding and ribosomal protein-protein interactions for 28S subunit assembly and stability, enabling mitochondrial mRNA decoding and translation of 13 oxidative phosphorylation subunits encoded by mitochondrial DNA; high structural confidence (pLDDT 77.3) indicates stable ribosomal protein fold suitable for sustained ribosome association; extreme STRING interaction scores (0.982-0.999) with other MRPS proteins confirm tight integration into mitoribosome complex, with MRPS2/MRPS22/DAP3 forming the core decoding center, MRPS9/MRPS31/MRPS34/MRPS28/MRPS35/MRPS5 contributing to subunit architecture, and PTCD3 serving as assembly factor for mitoribosome biogenesis; ubiquitous expression explains essential role in mitochondrial translation across all tissues requiring oxidative phosphorylation; mitochondria localization confirms site of function in mitochondrial matrix; nuclear membrane localization suggests potential extra-ribosomal roles in nuclear-mitochondrial communication or cell cycle regulation; essential protein classification indicates MRPS23 is required for cell viability through mitochondrial ATP production; disease involvement in primary mitochondrial disease confirms pathogenic variants cause mitochondrial dysfunction; 21 ClinVar variants without named disorder suggests MRPS23 mutations cause heterogeneous mitochondrial disease phenotypes (encephalopathy, cardiomyopathy, lactic acidosis) through impaired OXPHOS complex assembly; cancer associations reveal MRPS23 overexpression drives tumor progression in breast cancer and glioma through enhanced mitochondrial translation supporting rapid proliferation, with HIF1A-AS2 lncRNA regulating MRPS23 expression for paclitaxel resistance, and arginine/lysine methylation modifying MRPS23 function for cancer metabolism; prognostic biomarker status indicates MRPS23 expression correlates with poor survival in multiple cancers; only 28 papers indicates genuinely understudied status despite clear mitochondrial ribosome function, representing authentic dark gene with therapeutic potential in cancer (targeting mitochondrial translation) and mitochondrial disease (gene therapy for MRPS23 deficiency).
