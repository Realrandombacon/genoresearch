# LDLRAD4: TGF-β Signaling Inhibitor with LDL Receptor Domain and SMAD Interaction Network, Acting as a Tumor Suppressor in Gastrointestinal and Hematologic Malignancies

**Date:** 2026-04-24T12:34:07.273130

**Quality Score:** 3.85/10  (E=6.7, D=1.0) [MODERATE]

**Confidence:** MEDIUM

## Description
LDLRAD4 (Low-Density Lipoprotein Receptor Class A Domain Containing 4, alias PMEPA1-like) is a 306-amino acid transmembrane protein functioning as a negative regulator of the TGF-β signaling pathway. InterPro analysis confirms the presence of a canonical Low-Density Lipoprotein Receptor Class A domain (LDLa, IPR002172/PF00057, residues 14-49) containing the conserved calcium-binding signature (PS01209, residues 24-47), alongside a TMEPAI family domain (IPR043445, residues 2-304) and a helical domain (PF27646, residues 61-94). The LDLa domain suggests potential ligand binding or protein-protein interaction capabilities, while the TMEPAI homology indicates a role in sequestering R-SMADs. AlphaFold predicts a medium-confidence structure (pLDDT 58.7), consistent with a protein containing structured domains embedded in flexible regions, typical for signaling regulators that require conformational adaptability.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places LDLRAD4 within a TGF-β regulatory network, showing high confidence interactions with TSC22D1 (0.720, a transcriptional repressor), SMURF1 (0.678) and SMURF2 (0.655, E3 ubiquitin ligases targeting SMADs for degradation), and direct interaction with SMAD2 (0.421). This profile strongly supports a mechanism where LDLRAD4 recruits SMURF ligases to ubiquitinate and degrade activated SMAD2/3, thereby attenuating TGF-β signal transduction. Additional partners include AR (Androgen Receptor, 0.539), suggesting crosstalk with hormonal signaling pathways.\n\nHuman Protein Atlas classifies LDLRAD4 as 'tissue enhanced' with highest expression in brain (56.2 nTPM) and lymphoid tissue (42.1 nTPM). It is localized to vesicles, consistent with its role in endosomal sorting of signaling receptors. The gene is expressed in many tissues but shows low cancer specificity overall, though literature indicates context-specific downregulation in tumors.\n\nClinVar reports 84 pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains and losses spanning chromosome 18p11.32-11.21 (coordinates: chr18:136227-15181209), indicating dosage sensitivity. While no specific monogenic syndrome is named, the burden of CNVs suggests that disruption of LDLRAD4 contributes to developmental disorders or cancer susceptibility associated with 18p deletions/duplications.\n\nLiterature analysis (27 papers) establishes LDLRAD4 as a tumor suppressor frequently downregulated in colorectal cancer (CRC) and gastrointestinal stromal tumors (GIST). A pivotal 2020 study demonstrated that the antisense lncRNA LDLRAD4-AS1 promotes metastasis by decreasing LDLRAD4 expression, correlating with poor prognosis in CRC. In GISTs, LDLRAD4 serves as a prognostic indicator, with lower expression predicting aggressive disease. In myelodysplastic syndromes (MDS), LDLRAD4 correlates with immune infiltration and serves as a diagnostic biomarker. Mechanistically, LDLRAD4 downregulation leads to hyperactive TGF-β signaling, promoting epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) and cell proliferation.\n\nWe hypothesize that LDLRAD4 functions as a critical brake on TGF-β signaling in epithelial and hematopoietic tissues. Upon TGF-β stimulation, LDLRAD4 is induced (potentially via SMAD binding to its promoter) and localizes to vesicles where it binds phosphorylated SMAD2/3 via its cytoplasmic tail. It then recruits SMURF1/2 E3 ligases to polyubiquitinate SMADs, targeting them for proteasomal degradation. This negative feedback loop prevents excessive TGF-β responses that could drive fibrosis or tumorigenesis. In cancer, epigenetic silencing or lncRNA-mediated suppression of LDLRAD4 disrupts this brake, allowing constitutive TGF-β signaling to drive invasion and metastasis. The LDLa domain may also mediate interactions with extracellular matrix components or lipoproteins, linking metabolic status to growth factor signaling. LDLRAD4 represents a promising therapeutic target for restoring TGF-β homeostasis in cancers and a biomarker for 18p-associated genomic disorders.
