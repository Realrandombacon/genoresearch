## Description
ZNF66 (Gene ID: 7546, UniProt Q6ZN08) is a 573 amino acid Krüppel-associated box (KRAB) domain-containing C2H2 zinc finger protein located on chromosome 19p13.12 (NC_000019.10). This gene encodes a classical two-module transcriptional repressor architecture: N-terminal KRAB repression domain coupled to C-terminal DNA-binding zinc finger arrays. InterPro domain analysis reveals canonical KRAB-ZFP organization: KRAB box domain (IPR001909/PF01352, positions 4-75, ~72 residues forming transcriptional repression module), KRAB-A box subdomain (cd07765, positions 6-43), KRAB superfamily (IPR036051/G3DSA:3.30.160.60, positions 2-60), and multiple C2H2-type zinc finger domains (IPR013087/PF00096/PS00028/PS50157 at positions 145-172, 171-200, 201-228, 237-294, 293-350 and beyond—spanning C-terminal ~400 residues with 8-12 tandem zinc fingers). The KRAB domain recruits KAP1/TRIM28 corepressor complexes that recruit heterochromatin machinery (HP1, SETDB1 histone methyltransferase) for transcriptional silencing. C2H2 zinc fingers recognize specific DNA sequences through modular base contacts, with each finger typically recognizing 3-4 bp. STRING interaction network identifies 1 medium-confidence partner: RFX8 (0.415, regulatory factor X8, winged-helix transcription factor involved in ciliogenesis and immune regulation). The RFX8 interaction suggests potential cooperative DNA binding or shared target gene regulation, possibly at promoters/enhancers containing both ZNF66 and RFX recognition elements. Human Protein Atlas has no expression data for ZNF66 (ENSG00000275050 not profiled), limiting tissue specificity assessment. AlphaFold structure prediction (AF-Q6ZN08-F1) yields HIGH-confidence model (global pLDDT 73.6), indicating a well-folded stable architecture across all 573 residues—the KRAB domain likely forms an α-helical bundle while zinc fingers form compact ββα folds stabilized by Zn²⁺ coordination. High confidence reflects strong evolutionary constraint on both repression and DNA-binding modules. ClinVar lists 4 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all large chromosomal copy number gains spanning chromosome 19p-q regions (chr19:15133594-24193591x3, chr19:11227942-44626354x3, chr19:13974677-27839676x3, chr19:17176767-34924150x3); no specific monogenic disease is attributed solely to ZNF66, suggesting dosage sensitivity within larger chromosomal syndromes. Conservation is evidenced by orthologs across primates and mammals (Chelydra serpentina Q4G0J2 homolog exists), though detailed % identity requires BLAST analysis. Functional hypothesis: ZNF66 functions as a sequence-specific transcriptional repressor regulating developmentally important genes. The N-terminal KRAB domain (residues 4-75) recruits KAP1/TRIM28 corepressor complex, which recruits SETDB1 (H3K9me3 methyltransferase), HP1 heterochromatin proteins, and NuRD complex for chromatin compaction and transcriptional silencing. The C-terminal zinc finger array (residues ~140-573) recognizes specific DNA sequences through combinatorial zinc finger code—each finger contributes 3-4 bp specificity, enabling recognition of 24-48 bp target sites. RFX8 interaction suggests cooperative regulation at shared target genes, potentially linking ZNF66 to ciliogenesis or immune gene programs. High-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 73.6) enables precise structural modeling—the extended zinc finger array likely wraps around DNA major groove. Disease mechanism: ZNF66 overexpression from chromosome 19p CNVs may contribute to developmental disorders through aberrant transcriptional repression. The 4 ClinVar CNVs suggest dosage sensitivity; 19p deletions/duplications cause diverse phenotypes including intellectual disability and growth abnormalities. ZNF66 may contribute through ectopic silencing of target genes (developmental regulators, cell cycle controllers) or disruption of RFX8-mediated programs. Priority experiments include ChIP-seq to identify genomic binding sites, co-IP validation of KAP1/TRIM28 and RFX8 interactions, reporter assays testing repression activity, CRISPR knockout to assess phenotypic consequences, structural analysis leveraging AlphaFold model (pLDDT 73.6) to map DNA-binding interface, and analysis of ZNF66 expression in 19p syndrome patient cells.
