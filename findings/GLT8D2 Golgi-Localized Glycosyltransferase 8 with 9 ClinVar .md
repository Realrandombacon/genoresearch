# GLT8D2: Golgi-Localized Glycosyltransferase 8 with 9 ClinVar Variants, ECM Protein Interactions, and PD-L1-Mediated Immune Evasion in Cancer

## Description
GLT8D2 (Glycosyltransferase 8 Domain-Containing Protein 2; UniProt Q9H1C3) is a 349 amino acid Golgi-resident enzyme with only 17 publications, classifying it as a genuine dark gene despite emerging roles in cancer immune evasion and extracellular matrix remodeling.\n\nSTRUCTURAL ARCHITECTURE: InterPro analysis identifies GLT8D2 as a canonical glycosyltransferase family 8 member (IPR002495/PF01501, residues 53-322) within the nucleotide-diphospho-sugar transferase superfamily (IPR029044/SSF53448, residues 51-345). The protein belongs to the GT8 domain-containing family (IPR050748/PTHR13778, residues 14-339). AlphaFold predicts high structural confidence (global pLDDT 87.9), indicating a well-folded catalytic domain typical of processive glycosyltransferases that modify proteins or lipids in the secretory pathway.\n\nINTERACTION NETWORK: STRING-DB reveals medium-confidence interactions predominantly with extracellular matrix and membrane-associated proteins: FIBIN (0.534, fibronectin-type III domain protein), ECM2 (0.530, extracellular matrix protein 2), TMEM248 (0.479, transmembrane protein), LCN12 (0.460, lipocalin-12), DCN (0.457, decorin proteoglycan), SUSD5 (0.452, sushi domain-containing protein), ZNF469 (0.439, zinc finger associated with Ehlers-Danlos syndrome), OSBPL8 (0.436, oxysterol-binding protein), ARHGAP20 (0.435, RhoGAP), and VKORC1L1 (0.430, vitamin K epoxide reductase). This interaction profile suggests GLT8D2 modifies ECM components or cell surface receptors involved in tissue architecture and signaling.\n\nEXPRESSION & LOCALIZATION: HPA shows expression detected in many tissues with low tissue specificity, consistent with housekeeping glycosylation functions. Subcellular localization is restricted to the Golgi apparatus and vesicles—the expected compartment for glycosyltransferases that modify secretory pathway cargo. Classified as a metabolic protein.\n\nDISEASE LANDSCAPE: ClinVar contains 9 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all large copy number gains/losses spanning 12q23-24 that encompass GLT8D2. No monogenic disease is yet defined, but 12q24 deletions are associated with developmental anomalies. Recent 2024-2026 literature establishes GLT8D2 as an oncogenic driver: it mediates PD-L1 N-glycosylation to promote tumor immune evasion in ovarian cancer peritoneal metastasis (2026), serves as a prognostic biomarker correlated with tumor immunity in gastric cancer (2023-2024), and was identified via WGCNA as upregulated in pulmonary artery hypertension alongside ECM2 (2021).\n\nCONSERVATION: Orthologs confirmed in mouse (Q640P4, 349 aa), cattle (Q2HJ96, 350 aa), macaque (Q4R3U7, 349 aa), and trout (A0A673VTN9, 368 aa), indicating strong evolutionary conservation of the GT8 catalytic domain.\n\nFUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: GLT8D2 likely functions as a Golgi-resident glycosyltransferase that modifies specific acceptor substrates—potentially ECM proteins (decorin, ECM2, FIBIN) or immune checkpoint receptors (PD-L1). By adding carbohydrate moieties, GLT8D2 controls protein stability, trafficking, or ligand binding affinity. The PD-L1 glycosylation role suggests it stabilizes this immune checkpoint protein, enabling cancer cells to evade T-cell surveillance. The ECM protein interactions hint at additional functions in tissue remodeling, consistent with pulmonary hypertension associations. The 12q24 CNVs without defined phenotypes warrant investigation for haploinsufficiency effects on development or cancer susceptibility.
