## Description
LY6G6D (Lymphocyte Antigen 6 Complex Locus Protein G6D) is a 133-amino acid GPI-anchored cell surface protein belonging to the Ly-6/u-PAR superfamily, characterized by a conserved three-finger toxin-like fold formed by the Ly-6 domain (residues 21-105). InterPro analysis confirms membership in the Ly-6 antigen/uPA receptor-like family (IPR016054, PF00021) and the LY6G6D/G6F subfamily (IPR026524, PTHR32286), with the extracellular domain (cd23547) spanning residues 20-105. AlphaFold predicts a medium-confidence structure (pLDDT 66.2), consistent with the characteristic Ly-6 fold but suggesting potential flexibility in loop regions that may mediate ligand interactions.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals LY6G6D operates within a tightly clustered Ly-6 family network, with highest confidence for LY6G6F (0.866), followed by LY6G6E (0.595), LYPD5 (0.593), LYPD4 (0.561), and LY6H (0.543). This pattern suggests functional redundancy or co-regulation within the Ly-6 cluster at 6p21.33, potentially forming heteromeric complexes on the cell surface. Notably, TEX101 (0.516) and SPACA4 (0.479) interactions suggest possible roles in reproductive biology, as these are testis-specific Ly-6 proteins. ClinVar reports 8 pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants, all large copy number gains or losses spanning 6p21.33-q27, indicating dosage sensitivity but no monogenic disease phenotype yet defined. Human Protein Atlas has no expression data, reflecting limited antibody-based characterization.\n\nLiterature analysis (41 papers) reveals a striking disconnect: while basic mechanistic understanding remains limited (no defined endogenous ligand, unclear signaling pathway), LY6G6D has rapidly emerged as a promising immunotherapy target for microsatellite stable (MSS) colorectal cancer. Multiple 2025 abstracts describe LY6G6D-targeted T-cell engagers (ATG-110), LY6G6D/4-1BB bispecific antibodies (HEC921), and LY6G6D x CD3 bispecifics showing potent tumor killing with minimal cytokine release. We hypothesize that LY6G6D functions as a context-dependent cell surface marker whose expression is restricted to specific epithelial tissues and upregulated in MSS colorectal cancer, making it an ideal tumor-associated antigen. The Ly-6 domain's three-finger fold typically mediates protein-protein interactions with receptors or ligands, suggesting LY6G6D may modulate cell adhesion, proliferation, or immune recognition. The GPI anchor localizes it to lipid rafts, potentially enabling co-clustering with signaling receptors. The lack of defined endogenous function despite therapeutic development represents a critical knowledge gap: understanding LY6G6D's physiological role may reveal resistance mechanisms or combination therapy opportunities. The 6p21.33 location within the MHC region suggests potential co-regulation with immune genes, possibly explaining its immunogenicity in cancer.
