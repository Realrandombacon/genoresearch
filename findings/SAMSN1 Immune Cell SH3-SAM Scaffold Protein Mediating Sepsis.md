# SAMSN1: Immune Cell SH3-SAM Scaffold Protein Mediating Sepsis Immunosuppression and NK Cell Tumor Surveillance via KEAP1-NRF2 and CRK/CRKL Signaling

## Description
SAMSN1 (SAM Domain, SH3 Domain and Nuclear Localization Signals 1, alias SH3P7) is a 373-amino acid intracellular scaffold protein predominantly expressed in hematopoietic lineages, functioning as a critical regulator of immune cell activation and checkpoint signaling. InterPro analysis reveals a modular domain architecture: an N-terminal SLy proteins associated disordered region (IPR021090/PF12485, residues 17-164) suggesting intrinsically disordered protein interaction capacity, a variant SH3 domain (IPR001452/PF07653, residues 163-224) for proline-rich motif binding, and a C-terminal SAM domain (IPR001660/PF07647, residues 238-305) mediating protein-protein interactions and potential polymerization. AlphaFold predicts medium-confidence structure (pLDDT 63.3), consistent with ordered SH3/SAM domains flanked by disordered regions typical of signaling scaffolds.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis positions SAMSN1 within immune signaling networks, with partners including CRKL (0.636) and CRK (0.563), adaptor proteins in tyrosine kinase signaling; BCL2A1 (0.558), an anti-apoptotic BCL-2 family member; CD53 (0.518), a tetraspanin leukocyte marker; LCP2 (0.415), the SLP-76 adaptor in T-cell receptor signaling; and CRTAM (0.570), a cytotoxic T-cell activation marker. This network suggests SAMSN1 functions at the intersection of activation, survival, and checkpoint pathways.\n\nHuman Protein Atlas classifies SAMSN1 as 'tissue enhanced' with highest expression in bone marrow (182.2 nTPM) and lymphoid tissue (75.6 nTPM), localized to nucleoplasm and plasma membrane, and 'immune cell enhanced' in blood cells. ClinVar reports 70 pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains and losses spanning 21q11.2-22.3, indicating dosage sensitivity though no monogenic disease phenotype is yet defined.\n\nLiterature analysis (64 papers) reveals emerging mechanistic understanding: 2024-2025 studies demonstrate SAMSN1 causes sepsis immunosuppression by inducing macrophages to express coinhibitory molecules (PD-L1, etc.) that cause T-cell exhaustion via KEAP1-NRF2 signaling, protects against sepsis-induced acute lung injury, and restrains NK cell-mediated anti-tumor immunity in hepatocellular carcinoma. We hypothesize that SAMSN1 functions as a context-dependent immune checkpoint scaffold where the SH3 domain recruits proline-rich signaling effectors (potentially CRK/CRKL adaptors) while the SAM domain mediates oligomerization or membrane recruitment. In sepsis, SAMSN1 upregulation in macrophages activates KEAP1-NRF2 antioxidant signaling, paradoxically inducing immunosuppressive coinhibitory ligands that exhaust T-cells. In cancer, SAMSN1 in NK cells or tumor-associated macrophages dampens cytotoxic responses. The disordered N-terminal region (residues 17-164) may serve as a tunable interaction hub responsive to post-translational modifications. SAMSN1 represents a therapeutically targetable immune modulator where inhibition could reverse sepsis immunosuppression or enhance NK cell tumor surveillance.
