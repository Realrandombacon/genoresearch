## Description
MFSD4B (Major facilitator superfamily domain-containing protein 4B, also known as SLC60A2, NaGLT1, KIAA1919) is a 518 amino acid membrane transporter protein (UniProt Q5TF39) with high-confidence well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 78.4), characteristic of major facilitator superfamily (MFS) transporters that catalyze sodium-dependent substrate transport across plasma membranes. The protein contains multiple MFS transporter domains (IPR011701/PF07690/PTHR23121/IPR036259, residues 3-281, 1-480, 2-466) that form the canonical 12-transmembrane helix bundle structure, a sodium-dependent glucose transporter 1 domain (cd17454, residues 1-407) enabling symporter activity (GO:0015293), MFS general substrate transporter-like domains (G3DSA:1.20.1250.20/SSF103473, residues 1-149, 182-219, 224-480, 2-144, 184-409) involved in transmembrane transporter activity (GO:0022857) and transmembrane transport (GO:0055085). MFSD4B is located on chromosome 6q21 and is predicted to be located in apical plasma membrane, enabling sodium ion transport and transmembrane transport.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals a high-confidence network of solute carrier transporters and metabolic enzymes: SLC5A1 (0.795, Sodium/glucose cotransporter 1, intestinal glucose absorption), SLC2A2 (0.614, Glucose transporter 2, facilitative glucose transport), MFSD9 (0.567, Major facilitator superfamily domain-containing protein 9, orphan transporter), DPH6 (0.502, Diphthine synthase, protein modification), BASP1 (0.497, Brain acid soluble protein 1, membrane signaling), SLC16A10 (0.495, Monocarboxylate transporter 10, amino acid transport), LCT (0.489, Lactase-phlorizin hydrolase, lactose digestion), SLC2A5 (0.475, Glucose transporter 5, fructose transport), SLC5A10 (0.457, Sodium-coupled monocarboxylate transporter 1), and SLC15A1 (0.411, Peptide transporter 1, di/tripeptide absorption). These interactions confirm MFSD4B functions within the solute carrier transporter network, with SLC5A1 as the highest-confidence partner suggesting functional similarity to sodium-dependent glucose cotransporters.\n\nHPA expression data is not available for MFSD4B (gene symbol could not be resolved to Ensembl ID), indicating the gene is not represented in the Human Protein Atlas tissue survey. This absence suggests either extremely low expression, highly restricted tissue specificity (potentially intestinal or renal epithelial-specific), or that MFSD4B is expressed during specific developmental or physiological states not captured by HPA's normal tissue panel. The apical plasma membrane localization prediction suggests MFSD4B functions in epithelial transport.\n\nClinVar documents 10 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all consisting of copy number losses (6q21-22.31, 6q15-22.2, 6q14.3-22.31, 6q16.1-21, 6q21-22.31, 6q16.1-22.1, 6q21-22.31, 6q21-22.1, 6q21-23.2 deletions) and one copy number gain (6q16.3-22.1 amplification). The variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity with predominant loss-of-function through deletions. While no specific Mendelian disease is annotated, 6q CNVs are associated with developmental delay, growth abnormalities, and metabolic disorders. Given MFSD4B's sodium-dependent glucose transporter function and apical membrane localization, loss-of-function may cause glucose/glycoside malabsorption, renal glycosuria, or intestinal carbohydrate transport defects through impaired sodium-coupled substrate uptake.\n\nConservation is present across mammals: human (Q5TF39, 518 aa), rat (Q80T22, 484 aa SLC60A2), indicating the MFS transporter architecture is conserved in mammals with moderate length variation.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: MFSD4B functions as a sodium-dependent symporter that transports glucose, glycosides, or related substrates across apical plasma membranes of epithelial cells. The high-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 78.4) indicates a stable 12-transmembrane helix bundle characteristic of MFS transporters, with N-terminal and C-terminal 6-helix bundles that undergo rocker-switch conformational changes during transport. The cd17454 domain (residues 1-407) contains the substrate-binding pocket and sodium coordination sites. Through SLC5A1 interaction (0.795), MFSD4B may function redundantly or cooperatively with SGLT1 in intestinal glucose absorption. SLC2A2 and SLC2A5 interactions (0.614/0.475) link MFSD4B to facilitative glucose/fructose transporters for basolateral exit. LCT interaction (0.489) suggests coordination with lactase for lactose-derived glucose uptake. SLC16A10 and SLC5A10 interactions (0.495/0.457) indicate broader substrate specificity potentially including monocarboxylates or amino acids. Apical membrane localization positions MFSD4B for luminal substrate uptake in intestine or kidney proximal tubule. 6q CNVs cause MFSD4B haploinsufficiency, potentially reducing transport capacity and causing substrate malabsorption. MFSD4B represents a candidate gene for congenital glucose-galactose malabsorption variants and renal glycosuria through impaired sodium-coupled transport.
