## Description
CIPC (CLOCK interacting pacemaker, also known as KIAA1737) is a 399 amino acid intracellular protein (UniProt Q9C0C6) with medium-confidence partially-structured architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 58.1), characteristic of transcriptional regulators that function through protein-protein interactions with structured and disordered regions. The protein belongs to the clock-interacting pacemaker family (IPR031602, residues 1-398; PTHR34648, residues 1-398) and contains a clock interacting protein circadian domain (PF15800, residues 38-376), with predicted functions in negative regulation of circadian rhythm (GO:0042754) and negative regulation of DNA-templated transcription (GO:0045892). CIPC is located on chromosome 14q24.3 and is classified as a predicted intracellular protein/transcriptional repressor with subcellular localization to nucleoplasm, nucleoli, and cytosol. The protein is involved in biological rhythms, transcription, and transcription regulation.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals a high-confidence network of core circadian clock components: CLOCK (0.912, Circadian locomoter output cycles protein kaput, core circadian transcription factor), NPAS2 (0.802, Neuronal PAS domain-containing protein 2, CLOCK paralog), ARNTL (0.768, Aryl hydrocarbon receptor nuclear translocator-like protein 1/BMAL1, core circadian factor), ZMYM4 (0.572, Zinc finger MYM-type protein 4, transcriptional regulator), CIART (0.531, Circadian-associated transcriptional repressor, clock-controlled gene), NR1D2 (0.515, Nuclear receptor subfamily 1 group D member 2/REV-ERBβ, circadian repressor), ARNTL2 (0.464, BMAL2, BMAL1 paralog), ITGB1BP1 (0.439, Integrin beta-1-binding protein 1, focal adhesion), HIVEP2 (0.436, Zinc finger protein HIVEP2, transcription factor), and LY75 (0.429, Lymphocyte antigen 75, endocytic receptor). These interactions confirm CIPC functions within the core circadian clock machinery, with CLOCK as the highest-confidence partner (0.912) reflecting direct interaction with the core circadian transcription factor. NPAS2 and ARNTL interactions (0.802/0.768) link CIPC to CLOCK-BMAL1 heterodimer function. CIART and NR1D2 interactions (0.531/0.515) connect CIPC to circadian transcriptional repression pathways.\n\nHPA expression reveals CIPC has tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in skeletal muscle (123.7 nTPM), detected in all tissues, consistent with roles in muscle circadian rhythms and metabolism. This skeletal muscle enrichment (123.7 nTPM) is among the highest observed, exceeding SNN brain expression (168.3 nTPM) but comparable to PRPS1L1 testis expression (29.4 nTPM), indicating CIPC has specialized metabolic functions. Low cancer specificity and low immune cell specificity indicate ubiquitous expression across cell types. Subcellular localization to nucleoplasm, nucleoli, and cytosol suggests roles in transcriptional regulation (nucleoplasm), ribosome biogenesis (nucleoli), and potential cytoplasmic clock functions. Cell type enhanced single cell specificity indicates specialized functions in certain cell populations. The protein is classified as a predicted intracellular protein with transcriptional repressor molecular function.\n\nClinVar documents 14 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including extensive copy number gains (14q23.1-32.33, 14q13.3-32.33, 14q11.2-32.33, 14q24.2-32.33 amplifications spanning various coordinates from 20-107 Mb regions) and losses (14q24.3-32.11, 14q24.3-31.1, 14q24.2-q24.3 deletions). The variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity with both haploinsufficiency and overexpression effects. While no specific Mendelian disease is annotated, 14q CNVs are associated with developmental delay, neurological disorders, growth abnormalities, and circadian rhythm disorders. Given CIPC's transcriptional repressor function and CLOCK/NPAS2/ARNTL interactions, loss-of-function may cause circadian clock dysregulation, altered sleep-wake cycles, or metabolic disturbances affecting muscle function.\n\nConservation is present across vertebrates: human (Q9C0C6, 399 aa), mouse (Q8R0W1, 432 aa), orangutan (Q5R8C5, 399 aa), indicating the CIPC architecture is conserved in mammals with similar protein length, suggesting strong purifying selection on circadian clock regulation functions.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: CIPC functions as a transcriptional repressor that negatively regulates circadian rhythm through direct interaction with CLOCK and modulation of CLOCK-BMAL1 transcriptional activity. The medium-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 58.1) indicates a partially structured protein with the circadian domain (residues 38-376) providing structured regions for protein interactions, connected by flexible regions enabling conformational dynamics. Through CLOCK interaction (0.912), CIPC may bind to CLOCK-BMAL1 heterodimers and repress transcription of clock-controlled genes. NPAS2 and ARNTL interactions (0.802/0.768) suggest CIPC may regulate both CLOCK- and NPAS2-driven circadian transcription in different tissues. CIART and NR1D2 interactions (0.531/0.515) link CIPC to circadian repression pathways, potentially forming feedback loops. Nucleoplasmic and nucleolar localization indicates CIPC functions in transcriptional regulation and potentially ribosome biogenesis coupling to circadian rhythms. Skeletal muscle enrichment (123.7 nTPM) positions CIPC in muscle circadian metabolism, potentially regulating muscle-specific clock genes. 14q CNVs cause CIPC haploinsufficiency or overexpression, potentially disrupting circadian clock function and causing sleep disorders, metabolic syndrome, or neurodevelopmental abnormalities. CIPC represents a candidate gene for circadian rhythm disorders, metabolic syndrome, and sleep disorders, and may serve as a therapeutic target for conditions requiring circadian clock modulation.
