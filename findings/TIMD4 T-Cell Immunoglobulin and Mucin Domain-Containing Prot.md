## Description
TIMD4 (T-cell Immunoglobulin and Mucin Domain-containing protein 4, also known as TIM-4) is a 378-amino acid type I transmembrane protein characterized by a canonical immunoglobulin superfamily architecture (IPR003599/IPR007110/IPR013106/IPR013783/IPR036179/PF07686/PS50835/PTHR46608/SM00409/SSF48726/G3DSA:2.60.40.10, positions 24-116 for Ig V-set domain core, 21-126 for Ig-like domain, 24-136 for immunoglobulin fold, 25-128 for Ig subtype domain, 1-378 for TIM family) containing the N-terminal immunoglobulin V-type domain mediating phosphatidylserine (PtdSer) recognition on apoptotic cells, classified under immune system process (GO:0002376), adaptive immunity (GO:0002250), and innate immunity (GO:0045087). STRING interactions reveal a functional network connecting to efferocytosis and phosphatidylserine recognition proteins: HAVCR1 (0.857, hepatitis A virus cellular receptor 1/TIM-1 paralogue - highest confidence indicating shared TIM family function), ADGRB1 (0.852, adhesion G protein-coupled receptor B1/BAI1 phosphatidylserine receptor), STAB2 (0.835, stabilin-2 scavenger receptor), GAS6 (0.817, growth arrest-specific 6 bridging molecule), MERTK (0.710, MER proto-oncogene tyrosine kinase efferocytosis receptor), JMJD6 (0.708, jumonji domain containing 6 arginine demethylase), CD36 (0.698, scavenger receptor class B member 3), SCARB1 (0.479), TYRO3 (0.450, TAM receptor tyrosine kinase), and SCARB2 (0.447), indicating TIMD4 functions within an integrated apoptotic cell clearance network. HPA data shows group-enriched expression with highest levels in testis (137.3 nTPM) and lymphoid tissue (51.7 nTPM), detected in many tissues, cancer-enriched specificity, group-enriched blood cell specificity, cell type-enhanced single cell specificity, classified as a plasma membrane protein and plasma protein. ClinVar contains 15 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including one single nucleotide variant (c.242C>G/p.Ser81Ter nonsense mutation in IgV domain) and multiple large copy number losses and gains spanning 5q33.2-34, 5q33.1-35.2, 5q32-35.3, 5q33.3-35.1, 5p15.1-q35.2, and 5p15.33-q35.3 regions (deletions and duplications affecting chromosome 5 long arm), though specific monogenic disease phenotypes remain under characterization (significance marked '?' for most); the genomic location overlaps with regions implicated in immune dysregulation and hematologic disorders. AlphaFold structure (AF-Q96H15-F1) predicts medium confidence folding (pLDDT 63.4) consistent with a partially structured protein containing ordered immunoglobulin V-domain coupled with flexible mucin-like and transmembrane regions typical of TIM family receptors that undergo conformational changes during ligand binding. With 123 papers, TIMD4 represents a 'gray' gene with established immunological function but emerging clinical associations (2019-2026): pan-cancer study identifies TIMD4 as tumor-associated efferocytosis core gene with preliminary exploration in renal cell carcinoma; multi-omic analysis reveals ER stress-driven TIMD4+ IL1β+ macrophage subpopulation contributing to osteoarthritis progression; TIMD4 regulates proliferation and apoptosis of diffuse large B-cell lymphoma cells via Wnt/β-catenin pathway; differential TIMD4 expression in orbital adipose tissues of thyroid-associated ophthalmopathy patients; TIMD4 rs6882076 SNP associated with decreased disease risk; and TIMD4 expressed on antigen-presenting cells regulating T-cell activation through PtdSer-dependent co-stimulation. The mechanistic hypothesis posits that TIMD4 functions as a phosphatidylserine receptor that: (1) uses its IgV domain to recognize PtdSer exposed on apoptotic cell surfaces enabling efferocytosis without internalization (TIMD4 lacks phagocytic capacity itself but facilitates engulfment by partnering cells); (2) cooperates with integrins and TAM receptors (MERTK 0.710, TYRO3 0.450 scores) through GAS6 bridging molecules (0.817 score) creating multimodal apoptotic cell recognition; (3) regulates macrophage polarization toward pro-inflammatory IL1β+ states in osteoarthritis synovium linking efferocytosis dysfunction to chronic inflammation; (4) modulates Wnt/β-catenin signaling in DLBCL affecting tumor cell survival explaining cancer-enriched expression patterns; (5) exhibits testis-enriched expression (137.3 nTPM) suggesting roles in germ cell clearance during spermatogenesis; chromosome 5q33-35 CNVs may contribute to immune dysregulation phenotypes including autoimmunity or cancer susceptibility through dosage effects on efferocytic capacity, while the p.Ser81Ter variant in the IgV domain would abolish PtdSer binding producing loss-of-function.
