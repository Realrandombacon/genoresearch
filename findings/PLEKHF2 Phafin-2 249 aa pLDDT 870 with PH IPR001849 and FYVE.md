## Description
PLEKHF2 (Pleckstrin Homology Domain-Containing Family F Member 2, also known as Phafin-2 or EAPF, UniProt Q9H8W4) is a 249 amino acid dual lipid-binding protein representing a genuine dark gene with only 11 literature references despite critical structural architecture combining Phosphoinositide-binding modules. PLEKHF2 contains a defining N-terminal Pleckstrin Homology (PH) domain (IPR001849/PF00169, positions 35-133; IPR037871, positions 7-129) specific for phosphatidylinositol-3-phosphate [PI(3)P] and a C-terminal FYVE zinc finger domain (IPR000306/PF01363, positions 144-213; IPR017455, positions 152-212) also targeting PI(3)P, creating a unique tandem arrangement for high-affinity endosomal membrane anchoring. The AlphaFold structure shows high confidence (pLDDT 87.0), indicating a stable two-lobed architecture where the PH and FYVE domains are connected by a flexible linker, poised to clamp onto PI(3)P-enriched early endosomes. STRING interactions reveal medium-confidence partners including AKT1 (0.613), PIKFYVE (0.473), MBIP (0.448), PLEKHG1 (0.418), and SNX29 (0.410), placing PLEKHF2 in the context of PI3K/AKT signaling, endosomal sorting, and vesicle trafficking networks. HPA data confirms tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in bone marrow (96.7 nTPM) and lymphoid tissue (67.2 nTPM), ubiquitous detection across tissues, low cancer specificity, and classification as a predicted intracellular protein involved in protein transport, consistent with a role in immune cell endocytosis and phagosome maturation. Critically, ClinVar documents 39 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number variations (CNVs) on chromosome 8 affecting the 8q21-24 region: gains including chr8:158048-146295771x3 (8p23.3-q24.3), chr8:79046933-102008860x3 (8q21.12-22.3), chr8:84712253-146295771x3 (8q21.2-24.3); and losses including chr8:84127576-98263585x1 (8q21.13-22.1), chr8:89179899-97978274 (8q21.3-22.1). These extensive CNVs suggest dosage sensitivity, though specific Mendelian phenotypes remain undefined. Recent literature (2017-2022) establishes PLEKHF2 as: (1) Antibacterial defense factor where zebrafish PLEKHF2 is identified as an abundant egg/embryo protein with direct antibacterial activity against pathogens (2022, 7 citations); (2) Prostate cancer metastasis marker where PLEKHF2 is implicated in lymph node extension via machine learning analysis (2020, 17 citations); (3) SLE-associated gene where PLEKHF2 variants are linked to systemic lupus erythematosus pathogenic mechanisms (2017, 1 citation). Functional hypothesis: PLEKHF2 likely acts as (1) Endosomal PI(3)P sensor - the tandem PH-FYVE arrangement provides ultra-high affinity and specificity for PI(3)P, anchoring PLEKHF2 to early endosomes and phagosomes more stably than single-domain proteins; (2) Phagosome maturation regulator - in macrophages/neutrophils (high bone marrow expression), PLEKHF2 may recruit effectors to promote phagolysosome fusion and bacterial killing, explaining its antibacterial role in zebrafish embryos; (3) AKT signaling modulator - interaction with AKT1 suggests PLEKHF2 may localize active AKT to endosomes, regulating survival signals during immune activation; (4) Endosomal sorting coordinator - binding to PIKFYVE (lipid kinase) and SNX29 (sorting nexin) implies roles in generating PI(3)P pools and directing cargo into recycling vs. degradative pathways; (5) Autoimmunity driver - dysregulated endosomal trafficking in B cells/T cells could lead to aberrant antigen presentation or TLR signaling, contributing to SLE; (6) Cancer metastasis facilitator - altered endosomal dynamics may enhance integrin recycling or growth factor receptor signaling (e.g., EGFR, MET) to promote invasion in prostate cancer. Disease mechanism: PLEKHF2 dysfunction contributes to: (a) Immunodeficiency - loss-of-function may impair phagosome maturation, reducing bacterial clearance (consistent with zebrafish data); (b) Systemic Lupus Erythematosus - gain-of-function or mislocalization could hyperactivate endosomal TLRs (TLR7/9), driving autoantibody production; (c) Prostate cancer progression - overexpression may enhance endosomal recycling of pro-invasive receptors; (d) 8q CNV syndromes - haploinsufficiency or triplosensitivity may cause neurodevelopmental or immune defects via disrupted endosomal trafficking in neurons/immune cells. Conservation: PLEKHF2 is conserved across vertebrates with mouse ortholog (Q91WB4, 249 aa, ~85% identity), zebrafish ortholog (Q6PEK3, 248 aa), and Xenopus ortholog, indicating ancient endosomal function. Therapeutic implication: PLEKHF2 represents an emerging target where: (1) Small molecules disrupting PH-FYVE cooperation could modulate endosomal signaling in cancer; (2) PLEKHF2 expression levels might predict response to PI3K/AKT inhibitors; (3) Gene therapy restoring PLEKHF2 could treat immunodeficiencies linked to phagocyte defects; (4) 8q CNV analysis should include PLEKHF2 dosage for immune phenotype prediction. Validation priorities include: (1) Cryo-EM structure of PLEKHF2 bound to PI(3)P-containing membranes; (2) Live-cell imaging of PLEKHF2 dynamics during phagocytosis; (3) Generation of Plekhf2 knockout mice to assess antibacterial immunity and autoimmunity; (4) Proteomic mapping of PLEKHF2 interactome in primary human macrophages; (5) Investigation of PLEKHF2's role in TLR7/9 signaling in SLE patient B cells.
