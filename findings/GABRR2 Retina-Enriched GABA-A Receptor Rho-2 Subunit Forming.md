## Description
GABRR2 encodes Gamma-aminobutyric acid type A receptor subunit rho-2 (465 aa, P28476), a ligand-gated chloride channel functioning in inhibitory neurotransmission with distinctive retina-enriched expression. InterPro analysis reveals canonical Cys-loop receptor architecture: extracellular ligand-binding domain (cd19005, positions 76-261; G3DSA:2.70.170.10, positions 49-257) containing GABA binding pocket; transmembrane domain (cd19059, positions 262-462; IPR006029, positions 268-461; G3DSA:1.20.58.390, positions 258-359/435-465) forming ion channel pore; GABA-A/glycine receptor family signature (IPR006028, positions 264-284/290-311/324-345) with conserved cysteine loop; GO annotations confirm chloride channel activity (GO:0005254), GABA-A receptor activity (GO:0004888), and chloride transmembrane transport (GO:0006821). STRING interactions reveal GABA receptor subunit network: GABRR1 (0.928, rho-1 subunit - forms homomeric/heteromeric channels), GABRR3 (0.912, rho-3 paralog), GABRG2 (0.783, gamma-2 subunit), GABRQ (0.745, theta subunit), GABRG3 (0.708, gamma-3), GABRA1/2 (0.701/0.703, alpha subunits), GABBR2 (0.636, GABA-B receptor), GABRA5 (0.618), GABRE (0.569, epsilon) - indicating assembly into pentameric chloride channels. HPA shows tissue-enriched expression with striking retina specificity (29.1 nTPM); not detected in cancers or immune cells; cell type enriched specificity. ClinVar documents 14 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including chromosome 6q CNVs (losses at 6q14.3-16.1, gains at 6q12-16.1/6q13-24.1) and single allele deletions indicating dosage sensitivity, though specific disease phenotypes remain incompletely characterized. AlphaFold structure shows confident prediction (pLDDT 76.6) with well-folded extracellular domain and 4-transmembrane helix bundle typical of Cys-loop receptors. Literature (39 papers) reveals specialized roles: 2018 CRISPR/Cas9 editing studies in RGC-5 retinal ganglion cells; 2016 genetic polymorphism studies linking GABRR2 variants to general cognitive ability in Chinese Han population and epilepsy susceptibility/pharmacotherapy in North Indian population; 2010-2011 association studies with alcohol dependence; emerging 2025 research on GABA receptor subunit expression in glioblastoma, breast, and ovarian cancer EMT responses. Functional hypothesis: GABRR2 functions as a retina-specialized GABA-A receptor subunit forming homomeric or heteromeric (with GABRR1/GABRR3) chloride channels mediating inhibitory neurotransmission in retinal circuitry; distinct from classical GABA-A receptors (alpha/beta/gamma subunits) in pharmacological properties (bicuculline-insensitive, zinc-sensitive); cognitive and epilepsy associations suggest broader CNS roles beyond retina; alcohol dependence link indicates reward pathway involvement. Mechanistic proposal: Extracellular domain binds GABA inducing conformational change transmitted to transmembrane pore; chloride influx hyperpolarizes postsynaptic membrane reducing neuronal excitability; homomeric rho receptors show distinct gating kinetics and pharmacology versus heteromeric alpha-beta-gamma receptors; retina enrichment (29.1 nTPM) suggests specialized role in visual signal processing and retinal ganglion cell inhibition; 6q CNVs encompassing GABRR2 locus suggest haploinsufficiency could disrupt inhibitory tone contributing to epilepsy, cognitive impairment, or neurodevelopmental disorders; polymorphism-cognition associations indicate genetic variation affects channel function or expression levels; cancer EMT studies suggest non-canonical roles in tumor cell migration potentially through chloride flux regulation of cell volume and cytoskeletal dynamics. This gene represents a moderately characterized neurotransmitter receptor subunit with retina specialization and emerging disease associations.
