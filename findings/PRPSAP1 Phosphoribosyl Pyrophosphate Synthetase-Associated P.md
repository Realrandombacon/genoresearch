# PRPSAP1: Phosphoribosyl Pyrophosphate Synthetase-Associated Protein Regulating Nucleotide Biosynthesis and Purine Metabolism

## Description
PRPSAP1 (Q14558) encodes a 356 amino acid phosphoribosyl pyrophosphate synthetase-associated protein that functions as a regulatory subunit in nucleotide biosynthesis. InterPro analysis reveals the canonical ribose-phosphate pyrophosphokinase domain architecture (IPR005946, positions 8-351) with distinct N-terminal (PF13793, positions 16-126) and C-terminal associated domains (PF14572, positions 167-350), plus phosphoribosyltransferase-like fold (IPR029057). AlphaFold predicts a well-folded structure (pLDDT 88.9) consistent with stable protein-protein interactions. STRING interactions identify high-confidence partners including PRPS1L1 (0.824), EARS2 (0.672), EPRS1 (0.669), and direct associations with PRPS1 (0.413), PRPS2 (0.416), and PRPSAP2 (0.468), indicating PRPSAP1 functions within a multi-subunit PRPP synthetase complex that generates phosphoribosyl pyrophosphate (PRPP), a critical precursor for purine and pyrimidine nucleotide synthesis. HPA shows ubiquitous expression across all tissues with cytosolic localization and low tissue specificity, consistent with housekeeping roles in nucleotide metabolism. ClinVar contains 13 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly copy number gains on chromosome 17q24-q25. Literature (21 papers) links PRPSAP1 to hepatocellular carcinoma prognosis (exosomal PRPSAP1 predicts microvascular invasion) and Polynesian-specific variants associated with gout susceptibility, suggesting dysregulated purine metabolism when PRPSAP1 function is altered. The mechanistic hypothesis: PRPSAP1 acts as an allosteric regulator or scaffold protein that stabilizes the PRPS enzyme complex, modulating PRPP production rates to coordinate nucleotide biosynthesis with cellular demand; overexpression or copy number gains increase PRPP flux leading to hyperuricemia (gout) and enhanced nucleotide availability for tumor proliferation (HCC), while loss-of-function may impair DNA repair and cell division.
