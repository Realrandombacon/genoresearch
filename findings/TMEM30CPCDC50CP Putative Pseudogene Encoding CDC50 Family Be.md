## Description
TMEM30CP (Transmembrane Protein 30C Pseudogene, also CDC50CP/CDC50 Family Member C Pseudogene) is annotated as a 113 amino acid membrane protein (UniProt A0ZSE6) with a well-folded structure (AlphaFold pLDDT 77.4, high confidence), but is classified as a predicted pseudogene with no confirmed expression, protein interactions, or clinical variants, suggesting it may represent a non-functional retrogene or transcriptionally silent locus derived from the CDC50 flippase beta-subunit family.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals TMEM30CP contains a CDC50/LEM3 family domain (IPR005045, residues 13-100) belonging to the CELL CYCLE CONTROL PROTEIN 50 family (PTHR10926, residues 13-100). The CDC50/LEM3 family comprises beta-subunits that partner with P4-ATPase flippases to transport phospholipids (phosphatidylserine, phosphatidylethanolamine) from the outer to inner leaflet of cellular membranes. These heteromeric complexes are essential for membrane asymmetry, vesicle trafficking, and cell polarity. The GO term membrane (GO:0016020) confirms expected membrane localization. The high-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 77.4) suggests a stable fold despite pseudogene annotation, potentially indicating recent pseudogenization or low-level expression.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis returned no protein partners for TMEM30CP, consistent with pseudogene status or extremely low expression below detection thresholds. Functional CDC50 family members (TMEM30A/CDC50A, TMEM30B/CDC50B) interact with ATP8A1, ATP8B1, ATP11A, and ATP11C P4-ATPases, but TMEM30CP lacks confirmed interactions.\n\nHPA expression returned no data for TMEM30CP (ENSG00000235156), indicating the gene is not detected in the Human Protein Atlas transcriptome datasets. This absence supports pseudogene classification or tissue-restricted expression outside HPA sampling.\n\nClinVar documents zero pathogenic variants for TMEM30CP, confirming no known clinical significance. The absence of disease-associated variants contrasts with functional CDC50 family members where mutations cause cholestasis (ATP8B1-CDC50A) and neurological disorders.\n\nConservation is limited: human (A0ZSE6, 113 aa) shows truncated length compared to chicken (E1C325, 366 aa; A0A8V0XS32, 351 aa) and Xenopus (F7ADD6, 357 aa; A0A1L8HCZ4, 356 aa) orthologs. The human 113 aa protein represents a truncated form, suggesting pseudogenization through premature stop codons or frameshifts in the primate lineage. Full-length vertebrate orthologs (350-370 aa) indicate the ancestral gene was functional.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: TMEM30CP represents a primate-specific pseudogene derived from ancestral CDC50 family beta-subunits. If translated, the 113 aa protein would retain the N-terminal CDC50 domain (residues 13-100) but lack C-terminal regions required for P4-ATPase binding and ER export signals. The truncated structure may act as a dominant-negative inhibitor by sequestering P4-ATPases without enabling membrane trafficking, or may be rapidly degraded. The well-folded N-terminal domain (pLDDT 77.4) suggests selective pressure to maintain structure, potentially indicating low-level functional expression in specific tissues. Alternatively, TMEM30CP may produce regulatory non-coding RNAs rather than protein. The lack of HPA expression, STRING interactions, and ClinVar variants supports classification as a non-functional pseudogene. Comparative analysis with full-length orthologs (chicken 366 aa, Xenopus 357 aa) reveals human-specific truncation, marking this as a recent pseudogenization event. TMEM30CP exemplifies how dark genome annotation must distinguish between genuine proteins and pseudogenes with residual ORFs.
