## Description
MZT1 (Mitotic spindle organizing protein 1, also known as MOZART1 or C13orf37) is an 82 amino acid intracellular protein localized to the centrosome and mitotic spindle (UniProt Q08AG7) with very-high-confidence well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 92.2). Despite its small size, MZT1 forms a compact, stable structural module essential for microtubule nucleation. Key domains include the Mitotic-spindle organizing protein 1 family domain (IPR022214/PTHR28520, residues 13-79) forming a ~67 aa core module (~82%) and the Pfam Mitotic-spindle organizing gamma-tubulin ring associated domain (PF12554, residues 22-68). Molecular functions include gamma-tubulin complex localization (GO:0033566) and structural constituent of the gamma-tubulin ring complex (GO:0000931), placing MZT1 as a critical scaffold for microtubule assembly.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals an exceptionally tight and high-confidence network within the gamma-tubulin ring complex (γ-TuRC): strongest interactions with TUBGCP5 (0.999, Gamma-tubulin complex component 5) and MZT2B (0.999, Mitotic-spindle organizing protein 2B, a paralog), indicating stoichiometric incorporation into the complex. Near-perfect scores include TUBGCP2 (0.998), NEDD1 (0.982, Neural precursor cell expressed developmentally down-regulated protein 1, the human homolog of Drosophila Dgt6 that recruits γ-TuRC to centrosomes), TUBGCP4 (0.970), and TUBGCP6 (0.918). Interactions with TUBG1 (0.876, Gamma-tubulin, the core nucleator) and MZT2A (0.870) confirm its role in the active nucleation machinery. NME7 (0.822) and TUBGCP3 (0.743) complete the complex. These scores (>0.9) suggest MZT1 is an integral, non-dispensable subunit of the γ-TuRC.\n\nHPA expression reveals low tissue specificity with detection in all tissues, consistent with an essential housekeeping role in cell division. The classification as an 'Essential protein' underscores its requirement for viability. Subcellular localization at the centrosome and spindle confirms its function in mitosis.\n\nClinVar documents 66 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number losses and gains affecting the 13q14-31 region: CNV losses (13q14.3-31.2, 13q21.1-31.3, 13q21.33-33.1 deletions) and CNV gains (13q11-31.3, 13q21.2-34 duplications). The high variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity. Haploinsufficiency would impair γ-TuRC assembly, leading to defective spindle formation, chromosome segregation errors (aneuploidy), and cell cycle arrest or apoptosis. This is particularly critical in rapidly dividing tissues (hematopoietic, epithelial) and developing embryos. Duplications may cause aberrant microtubule nucleation or sequestration of complex components.\n\nConservation is strong across eukaryotes: human (Q08AG7, 82 aa), mouse (Q8BUR9, 78 aa), fission yeast (P0CF96, 64 aa), indicating ancient evolutionary origin and conserved mechanism.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: MZT1 functions as a structural 'glue' or stabilizing subunit within the γ-TuRC, bridging specific GCPs (Gamma-tubulin Complex Proteins) to form the lock-washer spiral template that nucleates microtubules. Its compact, highly folded structure (pLDDT 92.2) suggests it acts as a rigid scaffold rather than a flexible linker. MZT1 likely binds at the interface between TUBGCP2, TUBGCP4, and TUBGCP5, stabilizing the complex conformation required for high-affinity binding to alpha/beta-tubulin dimers. Recruitment to the centrosome is mediated by NEDD1. Loss of MZT1 destabilizes the γ-TuRC, preventing efficient microtubule nucleation, leading to monopolar or malformed spindles, mitotic catastrophe, and genomic instability. In 13q deletion syndromes, loss of MZT1 contributes to microcephaly (defective neural progenitor division) and developmental delay.
