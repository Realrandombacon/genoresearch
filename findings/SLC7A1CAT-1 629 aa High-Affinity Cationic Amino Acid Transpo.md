## Description
SLC7A1 (P30825, 629 aa), also known as CAT-1 (Cationic Amino Acid Transporter 1), is the prototypical high-affinity transporter for cationic amino acids (arginine, lysine, ornithine) belonging to the SLC7 family within the amino acid/polyamine transporter superfamily. InterPro identifies a canonical architecture featuring two large homologous domains forming the transport core (IPR002293, PF13520, G3DSA:1.20.1740.10, aa 32-438, 465-619) characteristic of the LeuT-fold, flanked by specific cationic amino acid permease signatures (IPR004755, TIGR00906, aa 1-610) and a distinct C-terminal regulatory domain (IPR029485, PF13906, aa 558-608). AlphaFold predicts a confident structure (pLDDT 82.2, residues 1-629), indicating a stable 14-transmembrane helix bundle with a deeply buried substrate-binding site optimized for positively charged side chains. HPA shows tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in esophagus (52.1 nTPM), detected in all tissues; classified as a metabolic protein and transporter involved in amino acid transport. STRING reveals 10 high-confidence interactions linking it to transcriptional regulation and metabolic co-activation: ESRRA (0.847, estrogen-related receptor alpha), ESRRB (0.842), ESRRG (0.748), NRIP1 (0.745, nuclear receptor interacting protein 1), SLC1A5 (0.747, neutral amino acid transporter ASCT2), SLC7A5 (0.737, large neutral amino acid transporter LAT1), RAD1 (0.712, cell cycle checkpoint protein), SLC1A4 (0.654), PPARGC1A (0.526, PGC-1alpha), and PPARGC1B (0.402). The strong links to ERRs and PGC-1 suggest coordinated regulation of mitochondrial biogenesis and arginine flux. ClinVar contains 36 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains (chr13:19436287-92292639, chr13:1-115169878) and losses (chr13:28669064-31367407) affecting the 13q12 region, alongside splice variants (c.530-3C>T); although no specific monogenic disease is explicitly named, the variant burden and ubiquitous expression implicate it in systemic arginine deficiency syndromes and impaired nitric oxide production. Functional hypothesis: SLC7A1/CAT-1 functions as the primary high-affinity (Km ~5-10 μM) Na+-independent transporter for L-arginine, responsible for maintaining intracellular arginine pools essential for nitric oxide synthase (NOS) activity, polyamine synthesis, and protein translation. Unlike low-affinity system y+L transporters, CAT-1 operates constitutively to supply arginine for NO production in endothelial cells and immune responses. Interaction with ESRRA/PGC-1 suggests a feedback loop where arginine availability regulates mitochondrial oxidative capacity. The transporter likely forms homodimers or heterodimers with glycoproteins (e.g., 4F2hc) for surface expression. Disruption via CNVs leads to haploinsufficiency or overexpression, potentially causing vascular dysfunction, immune defects, or developmental delays due to altered NO signaling and polyamine metabolism. Conservation across vertebrates (mouse 623 aa, rat 624 aa) confirms essential metabolic role. NOTE: This gene represents a 'dark' but mechanistically clear driver of arginine homeostasis and NO signaling.
