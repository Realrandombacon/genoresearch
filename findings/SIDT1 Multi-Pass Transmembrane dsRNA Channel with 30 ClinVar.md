# SIDT1: Multi-Pass Transmembrane dsRNA Channel with 30 ClinVar Variants, Brain-Enhanced Expression, and Phospholipase-Mediated RNA Uptake in Immune Response

## Description
SIDT1 (SID1 Transmembrane Family Member 1; UniProt Q9NXL6) is an 827 amino acid multi-pass transmembrane protein with only 54 publications, classifying it as a genuine dark gene despite critical roles in dietary microRNA absorption, antiviral immunity, and nucleic acid trafficking.\n\nSTRUCTURAL ARCHITECTURE: InterPro analysis identifies SIDT1 as a canonical member of the SID1 transmembrane family (IPR025958/PTHR12185, residues 14-827) containing a dsRNA-gated channel domain (PF13965, residues 184-827). The protein is predicted to span the membrane multiple times, forming a channel structure optimized for nucleic acid transport. AlphaFold predicts high confidence structure (global pLDDT 80.2), indicating a well-folded transmembrane architecture consistent with recent structural studies revealing cholesterol transport and hydrolase activity.\n\nINTERACTION NETWORK: STRING-DB reveals high-confidence interactions with RNA processing and trafficking machinery: UNC119 (0.780, lipid-binding chaperone for ciliary trafficking), DICER1 (0.733, RNase III endonuclease essential for miRNA/siRNA biogenesis), plus medium-confidence partners including IGDCC3 (0.696), CDC14A (0.696), POLR2F/POLR2G/POLR2K (RNA polymerase II subunits), AGO1 (0.531) and AGO2 (0.499, Argonaute proteins in RISC complex), and ERI1 (0.470, 3'-5' exonuclease). This network positions SIDT1 at the intersection of extracellular RNA uptake and intracellular RNA interference pathways.\n\nEXPRESSION & LOCALIZATION: HPA shows tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in brain (10.6 nTPM) and salivary gland (10.2 nTPM). Expression is cancer-enhanced and immune cell-enhanced, consistent with roles in tumor immunity and dendritic cell function. Classified as a membrane transporter with RNA-binding molecular function.\n\nDISEASE LANDSCAPE: ClinVar contains 30 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number losses at 3q13.2-13.31 encompassing SIDT1. No monogenic disease is yet definitively assigned, but 3q13 deletions cause developmental anomalies. Recent 2020-2024 literature establishes SIDT1's critical functions: mediating dietary and orally administered microRNA absorption in gastric pit cells (116 citations), enabling dsRNA uptake via phospholipase activity (14 citations), driving type I interferon responses in plasmacytoid dendritic cells and psoriasis pathogenesis (12 citations), and facilitating N-glycosylation-dependent RNA uptake mechanisms.\n\nCONSERVATION: Orthologs confirmed in mouse (Q6AXF6, 827 aa), rat (Q6Q3F5, 831 aa), and multiple bird species (~817-821 aa), indicating strong evolutionary constraint on the transmembrane channel architecture.\n\nFUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: SIDT1 likely functions as a multi-pass transmembrane channel that facilitates cellular uptake of extracellular double-stranded RNA and microRNAs through a phospholipase-dependent mechanism. The channel may undergo conformational changes upon binding dsRNA, enabling translocation across the membrane. In the stomach, SIDT1 mediates dietary miRNA absorption that can regulate host gene expression systemically. In immune cells (particularly plasmacytoid dendritic cells), SIDT1 enables detection of extracellular nucleic acids, triggering type I interferon responses critical for antiviral defense. The UNC119 interaction suggests potential roles in ciliary RNA trafficking. The 30 ClinVar CNVs at 3q13 warrant investigation for haploinsufficiency effects on neurodevelopment and immune function, potentially contributing to developmental delay or immunodeficiency phenotypes.
