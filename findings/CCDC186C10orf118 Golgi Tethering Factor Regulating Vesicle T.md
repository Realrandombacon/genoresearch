# CCDC186/C10orf118: Golgi Tethering Factor Regulating Vesicle Trafficking and Hyaluronan Synthesis

## Description
CCDC186 (C10orf118, UniProt Q7Z3E2) is an 898-amino acid coiled-coil domain-containing protein localized to the Golgi apparatus. InterPro analysis identifies the CCDC186 family domain (IPR038830, residues 86-897), a short helical domain (PF27942, residues 815-841), and membership in the CTCL tumor antigen family (PTHR18911). AlphaFold predicts a confident structure (pLDDT 73.8) consistent with an extended coiled-coil architecture typical of golgin family tethering factors. HPA confirms Golgi apparatus localization with ubiquitous tissue expression and immune cell enhancement. STRING interaction analysis reveals a functionally coherent network of Golgi vesicle trafficking partners: EIPR1 (0.717, GARP complex subunit), TMF1 (0.564, Golgi tethering factor), VPS50 (0.544), VPS54 (0.479), and VPS51 (0.457) - all components of retrograde trafficking machinery, plus RAB2A (0.410, small GTPase regulating ER-Golgi transport). This interaction profile strongly suggests CCDC186 functions as a Golgi tethering factor coordinating vesicle docking. ClinVar records 31 pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains/losses at 10q25, implicating dosage sensitivity in developmental disorders. Literature (only 3 papers) reveals CCDC186 is secreted and regulates hyaluronan synthesis in tumor-stroma cross-talk, with differential expression in kidney cancer. We hypothesize CCDC186 acts as a golgin-like tethering factor at the trans-Golgi network, where its coiled-coil structure captures incoming vesicles via interactions with GARP complex components and RAB2A, facilitating cargo delivery that influences hyaluronan synthase trafficking. Overexpression in cancers may enhance extracellular matrix remodeling through increased hyaluronan secretion, while haploinsufficiency disrupts Golgi trafficking causing developmental phenotypes.
