## Description
CYLC1 (Cylicin-1, also known as CYCL1, SPGFX8) is a 651 amino acid sperm head cytoskeletal protein (UniProt P35663) with low-confidence disordered architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 45.5), characteristic of intrinsically disordered proteins that function through flexible protein-protein interactions in structural assemblies. The protein contains a cylicin family domain (IPR026189/PTHR16742, residues 1-649) with molecular function as a structural constituent of the cytoskeleton (GO:0005200), and a cylicin N-terminal domain (IPR029354/PF15241, residues 8-114) unique to this protein family. CYLC1 is located on chromosome Xq21.1 and is specifically expressed in the calyx of spermatozoa and spermatids during spermatogenesis.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals a network of spermatogenesis-associated proteins with high to medium confidence scores: CCIN (0.982, Calicin, a testis-specific intermediate filament protein that forms the calyx structure), CYLC2 (0.663, Cylicin-2, the paralogous family member), SATL1 (0.583, Spermatogenic cell-specific actin-like protein 1), APOOL (0.579, Apolipoprotein O-like, lipid metabolism in spermatids), CCNL1 (0.578, Cyclin-L1, RNA processing in germ cells), PCDH11X (0.528, Protocadherin-11 X-linked, cell adhesion), TENT5D (0.526, Terminal nucleotidyltransferase 5D, mRNA polyadenylation), BRWD3 (0.525, Bromodomain and WD repeat domain-containing protein 3, X-linked intellectual disability gene), CPXCR1 (0.418, Cleft palate-associated protein), and RPS6KA6 (0.407, Ribosomal protein S6 kinase A6, X-linked mental retardation). These interactions confirm CYLC1 functions within the spermatid differentiation and calyx assembly pathway.\n\nHPA expression reveals CYLC1 is tissue-enriched in testis (29.7 nTPM), detected in single tissues, with group-enriched expression at the single-cell level. Subcellular localization includes the perinuclear theca and calyx, specialized structures that cap the posterior head region of mature spermatozoa. The calyx provides structural integrity to the sperm head and anchors the acrosome. CYLC1 is involved in differentiation and spermatogenesis biological processes.\n\nClinVar documents 146 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including large copy number gains (Xq13.1-22.2, Xq13.1-22.3, Xq13.1-27.1, Xq12-28, Xp22.33-q28 amplifications), copy number losses (Xq21.1-22.3, Xq13.2-21.31 deletions), and a missense variant (c.1377G>T p.Lys459Asn). The variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity. While no specific Mendelian disease is annotated, Xq CNVs are associated with male infertility, intellectual disability, and developmental anomalies. Given CYLC1's role in sperm head structure, loss-of-function may cause teratozoospermia (abnormal sperm morphology) and male infertility. The SPGFX8 alias suggests association with X-linked spastic paraplegia.\n\nConservation is strong across mammals: human (P35663, 651 aa), mouse (A0A1B0GR13, 642 aa), bovine (P35662, 667 aa), indicating an essential function in mammalian spermatogenesis.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: CYLC1 functions as a structural component of the sperm head calyx, a specialized cytoskeletal structure that provides mechanical stability to the acrosome and anchors it to the nuclear envelope during spermiogenesis. The intrinsically disordered nature (pLDDT 45.5) allows CYLC1 to form flexible cross-links with other calyx proteins (CCIN/calicin, CYLC2) and intermediate filaments, creating a resilient scaffold that withstands mechanical stress during fertilization. CYLC1 may interact with SATL1 (actin-like protein) for cytoskeletal organization and TENT5D for post-transcriptional regulation of spermatid mRNAs. Dosage imbalance from Xq CNVs disrupts calyx assembly, causing sperm head malformation, acrosome detachment, and male infertility. The X-linked location explains male-specific phenotypes. CYLC1 represents a candidate gene for non-obstructive azoospermia and teratozoospermia, with potential diagnostic value in male infertility workups.
