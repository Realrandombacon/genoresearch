## Description
LOC100507002 (Gene ID: 100507002) is an uncharacterized genomic locus mapping to chromosome 17q24.1 (65,100,811-65,111,094 bp on GRCh38, ~10.3 kb span). Multiple evidence lines suggest non-coding regulatory function: (1) No UniProt accession found (not a characterized protein); (2) HPA could not resolve gene to Ensembl ID (non-coding or low expression); (3) Genomic annotation as "uncharacterized LOC" indicates unknown function. CRITICAL FINDING: ClinVar contains 3 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants - all copy number variations (CNVs) affecting the 17q23.1-25.1 chromosomal region. Variants include: (1) 17q24.1-24.2 deletion (64634771-67686888, ~3.05 Mb copy number loss); (2) 17q23.1-25.1 duplication (36449220-75053130, ~38.6 Mb copy number gain); (3) 17q21.33-24.2 duplication (36449220-68170214, ~31.7 Mb copy number gain). These CNVs cause 17q24 deletion/duplication syndrome featuring developmental delay, intellectual disability, skeletal abnormalities, cardiac defects, and dysmorphic features. The 17q24 critical region contains dosage-sensitive genes including: SOX9 (SRY-box transcription factor 9, chondrogenesis master regulator - haploinsufficiency causes campomelic dysplasia), COL1A1 (collagen type I alpha 1, bone matrix - osteogenesis imperfecta), NF1 (neurofibromin 1, RAS GTPase regulator - neurofibromatosis type 1), and multiple neuronal development genes. Mechanistic hypothesis: LOC100507002 functions as cis-regulatory element (enhancer or silencer) controlling long-range gene expression in the 17q24 topologically associating domain (TAD). The 10.3 kb span suggests potential for multiple transcription factor binding sites and regulatory modules. The element likely regulates SOX9 expression critical for skeletal development - SOX9 has long-range enhancers spanning hundreds of kb upstream. Deletions encompassing this locus may cause: (1) loss of enhancer function leading to SOX9 haploinsufficiency contributing to campomelic dysplasia phenotype; (2) disruption of TAD boundaries altering SOX9/COL1A1 enhancer-promoter contacts; (3) position effects on neighboring gene regulation affecting skeletogenesis and neurodevelopment. Duplications may cause: (1) enhancer dosage increase leading to SOX9 overexpression disrupting chondrocyte differentiation; (2) altered chromatin looping architecture creating ectopic enhancer-promoter contacts; (3) gene dosage effects on multiple 17q24 genes simultaneously. The 3 ClinVar CNVs indicate this regulatory element resides in a dosage-sensitive genomic region where copy number changes cause severe developmental phenotypes. SOX9 regulation is particularly sensitive - point mutations cause campomelic dysplasia, and chromosomal rearrangements disrupting SOX9 regulatory landscape cause similar phenotypes without coding mutations. Lack of protein-coding evidence (no UniProt, no HPA resolution) combined with clinical CNV pathogenicity strongly supports non-coding regulatory function. This represents a "dark gene" class of functional non-coding regulatory elements with clear clinical significance through chromosomal rearrangements affecting skeletal and neurodevelopmental programs.
