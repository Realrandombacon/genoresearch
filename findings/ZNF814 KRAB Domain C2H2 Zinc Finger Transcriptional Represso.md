# ZNF814: KRAB Domain C2H2 Zinc Finger Transcriptional Repressor with Cancer Links and 19q Dosage Sensitivity

## Description
ZNF814 (Gene ID: 84667, UniProt B7Z6K7) is a 855 amino acid Krüppel-associated box (KRAB) domain-containing C2H2 zinc finger protein located on chromosome 19q13.43 (NC_000019.10). This gene encodes a classical two-module transcriptional repressor architecture: N-terminal KRAB repression domain coupled to C-terminal DNA-binding zinc finger arrays. InterPro domain analysis reveals canonical KRAB-ZFP organization: KRAB-A box domain (cd07765, positions 15-54), KRAB box domain (IPR001909/PF01352, positions 15-91, ~77 residues forming transcriptional repression module), KRAB superfamily (IPR036051/G3DSA:3.30.160.60, positions 10-69), and multiple C2H2-type zinc finger domains (IPR013087/PF00096/PS00028/PS50157 at positions 92-115, 120-147, 242-269, 242-264, 269-291, 296-318 and beyond—spanning C-terminal ~700 residues with 10+ tandem zinc fingers). The KRAB domain recruits KAP1/TRIM28 corepressor complexes that recruit heterochromatin machinery (HP1, SETDB1 histone methyltransferase) for transcriptional silencing. C2H2 zinc fingers recognize specific DNA sequences through modular base contacts, with each finger typically recognizing 3-4 bp. STRING interaction network identifies 4 medium-confidence partners forming a functionally relevant interactome: SMIM28 (0.506, small integral membrane protein 28, uncharacterized membrane protein), ZNF431 (0.472, zinc finger protein 431, another KRAB-ZFP suggesting cooperative transcriptional regulation), EDIL3 (0.405, EGF-like repeats and discoidin I-like domain-containing protein 3, extracellular matrix/adhesion protein), and CYP17A1 (0.402, cytochrome P450 family 17 subfamily A member 1, steroid hormone biosynthesis enzyme). The ZNF431 interaction suggests potential cooperative DNA binding or shared target gene regulation among KRAB-ZFPs. Human Protein Atlas classifies ZNF814 (ENSG00000204514) as a predicted intracellular protein and transcription factor with nucleoplasm subcellular localization. RNA expression shows low tissue specificity with detection in ALL tissues (ubiquitous housekeeping expression), low cancer specificity, low immune cell specificity, but cell type-enhanced single cell specificity—indicating fundamental transcriptional regulatory function with cell type modulation. Notably classified under cancer-related genes, suggesting oncogenic or tumor suppressor roles. AlphaFold structure prediction (AF-B7Z6K7-F1) yields MEDIUM-confidence model (global pLDDT 69.3), indicating a partially structured protein with mixed ordered and disordered regions across all 855 residues—the KRAB domain likely forms an α-helical bundle while zinc fingers form compact ββα folds stabilized by Zn²⁺ coordination. Medium confidence reflects the challenging nature of predicting large proteins with extensive linker regions between zinc fingers, typical of KRAB-ZFPs. ClinVar lists 15 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including multiple large chromosomal copy number gains spanning chromosome 19q13 regions (chr19:54196216-58759679x3, chr19:260912-58956888x3, chr19:50489390-59095359x3, chr19:52143873-58445521x3, chr19:50152520-58581203x3, chr19:52612432-58581203x3, chr19:52955056-58581203x3, chr19:56363208-58581203x3) and one frameshift variant (NM_001144989.2:c.1892del p.Pro631fs); no specific monogenic disease is attributed solely to ZNF814, suggesting dosage sensitivity within larger chromosomal syndromes. Conservation is evidenced by orthologs across vertebrates: Bos taurus (A0AAA9U006, 786 aa), Iconisemion striatum (A0A1A7XRT7, 336 aa), Nothobranchius pienaari (A0A1A8MAS7, 181 aa), Nothobranchius rachovii (A0A1A8P8A5, 247 aa)—indicating functional importance despite sequence divergence in zinc finger count. Functional hypothesis: ZNF814 functions as a sequence-specific transcriptional repressor regulating developmentally important genes and cancer-related pathways. The N-terminal KRAB domain (residues 15-91) recruits KAP1/TRIM28 corepressor complex, which recruits SETDB1 (H3K9me3 methyltransferase), HP1 heterochromatin proteins, and NuRD complex for chromatin compaction and transcriptional silencing. The C-terminal zinc finger array (residues ~92-855) recognizes specific DNA sequences through combinatorial zinc finger code—each finger contributes 3-4 bp specificity, enabling recognition of 30-40+ bp target sites. ZNF431 interaction suggests cooperative regulation with other KRAB-ZFPs, potentially forming repressive complexes at shared target genes. EDIL3 and CYP17A1 interactions unexpectedly link ZNF814 to extracellular matrix remodeling and steroid hormone biosynthesis—perhaps through transcriptional regulation of these pathways. Medium-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 69.3) suggests conformational flexibility consistent with scaffold/adapter function requiring multiple binding partners. Ubiquitous expression reflects fundamental role in transcriptional infrastructure. Cancer-related classification suggests roles in cell cycle control, differentiation, or apoptosis regulation. Disease mechanism: ZNF814 haploinsufficiency or overexpression from chromosome 19q13 CNVs may contribute to developmental disorders or cancer. The 15 ClinVar variants suggest strong dosage sensitivity; 19q13 deletions/duplications cause diverse phenotypes including intellectual disability, growth abnormalities, and cancer predisposition. The frameshift variant (p.Pro631fs) truncates the protein within the zinc finger array, likely abolishing DNA binding and causing loss-of-function. ZNF814 may contribute through ectopic silencing of target genes (developmental regulators, cell cycle controllers, steroid biosynthesis enzymes) or disruption of ZNF431-mediated programs. Priority experiments include ChIP-seq to identify genomic binding sites, co-IP validation of KAP1/TRIM28 and ZNF431 interactions, reporter assays testing repression activity, CRISPR knockout to assess phenotypic consequences particularly in cancer cell lines, structural analysis leveraging AlphaFold model (pLDDT 69.3) to map DNA-binding interface, investigation of ZNF814 role in steroid hormone regulation via CYP17A1 pathway, and analysis of ZNF814 expression in 19q syndrome patient cells and cancer tissues.
