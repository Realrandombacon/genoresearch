## Description
QTRT2 (Q9H974) encodes a 415 amino acid accessory subunit of the eukaryotic tRNA-guanine transglycosylase (TGT) enzyme complex responsible for queuosine (Q) modification at the wobble position of tRNA-His, tRNA-Tyr, tRNA-Asn, and tRNA-Asp. With only 9 publications in Semantic Scholar, QTRT2 remains a genuine dark gene despite its essential role in translational fidelity and emerging connections to cell proliferation and immune function.\n\nSTRUCTURAL FEATURES: InterPro analysis reveals a conserved tRNA-guanine transglycosylase fold (IPR002616, residues 13-413; PF01702, 13-413; IPR028592/QTRTD1 family, 26-409) with GO annotation for tRNA modification (GO:0006400). AlphaFold prediction yields exceptionally high confidence (global pLDDT 89.8), indicating a well-folded stable protein with a canonical TIM barrel catalytic domain architecture. The 2018 crystallography study confirms QTRT2 forms a homodimer that associates with the catalytic subunit QTRT1 to create the active eukaryotic TGT holoenzyme.\n\nINTERACTION NETWORK: STRING-DB identifies QTRT2 within a coherent tRNA modification machinery network through medium-confidence interactions with QTRT1 (0.492, catalytic partner), TRIT1 (0.509, tRNA-isopentenyltransferase), NSUN3 (0.540, mitochondrial tRNA methyltransferase), CDK5RAP1 (0.443, tRNA threonylcarbamoyladenosine synthase), TRDMT1 (0.644, DNA/tRNA methyltransferase), TRUB2 (0.629, pseudouridine synthase), PNPT1 (0.598, mitochondrial RNA processing), THUMPD2 (0.561, tRNA methyltransferase), GTPBP3 (0.427, mitochondrial tRNA modification), and PUS3 (0.407, pseudouridine synthase). This interaction profile positions QTRT2 within the broader epitranscriptomic regulatory network.\n\nEXPRESSION & LOCALIZATION: HPA shows ubiquitous expression across all tissues with low specificity, consistent with a housekeeping role in fundamental translation. Subcellular localization includes nucleoli (site of tRNA processing), endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi apparatus, and cytosol—suggesting potential roles beyond canonical tRNA modification, possibly in quality control or stress response pathways.\n\nDISEASE ASSOCIATION: ClinVar contains 31 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number losses and gains spanning 3q11.1-q13.31 that encompass the QTRT2 locus. While no specific monogenic disease phenotype is yet defined, queuosine deficiency has been linked to impaired cell proliferation, reduced MHC-II expression, and sex-dependent learning/memory deficits in recent literature. The 31 ClinVar variants without defined phenotypes suggest systematic screening for QTRT2 mutations in patients with unexplained developmental disorders, immunodeficiency, or neurological phenotypes could reveal novel epitranscriptopathic syndromes.\n\nCONSERVATION: Orthologs confirmed in mouse (B8ZXI1, 415 aa), zebrafish (Q7ZVJ6, 416 aa), and Xenopus (Q9JMA2, 415 aa), indicating deep evolutionary conservation from fish to mammals.\n\nFUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: QTRT2 likely functions as a structural scaffold that stabilizes the QTRT1 catalytic subunit and facilitates tRNA substrate recognition during queuosine insertion. The high-confidence AlphaFold structure suggests rigid domain architecture optimized for precise tRNA positioning. Recent 2025 literature demonstrates queuosine modification enhances translation of proliferation-associated codons and MHC-II antigen presentation, suggesting QTRT2 deficiency could impair immune surveillance and contribute to cancer progression or autoimmune susceptibility. The nucleolar localization hints at potential involvement in ribosome-tRNA coordination during cellular stress responses.
