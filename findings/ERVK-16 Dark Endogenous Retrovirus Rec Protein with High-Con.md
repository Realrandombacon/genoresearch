## Description
ERVK-16 (Endogenous Retrovirus Group K Member 16, Gene ID: 100616411) is a dark gene representing an understudied endogenous retroviral element located at chromosome 10p14. Despite the broad ERV literature (335,173 papers on endogenous retroviruses generally), ERVK-16 specifically lacks dedicated functional characterization—the returned literature focuses on ERVK-6, ERVK-7, ERVK-8, and other family members, not ERVK-16 itself. The gene encodes a 105 amino acid Rec regulatory protein (UniProt P61578), a retroviral trans-acting factor analogous to HIV-1 Rev. InterPro analysis reveals conserved retroviral regulatory domains: IPR059105 (Rec21/ENK19, positions 19-105) and PF15695 (Rec regulator of expression encoded by corf of HERV-K-113, 19-105), indicating a functional RNA-binding domain retained from ancestral retroviral infection. AlphaFold predicts high-confidence structure (global pLDDT 74.4), indicating a well-folded protein with stable 3D architecture—remarkable for an endogenous retroviral remnant and suggesting potential functionality. STRING interactions returned no partners, and HPA could not resolve the gene to an Ensembl ID, suggesting low/restricted expression or technical challenges in detecting retroviral-derived transcripts. ClinVar contains no variants, indicating no known germline disease associations. The canonical Rec/Rev proteins function as RNA export factors that bind the Rev Response Element (RRE) in unspliced viral transcripts and facilitate nuclear export via the CRM1/exportin-1 pathway, enabling production of structural proteins and genomic RNA packaging. ERVK-16's Rec protein likely retains this function and may: (1) regulate export of ERVK-16 transcripts or related ERVK family RNAs through CRM1-mediated nuclear export, (2) act as a trans-dominant inhibitor competing with functional ERVK Rec proteins (like ERVK-6) for RNA binding or nuclear export machinery, (3) modulate host gene expression by interacting with cellular RNAs containing Rev-like response elements, or (4) contribute to innate immune responses through retroviral protein sensing pathways. The high-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 74.4) and conserved Rec domain (IPR059105) suggest ERVK-16 produces a stable, functional protein worthy of investigation for roles in retroelement regulation, antiviral immunity, or potential oncogenic activity (ERVK expression is elevated in various cancers). Endogenous retroviral proteins are increasingly recognized as functional elements in human biology, from placental development (syncytins) to immune modulation.
