## Description
PRKRA encodes a 313 amino acid interferon-inducible dsRNA-binding protein (UniProt O75569), also known as PACT (Protein Activator of PKR), with dual functions in antiviral defense and RNA interference. With 172 publications, PRKRA sits at the boundary of characterized genes—its disease association is definitive but mechanistic nuances remain incompletely understood.\n\nSTRUCTURAL FEATURES: AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (pLDDT 75.4) across all 313 residues, indicating a stable multi-domain protein. InterPro analysis reveals a canonical triple dsRNA-binding motif architecture: three tandem dsRNA-binding domains (dsRBM1: cd19889/IPR044465/PF00035, residues 32-102; dsRBM2: cd19891/IPR044466/PF00035, residues 126-192; dsRBM3: cd19892/IPR044467/PF00035, residues 239-310). Each dsRBM adopts an αβββα fold (G3DSA:3.30.160.20) characteristic of dsRNA recognition. The RISC Loading Complex Component family signature (IPR051247, residues 1-308) spans nearly the entire protein, confirming its role in miRNA biogenesis.\n\nINTERACTION NETWORK: STRING-DB identifies 10 interaction partners with medium-high confidence (0.424-0.999), defining PRKRA's dual roles. Core RNAi machinery: DICER1 (0.999)—essential for pre-miRNA processing; DROSHA (0.940)—nuclear pri-miRNA cleavage; AGO1 (0.936), AGO4 (0.856), AGO3 (0.834), AGO2 (0.573)—Argonaute proteins loading siRNAs/miRNAs. dsRNA-binding partners: STAU1 (0.836)—staufen-mediated mRNA decay; TARBP2 (0.424)—TRBP, paralogous PKR activator and RISC loader. Kinase target: EIF2AK2 (0.497)—PKR, phosphorylated upon PRKRA binding to halt translation. Microprocessor component: DGCR8 (0.576)—Drosha cofactor. This network positions PRKRA at the intersection of antiviral response and gene regulation.\n\nEXPRESSION & LOCALIZATION: HPA confirms ubiquitous expression detected in all tissues with low tissue specificity. Subcellular localization includes nucleoplasm and cytosol—consistent with roles in both nuclear miRNA processing (Drosha/DGCR8 interaction) and cytoplasmic RISC loading (Dicer/AGO interaction). Biological processes include host-virus interaction and RNA-mediated gene silencing. Molecular function is RNA-binding. Classified as disease-related, essential protein with involvement in dystonia and parkinsonism.\n\nCLINICAL SIGNIFICANCE: ClinVar reports 45 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number losses (2q31.1-32.2), missense mutations (c.108T>G p.Ile36Met in dsRBM1, c.74A>G p.Lys25Arg, c.208G>A p.Val70Ile), splice variants (c.610-11C>G, c.785-3C>T, c.609+1_610-1del), and deletions/duplications. These cause Dystonia-PRKRA (DYT-PRKRA, formerly DYT16), an autosomal recessive early-onset generalized dystonia with parkinsonian features. Recent literature (2025) reveals Prkra dimerization senses dsRNA to control global translation efficiency.\n\nFUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: PRKRA functions through two coordinated mechanisms: (1) PKR activation during stress—dsRBM1 and dsRBM2 bind viral or endogenous dsRNA, inducing conformational change that recruits and activates PKR (EIF2AK2), leading to eIF2α phosphorylation and global translation shutdown. This is critical for antiviral defense. (2) RISC loading facilitation—dsRBM3 and the C-terminal region interact with Dicer and Ago2 to facilitate miRNA/siRNA loading into RISC, analogous to TRBP (TARBP2). The triple dsRBM architecture allows simultaneous binding to multiple RNA partners or cooperative binding to long dsRNA. Recent findings (2025) show Prkra dimerization enhances dsRNA sensing, explaining dominant-negative effects of certain mutations. Mechanistically, disease-causing mutations (e.g., p.Ile36Met) likely disrupt dsRBM folding or dimerization, impairing both PKR activation and miRNA biogenesis. Loss-of-function causes neuronal dysfunction via defective stress responses and altered miRNA profiles in basal ganglia. N1-methylpseudouridine mRNA modifications (2025) prevent Prkra-mediated translation repression, relevant for mRNA therapeutic design.
