## Description
GDPD4 (Glycerophosphodiester phosphodiesterase domain-containing protein 4, also known as GDE6, ugpQ) is a 623 amino acid membrane-associated hydrolase (UniProt Q6W3E5) with high-confidence well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 78.5), characteristic of enzymes with stable catalytic domains. The protein contains multiple defining domains: a glycerophosphodiester phosphodiesterase domain (IPR030395/PF03009/PS51704, residues 198-457/203-451) with phosphoric diester hydrolase activity (GO:0008081), a PLC-like phosphodiesterase TIM beta/alpha-barrel domain superfamily (IPR017946/G3DSA:3.20.20.190/SSF51695, residues 187-459/196-455) that forms the catalytic core, a mammalian GDE6-like glycerophosphodiester phosphodiesterase domain (cd08610, residues 179-491), and belongs to the glycerophosphoryl diester phosphodiesterase family (PTHR23344, residues 2-509). GDPD4 is located on chromosome 11q13.5 and is predicted to be involved in lipid metabolic processes (GO:0006629).\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals a network of lipid metabolism and mitochondrial enzymes with medium confidence scores: MECR (0.696, Mitochondrial trans-2-enoyl-CoA reductase, fatty acid elongation), GPD2 (0.627, Glycerol-3-phosphate dehydrogenase 2, mitochondrial, glycerolipid metabolism), L2HGDH (0.547, L-2-hydroxyglutarate dehydrogenase, mitochondrial metabolism), RAB30 (0.454, Ras-related protein Rab-30, vesicle trafficking), GDPD3 (0.445, Glycerophosphodiester phosphodiesterase domain-containing protein 3, paralogous family member), GCDH (0.444, Glutaryl-CoA dehydrogenase, amino acid metabolism), AQP11 (0.441, Aquaporin-11, water/glycerol transport), NADK (0.437, NAD kinase, NADP biosynthesis), RPS28 (0.426, 40S ribosomal protein S28), and ISYNA1 (0.420, Myo-inositol-1-phosphate synthase, inositol metabolism). These interactions confirm GDPD4 functions within lipid and glycerophospholipid metabolic pathways.\n\nHPA expression reveals GDPD4 is tissue-enhanced with highest expression in testis (3.6 nTPM) and retina (1.4 nTPM), detected in some tissues. The protein shows cancer-enriched expression, suggesting potential dysregulation in tumors. GDPD4 is classified as a predicted membrane protein with hydrolase molecular function and cell type-enhanced specificity at the single-cell level.\n\nClinVar documents 8 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including large copy number gains (11p13-q25, 11q13.4-23.3, 11p15.5-q25 amplifications), copy number losses (11q13.5-22.1, 11q13.4-14.1 deletions), and an intronic SNV (c.1472+77T>G). The variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity. While no specific Mendelian disease is annotated, 11q CNVs are associated with developmental delay, intellectual disability, and congenital anomalies. Given GDPD4's role in lipid metabolism and testis/retina expression, dosage imbalance may affect spermatogenesis, photoreceptor function, or membrane lipid homeostasis.\n\nConservation is strong across vertebrates and plants: human (Q6W3E5, 623 aa), mouse (Q3TT99, 632 aa), macaque (Q95JR7, 627 aa), Arabidopsis (F4I8H8, 328 aa), indicating an ancient function in glycerophospholipid metabolism conserved from plants to mammals.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: GDPD4 functions as a membrane-associated glycerophosphodiester phosphodiesterase that hydrolyzes glycerophosphodiesters (such as glycerophosphocholine, glycerophosphoethanolamine, glycerophosphoinositol) into glycerol-3-phosphate and corresponding head groups. The TIM beta/alpha-barrel fold forms the catalytic core with conserved active site residues for phosphodiester bond cleavage. GDPD4 may regulate local lipid signaling by controlling glycerophosphodiester levels, which serve as second messengers or metabolic intermediates. The testis-enriched expression suggests roles in spermatogenesis, potentially in sperm membrane biogenesis or acrosome formation. Retina expression implies involvement in photoreceptor membrane turnover or lipid signaling. Cancer-enriched expression suggests GDPD4 may be upregulated in tumors to support membrane biosynthesis for rapid proliferation. Through MECR and GPD2 interactions, GDPD4 may coordinate glycerophospholipid metabolism with fatty acid elongation and glycerolipid synthesis. 11q CNVs cause GDPD4 dosage imbalance, disrupting lipid homeostasis and contributing to neurodevelopmental phenotypes. GDPD4 represents a candidate gene for lipid metabolism disorders and a potential biomarker for cancers with 11q alterations.
