## Description
FASTKD2 encodes a 710 amino acid mitochondrial protein (UniProt Q9NYY8), a critical regulator of mitochondrial RNA stability and oxidative phosphorylation complex assembly. With 53 publications, FASTKD2 sits at the boundary of characterized and dark genes—its disease association is established but mechanistic details remain incompletely understood.\n\nSTRUCTURAL FEATURES: AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (pLDDT 75.2) across all 710 residues, indicating a well-folded mitochondrial protein. InterPro analysis reveals a unique multi-domain architecture: FAST kinase leucine-rich domain (IPR010622, PF06743, residues 456-529) despite lacking catalytic kinase activity; FAST kinase-like subdomain 2 (IPR013579, PF08368, residues 572-615); and a C-terminal RAP domain (IPR013584, PF08373, PS51286, SM00952, residues 634-692) characteristic of RNA-binding proteins. The FAST kinase domain-containing family signature (IPR050870, PTHR21228, residues 154-696) spans most of the protein. GO annotations confirm regulation of mitochondrial mRNA stability (GO:0044528), RNA-binding, and rRNA-binding functions.\n\nINTERACTION NETWORK: STRING-DB identifies 10 interaction partners with medium-high confidence (0.420-0.893), defining FASTKD2's role in mitochondrial RNA processing complexes. This network positions FASTKD2 at the intersection of RNA modification, stability, and ribosome assembly.\n\nEXPRESSION & LOCALIZATION: HPA confirms ubiquitous expression detected in all tissues with low tissue and cancer specificity. Classified as disease-related gene with primary mitochondrial disease involvement.\n\nCLINICAL SIGNIFICANCE: ClinVar reports 101 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including frameshift deletions (c.1424_1425del p.Lys475fs, c.1021del p.Ser341fs), missense variants (c.1082T>C p.Ile361Thr, c.1655C>A p.Thr552Asn, c.632C>G p.Pro211Arg, c.823A>C p.Thr275Pro, c.1149C>G p.Ile383Met, c.1208T>C p.Leu403Pro), splice variants (c.1115-1G>T), and large copy number gains (2q33.3-37.3). These cause Combined Oxidative Phosphorylation Deficiency 44 (COXPD44, MIM# 618855), presenting as adult-onset mitochondrial podocytopathy, acute severe encephalomyopathy with early death, and new-onset refractory status epilepticus (NORSE). The phenotype variability suggests tissue-specific thresholds for mitochondrial dysfunction.\n\nFUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: FASTKD2 functions as a scaffold regulator coordinating mitochondrial RNA metabolism through three mechanisms: (1) RNA stability control—the RAP domain (residues 634-692) binds specific mitochondrial transcripts (possibly ND6, CYTB based on LRPPRC overlap), protecting them from degradation while recruiting pseudouridine synthases (TRUB2, RPUSD3/4) for tRNA modification. (2) Ribosome assembly coordination—interaction with DDX28 helicase suggests FASTKD2 facilitates mitochondrial ribosome large subunit assembly by positioning rRNA for proper folding. (3) OXPHOS complex biogenesis—by stabilizing mitochondrial mRNAs encoding Complex I, III, IV, and V subunits, FASTKD2 ensures coordinated synthesis of OXPHOS components. Mechanistically, despite the 'kinase' name, FASTKD2 lacks catalytic activity and instead uses its leucine-rich and RAP domains as protein-protein and RNA-protein interaction platforms. The high-confidence AlphaFold structure suggests stable domain packing enabling simultaneous binding of multiple partners. Loss-of-function causes tissue-specific OXPHOS failure: podocytes (high energy demand) develop focal segmental glomerulosclerosis; neurons experience bioenergetic crisis leading to encephalomyopathy and NORSE. Recent literature (2024) reveals DHEA downregulates FASTKD2 to promote lung adenocarcinoma apoptosis, suggesting cancer therapeutic potential.
