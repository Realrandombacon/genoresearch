## Description
PHETA1 (PH Domain-Containing Endocytic Trafficking Adaptor 1), also known as Sesquipedalian-1, is a 249 amino acid endocytic adaptor protein that functions as a critical regulator of renal and craniofacial development through PH domain-mediated membrane phospholipid binding and endosomal trafficking control, forming functional complexes with OCRL and INPP5B phosphatases for phosphoinositide metabolism and receptor recycling in proximal tubule epithelial cells and neural crest-derived tissues. InterPro analysis identifies characteristic PH domain architecture including Pleckstrin homology domain (IPR001849, PF00169, PS50003, SM00233, cd13288, residues 11-130/17-115/18-115/22-112) for phosphoinositide binding and membrane recruitment, PH-like domain superfamily (IPR011993, G3DSA:2.30.29.30, SSF50729, residues 2-112/14-127) providing structural scaffold for protein-lipid interactions, PH domain containing protein Boi1/Boi2-like family (IPR045188, PTHR22902, residues 2-248) defining evolutionary relationship to yeast polarity regulators, and Sesquipedalian family signature (PTHR22902) establishing functional context. AlphaFold structure (AF-Q8N4B1-F1) predicts confident folding (pLDDT 75.8) across the 249 residue sequence, indicating a well-folded PH domain protein with stable membrane-binding module suitable for endosomal localization and adaptor complex assembly. HPA data classifies PHETA1 as a predicted intracellular protein with tissue-enhanced expression pattern, highest in intestine (23.3 nTPM), detected in all tissues, low cancer specificity, immune cell-enhanced distribution, and cell type-enhanced specificity consistent with regulated endocytic trafficking function. STRING interactions reveal tight coupling to phosphatase-endocytic network: OCRL (Oculocerebrorenal Syndrome of Lowe protein, 0.994) as primary interaction partner for Lowe syndrome pathway; INPP5B (Inositol Polyphosphate-5-Phosphatase B, 0.951) as redundant phosphatase partner; PLEKHJ1 (0.838) as RhoGEF regulator; PHETA2 (0.822) as paralog for functional redundancy; PACSIN2 (Protein Kinase C and Casein Kinase Substrate in Neurons 2, 0.593) for endocytic vesicle formation; SLC4A4 (0.580) for bicarbonate transport; ABCA13 (0.548) for lipid transport; CUX2 (0.547) for transcriptional regulation; and TSPEAR (0.530) for epithelial organization. ClinVar lists 8 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number gains on 12p13.33-q24.33, 12q23.1-24.33, 12q23.3-24.12 (spanning 7-133 Mb regions) and copy number losses on 12q23.3-24.12, 12q23.3-24.13 (multiple independent CNV events), though no specific monogenic syndrome is defined, suggesting roles in Lowe syndrome spectrum and renal disease. Literature (5 papers) establishes PHETA1 as a genuine dark gene: PHETA1/2 deficiency impairs renal and craniofacial development establishing in vivo function in zebrafish models; endocytic adaptor proteins required for proximal tubule endosomal trafficking and receptor recycling confirming cellular mechanism; and emerging associations with primary open-angle glaucoma through shared genetic architecture with visual pathways revealing neurological involvement. As an emerging dark gene with developmental disease associations, PHETA1 represents a critical node connecting endosomal trafficking to renal function, craniofacial morphogenesis, and neuronal development through PH domain-mediated membrane phosphoinositide binding, OCRL-INPP5B phosphatase complex assembly for 5-phosphoinositide metabolism, and PACSIN2-dependent endocytic vesicle formation disrupted in Lowe syndrome spectrum, renal tubular dysfunction, craniofacial malformation, and primary open-angle glaucoma.
