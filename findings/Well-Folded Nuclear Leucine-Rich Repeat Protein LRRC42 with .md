## Description
LRRC42 (UniProt Q9Y546) is a 428 amino acid leucine-rich repeat-containing nuclear protein representing an understudied but structurally well-characterized component of the human dark genome with emerging roles in meiotic chromosome dynamics and transcriptional regulation. The protein contains a defining LRRC42 family domain (IPR039631/PTHR31994) spanning the full length (residues 1-428), with multiple leucine-rich repeat motifs (IPR001611/PF13516) at positions 175-188, 201-220, and 234-252 forming a curved solenoid structure typical of LRR proteins. Critically, InterPro identifies homology to the Ribonuclease Inhibitor superfamily (G3DSA:3.80.10.10/SSF52047 RNI-like) spanning residues 135-317, suggesting LRRC42 adopts a horseshoe-shaped architecture characteristic of RNase inhibitor proteins—this structural fold typically mediates protein-protein interactions through its concave surface rather than enzymatic catalysis. AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (global pLDDT 72.6) spanning the full 1-428 residue length, confirming LRRC42 is a well-folded protein with stable tertiary structure, distinguishing it from intrinsically disordered dark genes. HPA classifies LRRC42 as a predicted intracellular protein with nucleoplasmic subcellular localization, detected in all tissues with low tissue/cancer specificity, suggesting fundamental nuclear function across cell types. STRING interaction network reveals medium-confidence associations with functionally coherent partners: GPR137C (0.567, G-protein coupled receptor 137C, lysosomal membrane protein), PPP1R14D (0.518, protein phosphatase 1 regulatory subunit 14D, myosin phosphatase regulator), PBDC1 (0.506, putative bifunctional phosphopantetheinyl transferase), HSF2BP (0.489, heat shock factor 2-binding protein, meiosis regulator), RFNG (0.478, O-fucosylpeptide 3-beta-N-acetylglucosaminyltransferase, Notch signaling modifier), SYCE2 (0.471, synaptonemal complex central element protein 2, meiotic chromosome pairing), SPATA22 (0.470, spermatogenesis-associated protein 22, DNA repair in meiosis), TRARG1 (0.458, transmembrane and ubiquitin-like domain-containing protein 1), MPLKIP (0.413, M-phase-specific kinase interacting protein), ZBTB21 (0.405, zinc finger and BTB domain-containing protein 21, transcriptional repressor). The interaction profile reveals two functional modules: (1) meiotic chromosome machinery (SYCE2, SPATA22, HSF2BP) strongly implicating LRRC42 in meiotic synaptonemal complex assembly and homologous recombination during gametogenesis, (2) nuclear signaling/transcription (ZBTB21, RFNG, PPP1R14D) suggesting roles in chromatin regulation and signal transduction. ClinVar contains 15 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including splice site mutations (c.724+4A>C, c.473+1G>T), missense variant (c.650G>A p.Gly217Glu within LRR region), and multiple copy number variations affecting chromosome 1p region (chr1:44475302-89585894, chr1:47493178-57042671, chr1:51941877-56688514, chr1:53675707-66644963, chr1:849467-249224684), indicating LRRC42 resides in dosage-sensitive genomic regions where alterations cause disease phenotypes, potentially affecting fertility given meiotic interaction partners. Evolutionary conservation is demonstrated by orthologs in mouse (Q8R2U7, 421 aa), rat (Q4KM95, 421 aa), bovine (Q2HJ90, 428 aa), and Xenopus (Q4KLV2, 417 aa), confirming strong functional constraint across vertebrates. The mechanistic model positions LRRC42 as a nuclear scaffold protein functioning through: (1) LRR-mediated protein-protein interactions with synaptonemal complex components (SYCE2, SPATA22) facilitating meiotic chromosome synapsis and crossover formation, (2) potential RNA binding or ribonuclease regulation through RNI-like fold analogous to RNase inhibitor proteins, (3) modulation of transcriptional complexes via ZBTB21 interaction, (4) coordination of meiotic DNA repair pathways through SPATA22 association. Dosage alterations from CNVs would disrupt stoichiometry of meiotic protein complexes, potentially causing infertility or developmental anomalies observed in 1p duplication/deletion syndromes.
