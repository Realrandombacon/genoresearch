## Description
CCDC78 (Gene ID: 55787, UniProt A2IDD5) is a 438 amino acid coiled-coil domain-containing protein located on chromosome 16p13.3 (NC_000016.10). InterPro domain analysis reveals a Domain of Unknown Function 4472 (DUF4472, IPR029329/PF14739, positions 56-165) embedded within the CCDC78-specific family region (IPR039873/PTHR22106, positions 1-436). While DUF4472's molecular function remains uncharacterized, its conservation across vertebrates suggests important structural or interaction roles. AlphaFold structure prediction (AF-A2IDD5-F1) yields a medium-confidence model (global pLDDT 65.7), consistent with a predominantly coiled-coil architecture suitable for scaffolding functions in multiprotein complexes. Human Protein Atlas classifies CCDC78 as a predicted intracellular protein involved in cilium biogenesis/degradation, with tissue-enhanced expression highest in fallopian tube (28.2 nTPM) and brain (11.1 nTPM); it is cancer-enriched and group-enriched at single-cell resolution. The fallopian tube expression is highly informative—this tissue contains multiciliated epithelial cells essential for oocyte transport. STRING database identifies 10 interaction partners overwhelmingly enriched for cilia and muscle-related proteins: DEUP1 (score 0.746, deuterosome protein critical for centriole amplification in multiciliogenesis), MCIDAS (0.604, master regulator of multiciliated cell differentiation), MTMR14 (0.643) and MTM1 (0.596, myotubularin phosphatases mutated in centronuclear myopathies), KBTBD13 (0.619, kelch protein linked to nemaline myopathy), BICDL2 (0.595, coiled-coil protein), SELENON (0.592, selenoprotein N essential for muscle function), BBOF1 (0.584, basal body orientation factor), MEGF10 (0.543, satellite cell regulator in muscle), and RYR1 (0.534, ryanodine receptor for calcium release in muscle contraction). This interaction network strongly implicates CCDC78 in two interconnected processes: multiciliogenesis (via DEUP1/MCIDAS/BBOF1) and muscle biology (via MTM1/SELENON/RYR1/MEGF10). ClinVar lists 72 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including a truncating nonsense mutation (TRP402TER, rs752371476), splice site mutations (c.1053+1G>A), and large copy number losses/gains spanning 16p13.3; no specific monogenic disease is yet attributed solely to CCDC78, but dosage sensitivity of the 16p13.3 region is linked to developmental delays and myopathy phenotypes. Conservation is evident across mammals (mouse D3Z5T1, 99% identity expected), amphibians (Xenopus Q66KE8), and even mollusks (Mytilus edulis A0A8S3R1I3). Functional hypothesis: CCDC78 functions as a scaffolding protein that stabilizes the deuterosome complex during centriole amplification in multiciliated cells, potentially bridging DEUP1-MCIDAS interactions. The DUF4472 domain may mediate specific protein-protein contacts. In muscle, CCDC78 may localize to the sarcoplasmic reticulum or neuromuscular junction, coordinating calcium signaling (RYR1) with membrane trafficking (myotubularins). Disease mechanism: Loss-of-function mutations disrupt multiciliogenesis in fallopian tube (contributing to infertility) and respiratory epithelium (potentially causing primary ciliary dyskinesia-like phenotypes), while muscle defects may cause congenital myopathy. The TRP402TER truncation would eliminate C-terminal interaction surfaces. Priority experiments include immunofluorescence colocalization with DEUP1/centrin in multiciliated cells, co-IP validation of DEUP1/MCIDAS/MTM1 interactions, CRISPR knockout in airway epithelial differentiation models, and investigation of CCDC78 mutations in ciliopathy/myopathy patient cohorts.
