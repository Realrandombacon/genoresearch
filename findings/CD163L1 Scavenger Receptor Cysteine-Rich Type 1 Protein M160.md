## Description
CD163L1 (CD163 Molecule Like 1, also known as Scavenger Receptor Cysteine-Rich Type 1 Protein M160) is a partially characterized scavenger receptor with 27 publications, emerging as a marker of inflammatory macrophage subsets critical for atherosclerosis progression and intestinal inflammation. This large 1453-amino acid protein (UniProt Q9NR16) features a modular extracellular architecture optimized for ligand recognition and immune signaling. InterPro analysis reveals three tandem SRCR (Scavenger Receptor Cysteine-Rich) domains (IPR001190/PF00530/SM00202, positions 48-148/155-255/262-362) with conserved signature motifs (PS00420, positions 53-90/160-197/267-304) characteristic of pattern recognition receptors binding modified lipoproteins and pathogens. Additional domains include SRCR-like domain superfamily (G3DSA:3.10.250.10/IPR036772/SSF56487, positions 41-151/152-260/259-365) and membership in the scavenger receptor cysteine-rich domain-containing protein family (PTHR19331, positions 793-1136). The MRC2 interaction (0.542) positions CD163L1 in endocytic clearance pathways. CD68 connection (0.444) validates macrophage-specific function. IL10RA interaction (0.411) suggests anti-inflammatory cytokine regulation.\n\nHPA shows tissue-enhanced expression with top levels in lymphoid tissue (30.0 nTPM) and intestine (20.6 nTPM), consistent with specialized roles in immune surveillance and mucosal inflammation. Subcellular localization includes nucleoplasm, centrosome, and cytosol—suggesting potential intracellular signaling functions beyond surface receptor activity. Protein class includes CD markers and predicted membrane/intracellular proteins. Cell type enhanced specificity indicates restricted expression in specific immune populations. Conservation across vertebrates indicates functional constraint on SRCR domain architecture.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: CD163L1 functions as a pattern recognition scavenger receptor that discriminates inflammatory macrophage subsets and modulates immune responses through ligand-dependent signaling and epigenetic regulation. Mechanistically: (1) The triple SRCR domain array binds modified lipoproteins (oxidized LDL, acetylated LDL) and pathogen-associated molecular patterns, triggering endocytic clearance similar to CD163 hemoglobin-haptoglobin uptake. (2) In atherosclerosis, homocysteine alters m6A methylation of CD163L1 transcript (2024 breakthrough), shifting macrophage polarization toward pro-inflammatory M1 phenotype and accelerating plaque formation. (3) In diverticulitis, CD163L1+CXCL10+ macrophages accumulate in colonic lamina propria (2021 study), where CD163L1 may recognize bacterial components and drive CXCL10 chemokine production recruiting T cells to inflamed tissue. (4) IL10RA interaction suggests CD163L1 modulates anti-inflammatory IL-10 signaling—loss may impair resolution of inflammation. (5) MRC2 connection indicates shared endocytic pathway for collagen turnover and extracellular matrix remodeling in vascular/intestinal tissues. (6) Nucleoplasmic localization hints at transcriptional regulatory function, potentially through SRCR domain nuclear translocation affecting inflammatory gene expression. (7) Centrosome association suggests roles in macrophage polarization during cell division or migration. (8) 12p CNVs affecting CD163L1 dosage may alter macrophage responsiveness in neurodevelopmental contexts.
