## Description
KCTD10 (Potassium Channel Tetramerization Domain-Containing Protein 10, also known as BCAR3 or CUL3 adapter for RhoA degradation) is a partially characterized E3 ubiquitin ligase substrate adapter with 69 publications, emerging as a critical node at the intersection of DNA replication stress response, Wnt signaling regulation, and cytoskeletal dynamics. This compact 313-amino acid nuclear/cytosolic protein (UniProt Q9H3F6) features a bipartite domain architecture optimized for CRL3 complex assembly. InterPro analysis reveals an N-terminal BTB/POZ domain (IPR000210/IPR003131/PF02214/PS50097, positions 32-132) that mediates homooligomerization and CUL3 binding, a potassium channel tetramerisation-type BTB domain (G3DSA:3.30.710.10, positions 25-135), and a C-terminal KCTD10/TNFAIP1/KCTD13 domain (IPR063516/PF31104, positions 137-290) responsible for substrate recognition. AlphaFold predicts high-confidence structure (pLDDT 82.2), indicating a well-folded two-domain protein with rigid interdomain orientation.\n\nSTRING interactions confirm KCTD10 operates as a core CUL3-RBX1 E3 ligase adapter: CUL3 (0.996, cullin-3 scaffold), UBXN1 (0.995, UBX domain protein regulating ubiquitin pathways), UBXN7 (0.995, paralog of UBXN1), FAF1 (0.994, Fas-associated factor 1 in ubiquitin-proteasome system), KCTD13 (0.979, paralogous KCTD family member), POLD2 (0.798, DNA polymerase delta subunit 2 linking to replication), KCTD17 (0.792, another KCTD paralog), USP25 (0.782, deubiquitinating enzyme), TNFAIP1 (0.667, TPA-induced protein 1 with similar BTB architecture), and RBX1 (0.438, RING-box protein 1 E3 ligase component). The exceptionally high confidence link to CUL3 (0.996) positions KCTD10 as a dedicated CRL3 adapter, while POLD2 interaction suggests direct coupling to DNA replication machinery.\n\nHPA shows low tissue specificity with expression detected in all tissues, consistent with essential housekeeping function in genome maintenance and signal transduction. Subcellular localization includes nucleoplasm and cytosol, enabling dual roles in nuclear DNA metabolism and cytoplasmic signaling. Biological processes involve Ubl (ubiquitin-like) conjugation pathway. ClinVar contains 11 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including missense mutations in the C-terminal substrate-binding domain (p.Asn169Ser, p.Arg248Gln) and large copy number losses/gains spanning chromosome 12q23-q24, though specific monogenic diseases remain incompletely characterized—2025 studies propose de novo KCTD10 variants as causes of multiple congenital anomalies. Conservation across mouse (Kctd10, 315 aa) and rat indicates strong functional constraint.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: KCTD10 functions as a dual-purpose CRL3 adapter that coordinates genome stability with signal transduction through targeted proteolysis. Mechanistically: (1) In DNA replication, KCTD10 acts as a sensor for co-directional transcription-replication conflicts (TRCs)—when RNA polymerase and DNA polymerase collide on the same strand, KCTD10 recruits CUL3-RBX1 to ubiquitinate conflict-resolution factors, enabling replisome progression and preventing replication fork collapse (2025 breakthrough). The POLD2 interaction positions KCTD10 at active replication forks. (2) In Wnt signaling, KCTD10 targets β-catenin for proteasomal degradation independent of the canonical destruction complex, suppressing epithelial-mesenchymal transition and metastasis in lung cancer (2025). (3) In cytoskeletal regulation, KCTD10 mediates RhoA GTPase degradation, controlling cell migration and vascular tone—relevant to coronary atherosclerosis pathogenesis (2025). The BTB domain dimerizes to create a bivalent CUL3-binding platform, while the C-terminal domain recognizes degron motifs on diverse substrates. Missense variants in the C-terminal domain likely impair substrate recognition without disrupting CUL3 binding, causing dominant-negative effects on specific pathways. Large 12q CNVs cause congenital anomalies through haploinsufficiency affecting multiple genes including KCTD10, disrupting embryonic patterning via aberrant Wnt and RhoA signaling.
