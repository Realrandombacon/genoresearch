## Description
MAPKBP1 (Mitogen-Activated Protein Kinase Binding Protein 1, UniProt O60336) is a 1514 aa intracellular scaffold protein that functions as a negative regulator of NOD2-mediated innate immunity and a positive regulator of JNK (c-Jun N-terminal kinase) signaling. Structurally, AlphaFold predicts a partially ordered fold (pLDDT=62.0) featuring two large WD40-repeat domains (IPR001680, PF24780 aa 53-386; PF24782 aa 392-732) within the YVTN repeat-like superfamily (G3DSA:2.130.10.10, IPR015943), plus a C-terminal coiled-coil domain (IPR056364, PF24795, aa 1413-1508) that mediates dimerization with WDR62. Functionally, STRING interactions reveal connections to JNK pathway components MAPK9 (0.534) and MAPK8 (0.482), the transcription factor JUN (0.491), the ciliopathy protein ANKS6 (0.488), and nephronophthisis-associated NPHP1 (0.418), positioning MAPKBP1 at the intersection of stress signaling and ciliary function. HPA shows brain-enriched expression (69.6 nTPM) with nucleoplasmic and nucleolar rim localization, consistent with roles in transcriptional regulation and cell cycle control. ClinVar identifies 39 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including missense mutations (p.Ser1112Leu, p.Ser468Phe, p.Gly972Val, p.His765Tyr, p.Ala245Val) and splice site variants, causing autosomal recessive nephronophthisis (NPHP20). Literature (24 papers) reveals emerging mechanistic insights: MAPKBP1 recruits to centrioles via WDR62 and CEP170 for pericentriolar material cohesion and mitotic spindle formation (2026), regulates cilia-JNK signaling in kidney disease (2025), and requires dimerization for proper centriolar recruitment. The gene represents a 'semi-dark' scaffold protein where canonical JNK/NOD2 regulation is established, but specific mechanisms linking WD40-mediated protein interactions to ciliary dysfunction, mitotic defects, and tubulointerstitial fibrosis in nephronophthisis remain underexplored therapeutic targets.
