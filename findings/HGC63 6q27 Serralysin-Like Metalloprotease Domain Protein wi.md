## Description
HGC6.3 (Gene ID: 100128124, UniProt Q9UM08) is a 171 amino acid protein located on chromosome 6q27 (NC_000006.12, positions 167975917-167979792, ~3.9kb region). Despite having a UniProt entry, this protein remains functionally uncharacterized. InterPro domain analysis reveals a serralysin-like metalloprotease C-terminal domain (IPR011049, G3DSA:2.150.10.10, positions 10-140/43-140) and an adhesin YadA collagen-binding domain (SSF101967, positions 10-120). Serralysins are bacterial metalloproteases that cleave extracellular matrix components, but the human homolog lacks the catalytic HEXXH zinc-binding motif typical of active metalloproteases, suggesting this may function as a substrate-binding module rather than an enzyme. The collagen-binding domain indicates potential extracellular matrix interactions. AlphaFold structure prediction (AF-Q9UM08-F1) shows medium confidence (global pLDDT 61.0), indicating a partially structured protein with both ordered domains and flexible regions. Human Protein Atlas could not resolve HGC6.3 to an Ensembl ID, suggesting limited or tissue-restricted expression. STRING database returns no interaction partners, consistent with poor characterization. ClinVar contains 25 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number variations affecting 6q27 including deletions (GRCh38/hg38 6q27 chr6:167201522-170610382)x1 and duplications (6q25.2-27 gains). The 6q27 region is associated with developmental disorders when disrupted - deletions cause intellectual disability, growth retardation, cerebellar hypoplasia, and distinctive facial features. Conservation data needed but bacterial domain homology suggests ancient evolutionary origin. Functional hypothesis: HGC6.3 functions as an extracellular matrix-associated protein that binds collagen through its YadA-like domain, potentially serving as a scaffold for cell-matrix adhesion or modulating extracellular protease activity through its serralysin-like domain. The lack of catalytic residues suggests it may act as a decoy receptor or competitive inhibitor of metalloproteases, regulating ECM remodeling during development. Disease mechanism: Haploinsufficiency from 6q27 deletions disrupts ECM organization or cell-matrix signaling critical for neurodevelopment, contributing to intellectual disability and cerebellar defects. Alternatively, HGC6.3 may regulate availability of growth factors sequestered in ECM. Priority experiments include determination of actual subcellular localization (secreted vs membrane-bound), identification of binding partners via co-immunoprecipitation, assessment of collagen binding affinity, investigation of expression patterns in developing brain, and genotype-phenotype correlation in patients with focal 6q27 rearrangements.
