# NRBP1: Nuclear Receptor Binding Protein 1 Pseudokinase Orchestrating WNK Kinase Signaling, L1 Retrotransposition Control, and Renal Electrolyte Homeostasis via TSC22D Scaffold Assembly and Elongin Complex Recruitment in Neurodevelopment and Cancer Progression

**Confidence:** MEDIUM

## Description
NRBP1 (Nuclear Receptor Binding Protein 1) is a 535 amino acid cytosolic pseudokinase that functions as a critical regulatory node in WNK (With-No-Lysine) kinase signaling pathways, controlling cation chloride cotransporter activity for renal electrolyte homeostasis, L1 retrotransposition suppression, and neuronal function through scaffolding interactions with TSC22D family proteins and Elongin complex recruitment despite lacking catalytic kinase activity. InterPro analysis identifies characteristic pseudokinase architecture including N-terminal pseudokinase domain (cd14034, residues 58-334) with conserved kinase-like fold but disrupted catalytic motifs, protein kinase domain signatures (IPR000719, IPR001245, PF07714, PS50011, residues 68-327) retaining ATP binding capability (GO:0005524) without phosphotransferase activity, serine-threonine/tyrosine-protein kinase catalytic domain homology (IPR001245, residues 106-321) with protein phosphorylation structural framework (GO:0006468), protein kinase-like superfamily (IPR011009, SSF56112, residues 80-336) providing stable fold, and WNK Ser/Thr protein kinase family classification (IPR050588, PTHR13902, residues 43-524) defining functional context. AlphaFold structure (AF-Q9UHY1-F1) predicts confident folding (pLDDT 75.2) across the 535 residue sequence, indicating a stable pseudokinase with intact structural scaffold suitable for protein-protein interactions despite catalytic inactivation. HPA data classifies NRBP1 as an essential intracellular enzyme with ubiquitous expression (detected in all tissues), low tissue/cancer specificity, cytosolic localization reflecting signaling complex assembly, and cell type-enhanced distribution consistent with housekeeping regulatory function. STRING interactions reveal tight functional coupling to WNK pathway regulators and transcriptional control machinery: TSC22D1 (TSC22 Domain Family Member 1, 0.891) as primary scaffold partner; ELOC (Elongin-C, 0.813) and ELOB (Elongin-B, 0.799) as ubiquitin ligase adaptors; TSC22D3 (0.709) and TSC22D4 (0.703) as related scaffold proteins; NRBP2 (0.709) as paralog with opposing L1 retrotransposition function; MLF1 (Myeloid Leukemia Factor 1, 0.708) for hematopoietic regulation; KRTCAP3 (Keratinocyte Associated Protein 3, 0.626) for epithelial function; CCDC121 (Coiled-Coil Domain Containing 121, 0.617) for ciliary/centrosomal roles; and TSC22D2 (0.438) completing the scaffold network. ClinVar lists 15 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number gains on 2p25.3-23.1, 2p24.1-22.2, 2p25.3-22.3, 2p25.3-q37.3, 2p23.3-21 (multiple independent CNV events spanning 17-242 Mb regions) and copy number loss on 2p25.1-q13, though no specific monogenic syndrome is defined, suggesting potential roles in neurodevelopmental disorder or renal electrolyte disorder susceptibility. Literature (74 papers) establishes NRBP1 as an emerging WNK pathway regulator: NRBP1 and TSC22D proteins modulate distal convoluted tubule physiology through WNK pathway affecting NCC (NaCl cotransporter) activity and blood pressure control; NRBP1 silencing improves cognitive function and pathological features in Alzheimer's disease rat model linking to neuroprotection; NRBP1 and NRBP2 play opposing roles in L1 retrotransposition control revealing paralog functional divergence; TRIM24 promotes gastric cancer proliferation and metastasis via NRBP1 ubiquitination establishing oncogenic role; and NRBP1 functions as pseudokinase scaffold rather than catalytic enzyme in WNK signaling complexes. As a well-characterized pseudokinase with emerging disease associations, NRBP1 represents a critical node connecting WNK kinase signaling to renal electrolyte handling, retrotransposon silencing, neurodegeneration, and cancer progression through TSC22D-dependent scaffold assembly and Elongin-mediated protein stability control.
