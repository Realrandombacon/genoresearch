# IQCH: Testis-Enriched IQ Motif/ATP-Grasp Protein Regulating Sperm Midpiece Energy Metabolism and Fertilization via Calmodulin-Dependent LDHA Interaction

**Quality Score:** 4.75/10  (E=7.4, D=2.1) [MODERATE]

## Description
IQCH (IQ Motif Containing H, also NYDSP5) is a 1027 amino acid intracellular protein (UniProt Q86VS3) with a well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 78.0, high confidence), localized to the sperm midpiece and nuclear speckles where it functions in fertilization and lncRNA transcription through calmodulin-dependent protein interactions and potential ATP-dependent enzymatic activity.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals IQCH contains an N-terminal IQ motif (PS50096, residues 369-398) that binds calmodulin in a calcium-dependent manner, and a central IQCH-like ATP-grasp domain (IPR056855/PF24923, residues 600-866) characteristic of ATP-dependent ligase or kinase activities. The protein defines the IQ Domain-Containing Protein H family (IPR038752/PTHR14465, residues 1-1023/1-1023). The IQ motif adopts an amphipathic alpha-helix that interacts with calmodulin's hydrophobic pockets, enabling calcium-regulated conformational changes. The ATP-grasp fold typically coordinates ATP between two lobes for phosphoryl transfer reactions.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals IQCH functions within sperm energy metabolism and testis-specific networks: strongest interactions with LDHA (0.774, lactate dehydrogenase A, glycolytic enzyme critical for sperm motility), TESMIN (0.459, testis-expressed metal-binding protein, spermatogenesis), C15orf61 (0.425, chromosome 15 open reading frame 61, testis-specific), and SPATA16 (0.400, spermatogenesis-associated protein 16, acrosome formation). The LDHA interaction is particularly significant as LDHA provides ATP through anaerobic glycolysis in sperm midpiece mitochondria.\n\nHPA expression shows testis-enhanced specificity (10.8 nTPM) with secondary expression in fallopian tube (6.4 nTPM), consistent with dual roles in spermatogenesis and female reproductive tract function. Subcellular localization includes vesicles, cytosol, and sperm midpiece. The protein is classified as predicted intracellular with cell type-enhanced single cell specificity.\n\nClinVar documents 16 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including a splice site deletion (c.1457-3del), chromosome 15q22.33-26.1 copy number gains, and 15q22.31-23 deletions. These CNVs suggest dosage sensitivity potentially causing male infertility, fertilization failure, or developmental disorders through impaired sperm energy metabolism.\n\nConservation is evident from mouse (Q9D2K4, 1071 aa) and zebrafish (Q1LXZ7, 1059 aa) orthologs, indicating ancient evolutionary conservation of fertilization mechanisms.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: IQCH acts as a calcium-regulated scaffold/enzyme in the sperm midpiece that couples energy metabolism to motility through LDHA interaction. The IQ motif binds calmodulin upon calcium influx during sperm capacitation, triggering conformational changes that activate the ATP-grasp domain. This domain may phosphorylate LDHA or other glycolytic enzymes to enhance ATP production during hyperactivation. Alternatively, IQCH may function as an ATP-dependent ligase modifying midpiece structural proteins. TESMIN interaction suggests metal ion coordination (possibly zinc or copper) for redox regulation. Nuclear speckle localization indicates additional roles in lncRNA transcription during spermatogenesis, potentially regulating meiotic gene expression. C15orf61 and SPATA16 partnerships link IQCH to acrosome biogenesis and sperm-egg recognition. The 15q23 chromosomal location places IQCH near other reproductive genes. CNV-mediated haploinsufficiency disrupts sperm midpiece organization, reducing ATP production and causing asthenozoospermia or complete fertilization failure. The fallopian tube expression suggests IQCH may also function in oocyte maturation or early embryogenesis.
