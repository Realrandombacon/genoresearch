## Description
CELA2A (Chymotrypsin-Like Elastase Family Member 2A) is a 269-amino acid secreted serine protease (UniProt P08217) representing a genuine dark gene with only 23 publications despite its extraordinary pancreas-specific expression and emerging roles in metabolic syndrome, diabetes, and cardiovascular disease. Structurally, CELA2A belongs to the peptidase S1 family with a canonical trypsin-like serine protease fold: trypsin domain (IPR001254/PF00089/cd00190, residues 28-267/29-262/29-265), chymotrypsin family signatures (IPR001314/PR00722, residues 59-74/117-131/209-221), PA clan peptidase superfamily (IPR009003/G3DSA:2.40.10.10, residues 14-268), and conserved catalytic triad residues including the histidine active site (IPR018114/PS00134, residues 69-74) and serine active site (IPR033116, residues 210-221). The elastase subfamily classification (IPR050850, residues 5-266) indicates substrate specificity for small hydrophobic residues. AlphaFold predicts exceptional structure confidence (pLDDT 93.4), consistent with a well-folded secreted enzyme with stable catalytic architecture. Functionally, CELA2A shows remarkable pancreas-specific expression at 153,459.5 nTPM (HPA: tissue enriched, detected in many, cancer enriched, cell type enriched)—among the highest tissue-specific expression values observed—classified as a secreted hydrolase/protease with disease involvement in diabetes mellitus and obesity. STRING interactions reveal connections to pancreatic enzymes and metabolic regulators: KLK5 (0.831, kallikrein-related peptidase), CEL (0.818, carboxyl ester lipase/cholesterol esterase), PNLIPRP1 (0.798, pancreatic lipase-related protein), CPA1 (0.769)/CPA2 (0.528)/CPB1 (0.734) (carboxypeptidases A1/A2/B1), CELA3A (0.726)/CELA3B (0.660) (elastase paralogs), PPY (0.654, pancreatic polypeptide hormone), and ELN (0.634, elastin substrate). This interaction network positions CELA2A within the pancreatic exocrine enzyme machinery with potential endocrine cross-talk. Critically, ClinVar documents 29 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including nonsense mutations (c.572G>A p.Trp191Ter), splice site mutations (c.639+1G>C), missense variants (c.209C>T p.Thr70Met, c.253C>A p.Leu85Met), and large copy number alterations spanning 1p36 (multiple CNV losses/gains/deletions/duplications affecting megabase regions), yet ZERO diseases are formally associated—a profound gap given the metabolic phenotypes. Recent literature establishes emerging metabolic roles: CELA2A loss-of-function mutations drive impaired pancreatic islet development and function associated with metabolic syndrome and diabetes (2025); opposing gastric and jejunal CELA2A regulation in obesity and after Roux-en-Y gastric bypass suggests gastrointestinal metabolic signaling roles (2026); and landmark 2019 studies (37 citations) demonstrated CELA2A mutations predispose to early-onset atherosclerosis and metabolic syndrome, affecting plasma insulin levels and platelet activation. The mechanistic hypothesis: CELA2A functions as a pancreatic elastase that processes peptide hormones or extracellular matrix components critical for islet architecture and insulin secretion. Beyond classical exocrine digestive functions, CELA2A may cleave circulating peptides regulating glucose homeostasis, process pro-hormones affecting insulin/glucagon balance, or remodel extracellular matrix surrounding islets to facilitate hormone release. The elastin interaction suggests CELA2A may regulate vascular elasticity contributing to atherosclerosis susceptibility. Through PPY (pancreatic polypeptide) interactions, CELA2A could modulate appetite/satiety signaling linking exocrine and endocrine pancreas function. Platelet activation effects suggest CELA2A may process coagulation factors or platelet agonists, explaining cardiovascular phenotypes. In obesity and post-bariatric surgery contexts, altered CELA2A expression may mediate metabolic improvements through gut-pancreas signaling axes. The absence of formal disease associations despite 29 pathogenic variants suggests CELA2A mutations contribute to common complex diseases (type 2 diabetes, metabolic syndrome, coronary artery disease) rather than Mendelian disorders, or that phenotypes manifest as quantitative traits (insulin resistance, lipid abnormalities) not captured by traditional disease classifications. Future work should identify CELA2A physiological substrates, define mechanisms linking elastase activity to islet development, and screen patients with early-onset metabolic syndrome or familial hypercholesterolemia for CELA2A mutations.
