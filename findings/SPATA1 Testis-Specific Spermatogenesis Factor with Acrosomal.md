# SPATA1: Testis-Specific Spermatogenesis Factor with Acrosomal Localization and Disease-Associated CNVs

## Description
SPATA1 (spermatogenesis associated 1) encodes a 459 amino acid protein (UniProt Q5VX52) specifically expressed in testis (HPA: 1.0 nTPM) with subcellular localization to plasma membrane and cytosol. The protein contains a conserved C-terminal spermatogenesis domain (InterPro IPR031478, residues 301-450; Pfam PF15743) and belongs to the SPATA1 family (IPR039062, PTHR14421). AlphaFold prediction shows medium confidence structure (pLDDT 68.4), suggesting partially ordered domains with potentially disordered regions. ClinVar documents 17 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly copy number losses and gains spanning 1p22.3-22.1, indicating dosage sensitivity. The testis-specific expression pattern, acrosomal vesicle localization prediction, and conserved spermatogenesis domain strongly implicate SPATA1 in acrosome formation during spermatid differentiation. The high burden of pathogenic CNVs suggests haploinsufficiency may cause male infertility phenotypes. Mechanistically, SPATA1 likely functions as a scaffold protein recruiting intraflagellar transport or acrosomal biogenesis machinery to the developing acrosomal vesicle, analogous to IFT20 (a STRING homolog) which traffics Golgi-derived vesicles to the acrosome. The medium-confidence AlphaFold structure with defined C-terminal domain supports a model where the structured C-terminus mediates protein-protein interactions while N-terminal regions may be intrinsically disordered for regulatory flexibility.
