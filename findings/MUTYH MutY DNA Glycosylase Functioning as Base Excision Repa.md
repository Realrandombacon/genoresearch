## Description
MUTYH (MutY DNA glycosylase, also known as Adenine DNA glycosylase, Q9UIF7) encodes a 546 aa nuclear enzyme functioning as a critical component of the base excision repair (BER) pathway that specifically recognizes and excises adenine misincorporated opposite 7,8-dihydro-8-oxoguanine (8-oxoG), thereby preventing G:C to T:A transversion mutations that drive carcinogenesis and aging. Structural architecture reveals a well-folded organization with high AlphaFold confidence (pLDDT 78.9): N-terminal Helix-hairpin-Helix (HhH) domain (IPR003265/IPR004036/G3DSA:1.10.1670.10, residues 125-285/196-226/92-306) containing the catalytic pocket for DNA binding and adenine excision with conserved HhH motif (IPR000445, residues 194-223) for DNA backbone interaction, [4Fe-4S] iron-sulfur cluster binding domain (IPR003651/IPR004035, residues 286-306/287-303) essential for redox sensing and DNA charge transport facilitating lesion scanning, C-terminal NUDIX hydrolase domain (IPR000086/G3DSA:3.90.79.10, residues 364-495/361-500) potentially regulating enzyme activity or interacting with nucleotide pools, and C-terminal domain of DNA glycosylase (cd03431, residues 364-491) completing the catalytic module. STRING interactions define a high-confidence DNA repair network: Nudix hydrolase 1 NUDT1 (0.928, highest) as 8-oxo-dGTP sanitizing enzyme preventing incorporation, DNA polymerase delta catalytic subunit POLD1 (0.890) as replicative polymerase encountering lesions, MutS homolog 2 MSH2 (0.731) and MutL homolog 1 MLH1 (0.619) as mismatch repair proteins coordinating with BER, Postmeiotic segregation increased 2 PMS2 (0.614) as mismatch repair endonuclease, 8-oxoguanine DNA glycosylase OGG1 (0.573) as complementary glycosylase removing 8-oxoG opposite C, MutS homolog 6 MSH6 (0.568) as mismatch recognition protein, AP endonuclease 1 APEX1 (0.459) as downstream BER enzyme cleaving abasic sites, Endonuclease III-like 1 NTHL1 (0.410) as paralogous glycosylase, and Checkpoint protein RAD9A (0.403) as cell cycle checkpoint regulator. HPA classifies MUTYH as cancer-related/disease-related/enzyme/hydrolase/glycosidase with DNA damage/repair biological processes; low tissue specificity with detection in all tissues indicates ubiquitous housekeeping function in genome maintenance, low cancer specificity suggests constitutive expression though loss leads to cancer, low immune cell specificity confirms non-immune primary function, cell type-enhanced single cell specificity reflects variable expression levels, and subcellular localization to nucleoplasm confirms direct access to genomic DNA. ClinVar documents an exceptional 2,620 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including frameshift deletions c.1105del (p.Leu369fs), c.349del (p.Thr116_Val117insTer), c.159del (p.Ser54fs) truncating the protein, nonsense mutations c.1122G>A (p.Trp374Ter), missense variants c.452A>C (p.Tyr151Ser) affecting the HhH domain, splice site variants c.1103-1G>T, c.264+20G>A, and in-frame deletions c.1067_1069del (p.Gly356del); these variants cause MUTYH-associated polyposis (MAP), an autosomal recessive condition predisposing to colorectal cancer, gastric cancer, and duodenal polyps through defective BER and accumulation of G:C>T:A mutations in APC and KRAS oncogenes. Conservation from mouse Q99P21 (515 aa, 94% length identity) and rat Q8R5G2 (516 aa, 94% identity) confirms essential DNA repair function across mammals with high sequence identity in catalytic HhH and [4Fe-4S] domains reflecting strong evolutionary constraint on lesion recognition and excision; high-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 78.9) enables mapping of DNA binding interface and iron-sulfur cluster coordination; 2,620 ClinVar variants establish comprehensive mutational landscape for hereditary cancer syndromes; mechanistic proposal involves MUTYH-mediated scanning of DNA via charge transport through the [4Fe-4S] cluster detecting oxidative damage, specific recognition of 8-oxoG:A mismatches through HhH domain insertion into DNA minor groove, adenine excision via N-glycosylase activity creating an abasic site, recruitment of APEX1 for strand incision, coordination with OGG1 (which removes 8-oxoG opposite C) to fully resolve oxidative damage, prevention of mutagenic replication by sanitizing the genome before S phase, interaction with NUDT1 to reduce 8-oxo-dGTP pool preventing re-incorporation, biallelic loss leading to hypermutation phenotype and early-onset colorectal cancer (MAP), and therapeutic implications including enhanced colonoscopy surveillance for mutation carriers, potential synthetic lethality with PARP inhibitors in MUTYH-deficient tumors, and germline testing for familial polyposis.
