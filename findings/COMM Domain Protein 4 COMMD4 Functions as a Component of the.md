## Description
COMMD4 (COMM domain-containing protein 4) is a 199 aa intracellular protein functioning as a component of the evolutionarily conserved CCC (COMMD/CCDC22/CCDC93) complex that regulates endosomal protein trafficking and receptor recycling. With 32 literature references, COMMD4 represents a 'partially characterized' dark gene where its role in the CCC complex is established, but its specific contributions to cancer progression (melanoma, lung cancer, glioma) and histone regulation are emerging (2020-2025). InterPro analysis reveals a bipartite domain architecture: an N-terminal HN domain (PF21672, aa 9-110) shared with COMMD2-7/10 family members, and a C-terminal COMM domain (IPR017920/PF07258, aa 128-199) that defines the COMMD family and mediates complex assembly. AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (pLDDT 80.8), indicating a well-folded protein with stable domain interfaces. HPA shows ubiquitous expression with low tissue specificity, detected in all tissues, localizing to vesicles, plasma membrane, and cytosol—consistent with a trafficking regulator shuttling between endosomal compartments and the cell surface. STRING interactions reveal COMMD4 exists almost exclusively within the COMMD family network: it binds strongly to all other COMMD proteins (COMMD1/2/3/5/6/7/8/9/10: 0.866-0.987) and the CCC complex scaffold CCDC22 (0.981), confirming its role as a complex subunit rather than a standalone factor. ClinVar lists 35 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number gains/losses on chromosome 15q24-26 and missense mutations (p.Arg122Gln), implicating COMMD4 dosage sensitivity in developmental disorders, though specific monogenic diseases remain undefined. Recent literature (2020-2025) establishes COMMD4 as: (1) Driver of skin cutaneous melanoma progression via PI3K-p85 binding and AKT pathway activation; (2) Therapeutic target in non-small cell lung cancer where it promotes drug resistance; (3) Prognostic biomarker in glioma correlating with poor survival; (4) Histone H2A-H2B dimer interactor that stabilizes chromatin and regulates transcription.
