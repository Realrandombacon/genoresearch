## Description
KLRC3 (UniProt Q07444), or killer cell lectin-like receptor subfamily C member 3 (also known as NKG2-E), encodes a 240-amino acid type II transmembrane protein belonging to the NKG2/CD94 natural killer receptor family (IPR001304/IPR016186/IPR016187/IPR033992/IPR050919/PF00059/PS50041/PTHR22800/SM00034/G3DSA:1.10.287.770/G3DSA:3.10.100.10/SSF56436). The protein architecture features a characteristic C-type lectin-like domain (CTLD, aa 117-228) that mediates ligand recognition, though unlike classical C-type lectins it binds protein ligands rather than carbohydrates. The CTLD adopts the canonical asialoglycoprotein receptor fold with conserved disulfide bonds stabilizing the carbohydrate recognition domain scaffold. AlphaFold predicts high-confidence structure (global pLDDT: 71.0), indicating a well-folded extracellular domain consistent with stable receptor-ligand interactions required for NK cell activation.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals KLRC3's integration into NK cell receptor signaling networks: KLRD1 (0.815, CD94 heterodimerization partner), KLRF1 (0.727, NKG2-F activating receptor), KLRK1 (0.700, NKG2-D activating receptor), GNLY (0.618, granulysin cytotoxic effector), B2M (0.610, beta-2-microglobulin MHC class I component), KLRB1 (0.592, NKR-P1A), KLRC2 (0.542, NKG2-C paralog), HLA-E (0.516, non-classical MHC class I ligand), TYROBP (0.425, DAP12 signaling adaptor), and KLRC4 (0.415, NKG2-D paralog). This partner profile positions KLRC3 within the activating NK cell receptor complex, likely heterodimerizing with CD94 (KLRD1) and signaling through DAP12 (TYROBP) to trigger cytotoxicity upon HLA-E recognition.\n\nWith 54 papers but emerging roles in cancer immunotherapy and reproductive medicine, KLRC3 qualifies as a dark gene with translational potential. Recent insights reveal: (1) DNA methylation of KLRC3 serves as biomarker for autoimmune thyroiditis with differential water iodine exposure effects, suggesting epigenetic regulation of NK cell tolerance; (2) identified as core diagnostic gene for repeated implantation failure in antiphospholipid syndrome patients, linking NK cell activation to pregnancy complications; (3) key node in lung adenocarcinoma immune phenotype networks, correlating with PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitor response rates; (4) cancer-enriched expression suggests tumor microenvironment modulation role; (5) immune cell-enriched expression confirms NK cell specificity. HPA data confirms group-enriched expression with brain (4.7 nTPM) and lymphoid tissue (3.9 nTPM) as top tissues, reflecting both neural NK cell populations and peripheral immune surveillance; bone marrow (1.8 nTPM) indicates NK cell progenitor expression; classified as predicted membrane protein with receptor function and vesicular localization. ClinVar lists 40 pathogenic variants including 12p13.33-11.1 copy number gains/duplications and splice site mutations (c.588-2A>G), confirming dosage sensitivity potentially linked to immunodeficiency or autoimmunity phenotypes. We hypothesize KLRC3 functions as a context-dependent activating receptor: the CTLD recognizes HLA-E presenting leader peptides from classical MHC class I molecules, providing 'missing-self' surveillance when tumors downregulate classical MHC; heterodimerization with CD94 (KLRD1) creates the functional receptor complex that recruits DAP12 (TYROBP) for ITAM-mediated signaling; in cancer, KLRC3 upregulation enhances tumor cell killing but may also contribute to immune-related adverse events; in pregnancy, aberrant KLRC3 methylation/expression disrupts maternal-fetal tolerance leading to implantation failure; the 12p CNVs alter receptor stoichiometry, potentially causing either immunodeficiency (reduced surveillance) or autoimmunity (excessive activation); therapeutic targeting could enhance checkpoint inhibitor efficacy or prevent pregnancy complications.
