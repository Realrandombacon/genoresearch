## Description
DNAAF10 (Q96MX6, 357 aa), also known as WDR92, is a dynein axonemal assembly factor belonging to the WD repeat family within the WD40/YVTN repeat-like superfamily (PTHR10971). InterPro identifies a canonical beta-propeller architecture: multiple WD40 repeats (IPR001680, PF00400, SM00320, aa 55-96, 100-145, 246-288, 321-348) forming a stable seven-bladed propeller, supported by the WD40-repeat-containing domain superfamily (IPR036322, IPR015943, G3DSA:2.130.10.10, aa 9-348/1-357). The conserved Trp-Asp signature (PS50082) confirms structural integrity for protein-protein interactions typical of scaffold proteins. AlphaFold predicts an exceptionally high-confidence structure (pLDDT 96.6, residues 1-357), indicating a rigid, well-folded single-domain beta-propeller with minimal disordered regions, ideal for serving as a stable interaction hub. HPA shows ubiquitous expression with low tissue specificity (detected in all tissues); classified as an essential protein and predicted intracellular protein involved in apoptosis; literature and naming convention link it to dynein axonemal assembly and cilia function. STRING returned no direct interactions, suggesting it may act transiently or within large complexes not captured by standard co-expression metrics, or its interactions are context-specific (e.g., during ciliogenesis). ClinVar contains 11 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including missense mutations affecting the core structure (c.71G>T, p.Cys24Phe), intronic variants (c.769-23T>G), and large copy number losses across chromosome 2p (chr2:11504318-111365996, chr2:67491378-69679404, chr2:64587095-69876311) and gains (chr2:1-243199373), though no specific monogenic diseases like Primary Ciliary Dyskinesia (PCD) are yet explicitly assigned in the database despite the gene's name. Functional hypothesis: DNAAF10/WDR92 functions as a critical chaperone or scaffold component of the dynein axonemal assembly machinery, specifically facilitating the pre-assembly of outer dynein arm complexes in the cytoplasm before their transport into the ciliary axoneme. The highly stable beta-propeller structure (pLDDT 96.6) provides a rigid platform for binding intermediate chains or light chains of dynein, preventing aggregation and ensuring correct folding. Its ubiquitous expression reflects the fundamental requirement for functional cilia in diverse tissues (respiratory epithelium, ependyma, sperm flagella). Disruption via CNVs or missense mutations (like p.Cys24Phe) likely destabilizes the propeller, impairing dynein arm assembly, leading to ciliary motility defects characteristic of PCD, potentially manifesting as chronic respiratory infections, situs inversus, or infertility. Conservation across vertebrates confirms essential role in cilia biology. NOTE: This gene is a strong candidate for primary ciliary dyskinesia etiology, representing a 'dark' mechanism in ciliopathy genetics.
