# LOC129390521: MPRA-Validated Silencer Element on 12q21.1 with Low Burden of 2 Recurrent Pathogenic CNVs

**Date:** 2026-03-20T20:33:33.427139

**Quality Score:** 2.2/10  (E=3.4, D=1.0) [WEAK]

## Description
LOC129390521 is a 200bp cis-regulatory element (CRE) located on chromosome 12q21.1 (GRCh38: 89263997-89264197), functionally validated as a transcriptional silencer through STARR-seq massively parallel reporter assays (MPRAs) in K562 erythroleukemia cells. This element lacks canonical promoter, enhancer, or CTCF-bound insulator chromatin signatures but demonstrates robust repressive activity against the super core promoter SCP1. As a non-coding regulatory locus, it has no UniProt accession, protein domains, or STRING interaction partners. Human Protein Atlas could not resolve this locus to an Ensembl ID, consistent with its non-coding regulatory classification.\n\nClinVar analysis reveals 2 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variants disrupting this region—a low burden compared to other characterized silencers. Variants include 1 large deletion (copy number loss spanning 12q21.1-q22, covering ~19.3Mb) and 1 arm-level duplication (copy number gain spanning 12p13.33-q24.33, covering ~133Mb). These recurrent CNVs are associated with 12q deletion/duplication syndromes featuring developmental delay, intellectual disability, craniofacial dysmorphism, skeletal anomalies, and congenital heart defects. The 12q21.1-q22 region is gene-dense, containing critical dosage-sensitive genes such as OTOF (Otoferlin, mutations cause auditory neuropathy), ACP2 (Acid Phosphatase 2), and multiple transcription factors.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: LOC129390521 functions as a critical distal silencer within the 12q21.1 topologically associating domain (TAD), regulating expression of dosage-sensitive developmental and sensory genes. Candidate targets include OTOF (essential for synaptic vesicle exocytosis in inner hair cells, hearing loss when disrupted) or nearby transcription factor genes. The silencer likely recruits co-repressor complexes (PRC2, NCoR/SMRT, HDACs) to deposit H3K27me3 marks at target promoters. The low CNV burden (2 variants, 1:1 deletion:duplication ratio) suggests that focal disruption of this element is rare, with most pathogenic events occurring as part of large chromosomal rearrangements. The arm-level duplication indicates that silencer dosage effects may be compounded by genome-wide 12q gene overexpression. Deletions may cause derepression of OTOF or developmental regulators leading to auditory/sensory phenotypes, while duplications may cause ectopic silencing of adjacent genes. The K562 validation suggests potential roles in hematopoietic lineage specification, though the 12q21 location points to broader developmental functions.
