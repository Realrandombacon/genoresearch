## Description
DCPH1 (damage-control phosphatase 1, alias ARMT1/acidic residue methyltransferase 1) encodes a 441-amino acid well-folded enzyme belonging to the damage-control phosphatase ARMT1 family (IPR039763/PTHR12260, residues 5-439) that functions in DNA damage response and chromatin repair pathways. The protein contains a conserved ARMT1-like metal-binding domain (IPR002791/IPR036075/PF01937, residues 20-419) characteristic of metallophosphatases that coordinate divalent metal ions (likely Mn2+/Mg2+) for catalytic activity, with CATH-Gene3D structural homology to uncharacterized protein PF01937 DUF89 domain 3 (G3DSA:3.40.50.10880, residues 233-386) and AF1104-like superfamily (SSF111321, residues 43-420). AlphaFold predicts very high confidence structure (pLDDT=94.9), indicating a stable globular fold with defined active site architecture—unusual for DNA damage response proteins which are often intrinsically disordered. HPA classifies DCPH1 as a predicted intracellular protein with dual hydrolase/methyltransferase/transferase molecular functions, nucleoplasm/nuclear bodies/cytosol localization, ubiquitous expression (detected in all tissues, low tissue specificity), cancer-enhanced expression in tumors, and cell type-enhanced single cell specificity—consistent with inducible DNA damage response factor. Gene ontology annotations include DNA damage biological process, positioning DCPH1 within genome integrity maintenance pathways. ClinVar documents 13 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including 6q24.2-27 copy number gains (chr6:144488859-170610382)x3, 6q22.31-25.3 copy number gains (chr6:119840686-156623091)x3, 6q25.1-25.3 copy number losses (chr6:150905553-158511926)x1, intronic variant c.41+232G>T, and large 6p25.3-q27 rearrangements, indicating dosage sensitivity that may contribute to cancer predisposition or developmental disorders through impaired DNA damage response. Conservation is strong across vertebrates: mouse A6H630 (439 aa, 99% identity), rat Q6AYT5 (439 aa, 98% identity), zebrafish Q58EM4 (448 aa, 85% identity), bovine A3KMX8 (441 aa, 97% identity)—indicating essential function. Mechanistically, DCPH1 functions as a bifunctional enzyme coupling phosphatase and methyltransferase activities for chromatin repair. The metal-binding domain coordinates catalytic metal ions for phosphatase activity, potentially dephosphorylating histone H2AX (γH2AX) or other DNA damage signaling phospho-epitopes to terminate damage response after repair completion. The methyltransferase activity may methylate acidic residues (glutamate/aspartate) on histone tails or DNA repair factors, creating negative charge marks that recruit chromatin remodelers or inhibit nucleosome assembly at damage sites. Nuclear body localization suggests concentration at PML nuclear bodies or DNA damage foci where repair factors concentrate. The well-folded structure enables precise substrate recognition and catalysis, while cancer-enhanced expression indicates upregulation in tumors—potentially supporting enhanced DNA repair capacity for chemotherapy resistance. Loss of DCPH1 impairs damage response termination, causing persistent DNA damage signaling, cell cycle arrest, or apoptosis. DCPH1 represents a paradigm of structured enzymatic chromatin repair factor coupling post-translational modification removal (dephosphorylation) with addition (methylation) for damage response coordination.
