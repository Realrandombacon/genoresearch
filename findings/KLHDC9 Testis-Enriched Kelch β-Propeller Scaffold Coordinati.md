## Description
KLHDC9 (Kelch Domain-Containing Protein 9) is a poorly characterized structural scaffold protein with only 4 publications, representing a genuine dark gene emerging as a potential regulator of cell division and germ cell development. This 349-amino acid nuclear protein (UniProt Q8NEP7) features a canonical kelch repeat architecture forming a stable β-propeller fold. InterPro analysis reveals multiple kelch-type β-propeller domains (IPR011043/IPR015915, positions 23-337; G3DSA:2.120.10.80, positions 11-234 and 244-349), a KLHDC2/KLHL20/DRC7 Kelch-repeats domain (PF24681, positions 22-143), and the KLHDC9-specific family signature (IPR042941, positions 1-348). AlphaFold predicts very high-confidence structure (pLDDT 92.9), indicating a well-folded, stable seven-bladed β-propeller typical of protein-protein interaction hubs.\n\nSTRING interactions reveal KLHDC9 operates within networks spanning cell adhesion, Rho GTPase regulation, and inflammation: CCDC190 (0.675, coiled-coil domain protein of unknown function), ARHGAP30 (0.582, Rho GTPase-activating protein controlling cytoskeleton dynamics), SLAMF8 (0.535, signaling lymphocytic activation molecule), DRG1 (0.471, developmentally regulated GTPase), NECTIN4 (0.467, adherens junction protein), GPS2 (0.462, G-protein pathway suppressor), PROCA1 (0.454), C15orf40 (0.446), INCA1 (0.446, inhibitor of cell proliferation), and GRN (0.439, granulin/progranulin involved in inflammation and neurodegeneration). This diverse network suggests KLHDC9 functions as a multi-context scaffold bridging cytoskeletal remodeling, cell-cell adhesion, and inflammatory signaling.\n\nHPA shows tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in testis (24.6 nTPM), consistent with roles in spermatogenesis. Subcellular localization includes nucleoplasm, nucleoli, nucleoli rim, and mitotic chromosomes—this mitotic chromosome association is particularly noteworthy and suggests direct involvement in chromosome segregation or spindle assembly checkpoint regulation. ClinVar contains 15 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including splice site mutations (c.886+1G>C, c.688-2A>T) and large copy number losses/gains on chromosome 1q21-25, though specific monogenic diseases remain uncharacterized. Conservation across mouse (350 aa) indicates strong functional constraint.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: KLHDC9 functions as a cell cycle-regulated scaffold that coordinates mitotic progression through its kelch β-propeller platform. The mitotic chromosome localization suggests KLHDC9 may recruit or stabilize proteins at centromeres or kinetochores during metaphase-anaphase transition. The interaction with ARHGAP30 implies a role in regulating RhoA/Rac1 GTPase activity at the cleavage furrow during cytokinesis, coupling chromosome segregation to cytoplasmic division. In testis, KLHDC9 may be essential for the rapid cell divisions of spermatogonia or the unique meiotic divisions of spermatocytes. The GRN interaction raises intriguing possibilities: progranulin is critical in frontotemporal dementia and lysosomal function; KLHDC9 may regulate GRN trafficking or secretion in specific contexts. Cancer methylation studies identify KLHDC9 as a lung adenocarcinoma biomarker, suggesting epigenetic silencing may contribute to tumorigenesis via mitotic checkpoint defects. Pathogenic splice variants likely cause male infertility (via spermatogenesis failure) or developmental disorders (via mitotic errors in proliferating tissues).
