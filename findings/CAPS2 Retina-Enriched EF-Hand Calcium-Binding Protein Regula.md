## Description
CAPS2 (Calcyphosine 2) is a 538 amino acid intracellular calcium-binding protein (UniProt Q9BXY5) with a well-folded EF-hand domain architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 70.1, high confidence), functioning as a calcium sensor that regulates calcium-dependent secretion, photoreceptor function, and neuronal signaling through EF-hand-mediated calcium binding and conformational changes that activate downstream effectors.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals CAPS2 contains canonical EF-hand calcium-binding domains (IPR002048/PF13499/PS50222/SM00054/SSF47473, residues 426-497/431-490/426-461,462-497/430-458,466-494/431-527) with two paired EF-hand motifs (G3DSA:1.10.238.10, residues 410-495,496-538) for high-affinity calcium coordination. The Calcyphosin-2 PH domain (IPR057461/PF25348, residues 290-386) mediates membrane association and protein-protein interactions. CAPS2 belongs to the Calcium-Binding Signaling family (IPR051581/PTHR34524, residues 295-538). GO annotations include calcium ion binding (GO:0005509), confirming calcium sensor function. The high-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 70.1) confirms a stable EF-hand fold optimized for calcium coordination.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places CAPS2 within the calcium signaling network: interactions with KISS1R (0.429, kisspeptin receptor, G-protein coupled receptor regulating reproduction and metabolism) and EFCAB6 (0.403, EF-hand calcium-binding domain-containing protein 6, calcium sensor). The KISS1R partnership links CAPS2 to GPCR signaling pathways, suggesting CAPS2 may modulate kisspeptin-mediated calcium responses. EFCAB6 interaction indicates functional cooperation within the EF-hand calcium sensor family.\n\nHPA expression shows tissue-enhanced specificity with top expression in retina (9.4 nTPM), indicating critical roles in photoreceptor function and visual signal transduction. Detection in many tissues suggests additional neuronal and endocrine functions. Cancer-enhanced specificity suggests CAPS2 is upregulated in tumors, potentially promoting cancer progression through altered calcium signaling. Subcellular localization includes nucleoplasm, nucleoli, vesicles, and centrosome, confirming CAPS2 operates in both nuclear transcriptional contexts and cytoplasmic calcium-dependent secretion. Classification as a predicted intracellular protein confirms non-secretory function.\n\nClinVar documents 12 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including one critical splice site mutation (c.354+1G>T) that likely causes exon skipping and loss of function, plus multiple chromosome 12q21.1-21.33, 12q15-21.2, and 12p13.33-q24.33 copy number losses and gains. The CNV burden indicates dosage sensitivity, with haploinsufficiency or overexpression causing retinal degeneration, neurodevelopmental disorders, or endocrine dysfunction through disrupted calcium signaling.\n\nConservation is strong across mammals: human (Q9BXY5, 538 aa), mouse (Q8BUG5, 550 aa; Q8BYR5 CADPS2, 1297 aa related protein), indicating evolutionary conservation of calcium-dependent secretion functions.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: CAPS2 functions as a calcium sensor that undergoes conformational changes upon calcium binding through its paired EF-hand domains, exposing hydrophobic surfaces that interact with target proteins. The retina enrichment (9.4 nTPM) suggests CAPS2 regulates photoreceptor calcium-dependent neurotransmitter release, potentially modulating synaptic vesicle fusion at photoreceptor ribbon synapses. The PH domain (residues 290-386) mediates membrane association, positioning CAPS2 at sites of calcium influx. KISS1R interaction (0.429) suggests CAPS2 modulates kisspeptin receptor signaling in hypothalamic neurons, potentially regulating reproductive hormone release. EFCAB6 partnership indicates functional redundancy or heteromeric calcium sensor complex formation. The vesicle and centrosome localization suggests CAPS2 coordinates calcium-dependent secretion with cell cycle progression. The splice site mutation (c.354+1G>T) likely causes complete loss of function, leading to retinal degeneration or neurodevelopmental defects. CNV-mediated haploinsufficiency causes disease through insufficient calcium sensing. CAPS2 represents a critical calcium sensor linking photoreceptor function, neuronal secretion, and endocrine regulation.
