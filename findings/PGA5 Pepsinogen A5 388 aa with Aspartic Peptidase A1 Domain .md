## Description
PGA5 (Pepsinogen A5) is a 388-amino acid secreted aspartic peptidase zymogen belonging to the peptidase family A1 that functions as a precursor to pepsin A, a major proteolytic enzyme in gastric juice responsible for protein digestion. InterPro analysis reveals a canonical two-lobed aspartic protease architecture: an N-terminal propeptide domain (IPR012848, residues 19-45; PF07966) that maintains enzymatic inactivity at neutral pH, and a catalytic pepsin domain (IPR001461, residues 58-385; IPR033121, residues 75-387; IPR034162, residues 66-386; PF00026, residues 75-387) containing the conserved active site motifs (IPR001969, residues 91-102 and 274-285) with the critical DTG catalytic triad essential for acid-dependent proteolysis. The protein belongs to the acid protease superfamily (G3DSA:2.40.70.10) with GO annotations for aspartic-type endopeptidase activity (GO:0004190) and proteolysis (GO:0006508). AlphaFold predicts a very high-confidence structure (pLDDT 91.2), consistent with a stable, well-folded digestive enzyme optimized for the harsh acidic gastric environment.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places PGA5 in a tight functional cluster of gastric digestive enzymes with exceptionally high confidence: PGA4 (0.976, pepsinogen A4, the closest paralog), PGA3 (0.904, pepsinogen A3), PROC (0.787, protein C), GLO1 (0.748, glyoxalase I), LGALS4 (0.649, galectin-4, a gut-specific lectin involved in epithelial barrier function), CCHCR1 (0.629, coiled-coil alpha-helical rod protein 1), F5 (0.537, coagulation factor V), CTSH (0.529, cathepsin H), and AMY2A/AMY2B (amylases). The strong PGA4/PGA3 interactions confirm PGA5's role in the gastric pepsinogen family, while the LGALS4 connection is notable as galectin-4 is specifically expressed in gastrointestinal epithelium and may coordinate mucosal defense with digestive enzyme secretion.\n\nHuman Protein Atlas classifies PGA5 as a tissue-enriched enzyme with extreme specificity for the stomach, showing massive expression levels (35,793.5 nTPM) in gastric mucosa, consistent with its role as a major secretory product of chief cells. It is also classified as cancer-enriched and cell type-enriched, likely reflecting its use as a biomarker for gastric cancer or tumors of gastric origin. Subcellular localization predictions indicate secretion, matching its function as a luminal digestive enzyme.\n\nClinVar reports 11 pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants, including one splice site variant (c.774-3C>T) and multiple large copy number variations spanning chromosome 11 (coordinates include 11p11.12-q12.2 duplication, 11q12.1-13.3 duplication, 11p11.12-q13.1 duplication, 11q12.2-13.5 duplication, 11p11.2-q12.2 duplication, 11p13-q25 duplication, 11q12.1-12.3 duplication, 11p15.5-q25 duplication). While no specific monogenic disease is named solely for PGA5, these CNVs overlap with regions associated with developmental disorders and potential gastric dysfunction. The presence of a splice variant suggests that subtle PGA5 defects may have clinical consequences.\n\nLiterature analysis (32 papers) indicates PGA5 is a moderately studied digestive enzyme, primarily investigated in the context of gastric physiology, pepsinogen isoform diversity, and as a serum biomarker for gastric mucosal integrity. Unlike PGA1 or the extensively characterized PGA3, PGA5 has fewer dedicated mechanistic studies, though its sequence and structure are well-conserved among mammals. Recent work includes studies on gastric function in piglets, liver cancer cellular similarity detection, and infantile hemangioma treatment.\n\nWe hypothesize that PGA5 functions as a specialized gastric protease with distinct substrate preferences or activation kinetics compared to PGA3/PGA4, potentially optimizing digestion of specific dietary proteins or exhibiting differential pH sensitivity. Its substantial stomach enrichment (35k+ nTPM) suggests it is a significant contributor to gastric proteolytic capacity. The LGALS4 interaction suggests potential coordination between digestive enzyme secretion and mucosal barrier function. The CNVs and splice variant on 11p/q may cause dosage effects altering the pepsinogen pool, potentially influencing susceptibility to gastric ulcers, protein malabsorption, Helicobacter pylori colonization, or gastric cancer progression. PGA5 represents a candidate biomarker for gastric mucosal atrophy, a potential target for modulating gastric digestion in metabolic disorders, and a component of the 'pepsinogen code' that determines individual digestive efficiency and gastric disease risk.
