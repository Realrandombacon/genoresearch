## Description
SSC5D (Scavenger receptor cysteine-rich family member with 5 domains, 1573 aa, UniProt A1L4H1) is a partially characterized innate immune receptor with emerging roles in bacterial recognition, inflammation, and cardiac pathology, representing an understudied scavenger receptor with clinical significance. HPA classifies SSC5D as predicted intracellular/secreted protein with low tissue specificity (detected in many tissues), cell type-enhanced expression, associated with immunity and innate immunity biological processes, functioning as developmental protein and receptor—localizes to nucleoplasm and cytosol. ClinVar contains 10 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including splice site mutation (c.1387+1G>T disrupting SRCR domain splicing), missense variant (p.Pro1311Ala), and multiple copy number gains/losses at 19q13.33-13.43 (chr19:48463931-59095359 range)—gene dosage sensitivity indicates clinical significance though specific diseases not yet fully defined, likely disrupting bacterial recognition, inflammatory responses, and cardiac repair. Literature (26 papers) reveals emerging roles: (1) Bacterial recognition—SSC5D physically interacts with bacteria through SRCR-containing N-terminal domain, functioning as pattern recognition receptor (PRR) for pathogen-associated molecular patterns (PAMPs); (2) Post-myocardial infarction pathology—SSC5D exacerbates cardiac dysfunction via p38/MAPK-mediated inflammatory activation, driving excessive inflammation after MI leading to adverse remodeling; (3) Innate immunity—SSC5D belongs to scavenger receptor cysteine-rich family (includes CD5, CD6, CD163, WC1) involved in pathogen recognition, immune cell regulation, and inflammation control. Functional hypothesis: SSC5D functions as (a) soluble/membrane-associated pattern recognition receptor binding bacterial surface components through N-terminal SRCR domains to initiate innate immune responses, and (b) inflammatory modulator regulating p38/MAPK signaling pathway in cardiac tissue post-injury, balancing protective inflammation versus pathological remodeling. Mechanistic proposal: SSC5D is secreted or membrane-anchored via C-terminal region (large 1573 aa protein with N-terminal SRCR domains exposed for ligand binding), where three tandem SRCR domains (aa 20-404) form cooperative bacteria-binding surface recognizing conserved microbial patterns (lipopolysaccharide, peptidoglycan, teichoic acids)—upon bacterial binding, SSC5D triggers downstream signaling through p38/MAPK cascade activating proinflammatory cytokine production (TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6) and immune cell recruitment; in myocardial infarction, SSC5D expression increases in damaged cardiac tissue, where it amplifies p38/MAPK-mediated inflammatory activation beyond physiological levels causing excessive neutrophil infiltration, cardiomyocyte apoptosis, and fibrotic remodeling (therapeutic target for post-MI heart failure); intrinsically disordered regions (pLDDT 52.3) provide conformational flexibility enabling SSC5D to bind diverse bacterial species and interact with signaling adaptors; copy number variants at 19q13.33-13.43 cause gene dosage effects—haploinsufficiency impairs bacterial clearance and innate immunity (increased infection susceptibility); overexpression drives chronic inflammation and tissue damage (autoimmune disease, post-MI cardiac dysfunction, inflammatory cardiomyopathy)—positioning SSC5D as diagnostic marker for infection risk and cardiac prognosis, and therapeutic target for post-MI inflammation (p38/MAPK inhibitors, SSC5D-neutralizing antibodies) and bacterial sepsis (SSC5D pathway modulation).
