## Description
ZNF281 (Zinc Finger Protein 281, UniProt Q9Y2X9) is a large 895-amino acid Krüppel-associated C2H2 zinc finger transcription factor functioning as a transcriptional repressor within the core pluripotency regulatory network. The protein contains multiple tandem C2H2 zinc finger DNA-binding domains (IPR013087/PF00096, residues 261-288, 289-316, 317-344) arranged in clusters that recognize specific genomic sequences, classified within the Krüppel C2H2-type zinc-finger protein family (IPR050826/PTHR24377, residues 258-366). AlphaFold predicts a predominantly disordered architecture (pLDDT=46.5) with localized structured zinc finger folds embedded within extended intrinsically disordered regions — characteristic of transcription factors requiring conformational flexibility for dynamic chromatin engagement and multi-protein complex assembly during cell state transitions. HPA classifies ZNF281 as a transcription factor with repressor activity, tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in bone marrow (31.7 nTPM), nucleoplasmic subcellular localization, and cell type-enriched expression pattern — suggesting specialized roles in hematopoietic differentiation and stem cell maintenance. STRING interaction network reveals exceptional associations with core pluripotency factors: NANOG (0.820, homeobox transcription factor essential for embryonic stem cell self-renewal), POU5F1/OCT4 (0.797, POU domain factor required for pluripotency), GATA4 (0.659, cardiac/endodermal differentiation factor), MYC (0.634, proto-oncogene regulating proliferation), CTNNB1/β-catenin (0.595, Wnt signaling effector), SOX2 (0.560, HMG-box pluripotency factor), RELA (0.500, NF-κB subunit), and STAT3 (0.483, cytokine signaling transcription factor). This interaction profile places ZNF281 at the heart of the pluripotency circuitry where NANOG, OCT4, and SOX2 form the master regulatory triad controlling embryonic stem cell identity. ClinVar documents 19 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number gains and losses across 1q25.3-32.1, indicating extreme dosage sensitivity where both haploinsufficiency and triplosensitivity cause disease, potentially developmental disorders or hematologic malignancies. The combination of C2H2 zinc finger DNA-binding capacity, transcriptional repression activity, core pluripotency factor interactions, bone marrow-enriched expression, and CNV-associated pathology supports a mechanistic model: ZNF281 functions as a context-dependent transcriptional repressor that fine-tunes the pluripotency network by recruiting co-repressor complexes to target genes, balancing self-renewal versus differentiation programs in embryonic stem cells and hematopoietic progenitors, with dosage disruption causing developmental defects through dysregulation of cell fate determination pathways.
