## Description
PLCH1 (Phospholipase C eta 1, UniProt Q4KWH8) is a 1693-amino acid enzyme belonging to the phosphoinositide-specific phospholipase C (PLC) superfamily that hydrolyzes phosphatidylinositol 4,5-bisphosphate [PtdIns(4,5)P2] to generate the second messengers inositol 1,4,5-trisphosphate (IP3) and diacylglycerol (DAG). InterPro analysis reveals a modular domain architecture characteristic of PLC enzymes: an N-terminal PH domain (G3DSA:2.30.29.30, aa 14-133) for membrane recruitment, tandem EF-hand motifs (cd16220, IPR000909, aa 134-295) for calcium sensing, a catalytic X/Y domain split (IPR000909/IPR001711, aa 298-723) forming the active site, and a C-terminal C2 domain (IPR000008, aa 715-843) for calcium-dependent membrane binding. The AlphaFold structure shows medium confidence (pLDDT 57.5), reflecting the flexible linker regions typical of signaling enzymes that undergo conformational changes upon activation. STRING interaction data places PLCH1 within the phosphoinositide metabolic network, with strongest partnerships to IPMK (0.912), the tumor suppressor PTEN (0.905), and multiple phosphatidylinositol phosphate kinases (PIP5K1A/B/C, PIP4K2A/B/C; scores 0.730-0.739), suggesting coordinated regulation of PtdIns(4,5)P2 pools. HPA expression shows tissue-enhanced patterns with highest levels in lymphoid tissue (15.5 nTPM), retina (11.5 nTPM), and testis (10.5 nTPM), with vesicular subcellular localization. ClinVar records document 22 pathogenic variants including copy number gains/losses at 3q25.31 and truncating mutations (p.Cys1071fs, p.Arg701Ter). Notably, HPA links PLCH1 to holoprosencephaly, a severe forebrain development defect, implicating disrupted IP3/DAG signaling in early embryonic patterning. We hypothesize that PLCH1 acts as a calcium-sensitive regulator of localized PtdIns(4,5)P2 hydrolysis in specific tissues, with retinal expression suggesting roles in phototransduction or retinal development, and lymphoid expression indicating potential immune signaling functions. The moderate AlphaFold confidence suggests intrinsically disordered regulatory regions that may mediate context-dependent protein interactions.
