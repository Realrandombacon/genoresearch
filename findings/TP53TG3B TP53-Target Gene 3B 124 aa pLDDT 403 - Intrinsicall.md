Disease mechanism: The 7 ClinVar CNVs suggest TP53TG3B dosage is critical - both gains (5 variants) and losses (1 variant) are pathogenic, indicating haploinsufficiency or triplosensitivity. 16p11.2-13.3 CNVs are associated with neurodevelopmental disorders (autism, intellectual disability, schizophrenia), and TP53TG3B may contribute to these phenotypes through disrupted centrosome/cilia function affecting neuronal migration or brain development. Cancer-enhanced expression suggests TP53TG3B is upregulated in tumors with p53 pathway activation, potentially as a failed tumor suppressor response or as a pro-survival adaptation. The disordered structure makes TP53TG3B vulnerable to aggregation or misregulation in disease states. Therapeutic implication: TP53TG3B represents a candidate biomarker for p53 pathway activation status and 16p CNV syndrome severity. Its disordered nature makes it challenging to target directly, but understanding TP53TG3B-centriolar satellite interactions could reveal therapeutic approaches for ciliopathies or centrosome amplification in cancer. The epididymis specificity suggests potential roles in male infertility. Validation priorities include: determining TP53TG3B binding partners at centriolar satellites, characterizing p53-dependent induction kinetics, assessing TP53TG3B function in ciliogenesis and centrosome duplication, correlating 16p CNV breakpoints with TP53TG3B dosage and neurodevelopmental phenotypes, and evaluating TP53TG3B expression as a prognostic marker in p53-wildtype vs mutant cancers.
