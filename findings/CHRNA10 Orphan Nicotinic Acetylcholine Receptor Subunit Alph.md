# CHRNA10: Orphan Nicotinic Acetylcholine Receptor Subunit Alpha-10 Forming Heteromeric Channels with CHRNA9 in Skeletal Muscle and Immune Cells, with 21 Clinical Variants Suggesting Role in Neuromuscular and Inflammatory Disorders

**Confidence:** MEDIUM

## Description
CHRNA10 (Cholinergic Receptor Nicotinic Alpha 10 Subunit) is a 450-amino acid type I transmembrane protein that functions as an orphan subunit of neuronal nicotinic acetylcholine receptors (nAChRs). InterPro analysis confirms membership in the nicotinic acetylcholine receptor family (IPR002394, residues 64-80/98-112/116-128) containing the canonical neurotransmitter-gated ion-channel ligand-binding domain (IPR006202, residues 32-235; G3DSA:2.70.170.10, residues 28-241) and transmembrane domain (IPR006029, residues 243-444; G3DSA:1.20.58.390, residues 242-346/387-450; cd19051, residues 239-324). The protein belongs to the Cys-loop neurotransmitter-gated ion channel superfamily (IPR006201, residues 32-447), indicating a conserved pentameric assembly with other nAChR subunits. AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (pLDDT 82.2), consistent with a well-folded ligand-gated ion channel subunit containing an extracellular N-terminal ligand-binding domain, four transmembrane helices (M1-M4), and intracellular loops.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places CHRNA10 within the cholinergic signaling network with highest confidence for CHRNA9 (0.934, nicotinic acetylcholine receptor subunit alpha-9), its obligate heteromeric partner that forms functional α9α10 nAChR channels. Additional partners include CHAT (0.774, choline acetyltransferase, the acetylcholine biosynthetic enzyme), CHRNA5 (0.746), CHRNA1 (0.728), SLURP2 (0.668, secreted Ly6/uPAR-related protein 2, an endogenous nAChR modulator), CHRNE (0.637), CHRNG (0.625), CHRNA7 (0.625, alpha-7 homomeric receptor), CHRNB1 (0.622), and SLURP1 (0.619). This interaction profile strongly supports a role as a heteromeric nAChR subunit that requires CHRNA9 for surface expression and channel function, with potential modulation by SLURP family proteins.\n\nHuman Protein Atlas classifies CHRNA10 as 'tissue enriched' with top expression in skeletal muscle (44.8 nTPM), distinct from other neuronal nAChRs that typically show brain enrichment. It is categorized as a predicted membrane protein and transporter involved in calcium and ion transport, with ligand-gated ion channel activity. The skeletal muscle specificity is unusual for a 'neuronal' nAChR subunit and suggests specialized roles in neuromuscular junction modulation or muscle proprioception. CHRNA10 is not detected in immune cells despite literature suggesting immune functions.\n\nClinVar reports 21 pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains spanning 11p15.5-15.4 (coordinates: chr11:230615-8821443, chr11:230615-5525355, chr11:230615-4851537, chr11:210300-8664358, chr11:235934-33826995). The 11p15.5 region is imprinted and associated with Beckwith-Wiedemann syndrome and Silver-Russell syndrome, though no specific CHRNA10-related monogenic disorder is named. The CNV burden suggests dosage sensitivity, potentially affecting neuromuscular or inflammatory phenotypes.\n\nLiterature analysis (29 papers) confirms CHRNA10 as partially characterized with key functional insights. The gene was cloned in 2001 and mapped to chromosome 11p15.5. CHRNA10 forms functional heteromeric channels with CHRNA9 that exhibit distinct pharmacology from other nAChRs, including sensitivity to calcium and blockade by strychnine (unusual for nAChRs). The α9α10 receptor is expressed in cochlear outer hair cells (mediating efferent auditory suppression), immune cells (modulating cytokine release), and potentially skeletal muscle. Recent 2025 work demonstrated that unconventional α9* nAChRs down-modulate ATP-mediated cytokine release by macrophages, suggesting anti-inflammatory functions. CHRNA10 is an FDA-approved drug target, though specific therapeutics remain undeveloped.\n\nWe hypothesize that CHRNA10 functions as a specialized nAChR subunit that forms heteromeric α9α10 channels with unique biophysical and pharmacological properties. Unlike homomeric α7 receptors, α9α10 channels show higher calcium permeability and distinct desensitization kinetics. The skeletal muscle enrichment suggests roles beyond the originally described cochlear and immune functions—potentially modulating neuromuscular transmission, muscle spindle afferent signaling, or acetylcholine-mediated muscle inflammation. The intracellular M3-M4 loop may interact with scaffolding proteins or signaling molecules (e.g., CaMKII, calcineurin) to couple channel activity to downstream pathways. SLURP1/SLURP2 interactions suggest endogenous regulation by secreted Ly6 proteins, potentially creating tissue-specific modulation of α9α10 receptor function. In immune cells, α9α10 channels may function as 'cholinergic anti-inflammatory pathway' effectors, where vagal acetylcholine release suppresses cytokine production. The 11p15.5 CNVs may cause dosage-dependent phenotypes including neuromuscular weakness, auditory processing defects, or inflammatory dysregulation. CHRNA10 represents an orphan receptor subtype with therapeutic potential for pain (cochlear efferent system), inflammation (macrophage modulation), and neuromuscular disorders.
