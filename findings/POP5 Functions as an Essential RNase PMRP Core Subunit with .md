## Description
POP5 (Ribonuclease P/MRP Protein Subunit POP5) is a 163 aa essential ribonucleoprotein representing a partially characterized gene with 62 literature references, functioning as a critical structural component of both RNase P and RNase MRP complexes required for tRNA 5'-leader removal and rRNA processing. Multi-omics data reveals POP5 as a vertebrate-conserved RNA-binding protein with defined structural domains yet emerging functional characterization through recent 2024 telomere length validation studies. UniProt accession Q969H6 confirms the 163 aa length with homologs in mouse (Q9DB28, 169 aa) and yeast (P28005, 173 aa), indicating strong evolutionary conservation. InterPro analysis confirms membership in the Rpp14/Pop5 family (IPR002759, aa 7-115; IPR016819, aa 1-150; PF01900, aa 7-115) containing a conserved Rnp2-like domain (G3DSA:3.30.70.3250, aa 1-120; SSF160350, aa 1-120) that mediates RNA binding and complex assembly. AlphaFold predicts a very high-confidence structure (pLDDT 90.5) across the full 163 aa length, indicating a well-folded stable domain suitable for RNA-protein interactions within the ribonucleoprotein complex. HPA shows ubiquitous expression (detected in all tissues) with low tissue specificity, essential protein classification, nucleoplasm/nucleoli localization, and rRNA/tRNA processing biological process annotation—consistent with a core ribonucleoprotein component functioning in all dividing cells. STRING interactions reveal a highly confident RNase P/MRP network: RPP38 (0.999), RPP40 (0.999), RPP14 (0.999), POP4 (0.999), POP1 (0.999), RPP30 (0.999), RPP21 (0.999), RPP25 (0.996), POP7 (0.991), RPP25L (0.814). These interactions confirm POP5 operates as an integral core subunit of both RNase P and RNase MRP holoenzymes. ClinVar lists 15 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, exclusively large chromosome 12 copy number gains (chr12:99532287-133777902x3, chr12:116422123-133777902x3, chr12:117533207-133777902x3, chr12:120880079-133777902x3, chr12:1-133851895x3) and one copy number loss (chr12:120829779-121489188x1), implicating POP5 dosage sensitivity in chromosome 12 duplication/deletion syndromes. The 62 papers (including 2024 GWAS validation identifying POP5 as a human telomere length regulation gene, 2016-2023 archaeal homolog functional studies) confirm POP5 as partially characterized—core RNase function defined but telomere regulation mechanism and disease phenotypes awaiting full characterization.
