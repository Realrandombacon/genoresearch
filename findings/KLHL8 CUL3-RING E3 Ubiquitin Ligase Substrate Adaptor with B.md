# KLHL8: CUL3-RING E3 Ubiquitin Ligase Substrate Adaptor with BTB-Kelch Architecture Regulating Protein Homeostasis in Epididymis and Nucleoplasm

**Quality Score:** 4.17/10  (E=7.25, D=1.1) [MODERATE]

## Description
KLHL8 (Kelch Like Family Member 8) is a 620 amino acid nucleoplasmic protein (UniProt Q9P2G9) with a very well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 90.7, very high confidence), characteristic of BTB-Kelch substrate adaptors that recruit specific proteins to CUL3-RING E3 ubiquitin ligase complexes for ubiquitination and proteasomal degradation.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals KLHL8 contains the canonical BTB-Kelch tripartite architecture: an N-terminal BTB/POZ domain (IPR000210/cd18238, residues 52-171/60-164; G3DSA:3.30.710.10, residues 39-164) conferring protein binding (GO:0005515) and CUL3 interaction, a BACK (BTB and C-terminal Kelch) domain (IPR011705/cd18448, residues 163-259/169-271; G3DSA:1.25.40.420, residues 165-287) for structural stability, and C-terminal Kelch repeats (IPR006652, residues 319-366/367-413/414-460; G3DSA:2.120.10.80, residues 310-440/441-606) forming a beta-propeller (IPR015915/IPR011043, residues 362-599) for substrate recognition. The protein belongs to the SKP1/BTB/POZ domain superfamily (IPR011333, residues 39-164).\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals KLHL8 functions within the CUL3-RING ubiquitin ligase network: strongest interactions with CUL3 (0.978, cullin-3, the scaffold protein), RBX1 (0.924, RING-box protein 1, E3 ligase catalytic component), and other BTB-Kelch family members including KLHL42 (0.920), KLHL13 (0.914), SPOP (0.914, speckle-type POZ protein), SPOPL (0.913), KLHL9 (0.913), KLHL41 (0.912), KBTBD6 (0.912), and KLHL7 (0.912). The CUL3 and RBX1 interactions confirm KLHL8's role as a CUL3 substrate adaptor.\n\nHPA expression shows tissue-enhanced specificity with highest expression in epididymis (57.1 nTPM), detected in all tissues with nucleoplasm subcellular localization. The protein is classified as predicted intracellular with involvement in Ubl conjugation pathway, cancer-related genes, and plasma protein.\n\nClinVar documents 26 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including both single nucleotide variants (c.786G>A/p.Pro262=) and large chromosome 4q21.21-22.3 copy number variations (gains and losses spanning 4q13.3-22.3 to 4q21.21-23 regions). These CNVs suggest dosage sensitivity potentially causing developmental disorders through impaired protein ubiquitination and homeostasis.\n\nConservation is evident from mouse ortholog (P59280, 629 aa) and C. elegans ortholog (G5ED84, kel-8, 690 aa), indicating ancient evolutionary conservation of the BTB-Kelch ubiquitination machinery.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: KLHL8 acts as a substrate-specific adaptor that bridges target proteins to the CUL3-RBX1 E3 ubiquitin ligase complex for polyubiquitination and proteasomal degradation. The BTB/POZ domain binds CUL3, while the Kelch beta-propeller recognizes specific substrate proteins via peptide motifs or post-translational modifications. The BACK domain stabilizes the BTB-Kelch interface. High epididymal expression suggests roles in sperm maturation through regulated degradation of proteins involved in spermatogenesis or epididymal fluid composition. Nucleoplasmic localization indicates nuclear substrates, potentially transcription factors, chromatin regulators, or DNA repair proteins. Interaction with other KLHL family members (KLHL7, KLHL9, KLHL13) suggests functional redundancy or cooperative substrate recognition. KLHL7/KLHL9 heterodimers are known to regulate circadian clock proteins; KLHL8 may participate in similar regulatory networks. CNV-mediated haploinsufficiency disrupts substrate ubiquitination, leading to protein accumulation and cellular dysfunction. The cancer-related classification suggests KLHL8 may ubiquitinate oncoproteins or tumor suppressors, with dysregulation contributing to tumorigenesis.
