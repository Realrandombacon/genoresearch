## Description
SLC22A9 (UniProt Q8IVM8, 553 aa), also known as Organic Anion Transporter 7 (OAT7), is a partially characterized solute carrier with 30 literature papers establishing its role in hepatic drug transport and steroid conjugate handling. The seminal 2015 study (40 citations) identified SLC22A9 as a novel pravastatin uptake transporter with significant variability in hepatic expression influenced by genetic and regulatory factors. InterPro reveals canonical Major Facilitator Superfamily (MFS) architecture with the characteristic 12-transmembrane helix fold: MFS domain (IPR011701, PF07690, aa 144-485), MFS general substrate transporter (G3DSA:1.20.1250.20, aa 121-531; IPR036259, aa 121-531), and MFS profile (PS50850, aa 96-521), forming the rock-and-roll alternating access mechanism typical of this superfamily. The cd17374 domain specifically identifies it as an organic anion transporter within the MFS family. AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (global pLDDT 85.7), indicating a stable transporter with well-folded transmembrane domains capable of substrate binding and conformational changes. HPA shows tissue-enriched expression with highest levels in liver (43.3 nTPM), consistent with its role in hepatic uptake of organic anions; cancer-enriched expression suggests potential roles in tumor metabolism. STRING interactions reveal high-confidence partners including SLC47A2/MATE2-K (0.767, multidrug and toxin extrusion protein for renal secretion), ABCC4/MRP4 (0.716, ATP-binding cassette transporter exporting organic anions), SLCO2B1/OATP2B1 (0.711, organic anion transporting polypeptide), SLCO1A2/OATP1A2 (0.709), SLCO4C1/OATP4C1 (0.690), SLC17A1/NPT1 (0.669, sodium/phosphate transporter), SLC47A1/MATE1 (0.620), and SLC2A9/GLUT9 (0.476, urate transporter). This interaction network places SLC22A9 within the hepatic organic anion transport system coordinating uptake (SLC22A9/OAT7, SLCOs) and efflux (ABCC4, SLC47s). ClinVar contains 8 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains/losses spanning 11q12.1-13.5 and 11p15.5-q25 regions, though specific SLC22A9 point mutations remain of uncertain significance (?), suggesting haploinsufficiency may contribute to metabolic phenotypes. Conservation across mammals is implied by SLC22 family expansion. Functional hypothesis: SLC22A9 functions as a liver-specific, sodium-independent organic anion transporter operating via the MFS rocker-switch mechanism, primarily mediating hepatic uptake of: (1) pravastatin and other HMG-CoA reductase inhibitors, explaining interindividual variability in statin response; (2) steroid conjugates (sulfates, glucuronides) from circulation for biliary excretion, linking to osteoporosis susceptibility via sex steroid hormone homeostasis; (3) endogenous organic anions (bile acids, eicosanoids) for metabolic clearance. The liver enrichment (43.3 nTPM) and interaction with efflux transporters (ABCC4, MATEs) suggests a coordinated uptake-efflux system for xenobiotic and endobiotic clearance. The SLC2A9/GLUT9 interaction hints at potential urate transport function, possibly linking to gout pathogenesis. The cancer-enriched expression suggests SLC22A9 may contribute to tumor drug resistance or metabolic reprogramming in hepatocellular carcinoma. Priority research: (1) substrate specificity profiling (pravastatin, steroid conjugates, bile acids, urate), (2) kinetic characterization (Km, Vmax, inhibition constants), (3) association studies linking SLC22A9 polymorphisms to statin response and osteoporosis risk, (4) investigation of SLC22A9 in liver disease and HCC progression, (5) structural analysis of substrate-binding pocket, (6) role in drug-drug interactions affecting hepatic clearance.
