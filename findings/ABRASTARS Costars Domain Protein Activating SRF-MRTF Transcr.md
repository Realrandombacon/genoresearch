## Description
ABRA (UniProt Q8N0Z2), also known as STARS (striated muscle activator of Rho signaling), encodes a 381-amino acid actin-binding protein containing a C-terminal Costars domain (IPR027817/PF14705, aa 305-380) within the actin-binding Rho-activating protein family (IPR026111, aa 230-381). AlphaFold predicts a partially structured protein (pLDDT: 66.4), consistent with scaffold proteins containing both ordered interaction domains and flexible regions for dynamic cytoskeletal coupling.\n\nSTRING interactions reveal ABRA's central role in actin-SRF signaling: strongest links to actin-binding LIM proteins ABLIM3 (0.957), ABLIM2 (0.956), and ABLIM1 (0.891), with direct connections to serum response factor SRF (0.665), transcriptional coactivator MRTFB (0.533), cardiac Z-disc protein MYOZ2 (0.609), and thin filament regulator LMOD2 (0.647). HPA shows striking skeletal muscle enrichment (135.7 nTPM), with tongue (59.6 nTPM) and heart muscle (29.5 nTPM) expression—consistent with its striated muscle-specific designation. ClinVar contains 38 pathogenic variants, predominantly 8q copy number gains/losses.\n\nThe literature search returned 6,149 papers but most reflect acronym confusion (benralizumab trials, geographic locations, algorithms). ABRA-specific mechanistic studies establish its role in G-actin sequestration triggering nuclear MRTF translocation and SRF-dependent transcription. This positions ABRA as a transitional dark gene—a muscle-specific mechanotransduction hub linking cytoskeletal dynamics to transcriptional programs, with untapped relevance in cardiomyopathy, muscular dystrophy, and exercise adaptation biology.
