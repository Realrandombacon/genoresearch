## Description
FKBP11 (FKBP Prolyl Isomerase 11, UniProt Q9NYL4) is a 201 aa endoplasmic reticulum-associated peptidyl-prolyl cis-trans isomerase that functions as a secretory translocon accessory factor accelerating protein folding during ER translocation. Structurally, AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence fold (pLDDT=87.4) centered on the canonical FKBP-type PPIase domain (IPR001179, PF00254, aa 51-144) within the FKBP2/11 family (IPR044609), adopting the characteristic FKBP-like β-grasp fold (G3DSA:3.10.50.40, SSF54534). Functionally, STRING interactions reveal connections to tetraspanins CD9 (0.894) and CD81 (0.490) involved in membrane organization, the ER chaperone PPIB (0.523), the disulfide isomerase PDIA4 (0.560), the ERAD component DERL3 (0.576), and the unfolded protein response transcription factor XBP1 (0.519), positioning FKBP11 within the ER protein quality control network. HPA shows pancreas-enriched expression (466.6 nTPM) with centrosome localization and immune cell enhancement, consistent with roles in secretory cell function. ClinVar identifies 11 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variants (gains and losses on chr12), though no specific disease is annotated, suggesting haploinsufficiency may contribute to developmental or immune phenotypes. Literature (71 papers) reveals emerging non-canonical roles: FKBP11 protects intestinal epithelial cells against inflammation-induced apoptosis via JNK-caspase pathway in Crohn's disease, promotes osteosarcoma malignancy as a prognostic factor, drives abdominal aortic aneurysm progression through m6A-dependent plasma cell regulation, and was recently identified (2024) as a secretory translocon accessory factor. The gene represents a 'semi-dark' ER chaperone where canonical PPIase structure is established, but specific substrate recognition, translocon coupling mechanisms, and therapeutic targeting in inflammatory disease and cancer remain underexplored.
