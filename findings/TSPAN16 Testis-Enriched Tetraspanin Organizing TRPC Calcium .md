# TSPAN16: Testis-Enriched Tetraspanin Organizing TRPC Calcium Channels and Gamma-Secretase Complex in Membrane Microdomains, with 13 Clinical Variants Suggesting Role in Male Fertility and Neurodevelopment

## Description
TSPAN16 (Tetraspanin-16) is a 245-amino acid plasma membrane protein belonging to the tetraspanin superfamily, characterized by four transmembrane domains and a large extracellular loop (EC2) that mediates protein-protein interactions. InterPro analysis confirms canonical tetraspanin architecture including the Tetraspanin animal family domain (IPR000301, residues 5-238), Tetraspanin/Peripherin family (IPR018499, residues 11-229), PF00335 tetraspanin family domain (residues 11-229), and the EC2 domain superfamily (IPR008952, residues 119-214). Notably, TSPAN16 contains a uroplakin_I_like_LEL domain (cd03156, residues 120-215) within its EC2 region, suggesting potential homophilic or heterophilic adhesion functions analogous to uroplakins in epithelial barrier formation. AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (pLDDT 84.9), indicating a well-folded tetraspanin with stable transmembrane helices and structured EC2 loop typical for proteins organizing membrane microdomains.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals TSPAN16 operates in a specialized membrane organization network with exceptionally high confidence for TPM3 (0.997, tropomyosin-3, an actin-binding protein linking membrane to cytoskeleton), TRPC4 (0.720) and TRPC5 (0.669, transient receptor potential canonical calcium channels), FBLN7 (0.644, fibulin-7 extracellular matrix protein), EPB41 (0.599, erythrocyte membrane protein 4.1), and SPTB (0.583, beta-spectrin). Critically, TSPAN16 interacts with APH1A (0.553, a component of the gamma-secretase complex responsible for Notch and APP processing), CFTR (0.550, cystic fibrosis transmembrane conductance regulator), MCHR1 (0.544, melanin-concentrating hormone receptor), and SLC6A4 (0.441, serotonin transporter). This interaction profile strongly supports a role as a membrane organizer that clusters ion channels, transporters, and signaling receptors into functional tetraspanin-enriched microdomains (TEMs).\n\nHuman Protein Atlas classifies TSPAN16 as 'tissue enriched' with predominant expression in testis (124.5 nTPM), consistent with roles in spermatogenesis, sperm maturation, or blood-testis barrier function. It is localized to the plasma membrane and detected in some tissues, with blood cell group enrichment suggesting potential immune cell functions. The testis-specific expression pattern aligns with literature linking tetraspanins to male infertility.\n\nClinVar reports 13 pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number losses and gains spanning 19p13.2-13.1 (coordinates: chr19:10441330-13077352, chr19:10957601-11672041, chr19:10642984-12810067). While specific disease associations are not yet named in ClinVar, the burden of CNVs affecting TSPAN16 suggests dosage sensitivity contributing to developmental disorders, potentially including neurodevelopmental phenotypes given APH1A/gamma-secretase interactions, or reproductive disorders given testis expression.\n\nLiterature analysis (7 papers) confirms TSPAN16 as a genuine dark gene with minimal characterization. Recent pan-cancer tetraspanin analyses (2022) include TSPAN16 among poorly understood family members. A 2025 fibromyalgia gene signature study identified TSPAN16 as an immune-related hub gene. Esophageal squamous cell carcinoma prognostic models (2025) include TSPAN16 among transmembrane-4 superfamily genes. Omics studies of male infertility (2022) highlight TSPAN16 expression in reproductive tissues, though functional validation is lacking.\n\nWe hypothesize that TSPAN16 functions as a membrane scaffold protein that organizes TRPC calcium channels and gamma-secretase components into specialized tetraspanin-enriched microdomains (TEMs) in testis and neuronal tissues. The EC2 domain mediates homophilic TSPAN16-TSPAN16 interactions and heterophilic binding to partner proteins like TRPC4/5, clustering these channels to regulate localized calcium influx critical for sperm motility, acrosome reaction, or neuronal signaling. The APH1A interaction suggests TSPAN16 may recruit gamma-secretase to specific membrane compartments, potentially regulating Notch signaling in spermatogonial stem cells or APP processing in neurons. The TPM3/spectrin/EPB41 interactions link TSPAN16 to the cortical cytoskeleton, stabilizing membrane microdomains against mechanical stress. In testis, TSPAN16 may organize the blood-testis barrier or sperm-egg fusion machinery analogous to CD9/CD81 in other tetraspanins. Loss of TSPAN16 function via 19p CNVs could disrupt calcium homeostasis in sperm (causing male infertility) or gamma-secretase trafficking in neurons (contributing to neurodevelopmental disorders). The uroplakin-like EC2 domain may mediate cell-cell adhesion in spermatogenic epithelia. TSPAN16 represents a candidate gene for idiopathic male infertility and a potential modifier of gamma-secretase-related disorders including Alzheimer's disease.
