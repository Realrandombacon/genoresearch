# SDE2: Ubiquitin-Like DNA Replication Stress Regulator Coupling Replication Fork Stability to Pre-mRNA Splicing via SAP Domain DNA Binding and NineTeen Complex Integration

**Quality Score:** 9/10 (EXCELLENT)

**Confidence:** MEDIUM

## Description
SDE2 (Q6IQ49) encodes a 451 amino acid multifunctional regulator that bridges DNA replication stress responses with pre-mRNA splicing through its modular domain architecture integrating ubiquitin-like signaling, DNA binding, and spliceosome association. InterPro analysis reveals a tripartite domain organization: an N-terminal ubiquitin-like domain (IPR053821/PF22781, positions 1-75) that undergoes proteolytic processing by USP5 to activate SDE2 function; a central SDE2-specific domain (IPR053822/PF22782, positions 76-171) of unknown molecular function but essential for protein stability; and a C-terminal SAP domain (IPR025086/PF13297, positions 372-444) providing sequence-specific DNA binding to replication forks and damaged DNA; the protein belongs to the RNA processing and DNA damage response regulator family (IPR051421/PTHR12786, positions 7-451). AlphaFold predicts a partially structured protein (pLDDT 65.2, medium confidence) consistent with the ubiquitin-like domain being well-folded while linker regions remain flexible for conformational switching. STRING interactions demonstrate exclusive high-confidence partnerships within the NineTeen (NTC) spliceosome activation complex and associated splicing factors: CDC40 (0.980), SLU7 (0.979), SYF2 (0.961), PPIL1 (0.958), CDC5L (0.940), CACTIN (0.915), PRKRIP1 (0.915), CWC15 (0.897), FAM32A (0.837), and PRPF19 (0.807), placing SDE2 at the heart of spliceosome assembly and catalytic activation. HPA shows ubiquitous expression across all tissues with low tissue specificity, localization to nuclear speckles (splicing factor compartments), Golgi apparatus, plasma membrane, and cytosol, classified as an essential protein with roles in cell cycle, DNA replication, mitosis, and mRNA processing. ClinVar contains 34 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including multiple copy number gains and losses spanning chromosome 1q21.1-44 regions (e.g., chr1:143932350-249224684 duplications, chr1:216147522-226765691 deletions), though specific monogenic disease associations remain undefined. Literature (56 papers) demonstrates SDE2 stabilizes DNA replication forks through extended DNA-binding interfaces beyond the canonical SAP domain, promotes branchpoint-distant exon splicing via Cactin/Tls1/Sde2 complex formation, is essential for ribosome biogenesis and alternative splicing regulation, undergoes USP5-controlled proteolytic switching, and inhibition promotes autophagy-dependent ferroptosis in multiple myeloma. The mechanistic hypothesis: SDE2 functions as a molecular coupler between genome maintenance and transcriptome fidelity through dual mechanisms: (1) at stalled replication forks, the SAP domain binds distorted DNA structures while the ubiquitin-like domain recruits repair factors, preventing fork collapse and mutagenesis during replication stress; (2) in the nucleus, SDE2 integrates into the NTC/prp19 complex via interactions with CDC5L, PRPF19, and CACTIN to promote spliceosome catalytic activation, specifically enabling splicing of exons with non-canonical branchpoint distances; USP5-mediated cleavage of the ubiquitin-like domain acts as a proteolytic switch converting SDE2 from inactive to active state, coordinating replication stress responses with splicing reprogramming; ubiquitous expression reflects housekeeping roles in maintaining genomic and transcriptomic integrity; copy number alterations on 1q disrupt SDE2 dosage, potentially causing combined replication stress sensitivity and splicing defects contributing to cancer predisposition or developmental disorders; ferroptosis induction upon SDE2 inhibition in multiple myeloma suggests SDE2 maintains redox homeostasis through splicing of antioxidant or iron metabolism genes.
