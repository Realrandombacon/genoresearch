## Description
CENPL (Centromere Protein L) is a 344-amino acid nuclear protein (UniProt Q8N0S6) representing a genuine dark gene with only 44 publications despite its essential role in kinetochore assembly and emerging significance in cancer chemoresistance. Structurally, CENPL belongs exclusively to the Centromere subunit L family (IPR025204/PTHR31740, residues 1-344) with a defined Centromere protein L domain (PF13092, residues 67-339). Unlike many centromere proteins with extensive domain architectures, CENPL appears as a single-domain protein without characterized sub-domains, suggesting it functions primarily through protein-protein interactions within the larger CCAN (Constitutive Centromere-Associated Network) complex. AlphaFold predicts high-confidence structure (pLDDT 83.1), consistent with a well-folded globular protein capable of stable complex formation. Functionally, CENPL localizes to the nucleus with tissue-enhanced expression highest in testis (9.1 nTPM) and bone marrow (8.9 nTPM), reflecting roles in highly proliferative tissues requiring robust chromosome segregation. HPA classifies it as an essential protein with cell type-enhanced specificity. STRING interactions reveal exclusive, ultra-high confidence connections to other constitutive centromere proteins forming the CCAN complex: CENPQ (0.999), CENPN (0.999), CENPI (0.999), CENPO (0.999), CENPK (0.999), CENPU (0.998), CENPH (0.997), CENPM (0.994), CENPC (0.989), plus ITGB3BP (0.998, integrin beta-3 binding protein). These near-perfect scores indicate CENPL is an obligate component of the multi-subunit CCAN that anchors centromeric chromatin to the kinetochore outer plate. Critically, ClinVar documents 38 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number gains/losses spanning 1q21-25 (multiple entries affecting megabase regions) and intronic SNVs (c.421-85A>T), yet ZERO diseases are formally associated—a profound gap given the essential mitotic function. Recent literature establishes emerging oncogenic roles: SP1 lactylation promotes endometrial cancer cell stemness via CENPL regulation (2025); E2F8-CENPL pathway contributes to homologous recombination repair and chemoresistance in breast cancer (9 citations, 2024); CENPL serves as a prognostic marker alongside DARS2 and PAICS in lung adenocarcinoma (10 citations, 2024); KDM1A-mediated ZFP64 demethylation activates CENPL to promote epithelial ovarian cancer progression (2 citations, 2024); and CENPL accelerates hepatocellular carcinoma proliferation, cell cycle progression, apoptosis resistance, and glycolysis via MEK1/2-ERK1/2 signaling (5 citations, 2023). The mechanistic hypothesis: CENPL functions as a structural scaffold within CCAN that stabilizes centromere-kinetochore attachments during mitosis, ensuring accurate chromosome segregation. In cancer, CENPL overexpression enhances DNA damage repair capacity (particularly homologous recombination), conferring resistance to DNA-damaging chemotherapy agents. The E2F8 transcriptional regulation suggests cell cycle-dependent expression peaking at G2/M. CENPL may also exhibit moonlighting functions outside the kinetochore, potentially regulating metabolic reprogramming (glycolysis enhancement in HCC) through ERK pathway activation. The absence of formal disease associations despite 38 pathogenic variants suggests either embryonic lethality of homozygous loss (consistent with essential protein classification), phenotypic variability in heterozygous carriers, or that CENPL variants act as cancer susceptibility modifiers rather than Mendelian disease drivers. The 1q21 copy number variant syndromes may include CENPL dosage effects contributing to chromosomal instability phenotypes. Future work should define CENPL's specific binding interfaces within CCAN, investigate whether CENPL mutations cause mosaic variegated aneuploidy syndromes, and evaluate CENPL as a therapeutic target for reversing chemoresistance.
