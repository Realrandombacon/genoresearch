## Description
NTNG2 (Netrin-G2) is a 530 aa glycosylphosphatidylinositol (GPI)-anchored membrane protein representing a borderline dark gene with 44 literature references, recently established as a causative factor for distinct neurodevelopmental disorders. Multi-omics data reveals NTNG2 as a well-folded extracellular scaffold mediating specific trans-synaptic adhesion. InterPro analysis confirms a canonical netrin-G architecture: an N-terminal Laminin N-terminal domain (IPR008211/PF00055, aa 33-286) providing structural rigidity, three Laminin-type EGF domains (IPR002049/PF00053, aa 286-344/353-409/409-456) facilitating protein-protein interactions, and a C-terminal EGF-like domain (IPR000742, aa 452-487). The full-length protein belongs to the Laminin/Netrin Extracellular Matrix family (IPR050440). AlphaFold predicts high-confidence structure (pLDDT 84.5), indicating a stable, elongated extracellular rod-like structure typical of synaptic adhesion molecules. HPA shows tissue-enhanced expression in brain (19.6 nTPM), bone marrow (23.6 nTPM), and retina (19.3 nTPM), with vesicular and plasma membrane localization, consistent with its role in synaptic organization and neurogenesis. STRING interactions reveal a highly specific synaptic network: NTNG2 associates strongly with LRRC4 (0.999, Netrin-G ligand 2/NGL-2, its primary postsynaptic receptor), LRRC4B (0.796, NGL-1), LRRC4C (0.624, NGL-3), and downstream effectors including CDKL5 (0.593, kinase linked to Rett syndrome), GDI1 (0.654, Rab GDP dissociation inhibitor), and NTN4 (0.647, Netrin-4). ClinVar lists 54 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including missense mutations (p.Arg258Leu, p.Cys438Trp, p.Thr248Pro, p.Asp315Tyr), nonsense mutations (p.Trp161Ter), splice site variants (c.857+2T>G), and copy number alterations (9q33.2-34.3 gains/losses), causing intellectual disability, autism spectrum disorder, hypotonia, and developmental delay. Recent literature (2019-2024) identifies NTNG2 as: (1) A cause of distinct neurodevelopmental disorder via homozygous missense variants disrupting presynaptic adhesion (2019, 40 citations); (2) A regulator of CaMKII signaling where compound heterozygous mutations inhibit kinase activity leading to intellectual disability (2024); (3) A candidate gene for a novel brain-skin disorder with early neuropsychiatric manifestations (2022); (4) A GWAS hit near tau-PET deposition in Alzheimer's disease (2020). The scarcity of mechanistic literature (44 papers) despite clear disease causality and defined receptor interactions makes NTNG2 a high-priority target for functional characterization.
