## Description
TESPA1 (Thymocyte-Expressed, Positive Selection-Associated Protein 1) is a 521 amino acid intracellular scaffold protein (UniProt A2RU30) with moderate characterization (38 papers) that functions in T-cell development, hematopoietic stem cell maintenance, and calcium signaling. The protein contains a conserved ITPR-interacting domain (IPR029325, PF14722, residues 126-291) that mediates binding to inositol 1,4,5-trisphosphate receptor ITPR1, positioning TESPA1 as a regulator of intracellular calcium release. STRING interactions reveal high-confidence partnerships with GRB2 (0.939), PLCG1 (0.900), and multiple COP9 signalosome subunits COPS2/3/5/6/8/9 (all 0.900), suggesting roles in both calcium signaling and protein complex assembly. HPA data shows group-enriched expression in brain (79.7 nTPM) and lymphoid tissue (70.0 nTPM) with cytosolic localization, consistent with functions in T-cell positive selection and neural development. ClinVar contains 5 pathogenic copy number gain variants at 12p13.33-q24.33, though no specific diseases are yet assigned. AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 53.9) indicates a partially disordered protein with flexible regions typical of signaling scaffolds. Literature demonstrates TESPA1 restricts c-Myc degradation in hematopoietic stem cells, modulates CD8+ T cell antitumor immunity, and regulates airway hyperreactivity through IL-4/STAT6 pathway. Functional hypothesis: TESPA1 acts as a calcium-dependent signaling scaffold that bridges ITPR1-mediated calcium release with GRB2/PLCG1 signaling cascades and COP9 signalosome-mediated protein stability control, coordinating T-cell receptor signaling during thymic positive selection and maintaining hematopoietic stem cell quiescence through c-Myc regulation.
