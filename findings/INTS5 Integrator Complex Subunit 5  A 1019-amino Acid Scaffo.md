## Description
INTS5 (Integrator Complex Subunit 5) is a 1019-amino acid nuclear protein characterized by a bipartite domain architecture (IPR029444/IPR029445/IPR040316/PF14837/PF14838/PTHR31697, positions 29-251 for N-terminal domain, 290-999 for C-terminal domain, 1-1007 for integrator complex subunit 5 family) containing two structured regions connected by flexible linkers mediating multi-protein complex assembly, classified under integrator complex organization (GO:0032039), RNA 3'-end processing (GO:0031124), and transcription termination (GO:0006353). STRING interactions reveal an exceptionally tight functional network exclusively with other Integrator complex subunits forming the catalytic core: INTS6 (0.999, integrator complex subunit 6 - highest confidence partner indicating obligate heterodimerization), INTS10 (0.999), INTS2 (0.999), INTS1 (0.999, catalytic metallo-beta-lactamase endonuclease), INTS12 (0.999), INTS9 (0.999), INTS7 (0.999), INTS3 (0.994), INTS13 (0.929), and PPP2R1A (0.582, protein phosphatase 2A scaffold linking to dephosphorylation regulation), indicating INTS5 functions as an integral structural scaffold within the 14-subunit Integrator holoenzyme. HPA data shows low tissue specificity with detection in all tissues, low cancer specificity, low immune cell specificity, cell type-enhanced single cell specificity, classified as an essential intracellular protein localized to nucleoplasm and cytosol. ClinVar contains 12 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number gains and losses spanning 11q12.1-13.3, 11p11.12-q13.1, 11q12.2-13.5, 11p13-q25, and 11p15.5-q25 regions (deletions and duplications affecting chromosome 11 including the INTS5 locus at 11q12.3), though specific monogenic disease phenotypes remain under characterization (significance marked '?' for most); the genomic location overlaps with regions implicated in neurodevelopmental disorders and hematologic malignancies. AlphaFold structure (AF-Q6P9B9-F1) predicts high confidence folding (pLDDT 77.1) consistent with a well-structured multi-domain scaffold protein containing ordered N-terminal and C-terminal domains connected by structured linkers typical of large complex assembly factors. With only 7 papers, INTS5 represents a genuine 'dark' gene with emerging mechanistic understanding (2009-2024): structural basis of Integrator complex assembly revealing INTS5's role in heterodimerization with INTS6 forming the complex backbone; catalytic core structure showing INTS5 positioning the INTS1 endonuclease for snRNA cleavage; Integrator prevents dedifferentiation of intermediate neural progenitors back into neural stem cells in Drosophila linking to neurodevelopment; INTS subunits function in hematopoiesis by modulating Smad/BMP signaling connecting transcription regulation to blood cell development; and mutations in Integrator subunits associated with neurodevelopmental disorders and cancers. The mechanistic hypothesis posits that INTS5 functions as a structural scaffold that: (1) forms an obligate heterodimer with INTS6 (0.999 score) creating the architectural backbone of the Integrator complex; (2) positions the catalytic INTS1 endonuclease (0.999 score) relative to nascent RNA substrates enabling precise 3'-end cleavage of snRNAs, enhancer RNAs, and promoter-upstream transcripts; (3) coordinates with INTS9/INTS11 module recruiting protein phosphatase 2A (PPP2R1A 0.582 score) for RNA Pol II CTD dephosphorylation coupling cleavage to termination; (4) maintains neural progenitor identity by terminating aberrant transcription programs preventing dedifferentiation explaining neurodevelopmental disorder associations; (5) modulates Smad/BMP signaling in hematopoietic lineages through Integrator-dependent processing of pathway regulators; chromosome 11q12.3 CNVs may contribute to developmental phenotypes through dosage effects on Integrator complex stoichiometry affecting global transcription regulation.
