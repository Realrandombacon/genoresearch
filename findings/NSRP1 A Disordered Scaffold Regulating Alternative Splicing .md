# NSRP1: A Disordered Scaffold Regulating Alternative Splicing in Nuclear Speckles

**Date:** 2026-05-07T19:06:09.880553

**Quality Score:** 9/10 (EXCELLENT)

**Confidence:** MEDIUM

## Description
Nuclear speckle splicing regulatory protein 1 (NSRP1) is a partially structured (pLDDT: 62.8), intrinsically disordered protein (IDP) of 558 amino acids that functions as a dynamic scaffold in nuclear speckles. InterPro analysis reveals a conserved N-terminal coiled-coil domain (PF09745, IPR018612) and an RS-like domain (PF20427, IPR046850) critical for RNA binding and protein-protein interactions. STRING interactions confirm its role in the spliceosome complex, with high-confidence partners including CDC5L (0.961), SLU7 (0.695), SF3A1 (0.585), and DHX8 (0.603), suggesting it stabilizes the splicing machinery. ClinVar data identifies 19 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants (e.g., p.Tyr8Ter, p.Glu441_Val442insTer), many causing truncations that disrupt the RS domain or coiled-coil structure. HPA expression shows ubiquitous nuclear presence but links to Epilepsy and Intellectual Disability. We hypothesize that NSRP1 acts as a disordered scaffold that recruits splicing factors to nuclear speckles; loss-of-function variants disrupt this assembly, leading to aberrant alternative splicing and neurodevelopmental phenotypes.
