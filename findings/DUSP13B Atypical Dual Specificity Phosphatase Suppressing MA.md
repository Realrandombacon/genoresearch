## Description
DUSP13B (UniProt Q9UII6), also known as MDSP/SKRP4/TMDP, encodes a compact 198-amino acid atypical dual specificity phosphatase containing a canonical DSP catalytic domain (IPR000340/PF00782, aa 53-186) within the protein tyrosine phosphatase superfamily (G3DSA:3.90.190.10, aa 25-197). The active site motif (IPR016130, aa 136-146) and tyrosine-specific phosphatase domain (IPR000387, aa 114-172) confirm enzymatic capacity for dephosphorylating both phosphotyrosine and phosphoserine/threonine residues. AlphaFold predicts an exceptionally well-folded structure (pLDDT: 90.8), rare among dark genes and indicative of stable catalytic architecture.\n\nSTRING interactions reveal connections to chromatin regulation (LMNA lamin A/C, 0.658; KAT6B histone acetyltransferase, 0.471), neurodegeneration (ATXN1 ataxin-1, 0.492), and ribosomal biogenesis (UTP15, 0.488), with homology to related phosphatase DUSP11 (0.407). ClinVar contains 16 pathogenic variants, predominantly 10q copy number gains/losses affecting the DUSP13B locus.\n\nWith only 8 papers, DUSP13B is a genuine dark gene with emerging oncogenic relevance. Mechanistic studies demonstrate: (1) inhibition of stress-activated MAPKs (JNK/p38) and suppression of AP-1-dependent transcription (2011, 16 citations); (2) critical role in the SHH/DUSP13B/p-STAT3 axis driving acquired osimertinib resistance in EGFR-mutant NSCLC (2024, 9 citations); (3) pharmacological inhibition by PTP inhibitor V and NSC 663284. Testis-enriched expression suggests roles in spermatogenesis. This positions DUSP13B as a therapeutically targetable phosphatase bridging developmental signaling (SHH) to oncogenic STAT3 activation in lung cancer.
