# CFAP53: Coiled-Coil Scaffold Protein Critical for Nodal Cilia Function and Left-Right Patterning

## Description
CFAP53 (aliases CCDC11, HTX6) encodes a 514 amino acid coiled-coil scaffold protein essential for cilia/flagella assembly and left-right body axis determination. The protein contains a Trichohyalin-plectin-homology (TPH) domain (IPR043597, PF13868, residues 158-485) that mediates intermediate filament binding and cytoskeletal organization. AlphaFold predicts a well-folded structure (pLDDT 86.0) consistent with stable coiled-coil dimerization.

HPA expression reveals testis-enriched transcription (76.5 nTPM) with secondary expression in fallopian tube (26.8 nTPM) and choroid plexus (19.4 nTPM), reflecting roles in spermatogenesis (flagella) and embryonic nodal cilia. Subcellular localization to basal body, nucleoplasm, and nuclear membrane suggests dual functions: (1) structural scaffolding at ciliary base for microtubule anchoring, and (2) potential transcriptional co-regulation of ciliogenesis genes.

ClinVar documents 58 pathogenic variants including frameshift c.286del (p.Glu96fs), nonsense c.1144G>T (p.Glu382Ter), splice site mutations, and copy number gains. These cause visceral heterotaxy-6 (HTX6), a ciliopathy featuring randomized organ situs due to defective nodal cilia-driven left-right signaling during embryogenesis.

Conservation is high: mouse ortholog Cfap53 (Q9D439, 514 aa) and zebrafish cfap53 (A0A0R4IFG5, 519 aa) show near-identical length, indicating strong purifying selection on TPH domain architecture.

MECHANISTIC HYPOTHESIS: CFAP53 functions as a coiled-coil homodimer that crosslinks basal body microtubules to intermediate filaments via its TPH domain, stabilizing the ciliary anchoring complex. Loss-of-function disrupts nodal cilia rotation, abolishing leftward nodal flow and Nodal/Pitx2 signaling cascade, resulting in heterotaxy. Testis-specific expression reflects parallel requirement for sperm flagella assembly.
