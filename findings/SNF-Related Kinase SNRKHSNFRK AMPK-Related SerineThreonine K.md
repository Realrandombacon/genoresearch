# SNF-Related Kinase (SNRK/HSNFRK): AMPK-Related Serine/Threonine Kinase with UBA Domain Regulating Energy Homeostasis, Inflammation, and Vascular Development

## Description
SNRK encodes a 765 aa cytoplasmic and nuclear serine/threonine kinase (UniProt Q9NRH2), also known as HSNFRK, that belongs to the AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK) subfamily of the CAMK group. The protein features a canonical N-terminal catalytic kinase domain (IPR000719, PF00069, cd14074, residues 12-269) containing the active site signature (PS00108, residues 135-147) and ATP-binding pocket (PS00107, residues 22-45). Uniquely, SNRK possesses a C-terminal Ubiquitin-Associated (UBA) domain (IPR015940, cd14339, residues 288-335) absent in many other kinases, suggesting a role in coupling phosphorylation signals to ubiquitin-mediated protein turnover or trafficking. STRING interactions place SNRK in a high-confidence network regulating metabolism and inflammation: DUSP5 (0.839) and DUSP2 (0.762), dual-specificity phosphatases that dephosphorylate MAP kinases; SFRP4 (0.720), a Wnt signaling antagonist; ADAMTS12 (0.715), a metalloprotease; HEY2 (0.667), a Notch pathway effector; STK11/LKB1 (0.629), the master upstream kinase activating AMPK family members; PIK3C2A (0.579), a class II phosphoinositide 3-kinase; CAB39 (0.550), a calcium-binding scaffold for LKB1; PTEN (0.527), a tumor suppressor phosphatase; and DUSP1 (0.436). HPA confirms ubiquitous expression across all tissues with localization to nucleoplasm, vesicles, plasma membrane, and cell junctions, consistent with roles in signal transduction and cellular architecture. ClinVar lists 7 pathogenic variants including large copy number gains/losses spanning chromosome 3p26.3-21.31 and duplications, demonstrating dosage sensitivity and linking SNRK disruption to developmental disorders, though no specific monogenic disease phenotype has been definitively assigned yet. AlphaFold structure prediction yields medium confidence (global pLDDT 57.1), reflecting a structured kinase core connected to flexible regulatory regions and the UBA domain, typical of signaling proteins requiring conformational plasticity. Mechanistically, SNRK functions as an energy-sensing node: (1) Activated by LKB1 (STK11) via phosphorylation of the activation loop, similar to AMPK, SNRK responds to metabolic stress; (2) The UBA domain may recruit ubiquitinated substrates or regulators, linking kinase activity to proteasomal degradation pathways; (3) Interaction with DUSP phosphatases suggests a feedback loop controlling MAPK signaling duration, critical for inflammatory responses; (4) Association with PTEN and PIK3C2A indicates crosstalk with the PI3K/Akt pathway, regulating cell growth and survival; (5) Expression at cell junctions and involvement with ADAMTS12/SFRP4 implies roles in vascular remodeling and endothelial integrity, potentially explaining links to preeclampsia and cardiovascular disease reported in literature. Disruption of SNRK would cause defective energy homeostasis, uncontrolled inflammation, and impaired vascular development.
