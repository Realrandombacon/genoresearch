# LCE1E: Skin-Enriched Late Cornified Envelope Protein Forming Cross-Linked Barrier Complex in Epidermal Differentiation via LCE Family Network

## Description
LCE1E (Late Cornified Envelope 1E, also LEP5) is a 118 amino acid intracellular protein (UniProt Q5T753) with a partially structured late cornified envelope architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 52.3, medium confidence), functioning as a structural component of the cornified cell envelope that forms cross-linked protein complexes during terminal epidermal differentiation to establish the skin barrier.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals LCE1E contains canonical late cornified envelope domains (IPR028205/PF14672, residues 1-25, 57-118) belonging to the late cornified envelope protein family. The protein contains proline-rich repeats (PR00021, residues 2-14, 15-24, 27-33) that mediate protein-protein interactions and cross-linking. GO annotations include epidermis development (GO:0008544), confirming roles in skin barrier formation. The medium-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 52.3) indicates a mix of ordered domains and intrinsically disordered regions, characteristic of cornified envelope proteins that require flexibility for cross-linking.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places LCE1E within the late cornified envelope protein network: strongest interactions with LCE1C (0.962, late cornified envelope protein 1C), LCE2C (0.896, late cornified envelope protein 2C), LCE2D (0.872, late cornified envelope protein 2D), LCE2B (0.860, late cornified envelope protein 2B), LCE3D (0.826, late cornified envelope protein 3D), LCE1B (0.814, late cornified envelope protein 1B), LCE1A (0.773, late cornified envelope protein 1A), LCE1D (0.738, late cornified envelope protein 1D), LCE1F (0.704, late cornified envelope protein 1F), and LCE3B (0.522, late cornified envelope protein 3B). The LCE1C-LCE2C-LCE2D-LCE2B cluster confirms LCE1E operates within the LCE family, forming heteromeric complexes during cornification.\n\nHPA expression shows tissue-enriched specificity with exclusive high expression in skin (57.8 nTPM), indicating critical roles in epidermal barrier function and keratinization. Cancer-enhanced specificity suggests LCE1E is upregulated in skin cancers, potentially promoting tumor progression through altered differentiation. Not detected in immune cells confirms skin-specific function. Subcellular classification as a predicted intracellular protein confirms cytoplasmic/nuclear localization before cross-linking to the cell envelope.\n\nClinVar documents 11 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly chromosome 1q21.3, 1q21.1-44, 1q21.1-23.1, and 1q12-23.1 copy number losses and gains. The high CNV burden indicates dosage sensitivity, with haploinsufficiency or overexpression causing skin barrier defects, ichthyosis, or psoriasis through disrupted cornified envelope formation. The 1q21 locus is the epidermal differentiation complex, containing multiple skin barrier genes.\n\nConservation is evident in the LCE family across mammals, indicating evolutionary conservation of skin barrier functions.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: LCE1E functions as a structural component of the cornified cell envelope that undergoes transglutaminase-mediated cross-linking during terminal epidermal differentiation. The skin enrichment (57.8 nTPM) indicates LCE1E is expressed in differentiating keratinocytes, where it incorporates into the insoluble protein scaffold beneath the plasma membrane. The proline-rich repeats (residues 2-33) provide flexible linkers for cross-linking to involucrin, loricrin, and other envelope proteins. The LCE family interaction network (LCE1C 0.962, LCE2C 0.896, LCE2D 0.872) indicates LCE1E forms heteromeric complexes with other LCE proteins, creating a robust barrier against environmental stress. The partially structured architecture (pLDDT 52.3) enables conformational flexibility for efficient cross-linking. Cancer-enhanced expression suggests LCE1E dysregulation contributes to squamous cell carcinoma through altered differentiation. CNV-mediated haploinsufficiency causes skin barrier defects through insufficient envelope protein production. LCE1E represents a critical skin barrier component linking epidermal differentiation, cornification, and environmental protection.
