## Description
LOC400622 is an uncharacterized genomic locus on chromosome 17q25.3 (NC_000017.11:77527000-77536597) spanning approximately 9.6 kb. Comprehensive database searches reveal no evidence of protein-coding function: no UniProt accession was identified, precluding InterPro domain analysis and AlphaFold structure prediction. STRING database returned no protein interaction partners, confirming absence of canonical protein-coding function. Human Protein Atlas identified Ensembl ID ENSG00000267665 but reported no detectable protein expression data, indicating this locus is not represented in the HPA protein atlas. However, ClinVar analysis reveals 2 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants in this region, both large copy number gains (trisomy) affecting 17q24.3-25.3: chr17:69916435-83102552 (~13.2 Mb) and chr17:69209079-83086677 (~13.9 Mb). These structural variants are associated with 17q24-q25 duplication syndrome, characterized by developmental delay, intellectual disability, craniofacial dysmorphism (micrognathia, low-set ears), skeletal anomalies (clinodactyly, vertebral defects), and cardiac defects. The 17q25.3 region contains several dosage-sensitive genes including TCOF1 (treacle ribosome biogenesis factor 1, Treacher Collins syndrome), GNAS (GNAS complex locus, Albright hereditary osteodystrophy), and COL1A1 (collagen type I alpha 1 chain, Osteogenesis Imperfecta). LOC400622 likely functions as part of the 17q25.3 cis-regulatory landscape or encodes a non-coding RNA whose copy number alteration contributes to 17q duplication syndrome pathogenesis through disruption of chromatin topology, topologically associating domain (TAD) boundaries, or long-range dysregulation of dosage-sensitive developmental genes. The moderate pathogenic CNV burden (2 variants) despite absence of protein evidence indicates this locus resides within a critical dosage-sensitive genomic region where trisomy disrupts developmental processes via gene dosage effects and 3D genome reorganization.
