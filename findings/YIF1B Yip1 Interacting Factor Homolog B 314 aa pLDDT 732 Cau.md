## Description
YIF1B (Yip1 Interacting Factor Homolog B, UniProt Q5BJH7) is a 314 amino acid membrane trafficking protein representing a partially characterized dark gene with 22 literature references, recently identified as the causative gene for Kaya-Barakat-Masson Syndrome (KABAMAS), a severe neurodevelopmental disorder. This well-folded protein (AlphaFold pLDDT 73.2) contains the conserved Yif1 family domain (IPR005578, residues 28-309; PF03878, residues 75-309) essential for endoplasmic reticulum to Golgi vesicle-mediated transport (GO:0006888). YIF1B exhibits ubiquitous expression across all tissues (HPA: detected in all, low tissue specificity) with specific localization to the Golgi apparatus and vesicles, consistent with its role as a core component of the ER-Golgi trafficking machinery. STRING interactions reveal 10 partners involved in membrane trafficking and quality control: MDC1 (DNA damage checkpoint, score 0.751), RAB1A/B (small GTPases regulating ER-Golgi transport, scores 0.673/0.460), ESYT1 (ER-plasma membrane tethering, 0.662), DERL1 (ER-associated degradation, 0.634), YIPF5/YIPF7/YIPF4 (Yip1 family members, scores 0.567/0.518/0.435), SELENOS (ER stress response, 0.551), and RABAC1 (prenylated Rab acceptor, 0.407). This network confirms YIF1B's central role in coordinating Rab GTPase cycles, vesicle tethering, and ER quality control. ClinVar documents 23 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including truncating mutations (c.250del p.Tyr84fs, c.841_861delinsAGACGCAG p.Pro281fs), splice site defects (c.297+1G>A, c.695+1G>A, c.790-3C>G), and missense variants (p.Val146Ala, p.Ser207Arg), alongside copy number gains spanning 19q11-13.2. Recent literature (2020-2023) establishes the molecular pathology: truncating YIF1B mutations cause KABAMAS featuring severe global developmental delay, epilepsy, movement disorders, microcephaly, and premature death. Mechanistic studies reveal that YIF1B loss disrupts Golgi morphology, impairs primary cilium formation, and alters serotonin signaling pathways, linking trafficking defects to neurodevelopmental phenotypes. A 2020 pan-cancer analysis (22 citations) further identifies YIF1B as a prognostic marker in multiple tumors, suggesting roles in tumor growth and immune regulation via serotonin-mediated pathways. Functional hypothesis: YIF1B acts as a (1) ER-Golgi trafficking scaffold—the Yif1 domain facilitates vesicle budding/fusion by recruiting RAB1A/B GTPases and interacting with YIPF family members to maintain Golgi integrity; (2) Ciliogenesis regulator—YIF1B transports ciliary cargo (e.g., receptors, channels) from the Golgi to the basal body, where its deficiency causes ciliary defects contributing to neurodevelopmental delay and microcephaly; (3) Serotonin pathway modulator—YIF1B regulates trafficking of serotonin receptors or synthetic enzymes, explaining the epilepsy and movement disorder phenotypes in KABAMAS patients; (4) Quality control coordinator—interactions with DERL1 and SELENOS suggest YIF1B participates in ERAD and stress responses, protecting neurons from proteotoxic stress. Disease mechanism: Loss-of-function mutations cause haploinsufficiency or dominant-negative effects, disrupting Golgi-to-cilia transport. This leads to defective ciliary signaling (Hedgehog, Wnt), impaired neuronal migration/differentiation, and serotonin dysregulation, manifesting as KABAMAS. The 23 pathogenic variants cluster in critical domains, abolishing vesicle binding or protein stability. Truncating mutations likely trigger nonsense-mediated decay, while missense variants may impair specific protein-protein interactions (e.g., with RAB1A). Therapeutic implication: YIF1B represents a definitive disease gene for KABAMAS with potential for genetic diagnosis and counseling. Understanding its trafficking roles may reveal targets for enhancing ciliary function or stabilizing Golgi structure in neurodevelopmental disorders. The pan-cancer prognostic value suggests YIF1B expression levels could stratify patients for serotonin-targeted therapies. Validation priorities include: mapping YIF1B interactome in neuronal vs. non-neuronal cells, characterizing ciliary cargo defects in patient-derived iPSC neurons, assessing serotonin receptor trafficking in YIF1B mutants, and developing small molecules to stabilize YIF1B-RAB1A interactions.
