## Description
ANKRD13A (Gene ID: 88455, UniProt Q8IZ07) is a 590 amino acid protein located on chromosome 12q24.11 (NC_000012.12, positions 109999212-110039762, ~40.5kb region). Also known as NY-REN-25 (cancer/testis antigen designation), this protein functions as a ubiquitin-modified protein reader involved in negative regulation of protein localization to endosomes and receptor internalization. InterPro domain analysis reveals a modular architecture: N-terminal ankyrin repeat domain (IPR002110, PF12796, positions 13-104) for protein-protein interactions, followed by ANKRD13 C-terminal domain (IPR055285, PF11904, positions 156-469), and three C-terminal ubiquitin-interacting motifs (UIMs; IPR003903, PS50330, positions 482-502, 519-538, 549-568, 574-590) that bind ubiquitinated substrates. AlphaFold structure prediction (AF-Q8IZ07-F1) shows high confidence (global pLDDT 73.2), indicating a well-folded stable protein. Human Protein Atlas shows ubiquitous expression across all tissues with low tissue specificity, localizing to vesicles, plasma membrane, and critically to primary cilium/cilium tip/transition zone - suggesting roles in ciliary signaling. STRING interactions reveal connections to EGFR (score 0.663, epidermal growth factor receptor), VCP (0.643, valosin-containing protein involved in ubiquitin-dependent protein degradation), KCNIP2 (0.598), UBA52 (0.491, ubiquitin A-52 residue ribosomal protein fusion), UBASH3A (0.476), RNF11 (0.472, E3 ubiquitin ligase), and ITCH (0.424, HECT-type E3 ubiquitin ligase) - placing ANKRD13A within ubiquitin-mediated endosomal sorting networks. ClinVar contains 10 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number gains (chr12:99532287-133777902)x3 and losses (chr12:103044333-111639805), plus splice site variant c.1234+2T>G, though clinical significance remains uncertain. Conservation evidenced by mouse ortholog Ankrd13a (Q80UP5, 588 aa). Functional hypothesis: ANKRD13A serves as a ubiquitin-dependent adaptor protein that recognizes ubiquitinated cargo proteins (particularly activated receptor tyrosine kinases like EGFR) via its UIM domains, then recruits them to the ANKRD13A ankyrin repeat scaffold for sorting into endosomal compartments. The primary cilium localization suggests specific roles in ciliary receptor trafficking and Hedgehog/Wnt signaling pathway regulation. Disease mechanism: Copy number alterations disrupt ANKRD13A dosage, leading to aberrant receptor internalization kinetics - haploinsufficiency may cause prolonged receptor signaling at plasma membrane while duplication may cause excessive receptor degradation. This could contribute to developmental disorders (12q24 region deletions/duplications) and potentially cancer (NY-REN-25 designation suggests cancer/testis antigen expression). Priority experiments include identification of ubiquitinated binding partners via UIM mutagenesis and mass spectrometry, characterization of ciliary trafficking defects in ANKRD13A knockout models, and investigation of EGFR signaling dynamics in cells with altered ANKRD13A expression.
