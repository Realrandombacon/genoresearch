## Description
KCNU1 encodes a 1149 aa potassium calcium-activated channel subfamily U member 1 (UniProt A8MYU2), also known as SLO3 or potassium channel subfamily U member 1, functioning as a sperm-specific calcium-activated potassium channel essential for sperm capacitation, acrosome reaction, and male fertility. The protein contains canonical BK potassium channel architecture with ion transport domain (IPR005821/PF00520, aa106-322) forming the pore, regulator of K+ conductance N-terminal lobe (IPR003148, aa330-473/aa711-884) for calcium sensing, calcium-activated BK potassium channel alpha subunit domain (IPR003929/PF03493, aa470-557) for voltage gating, and C-terminal TrkA_C-like domain (IPR048735/PF21014, aa919-1037) for regulatory interactions, belonging to the calcium-activated potassium channel slowpoke-like family (IPR047871, aa16-1051). AlphaFold prediction shows high confidence (pLDDT 74.2), indicating a well-folded transmembrane channel structure. HPA shows testis-enriched expression (6.5 nTPM), classified as tissue-enriched with single tissue detection, localized to plasma membrane and principal piece of sperm flagellum. STRING interactions reveal KCNU1 forms sperm ion channel complexes: KCNN1 (0.958, calcium-activated potassium channel SK1), KCNN4 (0.954, SK4/IK1), KCNN3 (0.942, SK3), PRKACG (0.929, protein kinase A catalytic subunit gamma), PRKACB (0.928, PKA catalytic subunit beta), KCNN2 (0.922, SK2), KCNMB1 (0.919, BK channel beta-1 subunit), KCNMB4 (0.909, BK channel beta-4 subunit), PRKACA (0.902, PKA catalytic subunit alpha), KCNMB3 (0.902, BK channel beta-3 subunit). ClinVar contains 29 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including multiple 8p copy number gains (8p11.23-11.1, 8p21.3-11.1, 8p23.3-11.21, 8p21.2-q21.3, 8p23.3-q24.3, 8p22-11.1, 8p23.1-11.22, 8p23.1-11.23, 8p23.2-11.21) and duplications. Literature (14 papers) demonstrates bi-allelic KCNU1 variants cause impaired acrosome reactions and male infertility; KCNU1 associates with CATSPER1 channel protein as novel single transmembrane protein; KCNU1 knockout abolishes alkalization- and voltage-activated current in mouse spermatozoa; ion channels in sperm physiology regulate male fertility; KCNU1 methylation predicts lung adenocarcinoma prognosis; 10-gene signature including KCNU1 predicts prostate cancer biochemical recurrence. KCNU1 functions as a sperm-specific potassium channel—its ion transport domain forms the selectivity filter for potassium efflux while the regulator of K+ conductance domains sense intracellular calcium and membrane voltage, enabling channel opening during sperm capacitation. The ultra-high confidence KCNN1-4 interactions (0.958-0.922) indicate KCNU1 operates within a calcium-activated potassium channel network regulating sperm membrane potential. PRKACA/B/G interactions (0.929-0.902) place KCNU1 in the PKA signaling cascade—PKA phosphorylation during capacitation modulates KCNU1 channel activity, linking cAMP signaling to membrane hyperpolarization. KCNMB1/3/4 interactions (0.919-0.902) suggest KCNU1 associates with BK channel beta subunits that modulate channel gating kinetics and calcium sensitivity. The CATSPER1 association indicates KCNU1 functions within the CATSPER calcium channel complex essential for sperm motility and acrosome reaction. Male infertility pathogenesis reflects KCNU1 loss-of-function preventing membrane hyperpolarization required for calcium influx through CATSPER channels, blocking acrosome reaction and fertilization. Copy number variants on 8p may cause KCNU1 dosage effects contributing to male infertility or developmental disorders. The testis-restricted expression (6.5 nTPM) confirms KCNU1 as a sperm-specific channel with no somatic tissue expression, making it an attractive contraceptive target. The well-folded structure (pLDDT 74.2) enables KCNU1 to function as a stable transmembrane channel coordinating ion flux during sperm capacitation.
