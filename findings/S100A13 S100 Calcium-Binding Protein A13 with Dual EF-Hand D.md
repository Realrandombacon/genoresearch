The mechanistic hypothesis: S100A13 functions as a calcium-dependent molecular switch that undergoes conformational change upon Ca²⁺ binding to its dual EF-hand domains, exposing hydrophobic surfaces that bind target proteins (FGF1, IL1A) lacking signal peptides. In resting cells (low Ca²⁺), S100A13 adopts closed conformation sequestering hydrophobic residues. Upon calcium influx (stress, injury, activation), EF-hands coordinate Ca²⁺ ions inducing helix reorientation that exposes binding interface. S100A13 then forms heterotetrameric complex with FGF1 or IL1A, facilitating their translocation across plasma membrane via direct membrane interaction or ABC transporter cooperation. In pancreatic adenocarcinoma, tumor cell-derived S100A13 binds FGF1 enabling secretion that activates FGFR on cancer-associated fibroblasts, promoting CAF activation, ECM remodeling, and immunosuppression. In osteosarcoma, S100A13 regulates stemness markers (OCT4, SOX2, NANOG) potentially through calcium-dependent transcription factor modulation or chromatin remodeling via PBRM1 interaction. In diabetes, elevated S100A13 levels correlate with insulin resistance and beta-cell dysfunction, possibly through RAGE-mediated inflammatory signaling. The FGF1 interaction (score 0.995) is most significant: FGF1 lacks signal peptide and requires S100A13 for stress-induced release during angiogenesis and tissue repair. The AGER/RAGE connection suggests extracellular S100A13 acts as damage-associated molecular pattern (DAMP) activating NF-κB inflammation. The S100A6/S100A10 paralogs suggest functional redundancy with tissue-specific specialization. The IL1A connection positions S100A13 in inflammasome-independent cytokine release. The 1q21 CNV dosage changes alter S100A13 expression: deletion reduces non-classical secretion impairing stress response (wound healing defects, immune dysfunction); duplication increases FGF1/IL1A release promoting chronic inflammation and tumorigenesis. The nucleolar localization suggests ribosome biogenesis or stress granule role.
