## Description
ZBTB26 (Zinc finger and BTB domain-containing protein 26, UniProt Q9HCK0) is a 441-amino acid nuclear protein located on chromosome 9q33.2. InterPro analysis reveals a canonical dual-domain architecture: an N-terminal BTB/POZ domain (IPR000210/PF00651, aa 23-127) mediating homodimerization and recruitment of transcriptional corepressors such as NCOR and SMRT, followed by a C-terminal array of three C2H2-type zinc fingers (IPR013087/PF00096, aa 273-353) conferring sequence-specific DNA binding to target gene promoters. The protein belongs to the BTB-zinc finger family (IPR050457, aa 3-203) known for transcriptional regulation in development and immunity. AlphaFold structure prediction achieves medium confidence (pLDDT 53.5), indicating a partially structured protein with ordered BTB and zinc finger domains connected by flexible linkers, consistent with transcription factors requiring conformational flexibility for chromatin engagement. Human Protein Atlas annotates ZBTB26 as a predicted intracellular transcription factor with DNA-binding molecular function, showing low tissue specificity and ubiquitous expression across all tissues, suggesting a broad regulatory role in cellular homeostasis. STRING interaction network reveals 10 partners enriched in transcriptional regulators: ZBTB6 (0.717, paralogous BTB-zinc finger protein), PBK (0.622, PDZ-binding kinase involved in mitosis), ELF2 (0.504, E74-like factor in lymphocyte development), TGIF2LX (0.442, TGFβ-induced transcription factor), ETV2 (0.437, ETS family transcription factor in hematopoiesis), IRF5 (0.428, interferon regulatory factor in innate immunity), PRDM10 (0.416, PR domain transcriptional regulator), ZBTB48 (0.415, telomere-associated zinc finger protein), ZNF740 (0.413, zinc finger protein), and POU2F3 (0.402, POU domain transcription factor in epithelial differentiation). This interactome positions ZBTB26 within transcriptional networks governing immune cell development, mitotic progression, and epithelial differentiation. ClinVar contains 29 pathogenic variants including multiple copy-number gains and losses across 9p-q, demonstrating extreme dosage sensitivity where both haploinsufficiency and overexpression disrupt normal gene regulation. Conservation across mammals (mouse Q8BZJ5, rat Q6AYA8) indicates ancient evolutionary origin. ZBTB26 likely functions as a transcriptional repressor utilizing its BTB domain to recruit chromatin-modifying complexes and its zinc fingers to bind specific promoter elements, potentially regulating immune response genes, cell cycle progression, or epithelial differentiation programs.
