## Description
LOC108281120 is a CAGE-defined low expression enhancer element on chromosome 6p21 (NC_000006.12:26550349-26550883) spanning approximately 534 bp. This genomic element is located in the intergenic region between HMGN4 (high mobility group nucleosomal binding domain 4) and the uncharacterized lncRNA LOC105374988. It was defined as a low expression actively transcribed enhancer based on the presence of balanced bidirectional capped transcripts by cap analysis of gene expression (CAGE) and validated as a functional enhancer by reporter assays. Comprehensive database searches confirm no protein-coding function: no UniProt accession was identified, precluding InterPro domain analysis and AlphaFold structure prediction. STRING database returned no protein interaction partners, consistent with non-coding regulatory element function. Human Protein Atlas could not resolve this locus to an Ensembl ID, as enhancers are not represented in protein expression atlases. However, ClinVar analysis reveals 2 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants: large copy number gains spanning 6p25.3-12.3 (chr6:156974-46789291, ~46.6 Mb whole-arm duplication) and 6p25.2-21.33 (chr6:3224310-30657190, ~27.4 Mb). The 6p21 region encompasses the Major Histocompatibility Complex (MHC) class I and II clusters, containing HLA-A, HLA-B, HLA-C, HLA-DR, HLA-DQ, HLA-DP, complement genes (C4, C2, factor B), heat shock proteins (HSPA1A/B), and cytokine genes (TNF, LTA). LOC108281120 likely functions as: (1) a cis-regulatory enhancer controlling expression of MHC class I/II genes or neighboring immune response genes, (2) a chromatin looping element facilitating long-range promoter-enhancer interactions within the highly polymorphic 6p21 topologically associating domain (TAD), (3) a transcription factor binding hub for immune-specific activators (e.g., CIITA, NLRC5), or (4) an enhancer RNA (eRNA) producer modulating local chromatin accessibility. The moderate pathogenic CNV burden (2 variants) combined with validated enhancer activity indicates this locus represents a critical immune regulatory element where copy number alterations disrupt: (1) enhancer RNA production affecting MHC gene transcription, (2) 3D genome organization of the MHC locus, (3) allele-specific expression balance of HLA genes, or (4) immune cell differentiation programs, leading to immunodeficiency, autoimmunity, or altered antigen presentation phenotypes associated with 6p duplications.
