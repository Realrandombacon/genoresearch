## Description
WDR12 (Q9GZL7, 423 aa) is a WD40 repeat-containing ribosome biogenesis protein functioning as an essential component of the PeBoW complex (PES1-BOP1-WDR12) required for 60S ribosomal subunit maturation. InterPro identifies complete domain architecture: N-terminal NLE/NUC135 domain (IPR012972, PF08154, aa 5-70) for complex assembly, followed by seven WD40 repeats (IPR001680, PF00400, aa 91-417) forming a β-propeller scaffold for protein-protein interactions, with conserved GH-WD motifs (PS00678). AlphaFold predicts high-confidence structure (pLDDT 89.1, residues 1-423) consistent with stable WD40 β-propeller folding. STRING reveals 10 exceptionally high-confidence interactions (all scores >0.978) forming the ribosome biogenesis machinery: PES1 (0.999), BOP1 (0.999), WDR74 (0.998), BRIX1 (0.998), NIFK (0.998), EBNA1BP2 (0.998), MAK16 (0.998), RSL1D1 (0.998), RSL24D1 (0.989), and NIP7 (0.978). HPA shows ubiquitous expression across tissues with nucleolar localization, consistent with ribosome biogenesis function; classified as essential protein. ClinVar contains 31 pathogenic variants including nonsense mutations (p.Tyr380Ter), splice variants, and copy number variations (deletions/duplications at 2q32-q34), though no specific diseases are yet assigned. Functional hypothesis: WDR12 serves as a structural scaffold within the PeBoW complex that coordinates late-stage 60S ribosomal subunit maturation in the nucleolus. The WD40 β-propeller provides interaction surfaces for recruiting processing factors (BRIX1, NIFK) and assembly chaperones (WDR74, MAK16), while the NLE domain mediates heterotrimerization with PES1 and BOP1. Near-perfect STRING scores indicate obligate complex formation - WDR12 cannot function independently. The complex likely facilitates rRNA processing, ribosomal protein incorporation, and nuclear export of pre-60S particles. Pathogenic variants disrupt complex assembly or nucleolar localization, causing ribosomopathy phenotypes through impaired protein synthesis capacity. Conservation across eukaryotes (mouse, zebrafish, Arabidopsis) underscores essential role in fundamental cellular machinery.
