## Description
LOC129389004 is a 200bp cis-regulatory element (CRE) located on chromosome 2q36.3 (GRCh38: 229822228-229822428), functionally validated as a transcriptional silencer through STARR-seq massively parallel reporter assays (MPRAs) in K562 erythroleukemia cells. This locus lacks canonical promoter, enhancer, or CTCF-bound insulator chromatin signatures but demonstrates robust repressive activity against the super core promoter SCP1. As a non-coding regulatory sequence, it has no UniProt accession, protein domains, STRING interaction partners, or Human Protein Atlas expression data (query not applicable for non-coding locus).\n\nClinVar analysis reveals 12 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variants disrupting this region, representing a HIGH CNV burden. The variant spectrum shows EXTREME DUPLICATION BIAS: 11 duplications (copy number gains spanning various 2q35-37.3 regions, 5-50 Mb) versus 1 deletion (copy number loss spanning 2q36.3-37.2 ~8 Mb). This 11:1 ratio (92% duplications) indicates STRONG gain-of-function dosage sensitivity where excessive silencing activity is far more pathogenic than reduced silencing. This pattern contrasts sharply with LOC129389584 (1:1 balanced) and LOC129390753 (~1:1), suggesting distinct regulatory mechanisms.\n\nThe genomic context places LOC129389004 within 2q36.3, a region associated with 2q36.3 microduplication syndrome and 2q37 monosomy syndrome. Nearby genes include SATB2 (special AT-rich sequence binding protein 2, chromatin organizer essential for craniofacial development and neuronal differentiation, haploinsufficiency causes glass syndrome), GPC1 (glypican-1, heparan sulfate proteoglycan), and PAX3 (paired box 3, neural crest development, Waardenburg syndrome). The 2q36.3 region is critical for skeletal and craniofacial development.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: LOC129389004 functions as a critical distal silencer within the 2q36.3 topologically associating domain (TAD), specifically regulating SATB2 and other craniofacial/neuronal development genes in neural crest and osteoblast contexts. The extreme 11:1 duplication bias indicates that GAIN-of-silencing function (triplication causing 150% repressive activity) is dramatically more pathogenic than LOSS-of-function (haploinsufficiency, 50% activity). This suggests the target gene(s) require MINIMAL repression - even modest increases in silencing cause severe developmental defects. The mechanism likely involves recruitment of neural crest-specific co-repressor complexes (possibly involving SATB2 autoregulation, or recruiting SIN3A/HDAC, Polycomb PRC2 complexes) that maintain low basal expression of differentiation genes. In K562 erythroleukemia cells, this silencer may be inactive or repurposed. The 2q36.3 location near SATB2 (master chromatin organizer for craniofacial patterning) raises the possibility that LOC129389004 prevents premature SATB2 expression in progenitor cells. SATB2 haploinsufficiency causes cleft palate, dental anomalies, and intellectual disability (glass syndrome). The duplication bias suggests that excessive silencing of SATB2 (or downstream targets) during critical developmental windows causes severe craniofacial malformations. The high CNV burden (12 variants) indicates this element has large mutational target size and strong phenotypic effects.
