## Description
PRRT4 (Proline-rich transmembrane protein 4, UniProt C9JH25) is a 899-amino acid integral membrane protein located on chromosome 7q31.33. InterPro analysis reveals characteristic proline-rich transmembrane architecture: proline-rich transmembrane domain-containing protein family (IPR052836/PTHR35578, aa 1-896/1-896) with dedicated proline-rich transmembrane protein 3/4 domain (IPR059081/PF25987, aa 350-640) forming a structured core region embedded within extensive proline-rich sequences. GO annotations suggest roles in membrane organization and protein-protein interactions. AlphaFold structure prediction achieves medium confidence (pLDDT 52.6), indicating a partially structured protein with ordered transmembrane/proline-rich domain core connected by extensive intrinsically disordered regions — architecture typical of scaffold proteins requiring conformational flexibility for dynamic complex assembly at membrane interfaces. Human Protein Atlas shows group-enriched expression (detected in many tissues) with highest expression in bone marrow (54.5 nTPM), followed by adipose tissue (15.6 nTPM), low cancer specificity, and subcellular localization to nucleoplasm, peroxisomes, and plasma membrane — consistent with dual roles in hematopoietic cell regulation and potential metabolic functions linking lipid storage to blood cell production. This interactome suggests PRRT4 functions within membrane trafficking, RNA processing, and cytoskeletal organization networks potentially linked to hematopoietic differentiation. ClinVar contains 23 pathogenic variants consisting of large copy-number gains and losses across 7q22.3-q36.3, demonstrating dosage sensitivity where genomic rearrangements disrupting chromosome 7q cause disease phenotypes potentially affecting hematopoiesis and development. Conservation across mammals (mouse B2RU40, 902 aa; rat D4A9R4, 902 aa) and vertebrates (fish A0A668TFY5, 529 aa; A0A3P8R4W6, 945 aa) indicates ancient evolutionary origin tied to fundamental membrane scaffold mechanisms. PRRT4 likely functions as a proline-rich membrane scaffold protein, using its structured transmembrane domain to anchor at plasma membrane or peroxisomal membranes while disordered proline-rich regions recruit SH3 domain-containing partner proteins to organize signaling complexes regulating hematopoietic cell differentiation, adipocyte metabolism, and membrane trafficking — mechanisms critical for blood cell production and frequently dysregulated in hematological malignancies where 7q deletions are common cytogenetic abnormalities.
