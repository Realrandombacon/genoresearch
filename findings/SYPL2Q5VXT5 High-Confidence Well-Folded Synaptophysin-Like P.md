## Description
SYPL2 (Synaptophysin-like protein 2, also known as MG29, Mitsugumin 29) is a 272 amino acid membrane protein (UniProt Q5VXT5) with high-confidence well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 80.8), characteristic of stable tetraspanin-like vesicle proteins that function in membrane trafficking and organelle organization. The protein belongs to the synaptophysin/synaptoporin family (IPR001285/PTHR10306, residues 13-260) involved in synaptic vesicle function, contains a MARVEL domain (IPR008253/PS51225, residues 30-238) that mediates membrane apposition and vesicle fusion, and has a membrane-associating domain (PF01284, residues 33-232) with four transmembrane helices typical of synaptophysin family members. SYPL2 is located on chromosome 1p13.3 and is involved in substantia nigra development, predicted to be located in membrane and synaptic vesicle, active in synaptic vesicle membrane.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals a high-confidence network of excitation-contraction coupling and calcium handling proteins: JPH2 (0.768, Junctophilin-2, T-tubule/SR junction), JPH4 (0.752, Junctophilin-4), SRL (0.662, Sarcalumenin, SR calcium binding), ASPH (0.643, Aspartate beta-hydroxylase, calcium homeostasis), CASQ1 (0.642, Calsequestrin-1, SR calcium storage), TRDN (0.637, Triadin, SR calcium release), RYR1 (0.634, Ryanodine receptor 1, SR calcium channel), JPH3 (0.591, Junctophilin-3), UNCX (0.575, Uncx homeobox protein), and JPH1 (0.453, Junctophilin-1). These interactions confirm SYPL2 functions within the skeletal muscle excitation-contraction coupling machinery, specifically at T-tubule/sarcoplasmic reticulum junctions.\n\nHPA expression reveals SYPL2 has group-enriched expression with highest levels in skeletal muscle (340.5 nTPM) and tongue (163.6 nTPM), detected in many tissues. This highly muscle-enriched expression pattern indicates SYPL2 plays a specialized role in skeletal muscle function. Subcellular localization includes plasma membrane and cytosol, consistent with T-tubule membrane localization. The protein is classified as a predicted membrane protein. Cell type-enriched single cell specificity suggests expression in skeletal muscle myocytes. Not detected in immune cells indicates SYPL2 is not involved in immune function.\n\nClinVar documents 16 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including copy number losses (1p13.3-13.2, 1p21.3-13.2, 1p22.1-11.2, 1p36.33-q44 deletions), copy number gains (1p22.1-11.2, 1p36.33-q44 amplifications), single allele deletions, and a microsatellite insertion (c.728GCCAGG[3] p.Gln246_Asp247insGlyGln). The variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity. While no specific Mendelian disease is annotated, 1p CNVs are associated with developmental delay, intellectual disability, and muscle weakness. Given SYPL2's role in excitation-contraction coupling and skeletal muscle enrichment, loss-of-function may cause congenital myopathy phenotypes through impaired calcium handling and SR organization.\n\nConservation is strong across vertebrates: human (Q5VXT5, 272 aa), mouse (O89104, 264 aa), rabbit (O62646, 264 aa), indicating an ancient and essential function in muscle physiology conserved in mammals.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: SYPL2/MG29 functions as a tetraspanin-like membrane protein at T-tubule/sarcoplasmic reticulum junctions that regulates excitation-contraction coupling in skeletal muscle. The well-folded MARVEL domain (pLDDT 80.8) mediates membrane apposition between T-tubules and SR, facilitating efficient calcium release. Through interactions with junctophilins (JPH1/2/3/4), SYPL2 stabilizes the physical coupling between voltage-gated calcium channels and ryanodine receptors. CASQ1 and TRDN interactions link SYPL2 to SR calcium storage and release machinery. SRL interaction connects SYPL2 to SR luminal calcium buffering. The synaptophysin family membership suggests SYPL2 may also regulate vesicle trafficking for SR membrane maintenance. Skeletal muscle-enriched expression (340.5 nTPM) indicates SYPL2 is critical for muscle contraction. 1p CNVs and microsatellite variants cause SYPL2 dysfunction, potentially disrupting T-tubule/SR junctions and causing myopathy phenotypes through impaired calcium-induced calcium release. SYPL2 represents a candidate gene for congenital myopathies and malignant hyperthermia susceptibility.
