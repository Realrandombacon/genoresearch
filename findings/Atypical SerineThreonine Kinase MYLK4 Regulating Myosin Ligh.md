## Description
MYLK4 (Myosin Light Chain Kinase Family Member 4, UniProt Q86YV6) is a 388-amino acid atypical serine/threonine protein kinase functioning in myosin light chain phosphorylation and cellular contractility regulation. InterPro analysis confirms canonical kinase architecture: protein kinase domain (IPR000719/PF00069, aa 108-361) with catalytic domain (cd14193, aa 101-361), active site signature (IPR008271/PS00108, aa 223-235), ATP binding site (IPR017441/PS00107, aa 112-135), and protein kinase-like superfamily fold (IPR011009, aa 107-367) with GO annotations for protein kinase activity (GO:0004672), ATP binding (GO:0005524), and protein phosphorylation (GO:0006468). STRING interactions reveal exceptionally high-confidence partnerships with myosin light chain substrates: MYL6B (0.940), MYL12A (0.937), MYL3 (0.935), MYL4 (0.927), MYL6 (0.927), MYL9 (0.925), MYL12B (0.920), MYL2 (0.906), plus catalytic subunits of PKA: PRKACG (0.915) and PRKACA (0.914), suggesting integration into cAMP signaling and contractility regulation. HPA shows group-enriched expression (detected in some tissues) with highest expression in tongue (66.7 nTPM) and skeletal muscle (27.0 nTPM), cancer-enhanced expression pattern, and nucleoli/Golgi/vesicle localization consistent with kinase trafficking and substrate access. AlphaFold predicts high-confidence structure (pLDDT 79.9) for the full 388 aa, indicating a well-folded compact kinase domain with structured catalytic core. ClinVar documents 59 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including missense mutations (p.Lys229Asn, p.Gln373Arg) and extensive copy number variations on chromosome 6p25.3-25.1 (losses and gains), though specific disease phenotypes remain undefined. Literature (46 papers) reveals emerging functions: (1) Dual ROCK1/2-MYLK4 kinase inhibition preserves visual function in neuromyelitis optica spectrum disorder optic neuritis rat model (2025); (2) Myofiber androgen receptor increases muscle strength via skeletal muscle splicing variant of MYLK4 (2025); (3) MYLK4 promotes colorectal cancer progression by regulating lipid metabolism reprogramming via targeting ferroptosis (1 citation, 2026); (4) Osimertinib inhibits MYLK4-mediated phosphorylation of CDKAL1 to suppress stemness and chemoresistance in rhabdomyosarcoma (2026); (5) Potent (iso)ellipticine-based inhibitor synthesis and evaluation (2023). Functional hypothesis: MYLK4 serves as an atypical myosin light chain kinase phosphorylating regulatory light chains (MYL2, MYL6, MYL9, MYL12A/B) to modulate actomyosin contractility, cell migration, and muscle function, with emerging roles in cancer metabolism and ferroptosis regulation. Mechanistic proposal: The kinase domain (aa 108-361) binds ATP via the conserved ATP-binding pocket (aa 112-135) and positions the catalytic loop (aa 223-235) for phosphotransfer to serine/threonine residues on myosin light chain substrates; phosphorylation of MYL2/MYL9 increases myosin II ATPase activity and actin filament sliding velocity, regulating cell contractility and migration; in skeletal muscle, androgen receptor signaling induces MYLK4 splicing variants enhancing muscle strength; in colorectal cancer, MYLK4 overexpression drives lipid metabolic reprogramming and ferroptosis resistance through unknown downstream effectors potentially involving CDKAL1 phosphorylation; pharmacological inhibition (dual ROCK-MYLK4 inhibitors, ellipticine derivatives) disrupts contractility signaling and cancer cell survival; copy number variants on 6p may alter MYLK4 dosage affecting muscle development and cancer susceptibility.
