## Description
TELO2 encodes an 837 aa intracellular scaffold protein (UniProt Q9Y4R8), also known as CLK2 or YHFS (You-Hoover-Fong Syndrome), that functions as an essential co-chaperone component of the R2TP-TELO2 complex required for assembly and stability of PIKK family kinases (MTOR, ATR, ATM, DNA-PKcs, SMG1, TRRAP) and mTORC1/2 signaling complexes. This gene is well-characterized with 70 literature publications but remains clinically significant as causative of autosomal recessive You-Hoover-Fong syndrome. The protein features extensive ARM repeat architecture: multiple armadillo-type fold domains (IPR016024/SSF48371, residues 176-826) providing protein-protein interaction scaffolding; a TELO2 ARM repeat domain (IPR057348/PF25320, residues 324-422) mediating specific partner recognition; a conserved telomere length regulation domain (IPR019337/PF10193, residues 512-620) critical for PIKK binding; C-terminal domain superfamily (IPR038528/G3DSA:1.25.40.720, residues 507-628/682-762) forming structured interaction surfaces; classified within TEL2 family (IPR051970/PTHR15830, residues 6-827). STRING interactions reveal TELO2 operates within extremely high-confidence PIKK assembly and mTOR signaling networks: MTOR (0.999), TTI2 (0.999), TTI1 (0.999) forming the core R2TP-TELO2 chaperone complex; FANCM (0.998), Fanconi anemia complementation group M in DNA interstrand crosslink repair; MLST8 (0.994), mTOR-associated lethal with SEC13 protein 8; RPTOR (0.991), regulatory-associated protein of mTOR (mTORC1 component); ATR (0.989), ataxia telangiectasia and Rad3-related kinase in DNA damage response; FAAP24 (0.988), FANCM-associated polypeptide 24; RICTOR (0.984), rapamycin-insensitive companion of mTOR (mTORC2 component); and RUVBL2 (0.983), RuvB-like AAA ATPase 2 in R2TP complex. HPA confirms ubiquitous expression with low tissue specificity (detected in all tissues), low cancer and immune cell specificity, nuclear bodies/cytosol subcellular localization, classified as disease-related/essential/predicted intracellular membrane protein with intellectual disability disease involvement. ClinVar lists 117 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including canonical splice site mutations (c.335+1G>C, c.1282-1G>T, c.2034+2T>C), frameshift deletion (c.800_801del p.Val27fs), synonymous variant (c.831G>T p.Gly277=), and numerous intronic variants (c.2407+197T>G, c.2407+171T>G, c.2407+169T>G, c.2407+97T>G, c.-37+107C>A), demonstrating extreme mutational sensitivity though specific monogenic diseases beyond YHFS remain undefined. AlphaFold structure prediction yields high confidence (global pLDDT 83.9), revealing a well-folded ARM repeat solenoid structure characteristic of scaffold proteins mediating multiprotein complex assembly. Mechanistically, TELO2 operates through integrated modes: (1) PIKK biogenesis—forms heterotrimeric complex with TTI1 and TTI2 (the "TTT complex") that binds newly synthesized PIKK kinases preventing aggregation and facilitating proper folding through HSP90 recruitment; (2) mTORC1/2 assembly—essential for both mTOR complex 1 (with RPTOR, MLST8) and complex 2 (with RICTOR, MLST8) formation, regulating cell growth, metabolism, and survival; (3) DNA damage response—ATR interaction links to replication stress sensing and checkpoint activation; FANCM/FAAP24 interactions connect to Fanconi anemia pathway for interstrand crosslink repair; (4) S-phase checkpoint—gene summary confirms function as S-phase checkpoint protein ensuring genomic integrity during DNA replication; (5) You-Hoover-Fong syndrome—2023 study (11 citations) describes 14 new affected individuals with global developmental delay, intellectual disability, distinctive facial features, and growth retardation caused by biallelic TELO2 mutations; (6) Breast carcinogenesis—2025 study implicates TELO2 in parabens-induced breast cancer through network analysis; (7) Chemotherapy resistance—2026 study shows TTI1-TELO2-mTORC1 axis activation by PIWIL1 promotes TOP mRNA translation in gastric cancer decreasing chemo-sensitivity; (8) Wnt/β-catenin signaling—2026 study identifies TTI1 as Wnt target modulating DNA damage responses in colorectal cancer. Loss-of-function causes defective PIKK kinase assembly, impaired mTOR signaling, defective DNA damage response, S-phase checkpoint failure, You-Hoover-Fong syndrome with developmental delay/intellectual disability, and increased cancer susceptibility.
