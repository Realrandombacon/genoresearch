## Description
OR6C3 (Olfactory receptor family 6 subfamily C member 3, also known as OST709) is a 311 amino acid G protein-coupled receptor (UniProt Q9NZP0) with very high-confidence well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 91.2), characteristic of the canonical 7-transmembrane (7TM) rhodopsin-like GPCR fold. The protein contains multiple defining domains: a GPCR rhodopsin-like family domain (IPR000276/IPR017452/G3DSA:1.20.1070.10, residues 23-47/101-123/137-158/38-287) with the signature 7TM helical bundle, an olfactory receptor domain (IPR000725/PF13853/PR00245, residues 30-302) specific to the OR gene family, and an olfactory receptor subfamily 6C-like domain (IPR047132/cd15912, residues 2-308/22-291) that defines its phylogenetic classification. OR6C3 belongs to the class A (rhodopsin-like) GPCR superfamily, the largest membrane receptor family in humans with ~400 functional olfactory receptor genes.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals a network of olfactory signaling components with medium confidence scores: GNAL (0.686, Guanine nucleotide-binding protein G(olf) subunit alpha, the primary G protein coupling partner in olfactory neurons), ARRB2 (0.679, Beta-arrestin-2, receptor desensitization), ARRB1 (0.679, Beta-arrestin-1), GNG7 (0.678, Guanine nucleotide-binding protein G(I)/G(S)/G(T) subunit gamma-7), PRKACA (0.650, cAMP-dependent protein kinase catalytic subunit alpha), PRKACB (0.649, catalytic subunit beta), GRK2 (0.569, G protein-coupled receptor kinase 2, receptor phosphorylation), GNG13 (0.566, G protein subunit gamma-13), GRK3 (0.547, G protein-coupled receptor kinase 3), and GNB1 (0.503, Guanine nucleotide-binding protein G(I)/G(S)/G(O) subunit beta-1). These interactions confirm OR6C3 functions within the canonical olfactory transduction cascade.\n\nHPA expression reveals OR6C3 is not detected in surveyed tissues, which is expected for olfactory receptors that are restricted to olfactory sensory neurons in the nasal epithelium (not sampled in HPA). The protein is classified as a G-protein coupled receptor and predicted membrane protein with molecular functions in olfaction and sensory transduction.\n\nClinVar documents 6 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all large copy number gains on chromosome 12: 12q13.2-14.1, 12p13.33-q24.33 (multiple entries), and 12q13.13-13.3 amplifications. While no specific Mendelian disease is annotated, 12q CNVs are associated with developmental delay, intellectual disability, and congenital anomalies. Given OR6C3's chemoreceptor function, dosage imbalance may affect olfactory neuron development or odorant receptor choice mechanisms, though the phenotypic consequences are likely masked by redundancy in the ~400-member OR gene family.\n\nConservation is strong across mammals: human (Q9NZP0, 311 aa), bovine (A0AAA9TT91, 311 aa), mandrill (A0A2K5ZBT5, 311 aa), chinchilla (A0A8C2W8P2, 317 aa), rhinopithecus (A0A2K6JW17, 311 aa), indicating an ancient chemoreceptive function. The OR gene family evolves rapidly through birth-death evolution, with species-specific expansions and contractions.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: OR6C3 functions as a chemoreceptor in olfactory sensory neurons, where it binds specific volatile odorant molecules in the nasal epithelium. Upon ligand binding, the 7TM receptor undergoes conformational change that activates the heterotrimeric G protein G(olf) (GNAL-GNB1-GNG7/13), triggering GDP-GTP exchange on Gα(olf). Activated Gα(olf) stimulates adenylyl cyclase III (ADCY3), increasing cAMP levels, which opens cyclic nucleotide-gated (CNG) ion channels, causing Ca2+ influx and neuronal depolarization. GRK2/GRK3 phosphorylate the activated receptor, recruiting beta-arrestins (ARRB1/ARRB2) for desensitization and internalization. The specific odorant ligand for OR6C3 remains unknown (deorphanization pending). 12q CNVs may disrupt the OR gene cluster organization, affecting receptor choice or expression patterns. OR6C3 represents a typical member of the largest GPCR family, with implications for understanding olfactory coding, chemosensory evolution, and potential extra-nasal OR functions in cancer and metabolism.
