## Description
ZNF550 (Zinc Finger Protein 550) is a 422 amino acid Krüppel-associated box (KRAB) zinc finger transcription factor localized to vesicles and microtubules. InterPro analysis reveals canonical repressor architecture: an N-terminal KRAB domain (residues 11-83, PF01352/IPR001909) that recruits transcriptional corepressors, followed by three C-terminal C2H2-type zinc finger DNA-binding domains (residues 203-225, 231-253, 259-278, PF00096/IPR013087) conferring sequence-specific DNA recognition. Additional domain signatures include IPR036051 (KRAB superfamily), IPR036236 (C2H2 superfamily), and IPR050329 (GLI C2H2-type zinc finger family). AlphaFold prediction yields medium confidence structure (pLDDT 66.5), consistent with structured zinc finger ββα motifs connected by flexible linkers. HPA subcellular localization to vesicles and microtubules suggests potential roles in mitotic chromatin organization or nucleocytoplasmic trafficking. STRING interaction network identifies 10 medium-confidence partners including TRIM28 (0.522) — the essential KRAB domain co-repressor that recruits HP1 heterochromatin protein and histone methyltransferases — plus related zinc finger proteins (ZNF432: 0.479, ZNF227: 0.467, ZNF548: 0.458, ZNF559: 0.421), C11orf94 (0.541), U2SURP (0.447), PPP1R14D (0.428), OR2A2 (0.447), and ERVMER34-1 (0.435). The TRIM28 interaction confirms ZNF550 functions within the canonical KRAB repression machinery. ClinVar documents 15 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number gains and duplications spanning 19q13.41-13.43, suggesting gene dosage sensitivity. The mechanistic model positions ZNF550 as a sequence-specific DNA-binding repressor: the KRAB domain recruits TRIM28/KAP1, which scaffolds SETDB1 histone methyltransferase and HP1 to establish H3K9me3 heterochromatin marks at target promoters, while the three C2H2 zinc fingers recognize specific DNA sequences (likely 5′-GCG(X)₃GCG-3′ variants). Microtubule association may facilitate mitotic bookmarking or chromosome segregation.
