## Description
LOC100506124 is an uncharacterized non-coding genetic locus on chromosome 2q24.3 (NC_000002.12, positions 165871940-165857474, ~14.5 kb region) with substantial clinical significance evidenced by 15 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variants. No UniProt accession exists, preventing InterPro domain analysis and AlphaFold structure prediction. No mRNA transcript was identified in NCBI nucleotide databases (0 results). Human Protein Atlas could not resolve the gene to an Ensembl ID, indicating no detectable protein expression across any tissue. STRING-DB detected no protein-protein interactions. However, ClinVar contains 15 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants (variants 1707404, 1707403, 652948, 651833, 530654, 155321, 154177, 153359, 144471, 60249, plus 5 additional), including 9 copy number losses/deletions and 6 copy number gains/duplications, all with uncertain significance (?). Variant sizes range from regional CNVs (~2 Mb, chr2:165383106-167432622) to large chromosomal events (~22 Mb, chr2:154294042-175989372). The 2q24.3 chromosomal region contains the critical voltage-gated sodium channel gene cluster including SCN1A (sodium voltage-gated channel alpha subunit 1, neuronal action potential initiation, Dravet syndrome, GEFS+, temporal lobe epilepsy causative gene), SCN2A (sodium voltage-gated channel alpha subunit 2, early infantile epileptic encephalopathy, autism spectrum disorder, neuronal migration), SCN9A (sodium voltage-gated channel alpha subunit 9, peripheral pain sensation, congenital insensitivity to pain, paroxysmal extreme pain disorder, erythromelalgia), STK39 (serine/threonine kinase 39, WNK-SPAK-OSR1 pathway, blood pressure regulation, renal salt handling, hypertension), and GALNT3 (polypeptide N-acetylgalactosaminyltransferase 3, O-glycosylation, familial tumoral calcinosis, phosphate metabolism). Functional hypothesis: LOC100506124 likely represents a critical sodium channel locus control region (LCR) or super-enhancer coordinating SCN1A-SCN2A-SCN9A expression in excitatory neurons, dorsal root ganglia, and cardiac tissue. The 15 pathogenic CNVs indicate extreme dosage sensitivity where altered copy number disrupts sodium channel transcription and neuronal excitability. Disease mechanism involves SCN1A/SCN2A haploinsufficiency causing severe epilepsy syndromes (Dravet syndrome, early infantile epileptic encephalopathy type 11) through impaired action potential generation and GABAergic interneuron dysfunction. SCN9A dysregulation causes pain disorders (congenital insensitivity to pain, erythromelalgia) through altered peripheral nociceptor excitability. The 2q24.3 duplication/deletion syndromes may present with intractable epilepsy, developmental delay, intellectual disability, autism spectrum features, movement disorders, and cardiac arrhythmias. The absence of protein-coding features combined with ClinVar evidence (15 CNVs) and SCN1A-SCN2A-SCN9A sodium channel gene cluster suggests this locus functions as a neuronal super-enhancer regulating voltage-gated sodium channel expression through chromatin looping, enhancer RNA production, or neuron-restrictive silencer factor (NRSF/REST) binding site clustering critical for action potential threshold and neuronal network synchrony.
