## Description
ZNF525 (Zinc Finger Protein 525) is a 479-amino acid Krüppel-associated box (KRAB) domain-containing zinc finger protein located on chromosome 19q13.42. The protein exhibits canonical KRAB-ZFP architecture with an N-terminal KRAB repression domain (IPR001909/PF01352, aa 8-81) followed by multiple C2H2-type zinc finger DNA-binding domains (IPR013087/PF00096, at least 3 clusters at aa 215-242, 243-270, 271-298, plus additional fingers at aa 313-377). This modular structure positions ZNF525 as a sequence-specific transcriptional repressor that recruits KAP1/TRIM28 co-repressor complexes via the KRAB domain to silence target genes through heterochromatin formation.\n\nSTRING interaction network reveals strong clustering with other KRAB-ZFPs: ZNF813 (score 0.766, high confidence), ZNF808 (0.643), ZNF701 (0.486), and ZNF28 (0.464), suggesting functional redundancy or cooperative binding at shared genomic loci. Additional partners include CCNI2 (cyclin-I2, 0.460) linking to cell cycle regulation, and cancer-testis antigen CT45A3 (0.402), hinting at potential roles in germ cell development or oncogenesis.\n\nHPA data shows ubiquitous expression across all tissues with low tissue specificity, consistent with housekeeping transcriptional regulation functions. Nuclear localization is predicted based on zinc finger DNA-binding domains. AlphaFold structure prediction yields moderate confidence (pLDDT 63.0), reflecting the characteristic architecture of structured zinc finger clusters connected by flexible linker regions—a common feature enabling conformational adaptability for DNA target recognition.\n\nClinVar documents 11 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly copy number gains spanning 19q13.41-13.43, indicating dosage sensitivity where ZNF525 overexpression may disrupt transcriptional programs. No specific disease phenotypes are yet assigned, but 19q13.4 duplications are associated with developmental abnormalities.\n\nEvolutionary context: KRAB-ZFPs represent the largest family of transcription factors in mammals (~400 genes), evolving rapidly to regulate endogenous retroelements and species-specific gene networks. The C2H2 zinc finger scaffold is ancient and highly conserved across metazoans.
