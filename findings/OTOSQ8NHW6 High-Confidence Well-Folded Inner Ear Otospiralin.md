## Description
OTOS (Otospiralin, also known as OTOSP) is an 89 amino acid secreted protein localized to the inner ear and endocrine tissues (UniProt Q8NHW6) with high-confidence well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 81.2). The protein defines its own unique family (IPR028224/PTHR35073, residues 1-89) and contains a specific Pfam Otospiralin domain (PF15182, residues 21-89) forming a compact ~69 aa module (~78%). Molecular functions include sensory perception of sound (GO:0007605), placing OTOS within the auditory machinery. Despite its small size, the high AlphaFold confidence suggests a stable, structured fold critical for its function in the cochlear fluids.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places OTOS within a specialized inner ear extracellular matrix network: strongest interaction with TECTA (0.592, Alpha-tectorin, a major component of the tectorial membrane), indicating a role in the structural integrity or acoustics of the cochlear partition. High confidence links include OTOA (0.537, Otoancorin, an anchor protein for the tectorial membrane), TECTB (0.500, Beta-tectorin), and SAMD7 (0.485, Sterile alpha motif domain containing 7, a transcriptional repressor linked to hearing loss). Connections to GPR160 (0.468), ENSA (0.444), OTOG (0.442, Otogelin, a component of the acellular membranes), OTOR (0.431, Otoraplin), COCH (0.429, Cochlin, the most abundant inner ear protein), and CDH23 (0.409, Cadherin-23, a stereocilia tip-link protein) confirm its integration into the complex ecosystem of the endolymph and perilymph. These interactions suggest OTOS may modulate the viscosity, ion composition, or mechanical properties of the cochlear fluids.\n\nHPA expression reveals a group-enriched pattern with highest levels in the pituitary gland (105.7 nTPM) and thyroid gland (41.0 nTPM). While the gene is named for its inner ear function, this endocrine expression suggests a potential secondary role in hormone secretion or storage, or cross-reactivity of probes. The 'cancer enriched' status implies upregulation in specific tumors, possibly reflecting re-activation of developmental programs. Subcellular localization as a predicted secreted protein aligns with its presence in cochlear fluids.\n\nClinVar documents 98 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains and losses affecting the 2q37 region: CNV gains (2q33.3-37.3, 2q37.2-37.3 duplications) and CNV losses (2q37.2-37.3, 2q35-37.3 deletions). The high variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity. Haploinsufficiency or overexpression may disrupt the delicate balance of cochlear fluid proteins, leading to sensorineural hearing loss, vestibular dysfunction, or syndromic features associated with 2q37 deletion syndrome (brachydactyly, intellectual disability). The specific link to deafness in guinea pig models confirms its critical auditory role.\n\nConservation is strong across mammals: human (Q8NHW6, 89 aa), mouse (Q8R448, 89 aa), rat (Q8K560, 89 aa), guinea pig (Q8K559, 88 aa), indicating evolutionary pressure to maintain the otospiralin fold.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: OTOS functions as a soluble modulator of the cochlear extracellular matrix, likely interacting with the tectorial membrane (via TECTA/TECTB) and otoconial membranes (via OTOA/OTOG). Its compact, stable structure allows it to survive in the harsh ionic environment of the endolymph. OTOS may act as a chaperone, a cross-linker, or a regulator of protease activity to maintain the precise mechanical properties required for sound transduction. Interaction with COCH (the most abundant inner ear protein) suggests it may prevent pathological aggregation (as seen in DFNA9 deafness). In the pituitary/thyroid, OTOS may play an analogous role in secretory granule stability. Dysregulation via 2q37 CNVs alters the stoichiometry of these interactions, leading to structural failure of the auditory apparatus and hearing loss.
