# YWHAG: A 14-3-3 Gamma Scaffold Protein Controlling RAF/MAPK and Hippo Signaling Pathways with Emerging Roles in Developmental Epileptic Encephalopathy (DEE56) and Cancer Progression

## Description
YWHAG (Tyrosine 3-Monooxygenase/Tryptophan 5-Monooxygenase Activation Protein Gamma, also known as 14-3-3γ) is a 247-amino acid scaffold protein characterized by a canonical 14-3-3 domain architecture (IPR000308/IPR023409/IPR023410/IPR036815/PF00244/SM00101/PTHR18860, positions 3-243) containing conserved phosphoserine/phosphothreonine-binding motifs (IPR023409/PS00796, 42-52; IPR023410/PS00797, 216-235) within the 14-3-3 domain superfamily (G3DSA:1.20.190.20/SSF48445, 1-244). STRING interactions reveal an exceptionally tight functional network with RAF kinase signaling components: BRAF (0.993), RAF1 (0.991), ARAF (0.985), all at highest confidence, indicating YWHAG functions as a critical regulator of the MAPK/ERK pathway; additional high-confidence partners include other 14-3-3 isoforms YWHAZ (0.985), YWHAE (0.851), YWHAH (0.769), cell cycle regulators CDC25C (0.979), CDC25B (0.412), Hippo pathway effector YAP1 (0.973), and histone deacetylase HDAC4 (0.838). HPA data shows tissue-enhanced expression with exceptionally high levels in brain (355.0 nTPM) and skeletal muscle (327.6 nTPM), detected in all tissues, low cancer and immune cell specificity, subcellular localization to cytosol and vesicles, classified as a predicted intracellular protein involved in signal transduction. ClinVar contains 79 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including frameshift mutations (c.652_653insT/p.Asp218fs), nonsense mutations (c.537C>G/p.Tyr179Ter, c.202C>T/p.Gln68Ter), missense variants disrupting the hydrophobic core (c.293A>G/p.Gln98Arg, c.237T>G/p.Ile79Met, c.169C>G/p.Arg57Gly, c.509C>G/p.Pro170Arg, c.707A>T/p.Asp236Val), duplications (c.623_626dup/p.Asp209_Thr210insTer), and large copy number losses (7q11.22-11.23 deletions); these cause Developmental and Epileptic Encephalopathy 56 (DEE56), a monogenic neurodevelopmental disorder with fewer than 30 cases reported globally. AlphaFold structure (AF-P61981-F1) predicts very high confidence folding (pLDDT 94.2) consistent with a well-structured scaffold protein containing the characteristic 14-3-3 bundle of nine antiparallel α-helices forming an amphipathic groove for phosphopeptide binding. With 186 papers, YWHAG represents a 'gray' gene: while the 14-3-3 protein family is well-established, YWHAG-specific disease mechanisms are emerging rapidly (2025 studies describe DEE56 genotype-phenotype correlations, L173S variant disrupting hydrophobic core, epithelial-mesenchymal transition in NSCLC, JAK2/STAT3 pathway activation in lung adenocarcinoma, circNOX4/miR-6884-5p/YWHAG axis in cholangiocarcinoma). The mechanistic hypothesis posits that YWHAG functions as a phosphorylation-dependent molecular scaffold that dimerizes to create dual binding grooves recognizing phosphoserine/phosphothreonine motifs on client proteins, simultaneously regulating multiple signaling pathways: (1) sequestering RAF kinases (BRAF/RAF1/ARAF) in inactive cytoplasmic complexes controlling MAPK signaling; (2) binding CDC25 phosphatases to regulate G2/M cell cycle checkpoint; (3) modulating YAP1 nuclear-cytoplasmic shuttling in Hippo pathway; (4) interacting with HDAC4 for epigenetic regulation; its brain-enriched expression (355.0 nTPM) explains DEE56 neurological phenotypes when mutations disrupt scaffold integrity or client binding, while cancer associations reflect dysregulated proliferation signaling through RAF/MAPK and JAK/STAT pathways.
