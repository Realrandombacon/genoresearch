## Description
HSH2D (hematopoietic SH2 domain containing, alias ALX, HSH2) encodes a 352-amino acid cytosolic adaptor protein belonging to the SH2 domain-containing family (IPR000980/IPR035047/PTHR14388, residues 10-281). The protein contains a canonical Src homology 2 (SH2) domain (IPR000980/PF00017/SM00252, residues 32-125) that mediates phosphotyrosine-dependent protein-protein interactions, characteristic of immune signaling adaptors. AlphaFold predicts medium confidence structure (pLDDT=62.2), indicating a partially folded protein with an ordered SH2 domain and intrinsically disordered regions typical of signaling scaffolds. HPA shows tissue-enhanced expression in hematopoietic lineages with highest levels in bone marrow (44.4 nTPM) and lymphoid tissue (36.2 nTPM), consistent with its role in T-cell activation. Subcellular localization includes plasma membrane, focal adhesion sites, and cytosol, supporting membrane-proximal signaling function. STRING interactions reveal strong coupling to T-cell receptor and costimulatory pathways: TNK2 (0.862, activated CDC42 kinase involved in TCR signaling), FPR2 (0.855, formyl peptide receptor, chemotaxis), ANXA1 (0.837, annexin A1, anti-inflammatory signaling), CMKLR1 (0.751, chemokine receptor), CD28 (0.550, T-cell costimulatory receptor), CRK (0.410, adaptor protein linking tyrosine kinases to Ras), and G-protein coupled receptors (GPR18, GPR32, LTB4R). This interaction network positions HSH2D at the nexus of TCR signaling, costimulation, and chemokine-mediated T-cell trafficking. ClinVar documents 13 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly chromosome 19p copy number losses and gains, indicating dosage sensitivity. Conservation is strong across vertebrates: mouse (Q6VYH9, 334 aa), zebrafish (Q56A36, 365 aa). Mechanistically, HSH2D likely functions as a phosphotyrosine-binding adaptor that recruits downstream effectors to activated TCR/CD28 complexes, amplifying costimulatory signals required for naive T-cell activation. The TNK2 interaction suggests roles in cytoskeletal reorganization during immunological synapse formation, while chemokine receptor partnerships indicate coordination between activation and migration programs.
