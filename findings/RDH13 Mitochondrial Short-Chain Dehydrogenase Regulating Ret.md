## Description
RDH13 (Retinol dehydrogenase 13, 331 aa, UniProt Q8NBN7) is a partially characterized mitochondrial short-chain dehydrogenase/reductase (SDR) with emerging roles in retinoid metabolism, placental biology, and hepatic detoxification, representing an understudied metabolic enzyme with clinical significance. Structural analysis reveals canonical SDR architecture: (1) Short-chain dehydrogenase/reductase SDR family domain (IPR002347/PF00106, aa 39-239) forming characteristic Rossmann fold for cofactor binding; (2) NAD(P)-binding Rossmann-like domain (G3DSA:3.40.50.720/SSF51735, aa 32-321) enabling NADP+/NADPH binding; (3) SDR conserved site (IPR020904/PS00061, aa 187-215) containing catalytic triad (Ser-Tyr-Lys) essential for dehydrogenase activity; (4) Retinol dehydrogenase classical SDR subfamily (cd09807, aa 38-313) defining substrate specificity for retinoids; (5) Oxidoreductase family (NF004846, aa 38-320) confirming redox enzyme function. ClinVar contains 9 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including missense (p.Gly254Arg), splice site (c.340+5_340+7del), and multiple copy number gains/losses at 19q13.33-13.43 (chr19:48463931-59095359), indicating clinical significance though specific diseases not yet fully defined - haploinsufficiency or overexpression likely disrupts retinoid homeostasis and mitochondrial redox balance. Literature (28 papers) reveals emerging roles: (1) Preeclampsia - miR-142-3p regulates RDH13 to impair trophoblast function via CDH5/LFA-1/L-SELECTIN axis, identifying RDH13 as diagnostic/therapeutic target; (2) Hepatic detoxification - Rdh13 deficiency weakens carbon tetrachloride-induced liver injury by regulating Spot14 (thyroid hormone-responsive protein) and Cyp2e1 (cytochrome P450 2E1) expression, linking RDH13 to oxidative stress response; (3) Retinaldehyde reductase activity - human RDH13 functions as mitochondrial NADP-dependent enzyme reducing all-trans-retinaldehyde to retinol, contributing to visual cycle and retinoid homeostasis; (4) Strabismus - RDH13 polymorphisms associated with bilateral convergent strabismus with exophthalmus in cattle, suggesting conserved role in ocular development. Functional hypothesis: RDH13 functions as (a) mitochondrial retinaldehyde reductase converting all-trans-retinaldehyde to retinol using NADPH, maintaining retinoid homeostasis critical for vision, embryonic development, and epithelial differentiation, and (b) metabolic regulator modulating hepatic detoxification capacity through Spot14/CYP2E1 axis affecting oxidative stress response to toxins (CCl4, drugs). Mechanistic proposal: RDH13 localizes to mitochondrial matrix via N-terminal targeting sequence, where it binds NADP+ through Rossmann fold domain and catalyzes retinaldehyde reduction via conserved Ser-Tyr-Lys catalytic triad— in placenta, RDH13 expression regulated by miR-142-3p controls trophoblast invasion and spiral artery remodeling through retinoid-dependent modulation of CDH5 (VE-cadherin), LFA-1 integrin, and L-SELECTIN adhesion molecules (dysregulation causes preeclampsia via shallow trophoblast invasion); in liver, RDH13 influences CYP2E1 expression and activity through Spot14-mediated transcriptional regulation, determining susceptibility to toxin-induced hepatotoxicity (RDH13 deficiency reduces CCl4 injury by lowering CYP2E1-mediated ROS production); copy number variants at 19q13.33-13.43 cause gene dosage effects disrupting retinoid metabolism (ocular defects, developmental abnormalities) and detoxification capacity (drug sensitivity, toxin susceptibility)— positioning RDH13 as therapeutic target for preeclampsia (miR-142-3p antagonists), hepatoprotection (RDH13 modulators), and retinoid disorders (RDH13 supplementation).
