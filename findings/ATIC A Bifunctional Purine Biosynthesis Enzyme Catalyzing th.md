# ATIC: A Bifunctional Purine Biosynthesis Enzyme Catalyzing the Final Two Steps of De Novo IMP Production via AICAR Transformylase and IMP Cyclohydrolase Domains

## Description
ATIC (5-aminoimidazole-4-carboxamide ribonucleotide formyltransferase/IMP cyclohydrolase, UniProt P31939) is a 592-amino acid bifunctional enzyme that catalyzes the last two steps of de novo purine biosynthesis, converting AICAR to IMP through formylation and cyclization reactions. InterPro analysis reveals a modular catalytic architecture: an N-terminal IMP cyclohydrolase domain (cd01421, aa 5-193) belonging to the methylglyoxal synthase-like superfamily (IPR011607, IPR036914, aa 1-198), and a C-terminal AICAR transformylase domain with duplicated structural repeats (IPR024051, aa 227-373 and 377-581) plus an insert domain (IPR024050, aa 476-518). The entire protein belongs to the PurH family (IPR002695) with GO annotations for IMP cyclohydrolase activity (GO:0003937) and phosphoribosylaminoimidazolecarboxamide formyltransferase activity (GO:0004643). The AlphaFold predicted structure shows exceptional confidence (pLDDT 97.4), indicating a highly stable, well-folded enzyme suitable for substrate channeling between active sites. STRING interaction data places ATIC at the terminus of the purinosome complex with near-perfect associations to all de novo purine biosynthesis enzymes: ADSL (0.999), GART (0.999), PAICS (0.997), PPAT (0.995), ADSS1 (0.996), and one-carbon metabolism enzymes MTHFD1 (0.998), SHMT1 (0.998), and SHMT2 (0.995), reflecting metabolic coupling of folate and purine pathways. HPA expression profiles confirm ubiquitous presence ('Detected in all') with cytosolic and plasma membrane localization, consistent with housekeeping metabolic function. ClinVar records document 49 pathogenic variants including frameshifts (p.Pro82fs, p.Lys357fs), splice defects (c.1227+2T>C), and missense changes, which cause AICA-ribosiduria—a rare autosomal recessive disorder characterized by developmental delay, dysmorphic features, epilepsy, and intellectual disability due to toxic AICAR accumulation. We hypothesize that ATIC functions as a substrate-channeling machine within the dynamic purinosome assembly: the transformylase domain first accepts 10-formyl-THF to formylate AICAR, then the intermediate is passed through a molecular tunnel to the cyclohydrolase active site for ring closure to IMP. This bifunctional arrangement prevents release of unstable intermediates and coordinates flux through the terminal steps. Loss of ATIC activity causes AICAR accumulation, which inhibits adenosine deaminase and disrupts polyamine metabolism, leading to neurodevelopmental toxicity. The enzyme represents a validated drug target for antifolate chemotherapy and immunosuppression.
