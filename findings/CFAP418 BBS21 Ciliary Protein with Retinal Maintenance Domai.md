## Description
CFAP418 (Gene ID: 157657, UniProt Q96NL8, aliases BBS21, C8orf37, CORD16, FAP418, MOT25, RP64, smalltalk) is a 207 amino acid cilia- and flagella-associated protein located on chromosome 8q22.1 (NC_000008.11, positions 95269200-95244912, ~24kb genomic region). This gene encodes a well-folded protein with clear subcellular localization and disease associations but incompletely characterized molecular mechanism. InterPro domain analysis reveals CFAP418 family membership (IPR029239/PF14996, positions 1-207; PTHR33958 C8ORF37 protein, positions 1-207) with a notable Retinal Maintenance domain (PF14996, positions 64-207) suggesting specialized role in photoreceptor biology. GO annotations are limited, reflecting incomplete functional characterization. STRING interaction network returned no detectable protein partners for CFAP418 in Homo sapiens (species 9606), suggesting either transient/weak interactions, technical limitations, or that CFAP418 functions independently or within large complexes not captured by affinity purification methods. Human Protein Atlas classifies CFAP418 (ENSG00000156172) as a predicted intracellular protein with remarkable multi-compartment localization: vesicles, plasma membrane, cell junctions, primary cilium, and centrosome—consistent with ciliary trafficking protein that shuttles between compartments. Disease involvement explicitly includes Bardet-Biedl syndrome, ciliopathy, cone-rod dystrophy, obesity, and disease variants. RNA shows low tissue specificity, detected in many tissues, not detected in immune cells, with cell type-enhanced single cell specificity. AlphaFold structure prediction (AF-Q96NL8-F1) yields high-confidence model (global pLDDT 74.9), indicating a well-folded stable architecture across all 207 residues—suitable for structural studies and drug discovery. ClinVar lists 77 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including frameshift mutations (p.Glu162fs, p.Ala180fs, p.Lys58fs), nonsense mutation (p.Trp147Ter), missense variant (p.Arg177Pro), splice site mutations (c.374+1G>A), and large copy number gains spanning chromosome 8; however, no specific monogenic disease name is assigned in ClinVar summary despite strong phenotypic associations. Conservation is strong: mouse ortholog (Q3UJP5, 209 aa) and zebrafish ortholog (P0DOC8, 202 aa) show nearly identical protein lengths indicating evolutionary constraint. Functional hypothesis: CFAP418 functions as a ciliary trafficking protein that localizes to the base of the primary cilium (co-localizing with polyglutamylated tubulin in retinal pigment epithelial cells) and participates in intraflagellar transport (IFT) or ciliary vesicle docking. The Retinal Maintenance domain (PF14996, 64-207) suggests specialized role in photoreceptor outer segment renewal—a cilia-derived structure requiring continuous membrane trafficking. Multi-compartment localization (vesicles, plasma membrane, cilium, centrosome) indicates CFAP418 may function in ciliary gate assembly, basal body docking, or cargo delivery to growing cilia. Disease mechanism: CFAP418 loss-of-function causes autosomal recessive ciliopathies through disruption of ciliary structure/function. The 77 ClinVar variants (frameshift, nonsense, missense, splice) indicate strong intolerance to mutation. Phenotypic spectrum includes: (1) Cone-rod dystrophy/retinitis pigmentosa (CORD16/RP64 aliases)—photoreceptor degeneration due to defective outer segment maintenance; (2) Bardet-Biedl syndrome (BBS21 alias)—syndromic ciliopathy with obesity, retinal dystrophy, polydactyly, renal anomalies; (3) Isolated obesity—hypothalamic ciliary dysfunction affecting leptin signaling. The absence of STRING interactions suggests CFAP418 may function as part of large stable ciliary complexes (e.g., BBSome, IFT particles) not captured by standard co-IP, or interacts with ciliary structural components (tubulins, transition zone proteins) not represented in STRING database. Priority experiments include identification of CFAP418 binding partners using cilia-specific co-IP/MS, super-resolution microscopy to determine precise ciliary subdomain localization, investigation of CFAP418 role in IFT using live-cell imaging of ciliated cells, assessment of CFAP418 function in photoreceptor outer segment renewal using retinal organoids, generation of Cfap418 knockout mice to characterize ciliopathy phenotypes, and structural studies leveraging high-confidence AlphaFold model (pLDDT 74.9) to identify functional surfaces and potential therapeutic targets.
