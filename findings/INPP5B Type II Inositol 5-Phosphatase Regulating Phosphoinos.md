## Description
INPP5B (Inositol Polyphosphate-5-Phosphatase B) is a 993 amino acid cytosolic and ciliary enzyme that hydrolyzes the 5-phosphate group from phosphatidylinositol 4,5-bisphosphate [PI(4,5)P2] and inositol 1,4,5-trisphosphate [Ins(1,4,5)P3], serving as a critical regulator of phosphoinositide signaling, membrane trafficking, and ciliary function with functional redundancy to OCRL (Lowe syndrome protein). InterPro analysis identifies complex multi-domain architecture including catalytic inositol polyphosphate 5-phosphatase domain (IPR000300, cd09093, residues 343-644) with phosphatase activity (GO:0016791) and phosphatidylinositol dephosphorylation capability (GO:0046856), C-terminal Rho GTPase-activating protein domain (IPR000198, cd04380, G3DSA:1.10.555.10, IPR008936, residues 821-993) for signal transduction (GO:0007165), N-terminal Pleckstrin homology-like domain (cd13383, residues 17-129) for membrane targeting, immunoglobulin-like fold (IPR013783, G3DSA:2.60.40.10, residues 668-793), and endonuclease/exonuclease/phosphatase superfamily homology (G3DSA:3.60.10.10, residues 320-667). AlphaFold structure (AF-P32019-F1) predicts confident folding (pLDDT 79.2) across the 993 residue sequence, indicating a stable multi-domain enzyme suitable for substrate binding and catalysis. HPA data classifies INPP5B as a metabolic enzyme and hydrolase involved in lipid metabolism, with primary cilium tip and cytosol localization reflecting roles in ciliary signaling and cytoplasmic phosphoinositide turnover, low tissue specificity (detected in all tissues), cell type-enhanced expression, and implications in metabolic disease. STRING interactions reveal tight functional coupling to the phosphoinositide metabolism network: PHETA1 (PH domain and ET domain containing 1, 0.951) for endosomal trafficking; INPP4B (0.942) and INPP4A (0.413) as related 4-phosphatases; PIP5K1C (Phosphatidylinositol-4-phosphate 5-kinase type 1 gamma, 0.938) and PIP4K2A (0.929) for PI(4,5)P2 synthesis; OCRL (0.936) as the disease-related paralog causing Lowe syndrome; SYNJ1 (Synaptojanin 1, 0.928) and SYNJ2 (0.925) as dual phosphatases; INPP5E (0.920) as the ciliary 5-phosphatase causing Joubert syndrome; and PIK3C2A (Phosphatidylinositol-4-phosphate 3-kinase C2 domain-containing subunit alpha, 0.914) for PI3P production. ClinVar lists 8 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including splice site mutations (c.-27+63G>T, c.2386+49T>A), missense changes (p.Ala51Val), chromosomal inversions, and large copy number gains on chromosome 1p35.1-33 and 1p36.33-q44, though no specific monogenic syndrome is defined, suggesting potential roles in ciliopathy or metabolic disorder susceptibility. Literature (75 papers) establishes INPP5B as a multifunctional phosphatase: recent reviews highlight cellular functions and disease roles alongside OCRL in phosphoinositide homeostasis; NIK (NF-κB-inducing kinase) stabilizes INPP5B during chronic ethanol exposure to suppress AKT/ACLY pathway-mediated lipogenesis in liver, protecting against alcohol-associated liver disease; crystal structures with synthetic inositol polyphosphate surrogates reveal mechanistic insights for the 5-phosphatase family; INPP5B regulates cancer stemness and tumorigenesis in hepatocellular carcinoma through phosphoinositide modulation; and functional redundancy with OCRL allows compensation in Lowe syndrome models. As a well-studied metabolic enzyme with emerging roles in liver disease and cancer, INPP5B represents a critical node connecting phosphoinositide signaling to membrane trafficking, ciliary function, and metabolic regulation through coordinated PI(4,5)P2 hydrolysis.
