# CCDC63/ODA5: Outer Dynein Arm Docking Complex Component Essential for Ciliary Motility and Spermatogenesis

**Date:** 2026-03-27T13:20:57.557018

**Quality Score:** 3.8/10  (E=5.5, D=2.1) [MODERATE]

## Description
CCDC63 (alias ODA5) encodes a 563 amino acid coiled-coil domain-containing protein (UniProt Q8NA47) located on chromosome 12q24.11 that functions as a critical component of the outer dynein arm docking complex (ODA-DC) required for ciliary and flagellar motility. InterPro analysis reveals two key structural domains: an ODAD1-like central coiled-coil region (IPR049258, PF21773, aa 151-434) forming an extended alpha-helical scaffold, and an outer dynein arm-docking protein family domain (IPR051876, PTHR21694, aa 10-527) characteristic of proteins that anchor dynein motor complexes to the axonemal microtubule doublets. AlphaFold structure prediction shows high confidence (pLDDT 78.4), indicating a well-folded stable coiled-coil architecture optimized for protein-protein interactions within the axonemal scaffold. HPA classifies CCDC63 as testis-enriched (16.6 nTPM) with group-enriched expression pattern, localized to nucleoplasm, cell junctions, and principal piece of sperm flagella, consistent with roles in spermatogenesis and sperm motility. STRING interaction network reveals strong associations with established cilia/dynein arm proteins: CCDC151 (ODA-DC component, score 0.863), TTC25 (ciliary trafficking, 0.774), DRC3 (dynein regulatory complex, 0.677), ARMC4 (axonemal assembly, 0.652), CCDC40 (ciliopathy gene, 0.621), TEKT3 (tektin filament protein, 0.619), MNS1 (meiosis-specific, 0.582), and RSPH4A (radial spoke head, 0.550). ClinVar documents 9 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number gains and losses spanning 12q23-24 regions, indicating dosage sensitivity. Conservation is evident across mammals (mouse Q8CDV6/558 aa, rat Q4V8F7/559 aa, bovine Q2T9W3/558 aa). Functional hypothesis: CCDC63/ODA5 acts as a structural scaffold protein within the outer dynein arm docking complex that bridges dynein motor proteins to the axonemal microtubule doublets through its extended coiled-coil domains, enabling proper assembly and anchoring of dynein arms required for ciliary beating and sperm flagellar propulsion. Disease mechanism involves haploinsufficiency or overexpression disrupting ODA-DC assembly, leading to primary ciliary dyskinesia phenotypes including chronic respiratory infections, situs inversus, and male infertility due to immotile sperm.
