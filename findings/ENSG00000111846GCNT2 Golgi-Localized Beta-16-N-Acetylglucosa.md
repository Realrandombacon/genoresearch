## Description

InterPro analysis reveals a canonical glycosyltransferase architecture: IPR003406/PF02485 (Glycosyl transferase family 14/Core-2/I-Branching enzyme, residues 97-357, GO:0016757 glycosyltransferase activity, GO:0016020 membrane), plus PTHR19297 (Mucin-type O-glycan biosynthesis glycosyltransferase, residues 5-402). The catalytic domain spans residues 97-357 within the Golgi lumen, while the N-terminal cytoplasmic tail (residues 1-20) and transmembrane region anchor the enzyme to Golgi membranes. AlphaFold structural prediction yields very high confidence (global pLDDT: 91.8), indicating an exceptionally well-folded stable glycosyltransferase with ordered catalytic core. These interactions confirm GCNT2's role within the glycosylation machinery network, working coordinately with sialyltransferases, galactosyltransferases, and fucosyltransferases to build complex cell surface glycans. Human Protein Atlas shows tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in prostate (13.7 nTPM), Golgi apparatus localization, immune cell-enhanced expression in blood cells, and group-enriched single cell specificity. ClinVar contains 29 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number losses (6p24.3-24.2), duplications, deletions, and nonsense mutations (p.Arg334Ter, p.His254fs), with disease associations including cataract (CTRCT13 alias), though specific phenotypes require further characterization. Recent literature (2025) reveals GCNT2's dual role in cancer: low GCNT2/I-branching glycan expression associates with bladder cancer aggressiveness, while GCNT2 hypomethylation correlates with poor survival in acute myeloid leukemia. Additional 2025 studies show GCNT2 knockdown promotes osteoblast differentiation via PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway activation, and miR-199b-5p targets GCNT2 in osteoarthritis pathology. Based on domain architecture (GT14 family catalytic domain), interaction partners (glycosyltransferase network), very high-confidence AlphaFold structure, Golgi localization, and emerging cancer/bone literature, GCNT2 functions as the I-branching enzyme that converts linear fetal i antigen to branched adult I antigen on erythrocytes during development, while in adult tissues it regulates cell surface glycan branching patterns that influence cancer cell invasion, immune cell trafficking, and osteoblast differentiation. The enzyme likely recognizes poly-N-acetyllactosamine chains and introduces beta-1,6 branches that serve as scaffolds for further modification by sialyltransferases and fucosyltransferases, explaining its extensive interaction network. Loss-of-function variants cause congenital cataract (CTRCT13), suggesting critical roles in lens glycoprotein maturation.
