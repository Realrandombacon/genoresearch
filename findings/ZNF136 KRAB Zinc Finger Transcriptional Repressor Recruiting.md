# ZNF136: KRAB Zinc Finger Transcriptional Repressor Recruiting Heterochromatin Machinery

## Description
ZNF136 (zinc finger protein 136) encodes a 540-amino acid Krüppel-associated box (KRAB) zinc finger transcriptional repressor (UniProt P52737) with confident AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 74.6). InterPro identifies canonical repressor architecture: N-terminal KRAB domain (IPR001909/PF01352, residues 3-90) followed by 14 tandem C2H2 zinc finger domains (IPR013087/PF00096, residues 143-386) that confer sequence-specific DNA binding. HPA classifies ZNF136 as a transcription factor with nucleolar localization, low tissue specificity (detected in all tissues), and molecular functions including DNA-binding and repression. STRING analysis reveals 4 interaction partners: TRIM28/KAP1 (0.666), the canonical KRAB co-repressor that recruits heterochromatin protein 1 (HP1) and histone methyltransferases; LGALS4 (0.557), a galectin involved in cell adhesion; MDFI (0.469), an IκB family regulator; and CLHC1 (0.426), a nuclear protein of unknown function. The TRIM28 interaction is mechanistically critical—KRAB domains recruit TRIM28 which assembles SETDB1 histone methyltransferase and HP1 to establish H3K9me3 heterochromatin marks at target promoters. ClinVar documents 15 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number variations spanning 19p13.2 (deletions and duplications), indicating dosage sensitivity. Evolutionary conservation is strong across vertebrates. Mechanistically, ZNF136 likely functions as a sequence-specific epigenetic silencer that binds GC-rich promoter elements via its zinc finger array and recruits TRIM28/KAP1 corepressor complexes to establish facultative heterochromatin, regulating genes involved in development, cell cycle, or immune responses. The nucleolar localization suggests potential roles in ribosomal DNA silencing or nucleolar stress responses.
