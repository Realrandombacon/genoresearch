## Description
IVNS1ABP (Influenza virus NS1A-binding protein, also known as NS1-BP or KLHL39) is a 642 amino acid BTB-Kelch family protein functioning as a substrate-specific adaptor for CUL3-based E3 ubiquitin ligase complexes. The protein contains an N-terminal BTB/POZ domain (IPR000210, PF00651, positions 22-129) that mediates CUL3 binding and dimerization, a BACK domain (positions 134-233) linking BTB to Kelch regions, and a C-terminal Kelch-type beta-propeller (IPR015915, IPR006652, positions 236-640) with four Kelch repeats forming the substrate recognition surface. AlphaFold prediction shows high structural confidence (pLDDT 84.7), indicating a well-folded adaptor with stable protein-protein interaction interfaces. HPA data reveals tissue-enriched expression with highest levels in bone marrow (427.4 nTPM), detected in all tissues, with cytosolic localization consistent with ubiquitination machinery functions. STRING interaction network identifies 10 partners in RNA processing and metabolism: phosphoribosyl pyrophosphate synthetase-associated proteins PRPSAP1 (0.919) and PRPSAP2 (0.804), heterogeneous nuclear ribonucleoprotein K HNRNPK (0.891), proline dehydrogenase PRODH (0.717), splicing factor SRSF2 (0.715), enolase ENO1 (0.628), spliceosome assembly factor SART1 (0.574), pre-mRNA processing factor SWT1 (0.539), nucleolin NCL (0.489), and transcription factor TLX2 (0.426). ClinVar reports 28 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variants though specific disease phenotypes remain under investigation. Literature (28 papers) reveals emerging disease biology: homozygous mutations cause a newly identified progeroid neuropathy syndrome with severe neuropathy and intellectual deficits through impaired proteostasis and actin filament disorganization leading to cellular senescence; serves as diagnostic biomarker for acute myocardial infarction through hyperlipidemia-lipophagy pathways; originally identified as influenza NS1A viral protein interactor suggesting antiviral defense roles. IVNS1ABP likely functions as a molecular bridge connecting CUL3 E3 ligase to specific substrates involved in RNA splicing and metabolic regulation through Kelch beta-propeller-mediated recognition, with BTB domain anchoring to the ubiquitination machinery—defects cause accelerated aging phenotypes through proteostatic collapse and cytoskeletal dysfunction.
