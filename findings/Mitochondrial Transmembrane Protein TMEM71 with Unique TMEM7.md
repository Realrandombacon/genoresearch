## Description
TMEM71 (UniProt Q6P5X7) is a 295 amino acid transmembrane protein representing an understudied component of the human dark genome with emerging roles in mitochondrial function and hematopoietic/cardiac biology. The protein contains a defining TMEM71 domain family (IPR027975/PF15121/PTHR35255) spanning residues 1-288, with the Pfam domain (PF15121) specifically covering residues 1-149, suggesting an N-terminal structured domain followed by a C-terminal extension. This domain architecture is unique to the TMEM71 family with no homology to characterized protein families, indicating TMEM71 represents a distinct functional class of transmembrane proteins. AlphaFold predicts a medium-confidence structure (global pLDDT 56.7) spanning the full 1-295 residue length, consistent with a partially structured protein containing both ordered transmembrane regions and potentially disordered segments—this structural profile is characteristic of membrane proteins with flexible cytoplasmic or luminal domains. HPA classifies TMEM71 as a predicted intracellular and membrane protein with mitochondrial subcellular localization, providing critical functional insight into its cellular role. Expression is tissue-enhanced with highest levels in heart muscle (34.7 nTPM) and bone marrow (31.6 nTPM), detected in many tissues with low cancer specificity but immune cell enhancement, suggesting housekeeping mitochondrial function with specialized roles in cardiac energetics and hematopoiesis. STRING interaction network reveals medium-confidence associations with functionally coherent partners: LRRC6 (0.561, leucine-rich repeat containing 6, involved in ciliogenesis and dynein assembly), KLHDC8A (0.495, kelch domain containing 8A, microtubule-associated protein), CCDC18 (0.445, coiled-coil domain containing 18, centrosomal protein), FAM228A (0.435, family with sequence similarity 228 member A, uncharacterized), RIMS2 (0.432, regulating synaptic membrane exocytosis 2, presynaptic active zone protein), RGS18 (0.426, regulator of G-protein signaling 18, hematopoietic-specific GTPase activator), RAB42 (0.420, Ras-related protein Rab-42, vesicle trafficking). The presence of RGS18 (hematopoietic regulator) among interaction partners aligns with bone marrow expression, while LRRC6 and CCDC18 suggest potential connections to cytoskeletal organization. ClinVar contains 55 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all consisting of copy number gains affecting chromosome 8q region (chr8:126446968-146295771 8q24.13-24.3, chr8:111432348-146295771 8q23.2-24.3, chr8:158048-146295771 8p23.3-q24.3, chr8:113392581-146364022 8q23.3-24.3, chr8:115586904-135607135 8q23.3-24.23, chr8:131138343-143473913 8q24.21-24.3, chr8:68912432-146295771 8q13.2-24.3, chr8:84712253-146295771 8q21.2-24.3, chr8:96496503-146295711 8q22.1-24.3), indicating TMEM71 resides in a dosage-sensitive genomic region where duplications cause disease phenotypes. Evolutionary conservation is demonstrated by mouse ortholog (Q149F5, 287 aa, 97% identity), confirming strong functional constraint across mammals. The mechanistic model positions TMEM71 as a mitochondrial inner or outer membrane protein functioning in: (1) mitochondrial metabolite transport or ion homeostasis through its transmembrane domain architecture, (2) coordination of mitochondrial dynamics with cytoskeletal elements via LRRC6/CCDC18 interactions, (3) regulation of hematopoietic differentiation through RGS18-mediated G-protein signaling modulation in bone marrow, (4) cardiac energy metabolism support given high heart muscle expression. Copy number gains causing 8q duplication syndromes would increase TMEM71 dosage, potentially disrupting mitochondrial membrane potential, metabolite flux, or protein complex stoichiometry, contributing to developmental anomalies observed in 8q duplication patients.
