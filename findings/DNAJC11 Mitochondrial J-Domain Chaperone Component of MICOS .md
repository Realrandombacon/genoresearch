## Description
DNAJC11/Q9NVH1 (559 aa) is a well-folded (AlphaFold pLDDT: 84.8) mitochondrial inner membrane protein functioning as an integral component of the MICOS (Mitochondrial Contact Site and Cristae Organizing System) complex required for cristae architecture maintenance and mitochondrial membrane organization. InterPro analysis reveals a characteristic J-domain chaperone architecture with an N-terminal DnaJ domain (IPR001623/PF00226, residues 14-79/3-97) containing the conserved HPD motif (IPR018253/PS00636, residues 59-78) essential for Hsp70 co-chaperone activity, a unique beta-barrel domain (IPR055225/PF22774, residues 162-374) potentially mediating membrane insertion or protein interactions, and a C-terminal domain specific to DNAJC11-like proteins (IPR024586/PF11875, residues 422-549), classifying it within the mitochondrial inner membrane organizing protein family (IPR052243/PTHR44157). HPA data confirms ubiquitous expression across all tissues with mitochondrial localization and chaperone molecular function, consistent with fundamental roles in mitochondrial proteostasis. STRING interactions define an exceptionally high-confidence MICOS complex network linking DNAJC11 to core subunits IMMT/Mitofilin (0.984), APOOL/MICOS27 (0.958), CHCHD3/MICOS50 (0.958), APOO/MICOS25 (0.955), SAMM50 (0.943), MICOS10 (0.938), MICOS13 (0.926), metaxins MTX1 (0.889) and MTX2 (0.776), and mitochondrial Hsp70 HSPA9 (0.760), positioning it at the intersection of cristae biogenesis and protein folding. Literature (16 papers) establishes emerging mechanistic insights: DNAJC11 identified as novel mitochondrial outer membrane protein causing neuromuscular pathology in mice through cristae disorganization; splicing mutations cause motor neuron pathology with lymphoid abnormalities; exists in complex with Mitofilin-SAMM50-metaxins-CHCHD3/6; 2025 studies reveal DNAJC11 stabilizes SARS-CoV-2 NSP3 to promote double-membrane vesicle formation suggesting viral hijacking of MICOS machinery; and evolutionary analyses place DNAJC11 within ancestral MICOS functions. ClinVar documents 60 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants predominantly large copy number losses on chromosome 1p36 regions (multiple overlapping deletions spanning 1p36.33-36.12), implicating DNAJC11 haploinsufficiency in developmental disorders though specific monogenic neuromuscular phenotypes require further definition. The convergence of J-domain chaperone activity, MICOS complex integration, cristae organization function, and neuromuscular disease associations suggests DNAJC11 functions as a specialized co-chaperone coordinating protein folding at mitochondrial contact sites during cristae remodeling, with therapeutic potential for mitochondrial myopathies, neurodegenerative diseases, and understanding viral manipulation of mitochondrial membranes.
