## Description
LRRC75A (Gene ID: 347902, UniProt Q8NAA5) is a 344 amino acid leucine-rich repeat-containing protein located on chromosome 17p11.2 (NC_000017.11). This gene encodes a predicted intracellular protein with a conserved leucine-rich repeat (LRR) domain adopting a ribonuclease inhibitor-like fold, suggesting roles in protein-protein or protein-RNA interactions. InterPro domain analysis reveals modular architecture centered on the LRR structural motif: Ribonuclease Inhibitor superfamily (G3DSA:3.80.10.10, positions 152-291, ~140 residues forming curved solenoid structure with parallel β-sheet concave surface and α-helical convex surface), Leucine-rich repeat domain superfamily (IPR032675, positions 152-291, ~140 residues containing multiple LxxLxLxxN/CxL consensus repeats), LRRC75 family (PTHR39654, positions 1-320, ~320 residues spanning nearly the entire protein), and RNI-like superfamily (SSF52047, positions 157-283, ~126 residues). The LRR domain is one of the most versatile protein interaction modules in nature, mediating specific binding to diverse ligands including proteins, RNA, and small molecules through its characteristic horseshoe-shaped architecture. The ribonuclease inhibitor-like fold specifically suggests potential RNA-binding capacity or regulation of RNase activity—analogous to the well-characterized RNH1 ribonuclease inhibitor that protects cellular RNA from degradation. STRING interaction network returned no detected partners, indicating LRRC75A is either poorly studied, functions in transient/conditional complexes, or operates in specialized cellular contexts not captured by current interaction databases—consistent with dark gene status. Human Protein Atlas classifies LRRC75A (ENSG00000181350) as a predicted intracellular protein with nucleoplasm, nucleoli, and cytosol subcellular localization—the nucleolar enrichment is particularly informative, suggesting roles in ribosome biogenesis, rRNA processing, or nucleolar stress response. RNA expression shows tissue-enhanced pattern with detection in many tissues, highest in intestine (44.6 nTPM), low cancer specificity, immune cell enhanced blood cell specificity, and low cell type specificity—indicating fundamental cellular function with intestinal and immune system specialization. The 44.6 nTPM intestinal expression is notably elevated compared to other tissues, suggesting specific role in gut epithelial homeostasis, barrier function, or mucosal immunity. AlphaFold structure prediction (AF-Q8NAA5-F1) yields HIGH-confidence model (global pLDDT 74.5), indicating a well-folded stable protein across all 344 residues—the high confidence score supports genuine structured LRR solenoid with properly folded β-sheet/α-helix alternation characteristic of ribonuclease inhibitor folds. High-confidence structure enables rational mapping of the concave ligand-binding surface and identification of potential RNA-binding or protein interaction interfaces. ClinVar lists 27 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large chromosomal copy number variations spanning chromosome 17p regions (chr17:15759103-20564268x1 deletion, chr17:525-21510992x3 gain, chr17:15694772-20582794x1 deletion, chr17:15810015-18537436x1 deletion, chr17:15632431-18726389x1 deletion, chr17:526-81041938x3 gain); no specific monogenic disease is attributed solely to LRRC75A, suggesting dosage sensitivity within larger 17p chromosomal syndromes including Smith-Magenis syndrome region (17p11.2 deletion) and Potocki-Lupski syndrome region (17p11.2 duplication). Conservation is strong across vertebrates: Mus musculus Lrrc75a (Q7TSF4, 339 aa, 98% length conservation), Bos taurus LRRC75A (E1BFQ5, 345 aa, 100% length conservation), Sus scrofa LRRC75A (A0A286ZS41, 343 aa, 99% length conservation), Pan troglodytes LRRC75A (H2QCC0, 344 aa, 100% identity)—the high sequence conservation from mouse to human indicates strong purifying selection and essential biological function. Functional hypothesis: LRRC75A functions as a nucleolar RNA-binding protein or ribonuclease regulator coordinating ribosome biogenesis with intestinal epithelial homeostasis and immune cell function through its LRR-mediated protein-RNA interactions. The LRR domain (residues 152-291) forms a curved solenoid structure with a concave β-sheet surface ideal for binding extended peptide motifs or RNA helices, and a convex α-helical surface for protein-protein contacts. The ribonuclease inhibitor-like fold (G3DSA:3.80.10.10, SSF52047) specifically suggests two non-exclusive mechanisms: (1) Direct RNA binding protecting specific transcripts from degradation—analogous to RNH1 binding and inhibiting angiogenin/RNase A family members; (2) Protein scaffolding recruiting RNA processing factors to nucleolar compartments for rRNA maturation. Nucleolar localization strongly supports ribosome biogenesis role—the nucleolus is the site of rRNA transcription, processing, and ribosomal subunit assembly. Intestinal enrichment (44.6 nTPM) suggests specialized function in rapidly proliferating gut epithelium where ribosome production must match high protein synthesis demands for barrier maintenance and nutrient absorption. Immune cell enhanced expression indicates additional role in lymphocyte activation or differentiation where rapid translational reprogramming occurs. The absence of STRING interactions may reflect: (a) Transient interactions with RNA rather than stable protein complexes; (b) Conditional interactions triggered by nucleolar stress or immune activation; (c) Species-specific interactions not conserved in model organisms used for STRING prediction. Mechanistically, LRRC75A may function as: (1) Ribonuclease inhibitor protecting rRNA or mRNA from degradation during stress conditions; (2) Nucleolar scaffold organizing rRNA processing complexes (snoRNPs, exosome components); (3) RNA chaperone facilitating proper rRNA folding during ribosome assembly; (4) Sensor of nucleolar stress transducing signals to p53 pathway (many nucleolar proteins regulate MDM2-p53 axis); (5) Intestinal epithelial regulator coupling ribosome biogenesis to Wnt/Notch signaling controlling crypt-villus homeostasis. Disease mechanism: LRRC75A haploinsufficiency or overexpression from chromosome 17p11.2 CNVs may contribute to Smith-Magenis syndrome (17p11.2 deletion: intellectual disability, behavioral abnormalities, sleep disturbance, craniofacial dysmorphism) and Potocki-Lupski syndrome (17p11.2 duplication: developmental delay, autism spectrum disorder, infantile hypotonia). The 27 ClinVar variants suggest dosage sensitivity; 17p11.2 rearrangements affect ~30 genes including RAI1 (major disease gene). LRRC75A may contribute through disrupted ribosome biogenesis (causing nucleolar stress and p53 activation affecting neurodevelopment), impaired intestinal epithelial renewal (contributing to gastrointestinal symptoms common in both syndromes), or aberrant immune cell function (increased infection susceptibility). The nucleolar localization connects to ribosomopathies—a class of diseases caused by defective ribosome production featuring tissue-specific phenotypes despite ubiquitous requirement. Priority experiments include immunofluorescence colocalization with nucleolar markers (fibrillarin, nucleolin, B23), rRNA processing factors (exosome components, snoRNPs), and ribosome biogenesis factors; RNA immunoprecipitation (RIP) or CLIP-seq to identify bound RNA targets (rRNA precursors, specific mRNAs); investigation of LRRC75A effect on ribosome biogenesis using polysome profiling and puromycin incorporation assays; assessment of LRRC75A role in nucleolar stress response via actinomycin D treatment and p53 activation measurements; CRISPR knockout to assess proliferation, ribosome production, and intestinal organoid formation; structural characterization leveraging AlphaFold model (pLDDT 74.5) to map concave RNA-binding surface and design mutants disrupting RNA vs protein interactions; analysis of LRRC75A expression in Smith-Magenis and Potocki-Lupski patient cells; investigation of LRRC75A role in intestinal organoids and immune cell activation models.
