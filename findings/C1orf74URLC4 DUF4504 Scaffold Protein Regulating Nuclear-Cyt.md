Functional hypothesis: C1orf74 operates as a DUF4504-domain scaffold coordinating nuclear-cytoplasmic trafficking and cell cycle progression. The high-confidence AlphaFold structure suggests the DUF4504 fold creates a stable protein-protein interaction platform. Interaction with NUP155 (nuclear pore) and RFC5 (replication factor C) implicates nucleocytoplasmic transport and DNA replication coordination. USP54/USP33 interactions indicate involvement in deubiquitination cascades, regulating protein stability of cell cycle or signaling substrates. CWF19L1 partnership directly links to cell cycle control, possibly at G2/M checkpoint. The plasma membrane-cytosol dual localization suggests shuttling between compartments, potentially transporting cargo or scaffolding signaling complexes at membrane interfaces. Skin-enriched expression may reflect specialized roles in epithelial cell proliferation or barrier function. Disease-associated CNVs likely disrupt dosage-sensitive scaffold function, causing cell cycle dysregulation or impaired nuclear transport contributing to developmental or oncogenic phenotypes.
