# LOC129389855: 7q31.31 MPRA-Validated Transcriptional Silencer with Deletion-Predominant Pathogenicity Near MET/BRAF/FOXP2 Region

**Quality Score:** 3.45/10  (E=5.4, D=1.5) [WEAK]

## Description
LOC129389855 is a functionally characterized cis-regulatory element on chromosome 7q31.31 (genomic coordinates: 127959881-127960081 on NC_000007.14, spanning approximately 200 bp). This genomic region was experimentally validated as a transcriptional silencer using STARR-seq massively parallel reporter assays (MPRAs) in K562 erythroleukemia cells, demonstrating ability to repress activity of super core promoter SCP1. The element lacks protein-coding evidence (no UniProt entry, no STRING interactions, no HPA protein expression), confirming its identity as a non-coding regulatory element. The accessible chromatin subregion annotation indicates active regulatory function in hematopoietic cell lineages. ClinVar analysis identified 8 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants with deletion-predominant pathogenicity: 6 copy number losses (deletions, 75%) and 2 copy number gains (duplications, 25%), indicating haploinsufficiency is the primary pathogenic mechanism. This 75% deletion bias contrasts with triplosensitivity patterns seen in other silencers (LOC129390366: 100% duplications; LOC129390416: 91% duplications), suggesting 7q31 silencers are predominantly haploinsufficiency-critical. The 7q31 chromosomal region contains critical dosage-sensitive genes including MET (proto-oncogene, hepatocyte growth factor receptor - papillary renal cell carcinoma), BRAF (serine/threonine kinase, RAS/MAPK pathway - melanoma, cardiofaciocutaneous syndrome), FOXP2 (forkhead box P2 - speech and language development, verbal dyspraxia), and EGFR (epidermal growth factor receptor - glioblastoma, lung cancer). As an experimentally validated silencer, LOC129389855 likely functions to repress transcription of target genes in cis during erythroid differentiation and potentially neural development. Loss of this silencer causes derepression/overexpression of target genes (potentially MET, BRAF, or FOXP2), contributing to 7q31 deletion syndrome phenotypes including developmental delay, speech/language disorders (via FOXP2 pathway), increased cancer predisposition (via MET/BRAF pathways), and growth abnormalities. The haploinsufficiency pattern suggests this element regulates proto-oncogenes or developmental transcription factors where loss of repression drives pathogenic overexpression.
