## Description
TTC21A (UniProt Q8NDW8), also known as IFT139A or Thm2, encodes a massive 1320 aa structural scaffold protein functioning as an essential component of the Intraflagellar Transport Complex A (IFT-A) which drives retrograde transport within cilia and flagella. InterPro analysis reveals an elaborate architecture dominated by protein-protein interaction modules: multiple Tetratricopeptide Repeat (TPR) domains (IPR019734, positions 110-143/180-213/334-367) forming helical hairpins for partner binding, a large N-terminal ARM repeat domain (IPR056833, residues 12-234), a central ARM domain (IPR056832, residues 278-549), and additional C-terminal ARM domains (IPR056834/056835/056836, residues 764-1315) creating a superhelical structure ideal for scaffolding large complexes, all embedded within the TTC21A/21B family (IPR040364, residues 9-1320) and the broader TPR-like superfamily (IPR011990). AlphaFold predicts high confidence structure (pLDDT 81.1) indicating a rigid, well-folded elongated scaffold where the stacked TPR and ARM repeats form a stable curved solenoid capable of binding multiple IFT-A subunits simultaneously without disorder. STRING interactions define a tight IFT-A core complex: IFT140 (0.838, largest IFT-A subunit, direct binding partner), WDR35 (0.805, IFT-A component linked to ciliopathies), IFT20 (0.791, IFT-B component linking A/B complexes), IFT122 (0.756), IFT74 (0.738), TTC29 (0.732, ciliary protein), IFT81 (0.724), IFT52 (0.706), CFAP43 (0.557, cilia and flagella associated protein), and WDR19 (0.557, IFT144). HPA classifies TTC21A as tissue-enriched with exclusive high expression in testis (43.1 nTPM), not detected in cancers or immune cells, localized to nucleoplasm though functionally ciliary, reflecting its specialized role in spermatogenesis and flagellar assembly. ClinVar lists 20 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including splice site mutations (c.435+1G>A, c.2459-2A>T, c.1192-1G>T), frameshift deletions (c.3025_3043del p.Leu1009fs, c.3450+2del), large copy number gains (3p26.3-14.3x3) and deletions, directly causing Spermatogenic Failure 37 (SPGF37) characterized by asthenoteratospermia (reduced motility and abnormal morphology) and Multiple Morphological Abnormalities of the Flagella (MMAF). Functional hypothesis: TTC21A operates as the structural backbone of the IFT-A complex through a coordinated mechanism: (1) Complex assembly - The extended TPR/ARM solenoid acts as a molecular ruler and scaffold, binding IFT140, WDR35, and other subunits to form the stable hexameric IFT-A core; (2) Retrograde transport - IFT-A loaded with cargo (turnover products, signaling receptors) moves from the ciliary tip to the base via dynein-2 motor, with TTC21A ensuring complex integrity during this long-distance transport; (3) Sperm flagellogenesis - In developing spermatids, TTC21A-mediated IFT is critical for building the axoneme and accessory structures of the sperm tail; loss leads to short, bent, or absent flagella causing male infertility; (4) Ciliary signaling - By regulating retrograde traffic, TTC21A controls the residence time of signaling molecules (e.g., Hedgehog components) in the cilium, linking it to broader ciliopathy phenotypes beyond infertility; (5) Disease mechanism - Truncating variants cause complete loss of IFT-A function leading to severe MMAF; hypomorphic variants may cause partial defects or syndromic ciliopathies (e.g., Jeune syndrome, Sensenbrenner syndrome) though currently only infertility is definitively assigned; (6) Conservation - Absolute conservation from humans to mice (Thm2) and lower eukaryotes indicates ancient role in ciliary biology. Dark aspects requiring investigation include: specific binding interfaces for each IFT-A subunit on the TTC21A scaffold, regulation of IFT-A assembly/disassembly at the ciliary base, potential roles in non-testicular ciliated tissues (kidney, retina, brain) given ubiquitous IFT requirement, contribution to syndromic ciliopathies beyond isolated infertility, interaction with dynein-2 motor machinery, post-translational modifications controlling IFT activity, and therapeutic strategies for restoring IFT function in ciliopathies.
