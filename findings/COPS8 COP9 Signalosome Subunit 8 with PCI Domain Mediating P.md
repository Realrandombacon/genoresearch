## Description
COPS8 encodes a 209-amino acid COP9 signalosome complex subunit 8 (UniProt Q99627), also known as CSN8, a well-folded regulatory protein functioning as an essential stoichiometric component of the COP9 signalosome (CSN) holocomplex that mediates protein deneddylation and cell cycle regulation through its PCI (Proteasome-CSI) domain architecture. The protein contains a canonical PCI domain (IPR000717, positions 8-179; PS50250 PCI domain profile, positions 8-179) characteristic of proteasome lid and COP9 signalosome subunits, classified within the CSN8/PSMD8/EIF3K family (IPR033464, positions 34-164; PF10075, positions 34-164) and specifically within the COP9 signalosome subunit CSN8 family (IPR033205, positions 1-209; PTHR13339, positions 1-209) with G3DSA:1.25.40.990 homologous superfamily architecture (positions 20-165). AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (pLDDT 85.1, residues 1-209) indicating stable folding with well-defined PCI domain suitable for protein-protein interactions within the CSN holocomplex. STRING interaction analysis reveals COPS8 forms the complete COP9 signalosome complex with ultra-high confidence interactions: COPS7A (0.999), GPS1/COPS1 (0.999), COPS5 (0.999), COPS7B (0.999), COPS4 (0.999), COPS6 (0.974), COPS2 (0.973), COPS3 (0.949), plus CUL4A (0.986, cullin substrate for deneddylation) and COPS9 (0.638), confirming COPS8's role as an integral CSN subunit for cullin-RING E3 ubiquitin ligase (CRL) regulation through NEDD8 removal. HPA data shows ubiquitous expression with low tissue specificity (detected in all tissues), nucleoplasm and cytosol subcellular localization consistent with nuclear-cytoplasmic shuttling for CRL substrate regulation, annotated for essential proteins and predicted intracellular proteins classes with protein deneddylation (GO:0000338), COP9 signalosome assembly (GO:0010387), and COP9 signalosome (GO:0008180) GO terms. ClinVar contains 77 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number gains (2q33.3-37.3, 2q37.2-37.3, 2q35-37.3, 2q37.3), copy number losses (2q37.2-37.3), deletions, and splice site variants (c.440-11T>C, c.439+161C>T, c.503-9del), though no specific Mendelian disorder is named, suggesting COPS8 dosage sensitivity affects cell cycle and DNA repair across multiple organ systems. With only 41 publications, COPS8 has emerging oncogenic roles: COPS8 accelerates malignant development in cutaneous melanoma as oncogene predicting poor prognosis (2022, 3 cit), COPS8 promotes hepatocellular carcinoma progression through PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway activation (2024), COPS8-mediated deneddylation of DHX9 (DEAH-box helicase 9) enhances radioresistance in non-small cell lung cancer by facilitating DNA damage repair (2025), exosome-like nanoparticles from mulberry bark prevent DSS-induced colitis via AhR/COPS8 pathway (2022, 157 cit - highly cited), COPS5-mediated nuclear exclusion of p27 responsible for vascular smooth muscle cell proliferation by COPS8 hypomorphism (2022). The functional hypothesis: COPS8 serves as a structural and catalytic component of the 8-subunit COP9 signalosome where the PCI domain (positions 8-179) mediates heterodimerization with other PCI-containing subunits (COPS1, COPS4, COPS7A/B) to form the CSN lid structure that positions the catalytic COPS5 (JAB1/MPN domain metalloenzyme) for cullin deneddylation; ultra-high confidence interactions with all CSN subunits indicate COPS8 is essential for holocomplex assembly and stability; CUL4A interaction confirms direct engagement with cullin-RING ligase substrates where CSN removes NEDD8 to inhibit CRL activity and prevent excessive substrate ubiquitination; nucleoplasm/cytosol dual localization suggests COPS8-CSN shuttles between compartments to regulate both nuclear (transcription factors, DNA repair proteins) and cytoplasmic (cell cycle regulators) CRL substrates; DHX9 deneddylation reveals specific substrate targeting where COPS8-CSN removes NEDD8 from DHX9 helicase to enhance its DNA damage repair activity, explaining NSCLC radioresistance when COPS8 is overexpressed; PI3K/AKT/mTOR activation in HCC suggests COPS8-CSN stabilizes pathway components through CRL regulation, potentially deneddylating and stabilizing PTEN-negative regulators or mTOR complex components; melanoma oncogenic activity indicates COPS8 dosage affects cell cycle progression through p27 regulation (COPS5-mediated nuclear exclusion), linking CSN function to CDK inhibitor localization; 77 pathogenic CNVs without named disorder suggests COPS8 dosage affects global CRL activity, causing cell cycle dysregulation and DNA repair defects across tissues; high structural confidence (pLDDT 85.1) indicates stable PCI domain fold suitable for CSN assembly, with potential for small molecule disruption of CSN-CRL interfaces; AhR/COPS8 pathway in colitis suggests COPS8 integrates aryl hydrocarbon receptor signaling with protein homeostasis, potentially regulating inflammatory cytokine production through CRL-mediated transcription factor turnover.
