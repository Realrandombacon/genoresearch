# SYT8: Dual C2 Domain Synaptotagmin Regulating Exocytosis with Oncogenic Roles in Gastrointestinal Cancers

## Description
SYT8 encodes a 387 aa synaptotagmin family membrane protein (UniProt Q8NBV8) that functions as a calcium sensor for regulated exocytosis. InterPro analysis reveals a canonical synaptotagmin architecture: N-terminal transmembrane region followed by two C2 domains (IPR000008/PF00168 at aa 103-221 and 231-357) that bind calcium and phospholipids, plus synaptotagmin-specific motifs (IPR001565, PTHR10024). AlphaFold predicts a well-folded structure (pLDDT 79.9) with the two C2 domains positioned for membrane interaction. STRING interactions confirm functional membership in the exocytotic machinery: synaptotagmin paralogs SYT1 (0.584), SYT2 (0.428), SYT9 (0.587), SYT11 (0.590); SNARE complex components VAMP2 (0.578), VAMP8 (0.552), STX2 (0.565); trafficking regulator STON2 (0.562); and TNNI2 (0.550), which literature links to SYT8-driven pancreatic cancer progression via TNNI2/ERRα/SIRT1 signaling. ClinVar reports 25 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly 11p15.5 copy number gains/duplications, suggesting haploinsufficiency or dosage sensitivity. Literature (20 papers) reveals emerging oncogenic roles: (1) SYT8 promotes pancreatic cancer progression and metastasis through TNNI2/ERRα/SIRT1 pathway (2021), (2) drives colorectal cancer progression and immune evasion via SETD1A-H3K4me3 epigenetic axis (2026), (3) biomarker for peritoneal metastasis in gastric cancer (2016, 77 citations), (4) prognostic DNA methylation marker in bladder cancer. Mechanistically, SYT8 likely serves as a calcium-triggered membrane fusion regulator that, when dysregulated in cancers, enhances secretory pathways supporting tumor growth, immune evasion, and metastatic spread. The 11p15.5 location overlaps with Beckwith-Wiedemann syndrome region, suggesting potential developmental roles. Unlike neuronal synaptotagmins (SYT1-4), SYT8 appears specialized for non-neuronal secretory processes co-opted in gastrointestinal malignancies.
