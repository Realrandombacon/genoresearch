# SUGCT: Mitochondrial Glutarate-CoA Transferase Linking Lysine/Tryptophan Catabolism to Glutaric Aciduria and Metabolic Syndrome

**Date:** 2026-05-01T20:21:26.032184

**Quality Score:** 5/10 (GOOD)

**Confidence:** MEDIUM

## Description
SUGCT (Succinyl-CoA:glutarate-CoA transferase, also known as C7orf10) encodes a 438 amino acid mitochondrial enzyme (UniProt Q9HAC7) that catalyzes the reversible transfer of CoA from succinyl-CoA to glutarate, producing glutaryl-CoA and succinate in the lysine and tryptophan catabolic pathways. InterPro analysis reveals a canonical CoA-transferase family III architecture (IPR003673, PF02515, residues 41-408) with three structural domains: an N-terminal domain 1 (G3DSA:3.40.50.10540, residues 37-262), a central domain 3 (G3DSA:3.30.1540.10, residues 263-350), and a C-terminal domain 1 continuation (residues 351-438). The CoA-transferase III family (PTHR48207, SSF89796) uses a ping-pong mechanism involving a catalytic glutamate residue that forms a covalent anhydride intermediate with CoA.\n\nAlphaFold prediction shows very high global confidence (pLDDT 91.8) across all 438 aa, indicating a well-folded, stable enzyme with defined active site architecture typical of CoA-transferases. The high structural confidence suggests SUGCT is a genuine metabolic enzyme rather than a disordered regulatory protein.\n\nSTRING interactions reveal SUGCT operates within mitochondrial amino acid catabolism and TCA cycle networks: GCDH (0.881) is glutaryl-CoA dehydrogenase that converts glutaryl-CoA to crotonyl-CoA in lysine catabolism—the direct downstream partner; MPLKIP (0.986) is a mitotic kinase interactor of unknown metabolic function; HSDL2 (0.824) is a hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase; IYD (0.823) is iodotyrosine deiodinase; RFK (0.802) is riboflavin kinase; SDHA (0.502) and SDHB (0.790) are succinate dehydrogenase subunits linking to the TCA cycle; CLYBL (0.771) is citrate lyase; ILVBL (0.468) is an ILV/leucine biosynthesis enzyme; HACL1 (0.421) is involved in alpha-oxidation. The GCDH interaction is particularly significant as GCDH deficiency causes glutaric aciduria type 1 (GA1).\n\nHPA expression shows SUGCT is tissue-enhanced with highest expression in kidney (41.4 nTPM) and liver (26.6 nTPM)—the primary organs for amino acid catabolism and urea cycle function. Subcellular localization includes mitochondria (consistent with enzymatic function), microtubules, mitotic spindle, primary cilium, and basal body, suggesting potential non-metabolic roles in cell division or ciliary function. Blood cell expression is immune cell-enhanced, and the lncRNA SUGCT-AS1 regulates macrophage proinflammatory responses (2023, 9 citations).\n\nClinVar reports 38 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number losses/gains at 7p14.1 and single nucleotide variants (e.g., c.313-1G>C splice site mutation). While no specific monogenic disease is definitively assigned, SUGCT is annotated for glutaricaciduria involvement. A 2023 case report describes paternal uniparental disomy 7 with homozygous SUGCT mutation in a fetus with postnatal overweight. Mouse knockout studies (2019, 27 citations) show SUGCT is non-essential but creates diet-linked, age-related microbiome disbalance with diabetes-like metabolic syndrome phenotype.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: SUGCT functions as a metabolic gatekeeper in lysine/tryptophan catabolism by generating glutaryl-CoA from glutarate, which is then oxidized by GCDH. Under normal conditions, this pathway prevents glutarate accumulation and maintains CoA pool balance. In SUGCT deficiency, glutarate accumulates and may be excreted or converted to alternative metabolites, potentially contributing to glutaric aciduria spectrum disorders. The kidney/liver enrichment reflects these organs' role in clearing amino acid catabolites. The metabolic syndrome phenotype in knockout mice suggests SUGCT deficiency alters microbiome composition through accumulated glutarate or related metabolites that affect gut bacteria. The ciliary/microtubule localization hints at non-canonical functions—possibly linking metabolic state to cell division or ciliary signaling. The SUGCT-AS1 lncRNA regulation of macrophage inflammation suggests metabolic-immune crosstalk, where SUGCT activity influences inflammatory responses through metabolite signaling (e.g., glutaryl-CoA, succinate). The high-confidence MPLKIP interaction may connect metabolic state to cell cycle regulation. Therapeutic targeting of SUGCT could modulate glutaryl-CoA levels in GA1 patients or alter microbiome composition in metabolic disease.
