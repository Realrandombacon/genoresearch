# MTLN: Mitochondrial Inner Membrane Micropeptide Regulating Membrane Potential and Calcium Homeostasis

**Date:** 2026-03-19T20:19:01.232618

**Quality Score:** 3.9/10  (E=6.3, D=1.5) [MODERATE]

## Description
MTLN (mitoregulin) encodes a 56-amino acid mitochondrial inner membrane micropeptide (UniProt Q8NCU8) that functions as a positive regulator of mitochondrial membrane potential and calcium ion sequestration. The protein contains a conserved Mitoregulin domain (IPR038778, PF22002, PTHR37154, residues 1-56) and is highly conserved across vertebrates (mouse Q8BT35: 56 aa, >90% identity). AlphaFold predicts a well-folded transmembrane structure with high confidence (pLDDT 89.9), consistent with its membrane protein classification. HPA data shows group-enriched expression with highest levels in skeletal muscle (736.2 nTPM), tongue (262.1 nTPM), and heart muscle (232.8 nTPM), reflecting its role in high-energy-demand tissues requiring tight mitochondrial calcium regulation. STRING interactions reveal associations with NBDY (0.583), C20orf204 (0.573), STRIT1 (0.544), and MRLN (0.507) — all small mitochondrial membrane proteins — suggesting MTLN functions as part of a micropeptide complex that modulates inner membrane permeability and calcium uniporter activity. ClinVar contains 11 pathogenic variants including copy number losses (2q13 deletions) and the c.5C>T (p.Ala2Val) missense variant, indicating that disruption of MTLN causes mitochondrial dysfunction phenotypes. The small size (56 aa), single transmembrane topology, and muscle-specific high expression suggest MTLN acts as a regulatory subunit that fine-tunes mitochondrial membrane potential during metabolic stress, potentially by stabilizing calcium retention or preventing permeability transition pore opening. Loss of MTLN would impair ATP production and calcium buffering, explaining the pathogenic variant burden in neuromuscular and cardiac tissues.
