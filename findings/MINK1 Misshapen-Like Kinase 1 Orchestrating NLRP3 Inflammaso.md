## Description
MINK1 (Misshapen-Like Kinase 1) is a 1332 amino acid STE20 family serine/threonine kinase that functions as a critical regulator of NLRP3 inflammasome activation, NK cell-mediated cytotoxicity, and intervertebral disc degeneration through N-terminal catalytic kinase domain-mediated phosphorylation cascades, JNK/c-Jun signaling activation, and TRAF2-NCK1 complex assembly for MAPK pathway regulation and Wnt signaling control. Literature (80 papers) establishes MINK1 as an emerging signaling kinase: MINK1 regulates NLRP3 inflammasome activation in common carp establishing innate immune function; resveratrol contributes to NK cell-mediated breast cancer cytotoxicity by upregulating ULBP2 through miR-17-5p downmodulation and MINK1/JNK/c-Jun signaling activation revealing immune surveillance mechanism; MINK1 deficiency stimulates nucleus pulposus cell pyroptosis and exacerbates intervertebral disc degeneration demonstrating tissue homeostasis role; TNIK and MINK1 implicated in triple-negative breast cancer tumorigenesis and invasion through Wnt signaling; and MINK1 roles in MAP kinase signaling cascades confirming pathway integration. As an emerging STE20 kinase with multifaceted disease associations, MINK1 represents a critical node connecting innate immunity to cancer immune surveillance, tissue degeneration, and Wnt pathway regulation through N-terminal kinase domain-mediated JNK/c-Jun phosphorylation, TRAF2-NCK1 complex assembly with NCK1/TNIK partners, CNH domain-dependent protein interactions, and PPP2CA-regulated dephosphorylation disrupted in NLRP3 inflammasome activation, NK cell cytotoxicity deficiency, intervertebral disc degeneration, triple-negative breast cancer invasion, and pyroptotic cell death.
