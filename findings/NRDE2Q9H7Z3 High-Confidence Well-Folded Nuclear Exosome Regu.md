## Description
NRDE2 (Nuclear exosome regulator NRDE2, C14orf102) is a 1164 amino acid intracellular protein localized to nuclear speckles and nucleolus (UniProt Q9H7Z3) with high-confidence well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 72.1) containing canonical RNA surveillance machinery architecture including MTR4-interacting domain (MID) (cd22200, residues 162-258) forming a ~97 aa module (~8%) essential for binding the RNA helicase MTREX/MTR4, multiple Tetratricopeptide repeat (TPR) domains (IPR011990/G3DSA:1.25.40.10, residues 290-377/411-510/728-815/963-1013/1068-1152) forming an extended helical solenoid (~860 aa, ~74%) for protein-protein interactions, siRNA-mediated silencing protein NRDE-2 family (IPR013633/PF08424/PTHR13471, residues 1-1163) encompassing nearly the full length, and molecular functions including RNA splicing regulation, mRNA stabilization, positive regulation of RNA export from nucleus, and RNA degradation, ubiquitous expression (detected in many tissues), low tissue/cancer specificity, predicted intracellular proteins classification, functioning as a large high-confidence well-folded nuclear scaffold that coordinates RNA fate through MID-mediated recruitment of the exosome cofactor MTREX, TPR-mediated assembly of regulatory complexes, and NRDE2 network assembly via partnerships with MTREX, ZFC3H1, ZCCHC8, PPP2R2A, PPP2R2B, CCDC174, GPATCH1, CRNKL1, PSMC1, and TBC1D10B.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals NRDE2 is a multi-domain adaptor critical for nuclear RNA quality control. The N-terminal MTR4-interacting domain (MID) specifically binds MTREX (MTR4), the RNA helicase that drives the nuclear exosome complex, positioning NRDE2 as a key recruiter of degradation machinery to specific substrates. The extensive array of five TPR repeats (residues 290-1152) creates a large interaction surface, typical of scaffolding proteins that assemble multi-protein complexes. The high-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 72.1) confirms a stable, elongated architecture suitable for bridging distant factors within nuclear speckles.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places NRDE2 at the center of a nuclear RNA processing and degradation network: strongest interactions with PPP2R2A (0.861) and PPP2R2B (0.859), regulatory subunits of Protein Phosphatase 2A, suggesting a role in dephosphorylating RNA processing factors or NRDE2 itself to modulate activity. High confidence links also include ZFC3H1 (0.711, nuclear exosome targeting factor) and ZCCHC8 (0.705, core component of the NEXT complex), directly connecting NRDE2 to the machinery that channels RNAs to the exosome. The interaction with MTREX (0.419) confirms the physical link predicted by the MID domain. Additional partners like CCDC174, GPATCH1, CRNKL1 (spliceosome-associated), PSMC1 (proteasome), and TBC1D10B suggest broader roles in coupling RNA surveillance to splicing, translation, and protein turnover.\n\nHPA expression shows detection in many tissues with low specificity, consistent with a fundamental housekeeping role in RNA metabolism. Subcellular location is confirmed as nucleoplasm, nuclear speckles, and nucleolus, aligning with its function in RNA surveillance. Biological processes include cell cycle, mitosis, DNA damage response, and mRNA processing, indicating involvement in stress responses and proliferation.\n\nClinVar documents 23 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains and losses affecting the 14q31.1-32.33 region: CNV gains (14q31.3-32.33 chr14:88580184-107285437x3, 14q23.1-32.33 chr14:58894502-107227240x3, 14q31.2-32.33 chr14:84537502-107285437x3) and CNV losses (14q31.1-32.2 chr14:79886061-96870809x1, 14q31.3-32.13 chr14:88401076-94725706x1). Several intronic SNVs (c.1412-30G>T, c.3159-10T>G) are also listed. The moderate variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity of the 14q locus. Haploinsufficiency (deletions) likely impairs nuclear RNA surveillance, leading to accumulation of aberrant transcripts and genomic instability, while duplications may cause sequestration of exosome components or dysregulated phosphatase activity, contributing to neurodevelopmental phenotypes associated with 14q deletion/duplication syndromes.\n\nConservation is strong across metazoans: human (Q9H7Z3, 1164 aa), mouse (Q80XC6, 1172 aa), C. elegans (G5EG51, 1270 aa), indicating strict evolutionary constraint on the MID-TPR architecture.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: NRDE2 functions as a master scaffold in the nuclear speckle that recruits the MTREX helicase (via its MID domain) to specific RNA substrates or complexes assembled via its TPR repeats. The strong interaction with PPP2R2A/B proposes a regulatory mechanism where PP2A dephosphorylates NRDE2 or its partners to switch between 'surveillance' (degradation) and 'export' modes. The links to ZFC3H1 and ZCCHC8 suggest NRDE2 acts as a bridge between the NEXT complex and the exosome, facilitating the handover of unstable RNAs for degradation. Dysregulation via 14q CNVs disrupts this balance: loss leads to toxic RNA accumulation and potential R-loop formation (DNA damage), while gain may hyper-activate degradation or mislocalize phosphatases, causing defects in mRNA maturation and cell cycle progression.
