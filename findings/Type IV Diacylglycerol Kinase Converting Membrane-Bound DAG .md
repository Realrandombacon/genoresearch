## Description
DGKD (Diacylglycerol kinase delta) is a 1214 amino acid type IV diacylglycerol kinase that phosphorylates diacylglycerol (DAG) to produce phosphatidic acid (PA), regulating two critical lipid second messengers in cellular signal transduction. The protein contains a modular domain architecture including an N-terminal Pleckstrin Homology domain (PH, cd13274, positions 55-151) for membrane recruitment, two C1 domains (cd20847 positions 140-224; cd20893 positions 231-291) binding phorbol esters and DAG, a central DGK accessory domain (IPR000756, positions 763-920) with catalytic function, and a C-terminal SAM domain (cd09575, positions 1141-1205) mediating protein-protein interactions. AlphaFold prediction shows high structural confidence (pLDDT 71.4), indicating a well-folded multi-domain enzyme. HPA data reveals tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in brain (29.7 nTPM) and skeletal muscle (26.7 nTPM), cytosolic localization consistent with lipid kinase functions. STRING interaction network identifies 10 partners in glycerolipid metabolism: lipoprotein lipase LPL (0.906), glycerol-3-phosphate dehydrogenase GPD2 (0.817), glyceronephosphate O-acyltransferase GNPAT (0.812), acylglycerol-3-phosphate acyltransferases AGPAT3 (0.779) and AGPAT2 (0.724), phospholipase C epsilon PLCE1 (0.751), diacylglycerol O-acyltransferases DGAT1 (0.727) and DGAT2 (0.536), lysophospholipid acyltransferase MBOAT2 (0.726), and adipose triglyceride lipase PNPLA2 (0.723). ClinVar reports 39 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number gains/losses and splice site mutations. Literature (49 papers) establishes critical neurological role: DGKD disruption causes seizures in humans and mice through altered DAG/PA balance affecting neuronal excitability; implicated in TKI-resistant kidney cancer via CIRCRNA-DGKD/miR-125-5P/VE-cadherin signaling inducing vasculogenic mimicry; genetic variants predispose to kidney stone disease. DGKD likely functions as a membrane-recruited lipid kinase converting growth factor-generated DAG to PA through PH domain membrane anchoring and C1 domain substrate recognition, with SAM domain enabling oligomerization—maintaining lipid second messenger homeostasis critical for neuronal firing thresholds and cancer cell angiogenic programs.
