## Description
SDSL (Serine dehydratase-like, also known as Serine/threonine dehydratase or L-serine deaminase, Q96GA7) encodes a 329 aa cytosolic enzyme functioning as a pyridoxal phosphate (PLP)-dependent lyase that catalyzes the deamination of L-serine and L-threonine to produce pyruvate and ammonia, serving as a critical node connecting amino acid catabolism with one-carbon metabolism, hepatic gluconeogenesis, and lipid homeostasis. Structural architecture reveals a canonical PLP-dependent enzyme fold: a Tryptophan synthase beta chain-like PALP domain (IPR001926/IPR036052/PF00291/G3DSA:3.40.50.1100/SSF53686, residues 16-310/14-316/16-310/18-50+142-312+51-141/14-316) forming the catalytic core containing the conserved pyridoxal phosphate binding site (IPR000634/PS00165, residues 39-52) with the characteristic lysine residue forming the internal aldimine Schiff base linkage to PLP cofactor, belonging to the Serine/Threonine Dehydratase family (IPR050147/PTHR48078, residues 8-325/8-325) with GO terms for pyridoxal phosphate binding (GO:0030170) and amino acid metabolic process (GO:0006520); AlphaFold prediction (pLDDT 95.2, very high confidence) confirms an exceptionally well-folded enzyme with organized PALP domain architecture consistent with stable PLP-dependent lyase requiring precise active site geometry for substrate binding and beta-elimination catalysis. STRING interactions define the complete amino acid metabolic network: cystathionine beta-synthase CBS (0.953, highest confidence) catalyzing cystathionine formation in transsulfuration pathway, alanine-glyoxylate aminotransferase AGXT (0.939) metabolizing glyoxylate in peroxisomes, phosphoserine phosphatase PSPH (0.933) dephosphorylating phosphoserine to serine, branched-chain aminotransferase BCAT1 (0.909) transaminating branched-chain amino acids, paralogous serine dehydratase SDS (0.906) with overlapping substrate specificity, cystathionine gamma-lyase CTH (0.898) cleaving cystathionine to cysteine in transsulfuration, serine hydroxymethyltransferases SHMT2 (0.534) and SHMT1 (0.513) converting serine to glycine for one-carbon metabolism, serine racemase SRR (0.471) producing D-serine neurotransmitter, and BCAT2 (0.470). HPA localizes SDSL to cytosol with tissue-enhanced expression highest in liver (102.4 nTPM) reflecting its primary role in hepatic amino acid catabolism and gluconeogenesis, detected in all tissues, low cancer specificity indicating housekeeping metabolic function, group-enriched expression in blood cells, and cell type enhanced single cell specificity; classified as enzyme, metabolic protein, predicted intracellular/membrane protein with isomerase and lyase molecular functions involved in lipid metabolism. ClinVar documents 9 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including multiple chromosome 12 copy number alterations: whole arm gains at 12p13.33-q24.33 (chr12:173787-133777902x3, chr12:1-133851895x3, chr12:121271-133196807x3) duplicating the locus, intronic variant c.796+107C>T potentially affecting splicing, and losses at 12q24.13-24.21 (chr12:113445811-114933860, chr12:113077775-114372366x1); these CNVs are associated with developmental disorders and metabolic disease susceptibility. Literature search returned 568 papers but notably these predominantly concern SDSL-EPR spectroscopy (Site-Directed Spin Labeling Electron Paramagnetic Resonance), a biophysical technique acronym unrelated to the gene itself, confirming SDSL as a genuine dark gene lacking direct functional characterization despite its clear enzymatic role inferred from homology to bacterial serine dehydratases; liver-specific expression (102.4 nTPM) positions SDSL at the center of hepatic metabolic flexibility during fasting when amino acids serve as gluconeogenic substrates; very high AlphaFold confidence (95.2) reflects strong evolutionary constraint on PLP-binding residues and catalytic machinery; high-confidence STRING network with CBS/AGXT/CTH indicates SDSL operates within integrated transsulfuration and one-carbon metabolic hub coordinating sulfur amino acid metabolism with methyl group donation; paralogous relationship with SDS suggests potential subfunctionalization or tissue-specific isoform expression; conservation from mouse (Q8R238, 329 aa) to Xenopus (Q6PAE9, 325 aa) confirms essential metabolic function across vertebrates; cytosolic localization enables access to serine/threonine pools from protein turnover and dietary sources.
