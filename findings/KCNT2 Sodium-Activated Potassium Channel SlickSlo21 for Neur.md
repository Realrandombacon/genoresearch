# KCNT2: Sodium-Activated Potassium Channel Slick/Slo2.1 for Neuronal Excitability Control in Developmental Epileptic Encephalopathy via RCK Domain Gating Mechanism

## Description
KCNT2 encodes a 1135 aa sodium-activated potassium channel subfamily T member 2 (UniProt Q6UVM3), also known as Slick or Slo2.1, functioning as a voltage-gated potassium channel activated by intracellular sodium and chloride ions to regulate neuronal excitability, action potential repolarization, and neurotransmitter release. The protein contains canonical potassium channel architecture: N-terminal RCK (Regulator of Conductance of K+) domains (IPR003148/PF22614, aa298-435 and aa717-858) forming cytoplasmic gating rings that sense intracellular Na+/Cl−, transmembrane S1-S6 pore-forming domain (IPR013099/PF07885, aa210-275), BK channel alpha subunit domain (IPR003929/PF03493, aa432-526) for voltage sensing, and slowpoke-like RCK domains (G3DSA:3.40.50.720) at both N- and C-termini. AlphaFold prediction shows high confidence (pLDDT 76.1), indicating a well-folded tetrameric channel structure. HPA shows tissue-enhanced expression with ovary (3.5 nTPM) as top tissue, vesicular subcellular localization consistent with ion channel trafficking, and cancer-enhanced specificity. STRING interactions reveal KCNT2 associates with other potassium channels: SCOC (0.680, short coiled-coil protein), KCNN1/2/4 (0.473-0.531, calcium-activated SK channels), KCNA2/4 (0.464-0.497, voltage-gated Kv channels), KCNV1 (0.450, modulatory subunit), TTC12 (0.492, tetratricopeptide protein), CSN3 (0.455, COP9 signalosome), and SLC35G1 (0.436, solute carrier). ClinVar contains 120 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including frameshift (p.Leu415fs), splice site (c.1185+1G>C), missense mutations across the protein (p.Gly915Val, p.Tyr1044Cys, p.Ala147Ser, p.His324Gln, p.Asp1075Gly), nonsense (p.Lys946Ter), and 1q25.3-32.1/1q21.1-44 copy number gains. Literature (71 papers) demonstrates KCNT2 mutations cause developmental and epileptic encephalopathy 57 (DEE57/EIEE57) featuring early-onset seizures, intellectual disability, and behavioral phenotypes; KCNT2 gain-of-function variants increase channel open probability causing neuronal hyperexcitability; KCNT2 regulates action potential duration in cortical neurons and cardiac myocytes; quinidine and bepridil show partial efficacy as KCNT2 blockers in seizure models; KCNT2 modulates dopamine release in striatum affecting motor control; and CFTR modulators (elexacaftor, vanzacaftor) unexpectedly inhibit KCNT2/KCNT1 channels. KCNT2 functions as a homeostatic brake on neuronal firing—elevated intracellular Na+ during high-frequency firing activates KCNT2, generating K+ efflux that hyperpolarizes the membrane and limits further action potentials. DEE57 mutations disrupt this negative feedback: gain-of-function variants cause excessive hyperpolarization impairing synaptic transmission (intellectual disability), while loss-of-function variants permit uncontrolled firing (epilepsy). The dual RCK domain architecture enables cooperative Na+/Cl− sensing, positioning KCNT2 as a metabolic sensor coupling ionic homeostasis to excitability control.
