## Description
NEMF (Nuclear Export Mediator Factor, UniProt O60524) is a 1076 aa scaffold protein that serves as a key component of the ribosome quality control (RQC) complex, mediating recognition and ubiquitination of stalled 60S ribosomal subunits through collaboration with the E3 ubiquitin ligase Listerin (LTN1). Structurally, AlphaFold predicts a partially ordered architecture (pLDDT=69.7) featuring an N-terminal fibrinogen-binding protein-like domain (G3DSA:2.30.310.10, aa 4-155), a central NFACT RNA-binding domain (IPR008532/PF05670, aa 529-639), and a C-terminal NFACT domain (IPR021846/PF11923, aa 975-1066), characteristic of the NEMF family (IPR051608/PTHR15239, aa 1-1064). Functionally, STRING interactions reveal tight coupling to the RQC machinery with LTN1 (0.999), TCF25 (0.954), and multiple large ribosomal subunit proteins including RPL11 (0.948), RPL5 (0.914), RPL23 (0.909), RPL23A (0.899), RPL9 (0.895), RPL13A (0.892), RPLP0 (0.890), and RPL12 (0.890), positioning NEMF at the heart of translational surveillance. HPA shows ubiquitous tissue distribution with nucleoplasmic, nuclear body, and cytosolic localization, classified as a plasma protein with disease involvement in intellectual disability and neuropathy. ClinVar identifies 53 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including frameshift deletions (p.Thr1016fs, p.Asn642fs), nonsense mutations (p.Arg327Ter), and missense variants (p.Glu801Gly, p.Pro830Ser, p.Glu537Gln, p.Arg934Ser, p.Thr1007Ala), though no specific Mendelian disease is annotated. Literature (149 papers) reveals emerging mechanistic insights: NEMF-mediated CAT-tailing (C-terminal alanine and threonine addition) facilitates translocation-associated quality control at the ER translocon (2025), enables Listerin-independent mitochondrial translational surveillance via E3 ligase Pirh2 and protease ClpXP (2024), and mutations illustrate Importin-β-specific nuclear transport defects. The gene represents a 'semi-dark' quality control factor where canonical RQC function is established, but specific mechanisms linking CAT-tailing chemistry to neurodevelopmental phenotypes, mitochondrial proteostasis, and tissue-specific vulnerability in intellectual disability remain underexplored therapeutic targets.
