## Description
PCYOX1 (Prenylcysteine Oxidase 1) is a 505 amino acid FAD-dependent oxidoreductase enzyme that functions as a critical regulator of prenylated protein catabolism, lipid metabolism, and vascular homeostasis through prenylcysteine lyase-mediated oxidative cleavage of CAAX-box prenylated proteins and interaction with the apolipoprotein-lipoprotein network for thrombosis modulation and exosomal signaling in vascular and blood cells. InterPro analysis identifies characteristic prenylcysteine oxidase architecture including Prenylcysteine oxidase 1 family (IPR017046, PIRSF036292, residues 5-504) defining evolutionary relationship to prenylated protein degradative enzymes with prenylcysteine oxidase activity (GO:0001735) and oxidoreductase function (GO:0016491), Prenylcysteine lyase domain (IPR010795, PF07156, residues 128-494) forming the catalytic core for oxidoreductase activity acting on sulfur group donors with oxygen as acceptor (GO:0016670) and prenylcysteine catabolic process (GO:0030328), FAD/NAD(P)-binding domain superfamily (IPR036188, G3DSA:3.50.50.60, SSF51905, residues 33-349/36-333) providing nucleotide cofactor binding for redox catalysis, NAD(P)-binding Rossmann-like domain (PF13450, residues 39-106) for dinucleotide coordination, and Farnesylcysteine lyase Panther family (PTHR15944, residues 17-500) establishing orthology across vertebrates. AlphaFold structure (AF-Q9UHG3-F1) predicts very high confidence (pLDDT 91.3) across the 505 residue modeled sequence, indicating a well-folded stable enzyme with ordered catalytic domains suitable for FAD-dependent oxidoreductase activity and prenylated substrate engagement. HPA data classifies PCYOX1 as an enzyme, metabolic protein, and plasma protein with predicted intracellular localization, ubiquitous expression (detected in all tissues), low tissue specificity, low cancer specificity, low immune cell specificity, and cell type-enhanced distribution consistent with regulated metabolic enzyme function in vascular and blood compartments. ClinVar lists 8 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including splice site mutation (c.494+1G>T), large copy number losses on 2p25.1-q13 (chr2:11504318-111365996)x1, copy number gains on 2p25.3-q37.3 (chr2:1-243199373, chr2:12771-242783384x3), 2p16.1-11.2 duplication (chr2:58279519-83586962)x3, and uncharacterized deletions/duplications, though no specific monogenic syndrome is defined, suggesting roles in developmental disorders and metabolic disease at the 2p locus.
