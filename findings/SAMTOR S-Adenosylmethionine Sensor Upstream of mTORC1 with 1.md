## Description
SAMTOR encodes a 405 amino acid S-adenosylmethionine (SAM) sensor upstream of mTORC1 (UniProt Q1RMZ1), also known as base methyltransferase of 25S rRNA 2 homolog, that functions as the primary cellular sensor linking methionine availability to mTORC1 signaling and cell growth control. The protein contains a canonical S-adenosyl-L-methionine-dependent methyltransferase fold (IPR029063/G3DSA:3.40.50.150 at positions 144-348; SSF53335 at 156-312) and belongs to the Bmt2-like methyltransferase family (IPR021867/PF11968 at positions 226-320; MF_03044 at 30-351). Despite structural homology to rRNA methyltransferases, SAMTOR has evolved a specialized regulatory role: it binds SAM directly, and upon SAM binding, inhibits the GATOR1 complex to activate mTORC1, thereby coupling cellular methionine status to anabolic processes.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals SAMTOR forms a tight regulatory network with the mTORC1 nutrient-sensing machinery: C12orf66/KICSTOR (score 0.987—scaffold for GATOR1), SZT2 (0.968—GATOR1 regulator), DEPDC5 (0.966—GATOR1 catalytic subunit), KPTN (0.932—GATOR1 component), NPRL3 (0.932), NPRL2 (0.776), RRAGA (0.821—Rag GTPase), CASTOR1 (0.763—arginine sensor), ITFG2 (0.704), and SESN2 (0.687—leucine sensor). This positions SAMTOR as a central node in the lysosomal nutrient-sensing supercomplex, coordinating methionine signals with arginine (CASTOR1) and leucine (SESN2) sensing.\n\nHPA expression data classifies SAMTOR as detected in all tissues with low tissue specificity and cell type-enhanced expression patterns. Subcellular localization is cytosolic, consistent with its role in sensing cytosolic SAM pools and translocating to lysosomes upon nutrient stimulation. Molecular functions include methyltransferase and transferase activity.\n\nClinVar documents 19 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number gains (7q22.3-32.1 duplications spanning 22 Mb), copy number losses (7q22.1-31.31 deletions), splice site variants (c.302+186G>T, c.474-31G>T), and small deletions (c.474-10_474-9del). Critically, ClinVar lists 0 associated diseases—a major knowledge gap given SAMTOR's essential role in growth regulation. This suggests SAMTOR dysfunction may cause undiagnosed metabolic disorders affecting growth, neurodevelopment (methionine cycle is critical for brain), or cancer predisposition (mTORC1 hyperactivation).\n\nAlphaFold structure prediction (AF-Q1RMZ1-F1) yields a global pLDDT score of 79.2 (high confidence) across all 405 residues, indicating a well-folded methyltransferase domain capable of SAM binding. The 2026 cryo-EM structure of the KICSTOR-GATOR1-SAMTOR supercomplex validates this prediction and reveals the precise mechanism of SAM-dependent inhibition.\n\nLiterature analysis (22 papers) shows rapid recent progress: landmark 2017 discovery as SAM sensor, 2022 mechanistic elucidation of SAM binding, 2023 identification of PRMT1-mediated arginine methylation regulating SAMTOR-NPRL2 interaction, 2025 discovery of AMPK-SAMTOR axis in prostate cancer metabolic vulnerability, and 2026 supercomplex structure. Despite this, no Mendelian disease phenotype is defined. Key unknowns include: specific disease phenotypes from loss-of-function mutations, contribution to methionine-restricted diet responses, role in aging (methionine restriction extends lifespan), and therapeutic potential for mTOR-driven cancers or metabolic syndromes.
