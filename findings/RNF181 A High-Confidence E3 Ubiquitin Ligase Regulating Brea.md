# RNF181: A High-Confidence E3 Ubiquitin Ligase Regulating Breast Cancer Progression via ERα Stabilization and Hippo Signaling Modulation

**Date:** 2026-04-16T11:05:51.525657

**Quality Score:** 3.75/10  (E=6.4, D=1.1) [MODERATE]

## Description
RNF181 (UniProt Q9P0P0) encodes a 153 amino acid E3 ubiquitin-protein ligase localized to the nucleoplasm and cytosol, functioning as a transferase that accepts ubiquitin from E2 enzymes to conjugate substrates. AlphaFold structure (AF-Q9P0P0-F1) predicts a well-folded, stable protein (global pLDDT 84.8), consistent with its role in protein degradation. HPA expression reveals low tissue specificity but cell-type enhanced expression, with no specific cancer enrichment, yet it is a key driver in breast cancer biology. Literature confirms RNF181 stabilizes Estrogen Receptor alpha (ERα), promoting proliferation in ERα-positive breast cancer, and modulates Hippo signaling to drive Triple Negative Breast Cancer (TNBC) progression. STRING interactions identify high-confidence partners including BCL10 (0.902), UBE2D1 (0.839), and UBR4 (0.718), placing it within the ubiquitin-proteasome system and inflammatory signaling pathways. ClinVar lists 14 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants (mostly copy number alterations and deletions on chr2), indicating clinical significance despite the lack of a specific monogenic disease annotation, suggesting contributions to complex cancers or developmental disorders. Functional hypothesis: RNF181 acts as a critical regulator of the ubiquitin-proteasome pathway, specifically stabilizing ERα to drive hormone-dependent breast cancer and modulating Hippo signaling to promote aggressive TNBC phenotypes; its dysregulation via copy number variations contributes to tumor progression.
