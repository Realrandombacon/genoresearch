## Description
LOC126861786 is an uncharacterized cis-regulatory element (CRE) at chromosome 13q13.1 (genomic coordinates 61557307-61558506, ~1.2kb span). This locus is NOT a protein-coding gene but a functional regulatory DNA element validated through STARR-seq massively parallel reporter assays (MPRAs). The gene_info summary identifies this as a MED14-INDEPENDENT GROUP 3 ENHANCER that was experimentally validated in HCT116 colorectal carcinoma cells. Critically, this enhancer depends on BRD2, BRD4, P300/CBP, and CDK7 cofactors but shows LIMITED OR NO DEPENDENCE on the MED14 core Mediator complex subunit. This cofactor profile distinguishes it from canonical Mediator-dependent enhancers and suggests it operates through BET-dependent chromatin remodeling mechanisms independent of core Mediator recruitment. ClinVar contains 30 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants affecting this locus through large-scale chromosomal rearrangements. STRIKINGLY, this locus shows BIDIRECTIONAL pathogenicity: multiple copy number GAINS (chr13:31363472-90575292x3, chr13:19837395-114327173x3, chr13:18862146-114342258x3, chr13:19671934-114340331x3, chr13:18456040-114340285x3, chr13:19833130-114327106x3, chr13:32531486-86757044x3, and additional amplifications) AND copy number LOSSES (chr13:47765202-62058520x1, chr13:37864226-67963788x1, and a large deletion NC_000013.11:g.46968080_87381985del40413906). This bidirectional pattern indicates strict dosage requirements — both enhancer overdosage and haploinsufficiency cause disease. The 13q region contains the RB1 retinoblastoma tumor suppressor gene (13q14.2), FLT3 receptor tyrosine kinase (13q12.2), and numerous genes involved in cell cycle control and development. Deletions of 13q cause retinoblastoma predisposition and developmental delay; duplications cause 13q duplication syndrome. No UniProt accession exists (non-coding DNA), STRING found no interactions (not a protein), HPA could not resolve (not protein-coding), and PubMed has zero publications for this specific locus. This represents a genuine dark regulatory element with strong oncogenic relevance — the BRD2/BRD4/P300/CBP/CDK7-dependent enhancer activity in HCT116 cells combined with bidirectional pathogenicity suggests LOC126861786 functions as a dosage-critical enhancer controlling 13q tumor suppressor or oncogene expression (potentially RB1, FLT3, or neighboring genes), where both amplification (oncogenic overexpression) and deletion (tumor suppressor haploinsufficiency) drive cancer and developmental pathology.
