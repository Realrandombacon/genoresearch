## Description
SYNPR (Synaptoporin) is a 265-amino acid integral membrane protein with moderate characterization (44 publications) that functions as a synaptic vesicle component in the brain, with significant gaps in understanding its specific physiological role versus the related synaptophysin (SYP). The protein contains a canonical MARVEL domain (IPR008253, PS51225, residues 1-202) within the synaptophysin/synaptoporin family (IPR001285, PTHR10306, residues 2-221), featuring four transmembrane helices characteristic of the synaptophysin superfamily. The MARVEL domain (Mal, T-cell lymphoma, and Vesicle transport) mediates membrane apposition events critical for vesicle trafficking and fusion. AlphaFold predicts a well-folded structure (global pLDDT 76.1) consistent with stable transmembrane bundle architecture.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals a synaptic vesicle network centered on neurotransmitter release machinery: SYNGR2 (synaptogyrin-2, score 0.744), VAMP2 (vesicle-associated membrane protein 2, 0.624), SYT1 (synaptotagmin-1, 0.622), LNPK (lipin-1, 0.593), SLC17A7 (vesicular glutamate transporter 1, 0.587), SYNGR1 (0.577), SV2B (synaptic vesicle glycoprotein 2B, 0.522), SNAP25 (0.520), RAB3A (0.475), and SYN1 (synapsin-1, 0.415). These partners span the synaptic vesicle cycle: VAMP2 and SNAP25 form the SNARE complex for membrane fusion, SYT1 is the calcium sensor triggering exocytosis, SLC17A7 loads glutamate into vesicles, and RAB3A/SYN1 regulate vesicle docking and mobilization.\n\nHuman Protein Atlas shows brain-enriched expression (262.1 nTPM) with 'tissue enriched' specificity and 'detected in some' distribution, localized to vesicles, nucleoplasm, and cytosol. This confirms SYNPR as a neuron-specific synaptic protein, though its expression pattern is more restricted than ubiquitous synaptophysin. ClinVar reports 14 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number losses and gains spanning chromosome 3p14.2-14.1 (the gene's locus), though no specific Mendelian disease is yet assigned. The 3p deletions are associated with intellectual disability syndromes, suggesting SYNPR haploinsufficiency may contribute to neurodevelopmental phenotypes.\n\nWe hypothesize that SYNPR functions as a specialized synaptic vesicle protein that regulates vesicle trafficking, neurotransmitter loading, or calcium-dependent exocytosis in specific neuronal subpopulations, potentially modulating synaptic plasticity or vesicle pool dynamics. The MARVEL domain suggests a role in membrane apposition during vesicle docking or fusion pore formation. Unlike ubiquitous synaptophysin, SYNPR's restricted brain expression implies specialized functions in particular neural circuits. Chromosome 3p CNVs disrupting SYNPR may contribute to intellectual disability, autism spectrum disorders, or epilepsy through impaired synaptic transmission, explaining the pathogenic variant burden without assigned monogenic disease.
