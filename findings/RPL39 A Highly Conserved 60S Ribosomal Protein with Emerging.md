## Description
RPL39 (Ribosomal Protein L39, UniProt P62891) is a 51 aa component of the large 60S ribosomal subunit that functions as an RNA-binding structural constituent essential for translation. Despite its small size, AlphaFold predicts a very high confidence structure (pLDDT=94.0), indicating a stable, well-folded domain. The protein contains the conserved eL39 family domain (IPR000077, PF00832, aa 1-51) with a signature motif (PS00051, aa 30-46) critical for ribosomal assembly. STRING interactions reveal exclusive high-confidence connections (0.998-0.999) to other ribosomal proteins including RPL18A, RPL19, RPL38, RPL8, RPL21, and small subunit proteins RPS11, RPS12, RPS15A, RPS21, RPS23, confirming its role as an integral ribosomal hub. HPA shows ubiquitous expression across all tissues with low specificity, consistent with housekeeping ribosomal function. ClinVar identifies 163 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants (mostly copy number losses/gains on chromosome Xq24), though no specific disease is annotated, suggesting haploinsufficiency may contribute to X-linked developmental phenotypes. Literature (79 papers) reveals emerging non-canonical roles: RPL39 and its paralog RPL39L regulate cell proliferation via protein synthesis and mitochondrial activity, are implicated in metaplastic breast cancer aggressiveness, and show sex-differential expression in pulmonary arterial hypertension. The gene represents a 'semi-dark' ribosomal protein where canonical translation function is established, but specific regulatory roles in cancer metabolism, sex-biased disease susceptibility, and potential extraribosomal functions remain underexplored.
