# TRIM50: E3 Ubiquitin-Protein Ligase with Canonical TRIM Domain Architecture and 7q11.23 Genomic Location

## Description
TRIM50 (Q86XT4, 487 aa) is a tripartite motif-containing E3 ubiquitin-protein ligase with complete domain architecture: RING finger domain (IPR001841, aa 16-57), B-box type-2 zinc finger (IPR000315, aa 84-125), and C-terminal SPRY/B30.2 domain (IPR001870, aa 276-475). AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (pLDDT 86.2) consistent with stable folding across all domains. HPA shows cancer-enriched expression with highest levels in stomach (46.0 nTPM), parathyroid gland (31.1 nTPM), and pancreas (28.0 nTPM), localized to cytosol. STRING reveals 10 high-confidence interactions including HDAC6 (0.793), BBOX1 (0.762), GTF2IRD1 (0.744), TBL2 (0.738), and SNAI1 (0.725) - all located in the 7q11.23 Williams-Beuren syndrome critical region. ClinVar contains 136 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly copy number variations (deletions and duplications) affecting the 7q11.23 locus, strongly implicating TRIM50 in Williams-Beuren syndrome and related neurodevelopmental disorders. Functional hypothesis: TRIM50 functions as an E3 ubiquitin ligase that regulates protein stability and localization through ubiquitination of substrates involved in chromatin remodeling (HDAC6 interaction) and transcriptional regulation (GTF2IRD1, TBL2). The strong interaction with BBOX1 (another TRIM protein) suggests homo/hetero-oligomerization typical of TRIM family members. The SPRY domain likely mediates substrate recognition, while the RING domain catalyzes ubiquitin transfer. Disruption of TRIM50 via 7q11.23 CNVs may contribute to Williams-Beuren syndrome phenotypes through dysregulated ubiquitin-mediated protein turnover affecting neural development and cytoskeletal dynamics.
