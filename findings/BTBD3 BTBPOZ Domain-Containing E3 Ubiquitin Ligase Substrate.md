## Description
BTBD3 encodes BTB/POZ domain-containing protein 3 (UniProt Q9Y2F9), a 522 aa substrate adaptor protein for CUL3-based E3 ubiquitin ligase complexes. InterPro analysis reveals a modular domain architecture: N-terminal BTB/POZ domain (IPR000210, PF00651, aa 113-219) mediating CUL3 binding and dimerization, central BACK domain (IPR011705, PF07707, aa 226-335) characteristic of BTB-Kelch proteins, and C-terminal PHR domain (IPR012983, PF08005, aa 376-521) potentially involved in substrate recognition. AlphaFold predicts high-confidence structure (pLDDT 83.5), indicating a well-folded protein with stable tertiary structure suitable for protein-protein interactions. STRING interactions reveal connections to ubiquitination machinery and neuronal proteins: TRIM2 (0.545, E3 ubiquitin ligase), CUL3 (0.491, cullin 3 scaffold), DLGAP1 (0.500, postsynaptic density protein), BTBD6 (0.449, paralog), KIF16B (0.443, kinesin motor), SYT1 (0.432, synaptotagmin 1), NAA25 (0.421, N-alpha-acetyltransferase), and DOC2A (0.419, vesicle trafficking), suggesting dual roles in ubiquitin-mediated proteolysis and neuronal vesicle dynamics. HPA shows brain-enriched expression (65.8 nTPM), vesicular localization, and neurogenesis annotation, consistent with neuronal function. ClinVar reports 17 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number losses/gains at 20p12.3-12.1 and SNVs (e.g., c.357G>T p.Met119Ile), though specific disease phenotypes remain undefined. Literature analysis (42 papers) reveals emerging roles in: (1) Colorectal cancer suppression via TYRO3/Wnt/β-catenin signaling axis inhibition (2024); (2) Hippocampal neurite outgrowth regulation through NAA25-mediated N-α-acetylation of BTBD3 affecting actin dynamics (2024); (3) Compulsive-like and exploratory behavior regulation - BTBD3 identified as OCD risk gene in GWAS, with knockout mice showing altered behaviors (2019); (4) ADHD associations in pediatric populations (2018). Functional hypothesis: BTBD3 functions through: (1) CUL3 E3 Ligase Complex - BTB/POZ domain binds CUL3 while PHR domain recruits specific substrates (e.g., TYRO3 receptor kinase) for ubiquitination and proteasomal degradation; (2) Wnt/β-Catenin Regulation - targets TYRO3 for degradation, preventing downstream Wnt pathway activation and β-catenin stabilization, thereby suppressing colorectal cancer tumorigenesis; (3) Neuronal Development - NAA25-mediated N-α-acetylation modulates BTBD3 conformation and substrate specificity, regulating actin cytoskeleton dynamics during neurite extension; (4) Synaptic Vesicle Trafficking - interactions with SYT1, DOC2A, and KIF16B suggest roles in calcium-dependent vesicle fusion and retrograde transport at synapses; (5) Behavioral Regulation - BTBD3 dosage sensitivity (ClinVar CNVs) affects neuronal circuit development in brain regions controlling compulsive behaviors and attention. Disease mechanism: 20p12.3 CNVs disrupt BTBD3 dosage, leading to aberrant TYRO3/Wnt signaling (cancer risk), impaired neurite outgrowth (neurodevelopmental disorders), or altered synaptic vesicle dynamics (OCD/ADHD). The high-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 83.5) and moderate literature coverage (42 papers) suggest BTBD3 is transitioning from 'dark' to 'characterized' but mechanistic details of substrate recognition and tissue-specific functions remain unclear.
