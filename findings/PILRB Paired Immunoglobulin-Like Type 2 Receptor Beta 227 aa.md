## Description
PILRB (Paired Immunoglobulin-Like Type 2 Receptor Beta, UniProt Q9UKJ0) is a 227 amino acid type I transmembrane receptor with limited characterization (47 papers) that functions as an inhibitory receptor on myeloid cells, balancing inflammatory responses through DAP12-independent signaling and recently identified as a driver of gastric tumorigenesis via PI3K/AKT activation and cholesterol metabolism reprogramming. The protein contains a single N-terminal Immunoglobulin-like V-type domain (IPR007110/IPR003599/IPR036179/G3DSA:2.60.40.10/SSF48726 residues 8-143/31-150/35-149) for ligand binding, belonging to the Immunoregulatory Receptor-like family (IPR051694/PTHR15549 residues 21-219) related to the Siglec family, with no cytoplasmic ITIM motifs suggesting reliance on associated adaptors or alternative signaling mechanisms. STRING interactions reveal high-confidence partnerships with phosphatases and immune regulators: PTPN11 (0.941, SHP-2 phosphatase—positive regulator of RAS/MAPK), CD99L2 (0.936, CD99-like protein 2), CD99 (0.924), LAIR1 (0.767, leukocyte-associated immunoglobulin-like receptor 1—inhibitory collagen receptor), ZCWPW1 (0.608), PILRA (0.577, paired receptor alpha—activating counterpart), CD300C (0.575), GYPC (0.528), LILRB1 (0.501, leukocyte immunoglobulin-like receptor B1), and KLRG1 (0.476), confirming integration into the myeloid inhibitory receptor network. HPA data shows low tissue specificity but immune cell-enhanced expression, detected in all tissues, low cancer specificity, cell type-enhanced single cell specificity, predicted mitochondrial and membrane localization, classification as predicted intracellular/membrane proteins and receptors. ClinVar contains 8 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large deletions at 7q22.1/7p22.3-q36.3 (NC_000007.14:g.99487704_102394107del, chr7:98454022-100723798x1, chr7:99195836-102258175x1, chr7:54185-159282390x1, chr7:99219420-100902269x1) and copy number gains at 7q21.3-36.3/21.3-22.1 (chr7:97419852-158923762x3, chr7:98288474-101259804x3), though no specific diseases are directly assigned despite associations with gastric cancer and inflammation. AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 80.2) indicates high confidence with a well-folded Ig-like domain typical of immune receptors. Functional hypothesis: PILRB acts as a myeloid checkpoint receptor where recent research demonstrates: (1) PILRB potentiates PI3K/AKT signaling and reprograms cholesterol metabolism to drive gastric tumorigenesis and metastasis (2024, 10 citations)—direct oncogenic role in gastric cancer, (2) PILRB regulates balance of inflammatory responses through IL-27 production control (2012, 26 citations)—anti-inflammatory function in innate immunity, (3) PILRB binds sialylated ligands similar to Siglecs to transmit inhibitory signals, (4) STRING partner PTPN11/SHP-2 (0.941) suggests recruitment of this phosphatase to dephosphorylate inhibitory motifs or activate RAS/MAPK pathways depending on context, (5) Pairing with PILRA (0.577) creates a balanced activating/inhibitory system analogous to KIR or LILR pairs, (6) Immune cell-enhanced expression confirms restriction to myeloid lineage (monocytes, macrophages, dendritic cells), (7) Mitochondrial prediction by HPA may reflect alternative localization or artifact, but primary function is plasma membrane signaling, (8) 8 ClinVar CNVs at 7q22 indicate potential haploinsufficiency or dosage sensitivity affecting immune regulation, (9) Mechanistic proposal: PILRB engages sialylated glycan ligands on pathogens or host cells via its Ig-like domain, recruiting SHP-2 (PTPN11) or other phosphatases to its cytoplasmic tail (potentially via short motifs or adaptors), resulting in: (a) dephosphorylation of activating signaling components to dampen inflammation (IL-27 regulation), (b) in gastric cancer contexts, aberrant PILRB overexpression hijacks PI3K/AKT signaling to promote survival and alters cholesterol metabolism to support membrane synthesis for metastasis, (c) balanced interaction with activating PILRA fine-tunes myeloid activation thresholds, with PILRB dysfunction contributing to chronic inflammation, autoimmunity, or cancer progression, making PILRB a therapeutic target for gastric cancer (blocking PI3K/AKT hyperactivation) and inflammatory diseases.
