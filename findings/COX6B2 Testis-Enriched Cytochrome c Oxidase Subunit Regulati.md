## Description

Functional hypothesis: COX6B2 functions as (a) testis-specific Complex IV subunit optimizing mitochondrial respiration for sperm flagellar energy production and motility, and (b) metabolic regulator enabling cancer cell adaptation to therapeutic stress through OXPHOS enhancement and p53 pathway modulation. Mechanistic proposal: COX6B2 integrates into Complex IV holoenzyme via interactions with COX5A/COX5B catalytic core and COX6A/COX6C assembly factors, positioning CHCH domain to stabilize supercomplex formation with Complex III (UQCRB/UQCRQ/UQCRC2) for efficient electron transfer and proton pumping— in sperm, testis-enriched COX6B2 expression ensures high OXPHOS capacity in mitochondrial sheath surrounding flagellar axoneme, providing ATP for dynein motor activity and progressive motility (loss causes subfertility); in cancer cells, COX6B2 upregulation (driven by IGF2BP3 m6A modification or transcriptional activation) shifts metabolism from glycolysis to OXPHOS, enabling survival under therapeutic stress (EGFR-TKIs) and supporting metastatic colonization (PDAC) through enhanced mitochondrial ATP production and ROS signaling that inhibits p53-mediated cell cycle arrest— in trophoblasts, COX6B2 maintains stemness by favoring OXPHOS metabolism characteristic of pluripotent states— copy number variants at 19q13.33-13.43 cause haploinsufficiency or overexpression disrupting metabolic homeostasis, positioning COX6B2 as therapeutic target for male infertility (COX6B2 supplementation), cancer metabolism (OXPHOS inhibitors), and placental disorders (preeclampsia, intrauterine growth restriction).
