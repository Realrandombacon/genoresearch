## Description
GREB1L (GREB1-like retinoic acid receptor coactivator, also known as C18orf6, KIAA1772, RHDA3, or DFNA80) is a massive 1923 amino acid intracellular protein localized to mitochondria (UniProt Q9C091) with high-confidence well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 71.9). The protein defines the GREB1-related family (IPR028422/PTHR15720, residues 1-1923) and contains a remarkable multi-domain architecture: GREB1 N-terminal domain (IPR046926/PF15782, residues 87-236, ~150 aa), GREB1-like second domain (IPR048659/PF20688, residues 319-613, ~295 aa), circularly permuted SF2 helicase domain (IPR048657/PF20692, residues 668-1352, ~685 aa), TET-Associated Glycosyltransferase domain (IPR049100/PF20691, residues 1518-1745, ~228 aa), and GREB1 C-terminal region (IPR046927/PF20267, residues 1763-1923, ~161 aa). This ~1519 aa of structured domains (~79% of protein) suggests GREB1L is a multifunctional enzyme/scaffold. Molecular functions include retinoic acid receptor coactivator activity and developmental protein function, placing GREB1L within embryonic patterning and organogenesis pathways.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals a network linking RNA processing, transcriptional regulation, and cytoskeletal dynamics: strongest interaction with CLP1 (0.538, Cleavage and polyadenylation factor I subunit, essential for tRNA splicing and mRNA 3' end processing), indicating involvement in RNA maturation. High confidence links include TSEN2 (0.521, tRNA splicing endonuclease subunit 2, part of the tRNA splicing complex), VGLL3 (0.504, Vestigial-like protein 3, a transcriptional coactivator in Hippo/YAP signaling), and ROCK1 (0.492, Rho-associated coiled-coil containing protein kinase 1, regulating actin cytoskeleton and cell migration). Connections to TMEM241 (0.464, Transmembrane protein 241), CAAP1 (0.459, Caspase activity and apoptosis inhibitor 1), TULP4 (0.422, Tubby-like protein 4), and DZIP1L (0.414, DAZ interacting protein 1-like, a ciliary gate component) suggest roles in apoptosis regulation, ciliogenesis, and membrane trafficking.\n\nHPA expression reveals a tissue-enhanced pattern with highest levels in skeletal muscle (8.3 nTPM), consistent with roles in muscle development or metabolism. Mitochondrial localization suggests involvement in energy metabolism or mitochondrial RNA processing. The 'cancer enhanced' status implies dysregulation in tumors. Disease involvement includes non-syndromic deafness (DFNA80) and renal agenesis (RHDA3), confirming critical roles in otic and kidney development.\n\nClinVar documents 173 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including missense mutations (p.Arg1057Trp, p.Gln484Arg, p.Glu544Ala, p.Gly1848Arg, p.Tyr1063Phe), nonsense mutations (p.Tyr1448Ter, p.Gln1670Ter, p.Arg241Ter), and large CNVs (18q11.1-23 duplications). The high variant burden indicates strong dosage and functional sensitivity. Heterozygous loss-of-function mutations cause autosomal dominant nonsyndromic deafness 80 (DFNA80) and renal hypodysplasia/agenesis 3 (RHDA3). Haploinsufficiency disrupts retinoic acid signaling during embryogenesis, leading to defective kidney morphogenesis (agenesis/hypodysplasia) and inner ear development (cochlear/vestibular malformations). Missense mutations in the helicase or glycosyltransferase domains likely impair enzymatic activity or protein interactions.\n\nConservation is strong across vertebrates: human (Q9C091, 1923 aa), mouse (B9EJV3, 1913 aa), zebrafish (B8JKP6, 1942 aa), rat (F1LTR0, 1955 aa), cow (E1BHK3, 1965 aa), indicating ancient evolutionary origin and conserved mechanism.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: GREB1L functions as a multifunctional coactivator integrating retinoic acid signaling with RNA processing and cytoskeletal remodeling during organogenesis. The SF2 helicase domain likely unwinds RNA/DNA substrates for transcriptional activation or RNA splicing (via CLP1/TSEN2 interaction). The glycosyltransferase domain may modify proteins or lipids involved in signaling cascades. Mitochondrial localization suggests a role in mitochondrial RNA processing or metabolic regulation. During kidney development, GREB1L coactivates retinoic acid receptors to drive nephrogenesis programs; loss causes renal agenesis. In the inner ear, GREB1L regulates hair cell differentiation and cochlear morphogenesis; mutations cause deafness. Interaction with VGLL3 links GREB1L to Hippo/YAP signaling for organ size control. ROCK1 interaction suggests cytoskeletal remodeling during tubulogenesis. The multi-domain architecture enables GREB1L to coordinate transcriptional, post-transcriptional, and structural processes essential for complex organ formation.
