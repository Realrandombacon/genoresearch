## Description
LLCFC1 (LLLL and CFNLAS motif-containing protein 1), also known as SOFU1 (Sperm-oocyte fusion factor 1) or SOF1, is a 122 amino acid secreted/extracellular protein (UniProt Q96L11) with medium-confidence partially structured architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 60.8) containing the canonical sperm-egg fusion protein LLCFC1 family domain (IPR031684/PF15838/PTHR37348, residues 6-102) forming a ~97 aa core module (~79%) that defines the protein's function in fertilization. The protein features the distinctive LLLL and CFNLAS motifs within this domain, which likely mediate protein-protein interactions during gamete fusion. Molecular functions include fusion of sperm to egg plasma membrane involved in single fertilization (GO:0007342), consistent with its annotation as a fertilization factor. HPA expression reveals a tissue-enriched pattern with exceptional specificity to testis (61.0 nTPM), detected in some tissues, with no detection in cancer or immune cells, confirming its role as a reproductive-specific factor. Subcellular location predictions indicate both intracellular and secreted protein classification, suggesting it may be processed and released during sperm maturation or capacitation.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places LLCFC1 within a focused fertilization machinery network: strongest interaction with TMEM95 (0.638, Transmembrane protein 95, essential for sperm-egg fusion), directly linking it to the core fusion apparatus. Moderate confidence links include SPACA6 (0.541, Sperm acrosome associated 6, a sperm surface protein), IZUMO1 (0.466, the canonical sperm-egg fusion protein), and SETBP1 (0.512, SET binding protein 1). The connection to IZUMO1 is particularly significant, as IZUMO1 is the well-established sperm ligand that binds JUNO on the oocyte surface. LLCFC1 may act as a co-factor or regulator of the IZUMO1-JUNO interaction, or function in a parallel fusion pathway. ZBTB48 (0.412) and NBEAL1 (0.400) connections suggest potential roles in transcriptional regulation or vesicle trafficking during spermatogenesis.\n\nClinVar documents 45 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains and losses affecting the 7q34-36.3 region: CNV losses (7q33-36.3, 7q34-36.1, 7q33-36.1 deletions) and CNV gains (7q34-36.3, 7q33-36.3 duplications). The high variant burden indicates strong dosage sensitivity. Haploinsufficiency would likely disrupt sperm-egg fusion, causing male infertility due to fertilization failure, while duplications may cause aberrant fusion events or sequestration of binding partners, potentially contributing to 7q deletion/duplication syndrome phenotypes (developmental delay, growth abnormalities, and reproductive defects).\n\nConservation is observed across mammals: human (Q96L11, 122 aa), mouse (Q9D9P8, 108 aa), fox (A0A3Q7R7G5, 106 aa), ferret (A0A8U0NHQ9, 110 aa), indicating evolutionary pressure to maintain the LLLL/CFNLAS motif architecture despite the small protein size.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: LLCFC1/SOFU1 acts as a sperm surface or acrosomal protein that facilitates the final fusion step between sperm and oocyte membranes. The LLLL and CFNLAS motifs may form a hydrophobic interaction interface that binds to complementary receptors on the oocyte surface or stabilizes the IZUMO1-JUNO complex. Its medium-confidence structure (pLDDT 60.8) suggests flexibility that allows adaptation to membrane curvature during fusion. In testis, it is highly expressed (61.0 nTPM) during spermatogenesis and loaded onto the sperm acrosome. Upon capacitation, it may be released or activated to promote membrane fusion. Dysregulation via 7q CNVs disrupts this process: loss causes complete fertilization failure (azoospermia or oligozoospermia with normal sperm count but inability to fertilize), while gain may cause premature fusion or polyspermy, contributing to embryonic lethality or developmental abnormalities seen in 7q syndromes.
