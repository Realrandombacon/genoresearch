## Description
SLC5A10 (UniProt A0PJK1, 596 aa), also known as Sodium/Glucose Cotransporter 5 (SGLT5), is a partially characterized solute carrier with 27 literature papers establishing its critical role in renal sugar handling and diabetes biomarker regulation. The seminal 2019 ARIC study (11 citations) identified rare SLC5A10 variants significantly associated with serum 1,5-anhydroglucitol (1,5-AG) levels, a key inverse marker of glycemic excursions in diabetes. InterPro reveals canonical sodium/solute symporter architecture (IPR001734, PF00474, aa 50-479) with the conserved signature motif (PS00457, aa 461-481) essential for sodium coupling, and G3DSA:1.20.1730.10 superfamily domains (aa 39-594) forming the characteristic LeuT-fold with 14 transmembrane helices typical of the SSS family. The cd11489 domain specifically identifies it as an SGLT5-related protein with a distinct substrate-binding pocket optimized for mannose and polyols. AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (global pLDDT 87.9), indicating a stable transporter capable of alternating access mechanisms. HPA shows tissue-enriched expression with highest levels in kidney (38.9 nTPM), consistent with its role in renal reabsorption of filtered sugars; subcellular localization suggests plasma membrane targeting in proximal tubule epithelium. STRING interactions reveal medium-confidence partners including SLC2A2/GLUT2 (0.614, basolateral glucose efflux), SLC2A5/GLUT5 (0.475, fructose transporter), SLC1A1 (0.491, amino acid transporter), FAM83G (0.460, neighboring gene on 17p11.2), MFSD4B (0.457), PRODH/PRODH2 (0.435-0.442, proline dehydrogenases linking amino acid and sugar metabolism), and APRT (0.413). ClinVar contains 103 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number losses/gains spanning 17p11.2 (Potocki-Lupski/Smith-Magenis syndrome regions), though specific SLC5A10 point mutations remain of uncertain significance (?), suggesting dosage sensitivity may contribute to metabolic phenotypes in CNV syndromes. Conservation is strong across mammals: mouse Q5SWY8 (596 aa), bovine Q6R4Q5 (597 aa), porcine Q5FY69 (597 aa), rabbit Q28610 (597 aa), indicating evolutionary constraint on renal sugar transport. Functional hypothesis: SLC5A10 functions as a low-affinity, high-capacity Na+-coupled symporter primarily for mannose and 1,5-anhydroglucitol (1,5-AG) in the renal proximal tubule (S2/S3 segments), operating via the rock-and-roll alternating access mechanism. Its kidney enrichment (38.9 nTPM) suggests a primary role in reclaiming filtered mannose (from glycoprotein turnover) and 1,5-AG (a dietary polyol structurally similar to glucose but not metabolized), preventing their urinary loss. Unlike SGLT1/SGLT2 which handle glucose/galactose, SGLT5's specificity for 1,5-AG explains the genetic association with serum 1,5-AG levels: loss-of-function variants reduce renal reabsorption, lowering serum 1,5-AG independently of glycemia, potentially confounding its use as a diabetes biomarker. The interaction with GLUT2 suggests coordinated apical uptake (SGLT5) and basolateral efflux (GLUT2) for transcellular transport. The proximity to PRODH/PRODH2 hints at metabolic coupling between proline catabolism and sugar handling in the kidney. Priority research: (1) kinetic characterization of mannose/1,5-AG/fructose transport (Km, Vmax, Na+ stoichiometry), (2) association studies linking SLC5A10 rare variants to 1,5-AG levels and diabetes diagnosis accuracy, (3) investigation of SGLT5 expression in Potocki-Lupski/Smith-Magenis syndrome patients to assess metabolic contributions, (4) structural analysis of substrate-binding pocket to explain 1,5-AG specificity vs. glucose, (5) role in renal Fanconi-like syndromes or unexplained glycosuria.
