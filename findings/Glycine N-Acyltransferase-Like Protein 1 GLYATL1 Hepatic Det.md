## Description
GLYATL1 (Glycine N-Acyltransferase-Like Protein 1) is a 302-amino acid mitochondrial enzyme with 23 publications confirming its partially characterized dark gene status. The protein contains conserved glycine N-acyltransferase domains including N-terminal acyltransferase domain (IPR015938, positions 1-206; PF06021), C-terminal acyltransferase domain (IPR013652, positions 208-296; PF08444), and membership in the glycine N-acyltransferase family (IPR010313, positions 1-299; PTHR15298) with acyl-CoA N-acyltransferase superfamily architecture (IPR016181, SSF55729, positions 39-292) that mediates conjugation of acyl-CoA substrates with glycine. AlphaFold predicts a very well-folded structure (pLDDT 90.9), consistent with stable enzymatic architecture required for catalytic function. HPA reveals extreme liver-enriched expression (508.1 nTPM) and kidney expression (130.7 nTPM), group-enriched tissue distribution, cancer-enhanced expression pattern, and Golgi apparatus/cytosol localization with acyltransferase/transferase molecular function. ClinVar documents 9 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants (primarily chromosome 11p11.12-q13.3/11q11-12.1 copy number gains and deletions), though specific disease phenotypes beyond metabolic disorders remain uncharacterized. STRING interactions reveal GLYATL1 functions within an acyl-CoA metabolism and chromatin modification network with high-confidence connections to ESCO2 (0.878), NAA80 (0.806), OPN4 (0.851), SAT1 (0.806), SAT2 (0.800), RGS9BP (0.783), KAT2A/GCN5 (0.761), KAT2B/PCAF (0.758), ESCO1 (0.721), and CNGA3 (0.524). Recent studies (2020-2025) established GLYATL1 and GLYAT contributing to isovaleryl-CoA detoxification in isovaleric acidemia, decreased GLYATL1 expression predicting poor prognosis in clear cell renal cell carcinoma, GLYATL1 expression and prognostic value in hepatocellular carcinoma, PLGLA pseudogene modulating miR-324-3p/GLYATL1 axis in hepatocellular carcinoma, and GLYATL1 association with metabolic/epigenetic changes and endocrine resistance in luminal breast cancer. We hypothesize GLYATL1 functions as a hepatic detoxification enzyme that conjugates toxic acyl-CoA metabolites (particularly isovaleryl-CoA from branched-chain amino acid catabolism) with glycine to form excretable hippurate-like conjugates, preventing metabolite accumulation in isovaleric acidemia. The SAT1/SAT2 interactions (spermidine/spermine N1-acetyltransferases) suggest coordinated polyamine metabolism regulation, while KAT2A/KAT2B connections (histone acetyltransferases) hint at epigenetic regulation of metabolic genes consistent with breast cancer endocrine resistance associations. Liver/kidney enrichment confirms classical detoxification organ targeting, and cancer prognostic value indicates GLYATL1 dysregulation contributes to tumor metabolism reprogramming—a mechanism exploitable for therapeutic intervention in renal cell carcinoma and hepatocellular carcinoma.
