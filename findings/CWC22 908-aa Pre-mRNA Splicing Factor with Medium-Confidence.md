## Description
CWC22 (UniProt Q9HCG8), or CWC22 spliceosome associated protein homolog, encodes a large 908-amino acid nuclear protein essential for pre-mRNA splicing and exon junction complex (EJC) assembly. The protein features multiple RNA-binding domains including a MIF4G domain (IPR003890/PF02854/SM00543, aa 163-346) for protein-protein interactions, an MA3 domain (IPR003891/PF02847/SM00544/PS51366, aa 454-570) characteristic of translation initiation factors, and an Armadillo-type fold (IPR016024/SSF48371, aa 154-394) for scaffold functions. AlphaFold predicts a medium-confidence structure (global pLDDT: 65.1), consistent with a partially structured protein containing ordered RNA-binding domains connected by flexible linker regions typical of spliceosomal factors.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places CWC22 at the core of spliceosome assembly: CDC5L (0.999), CRNKL1 (0.999), SNRNP200 (0.996), SRRM2 (0.994), PRPF19 (0.993), CDC40 (0.987), RNF113A (0.958), XAB2 (0.939), YJU2 (0.862), SLU7 (0.680). These partners confirm its role in the NineTeen Complex (NTC) and catalytic activation of the spliceosome. HPA data shows ubiquitous expression with localization to nuclear speckles and cytosol, reflecting its dual roles in splicing and post-splicing mRNA surveillance.\n\nWith 53 papers, CWC22 is moderately characterized with breakthrough disease mechanisms emerging in 2023-2026. Recent studies reveal: (1) CWC22 targeting induces mitotic slippage in cancer cells through BubR1 splicing repression and CDK1 activity suppression; (2) CWC22-mediated alternative splicing of Spp1 regulates nociception in inflammatory pain models; (3) CWC22/CWC27 heterodimer structurally links EJC to spliceosomes (2020 crystallography); (4) prognostic marker for lymph node involvement in colon cancer. ClinVar lists 30 pathogenic variants including chromosome 2q31 copy number losses/gains and missense mutations (p.Tyr33Phe), confirming dosage sensitivity. We hypothesize CWC22 functions as a molecular bridge: the MIF4G/MA3 domains recruit EJCs to newly spliced mRNAs while simultaneously interacting with NTC components, coupling splicing completion to mRNA export licensing—a mechanism co-opted by cancers to regulate mitotic checkpoint genes (BubR1) and by nociceptive neurons to control pain signaling via Spp1 isoform switching.
