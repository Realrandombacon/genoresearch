# DCLK2: Brain-Enriched Doublecortin-Kinase Regulating Neuronal Pyroptosis and TBK1 Activation in Renal Cancer via Calcium-Calmodulin Signaling

## Description
DCLK2 (Doublecortin-Like Kinase 2) is a partially characterized serine/threonine kinase with 33 publications, emerging as a critical regulator of neuronal cell death (pyroptosis) in chronic cerebral ischemia and an oncogenic activator of TBK1 in clear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC). This 766-amino acid cytosolic/nuclear protein (UniProt Q8N568) features a unique domain architecture: two N-terminal doublecortin (DCX) domains (IPR003533, positions 67-158 and 192-280) that bind microtubules, followed by a C-terminal catalytic kinase domain (IPR000719, positions 394-651) with a conserved active site (positions 511-523). AlphaFold predicts medium-confidence structure (pLDDT 69.6), reflecting the flexible nature of the DCX domains linked to the structured kinase core.\n\nSTRING interactions reveal DCLK2 operates within calcium signaling and cytoskeletal regulation networks: KLHL36 (0.602, substrate adaptor for Cullin3 ubiquitin ligases), PPP1R9B (0.583, neuronal protein phosphatase regulator), TANC2 (0.513, synaptic scaffolding protein), HSP90AA1 (0.495, chaperone), CRTC2 (0.461, CREB regulated transcription coactivator), and multiple calmodulin family members (CALML3/4/5/6, CALM3; scores ~0.45). This interaction profile suggests DCLK2 activity is modulated by calcium-calmodulin binding and targets substrates involved in transcriptional regulation and synaptic function.\n\nHPA shows tissue-enhanced expression specifically in the brain (45.8 nTPM) and retina (17.1 nTPM), with cancer-enriched expression in various tumors. Subcellular localization includes cytosol and nucleus. ClinVar contains 28 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains on chromosome 4q28-35 and intronic/splice region mutations, though specific monogenic diseases remain uncharacterized. Conservation across mouse (756 aa) and rat (767 aa) indicates strong functional constraint.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: DCLK2 functions as a dual-mode sensor integrating microtubule dynamics with kinase signaling to control cell fate decisions in neurons and cancer cells. The DCX domains anchor DCLK2 to dynamic microtubule populations, positioning the kinase domain to phosphorylate specific substrates like EHF (as recently shown in ischemic pyroptosis) or to directly activate TBK1 in renal cancer. The calmodulin interactions suggest DCLK2 acts as a calcium-responsive switch: upon calcium influx, calmodulin binding may relieve autoinhibition or recruit DCLK2 to specific membrane compartments. In ccRCC, DCLK2-mediated TBK1 activation drives oncogenic NF-κB signaling independent of upstream kinases, making it a therapeutic target. In neurons, DCLK2 phosphorylation of EHF triggers gasdermin-mediated pyroptosis, linking cytoskeletal stress to inflammatory cell death. Pathogenic CNVs causing DCLK2 overexpression may predispose to cancer or neurodegeneration by dysregulating these critical signaling thresholds.
