## Description
XPNPEP2 (X-Prolyl Aminopeptidase 2, alias Aminopeptidase P2, X-Pro aminopeptidase 2) is a 674-amino acid metalloprotease enzyme belonging to the peptidase M24 family that catalyzes the release of N-terminal amino acids from peptides containing a proline residue at the penultimate position (X-Pro dipeptides). InterPro analysis reveals a characteristic two-domain architecture: an N-terminal creatinase/prolidase domain (IPR000587, residues 55-185; IPR029149, residues 44-192/194-354; G3DSA:3.40.350.10, residues 44-192/194-354; PF01321) that may mediate protein-protein interactions or substrate recognition, and a C-terminal peptidase M24 catalytic domain (IPR000994, residues 365-575; IPR032416, residues 585-648; IPR033740, residues 365-583; PF00557, residues 365-575; G3DSA:3.90.230.10, residues 355-651) containing the conserved M24B active site motif (IPR001131, residues 520-532) with manganese-binding residues essential for catalytic activity. The enzyme belongs to the Xaa-Pro aminopeptidase P family (IPR050422, residues 33-649) with GO annotations for metalloaminopeptidase activity (GO:0070006). AlphaFold predicts a very high-confidence structure (pLDDT 91.6), consistent with a well-folded, stable enzyme with defined active site architecture suitable for substrate binding and catalysis.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places XPNPEP2 at the center of a peptidase/protease regulatory network with exceptionally high confidence interactions: ACE2 (0.955, angiotensin-converting enzyme 2, the SARS-CoV-2 receptor and vasoprotective enzyme), MME (0.936, membrane metallo-endopeptidase/neprilysin), PRCP (0.935, prolylcarboxypeptidase, another X-Pro cleaving enzyme), XPNPEP3 (0.916, the cytosolic paralog performing similar reactions), KNG1 (0.806, kininogen-1, substrate for bradykinin generation), MEP1A (0.636, meprin A subunit alpha), DPP4 (0.511, dipeptidyl peptidase 4/CD26), LAP3 (0.443, leucine aminopeptidase 3), APP (0.443, amyloid precursor protein), and MEP1B (0.407, meprin A subunit beta). This interaction profile reveals XPNPEP2's integration into peptide hormone regulation, vasoactive peptide metabolism (bradykinin, angiotensin), and amyloid processing. The ACE2 and PRCP interactions are particularly notable as both enzymes process X-Pro containing peptides involved in cardiovascular regulation.\n\nHuman Protein Atlas classifies XPNPEP2 as a disease-related enzyme with group-enriched RNA tissue specificity, highest expression in intestine (156.9 nTPM) and kidney (145.6 nTPM). Subcellular localization includes plasma membrane and cytosol, consistent with access to both extracellular and intracellular peptide substrates. The cancer-enhanced expression pattern and cell type-enriched single cell classification suggest elevated expression in specific cell types, potentially endothelial cells given the angiogenesis literature.\n\nClinVar reports 173 pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number variations spanning Xq (coordinates include Xq23-28 deletion, Xq26.1-26.3 duplication, Xq13.1-27.1 duplication, Xq24-26.2 duplication, Xq21.32-26.1 deletion). The X-chromosome location means hemizygous males are fully affected by loss-of-function variants. While no specific monogenic disease is named solely for XPNPEP2, the pathogenic variant classification and X-linked inheritance suggest XPNPEP2 dosage is clinically relevant, potentially contributing to X-linked developmental disorders or nephrotic syndrome (one 2022 report links XPNPEP2 variants to nephrotic syndrome).\n\nLiterature analysis (55 papers) confirms XPNPEP2 as a partially characterized dark gene with emerging functional insights. The landmark 2026 study demonstrated that XPNPEP2 regulates angiogenesis via modulation of mitochondrial function through SLC25A6 (adenine nucleotide translocator), establishing a direct link between peptide metabolism and mitochondrial energetics in endothelial cells. A 2022 study identified X-linked recessive variants in XPNPEP2 as a potential new cause of nephrotic syndrome. Cancer studies (2017-2019) showed XPNPEP2 overexpression in cervical cancer and association with lymph node metastasis in prostate cancer via TMTP1 receptor binding. A 2025 study linked XPNPEP2 expression to growth traits in sheep, suggesting conserved metabolic functions.\n\nWe hypothesize that XPNPEP2 functions as a multifunctional peptidase with roles in vasoactive peptide metabolism, mitochondrial regulation, and cancer progression. The canonical role is cleavage of X-Pro dipeptides from peptide hormones (bradykinin, substance P, neuropeptides), regulating their bioactivity and half-life. However, emerging evidence suggests additional functions: (1) Mitochondrial-angiogenesis coupling—XPNPEP2 may regulate SLC25A6 expression or activity, linking peptide metabolism to ADP/ATP exchange and endothelial cell energetics during angiogenesis; (2) ACE2 functional partnership—XPNPEP2 may process ACE2 substrates or products, modulating the renin-angiotensin system and cardiovascular protection; (3) Cancer metastasis—XPNPEP2 may cleave extracellular matrix peptides or cell surface receptors (TMTP1 binding) to facilitate invasion; (4) Amyloid processing—APP interaction suggests potential role in amyloid-beta peptide metabolism. The Xq CNVs may cause dosage-sensitive phenotypes affecting peptide metabolism, potentially contributing to nephrotic syndrome (kidney peptide handling), cardiovascular disorders (vasoactive peptide imbalance), or neurodevelopmental disorders (neuropeptide dysregulation). XPNPEP2 represents a candidate regulator of vascular-metabolic coupling, a potential biomarker for X-linked nephrotic syndrome, a therapeutic target for angiogenesis modulation, and an uncharacterized contributor to Xq deletion/duplication syndrome phenotypes through peptide metabolism disruption.
