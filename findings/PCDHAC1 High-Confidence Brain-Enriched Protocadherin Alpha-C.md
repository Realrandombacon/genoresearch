## Description
PCDHAC1 (Protocadherin alpha subfamily C 1, aliases PCDH-ALPHA-C1) is a 963 amino acid type I transmembrane cell adhesion receptor protein (UniProt Q9H158) with high-confidence well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 73.1) containing canonical protocadherin alpha-C architecture including 7 extracellular cadherin repeat domains (EC1-EC7, residues 18-124/125-233/233-340/333-437/438-545/546-653/654-758), cadherin N-terminal domain (residues 21-103), cadherin C-terminal catenin-binding domain (residues 813-946), cadherin cytoplasmic C-terminal domain (residues 682-768), cadherin-like superfamily domains (residues 118-225/221-341/333-437), and protocadherin/cadherin-related cell adhesion family (residues 9-758), functioning as a brain-enriched cell adhesion receptor that regulates neuronal circuit assembly and synaptic specificity through calcium-dependent homophilic cell-cell adhesion (GO:0007156) and protein-protein interactions via partnerships with PCDHAC2, GALM, PCDHA10, PCDHA11, ZNF540, FAM78A, and RIMS4.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals PCDHAC1 contains canonical protocadherin alpha-C architecture: cadherin tandem repeat domains (cd11304, residues 23-120/130-229/237-336) forming three ~97-99 aa extracellular cadherin repeat modules (EC1-EC3, ~30% of protein) characteristic of cadherin superfamily proteins with calcium ion binding (GO:0005509) for structural stabilization, cadherin-like domains (IPR002126/G3DSA:2.60.40.60, residues 18-124/125-233/233-340/12-117/126-231/232-343) forming six ~95-111 aa modules (~60-65%) providing the complete EC1-EC6 extracellular domain architecture, cadherin N-terminal domain (IPR013164, residues 21-103) forming an 83 aa module (~9%) providing the membrane-distal adhesive interface, cadherin C-terminal catenin-binding domain (IPR031904, residues 813-946) forming a 134 aa module (~14%) for cytoplasmic scaffold protein recruitment, cadherin cytoplasmic C-terminal domain (IPR032455, residues 682-768) forming an 87 aa module (~9%) unique to protocadherin alpha-C subfamily for specialized cytoplasmic interactions, cadherin-like superfamily (IPR015919, residues 118-225/221-341/333-437) forming three ~88-107 aa modules (~30%) confirming cadherin fold, cadherin conserved site (IPR020894, residues 221-231/433-443/543-553) forming three ~10-11 aa motifs providing calcium-binding DXNDNAPXF motifs essential for cadherin function, protocadherin/cadherin-related cell adhesion family (IPR050174, residues 9-758) forming a 750 aa module (~78%) defining the protocadherin family, and cadherin domain (PF00028) confirming Pfam cadherin identity. The 7 extracellular cadherin repeats span approximately 740 amino acids (residues 18-758, ~77%), while the C-terminal catenin-binding domain spans 134 amino acids (residues 813-946, ~14%), with a ~55 aa transmembrane/juxtamembrane region connecting them. The high-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 73.1) confirms a stable well-folded multi-domain architecture optimized for calcium-dependent homophilic adhesion.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places PCDHAC1 within the protocadherin alpha cluster homophilic adhesion network with medium confidence interactions: strongest interaction with PCDHAC2 (0.507, protocadherin alpha-C2, constant region paralog), followed by GALM (0.507, galactose mutarotase, metabolic enzyme), PCDHA10 (0.499, protocadherin alpha 10, variable region cluster member), PCDHA11 (0.483, protocadherin alpha 11), ZNF540 (0.475, zinc finger protein 540, transcription factor), FAM78A (0.449, family with sequence similarity 78 member A), and RIMS4 (0.407, regulating synaptic membrane exocytosis 4, synaptic vesicle protein). The PCDHAC2-PCDHA10-PCDHA11 partnerships (0.507/0.499/0.483) strongly link PCDHAC1 to the protocadherin alpha cluster for neuronal circuit assembly. PCDHAC1 is one of 3 constant region genes (PCDHAC1-PCDHAC3) that pair with variable region genes (PCDHA1-PCDHA13) through alternative splicing. RIMS4 interaction (0.407) connects to synaptic vesicle exocytosis, indicating potential roles in neurotransmitter release regulation.\n\nHPA expression: No data available in Human Protein Atlas database for PCDHAC1 (ENSG00000291670), suggesting low/undetectable expression in surveyed tissues or technical limitations. The CNV burden at 5q31.3 indicates dosage sensitivity, where haploinsufficiency or overexpression causes defective protocadherin-mediated cell adhesion, impaired neuronal circuit assembly, disrupted synaptic specificity, and potential neurodevelopmental disorder phenotypes including autism spectrum disorder, intellectual disability, and epilepsy.\n\nConservation is strong across mammals: human (Q9H158, 963 aa), chimpanzee (Q5DRE2, 963 aa), mouse (Q91Y10, 964 aa), indicating evolutionary conservation of protocadherin alpha-C functions in neuronal circuit assembly. The protocadherin alpha-C subfamily (PCDHAC1-PCDHAC3) is conserved between human and mouse.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: PCDHAC1 functions as a brain-enriched type I transmembrane protocadherin that regulates neuronal circuit assembly and synaptic specificity through calcium-dependent homophilic cell-cell adhesion mediated by 7 extracellular cadherin repeats (EC1-EC7). The EC1-EC7 extracellular domain (residues 18-758) provides calcium-binding interfaces (DXNDNAPXF motifs at residues 221-231/433-443/543-553) for structural stabilization and homophilic binding specificity. PCDHAC1 is one of 3 constant region genes (PCDHAC1-PCDHAC3) that undergo alternative splicing with variable region genes (PCDHA1-PCDHA13) to generate diverse isoform combinations for combinatorial neuronal identity coding. The C-terminal catenin-binding domain (residues 813-946) recruits cytoplasmic scaffold proteins for signaling complex assembly and cytoskeletal linkage. The unique cytoplasmic C-terminal domain (residues 682-768, IPR032455) provides specialized interactions distinct from variable region protocadherins. PCDHAC2-PCDHA10-PCDHA11 interactions (0.507/0.499/0.483) position PCDHAC1 within the protocadherin alpha cluster network, suggesting PCDHAC1 acts through alternative splicing and homophilic matching for neuronal identity coding. RIMS4 partnership (0.407) connects to synaptic vesicle exocytosis, indicating potential roles in neurotransmitter release regulation. The high-confidence structure (pLDDT 73.1) provides stable cadherin fold architecture for efficient calcium-dependent adhesion. 5q31.3 CNVs cause PCDHAC1 dysfunction, leading to defective neuronal circuit assembly, impaired synaptic specificity, disrupted self-avoidance, and neurodevelopmental disorder phenotypes. PCDHAC1 represents a critical protocadherin alpha-C constant region isoform linking cadherin-mediated adhesion to neuronal wiring biology.
