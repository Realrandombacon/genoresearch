## Description
SIGLEC16 (sialic acid binding Ig like lectin 16, aliases SIGLECP16, Siglec-P16, Gene ID: 400709) is a 481-amino acid type I transmembrane receptor encoded on chromosome 19q13.33 (49969663-49975813) that represents a genuine dark gene with only 10 publications on Semantic Scholar. The landmark 2008 study (106 citations) established that SIGLEC16 encodes a DAP12-associated activating receptor expressed in macrophages that evolved from its inhibitory counterpart SIGLEC11 via gene conversion, and exists as both functional and non-functional alleles in humans—a rare example of a polymorphic gene/pseudogene in the human population. Recent 2023 research (8 citations) linked the polysialic acid-Siglec-16 axis to proinflammatory macrophage activation and increased glioblastoma patient survival. UniProt entry A6NMB1 exists for the 481-aa human protein with conservation in primates: rhesus macaque F7GYG9 (452 aa) and gorilla A0A2Z5VIG2 (431 aa), reflecting recent evolutionary emergence in hominins. InterPro scan reveals canonical Siglec domain architecture: N-terminal IgV domain (cd05712, positions 22-141) for sialic acid binding, followed by two Ig C2-set domains (cd20987, positions 147-234; IPR003598, positions 250-313 and 354-415), and additional Ig I-set domains (IPR013098, positions 247-323). This multi-Ig domain structure is characteristic of Siglec family receptors that recognize sialylated glycans on cell surfaces. AlphaFold structure prediction shows HIGH confidence (global pLDDT 83.0), indicating a well-folded protein with stable extracellular Ig domains, transmembrane helix, and cytoplasmic tail—remarkable for a gene with polymorphic pseudogene status in some humans. STRING database returned no interactions, likely reflecting the gene's dark status and specialized immune cell expression not captured in standard interaction databases. HPA expression reveals SIGLEC16 has group-enriched specificity with highest expression in ovary (21.7 nTPM) and lymphoid tissue (6.1 nTPM), with immune cell-enhanced specificity and detection in some tissues. The ovary enrichment is notable and may reflect roles in reproductive immunity or tissue-specific macrophage populations. ClinVar contains 3 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all copy number gains spanning 19q13.33-13.43 regions, though no specific monogenic disease is attributed solely to SIGLEC16. The 19q13.33 locus contains multiple immune-related genes. SIGLEC16's unique biology as a polymorphic activating Siglec distinguishes it from most inhibitory Siglecs: (1) It associates with DAP12 (TYROBP), an ITAM-bearing adaptor protein that triggers activating signaling cascades upon receptor engagement, (2) It recognizes polysialic acid (polySia) as a ligand, linking it to tumor-associated glycan recognition, (3) It exists as functional and non-functional alleles in humans due to a frameshift mutation in some individuals, creating population-level variation in immune responsiveness, (4) It co-evolved with SIGLEC11 via gene conversion in primates, with SIGLEC11 being inhibitory (ITIM-bearing) while SIGLEC16 is activating (DAP12-associated). The glioblastoma survival association suggests Siglec-16-mediated macrophage activation may promote anti-tumor immunity. The functional allele frequency varies across human populations, raising questions about evolutionary selection pressures. This represents a dark gene (10 papers) with strong mechanistic predictions based on Siglec family domain architecture, DAP12 association, polysialic acid recognition, polymorphic functional status, and immune cell expression, warranting experimental validation of ligand specificity, DAP12-dependent signaling, population genetics of functional alleles, and roles in cancer immunity, reproductive immunology, and infectious disease responses.
