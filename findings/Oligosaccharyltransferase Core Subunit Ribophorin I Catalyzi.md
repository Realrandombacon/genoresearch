## Description
RPN1 encodes ribophorin I, a 607 aa type I integral membrane protein (UniProt P04843) serving as an essential catalytic/regulatory subunit of the oligosaccharyltransferase (OST) complex in the rough endoplasmic reticulum. InterPro reveals distinctive Ribophorin I architecture: Ribophorin I family domain (IPR007676, residues 17-606), Pfam Ribophorin I domain (PF04597, residues 32-456), and PANTHER family membership (PTHR21049, residues 17-606) mediating glycosyltransferase activity and ER membrane integration. AlphaFold predicts very high confidence structure (pLDDT 90.1) indicating stable folded conformation typical of well-conserved enzymatic subunits. STRING reveals obligate OST complex membership with ultra-high confidence interactions: DAD1 (0.999), OST4/OSTC (0.999), MAGT1 (0.999), DDOST (0.999), STT3A (0.999, catalytic subunit), TMEM258 (0.997), RPN2/ribophorin II (0.995, paralogous partner), STT3B (0.994, alternative catalytic subunit), MLEC (0.899, malectin), and SEC61A1 (0.792, translocon component). HPA shows ubiquitous expression across all tissues with endoplasmic reticulum and cytosolic localization, classified as essential protein, metabolic protein, cancer-related gene, and plasma protein. ClinVar lists 15 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number gains at 3q13.31-26.31, deletions at 3q21.3, and missense variant His238Tyr though specific monogenic diseases remain undefined. Functional mechanism: Ribophorin I forms the regulatory core of the OST complex that catalyzes en bloc transfer of pre-assembled Glc3Man9GlcNAc2 oligosaccharide from dolichyl pyrophosphate donor to asparagine residues within Asn-X-Ser/Thr consensus motifs of nascent polypeptide chains during co-translational translocation through SEC61 translocon. This N-linked glycosylation is essential for proper protein folding, quality control via calnexin/calreticulin cycle, ER-associated degradation (ERAD) targeting of misfolded proteins, and cell surface receptor maturation. The cancer-related classification reflects elevated expression in multiple tumor types where enhanced glycosylation supports oncogenic signaling, metastasis, and chemoresistance. Despite mechanistic understanding of OST catalysis, tissue-specific substrate selectivity between STT3A vs STT3B-containing complexes, regulatory mechanisms linking glycosylation efficiency to cellular stress responses, and precise contribution of RPN1 variants to congenital disorders of glycosylation remain incompletely characterized - representing genuine dark aspects of this essential glycosylation machinery component.
