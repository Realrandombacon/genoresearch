## Description
LOC129388564 is a 200bp cis-regulatory element (CRE) located on chromosome 1p31.1 (GRCh38: 93839379-93839579), functionally validated as a transcriptional silencer through STARR-seq massively parallel reporter assays (MPRAs) in K562 erythroleukemia cells. This element lacks canonical promoter, enhancer, or CTCF-bound insulator chromatin signatures but demonstrates robust repressive activity against the super core promoter SCP1. As a non-coding regulatory locus, it has no UniProt accession, protein domains, or STRING interaction partners. Human Protein Atlas could not resolve this locus to an Ensembl ID, consistent with its classification as a non-coding regulatory element rather than a protein-coding gene. ClinVar analysis reveals 1 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variant disrupting this region: a large 1p31.1-21.1 duplication (~21Mb, chr1:83457325-104273917). This recurrent CNV is associated with 1p duplication syndromes featuring developmental delay, intellectual disability, and hematologic abnormalities.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: LOC129388564 functions as a critical distal silencer within the 1p31.1 topologically associating domain (TAD), likely regulating expression of neighboring hematopoietic or tumor suppressor genes such as IL10RA, PRDM16, or CAMTA1 through long-range chromatin interactions. The silencer recruits co-repressor complexes (e.g., Polycomb Repressive Complex 2, NCoR/SMRT, HDACs) to deposit repressive histone marks (H3K27me3) at target promoters, maintaining basal repression of proto-oncogenes in erythroid lineages. Duplication of this locus may create dominant-negative effects by titrating away co-repressor complexes or altering chromatin topology, leading to dysregulated gene expression. The recurrent nature of pathogenic CNVs encompassing this locus suggests strong selective pressure against silencer disruption, positioning LOC129388564 as a non-coding regulatory element whose dosage sensitivity contributes to 1p duplication syndrome phenotypes. The K562 erythroleukemia cell line validation context supports a hematopoietic-specific regulatory role, potentially linking silencer dysfunction to myeloid differentiation defects.
