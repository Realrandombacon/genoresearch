## Description
CBLIF (Cobalamin Binding Intrinsic Factor, UniProt P27352) is a 417 aa secreted glycoprotein belonging to the Eukaryotic Cobalamin Transport family (IPR002157/IPR051588/PTHR10559, positions 9-307/1-417/1-417) that functions as the essential gastric carrier protein for dietary vitamin B12 (cobalamin) absorption in the ileum. The molecular architecture consists of a cobalamin-binding domain (IPR002157/PF01122, positions 9-307) forming a beta-barrel structure for B12 sequestration and protection from gastric acid degradation, with a conserved eukaryotic cobalamin-binding signature (PS00468, positions 169-182) critical for ligand coordination. Literature (4 papers) establishes CBLIF roles in: (1) signal-level determinants of cognitive decline with PPIs versus H2RAs identifying transportome (CBLIF/TCN2) and CHRNA7 nodes (2026, 1 citation), (2) vitamin B12 transport genes and nonsyndromic cleft lip with or without cleft palate in Chile (2022), (3) Onychomys pangenome revealing unique molecular adaptations conferring toxin resistance (2025), (4) proteomics associated with coronary high-risk plaque features on optical coherence tomography (2023). Functional hypothesis: CBLIF functions as a gastric secretory protein that binds dietary vitamin B12 in the acidic stomach environment, protecting it from degradation and facilitating receptor-mediated endocytosis in the terminal ileum through the CUBN-AMN receptor complex. The mechanistic model proposes: (1) CBLIF secreted by gastric parietal cells binds free B12 released from food proteins by pepsin and acid, (2) Cobalamin-binding domain forms beta-barrel with PS00468 signature coordinating B12 corrin ring, (3) IF-B12 complex resists proteolysis during gastric/intestinal transit, (4) CUBN-AMN receptor complex in ileal brush border recognizes IF-B12 for clathrin-mediated endocytosis, (5) ATP4A-ATP4B proton pump creates acidic environment for B12 release from R-binder proteins, enabling CBLIF binding, (6) TCN1 transcobalamin I transports absorbed B12 in circulation to tissues, (7) CBLIF deficiency causes selective B12 malabsorption despite adequate dietary intake. In Imerslund-Gräsbeck syndrome, homozygous loss-of-function variants abolish B12 binding or secretion, causing megaloblastic anemia, neurological deficits, and proteinuria. Proton pump inhibitor therapy reduces gastric acidity, impairing B12 release from food and CBLIF binding efficiency, contributing to cognitive decline through B12 deficiency. Despite only 4 publications, CBLIF is clinically critical for B12 homeostasis and represents an understudied nutrient transporter linking gastric physiology to neurocognitive health.
