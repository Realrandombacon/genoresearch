## Description
LRRC31 (Leucine-rich repeat-containing protein 31, Gene ID: 79782) is a 552 amino acid protein located on chromosome 3q26.2 that exhibits substantial molecular characterization despite being classified as a "dark gene." UniProt accession Q6UY01 confirms this is a real, annotated protein. InterPro domain analysis reveals extensive leucine-rich repeat architecture: IPR001611 (LRR) at positions 451-469, IPR032675 (LRR domain superfamily) spanning residues 71-538 in two segments, and IPR042419 (LRRC31-specific family) covering the full length. The protein contains a ribonuclease inhibitor-like fold (G3DSA:3.80.10.10, SSF52047) suggesting potential protein-protein interaction capabilities typical of LRR-containing proteins. STRING interaction network identifies 10 partners with high-confidence connections to LRRIQ4 (score 0.708), LRRC34 (0.652), and notably XRCC5/Ku80 and XRCC6/Ku70 (both 0.620) — core components of the non-homologous end joining (NHEJ) DNA double-strand break repair pathway. Additional partners include MYNN (0.584), CAPN14 (0.571), ACTRT3 (0.525), TRIP10 (0.514), CEP295NL (0.501), and TMEM174 (0.429). Human Protein Atlas shows tissue-enhanced RNA expression with highest levels in intestine (20.4 nTPM), stomach (9.5 nTPM), and salivary gland (5.9 nTPM). Subcellular localization is predicted in nucleoplasm and cytosol, consistent with DNA repair function. ClinVar contains 21 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains affecting 3q26-29 regions. AlphaFold structure prediction (AF-Q6UY01-F1) shows high confidence (pLDDT 85.6) across the modeled range (1-552), indicating a well-folded stable protein. Functional hypothesis: LRRC31 likely functions as a scaffold protein in DNA damage response pathways, using its LRR domains to mediate protein-protein interactions within the NHEJ machinery. The XRCC5/6 interactions suggest direct involvement in Ku complex recruitment or stabilization at DNA break sites. Pathogenic CNVs may disrupt dosage-sensitive interactions critical for genome stability.
