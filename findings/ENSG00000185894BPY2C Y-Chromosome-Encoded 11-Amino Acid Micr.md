## Description
BPY2C (UniProt: R4GMN0, aliases VCY2C) encodes an extremely short 11 amino acid basic micropeptide representing a genuine dark gene with only 1 publication in Semantic Scholar. Located in the AZFc (azoospermia factor c) interval of Yq11.23, BPY2C is one of three nearly identical copies on chromosome Y, with two copies embedded in a palindromic region - this record represents the more telomeric copy. The exceptionally short protein length (11 aa) explains the failed InterPro domain scan (too short for structured domains) and absent AlphaFold structure (below modeling threshold for peptides <50 aa). Despite its minimal size, BPYC2 shows strong functional evidence: STRING interaction analysis identifies 10 high-confidence partners, all Y-chromosome genes involved in spermatogenesis: CDY2A (chromodomain protein Y-linked 2A, score 0.954), PRYP3 (PTPRZ1-related Y-linked pseudogene 3, 0.941), CDY2B (0.936), DAZ1 (deleted in azoospermia 1, 0.932), DAZ2 (0.898), CDY1 (chromodomain protein Y-linked 1, 0.810), PRY (PTPRZ1-related Y-linked, 0.777), PRY2 (0.768), VCY1B (0.717), and VCY (0.717). This interaction network places BPY2C within the Y-chromosome spermatogenesis machinery alongside DAZ family RNA-binding proteins and CDY chromatin modifiers. Human Protein Atlas shows tissue-enriched expression with testis specificity (1.1 nTPM, detected in single tissue), no detection in cancer or immune cells. ClinVar contains 40 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all copy number losses (deletions) and gains (duplications) spanning Yq11.221-q12, consistent with AZFc microdeletion syndromes causing male infertility and spermatogenic failure. Gene info indicates BPY2C interacts with UBE3A (ubiquitin protein ligase E3A), suggesting a role in ubiquitin-mediated protein degradation during germ cell development. The single literature report (2020) describes an AC006262.5/miR-7855-5p/BPY2C axis facilitating hepatocellular carcinoma proliferation and migration, suggesting BPY2C may have functions beyond spermatogenesis. Based on its 11 aa length, BPY2C functions as a micropeptide - a class of small proteins increasingly recognized for regulatory roles. The basic charge and Y-chromosome location suggest it may act as a charged scaffold or adaptor facilitating protein-protein interactions within the spermatogenesis complex. The high-confidence STRING interactions with DAZ/CDY proteins despite minimal length suggest BPY2C may contain a critical interaction motif that recruits or stabilizes larger spermatogenesis factors. The UBE3A interaction implies BPY2C may serve as a substrate or cofactor for ubiquitin ligase activity, potentially regulating the stability of spermatogenesis proteins through the ubiquitin-proteasome system. The palindromic arrangement suggests gene conversion maintains functional copies, while AZFc deletions cause male infertility. BPY2C exemplifies the emerging class of functional micropeptides encoded by Y-chromosome genes that coordinate male germ cell development through protein interaction networks rather than enzymatic activity.
