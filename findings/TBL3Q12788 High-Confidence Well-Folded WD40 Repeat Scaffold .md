## Description
TBL3 (Transducin beta-like protein 3, also known as UTP13, SAZD) is an 808 amino acid nucleolar scaffold protein (UniProt Q12788) with high-confidence well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 84.2), characteristic of WD40 repeat-containing proteins involved in ribosome biogenesis. The protein contains multiple WD40 repeats (IPR001680, positions 54-94, 97-138, 140-232, 144-449, 328-633) forming a beta-propeller structure (G3DSA:2.130.10.10/SSF50978) that mediates protein-protein interactions. A conserved U3 small nucleolar RNA-associated protein 13 C-terminal domain (IPR013934, residues 654-788) is present, explicitly linking TBL3 to rRNA processing (GO:0006364) and small-subunit processome assembly (GO:0032040). The YVTN repeat-like superfamily (IPR015943/IPR019775) further supports its role as a molecular scaffold.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals an exceptionally tight network exclusively composed of ribosome biogenesis factors and small-subunit processome components: strongest interactions with PWP2 (0.999, Periodic tryptophan protein 2, UTP-A complex), RRP9 (0.998, U3 small nucleolar RNA-associated protein 9, UTP-B complex), UTP20 (0.998, U3 small nucleolar RNA-associated protein 20, Imp3), UTP6 (0.998, U3 small nucleolar RNA-associated protein 6), NOP58 (0.998, Nucleolar protein 58, box C/D snoRNP core), UTP18 (0.997, U3 small nucleolar RNA-associated protein 18), NAT10 (0.997, N-acetyltransferase 10, rRNA acetyltransferase), MPHOSPH10 (0.996, M-phase phosphoprotein 10, UTP-C complex), KRR1 (0.995, KRR1 small subunit processome component), and IMP4 (0.995, Imp4 small subunit processome component). The near-perfect confidence scores (all >0.995) confirm TBL3/UTP13 is a core, stoichiometric component of the 90S pre-ribosome/small-subunit processome, specifically within the UTP-B subcomplex that binds the 5' external transcribed spacer (5' ETS) of pre-rRNA.\n\nHPA expression data is unavailable, but the nucleolar localization is well-established based on its UTP13 designation and interaction partners. The protein is predicted to be nuclear/nucleolar based on its function in rRNA processing.\n\nClinVar documents 46 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including splice site mutations (c.1958+4A>C, c.1671+6T>C, c.237+11C>T) and numerous large copy number gains and losses affecting chromosome 16p13.3 (16p13.3 duplications, 16p13.3-13.13 gains, 16p13.3 deletions). The variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity. While no specific Mendelian disease is annotated, 16p13.3 CNVs are associated with developmental delay, intellectual disability, autism spectrum disorders, and microcephaly. Given TBL3's essential role in 18S rRNA maturation, dosage imbalance likely causes ribosome biogenesis defects (ribosomopathy), impairing 40S subunit production, reducing translational capacity, and triggering nucleolar stress responses with p53 activation.\n\nConservation is strong across vertebrates: human (Q12788, 808 aa), mouse (Q8C4J7, 801 aa), rat (Q5U2W5, 800 aa), cow (Q2KJJ5, 800 aa), indicating an ancient and indispensable function in ribosome assembly.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: TBL3/UTP13 functions as a WD40 beta-propeller scaffold within the UTP-B subcomplex of the 90S pre-ribosome, providing a platform for assembly of multiple ribosome biogenesis factors. The WD40 repeats mediate interactions with UTP6, UTP18, UTP20/Imp3, and other processome components, while the C-terminal domain may bind pre-rRNA or recruit additional factors. TBL3 stabilizes the association of the UTP-B complex with the 5' ETS of 35S/47S pre-rRNA, facilitating correct folding and cleavage at sites A0, A1, and A2 to generate 18S rRNA. Loss of TBL3 destabilizes the UTP-B complex, causing defective 18S rRNA processing, accumulation of aberrant pre-rRNA intermediates, and reduced 40S subunit production. This triggers nucleolar stress, p53 stabilization, and cell cycle arrest, particularly affecting rapidly dividing tissues and neural progenitors, explaining the neurodevelopmental phenotypes associated with 16p13.3 CNVs. TBL3 represents a critical node in ribosome biogenesis and a candidate ribosomopathy gene.
