## Description
ATP2B4 (UniProt P23634), also known as PMCA4, PMCA4b, or PMCA4x, encodes a 1241 aa P-type cation-transporting ATPase functioning as the primary plasma membrane calcium efflux pump maintaining intracellular calcium homeostasis against steep concentration gradients. InterPro analysis reveals conserved multi-domain P-type ATPase architecture characteristic of PMCA family: animal plasma membrane Ca2+-ATPase domain (cd02081, residues 73-916) defining PMCA4 membership within ATP2B1-4/PMCA1-4 subfamily; P-type ATPase catalytic core (IPR001757, residues 155-281/400-485/746-864) mediating ATP binding (GO:0005524), ATP hydrolysis (GO:0016887), and transporter activity (GO:0005215) through aspartyl phosphate intermediate formation; cation-transporting P-type ATPase N-terminal domain (IPR004014, residues 45-121) for regulatory interactions; cation-transporting P-type ATPase C-terminal domain (IPR006068, residues 868-1046) containing calmodulin-binding autoinhibitory region; P-type ATPase subfamily IIB membership (IPR006408) placing it within calcium/sodium/potassium transporter group; and multiple transmembrane/cytoplasmic domains (G3DSA:1.20.1110.10 transmembrane 99-173/289-292/306-489/800-1056, G3DSA:2.70.150.10 cytoplasmic transduction A 70-98/174-288, G3DSA:3.40.1110.10 cytoplasmic N 523-672, G3DSA:3.40.50.1000 HAD-like 673-799) forming the ion translocation pathway. The NOS1-PRKG1-CALM axis confirms PMCA4 integration into nitric oxide-cGMP-PKA/PKG calcium signaling pathways. HPA classifies ATP2B4 as enzyme/metabolic protein/FDA approved drug target with tissue enhanced expression highest in smooth muscle (164.7 nTPM), detected in all tissues, low cancer/immune specificity, localized to plasma membrane/primary cilium/mitochondria/basal body consistent with calcium efflux function, involved in calcium/ion transport and calmodulin-binding/translocase activity. ClinVar lists 23 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number gains (1q21.1-44x3, 1q32.1x3), losses (1q31.3-32.1x1), deletions, duplications, and missense variants (p.Gly126Arg, p.Glu559Asp, p.Asn851Ser, p.Ile640Val) though no single-gene disorder name is consistently assigned; 1q32.1 location places it in region associated with various syndromes and PMCA4 dysfunction may contribute to cardiovascular, neurological, or reproductive phenotypes. Functional hypothesis: ATP2B4 operates through integrated calcium homeostasis mechanisms: (1) Transport catalysis - P-type ATPase cycle uses ATP hydrolysis energy to pump 2 Ca2+ ions per ATP from cytosol to extracellular space against 10,000-fold gradient via E1-E2 conformational changes with aspartyl phosphate intermediate; (2) Calmodulin regulation - Apo-PMCA4 autoinhibited by C-terminal domain binding catalytic site; calcium-bound calmodulin binds C-terminus relieving inhibition enabling high-activity state; (3) Phosphorylation modulation - PKA/PKG phosphorylate specific serine/threonine residues enhancing pump activity and calcium affinity; (4) NOS1 coupling - Physical interaction with neuronal NOS creates localized calcium-NO signaling microdomain where PMCA4 controls calcium availability for NOS activation; (5) Smooth muscle function - High expression (164.7 nTPM) reflects critical role in vascular/visceral smooth muscle calcium clearance determining relaxation kinetics and contractility; (6) Disease mechanism - Haploinsufficiency or dysfunction causes intracellular calcium overload impairing cellular function; missense mutations in catalytic domains disrupt transport while regulatory domain mutations alter calmodulin/phosphorylation sensitivity; CNVs cause dosage effects; potential phenotypes include hypertension (vascular smooth muscle dysfunction), cardiomyopathy, neurological disorders, male infertility (sperm calcium regulation); (7) Conservation - Mouse Atp2b4 (Q6Q477, 1205 aa) indicates essential conserved function; knockout mice show male infertility and vascular defects.
