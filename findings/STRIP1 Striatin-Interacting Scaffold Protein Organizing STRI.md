# STRIP1: Striatin-Interacting Scaffold Protein Organizing STRIP Complex for Golgi Localization, Cytoskeletal Organization, Epidermal Barrier Formation via CTTNBP2NL Stabilization, and Cardiac Hypertrophy Regulation through PP2A Phosphatase Assembly

## Description
STRIP1 (Striatin-Interacting Protein 1, also known as FAM40A, FAR11A, UniProt Q5VSL9) is an 837-amino acid intracellular scaffold protein that functions as a core component of the striatin-interacting phosphatase and kinase (STRIP) complex regulating Golgi body localization, cytoskeletal organization, and cell polarity through PP2A phosphatase recruitment and kinase coordination. The protein architecture features an N-terminal Far11/STRP domain (IPR012486, SM01292, PF07923 N1221-like protein aa 65-363) of unknown molecular function but essential for complex assembly, a C-terminal Far11/STRP domain (IPR021819, SM01293, PF11882 DUF3402 aa 460-817) containing a domain of unknown function characteristic of the Far11 family required for hyphal anastomosis in fungi, and belongs to the complete Far11/STRP family (IPR040185 aa 8-837, PTHR13239 protein required for hyphal anastomosis HAM-2 homolog). The AlphaFold predicted structure shows high confidence (pLDDT 80.9), indicating a well-folded stable protein with ordered N-terminal and C-terminal domains optimal for scaffolding multi-protein complexes. STRING interaction network places STRIP1 at the center of the STRIP complex with very high-confidence associations to: MOB4 (0.999), MOB family member 4 regulator of Hippo pathway; PPP2CA (0.999), protein phosphatase 2A catalytic subunit alpha forming core phosphatase activity; PDCD10 (0.999), programmed cell death 10 linking to apoptosis regulation; STRN4 (0.999), striatin-4 scaffold protein; STK26 (0.999), serine/threonine kinase 26 (MST4) regulating cell morphology; STRN3 (0.998), striatin-3 paralog; CTTNBP2NL (0.996), cortactin-binding protein 2 N-terminal-like stabilized by STRIP1 for epidermal barrier formation; STK25 (0.996), serine/threonine kinase 25 (MST3) paralog; STRN (0.980), striatin family scaffold; and PPP2R1A (0.876), protein phosphatase 2A scaffolding subunit alpha. HPA expression profiles demonstrate low tissue specificity with detection in all tissues indicating ubiquitous housekeeping function, cytosol subcellular localization consistent with STRIP complex assembly, low cancer specificity, low immune cell specificity, and cell type-enhanced expression patterns. ClinVar records document 22 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including splice site mutations (c.2062-20T>G, c.1789-1G>A, c.1416+3G>C affecting intron boundaries), missense variants (c.757G>T p.Gly253Cys in N-terminal domain), intronic variants (c.181-298G>A), and large copy number losses on chromosome 1p13.3-13.1 (110-116Mb), 1p13.3-13.2 (109-112Mb), 1p21.3-13.2 (95-114Mb), and 1p21.2-12 (102-119Mb) suggesting haploinsufficiency sensitivity though no specific monogenic syndrome has been definitively assigned. Literature analysis (38 papers) reveals emerging multi-system functionality: STRIP1 stabilizes CTTNBP2NL ensuring proper barrier formation in developing mouse skin epidermis through cytoskeletal organizer maintenance and planar cell polarity regulation; STRIP1 functions as novel negative regulator of cardiomyocyte hypertrophy preventing pathological cardiac remodeling; FARL-11 (STRIP1/2 homolog) required for sarcomere and sarcoplasmic reticulum organization in C. elegans muscle; STRIP complex localizes Golgi apparatus through coordinated action of striatin family scaffolds and PP2A phosphatase; STRIP1 participates in cytoskeletal organization essential for cell migration and polarity. We hypothesize that STRIP1 functions through a multi-mechanism scaffolding model: (1) The N-terminal Far11/STRP domain recruits PP2A phosphatase holoenzyme (PPP2CA/PPP2R1A) providing catalytic activity for dephosphorylating cytoskeletal regulators; (2) C-terminal DUF3402 domain binds striatin family members (STRN3/STRN4) organizing the core STRIP complex platform; (3) STRIP1 stabilizes CTTNBP2NL preventing its degradation, enabling cortactin-binding protein to organize actin cytoskeleton at epidermal cell junctions for barrier integrity; (4) Kinase recruitment (STK25/STK26 MST3/MST4) provides phosphorylation counterbalance to PP2A creating dynamic signaling node regulating cell morphology; (5) MOB4 interaction links STRIP1 to Hippo pathway controlling organ size and cell proliferation; (6) PDCD10 association suggests roles in apoptosis regulation potentially through cytoskeletal-mediated survival signaling; (7) Cytosolic localization enables rapid response to extracellular cues affecting cell polarity and migration; (8) High-confidence structure suggests stable platform for assembling large multi-subunit complexes; (9) Cardiac hypertrophy regulation indicates STRIP1 may dephosphorylate hypertrophic signaling components (HDACs, transcription factors) via PP2A recruitment; (10) Sarcomere organization in muscle suggests conserved role in contractile apparatus assembly across species. Therapeutic targeting of STRIP1-CTTNBP2NL interface could enhance epidermal barrier function in skin disorders, while STRIP1-PP2A modulation may treat cardiac hypertrophy.
