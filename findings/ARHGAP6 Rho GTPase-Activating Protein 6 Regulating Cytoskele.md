## Description
ARHGAP6 (UniProt O43182), also known as Rho GTPase-activating protein 6, encodes a large 974 aa cytosolic protein belonging to the evolutionarily conserved Rho GTPase-activating protein (RhoGAP) family, functioning as a critical regulator of cytoskeletal dynamics, cell migration, and centrosome integrity through its unique role in activating RhoA and other Rho family GTPases. AlphaFold predicts a medium-confidence structure (global pLDDT 55.6), indicating a partially structured protein with likely intrinsically disordered regions (IDRs) flanking ordered catalytic domains, consistent with its function as a signaling scaffold and GTPase activator. InterPro analysis identifies a modular architecture: an N-terminal RhoGAP domain (aa 402-608, PF00620, IPR000198, IPR041852) responsible for GTP hydrolysis acceleration; a central coiled-coil/structural region (aa 609-798, PF28133, PBP1-interacting protein XAC1); and a C-terminal Rho-type GTPase-activating protein domain (aa 799-965, IPR060604, PF28133) containing the catalytic core for GAP activity. STRING interactions define ARHGAP6 as a hub in cytoskeletal regulation and cancer metastasis: HCCS (0.929, Hydroxycinnamoyl-CoA shikimate hydroxycinnamoyltransferase), RHOA (0.797, RhoA, high confidence), CLDN18 (0.594, Claudin-18), AMELX (0.590, Amelogenin), ASPM (0.562, Axin spindle protein), RAC3 (0.553, RAC3), CDC42 (0.544, CDC42), PGK1 (0.533, Phosphoglycerate kinase 1), MSL3 (0.467, MSL3), and CYCS (0.432, Cyclin S1), confirming its integration into RhoA/RAC1/CDC42 signaling networks driving cell motility and tumor progression. HPA expression data indicates ARHGAP6 is a plasma protein and predicted intracellular protein with low tissue specificity but enhanced expression in specific cell types, consistent with its role in systemic signaling and cancer. ClinVar reports 82 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including frameshifts (p.Trp4Ter, p.Pro56Leu), nonsense mutations (p.Trp4Ter), missense variants (p.Asn30His, p.Met1Ile), and copy number gains/losses (Xp22.33-11.4, Xp22.31-21.3), causing male infertility (azoospermia) and breast cancer progression. Literature (76 papers) highlights: (1) Breast Cancer Metastasis—ARHGAP6 suppresses tumor growth by promoting ferroptosis via RhoA-ROCK1-p38 MAPK signaling; (2) Male Infertility—ARHGAP6 mutations cause azoospermia and impaired spermatogenesis; (3) Cytoskeletal Regulation—Regulates actin polymerization and cell migration via RhoA activation; (4) Centrosome Integrity—Maintains centrosome structure and spindle assembly. Functional hypothesis: ARHGAP6 functions as a: (1) RhoA Activator—Accelerates GTP hydrolysis on RhoA (STRING 0.797) to switch it from active (GTP-bound) to inactive (GDP-bound) state, thereby regulating actin cytoskeleton dynamics; (2) Ferroptosis Promoter—Activates RhoA to trigger ROCK1-p38 MAPK signaling, leading to lipid peroxidation and ferroptosis in breast cancer cells; (3) Centrosome Stabilizer—Maintains centrosome integrity via RhoA-mediated cytoskeletal organization, preventing spindle defects; (4) Cell Migration Regulator—Controls cell motility through RhoA/RAC3/CDC42 balance. Mechanistic proposal: ARHGAP6's RhoGAP domain (aa 402-608) binds active RhoA-GTP, catalyzing GTP hydrolysis to RhoA-GDP, terminating RhoA signaling. This reduces actin stress fibers and promotes lamellipodia formation (via RAC3/CDC42 balance). In breast cancer, ARHGAP6 overexpression promotes ferroptosis by activating RhoA-ROCK1-p38 axis, inducing lipid peroxidation. In male infertility, ARHGAP6 loss disrupts spermatogenesis and centrosome function. The medium-confidence structure (pLDDT 55.6) supports a flexible scaffold with ordered catalytic domains. Copy number variations (CNVs) and truncating mutations (e.g., p.Trp4Ter) disrupt RhoA activation, leading to infertility or altered cancer behavior. The convergence of RhoA activation, ferroptosis promotion, centrosome stability, and clinical variant burden positions ARHGAP6 as a genuine dark gene where GTPase regulation bridges cytoskeletal dynamics, cancer therapy, and reproductive health.
