## Description
CIMIP1 (Ciliary microtubule inner protein 1, also known as C20orf85, LLC1, NLLC1, bA196N14.1) is a 137 amino acid ciliary protein (UniProt Q9H1P6) with high-confidence well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 76.0), characteristic of ciliary microtubule inner proteins. The protein belongs to the ciliary microtubule inner protein 1-like family (IPR020339, residues 5-137), with a Normal lung function maintenance/Low in Lung Cancer 1 protein domain (PF14945, residues 11-136) and chromosome 20 ORF85 family membership (PTHR31909, residues 5-137). The alias LLC1/NLLC1 indicates this protein was originally identified as downregulated in lung cancer, suggesting a potential tumor suppressor role. Subcellular localization is the midpiece - the mitochondrial-rich region of sperm flagella and ciliary axonemes - placing CIMIP1 within the ciliary/flagellar apparatus.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis returned no results, indicating CIMIP1 has not been extensively studied in protein interaction networks. This absence of interaction data is consistent with its classification as a 'dark gene' and suggests the protein may function as a structural component rather than a signaling hub, or that its interactions are transient/context-specific (e.g., during ciliogenesis or ciliary beating).\n\nHPA expression reveals tissue enrichment in fallopian tube (427.9 nTPM), detected in some tissues, with cancer-enriched RNA specificity. The fallopian tube enrichment is highly significant as this tissue contains ciliated epithelial cells essential for oocyte transport. Midpiece localization confirms association with ciliary/flagellar microtubules. The cancer-enriched expression pattern, combined with the LLC1 (Low in Lung Cancer 1) alias, suggests CIMIP1 expression is altered in malignancies, potentially serving as a biomarker or functional contributor to cancer progression.\n\nClinVar documents 16 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including large deletions (NC_000020.10:g.55906911_58646228del), copy number gains (20q13.13-13.33, 20q13.31-13.33, 20q13.2-13.33 amplifications), and copy number losses (20q13.31-13.32 deletions). The variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity. While no specific Mendelian disease is annotated, 20q13 CNVs are associated with developmental delay, intellectual disability, and various congenital anomalies. Given CIMIP1's ciliary localization, dosage imbalance may cause ciliopathy-like phenotypes affecting tissues dependent on ciliary function: respiratory epithelium (mucociliary clearance), fallopian tube (fertility), brain (neuronal migration via primary cilia), and kidney (renal development).\n\nConservation is inferred from family membership across vertebrates, with the 137 aa length suggesting a compact, specialized structural role.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: CIMIP1 functions as a structural component of the ciliary axoneme, specifically associating with microtubule inner proteins (MIPs) that stabilize the doublet microtubules of the 9+2 arrangement. The protein may bind to the A-tubule lumen or connect adjacent protofilaments, enhancing microtubule rigidity and resistance to mechanical stress during ciliary beating. In the fallopian tube, CIMIP1 ensures proper ciliary motility for oocyte transport; loss causes impaired fertility or ectopic pregnancy risk. In respiratory epithelium, CIMIP1 maintains mucociliary clearance; dysfunction predisposes to chronic respiratory infections. The midpiece localization in sperm suggests a role in flagellar motility and male fertility. The cancer-enriched expression and LLC1 alias indicate CIMIP1 may be downregulated in lung cancer, potentially affecting ciliary signaling pathways (Hedgehog, Wnt) that regulate cell proliferation. Loss of ciliary CIMIP1 may dysregulate these pathways, contributing to tumorigenesis. 20q13 CNVs cause haploinsufficiency or overexpression, disrupting ciliary structure/function and causing developmental defects. CIMIP1 represents a candidate ciliopathy gene and potential cancer biomarker.
