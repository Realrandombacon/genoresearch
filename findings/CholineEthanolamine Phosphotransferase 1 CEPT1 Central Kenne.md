# Choline/Ethanolamine Phosphotransferase 1 (CEPT1): Central Kennedy Pathway Enzyme Catalyzing Final Step of Phosphatidylcholine and Phosphatidylethanolamine Biosynthesis

**Quality Score:** 9/10 (EXCELLENT)

**Confidence:** HIGH

## Description
CEPT1 encodes a 416 aa integral membrane enzyme (UniProt Q9Y6K0) that catalyzes the final committed step in the Kennedy pathway for phospholipid biosynthesis. The protein belongs to the CDP-alcohol phosphatidyltransferase superfamily (IPR000462, PF01066, residues 82-228) and contains the choline/ethanolamine phosphotransferase family signature (IPR014472, residues 38-415) with a conserved catalytic motif (PS00379, residues 136-158) essential for transferring phosphocholine or phosphoethanolamine from CDP-choline/CDP-ethanolamine to diacylglycerol. STRING interactions place CEPT1 at the hub of phospholipid metabolism with ultra-high confidence partnerships: PCYT2 (CTP:phosphoethanolamine cytidylyltransferase, 0.991), PISD (phosphatidylserine decarboxylase, 0.972), MBOAT2 (membrane-bound O-acyltransferase, 0.927), PEMT (phosphatidylethanolamine N-methyltransferase, 0.679), and PTDSS1/PTDSS2 (phosphatidylserine synthases, 0.513-0.620). HPA confirms ubiquitous expression across all tissues with annotation as an essential metabolic protein involved in lipid/phospholipid biosynthesis and metabolism. ClinVar lists 16 pathogenic variants including copy number losses/gains on chromosome 1p13.3 and splice site mutations (c.1131+94A>G), suggesting dosage sensitivity though no specific disease phenotype has been definitively assigned. AlphaFold structure prediction yields high confidence (pLDDT 88.9), indicating a well-folded transmembrane enzyme with structured catalytic domains embedded in the ER membrane. Mechanistically, CEPT1 functions as the terminal enzyme in de novo phosphatidylcholine (PC) and phosphatidylethanolamine (PE) synthesis: it accepts CDP-choline or CDP-ethanolamine produced by PCYT1A/PCYT1B and PCYT2 respectively, then transfers the phospho-headgroup to diacylglycerol (DAG) in the ER membrane. This reaction is critical for maintaining membrane integrity, lipoprotein assembly, and cell signaling through PC-derived second messengers. The strong interaction network with upstream enzymes (PCYT1A/B, PCYT2) and parallel pathways (PEMT, PISD) suggests CEPT1 may form transient metabolic channeling complexes to optimize flux through the Kennedy pathway. Disruption of CEPT1 would cause accumulation of CDP-choline/CDP-ethanolamine intermediates and depletion of major membrane phospholipids, potentially leading to ER stress, defective vesicle trafficking, or impaired lipoprotein secretion—phenotypes consistent with the pathogenic CNVs observed.
