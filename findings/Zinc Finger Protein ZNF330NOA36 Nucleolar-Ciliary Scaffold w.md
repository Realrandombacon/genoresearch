## Description
ZNF330 (alias NOA36) encodes a 320 amino acid zinc finger protein (UniProt Q9Y3S2) with a conserved NOA36 domain family (IPR010531, PF06524) spanning residues 1-295. The protein shows high structural confidence in AlphaFold predictions (pLDDT 76.7), indicating a well-folded stable tertiary structure. Subcellular localization data from HPA reveals dual targeting to nucleolar compartments (nucleoli, nuclear lumen) and ciliary apparatus (primary cilium, centrosome, basal body), suggesting functional roles in both ribosomal biogenesis and ciliogenesis. STRING interaction network identifies 10 high-confidence partners, with GSC (gremlin-1, score 0.954) and UBTF (upstream binding transcription factor, score 0.803) representing the strongest associations—implicating ZNF330 in BMP signaling modulation and RNA polymerase I transcriptional regulation respectively. Additional interactors include RWDD4, C12orf4, PPP1R21, and RPF1 (ribosome production factor), supporting a role in ribosomal assembly. ClinVar documents 35 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly copy number losses and gains on chromosome 4q31.21, though specific disease associations remain undefined. The gene exhibits ubiquitous expression across all tissues (low tissue specificity) with cell type-enhanced expression patterns. Conservation analysis shows the NOA36 family is vertebrate-specific with orthologs in mammals (Bos taurus, Pongo abelii). Mechanistic hypothesis: ZNF330 functions as a nucleolar-ciliary scaffold protein that coordinates ribosomal RNA transcription (via UBTF interaction) with ciliary assembly, potentially serving as a cell cycle checkpoint regulator at the midbody during cytokinesis. Copy number variants may disrupt nucleolar integrity and ciliary signaling, contributing to developmental disorders.
