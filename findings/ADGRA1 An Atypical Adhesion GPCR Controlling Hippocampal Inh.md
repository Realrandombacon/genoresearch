## Description
ADGRA1 (Adhesion G Protein-Coupled Receptor A1, also known as GPR123) is a 560-amino acid atypical member of the adhesion GPCR family characterized by a secretin-like family 2 GPCR domain architecture (IPR000832/IPR017981/PF00002/PS50261, positions 17-310) containing the canonical 7-transmembrane helix bundle (G3DSA:1.20.1070.10, positions 13-208 and 245-309) with conserved GPCR family 2 signature motifs (IPR017983/PS00650, positions 298-313), classified under the adhesion GPCR A family (IPR051963/PTHR45930, positions 1-560). STRING interactions reveal a specialized network with other adhesion GPCRs and related receptors: SCTR (0.817, secretin receptor - highest confidence partner suggesting shared ligand/signaling mechanisms), KNDC1 (0.698, kinase non-catalytic C-lobe domain-containing protein 1), ADGRG5 (0.664), ADGRL1 (0.659, latrophilin-1), ADGRF2 (0.645), ADGRL2 (0.639), ADGRL3 (0.630), ADGRG7 (0.629), ADGRD2 (0.621), and ADGRG3 (0.435), indicating functional clustering within the adhesion GPCR subfamily. HPA data shows tissue-enriched expression exclusively in brain (26.2 nTPM), cancer-enriched specificity, not detected in immune cells, cell type-enhanced single cell specificity, consistent with its neuronal function; classified as a G-protein coupled receptor and predicted membrane protein with transducer activity. ClinVar contains 100 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number losses spanning 10q26.11-26.3 regions (deletions affecting the terminal chromosomal region), though specific monogenic disease phenotypes remain under characterization (significance marked '?' for most); the high variant count suggests strong selective pressure and potential contribution to neurodevelopmental disorders when disrupted. AlphaFold structure (AF-Q86SQ6-F1) predicts medium confidence folding (pLDDT 63.5) consistent with a GPCR containing ordered transmembrane helices coupled with flexible extracellular N-terminal and intracellular C-terminal regions typical of adhesion GPCRs. With only 17 papers, ADGRA1 represents a 'gray' gene rapidly transitioning from dark due to breakthrough studies (2021-2026): ADGRA1 knockout male mice (but not females) exhibit increased anxiety-like behaviors attributable to elevated dendritic density, upregulated PSD95 expression, and abnormal activation of PI3K/AKT/GSK-3β and MEK/ERK pathways in hippocampal neurons; ADGRA1 negatively regulates energy expenditure and thermogenesis through both sympathetic nervous system and hypothalamus-pituitary-thyroid axis; and most recently (2025/2026), ADGRA1 controls hippocampal inhibitory circuit function by modulating cell type-specific inhibitory synapse formation and function. The mechanistic hypothesis posits that ADGRA1 functions as an atypical adhesion GPCR that senses extracellular matrix or cell surface ligands through its N-terminal region, triggering conformational changes that activate intracellular G-protein signaling cascades (potentially Gs/Gq-mediated cAMP or calcium signaling) to regulate synaptic plasticity genes via PI3K/AKT/GSK-3β and MEK/ERK pathways; its male-specific behavioral phenotype suggests interaction with sex hormone signaling or sexually dimorphic neural circuit development; the high brain expression (26.2 nTPM) and exclusive neuronal localization align with roles in inhibitory interneuron function controlling information processing, anxiety regulation, and metabolic homeostasis through central-autonomic integration, making it a potential therapeutic target for anxiety disorders and metabolic diseases.
