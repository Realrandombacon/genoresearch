## Description
GCC1 (GRIP and Coiled-Coil Domain Containing 1, also known as GCC88, UniProt Q96CN9) is a 775 aa peripheral membrane protein functioning as a golgin involved in maintaining Golgi structure and organizing trans-Golgi network (TGN) subcompartments for membrane transport. Structurally, AlphaFold predicts a confident architecture (pLDDT=76.9) featuring a canonical C-terminal GRIP domain (IPR000237/PF01465/PS50913/SM00755, aa 713-763/715-772) which mediates Arl1-GTP dependent targeting to the TGN, and classification within the Golgi-associated and autophagy-related protein family (IPR051952/PTHR23157, aa 1-774). The N-terminal region contains extensive coiled-coil domains typical of golgins, facilitating dimerization and tethering functions. Functionally, STRING interactions reveal robust coupling to Golgi machinery and trafficking factors: GOLGA4/Golgin-97 (0.958, highest confidence) as a paralogous GRIP-domain golgin, TGOLN2/TGN38 (0.934) as TGN integral membrane protein, BYSL/Bystin (0.933) involved in ribosome biogenesis and potentially linked to secretory pathway, AATF/Apoptosis Antagonizing Transcription Factor (0.926), ITSN1/Intersectin-1 (0.913) as endocytic scaffold, GRIP1 (0.913) as another GRIP-domain protein, LTV1 (0.886), TSR1 (0.834), NOB1 (0.689), and RIOK2 (0.646) suggesting potential links to ribosome assembly or quality control at the Golgi. HPA shows low tissue specificity with detection in all tissues, group enriched single cell type specificity, and localization to Golgi apparatus, plasma membrane, and cytosol, consistent with its role in vesicle tethering and membrane dynamics. ClinVar identifies 24 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants predominantly as large copy number losses and gains spanning 7q31.32-36.1 (e.g., chr7:122190535-149944340 deletion, chr7:121480906-129389003 deletion, chr7:109251060-159119707 duplication), though no specific Mendelian disease is directly annotated, suggesting GCC1 dosage sensitivity may contribute to 7q deletion/duplication syndrome phenotypes including developmental delay and dysmorphism. Literature (28 papers) reveals partial characterization: recent studies focus on fungal homologs regulating growth and oxidative stress (2023, 2 citations), while human studies include SWOG S1823/CCTG GCC1 trial design for germ cell tumor biomarkers (2023, 4 citations). GCC1 represents a 'partially characterized golgin with emerging roles in TGN organization' where: (1) GRIP domain anchors GCC1 to Arl1-positive TGN membranes, (2) coiled-coil regions mediate homodimerization and heterodimerization with GOLGA4 to form tethering complexes capturing incoming vesicles, (3) interaction with TGOLN2 suggests role in retrograde trafficking or TGN identity maintenance, (4) links to ribosome biogenesis factors (BYSL, LTV1, TSR1, NOB1, RIOK2) hint at potential coordination between protein synthesis and secretory pathway flux, (5) 7q CNV pathogenesis implies haploinsufficiency or triplosensitivity effects on Golgi integrity and secretion. Therapeutic modulation of GCC1-mediated tethering could impact secretory disorders or viral exploitation of the Golgi, while 7q CNV screening should consider GCC1 dosage in patients with developmental anomalies. GCC1 exemplifies the golgin family expansion required for complex Golgi architecture in higher eukaryotes.
