# EXOSC4: RNA Exosome Core Subunit RRP41 Essential for rRNA Processing and 3'-5' RNA Decay with Roles in Neurodevelopmental Disorders, Rheumatoid Arthritis-ILD, and Hepatocellular Carcinoma via PH-Domain Ribonuclease Architecture

## Description
EXOSC4 (Exosome Complex Component 4, also known as RRP41, UniProt Q9NPD3) is a 245 aa catalytically inactive core subunit of the RNA exosome complex, a multi-subunit machinery responsible for 3'-5' RNA processing and degradation. InterPro analysis reveals a bipartite phosphorolytic exoribonuclease architecture characteristic of the PNPase/RNase PH family: an N-terminal exoribonuclease phosphorolytic domain 1 (IPR001247, PF01138, positions 21-152; cd11370, positions 11-237) and a C-terminal exoribonuclease phosphorolytic domain 2 (IPR015847, PF03725, positions 155-219) within the ribonuclease PH domain superfamily (IPR027408, IPR036345, G3DSA:3.30.230.70, SSF55666, positions 20-245/152-238). The protein belongs to the ribosomal protein uS5 domain 2-type superfamily (IPR020568, SSF54211, positions 8-179) and ribonuclease PH family (IPR050080, PTHR11953, positions 13-230). Despite the phosphorolytic domain architecture, EXOSC4 lacks catalytic residues and functions as a structural scaffold. AlphaFold structure shows very high confidence (pLDDT 90.2), indicating a well-folded stable protein with defined PH-domain geometry. HPA data confirms testis-enriched expression (94.0 nTPM, tissue enhanced), detected in all tissues with low cancer specificity, nucleoplasm/vesicles/cytosol localization, classified as an essential protein involved in rRNA processing with RNA-binding molecular function. STRING interactions reveal exclusive clustering with RNA exosome complex components at maximum confidence: EXOSC5/EXOSC7/EXOSC3/EXOSC2/EXOSC1 (all 0.999, core ring subunits), DIS3/DIS3L (0.999, catalytic exoribonucleases), EXOSC10 (0.999, nuclear exosome component), MPHOSPH6 (0.977, M-phase phosphoprotein 6). ClinVar contains 61 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly copy number gains/losses at 8q24.13-24.3 affecting the EXOSC4 locus. Literature (42 papers) establishes EXOSC4 roles in: (1) neurodevelopmental defects via biallelic variants impairing RNA exosome function and translation (2024, 5 citations), (2) rheumatoid arthritis-associated interstitial lung disease (RA-ILD) as a novel autoantigen in alveolar type II cells (2025, 3 citations), (3) MASLD-HCC progression via RNA exosome machinery dysregulation (2025), (4) tumor suppression via RUNX3-mediated EXOSC4 inhibition (2024, 5 citations), and (5) non-coding transcription surveillance via H3K9me3/acetylation co-modification recruitment (2024). FUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: EXOSC4 serves as a structural scaffold stabilizing the hexameric exosome core ring (EXOSC1-4/EXOSC7/EXOSC8), positioning catalytic subunits (DIS3/DIS3L/EXOSC10) for substrate RNA processing while lacking intrinsic ribonuclease activity itself. MECHANISTIC PROPOSAL: The PH-domain architecture mediates heterodimerization with EXOSC7 (RRP42) to form the exosome core scaffold. EXOSC4 dysfunction impairs rRNA processing and 3'-5' RNA decay, causing accumulation of aberrant RNA species that trigger cellular stress. In neurodevelopmental disorders, biallelic variants disrupt exosome assembly, impairing RNA quality control in developing neurons. In RA-ILD, EXOSC4 becomes an autoantigen in alveolar type II cells, suggesting tissue-specific immune recognition. In cancer, RUNX3-mediated EXOSC4 suppression alters RNA metabolism favoring tumor progression. The 61 ClinVar CNVs indicate EXOSC4 dosage sensitivity, with both gains and losses potentially disrupting exosome stoichiometry.
