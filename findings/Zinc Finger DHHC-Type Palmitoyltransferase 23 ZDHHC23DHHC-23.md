# Zinc Finger DHHC-Type Palmitoyltransferase 23 (ZDHHC23/DHHC-23): Golgi-Resident S-Acyltransferase Regulating Plasma Membrane Targeting via Reversible Protein Palmitoylation

## Description
ZDHHC23 encodes a 409 aa integral membrane enzyme (UniProt Q8IYP9), also known as DHHC-23 or NIDD, that functions as a protein S-palmitoyltransferase catalyzing the attachment of palmitate (C16:0) to cysteine residues of substrate proteins via a thioester bond. This reversible post-translational modification regulates protein trafficking, membrane anchoring, and signal transduction. The protein contains a highly conserved catalytic DHHC domain (IPR001594, PF01529, PS50216, residues 256-386) featuring the essential Asp-His-His-Cys motif required for acyl-CoA binding and transferase activity, embedded within the PFA4/ZDHHC16/ZDHHC20-like family scaffold (IPR039859, PTHR22883, residues 145-385). STRING interactions place ZDHHC23 in a network of lipid-modifying enzymes and paralogs: ZFP69 (0.680), a zinc finger protein potentially regulated by palmitoylation; SULT6B1 (0.500), a sulfotransferase; LYPLA2 (0.490) and LYPLA1 (0.419), acyl-protein thioesterases that reverse palmitoylation; and multiple DHHC paralogs including ZDHHC22 (0.467), ZDHHC11 (0.459), ZDHHC18 (0.457), ZDHHC6 (0.432), and ZDHHC24 (0.419), suggesting functional redundancy or cooperative substrate specificity. HPA confirms tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in testis (10.2 nTPM) and cancer-enhanced expression in various tumors, with subcellular localization to the nucleoplasm (though enzymatic activity is predicted at Golgi/ER membranes per gene ontology), consistent with roles in secretory pathway processing and spermatogenesis. ClinVar lists 33 pathogenic variants including large copy number losses spanning chromosome 3q13.12-q13.31, deletions, and splice variants (c.162-8C>G), demonstrating dosage sensitivity and linking ZDHHC23 disruption to developmental disorders and potential metabolic defects. AlphaFold structure prediction yields high confidence (global pLDDT 78.6), revealing a well-folded multi-pass transmembrane architecture with the catalytic DHHC domain positioned for access to cytosolic acyl-CoA and substrate proteins. Mechanistically, ZDHHC23 functions through a coordinated cycle: (1) The DHHC domain undergoes auto-palmitoylation on the conserved cysteine, forming an acyl-enzyme intermediate; (2) Substrate proteins (potentially including signaling receptors or trafficking adaptors) are recruited to the Golgi membrane; (3) Palmitate is transferred to substrate cysteines, increasing hydrophobicity and driving membrane association or raft localization; (4) This modification facilitates transport from the Golgi to the plasma membrane, critical for polarized secretion in testis and neuronal tissues; (5) Reversal by LYPLA thioesterases allows dynamic regulation of protein localization in response to signals. Disruption of ZDHHC23 would cause defective protein palmitoylation, mislocalization of membrane proteins, impaired spermatogenesis, and altered signal transduction—phenotypes consistent with the developmental disorders observed in patients with 3q13 deletion syndromes.
