## Description
FSAF1 (C1orf131, Q8NDD1) encodes a 293 amino acid protein essential for the biogenesis of the 40S ribosomal small subunit. Despite its classification as an 'essential protein' by the Human Protein Atlas, FSAF1 is a genuine dark gene with ZERO publications in the Semantic Scholar database, indicating a complete lack of functional characterization in the literature.\n\nSTRUCTURAL FEATURES: InterPro analysis identifies two conserved domains: a core '40S small subunit processome assembly factor 1' domain (PF15375, residues 134-254; IPR027973) and a broader 'SSU Processome Component' family signature (IPR052852, residues 15-293). AlphaFold prediction yields a medium-confidence structure (global pLDDT 68.1), suggesting a partially folded protein with flexible regions typical of transient assembly factors that must undergo conformational changes during ribosome maturation.\n\nEXPRESSION & LOCALIZATION: HPA data shows ubiquitous expression across all tissues with low specificity, consistent with a housekeeping role in fundamental ribosome production. The protein is predicted to be intracellular, likely nucleolar given its involvement in the small-subunit (SSU) processome complex.\n\nDISEASE ASSOCIATION: ClinVar contains 40 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains and losses spanning 1q42.13-q44 that encompass the FSAF1 locus. While no specific monogenic disease phenotype is yet defined, the presence of these structural variants suggests that FSAF1 haploinsufficiency or dosage imbalance contributes to genomic disorders, potentially manifesting as developmental delays or ribosomopathies similar to other SSU processome defects (e.g., Treacher Collins syndrome).\n\nCONSERVATION: Orthologs are confirmed in mouse (Q8CIL4, 281 aa), rat (Q3KRF3, 282 aa), fission yeast (C3F10.08c), and platypus, indicating deep evolutionary conservation of this assembly factor from fungi to mammals.\n\nFUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: FSAF1 likely acts as a transient scaffold or chaperone within the 90S pre-ribosome (SSU processome), facilitating the correct folding and cleavage of 18S rRNA precursors. Its absence would halt 40S subunit production, triggering nucleolar stress and p53 activation. The complete lack of literature despite essentiality makes FSAF1 a high-priority target for experimental validation of its specific binding partners and mechanistic role in early ribosome assembly.
