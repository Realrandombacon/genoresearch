## Description
PRRG3 (Proline Rich and Gla Domain 3, also known as Transmembrane Gamma-Carboxyglutamic Acid Protein 3) encodes a 231-amino acid type I transmembrane protein (UniProt Q9BZD7) representing a genuine dark gene with only 5 publications despite its distinctive vitamin K-dependent architecture and emerging clinical significance. Structurally, PRRG3 possesses a defining N-terminal gamma-carboxyglutamic acid-rich (GLA) domain (IPR000294/PF00594/SM00069, residues 1-65) containing multiple vitamin K-dependent carboxylation sites (PS00011, residues 35-60; PS50998, residues 19-65) that enable high-affinity calcium ion binding (GO:0005509) and extracellular localization (GO:0005576); this GLA domain belongs to the coagulation factor-like superfamily (IPR017857/G3DSA:4.10.740.10, residues 20-64) and peptidase S1 family of coagulation factors (IPR050442/PTHR24278, residues 3-65), though PRRG3 lacks catalytic activity and functions as a receptor/adaptor rather than enzyme. AlphaFold predicts a medium-confidence structure (pLDDT 63.8 across all 231 residues), indicating a partially structured protein with ordered GLA domain embedded in more flexible/disordered C-terminal regions—characteristic of signaling adaptors that undergo conformational changes upon ligand binding. Functionally, Human Protein Atlas data reveals brain-enriched expression (11.0 nTPM in brain, 7.0 nTPM in epididymis, 'Tissue enhanced', 'Detected in many'), with low cancer specificity and low immune cell specificity, consistent with a neuronal development or synaptic function role; subcellular localization is predicted as membrane protein with extracellular GLA domain and intracellular proline-rich C-terminus. STRING interactions reveal connections to diverse functional partners: FGF23 (0.665), a phosphate-regulating hormone; NEUROD2 (0.665), a neurogenic differentiation transcription factor; LRRC69 (0.650), leucine-rich repeat protein; NEDD4L (0.625) and NEDD4 (0.612), HECT-type E3 ubiquitin ligases; NDFIP1 (0.613), Nedd4 family interacting protein; UCMA (0.585), upper zone cartilage matrix protein (another GLA domain protein); SSX3 (0.544), synovial sarcoma X breakpoint protein; GGCX (0.528), gamma-glutamyl carboxylase that modifies the GLA domain; and ZNF302 (0.478), zinc finger transcription factor—suggesting roles in neuronal development, ubiquitination-dependent trafficking, and vitamin K-dependent signaling. ClinVar documents 192 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large-scale copy number losses (e.g., chrX:89027698-156040895 x1, chrX:131207885-155233731 x1, chrX:113417246-155233731 x1) and gains (e.g., chrX:61882086-155233731 x3, chrX:139515884-153242568 x2) spanning chromosome Xq11.1-q28, plus splice site variants (e.g., c.7+2T>G); while no specific monogenic disease is formally linked solely to PRRG3 point mutations, the 2010 linkage study (cited 37x) mapped nonsyndromic high myopia to MYP1 locus on Xq, and recent 2022 research identified PRRG3 as a candidate prognostic gene in glioblastoma. Recent literature (5 papers total) confirms its understudied status but highlights emerging roles in ocular development and cancer. The mechanistic hypothesis: PRRG3 functions as a calcium-dependent transmembrane receptor/adaptor that: (1) In normal brain—binds extracellular ligands (potentially matrix proteins or growth factors) via its vitamin K-dependent GLA domain in a calcium-regulated manner, triggering intracellular signaling through its proline-rich C-terminus that recruits SH3-domain proteins or undergoes ubiquitination by NEDD4/NEDD4L for endocytic trafficking; (2) In eye development—regulates retinal or scleral extracellular matrix organization via GLA domain-mediated calcium binding, with loss-of-function contributing to axial elongation and high myopia (MYP1 locus); (3) In cancer—becomes dysregulated in glioblastoma where it may promote invasion via interactions with matrix proteins or growth factor pathways (FGF23 link); (4) The GGCX interaction suggests PRRG3 requires vitamin K-dependent gamma-carboxylation for function, linking it to vitamin K metabolism and potentially explaining why vitamin K deficiency affects bone/brain health. The medium-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 63.8) enables rational design of GLA domain-blocking antibodies. The 192 ClinVar CNVs suggest haploinsufficiency may contribute to X-linked neurodevelopmental disorders, myopia, or skeletal defects when PRRG3 is lost alongside neighboring genes. Future work should identify specific extracellular ligands via GLA domain pull-down assays; characterize PRRG3 knockout phenotypes in mice for ocular/neurological defects; determine whether PRRG3 requires vitamin K supplementation for proper folding/function; assess association of PRRG3-specific mutations with high myopia cohorts; investigate ubiquitination by NEDD4/NEDD4L and trafficking dynamics; explore conservation of GLA domain across vertebrates; test whether PRRG3 modulates FGF23 signaling in bone-brain axis; and solve crystal structure of GLA domain with calcium ions.
