## Description
ZNF277 (Gene ID: 64229, UniProt Q9NRM2) is a 450 amino acid Krüppel-associated box (KRAB)-less C2H2 zinc finger transcription factor located on chromosome 7q31.1 (NC_000007.14). InterPro domain analysis reveals a tandem array of three classical C2H2-type zinc finger domains (IPR013087/PF12756/SM00355) at positions 58-83, 173-197, and 224-253, plus additional superfamily matches extending to position 427, indicating an extended DNA-binding interface. The protein belongs to the specific ZNF277 family (IPR040048/PTHR13267, positions 31-449) and contains ZN622/Rei1/Reh1-type zinc finger motifs (IPR041661), suggesting evolutionary conservation of this specific finger architecture. Unlike many KRAB-ZFPs, ZNF277 lacks an N-terminal KRAB repression domain, implying it may function as a transcriptional activator or require alternative co-factor recruitment mechanisms. AlphaFold structure prediction (AF-Q9NRM2-F1) shows high global confidence (pLDDT 73.9), consistent with well-ordered zinc finger domains connected by flexible linkers, enabling adaptation to diverse DNA major groove sequences. Human Protein Atlas classifies ZNF277 as a transcription factor with DNA-binding molecular function involved in transcription and transcription regulation. Subcellular localization includes nucleoplasm (primary site of action) and plasma membrane (potentially indicating non-canonical roles or trafficking intermediates). Expression is ubiquitous (detected in all tissues) with low tissue and cancer specificity, but cell type-enhanced single-cell specificity suggests regulated expression in specific cellular contexts. STRING database identifies 10 interaction partners revealing unexpected connections to translation and immune regulation: GNGT1 (score 0.711, guanine nucleotide-binding protein G(T) subunit gamma-T1 - phototransduction/G-protein signaling), PDCD2 (0.700, programmed cell death 2 - nucleolar protein regulating ribosome biogenesis and apoptosis), RPS2 (0.633, ribosomal protein S2 - small subunit component), DOCK4 (0.588, dedicator of cytokinesis 4 - Rac/Cdc42 GEF regulating cell migration), IKZF1 (0.548, Ikaros family zinc finger 1 - lymphoid transcription factor), TMEM186 (0.495), STX19 (0.469, syntaxin 19 - vesicle trafficking), TMEM258 (0.466), RSRC2 (0.454, arginine/serine-rich coiled-coil 2 - splicing factor), and PDCD2L (0.447, PDCD2-like). The PDCD2-RPS2-IKZF1 cluster suggests ZNF277 may coordinate transcriptional programs with ribosome biogenesis and lymphoid development. ClinVar contains 23 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants affecting ZNF277: predominantly large copy number losses spanning 7q22-31 regions (ranging from ~4Mb deletions at chr7:106617406-123217914 to >20Mb losses), plus copy number gains and splice site mutations (c.801+1G>A), indicating dosage sensitivity. No specific diseases are associated with ZNF277 alone, but 7q31 deletions cause language disorders (FOXP2 locus adjacent), developmental delay, and Miller-Dieker-like syndromes. Conservation across vertebrates is implied by family classification. Functional hypothesis: ZNF277 functions as a sequence-specific transcriptional regulator recognizing distinct DNA motifs via its triple C2H2 finger array (~9bp recognition site). Absence of KRAB domain suggests it recruits activators (e.g., via proline-rich regions) or acts as a competitive inhibitor of repressive ZFPs. Interaction with PDCD2/RPS2 implies a role in coupling gene expression to ribosome production, potentially regulating growth-related genes. IKZF1 interaction suggests involvement in hematopoietic differentiation. Plasma membrane localization hints at potential non-nuclear functions, perhaps in signal transduction or mRNA transport. Disease mechanism: ZNF277 haploinsufficiency in 7q31 deletions disrupts transcriptional networks controlling neurodevelopment (language/cognition) and cell proliferation. Altered expression of target genes leads to developmental phenotypes. Splice site mutations likely cause exon skipping and loss of functional fingers. Priority experiments include ChIP-seq to define genomic binding sites, SELEX to determine DNA consensus sequence, reporter assays to test activation vs. repression activity, co-IP mass spectrometry to validate PDCD2/IKZF1 interactions, CRISPR knockout to assess effects on ribosome biogenesis and lymphoid differentiation, and investigation of plasma membrane association mechanism.
