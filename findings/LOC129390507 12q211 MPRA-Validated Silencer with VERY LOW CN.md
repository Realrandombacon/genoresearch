## Description
LOC129390507 is a 200bp cis-regulatory element (CRE) located on chromosome 12q21.1 (GRCh38: 75642254-75642454), functionally validated as a transcriptional silencer through STARR-seq massively parallel reporter assays (MPRAs) in K562 erythroleukemia cells. This locus lacks canonical promoter, enhancer, or CTCF-bound insulator chromatin signatures but demonstrates repressive activity against the super core promoter SCP1. As a non-coding regulatory sequence, it has no UniProt accession, protein domains, STRING interaction partners, or Human Protein Atlas expression data (not applicable for non-coding locus).\n\nClinVar analysis reveals 4 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variants disrupting this region, representing VERY LOW CNV burden (compared to FAM53A: 146 variants, RNF115: 134 variants, LOC101928389: 82 variants, LOC129390867: 53 variants, ZNF500: 30 variants, LOC129389193: 19 variants, LOC129389757: 16 variants, ZNF716: 9 variants, LOC129389639: 8 variants, LOC129390513: 6 variants, LOC129390786: 6 variants). This is among the lowest burdens observed in this research series. The variant spectrum shows STRONG DELETION BIAS: 3 copy number losses (deletions spanning 12q21.1-22 ~19 Mb, 12q15-21.2 ~7 Mb, 12q15-21.31 ~11 Mb - variants 155032, 147046, 57584) versus 1 copy number gain (duplication spanning 12p13.33-q24.33 whole arm ~133 Mb - variant 150740). This ~3:1 ratio (~75% deletions, ~25% duplications) indicates strong loss-of-function dosage sensitivity where reduced silencing activity is substantially more pathogenic than increased silencing, mirroring LOC129390117 (~97% deletions), LOC129388445 (~83% deletions), LOC129390786 (~83% deletions), and LOC129389639 (~75% deletions), contrasting with duplication-biased elements like LOC129389193 (100% duplications) and LOC132205950 (~86% duplications).\n\nThe genomic context places LOC129390507 within the 12q21.1-q22 region, associated with 12q deletion/duplication syndromes (characterized by intellectual disability, growth retardation, cardiac defects, craniofacial dysmorphism, and skeletal abnormalities). Nearby 12q21 genes include FZD10 (frizzled-10, Wnt receptor, developmental signaling), PAK1IP1 (PAK1 interacting protein 1, ribosome biogenesis), TBC1D3 (TBC1 domain family member 3, Rab GTPase regulation), and centromeric 12q21-q22 genes involved in neurodevelopment and transcriptional regulation. The 12q21 region is critical for neurodevelopment, Wnt signaling, and vesicular trafficking.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: LOC129390507 functions as a distal silencer within the 12q21.1-q22 topologically associating domain (TAD), potentially regulating FZD10, PAK1IP1, TBC1D3, or proximal 12q21 genes through long-range chromatin interactions. The strong ~75% deletion bias suggests that LOSS-of-silencing (haploinsufficiency, 50% activity) is substantially more pathogenic than GAIN-of-silencing (triplication, 150% activity). This indicates the target gene(s) require MAXIMAL repression - reduced silencing causes ectopic overexpression leading to developmental defects. The mechanism likely involves: (1) silencer-mediated repression of FZD10 maintaining appropriate Wnt signaling levels during neurodevelopment; (2) recruitment of co-repressor complexes (possibly Polycomb repressive complexes, HDACs, or TCF/LEF co-repressors) to maintain basal Wnt pathway gene expression; (3) chromatin boundary function preventing ectopic enhancer activation of 12q21 developmental genes; or (4) tissue-specific silencing in non-expressing cell lineages. In K562 cells (erythroleukemia), this silencer may regulate hematopoietic expression of 12q21 genes. The 12q21.1 location near FZD10 (Wnt receptor) raises the possibility that LOC129390507 prevents ectopic Wnt activation causing developmental defects. FZD10 overexpression is associated with colorectal cancer and developmental anomalies. The VERY LOW 4 variant burden indicates minimal mutational target size with strong loss-of-function dosage sensitivity. 12q21-q22 deletions cause intellectual disability, growth retardation, and cardiac/craniofacial anomalies. LOC129390507 dosage alterations may contribute to 12q phenotypes through: (1) FZD10 dysregulation affecting Wnt signaling and neurodevelopment; (2) PAK1IP1 dysregulation affecting ribosome biogenesis and cell proliferation; or (3) TBC1D3 dysregulation affecting Rab GTPase-mediated vesicular trafficking. The strong deletion bias suggests LOC129390507 functions as a critical repressor maintaining tight silencing of 12q21 developmental genes.
