## Description
GSTT2 (Gene ID: 2953) encodes a 244 amino acid glutathione S-transferase theta-2 (UniProt P0CG29) located at chromosome 22q11.23. This gene is annotated as 'gene/pseudogene' reflecting known population polymorphism — some individuals carry functional alleles while others have gene deletions (null genotype), similar to the well-characterized GSTT1 polymorphism. This creates interindividual variability in detoxification capacity.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals comprehensive theta-class GST domain architecture: IPR004045 (GST N-terminal, aa 1-82), IPR004046 (GST C-terminal, aa 128-198), IPR040075 (GST Theta N-terminal, aa 3-78), IPR040077 (GST Theta C-terminal, aa 91-216), IPR040079 (glutathione transferase family, aa 2-224), IPR051369 (GST Theta family, aa 1-223), PF00043 (GST family), cd03050 (GST_N_Theta, aa 3-78), cd03183 (Theta C-terminal helical domain, aa 91-216), and G3DSA superfamily domains (1.20.1050.10, 3.40.30.10). The thioredoxin-like fold (IPR036249) is characteristic of GST catalytic domains.\n\nAlphaFold predicts an exceptionally confident 3D structure (pLDDT 97.6), indicating a well-folded, stable enzyme with the canonical GST bipartite structure: N-terminal thioredoxin-like domain containing the glutathione-binding site (G-site) and C-terminal alpha-helical domain forming the substrate-binding pocket (H-site).\n\nSTRING reveals 10 high-confidence interaction partners in xenobiotic metabolism pathways: HPGDS (0.961), CYP1A1 (0.954), GSTP1 (0.947), EPHX1 (0.944), CYP2E1 (0.932), CYP1B1 (0.931), PRDX6 (0.928), CYP1A2 (0.927), GPX4 (0.925), and GSR (0.450). This network places GSTT2 within Phase II detoxification alongside cytochrome P450 enzymes (Phase I) and epoxide hydrolases.\n\nClinVar reports 66 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly copy number gains at 22q11.23, though no specific disease associations are established. These CNVs likely reflect the complex genomic architecture of this region rather than direct pathogenicity.\n\nFunctional hypothesis: GSTT2 catalyzes glutathione conjugation of electrophilic compounds including halogenated hydrocarbons, epoxides, and oxidative stress products. Population-level gene deletion polymorphism creates variable detoxification capacity, potentially influencing cancer susceptibility, drug metabolism, and environmental toxin sensitivity. The enzyme likely functions as a homodimer in cytosol, cooperating with CYP enzymes for complete xenobiotic clearance.
