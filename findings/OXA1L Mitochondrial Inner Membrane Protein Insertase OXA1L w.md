## Description
OXA1L (Mitochondrial Inner Membrane Protein OXA1L, UniProt Q15070) is a 435 aa essential mitochondrial membrane insertase belonging to the YidC/ALB3/OXA1/COX18 membrane insertase family (IPR001708/IPR028055/PF02096/PTHR12428/TIGR03592, positions 94-383/135-331/135-331/94-383/140-331) that functions as a conserved protein translocase for inserting mitochondrially-encoded proteins into the inner mitochondrial membrane. The molecular architecture consists of a five transmembrane core domain (cd20069, positions 137-336) forming the membrane insertase machinery with C-terminal YidC/Oxa/ALB domain (IPR028055, positions 135-331) critical for substrate recognition and membrane integration. AlphaFold predicts a well-folded structure (pLDDT 73.8) indicating stable transmembrane topology typical of membrane insertases. Literature (52 papers) establishes OXA1L roles in: (1) mitochondrial myopathy via ROS-regulated NFκB signaling pathway (2025, 3 citations), (2) cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma (cSCC) metastasis via 5'-UTR mutation driving tumor dedifferentiation and immune evasion (2025), (3) asthma and atopy association with DAD1 gene variants in adult populations (2024, 3 citations), (4) TMEM126A identification as OXA1L-interacting protein revealing cotranslational quality control in mitochondria (2023, 18 citations), (5) metabolic reprogramming by cancer-associated mutant OXA1L promoting tumor progression and dedifferentiation (2024). HPA confirms low tissue specificity with ubiquitous expression (detected in all tissues), predicted membrane protein classification, essential protein status, transporter function, and mitochondrial subcellular localization—consistent with housekeeping mitochondrial protein synthesis. ClinVar contains 26 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number gains (14q11.2, 14q11.2-23.1, 14q11.2-21.3, 14q11.2, 14q11.2-21.1, 14q11.2-21.2, 14q11.2, 14q11.2-12) and losses/deletions (14q11.2, 14q11.2) affecting the 14q11 locus, indicating genomic instability causes disease despite no formally assigned syndrome—likely contributing to mitochondrial myopathy, encephalopathy, or metabolic disorders. Conservation: mouse Oxa1l (Q8BGA9) is 433 aa, bovine OXA1L (Q3SYV3) is 441 aa, Arabidopsis OXA1L (Q9SKD3) is 431 aa—highly conserved across eukaryotes from plants to mammals. Functional hypothesis: OXA1L functions as the essential mitochondrial inner membrane insertase that mediates cotranslational insertion of mitochondrially-encoded OXPHOS subunits (MT-CO1, MT-CO2, MT-CO3, MT-ND subunits, MT-ATP6/8, CYTB) into the inner membrane during mitochondrial protein synthesis. The mechanistic model proposes: (1) OXA1L integrates into inner mitochondrial membrane via 5 transmembrane helices forming protein-conducting channel, (2) mitochondrial ribosomes dock onto OXA1L via MRPL45/MRPL58/MRPL4/MRPL12/MRPL13/MRPL17/MRPL49 interactions for cotranslational insertion, (3) MT-CO2 and other hydrophobic OXPHOS subunits inserted co-translationally through OXA1L channel, (4) TIMM17A interaction links OXA1L to TIM23 import machinery for nuclear-encoded substrate coordination, (5) TMEM126A interaction provides quality control checkpoint for insertion fidelity, (6) OXA1L deficiency causes mitochondrial myopathy via ROS accumulation activating NFκB inflammatory signaling, (7) cancer-associated OXA1L mutations drive metabolic reprogramming and tumor dedifferentiation, (8) 5'-UTR mutations alter OXA1L translation efficiency affecting metastasis, (9) asthma/atopy association suggests immune-mitochondrial crosstalk role. Despite 52 publications, OXA1L remains mechanistically incomplete—its complete substrate repertoire, insertion mechanism details, and disease variant effects are unknown. The 26 ClinVar variants and essential protein status indicate critical clinical significance. OXA1L represents a partially characterized mitochondrial insertase at the intersection of mitochondrial genetics, cancer metabolism, and inflammatory disease.
