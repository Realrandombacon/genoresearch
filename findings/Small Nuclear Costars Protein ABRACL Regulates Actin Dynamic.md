## Description
ABRACL (ABRA C-terminal Like, also known as Costars family protein) is a small 81 aa nuclear and cytosolic protein functioning as a potential regulator of actin dynamics and transcriptional programs, with emerging roles in glioma, gastric cancer, renal cell carcinoma, and neurodevelopment. InterPro analysis confirms ABRACL as a canonical member of the Costars family: it contains a single Costars domain (IPR027817/PF14705, aa 2-79) belonging to the Costars superfamily (IPR038095/G3DSA:1.10.10.1540, aa 1-81), which shares structural homology with the C-terminal domain of ABRA (Actin-Binding Rho Activating protein). This domain architecture suggests ABRACL functions as a small winged-helix fold protein capable of binding actin or regulating actin-associated signaling. AlphaFold predicts an exceptionally high-confidence structure (pLDDT 94.8), indicating a rigid, well-folded globular domain despite its small size, consistent with a stable scaffold or interaction module. STRING interactions place ABRACL in networks involving SMIM18 (0.554, small integral membrane protein), IRGQ (0.494, immunity-related GTPase), TTC9B (0.468, tetratricopeptide repeat protein), and notably MEF2C (0.415, Myocyte Enhancer Factor 2C), a critical transcription factor for neuronal development and muscle differentiation, suggesting a link to transcriptional regulation. HPA shows ubiquitous expression (detected in all tissues) with low tissue and cancer specificity, subcellular localization to nucleoplasm and cytosol, and classification as a plasma and intracellular protein, supporting dual roles in signaling and gene regulation. ClinVar contains 13 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains and losses on chromosome 6q (6q23.2-24.2, 6q22.31-25.3), though no specific ABRACL-associated Mendelian disease is definitively established; these CNVs likely contribute to 6q deletion/duplication syndrome phenotypes including intellectual disability and developmental delay. Literature (19 papers) establishes ABRACL as: upregulated by transcription factor CBX4 to promote gastric cancer proliferation and migration while inhibiting apoptosis (2024); driven by MYBL2 to enhance proliferation, invasion, and migration in cancers (2022); modulated by the LncRNA DRAIC/miR-145-3p axis in renal cell carcinoma progression (2025); and highly expressed in glioma where it serves as a potential therapeutic target (2024). Additionally, ABRACL-expressing cell populations have been characterized in the embryonic mammalian telencephalon (2023), implicating it in brain development.
