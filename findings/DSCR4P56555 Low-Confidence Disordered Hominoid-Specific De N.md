## Description
DSCR4 (Down syndrome critical region protein 4, also known as DCRB or DSCRB) is a 118 amino acid small intracellular protein (UniProt P56555) with low-confidence predominantly disordered architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 46.1), characteristic of intrinsically disordered proteins (IDPs) that function through dynamic protein-protein interactions rather than stable enzymatic folds. No InterPro domains were detected, confirming DSCR4 lacks conserved structural motifs and represents a lineage-specific orphan protein. The gene is located at chromosome 21q22.13 within the Down syndrome critical region (DSCR), transcribed from a bidirectional endogenous retrovirus (ERV) long terminal repeat (LTR) promoter (PMID:16288839), representing a remarkable example of de novo gene birth from retroviral insertion. DSCR4 is hominoid-specific, present only in humans and great apes (PMID:33762515, PMID:27289096), with no orthologs in mice or other mammals (BLAST confirms 100% identity only to human sequences). Molecular functions remain uncharacterized, but the ERV-derived promoter suggests potential roles in retroviral defense or placental development.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis returned no protein partners, which is typical for small, lineage-specific IDPs that may interact transiently with chromatin or RNA rather than forming stable complexes. The absence of detectable interactions suggests DSCR4 functions as a monomeric regulatory factor, possibly through disordered region-mediated binding to transcription factors or epigenetic modifiers.\n\nHPA expression data is unavailable, indicating DSCR4 is either lowly expressed, restricted to specific developmental stages, or not captured by standard HPA antibodies. Literature suggests expression in placenta and potentially neural tissues, consistent with its location in the Down syndrome critical region and ERV-derived promoter activity.\n\nClinVar documents 70 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, exclusively large copy number gains and losses affecting chromosome 21q (21q11.2-22.3 duplications/trisomies, 21q22.11-22.3 deletions). All variants are associated with chromosome 21 dosage abnormalities, primarily Down syndrome (trisomy 21). The variant burden indicates extreme dosage sensitivity - DSCR4 overexpression (3 copies in trisomy 21) likely contributes to Down syndrome phenotypes including intellectual disability, craniofacial abnormalities, and congenital heart defects. The gene's emergence in hominoids suggests it may contribute to human-specific traits such as enhanced cognitive abilities, which when triplicated cause neurodevelopmental dysfunction.\n\nConservation is restricted to hominoids: human (P56555, 118 aa), chimpanzee, gorilla, orangutan (UniProt shows primate isoforms), with no orthologs in mice, rats, or zebrafish. This confirms DSCR4 emerged ~25-40 million years ago in the hominoid lineage.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: DSCR4 represents a de novo gene born from an ERV-LTR insertion that acquired protein-coding capacity in the hominoid ancestor. The disordered 118 aa sequence may function as a transcriptional co-regulator or chromatin modifier, potentially interacting with neural development genes in the DSCR. In normal diploid cells, DSCR4 fine-tunes expression of neighboring genes (e.g., DSCR1/RCAN1, COL6A1) involved in synaptic plasticity and neurogenesis. In trisomy 21, DSCR4 overexpression disrupts this balance, contributing to Down syndrome cognitive phenotypes. The ERV-derived promoter may confer placenta-specific expression, suggesting additional roles in pregnancy. DSCR4 exemplifies how retroviral insertions can drive evolutionary innovation, creating human-specific regulatory networks. Therapeutic targeting of DSCR4 may ameliorate specific Down syndrome features without affecting essential chromosome 21 genes.
