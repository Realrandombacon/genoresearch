## Description
YME1L1 encodes a 773 aa integral membrane ATP-dependent zinc metalloprotease (UniProt Q96TA2), also known as FTSH, MEG4, OPA11, or PAMP, that functions as the human ortholog of yeast Yme1p within the inner mitochondrial membrane (IMM). As an i-AAA (intermembrane space-facing AAA) protease, it forms hexameric complexes essential for protein quality control, regulated intramembrane proteolysis, and mitochondrial dynamics. The protein features a canonical FtsH architecture: an N-terminal transmembrane anchor; a central AAA+ ATPase domain (IPR003593/IPR003959/IPR003960/IPR027417/cd01851/G3DSA:3.40.50.300, residues 371-508/375-505/476-494/321-506) providing the energy for substrate unfolding and translocation; and a C-terminal peptidase M41 zinc metalloprotease domain (IPR000642/IPR005936/cd19501/G3DSA:1.20.58.760, residues 588-762/11-773/336-504/581-772) containing the HEXXH zinc-binding motif for catalytic cleavage. STRING interactions place YME1L1 at the core of mitochondrial proteostasis and respiratory chain assembly: TIMMDC1 (0.994) and NDUFA13 (0.994), critical factors for Complex I assembly; PARL (0.951) and PARL-2 (0.902), rhomboid proteases coordinating IMM proteolysis; OMA1 (0.940), the complementary m-AAA protease regulating stress-induced OPA1 cleavage; STOML2 (0.857) and PHB2 (0.841), stomatin-like and prohibitin proteins forming scaffolds for YME1L1/OMA1 activity; LONP1 (0.827), matrix AAA protease; and CLPP (0.542)/CLPX (0.499), matrix Clp protease system. HPA confirms ubiquitous expression (detected in all tissues) with mitochondrial and nuclear body localization, annotated as hydrolase/metalloprotease with disease variant involvement. ClinVar lists 26 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including missense mutations (p.Arg149Gln, p.His546Asn, p.Leu667Val, p.Lys89Ile, p.His94Arg) and splice variants (c.2007+3A>T, c.1102+3A>G), associated with 3-methylglutaconic aciduria (3-MGCA)—a disorder of mitochondrial energy metabolism—and recent studies (2025) link dysfunction to acute kidney injury via SREBP1c-mediated repression. AlphaFold structure prediction yields high confidence (global pLDDT 71.1), revealing a well-folded AAA+ ring and protease domain consistent with active hexameric assembly. Mechanistically, YME1L1 functions through multiple integrated modes: (1) OPA1 processing—cleaves long isoforms of OPA1 to regulate mitochondrial fusion and cristae junction tightness, balancing against OMA1-mediated stress cleavage; (2) Complex I assembly—stabilizes TIMMDC1 and NDUFA13 to ensure proper NADH dehydrogenase assembly and prevent respiratory deficiency; (3) Protein quality control—degrades misfolded or damaged IMM proteins to maintain proteostasis; (4) Metabolic regulation—loss of function leads to 3-MGCA biochemistry (elevated 3-methylglutaconic acid) due to impaired mitochondrial metabolism; (5) Disease pathogenesis—mutations cause cristae disorganization, fragmented mitochondria, reduced oxidative phosphorylation, and tissue-specific vulnerabilities (renal tubular cells, neurons). Disruption of YME1L1 causes severe mitochondrial fragmentation, loss of membrane potential, defective respiration, and cell death—phenotypes observed in patient-derived cells and mouse models.
