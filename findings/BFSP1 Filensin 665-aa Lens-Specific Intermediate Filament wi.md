## Description
BFSP1 (Beaded Filament Structural Protein 1, alias Filensin) is a partially characterized dark gene with only 63 publications, representing a critical structural component of the eye lens cytoskeleton. UniProt accession Q12934 identifies it as a 665-amino acid intracellular protein. InterPro scan reveals a canonical intermediate filament architecture: a central rod domain (IPR039008/PF00038, residues 39-320) responsible for coiled-coil dimerization, flanked by a unique N-terminal head domain with homology to vasodilator-stimulated phosphoprotein (G3DSA:1.20.5.1160, residues 54-188) and a C-terminal tail. This domain arrangement is specific to the 'beaded filament' system found exclusively in vertebrate lenses.\n\nAlphaFold predicts a medium-confidence structure (global pLDDT 63.8), consistent with the known properties of intermediate filaments which possess a structured alpha-helical rod core surrounded by intrinsically disordered head and tail regions. The lower confidence in the terminal regions suggests high flexibility, essential for the dynamic assembly of beaded filaments and their interaction with diverse lens crystallins.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals 10 partners with high-to-medium confidence, defining a functional network centered on lens transparency and refraction: MIP (0.940 - Major Intrinsic Protein/Aquaporin-0, the most abundant lens membrane protein), CRYBB1 (0.859 - Beta-crystallin B1), GJA8 (0.812 - Connexin 50), GJA3 (0.806 - Connexin 46), CRYGS (0.803 - Gamma-crystallin S), BFSP2 (0.785 - CP49, the obligate heteropolymeric partner of Filensin), CRYAA (0.743 - Alpha-crystallin A), LIM2 (0.694 - Lens intrinsic membrane protein 2), CRYBB3 (0.526), and CRYBA1 (0.445). The striking cluster of crystallins (CRYAA, CRYBB1, CRYGS) and membrane proteins (MIP, GJA3/8) confirms BFSP1's role as a scaffold that organizes the dense packing of lens proteins and anchors them to the fiber cell membrane.\n\nHuman Protein Atlas classifies BFSP1 as an eye lens protein involved in cataract, localized to the plasma membrane and cytosol. While RNA tissue specificity is listed as 'Low', the protein is functionally restricted to lens fiber cells, where it forms the core of beaded filaments alongside BFSP2.\n\nClinVar reports 43 pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants, including truncating mutations (e.g., p.Cys131Ter, p.Ala42fs), missense changes (p.His65Arg, p.Phe484Leu, p.Lys398Asn), and large copy number deletions/duplications on chromosome 20. These variants are directly linked to autosomal recessive juvenile cataract, as confirmed by recent literature describing novel mega-deletions and specific point mutations (e.g., D348N) in consanguineous families.\n\nLiterature (63 papers) highlights three key contexts: (1) Cataract Genetics—novel mega-deletions and missense mutations in BFSP1 cause juvenile cataract (2026, 0 citations; (2) Crystallin scaffolding—interactions with CRYAA (0.743) and CRYBB1 (0.859) suggest BFSP1 organizes crystallins into short-range ordered arrays to minimize light scattering; (3) Membrane anchoring—binding to MIP (0.940) and connexins (GJA3/8) tethers the cytoskeleton to the fiber cell membrane, maintaining cell shape and mechanical stability; The combination of a unique IF domain architecture, specific lens interactome, high variant burden causing cataract, and emerging roles in cancer/oocytes positions BFSP1 as a high-priority candidate for functional characterization in vision science and cytoskeletal biology.
