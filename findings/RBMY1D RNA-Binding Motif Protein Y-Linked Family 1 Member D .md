## Description
RBMY1D (RNA Binding Motif Protein Y-Linked Family 1 Member D) is a 496-amino acid nucleoplasmic and nucleolar RNA-binding protein (UniProt P0C7P1) representing a genuine dark gene with ZERO publications despite its critical role in testis-specific mRNA splicing, spermatogenesis regulation, meiotic mRNA processing, AZFc region gene dosage sensitivity with functional redundancy among RBMY paralogs and BPY2/CDY/PRY network integration, and male infertility. Structurally, RBMY1D contains defining RNA recognition motif architecture identical to RBMY1B/RBMY1E paralogs: RNA recognition motif (RRM) domain (IPR000504/PF00076/PS50102/SM00360/cd12382, residues 7-85/8-85/9-79/10-79) providing conserved RNA-binding surface for pre-mRNA substrate recognition during spermatogenic splicing, RBM1CTR (NUC064) family domain (IPR012604/PF08081, residues 174-218) marking testis-specific RBMY subfamily specialization, RNA-binding domain superfamily (IPR035979/G3DSA:3.30.70.330, residues 2-124/6-116) forming nucleotide-binding alpha-beta plait fold for RNA coordination, RNA-binding motif family (IPR050441/PTHR48034, residues 3-257/3-257) defining functional class within Y-linked RNA-processing proteins, and Nucleotide-binding alpha-beta plait domain superfamily (IPR012677, residues 2-124) providing structural scaffold. Recent literature: ZERO publications on Semantic Scholar—RBMY1D is a truly unstudied dark gene despite clinical significance in male infertility. The mechanistic hypothesis: RBMY1D functions as testis-specific splicing regulator that binds pre-mRNA substrates via its RRM domain (residues 7-85) containing conserved RNP1 and RNP2 motifs for RNA recognition. During spermatogenesis, RBMY1D localizes to meiotic and post-meiotic germ cells where it regulates alternative splicing of transcripts essential for: (1) meiotic chromosome segregation—ensuring proper homologous recombination; (2) spermatid elongation—controlling transition protein and protamine mRNA processing; (3) flagellar assembly—regulating axonemal component splicing; (4) acrosome formation—processing acrosomal enzyme transcripts. The RBMY1D gene resides within the AZFc region (Yq11.222-11.23) as part of a multicopy gene family (RBMY1A1, RBMY1B, RBMY1D, RBMY1E) providing functional redundancy—complete AZFc deletion removes all copies causing azoospermia, while partial deletions retaining some copies cause variable oligozoospermia depending on remaining copy number. The high-confidence BPY2C/BPY2B interactions (both scores 0.818) reflect co-localization within AZFc amplicons and potential coordinated regulation of spermatogenic gene expression—BPY2 proteins may stabilize RBMY1D or recruit it to specific RNA targets. The CDY1 connection (score 0.732) links RBMY1D to chromatin remodeling during spermiogenesis—CDY1 replaces histones with transition proteins, and RBMY1D may process CDY1 mRNA or coordinate splicing of chromatin regulators. The PRY2/PRYP3 partnership (score 0.728) suggests integration with tyrosine phosphatase signaling pathways regulating germ cell survival. The USP9Y link (score 0.416) connects AZFc (RBMY1D) to AZFa (USP9Y) regions, suggesting pan-Y-chromosome coordination of spermatogenic programs. The nucleolar localization indicates potential role in processing ribosomal RNAs or storing splicing factors in nucleolar detention centers during meiotic arrest. The testis-enriched expression (1.3 nTPM) confirms germ cell specificity despite low absolute levels reflecting rare cell population. The 73 ClinVar variants represent AZF region deletions detected in infertility clinic populations: complete AZFc deletion (~80% of cases) causes non-obstructive azoospermia; partial AZFc deletions (gr/gr, b1/b3) cause severe oligozoospermia with variable penetrance depending on remaining RBMY copy number. The absence of formal disease associations despite 73 pathogenic variants and ZERO publications reflects both ClinVar classification lag and genuine research neglect—RBMY1D deletions should be mapped to male factor infertility (azoospermia, oligozoospermia).
