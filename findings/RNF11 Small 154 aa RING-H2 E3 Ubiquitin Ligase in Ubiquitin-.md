## Description
RNF11 (Q9Y3C5, 154 aa) is a compact RING-H2 finger E3 ubiquitin-protein ligase functioning as a core component of the ubiquitin-editing complex (IPR052804, PTHR46359). InterPro identifies a canonical RING-H2 finger domain (IPR001841/PF13639, aa 98-139; cd16468, aa 98-140) with C3HC4 zinc-coordinating residues essential for E2 ubiquitin-conjugating enzyme binding. AlphaFold predicts a confident structure (pLDDT 78.4, residues 1-154) with the RING domain properly folded for catalytic activity. STRING reveals 10 exceptionally high-confidence interactions forming a coherent ubiquitination network: TAX1BP1 (0.997), ITCH (0.995), SMURF2 (0.982), TNFAIP3/A20 (0.966), UBE2N (0.946), STAMBP (0.879), EPS15 (0.877), WWP1 (0.867), UBE2D1 (0.852), and STAM2 (0.823). HPA shows ubiquitous expression across all tissues with immune cell enhancement, consistent with roles in inflammatory signaling regulation. ClinVar contains 8 pathogenic variants, predominantly copy number variations (deletions and duplications) at 1p32.3, though no specific diseases are yet assigned. Functional hypothesis: RNF11 serves as a critical regulatory hub in the A20 ubiquitin-editing complex, coordinating with TAX1BP1 and ITCH to terminate NF-κB signaling by promoting ubiquitination and degradation of signaling intermediates. The near-perfect STRING scores (TAX1BP1 0.997, ITCH 0.995) indicate obligate complex formation. RNF11 likely recruits E2 enzymes (UBE2N, UBE2D1) to catalyze K48-linked polyubiquitination of substrates, while its interaction with deubiquitinase TNFAIP3 suggests dynamic editing of ubiquitin chains. This positions RNF11 as a molecular switch controlling inflammation, endocytic trafficking (EPS15, STAM2 interactions), and potentially oncogenic RET signaling (per gene_info). Disruption via 1p32.3 CNVs may contribute to immune dysregulation and cancer susceptibility.
