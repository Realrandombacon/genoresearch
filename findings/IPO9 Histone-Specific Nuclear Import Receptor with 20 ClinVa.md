## Description
IPO9 (Importin-9; UniProt Q96P70) is a 1041 amino acid karyopherin-β family nuclear import receptor with only 30 publications, classifying it as a genuine dark gene despite essential roles in histone nuclear import, nuclear actin dynamics, ferroptosis regulation, and ovarian cancer progression.\n\nSTRUCTURAL ARCHITECTURE: InterPro analysis reveals canonical importin-β architecture with an N-terminal importin-β domain (IPR001494/PF03810/SM00913, residues 43-119) essential for RanGTP binding and cargo release regulation. The protein contains extensive HEAT repeat architecture (IPR056840/PF25018, residues 355-604) forming the elongated superhelical scaffold characteristic of karyopherins. TPR-like repeats (IPR058669/PF25758, residues 627-881) contribute additional binding surfaces. The entire protein adopts an armadillo-like helical fold (IPR011989/IPR016024/G3DSA:1.25.10.10, residues 23-1039) optimized for flexible cargo recognition. AlphaFold predicts high confidence structure (global pLDDT 88.2), indicating a well-folded import receptor capable of conformational changes during the transport cycle.\n\nINTERACTION NETWORK: STRING-DB identifies ultra-high confidence interactions exclusively with core histones: H2AC8 (0.922), H2BC11 (0.921), H2AC17 (0.918), H2AC6 (0.916), H2AC13 (0.915), H2AC16 (0.915), H2AC4 (0.915), H2AC15 (0.915), H2AC11 (0.910), plus IPO7 (0.907, importin-7 paralog). This exclusive histone interaction profile establishes IPO9 as a specialized nuclear import receptor for histone H2A-H2B dimers, critical for chromatin assembly during DNA replication and repair.\n\nEXPRESSION & LOCALIZATION: HPA shows ubiquitous expression detected in all tissues with low tissue specificity, consistent with fundamental requirements for chromatin assembly in all proliferating cells. Subcellular localization includes nucleoplasm and cytosol, reflecting shuttling between compartments during nuclear import cycles. Classified as an essential transporter/chaperone involved in protein transport.\n\nDISEASE LANDSCAPE: ClinVar contains 20 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number gains/losses at 1q21-32 and coding mutations such as p.Glu980Ter (nonsense truncation). No monogenic disease is yet definitively assigned, but 1q deletions/amplifications cause developmental anomalies. Recent 2026 literature establishes IPO9 as an oncogene in ovarian cancer where it suppresses HMOX1-dependent ferroptosis, promoting tumor progression and chemoresistance. Additional studies reveal IPO9 competes with cofilin and profilin for nuclear actin binding, regulating nuclear F-actin assembly during ferroptosis and DNA damage response.\n\nCONSERVATION: Orthologs confirmed in mouse (Q91YE6, 1041 aa) and Drosophila (Q9VGP4, 1018 aa), indicating extreme evolutionary constraint on the histone import function across metazoans.\n\nFUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: IPO9 functions as a RanGTP-regulated nuclear import receptor specifically recognizing histone H2A-H2B dimers via their nuclear localization signals, transporting them from cytoplasm to nucleoplasm for chromatin assembly. The HEAT-repeat superhelix undergoes conformational changes upon RanGTP binding in the nucleus, triggering histone release for nucleosome assembly. In cancer contexts, IPO9 overexpression suppresses ferroptosis by maintaining histone supply for DNA repair and potentially regulating HMOX1 expression, enabling tumor survival under oxidative stress. The competition with cofilin/profilin for nuclear actin suggests IPO9 also coordinates cytoskeletal-chromatin coupling during cell division and DNA damage response. The 20 ClinVar variants warrant investigation for haploinsufficiency effects on neurodevelopment and cancer predisposition.
