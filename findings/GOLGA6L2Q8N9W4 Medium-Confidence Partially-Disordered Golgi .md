## Description
GOLGA6L2 (Golgin A6 family like 2, also known as CT105) is a 909 amino acid large intracellular protein localized to the cis-Golgi network (UniProt Q8N9W4) with medium-confidence partially-disordered architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 59.5), characteristic of golgin family proteins that function as extended coiled-coil tethers. The protein defines the Golgin subfamily A member 6-like family (IPR026737/PTHR23143, residues 4-428), which shares homology with trichohyalin-related proteins. Golgins are structural scaffolds that maintain Golgi architecture and mediate vesicle tethering before SNARE-mediated fusion. The N-terminal region likely contains coiled-coil domains for dimerization and Golgi membrane anchoring, while the C-terminal region may interact with vesicle-associated proteins. Molecular functions include intracellular protein transport, Golgi organization, and vesicle tethering, placing GOLGA6L2 within the secretory pathway.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals a moderate network of Golgi and mitotic proteins: interactions with TEX55 (0.522, Testis-expressed protein 55), NUF2 (0.504, NDC80 kinetochore complex component), STX5 (0.468, Syntaxin-5, Golgi SNARE), GOSR1 (0.445, Golgi SNAP receptor complex member 1), and FAM186A (0.447). The interaction with STX5 and GOSR1 confirms GOLGA6L2's role in Golgi vesicle trafficking, while the link to NUF2 suggests potential involvement in mitotic Golgi fragmentation/reassembly. TEX55 interaction indicates testis-specific functions, possibly in acrosome biogenesis during spermatogenesis.\n\nHPA expression reveals testis-enriched pattern (46.8 nTPM) with detection in some tissues. This restricted expression suggests GOLGA6L2 has specialized functions in male germ cell development, potentially in acrosome formation or sperm maturation. The cis-Golgi localization aligns with roles in processing secretory proteins required for spermatogenesis. Not detected in cancer or immune cells indicates physiological rather than pathological roles.\n\nClinVar documents 275 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, exclusively large copy number gains and losses affecting chromosome 15q11.2-13.1 (Prader-Willi/Angelman syndrome critical region). Variants include 15q11.2 duplications, 15q11.2-13.1 deletions, and 15q11.2 copy number losses. The variant burden indicates extreme dosage sensitivity. While no specific Mendelian disease is annotated, GOLGA6L2 disruption likely contributes to 15q11.2 microdeletion syndrome (Burnside-Butler syndrome) phenotypes including developmental delay, autism spectrum disorder, and schizophrenia susceptibility. Golgi dysfunction in this region may impair neuronal protein trafficking.\n\nConservation is restricted to primates: human (Q8N9W4, 909 aa), with limited orthologs in other mammals, suggesting GOLGA6L2 emerged as a primate-specific golgin paralog through gene duplication from GOLGA6.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: GOLGA6L2 functions as a Golgi tethering factor that captures incoming vesicles and positions them for SNARE-mediated fusion. The coiled-coil domain extends from the Golgi membrane to capture vesicles carrying cargo proteins. In testis, GOLGA6L2 may specifically tether vesicles containing acrosomal enzymes or sperm surface proteins. During mitosis, interaction with NUF2 suggests GOLGA6L2 participates in Golgi fragmentation and post-mitotic reassembly. Loss of GOLGA6L2 impairs Golgi structure and secretory trafficking, leading to neuronal dysfunction in 15q11.2 deletion syndromes. The primate-specific emergence suggests GOLGA6L2 may contribute to human-specific traits in brain development or reproduction. GOLGA6L2 represents a potential biomarker for 15q11.2 CNV syndromes and a target for understanding Golgi-related neurodevelopmental disorders.
