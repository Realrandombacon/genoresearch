# Micro-uORF Peptide Regulator of ANKRD11 Translation with Clinical Significance

**Date:** 2026-03-27T11:37:36.255720

**Quality Score:** 3.2/10  (E=3.2, D=3.2) [WEAK]

## Description
LOC128462377 encodes an extremely short upstream open reading frame (uORF) peptide of only 12-13 amino acids located upstream of ANKRD11 (ankyrin repeat domain 11) on chromosome 16q24.3. Two UniProt entries exist: A0A804HJT0 (13 aa) and A0AAA9YHJ5 (12 aa), representing alternative translation products. Despite its minimal size precluding detectable protein domains (InterPro scan failed) or AlphaFold structural prediction, this locus demonstrates significant clinical relevance with 32 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants in ClinVar, including deletions and copy number variations affecting 16q24.3. Human Protein Atlas classifies it as a novel predicted intracellular protein with RNA not detected in bulk tissues but enriched in immune cells within blood. The genomic positioning immediately upstream of ANKRD11 is highly suggestive of uORF-mediated translational regulation - a mechanism where ribosomes translating the uORF modulate access to the main ANKRD11 coding sequence. ANKRD11 haploinsufficiency causes KBG syndrome (developmental delay, skeletal anomalies, distinctive facial features), and pathogenic variants in this uORF region may disrupt normal ANKRD11 protein production through translational dysregulation rather than direct coding mutation. The 32 ClinVar variants spanning deletions and CNVs suggest this regulatory element is dosage-sensitive. This represents a class of 'dark' genes that function not as traditional enzymes or structural proteins but as cis-regulatory translational controllers.
