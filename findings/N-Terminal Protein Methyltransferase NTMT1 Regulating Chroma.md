## Description
NTMT1 (N-terminal Xaa-Pro-Lys N-methyltransferase 1, alias METTL11A, UniProt Q9BV86) is a 223-amino acid nuclear/cytosolic enzyme catalyzing Nα-methylation of proteins with N-terminal X-Pro-Lys motifs. InterPro analysis confirms membership in the Alpha-N-methyltransferase family (IPR008576, aa 1-223) with AdoMet-dependent methyltransferase domain (cd02440, aa 66-166; PF05891, aa 8-223) and S-adenosyl-L-methionine binding capability (IPR029063, SSF53335), with GO annotations for methyltransferase activity (GO:0008168) and N-terminal protein amino acid methylation (GO:0006480). STRING interactions reveal high-confidence partnerships with chromatin/nuclear regulators: RCC1 (0.928, chromatin color regulator/RanGEF), DRG1 (0.686, GTP-binding protein), RANBP1 (0.661, Ran-binding protein), DDB2 (0.644, DNA damage-binding protein), METTL17 (0.624, ribosome biogenesis methyltransferase), PRMT6 (0.600, protein arginine methyltransferase), KLHL31 (0.607), RPL23A (0.501), RABIF (0.429), and EEF1AKMT2 (0.415), suggesting roles in chromatin dynamics, nucleocytoplasmic transport, and DNA damage response. HPA shows ubiquitous expression (low tissue specificity, detected in all tissues) with nucleoplasmic and cytosolic localization consistent with nuclear substrate methylation. AlphaFold predicts exceptionally high-confidence structure (pLDDT 97.6) for the full 223 aa, indicating a well-folded compact methyltransferase with defined active site architecture. ClinVar documents 35 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number losses/gains on chromosome 9q33-34 and single nucleotide variants, though specific disease phenotypes remain undefined. Literature (52 papers) reveals emerging oncogenic functions: (1) Upregulation in colorectal cancer via m6A-YTHDF1-dependent epitranscriptomic regulation (3 citations, 2024); (2) Nα-methylation of PAD1 (protein arginine deiminase 1) affecting protein stability and interactions (2 citations, 2024); (3) Inhibition by venglustat with structure-activity relationships characterized (7 citations, 2023); (4) Prognostic significance in oral squamous cell carcinoma (2026); (5) Implication in neurogenesis, retinoblastoma, and cervical cancer. Functional hypothesis: NTMT1 serves as a substrate-specific N-terminal methyltransferase regulating chromatin-associated proteins and DNA damage response factors, with RCC1 methylation potentially modulating Ran-GTPase cycling and nucleocytoplasmic transport, while PAD1 methylation affects citrullination activity in epigenetic regulation. Mechanistic proposal: NTMT1 binds S-adenosylmethionine (SAM) via the AdoMet_MTase domain (aa 66-166), positioning the catalytic site for sequential methyl transfer to N-terminal α-amino groups of X-Pro-Lys substrates; in cancer contexts, NTMT1 overexpression enhances methylation of chromatin regulators (RCC1, histone variants) and DNA repair proteins (DDB2), promoting cell cycle progression and genomic instability; epitranscriptomic upregulation via m6A-YTHDF1 pathway creates positive feedback loops enhancing NTMT1 translation in colorectal and oral cancers; pharmacological inhibition (venglustat derivatives) may disrupt these methylation-dependent oncogenic pathways.
