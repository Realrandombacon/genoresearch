## Description
IKZF3 (UniProt Q9UKT9), or IKAROS family zinc finger 3 (also known as Aiolos), encodes a 509-amino acid nuclear transcription factor belonging to the Ikaros C2H2-type zinc-finger family (IPR050589/PTHR24404, aa 69-499). The protein architecture features three N-terminal C2H2 zinc finger domains (IPR013087/IPR036236/PF00096/PS00028/PS50157/SM00355, aa 118-145/146-173/174-201) for sequence-specific DNA recognition, plus two C-terminal zinc fingers (aa 451-499) mediating protein-protein interactions and dimerization with other Ikaros family members. AlphaFold predicts low-confidence structure (global pLDDT: 47.9), indicating a predominantly intrinsically disordered protein with structured zinc finger modules—consistent with transcription factors that require conformational flexibility for chromatin remodeling complex recruitment and partner exchange.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals IKZF3's integration into B-cell development and lymphoid malignancy networks: CRBN (0.999, cereblon E3 ligase substrate receptor), ZPBP2 (0.938), PAX5 (0.899, B-cell specificity transcription factor), GSDMB (0.859, gasdermin B), IKZF4 (0.856, Eos), RUNX3 (0.850), IKZF5 (0.838, Helios), RUNX1 (0.618), IKZF1 (0.582, Ikaros), and EBF1 (0.455, early B-cell factor). The CRBN interaction is therapeutically critical—lenalidomide/pomalidomide induce IKZF3 ubiquitination and degradation, explaining their efficacy in multiple myeloma and B-cell lymphomas.\n\nWith substantial but incomplete mechanistic understanding, IKZF3 qualifies as a partially dark gene with breakthrough therapeutic implications. Recent studies reveal: (1) IKZF3 deletion/mutation drives high-risk B-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia (B-ALL) with poor prognosis; (2) IKZF3 cooperates with PAX5 to repress plasma cell differentiation genes, maintaining germinal center B-cell identity; (3) GSDMB-IKZF3 fusion creates oncogenic driver in lymphoid malignancies; (4) IKZF3 regulates immunoglobulin heavy chain locus accessibility for V(D)J recombination; (5) lenalidomide-induced IKZF3 degradation sensitizes multiple myeloma to immunotherapy. HPA data confirms lymphoid tissue-enriched expression (38.4 nTPM top tissue, intestine 11.1 nTPM) with nucleoplasmic and cytosolic localization, classified as transcription factor involved in B-cell activation. ClinVar lists 11 pathogenic variants including frameshift (p.Met1fs), missense (p.Met77Leu, p.Gly159Arg in zinc fingers), and chromosome 17q12-21.2/17q23.1-25.1 copy number gains/losses, confirming dosage sensitivity and domain-critical residues. We hypothesize IKZF3 functions as a chromatin accessibility gatekeeper for B-cell lineage commitment: the disordered linker regions between zinc fingers enable dynamic binding to nucleosome-packed Ig loci, recruiting SWI/SNF remodelers via IKZF1 heterodimerization; the CRBN interaction represents an evolutionary vulnerability where immunomodulatory drugs hijack the CRL4-CRBN E3 ligase to degrade IKZF3, derepressing fetal hemoglobin and T-cell activation genes—this explains why IKZF3 loss-of-function mutations cause aggressive B-ALL (failed differentiation arrest) while pharmacological degradation treats myeloma (enhanced tumor antigen presentation); the p.Met77Leu/p.Gly159Arg variants in zinc fingers likely disrupt DNA recognition specificity rather than structural integrity.
