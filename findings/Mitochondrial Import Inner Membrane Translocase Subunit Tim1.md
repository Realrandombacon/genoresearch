## Description
TIMM17B (Mitochondrial Import Inner Membrane Translocase Subunit Tim17-B) is a 172-amino acid integral membrane protein with 13 publications confirming its partially characterized dark gene status. The protein contains conserved Tim17 family domains including mitochondrial inner membrane translocase complex subunit Tim17 domain (IPR005678, positions 1-171) that mediates protein transmembrane transporter activity and protein import into mitochondrial matrix, Tim17/Tim22/Tim23/Pmp24 family domain (PF02466, positions 13-122), and mitochondrial import inner membrane translocase architecture (PTHR10485, positions 1-164; TIGR00980, positions 1-171). AlphaFold predicts a well-folded structure (pLDDT 82.3), consistent with stable transmembrane protein architecture required for TIM23 complex assembly. HPA returned no data (gene not in database), reflecting limited antibody-based characterization. ClinVar documents 159 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants (primarily X chromosome Xp22.33-q28/Xp22.33-11.23/Xp21.1-11.22 copy number losses and gains), though specific disease phenotypes beyond developmental disorders remain uncharacterized. STRING interactions reveal TIMM17B functions as core component of the TIM23 mitochondrial protein import complex with very high confidence connections to TIMM23 (0.999) - the central channel-forming subunit, TIMM50 (0.996) - the receptor subunit that gates the channel, DNAJC19 (0.909) - the J-protein co-chaperone, HSPA9 (0.881) - the mitochondrial Hsp70 motor ATPase, TIMM44 (0.743) - the membrane anchor for the import motor, plus interactions with TIMM10 (0.712), TOMM20 (0.609), TIMM21 (0.578), TIMM23B (0.545), and PAM16 (0.503). Recent studies (2023-2025) established TIMM17B as high expression diagnostic and prognostic marker in breast cancer, differentially expressed gene in metastatic breast cancer (brain and lymph node metastases), and MYC-amplified cancer synthetic lethality target where MYC primes paralog synthetic lethality involving TIMM17A and TIMM17B with N-acetylaspartate metabolism. We hypothesize TIMM17B functions as an essential structural and regulatory subunit of the TIM23 complex that stabilizes the protein-conducting channel during presequence-containing protein import into the mitochondrial matrix, working cooperatively with TIMM23 (channel), TIMM50 (receptor/gate), DNAJC19 (co-chaperone), and HSPA9 (motor ATPase) to drive ATP-dependent translocation. The X-chromosome location suggests potential sex-specific dosage effects in mitochondrial function, and the 159 ClinVar variants indicate TIMM17B haploinsufficiency or overexpression contributes to X-linked developmental disorders. The MYC-dependent synthetic lethality with TIMM17A suggests functional redundancy between paralogs that becomes essential under oncogenic stress, revealing TIMM17B as a potential therapeutic target in MYC-amplified cancers where mitochondrial protein import capacity is limiting for tumor growth.
