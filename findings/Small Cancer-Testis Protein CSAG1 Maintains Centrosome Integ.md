# Small Cancer-Testis Protein CSAG1 Maintains Centrosome Integrity via MAGE Family Network

## Description
CSAG1 (Chondrosarcoma-Associated Gene 1) is a 78-amino acid cancer-testis antigen representing a poorly characterized small protein with only 16 publications. Despite its small size, CSAG1 plays a critical role in maintaining mitotic centrosome integrity in cells with defective p53, as demonstrated in a key 2019 study (10 citations). AlphaFold predicts a partially structured protein (pLDDT 62.4), consistent with potential intrinsically disordered regions common in small regulatory proteins. STRING interactions reveal CSAG1 functions within the MAGE cancer-testis antigen network with very high confidence connections to MAGEA12 (0.999), MAGEA3 (0.980), MAGEA6 (0.925), MAGEA2 (0.868), MAGEA1 (0.682), and MAGEF1 (0.496), plus interactions with SSX2 (0.489), NSMCE3 (0.478), and NPAS4 (0.470). This interaction profile suggests CSAG1 may function as a MAGE family co-factor or substrate recruiter. ClinVar documents 187 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants (primarily Xq26.3-28 copy number gains/losses and one splice site variant c.168-1G>C), though specific disease phenotypes remain uncharacterized beyond the chondrosarcoma association. Recent 2025 Mendelian randomization analysis identified causal effects of gut microbiota metabolic pathways on CSAG1 expression in chondrosarcoma. HPA has no expression data, consistent with cancer-testis antigen biology (testis-restricted, absent from somatic tissues). We hypothesize CSAG1 functions as a small scaffold protein that stabilizes centrosome structure during mitosis in p53-deficient cells by bridging MAGE family E3 ligase complexes to centrosomal substrates, enabling chromosome segregation fidelity despite checkpoint defects—a mechanism that may be exploited in chondrosarcoma and other MAGE-expressing tumors.
