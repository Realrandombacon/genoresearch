## Description
ZNF268 (UniProt Q14587) encodes a 947 amino acid Krüppel-associated box (KRAB) zinc finger protein localized to the nucleus and cytosol. The protein architecture features an N-terminal KRAB domain (PF01352, IPR001909, residues 80-152) responsible for recruiting transcriptional corepressors like TRIM28/KAP1 to silence target genes via heterochromatin formation. The C-terminal region contains multiple C2H2-type zinc finger domains (PF00096, IPR013087) at positions 306-326, 332-354, 360-382, and extended clusters up to residue 722, providing sequence-specific DNA binding capability. Notably, InterPro identifies a ZIC/GLI-like zinc finger motif (IPR056436, residues 697-722), suggesting potential functional convergence with Hedgehog signaling regulators. HPA data indicates ubiquitous expression across all tissues with low specificity, consistent with a housekeeping role in transcriptional regulation. Functional annotations link ZNF268 to positive regulation of non-canonical NF-kappaB signaling, apoptosis control, and primary metabolic processes, positioning it as a node integrating stress responses and cell survival. ClinVar documents 22 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains (duplications) on 12q24.33 and a missense variant (p.Lys680Asn), implying that ZNF268 dosage is critical for cellular homeostasis; overexpression may lead to aberrant repression of tumor suppressors or dysregulation of NF-kappaB targets. The AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 68.6) reflects a modular organization with structured zinc finger clusters and potentially disordered linker regions, typical of transcription factors that undergo conformational changes upon DNA binding or cofactor recruitment. Mechanistically, ZNF268 likely functions as a sequence-specific repressor that modulates the expression of genes involved in inflammation (NF-kappaB pathway) and programmed cell death, with dysregulation contributing to developmental defects or oncogenesis.
