## Description
DUSP28 encodes Dual specificity phosphatase 28 (176 aa, Q4G0W2), a small secreted phosphatase of the DSP family functioning in dephosphorylation of both phosphotyrosine and phosphothreonine residues. InterPro analysis reveals canonical DSP catalytic architecture: dual specificity phosphatase catalytic domain (IPR000340/PF00782, positions 26-155), tyrosine-specific phosphatase domain (IPR000387, positions 76-138), and protein tyrosine phosphatase superfamily fold (G3DSA:3.90.190.10, positions 19-167) with conserved catalytic cysteine motif essential for phosphatase activity. GO annotations confirm dephosphorylation (GO:0016311) and protein dephosphorylation (GO:0006470). STRING interactions reveal moderate-confidence partners: AK2 (0.602, adenylate kinase 2 - mitochondrial energy metabolism), ZC3H15 (0.479, zinc finger protein), METTL22 (0.460, methyltransferase), C1QTNF8 (0.458, complement C1q tumor necrosis factor-related protein), PMPCA (0.448, mitochondrial processing peptidase), AFG3L2 (0.420, mitochondrial AAA protease), WDR33 (0.417, cleavage/polyadenylation factor), GAB2 (0.402, GRB2-associated binder) - suggesting connections to mitochondrial function, RNA processing, and growth factor signaling. HPA shows immune cell-enriched expression with low tissue specificity; subcellular localization to nuclear speckles and cytosol; literature reveals secreted form in pancreatic cancer enabling autocrine/paracrine signaling. ClinVar documents 100 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly chromosome 2q35-37.3 CNVs (gains and losses) indicating strong dosage sensitivity, though specific disease phenotypes remain incompletely characterized. AlphaFold structure shows exceptional confidence (pLDDT 90.1) with well-folded catalytic domain typical of dual-specificity phosphatases. Literature (16 papers) reveals emerging oncogenic roles: 2024 review establishes DUSP28 as 'wandering phosphatase' linking ER stress and mitochondrial dynamics; 2022 study demonstrates DUSP28 promotes breast cancer proliferation/migration/invasion via Akt/β-catenin/Slug axis; 2017/2019 pancreatic cancer studies identify DUSP28 as secreted biomarker aggravating malignancy via autocrine/paracrine PDGF-A regulation; scattered DUSP28 expression correlates with metastatic potential. Functional hypothesis: DUSP28 functions as an oncogenic phosphatase with dual intracellular and secreted forms regulating cancer progression through multiple pathways - intracellularly modulating Akt/β-catenin signaling and ER stress responses, extracellularly acting as secreted factor promoting PDGF-A-mediated autocrine/paracrine signaling in pancreatic cancer; immune cell enrichment suggests potential immunomodulatory roles. Mechanistic proposal: Catalytic cysteine in DSP domain dephosphorylates MAPK pathway components (ERK, JNK, p38) or Akt substrates relieving inhibitory phosphorylation; secreted form binds cell surface receptors (potentially PDGFR) activating downstream signaling; mitochondrial interaction partners (AK2, PMPCA, AFG3L2) suggest role in ER-mitochondria crosstalk regulating apoptosis and metabolic stress; nuclear speckle localization indicates potential RNA processing regulation; β-catenin/Slug axis activation promotes EMT and metastasis; dosage sensitivity (100 CNVs) suggests haploinsufficiency could disrupt phosphatase balance contributing to developmental defects or cancer susceptibility. This gene represents a moderately characterized oncogenic phosphatase with established cancer roles but incompletely understood substrate specificity and physiological functions.
