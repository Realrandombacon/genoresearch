# PPEF2: Retina-Enriched Serine/Threonine Phosphatase Antagonizing PINK1-Mediated Mitophagy and Regulating Photoreceptor Survival via Ubiquitin Dephosphorylation

## Description
PPEF2 (Protein Phosphatase with EF-hands 2) is a 753-amino acid serine/threonine phosphatase uniquely characterized by the combination of a calcineurin-like metallophosphatase domain and multiple EF-hand calcium-binding motifs. InterPro analysis confirms the presence of a PPP domain (IPR013235), a calcineurin-like phosphoesterase domain (IPR004843, residues 174-316), and two functional EF-hand domains (IPR002048, residues 572-600 and 652-727) that likely confer calcium-dependent regulation of phosphatase activity. AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (pLDDT 75.2), indicating a stable fold where the catalytic core is spatially distinct from the C-terminal calcium-sensing region.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals PPEF2 operates in specific neuronal and sensory contexts, with highest confidence for NRSN1 (0.972), a neuron-specific protein implicated in neurite outgrowth; RHO (0.731), rhodopsin, linking it directly to phototransduction; and NAAA (0.645), an N-acylethanolamine acid amidase involved in lipid signaling. Notably, interactions with PPP2R2A/B (0.487) suggest functional redundancy or heterodimerization with other PP2B-family phosphatases. Human Protein Atlas classifies PPEF2 as 'tissue enriched' with extreme specificity for the retina (221.1 nTPM), consistent with its role in sensory transduction and vision. ClinVar reports 23 pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number losses and gains spanning 4q12-21.1, indicating dosage sensitivity, though specific monogenic phenotypes are often masked by the size of these structural variants.\n\nLiterature analysis (22 papers) highlights a dual role in retinal health and mitochondrial quality control. Recent studies (2023) demonstrate that the m6A reader YTHDC2 maintains visual function by modulating PPEF2 translation, while exome sequencing links PPEF2 variants to high myopia. Mechanistically, a landmark 2019 study established that PPEF2 opposes PINK1-mediated mitochondrial quality control by directly dephosphorylating ubiquitin at Ser65, thereby preventing Parkin recruitment and suppressing mitophagy. We hypothesize that PPEF2 functions as a calcium-gated 'brake' on mitophagy in post-mitotic cells like photoreceptors and neurons. Under basal conditions, PPEF2 prevents excessive turnover of mitochondria, preserving metabolic capacity. However, in response to severe stress or calcium dysregulation, reduced PPEF2 activity may be required to allow PINK1/Parkin-mediated clearance of damaged organelles. The strong interaction with Rhodopsin (0.731) suggests a specialized role in the outer segment of photoreceptors, where high metabolic demand and light-induced oxidative stress require precise tuning of mitochondrial turnover. Dysregulation of this balance leads to photoreceptor death (retinal dystrophy) or refractive errors (myopia). PPEF2 represents a critical node connecting calcium signaling, protein phosphorylation, and organelle homeostasis in sensory tissues.
