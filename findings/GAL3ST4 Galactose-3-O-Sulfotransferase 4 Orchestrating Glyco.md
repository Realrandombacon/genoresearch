## Description
GAL3ST4 (Galactose-3-O-Sulfotransferase 4) is a 486 amino acid Golgi-resident sulfotransferase that catalyzes the transfer of sulfate from 3'-phosphoadenosine 5'-phosphosulfate (PAPS) to the C3 position of galactose residues in glycolipids and mucin-type O-glycans, serving as a critical regulator of glycolipid biosynthesis, skin barrier integrity, and immune recognition through sulfated glycan epitope generation. InterPro analysis identifies characteristic sulfotransferase architecture including galactose-3-O-sulfotransferase family domains (IPR009729, PF06990, residues 18-227/286-469) performing galactosylceramide sulfotransferase activity (GO:0001733) and glycolipid biosynthetic process (GO:0009247), P-loop containing nucleoside triphosphate hydrolase superfamily (IPR027417, G3DSA:3.40.50.300, SSF52540, residues 52-399) for PAPS binding and sulfate donor activation, and galactose-3-O-sulfotransferase family signature (PTHR14647, residues 49-220) defining the enzyme specificity. AlphaFold structure (AF-Q96RP7-F1) predicts confident folding (pLDDT 79.3) across the 486 residue sequence, indicating a stable Type II transmembrane enzyme with structured catalytic domain suitable for Golgi membrane anchoring and substrate recognition. HPA data classifies GAL3ST4 as a predicted intracellular and membrane metabolic protein with transferase molecular function, tissue-enhanced expression in skin (26.6 nTPM), cancer-enhanced distribution suggesting roles in tumor glycosylation remodeling, nucleoplasmic and cytosolic localization reflecting Golgi processing and potential nuclear functions, and cell type-enhanced specificity. STRING interactions reveal moderate-confidence functional coupling to glycosylation and immune signaling partners: SBNO2 (Strawberry Notch Homolog 2, 0.504) for transcriptional regulation; TINAG (Tubulointerstitial Nephritis Antigen, 0.484) for extracellular matrix interactions; SRPX2 (Sushi Repeat-Containing Protein X-Linked 2, 0.477) for synapse formation and glycosylation; SYT14 (Synaptotagmin-14, 0.465) for vesicular trafficking; H3C7 (Histone H3.7, 0.461) for chromatin regulation; INPP5D (Inositol Polyphosphate-5-Phosphatase D/SHIP1, 0.461) for immune cell signaling; ABI3 (ABL Interactor 3, 0.459) for cytoskeletal dynamics; LRRC25 (Leucine Rich Repeat Containing 25, 0.459) for innate immunity; GCNT4 (Glucosaminyl (N-Acetyl) Transferase 4, 0.456) as a related glycosyltransferase for O-glycan extension; and LRRN1 (Leucine Rich Repeat Neuronal 1, 0.421) for cell adhesion. ClinVar lists 24 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large deletions on 7q22.1 (multiple independent CNV events spanning 2-12 Mb regions: chr7:98396469-102108193, chr7:99114000-101878272, chr7:98454022-100723798, chr7:99417471-111586308), complex rearrangements, and missense variant c.371G>A p.Gly124Glu affecting the catalytic domain, though no specific monogenic syndrome is defined, suggesting potential roles in connective tissue disorder (pectus excavatum association) and infectious disease susceptibility. Literature (16 papers) establishes GAL3ST4 as an emerging glycosylation regulator: genome-wide association studies identify GAL3ST4 as a novel susceptibility locus for leprosy linking sulfated glycan epitopes to Mycobacterium leprae recognition and immune evasion; GAL3ST4 mutation identified as potential cause of pectus excavatum suggesting roles in connective tissue development through glycolipid-mediated signaling; sulfated O-glycans on mucin-type proteins from serous ovarian epithelial tumors implicate GAL3ST4 in cancer glycosylation remodeling; and GAL3ST4 expression correlates with abdominal aortic aneurysm disease severity indicating vascular wall glycosylation involvement. As a partially characterized sulfotransferase with emerging disease associations, GAL3ST4 represents a critical node connecting PAPS-dependent sulfation to skin barrier function, pathogen recognition, connective tissue morphogenesis, and tumor glycosylation through generation of sulfated galactose epitopes on glycolipids and mucin-type O-glycans.
