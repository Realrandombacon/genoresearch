## Description
MYO9A encodes a 2548 amino acid unconventional myosin motor protein that functions as an actin-based transporter and GTPase-activating protein (RhoGAP), playing essential roles in cellular trafficking, cytoskeletal organization, and signal transduction. Despite extensive literature coverage (47 papers) linking it to congenital myasthenic syndrome, focal segmental glomerulosclerosis (FSGS), rheumatoid arthritis, and colorectal cancer signatures, MYO9A remains a partially characterized 'dark gene' regarding its precise mechanistic roles in specific cellular contexts beyond general motility. The protein exhibits unique structural features including an N-terminal Ras-associating domain (RA) for membrane tethering, a central motor domain with IQCD motifs for calmodulin binding and actin interaction, and a C-terminal RhoGAP domain regulating small GTPases—creating a dual-function architecture that integrates mechanical force generation with signaling modulation.\n\nSTRUCTURAL ARCHITECTURE: InterPro analysis of UniProt B2RTY4 reveals a modular design critical for its unconventional myosin function. The N-terminal region (residues 1-110) contains the Ras-associating domain found in Myosin-IXa, facilitating membrane association and cargo recruitment via lipid binding or protein interactions. The central motor domain (cd01385, residues 160-1004, ~844 aa) houses the ATPase/actin-binding core responsible for processive movement along actin filaments; this region shows homology to Myosin VI head domains but lacks typical myosin II heavy chain features. The C-terminal regulatory domain (cd23767/IQCD, residues 1109-1141) contains IQ motifs essential for calmodulin binding and actin activation under calcium signaling conditions. Distinctly, the RhoGAP_myosin_IX domain (cd04377, residues 2061-2246, ~185 aa) provides GTPase activating activity toward RhoA/RhoV family members, linking mechanical transport to cytoskeletal remodeling. Additional domains include a protein kinase C conserved region (C1 domain, cd23767/positions 1995-2052), potentially involved in lipid binding or signaling modulation.
