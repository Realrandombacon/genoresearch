## Description
ZNF480 (zinc finger protein 480, Gene ID: 147657, UniProt Q8WV37) is a 535 amino acid KRAB domain-containing zinc finger transcription factor located on chromosome 19q13.41. DOMAIN ARCHITECTURE: InterPro analysis reveals a canonical KRAB repressor domain (PF01352, IPR001909, positions 27-98) associated with regulation of DNA-templated transcription (GO:0006355), followed by multiple C2H2-type zinc finger domains (PF00096, IPR013087) at positions 201-286 and 413-437 that mediate sequence-specific DNA binding. The protein belongs to the Krueppel-associated box domain superfamily (IPR036051) and C2H2 zinc finger superfamily (IPR036236). STRUCTURAL CONFIDENCE: AlphaFold predicts a well-folded structure with global pLDDT score of 70.4 (confident range), modeling all 535 residues. INTERACTION NETWORK: STRING-DB identifies 4 medium-confidence interaction partners, most notably TRIM28/TIF1β (score 0.583) - the canonical corepressor that binds KRAB domains and recruits heterochromatin machinery including HP1 and histone methyltransferases. Additional partners include SHPRH (0.464, ubiquitin ligase involved in DNA damage response), PUS7L (0.460, pseudouridine synthase), and ALS2CL (0.403, endosomal protein). TISSUE EXPRESSION: Human Protein Atlas shows low tissue specificity with detection in all tissues examined, consistent with a housekeeping transcriptional regulator. Subcellular localization includes nucleoplasm and cytosol, classified as a transcription factor with activator molecular function. CLINICAL VARIANTS: ClinVar contains 16 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains spanning chromosome 19q regions (variants #2684892, #1526670, #980768, #972980, #816084, #443499, #443498, #394342, #153721) and one frameshift mutation (c.9_10del, p.Cys3_Asp4delinsTer, variant #403625) that truncates the KRAB domain. FUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: ZNF480 functions as a sequence-specific transcriptional repressor through the canonical KRAB-ZFP mechanism. The N-terminal KRAB domain recruits TRIM28/KAP1, which scaffolds a repressive complex containing SETDB1 histone methyltransferase, HP1 heterochromatin proteins, and NuRD chromatin remodelers. The C-terminal C2H2 zinc fingers recognize specific DNA sequences, likely in promoter or enhancer regions of target genes. Ubiquitous expression suggests regulation of fundamental cellular processes. Pathogenic copy number gains likely cause disease through dosage effects - overexpression of this repressor could silence critical genes, while the frameshift variant would abolish repressor function. The interaction with SHPRH suggests potential crosstalk between transcriptional repression and DNA damage response pathways.
