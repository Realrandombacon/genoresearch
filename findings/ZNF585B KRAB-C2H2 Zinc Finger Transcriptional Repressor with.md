## Description
ZNF585B (Zinc finger protein 585B, UniProt Q52M93) is a 769-amino acid nuclear transcription factor located on chromosome 19q13.42 within the large cluster of primate-specific KRAB-ZNF genes. InterPro analysis reveals canonical KRAB-ZNF architecture: N-terminal KRAB (Krüppel-associated box) domain (IPR001909/IPR036051/PF01352/cd07765, aa 26-97/22-80/26-67/27-65) that functions as a transcriptional repression module, followed by multiple C-terminal C2H2-type zinc finger domains (IPR013087/IPR036236/PF00096/G3DSA:3.30.160.60/PS00028/PS50157 at aa 130-157/158-185/186-213/128-223/223-279/278-335/158-180/186-208/214-236) arranged in tandem arrays that confer sequence-specific DNA binding through variable residue positions in the alpha-helix recognition surface. GO annotations confirm roles in regulation of DNA-templated transcription (GO:0006355). AlphaFold structure prediction achieves high confidence (pLDDT 75.2), indicating a well-folded protein with stable zinc finger folds and structured KRAB domain — architecture essential for maintaining proper geometry for DNA major groove insertion and co-receptor binding. Human Protein Atlas shows ubiquitous expression detected in all tissues with low tissue specificity, low cancer specificity, low immune cell specificity, cell type enhanced single cell specificity, nucleoplasm and cytoplasmic bodies subcellular localization — consistent with housekeeping transcriptional regulator functions across cell types. STRING interaction network reveals 4 partners: TRIM28 (0.675, tripartite motif-containing protein 28/KAP1 — the universal KRAB domain co-repressor that recruits SETDB1 histone methyltransferase and HP1 heterochromatin proteins), OR4D6 (0.450, olfactory receptor 4D6), ZNF568 (0.419, zinc finger protein 568 — paralogous KRAB-ZNF), and GXYLT1 (0.414, glucoside xylosyltransferase 1). The TRIM28 interaction is mechanistically definitive, placing ZNF585B within the canonical KRAB-ZNF repression pathway. ClinVar contains 126 total variants but 0 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, demonstrating remarkable genetic tolerance — consistent with functional redundancy among the ~400 human KRAB-ZNF genes where loss of one family member is compensated by paralogs with overlapping DNA binding specificities, or where target genes are non-essential. Conservation across vertebrates (orangutan Q5RB30, 769 aa; Xenopus laevis P70045/A0A1L8FUD8, 573 aa) indicates ancient evolutionary origin of the KRAB-ZNF architecture, though the specific gene may be primate-expanded. ZNF585B likely functions as a sequence-specific transcriptional repressor that binds genomic DNA through its array of C2H2 zinc fingers recognizing 3-4 base pair subsequences each (collectively specifying ~12-20 bp target sites), while its KRAB domain recruits TRIM28/KAP1 which scaffolds a repressive complex containing SETDB1 (H3K9 trimethyltransferase), HP1 heterochromatin proteins, and NuRD chromatin remodelers — establishing localized heterochromatin and silencing target genes involved in development, immunity, or transposon control, with ubiquitous expression suggesting broad regulatory roles and genetic redundancy explaining lack of disease associations despite high variant counts.
