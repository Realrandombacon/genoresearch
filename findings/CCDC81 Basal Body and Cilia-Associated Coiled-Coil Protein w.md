# CCDC81: Basal Body and Cilia-Associated Coiled-Coil Protein with HU Domains Regulating Ciliogenesis and Cellular Motility

**Date:** 2026-03-28T03:57:43.020459

**Quality Score:** 3.8/10  (E=4.8, D=2.8) [MODERATE]

## Description
CCDC81 (Gene ID: 55788, UniProt Q6ZN84) is a 652 amino acid coiled-coil domain-containing protein located on chromosome 11q14.1 (NC_000011.10). InterPro domain analysis reveals a distinctive architecture featuring two eukaryotic-specific HU-like domains (IPR028034/PF14908, positions 8-93; IPR040673/PF18289, positions 103-177) at the N-terminus, followed by an extended coiled-coil region spanning most of the protein (IPR026295/PTHR14362, positions 1-645). While bacterial HU proteins are DNA-binding architectural proteins, these eukaryotic CCDC81 HU domains have likely diverged to mediate protein-protein interactions within the ciliary apparatus rather than DNA binding. AlphaFold structure prediction (AF-Q6ZN84-F1) yields a high-confidence model (global pLDDT 70.5), consistent with a well-folded elongated structure dominated by coiled-coil helices suitable for scaffolding functions. Human Protein Atlas classifies CCDC81 as a predicted intracellular protein localized to the plasma membrane and basal body, with tissue-enhanced expression highest in fallopian tube (25.8 nTPM) and choroid plexus (15.8 nTPM); it is cancer-enhanced and cell type-enhanced at single-cell resolution. The basal body localization is highly informative—basal bodies are microtubule-based structures that nucleate cilia and flagella. STRING database identifies 10 interaction partners overwhelmingly enriched for cilia/flagella-associated proteins: CFAP57 (score 0.597, cilia and flagella associated protein 57), CFAP70 (0.574), CCDC113 (0.556, coiled-coil domain containing 113), RSPH10B2 (0.528, radial spoke head 10 homolog B2), CCDC96 (0.480), CFAP44 (0.480), FAM217A (0.479), TSNAXIP1 (0.479), C7orf31/CFAP45 (0.472), and CCDC146 (0.466). This interaction network strongly implicates CCDC81 in ciliary assembly, maintenance, or function, potentially as a structural component of the basal body transition zone or axoneme. ClinVar lists 20 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number losses and gains spanning 11q14.1-22.1 (multi-megabase regions affecting many genes); no specific monogenic disease is yet attributed solely to CCDC81, but dosage sensitivity of the 11q14 region is linked to developmental delays and ciliopathy-related phenotypes. Conservation is evident across mammals (mouse Q9D5W4, rat Q5XIN9) and even distant eukaryotes (Trypanosoma Q383J9). Functional hypothesis: CCDC81 functions as a basal body scaffolding protein that stabilizes the ciliary rootlet or transition zone through its coiled-coil domains, while the HU-like domains may mediate specific protein-protein interactions with ciliary cargo or regulatory factors. It likely plays a critical role in ciliogenesis, particularly in tissues with high ciliary demand such as fallopian tube (ciliated epithelium for oocyte transport) and choroid plexus (ependymal cilia for CSF flow). Disease mechanism: Haploinsufficiency or overexpression due to 11q CNVs disrupts ciliary assembly or function, potentially contributing to ciliopathy phenotypes including hydrocephalus (choroid plexus dysfunction), infertility (fallopian tube cilia defects), and neurodevelopmental abnormalities. Priority experiments include immunofluorescence colocalization with basal body markers (gamma-tubulin, pericentrin), co-IP validation of CFAP57/CFAP70 interactions, CRISPR knockout in ciliated cell lines to assess effects on ciliogenesis and ciliary length, and investigation of CCDC81 mutations in ciliopathy patient cohorts.
