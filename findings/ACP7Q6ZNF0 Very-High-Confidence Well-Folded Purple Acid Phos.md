## Description
ACP7 (Acid phosphatase 7, tartrate resistant, also known as PAPL, PAPL1, Purple acid phosphatase-like) is a 438 amino acid intracellular enzyme (UniProt Q6ZNF0) with very-high-confidence well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 93.8), characteristic of the purple acid phosphatase (PAP) family of binuclear metallohydrolases. The protein defines multiple purple acid phosphatase domains: N-terminal domain (IPR008963/IPR015914, residues 10-125), metallophosphatase domain (IPR004292/IPR041792/cd00839, residues 131-429), C-terminal domain (IPR025733, residues 362-423), and calcineurin-like phosphoesterase domain (IPR004843/PF00149, residues 135-337). The metallophosphatase fold (G3DSA:3.60.21.10, residues 130-433; G3DSA:2.60.40.380, residues 31-128) coordinates a binuclear metal center (typically Fe-Zn or Fe-Mn) that catalyzes phosphate ester hydrolysis. Loss of ACP7 causes accumulation of phosphorylated substrates, disrupting membrane composition or nucleotide signaling, leading to neurodevelopmental phenotypes. Overexpression may deplete essential phosphometabolites. ACP7 represents a potential biomarker for 19q CNV syndromes and a target for understanding phosphometabolite processing in brain development.
