## Description
SERINC2 (Serine Incorporator 2) is a 455-amino acid multi-pass transmembrane protein (UniProt Q96SA4) representing a genuine dark gene with only 52 publications despite its critical roles as a regulator of serine metabolism promoting cervical cancer progression and T cell exhaustion, bipolar disorder pathogenesis via microglial phospholipid synthesis, cardiac hypertrophy suppression via amino acid/mTORC1 signaling, alcoholism susceptibility, and membrane lipid homeostasis. Structurally, SERINC2 contains the canonical Serine incorporator/TMS membrane protein family domain (IPR005016/PF03348/PTHR10383, residues 16-454/16-452/18-454) defining a multi-pass transmembrane topology essential for substrate transport or membrane remodeling, with AlphaFold predicting high-confidence structure (pLDDT 78.5) consistent with a well-folded integral membrane protein containing stable transmembrane helices forming a potential transport pore or lipid-binding cavity. Functionally, SERINC2 localizes to nucleoplasm and plasma membrane with liver-enriched expression (HPA: tissue enhanced, detected in many, liver 241.0 nTPM, low cancer specificity, immune cell enriched, cell type enhanced), classified as a predicted membrane protein functioning in serine incorporation and lipid metabolism—dual localization suggests roles in both nuclear signaling and plasma membrane lipid dynamics. Recent literature establishes emerging specialized roles: SERINC2-mediated serine metabolism promotes cervical cancer progression and drives T cell exhaustion, establishing a metabolic checkpoint where tumor-derived serine depletion impairs CD8+ T cell function (10 citations, 2025); phenome-wide association studies link SERINC2 to alcoholism, bipolar disorder, and autism with specificity challenges, confirming genetic association with neuropsychiatric phenotypes (3 citations, 2025); Serinc2 antagonizes pressure overload-induced cardiac hypertrophy via regulating the amino acid/mTORC1 signaling pathway, demonstrating cardioprotective function by limiting mTOR-driven protein synthesis (2 citations, 2025); microglia contribute to bipolar depression through Serinc2-dependent phospholipid synthesis, revealing cell-type-specific mechanism where microglial SERINC2 alters lipid mediator production affecting neuronal function (2 citations, 2025). The mechanistic hypothesis: SERINC2 functions as a multi-pass transmembrane transporter or flippase that incorporates serine into membrane phospholipids (phosphatidylserine, sphingolipids) or transports serine across membranes to regulate intracellular pools. In cervical cancer, tumor-upregulated SERINC2 depletes extracellular serine, starving infiltrating T cells and inducing exhaustion via metabolic stress—SERINC2 inhibition would restore T cell serine uptake and anti-tumor immunity. In bipolar disorder, microglial SERINC2 drives excessive phospholipid synthesis (potentially lysophosphatidylserine or specialized pro-resolving mediators), altering neuroinflammatory tone and synaptic function—SERINC2 variants disrupt this balance contributing to mood instability. In cardiac hypertrophy, SERINC2 limits amino acid availability to mTORC1, preventing pathological protein synthesis and myocyte enlargement—SERINC2 loss unleashes mTOR signaling causing hypertrophy. The STOM (stomatin) interaction suggests SERINC2 operates within lipid rafts, potentially organizing membrane microdomains for signaling. The TMBIM6 link implies roles in ER stress and apoptosis regulation. The immune cell enrichment indicates specific functions in lymphocytes or myeloid cells beyond general metabolism. The absence of formal disease associations despite 12 pathogenic variants suggests SERINC2 dysfunction contributes to cervical cancer susceptibility/progression, bipolar disorder type I/II, alcohol use disorder, cardiac hypertrophy/cardiomyopathy, autism spectrum disorder, or inborn errors of serine/lipid metabolism not yet mapped to Mendelian phenotypes. Future work should define SERINC2's precise transport substrate (serine, phosphatidylserine, sphinganine) and directionality, determine crystal structure of transmembrane domain with bound substrate, investigate whether germline SERINC2 mutations cause familial bipolar disorder or cardiomyopathy, develop SERINC2 inhibitors for cervical cancer immunotherapy combination, explore SERINC2 as a biomarker for T cell exhaustion status, and assess SERINC2 modulation for treating alcoholism or neuroinflammation.
