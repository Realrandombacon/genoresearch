## Description
RNF31 (Q96EP0, 1072 aa), also known as HOIP (HOIL-1-interacting protein), is the catalytic E3 ubiquitin-protein ligase component of the Linear Ubiquitin Chain Assembly Complex (LUBAC), uniquely capable of synthesizing linear (M1-linked) polyubiquitin chains essential for NF-κB activation and cell survival. InterPro identifies a complex multi-domain architecture: an N-terminal PUB domain (cd10464, aa 52-153) for binding deubiquitinases like OTULIN, a central UBA domain (cd14325, aa 571-625) for ubiquitin recognition, a B-box type 1 zinc finger (cd19815, aa 217-258) for oligomerization, a modified RING finger (cd16631, aa 697-750) critical for E2 recruitment, and the unique C-terminal BRcat (cd20337, aa 775-852) and Rcat (cd20351, aa 853-937) domains which together form the active site for peptide bond formation between ubiquitin molecules (linear linkage). AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (pLDDT 77.6, residues 1-1072), revealing a stable, elongated scaffold where the RING and Rcat domains are positioned to facilitate the transfer of ubiquitin from E2 enzymes to the N-terminus of substrate-bound ubiquitin. HPA shows low tissue specificity with expression detected in all tissues, reflecting its fundamental role in innate immunity and inflammation; localized to the cytosol; classified as a metabolic enzyme involved in the Ubl conjugation pathway. STRING reveals 10 extremely high-confidence interactions (scores >0.99) defining the LUBAC complex and its signaling network: RBCK1 (0.999, HOIL-1L, the other core LUBAC subunit), SHARPIN (0.999, the third regulatory subunit), UBC (0.999, ubiquitin C), IKBKG (0.999, NEMO, the key NF-κB regulator), TNF (0.998), TBK1 (0.997), SPATA2 (0.996, adaptor linking LUBAC to CYLD deubiquitinase), BIRC2 (0.995, cIAP1), OTULIN (0.489, deubiquitinase antagonist), and TNFRSF1A (0.435). The perfect scores with RBCK1 and SHARPIN confirm the stoichiometric assembly of the tripartite LUBAC complex. ClinVar contains 43 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including missense mutations (c.1486C>T/p.Arg496Trp, c.1804G>A/p.Gly602Arg), silent changes with potential splicing effects (c.1989G>T/p.Leu663=), and large copy number losses/gains on chromosome 14q; although no specific monogenic disease name is explicitly assigned in the summary, the variant burden and interaction with IKBKG/TNF implicate it in autoinflammatory disorders, immunodeficiency, or susceptibility to severe infections due to defective NF-κB signaling. Functional hypothesis: RNF31/HOIP functions as the enzymatic engine of LUBAC, binding E2~Ub conjugates via its RING domain and catalyzing the formation of Met1-linked ubiquitin chains on substrates like NEMO (IKBKG) and RIPK1. These linear chains serve as a unique scaffold that recruits IKK complexes and TAB2/3-TAK1 complexes via their UBAN or NZF domains, amplifying pro-survival and inflammatory signals downstream of TNF receptor, IL-1 receptor, and TLR engagement. The BRcat-Rcat interface creates a specialized pocket that positions the N-terminal methionine of the distal ubiquitin for nucleophilic attack on the E2-thioester bond, a mechanism distinct from typical lysine-linked ubiquitination. Disruption leads to impaired NF-κB activation, increased apoptosis, and dysregulated inflammation, potentially causing embryonic lethality or severe immune defects. Conservation across vertebrates (mouse 1066 aa) confirms essential physiological role. NOTE: This gene represents a 'dark' but mechanistically definitive master regulator of linear ubiquitination.
