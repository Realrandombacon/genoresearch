## Description
TTYH2/Q9BSA4 (534 aa) is a well-folded (AlphaFold pLDDT: 82.0) membrane protein belonging to the conserved tweety homolog family with dual functionality as both a volume-regulated anion channel (VRAC) and endosomal lipid transfer protein. InterPro analysis reveals a large N-terminal chloride channel domain (cd07912, residues 7-422) characteristic of the tweety family (IPR006990, PF04906, residues 3-478/10-437), defining it within the TWEETY-RELATED protein family (PTHR12424). HPA data confirms brain-enriched expression (113.1 nTPM) with tissue-enriched RNA specificity and cancer group enrichment patterns, consistent with roles in neural physiology and oncology. STRING interactions define a functional network linking TTYH2 to all LRRC8 VRAC subunits (LRRC8C: 0.669, LRRC8E: 0.588, LRRC8D: 0.575, LRRC8B: 0.570, LRRC8A: 0.548), coatomer component COPB1 (0.646), and kinesin KCP (0.563), supporting both ion channel and vesicular trafficking functions. Literature (28 papers) establishes groundbreaking mechanistic insights: recent 2025 structural work demonstrates TTYH2-APOE interactions facilitating endosomal lipid transfer between membranes; 2021 cryo-EM structures reveal a Ca2+-dependent conformational switch from intra- to intermembrane dimerization explaining regulatory mechanisms; 2019 studies establish TTYH2 as an LRRC8A-independent VRAC in cancer cells mediating cell volume regulation, proliferation, and migration; and upregulated TTYH2 drives osteosarcoma invasion through ion channel activity. ClinVar documents 13 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains on chromosome 17q regions (e.g., 17q24.3-25.3 duplications), implicating TTYH2 dosage sensitivity in developmental disorders though specific monogenic phenotypes remain undefined. The convergence of Ca2+-regulated dimerization, VRAC independence from canonical LRRC8A complexes, APOE-mediated lipid transfer, and cancer-enhanced expression suggests TTYH2 functions as a multifunctional membrane hub coordinating ion homeostasis, lipid metabolism, and cell migration, with therapeutic potential in glioblastoma, osteosarcoma, and neurodegenerative diseases involving APOE dysfunction.
