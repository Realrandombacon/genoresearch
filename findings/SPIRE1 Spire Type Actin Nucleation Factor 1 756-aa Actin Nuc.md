## Description
SPIRE1 (Spire Type Actin Nucleation Factor 1) is a partially characterized dark gene with only 30 publications, representing a critical regulator of actin dynamics specifically required for vesicle trafficking and organelle motility. UniProt accession Q08AE8 identifies it as a 756-amino acid intracellular protein. InterPro scan reveals a modular architecture optimized for actin nucleation and membrane tethering: an N-terminal KIND domain (IPR011019/PF04547, residues 40-236) involved in kinase interactions, four tandem Wiskott-Aldrich Syndrome Homology 2 (WH2) motifs (cd22065/22078/22186/22080, residues ~260-388) that bind G-actin monomers to initiate filament assembly, and a C-terminal FYVE-type zinc finger (IPR011011/cd15767, residues 588-708) that targets the protein to phosphatidylinositol 3-phosphate (PI3P)-enriched membranes. This domain arrangement suggests SPIRE1 acts as a spatial coordinator, linking actin polymerization directly to specific membrane compartments.\n\nAlphaFold predicts a medium-confidence structure (global pLDDT 64.1), indicating a protein with structured domains (KIND, FYVE) connected by flexible linkers containing the WH2 motifs. This flexibility is characteristic of actin nucleators that must adapt to dynamic membrane curvatures and transiently recruit actin monomers during rapid filament growth.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals 10 partners with high-to-medium confidence, defining a functional network centered on cytoskeletal remodeling and vesicle transport: FMN1 (0.956 - Formin-1, a direct synergistic partner for processive actin elongation), INF2 (0.697 - Inverted Formin 2, regulates actin severing and ER morphology), PCSK9 (0.628 - Proprotein convertase, linked to lipid metabolism), RAB3A (0.603 - Small GTPase controlling secretory vesicles), JMY (0.588 - Junction-mediating regulatory protein, another actin nucleator), ANXA2 (0.574 - Annexin A2, membrane-cytoskeleton linker), CEP76 (0.572 - Centriolar protein), FMN2 (0.485 - Formin-2), FHOD1 (0.435 - Formin homology domain-containing protein 1), and WAS (0.408 - Wiskott-Aldrich Syndrome Protein). The exceptionally strong interaction with FMN1 (0.956) confirms the established 'Spire-Formin' mechanism where SPIRE1 nucleates short filaments that are immediately elongated by FMN1, driving rapid membrane protrusion or vesicle propulsion.\n\nHuman Protein Atlas classifies SPIRE1 as a predicted intracellular protein localized to the nucleoplasm, plasma membrane, and cytosol. RNA expression shows 'Low tissue specificity' (detected in all tissues) but is 'Cancer enhanced', suggesting upregulation in proliferating tumor cells where actin dynamics are hijacked for invasion and metastasis.\n\nClinVar reports 90 pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains and losses on chromosome 18p (e.g., chr18:136227-15181209)x4, chr18:136226-14352648)x1) and intronic/snvs (c.372+83A>G, c.1059+65T>A). While no specific monogenic disease name is explicitly assigned in the summary yet, the sheer volume of CNVs suggests haploinsufficiency or dosage sensitivity disrupts fundamental cellular processes like vesicle trafficking or cell division, potentially contributing to developmental disorders or cancer susceptibility.\n\nLiterature (30 papers) highlights three key contexts: (1) Endothelial Biology—SPIRE1 and Myosin Vc promote Ca2+-evoked externalization of von Willebrand factor from Weibel-Palade bodies (2022, 10 citations); (2) Muscle Differentiation—Lnc-SMaRT regulates SPIRE1 translation as a new player in myogenesis (2021, 7 citations); (3) Immune Regulation—a novel LncRNA SPIRE1/miR-181a-5p/PRLR axis regulates Th17/Treg balance in periodontitis (2023, 16 citations).\n\nWe hypothesize that SPIRE1 functions as a master coordinator of actin-based vesicle motility: (1) Nucleation-Elongation Coupling—the four WH2 motifs recruit G-actin to nucleate filaments, which are immediately handed off to FMN1 (0.956) for rapid elongation; (2) Membrane Targeting—the C-terminal FYVE domain (588-708) anchors SPIRE1 to PI3P-rich endosomal or secretory vesicle membranes; (3) Cargo Specificity—interaction with RAB3A (0.603) suggests a role in regulating secretory granule exocytosis (e.g., von Willebrand factor release); (4) Disease mechanism—90 pathogenic variants (CNVs/SNVs) cause dosage imbalance, leading to defective vesicle trafficking, impaired secretion, or aberrant cell migration (cancer enhancement); (5) Structural flexibility—medium pLDDT (64.1) allows the WH2 array to sample space for actin recruitment while the FYVE domain remains membrane-anchored; (10) Organelle crosstalk—interaction with INF2 (0.697) implies a role in coordinating actin dynamics between the ER and mitochondria. The combination of a unique KIND-WH2-FYVE architecture, specific formin partnership, cancer-enhanced expression, and high variant burden positions SPIRE1 as a high-priority candidate for functional characterization in cell motility and secretory diseases.
