# PCID2: Well-Folded TREX-2 Complex Scaffold Regulating mRNA Export and Mitotic Checkpoint Control

## Description
PCID2 (PCI domain containing 2) encodes a 399-amino acid nuclear protein that serves as an essential scaffold component of the TREX-2 (transcription and export complex 2) machinery. The protein contains a canonical PCI domain (IPR000717/PF01399, residues 210-391) characteristic of proteasome lid and COP9 signalosome subunits, plus a C-terminal winged helix DNA-binding domain (G3DSA:1.10.10.10, residues 328-399) that may mediate nucleic acid interactions. AlphaFold predicts exceptional structural confidence (pLDDT=95.6), indicating a stable globular fold suitable for multi-protein complex assembly. HPA localizes PCID2 to nucleoplasm and nucleoli with ubiquitous expression across all tissues, consistent with its housekeeping role in mRNA metabolism. STRING interactions reveal tight coupling to the TREX-2 mRNA export machinery: SEM1 (0.994, TREX-2 subunit), ENY2 (0.983, TREX-2 scaffold), NUP153 (0.939, nuclear pore protein), NUP98 (0.914, nucleoporin), MAD1L1 (0.907, spindle assembly checkpoint), MAD2L1 (0.902, mitotic arrest protein), and MCM3AP (0.902, DNA replication licensing). This interaction network positions PCID2 at the nexus of transcription, mRNA export, and cell cycle control. ClinVar documents 125 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly chromosome 13q copy number losses, indicating strong dosage sensitivity. Conservation is strong across vertebrates: mouse ortholog (Q8BFV3, 399 aa, ~95% identity), Drosophila (Q9VTL1, 395 aa), and bovine (Q2TBN6, 408 aa). Mechanistically, PCID2 likely stabilizes the TREX-2 complex through PCI-mediated protein-protein interactions, facilitating mRNA 3-end processing and nuclear export coupling. The MAD1L1/MAD2L1 interactions suggest additional roles in coordinating mRNA export with mitotic checkpoint signaling, potentially preventing cell division when mRNA processing is compromised.
