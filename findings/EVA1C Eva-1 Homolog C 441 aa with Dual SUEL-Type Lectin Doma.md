## Description
EVA1C (Eva-1 Homolog C) is a 441-amino acid intracellular and membrane-associated protein that functions as a carbohydrate-binding lectin receptor involved in axon guidance, neural development, and alternative RNA splicing regulation. InterPro analysis reveals a specialized domain architecture: two N-terminal SUEL-type lectin domains (cd22828, residues 61-160; cd22829, residues 164-261; IPR000922, residues 67-159/168-260; PF02140, residues 75-158/176-259; G3DSA:2.60.120.740, residues 56-164/165-266; PS50228, residues 67-159/168-260) that bind D-galactoside and L-rhamnose carbohydrates, and a C-terminal EVA1/FAM176 domain (IPR039500, residues 301-441; PF14851, residues 301-441) of the FAM176 family with unknown molecular function. The protein belongs to the rhamnose-binding lectin/heparin-binding family (PTHR46780, residues 30-430). AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (pLDDT 70.2), indicating a well-folded protein with stable lectin domains capable of specific carbohydrate recognition—a feature consistent with its proposed role as a Slit receptor in axon guidance.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places EVA1C in a network of glycosylation, neural development, and cell signaling regulators with medium confidence: URB1 (0.587, URB1 ribosome biogenesis factor), POFUT2 (0.587, protein O-fucosyltransferase 2, Notch pathway glycosylation), DONSON (0.581, downstream neighbor of SON, DNA replication fork stability), N6AMT1 (0.575, N-6 adenine-specific DNA methyltransferase), PNPLA6 (0.475, patatin-like phospholipase domain-containing protein 6, neuropathy target esterase), DOP1B (0.471, DOP1 family protein), PAXBP1 (0.471, PAX3 binding protein), UNC5D (0.454, unc-5 netrin receptor D, axon guidance), BMPR1B (0.454, bone morphogenetic protein receptor 1B, TGF-beta signaling), and PDXK (0.401, pyridoxal kinase, vitamin B6 metabolism). This network suggests EVA1C functions in glycosylation-dependent signaling (POFUT2), axon guidance (UNC5D netrin receptor homology), and neural development pathways.\n\nHuman Protein Atlas classifies EVA1C as a low tissue specificity intracellular and membrane protein detected in many tissues with cytosol localization. It shows low cancer specificity but is group-enriched in blood cells. The ubiquitous expression pattern suggests housekeeping or broadly distributed signaling functions.\n\nClinVar reports 56 pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number variations spanning 21q11.2-22.3 (coordinates include chr21:13644166-44968483x3 gain, chr21:32634806-43353470x1 loss, chr21:32925452-35162911x1 loss, chr21:15380398-48100790x3 gain, chr21:15023401-48097372x3 gain, chr21:26929299-48097372x3 gain, chr21:30685776-48097372x3 gain, chr21:33015681-48097372x3 gain, chr21:15006458-45674637x3 gain, chr21:14420615-48080926x3 gain). While no single-gene Mendelian disorder is exclusively named, recent literature identifies EVA1C as a prognostic biomarker in WHO grade II/III glioma correlated with immune infiltration levels (2021, 22 citations), a NAD+-regulated alternative splicing target in Alzheimer's disease neurological deficits (2025, 4 citations), and a downregulated gene promoting neuron growth after neonatal hypoxic-ischemic encephalopathy injury via alternative splicing mechanisms (2022, 3 citations).\n\nLiterature analysis (18 papers) reveals EVA1C is partially characterized with emerging roles in neurology and oncology. Key findings include its 2013 identification as a novel Slit receptor with expression pattern consistent with axon guidance in the mouse nervous system, its 2021 discovery as a glioma prognostic biomarker, its 2025 association with NAD+-mediated splicing reversal in Alzheimer's disease, and its 2022 role in neonatal hypoxic-ischemic brain injury recovery.\n\nWe hypothesize that EVA1C functions through a multi-domain lectin signaling mechanism: (1) Carbohydrate recognition—dual SUEL lectin domains bind specific glycan structures on Slit proteins or extracellular matrix components, positioning EVA1C as a co-receptor for axon guidance cues; (2) Slit/Robo pathway integration—interaction with UNC5D (netrin receptor) suggests cross-talk between Slit and netrin guidance systems; (3) Glycosylation dependence—POFUT2 interaction implies EVA1C function requires O-fucosylation, linking to Notch pathway regulation; (4) Alternative splicing regulation—nuclear or cytosolic EVA1C isoforms may participate in RNA splicing complexes, explaining Alzheimer's and hypoxic-ischemic encephalopathy associations where EVA1C splicing patterns change; (5) Glioma prognosis—EVA1C expression correlates with immune infiltration, possibly via lectin-mediated immune cell recruitment or tumor microenvironment modulation; (6) NAD+ metabolism link—Alzheimer's studies show NAD+ supplementation reverses EVA1C splicing defects, suggesting metabolic regulation of EVA1C function; (7) Neural repair—downregulation promotes neuron growth after hypoxic-ischemic injury, indicating EVA1C may normally restrain neurite outgrowth. The 56 ClinVar CNVs represent a significant burden of 21q genomic instability potentially affecting neural development, cognitive function, and cancer susceptibility. EVA1C represents a validated Slit/axon guidance receptor, an emerging glioma prognostic biomarker, an Alzheimer's disease splicing target, and a candidate gene for neurodevelopmental disorders involving chromosome 21.
