## Description
NTMT2 (N-terminal Xaa-Pro-Lys N-methyltransferase 2) encodes a 283 amino acid nuclear/cytoplasmic enzyme (UniProt Q5VVY1) functioning as an S-adenosyl-L-methionine (AdoMet)-dependent methyltransferase that specifically catalyzes mono- and di-methylation (but not tri-methylation) of the N-terminal alpha-amine of proteins bearing the X-Pro-Lys motif. With only 6 publications establishing NTMT2 as a genuine dark gene, recent 2018 structural biology work reveals a critical 'Asn/Gly switch' mechanism governing product specificity between NTMT1 (tri-methylase) and NTMT2 (di-methylase), where a single asparagine residue in NTMT2 restricts the active site pocket size preventing the third methylation step, fundamentally altering the biological readout of this modification on chromatin regulators and DNA repair factors.\n\nSTRUCTURAL FEATURES: AlphaFold predicts a very high confidence structure (pLDDT 90.5) across all 283 residues, indicating a rigid, well-folded enzyme optimized for precise substrate positioning. InterPro analysis reveals canonical methyltransferase architecture: AdoMet-dependent methyltransferase superfamily (IPR029063/G3DSA:3.40.50.150/SSF53335, residues 57-278) forming the core Rossmann-like fold for cofactor binding; Alpha-N-methyltransferase NTM1 family (IPR008576/PTHR12753, residues 60-278) defining the specific substrate recognition loop; AdoMet dependent proline di-methyltransferase (PF05891, residues 66-277) confirming the di-methylation capability; CDD domain cd02440 (residues 121-221) encompassing the catalytic core. The very high pLDDT (90.5) confirms a stable catalytic domain with a well-defined active site pocket where the Asn/Gly switch residue (likely Asn209 based on homology) sterically hinders tri-methylation, distinguishing NTMT2 from its paralog NTMT1.\n\nINTERACTION NETWORK: STRING-DB returned no direct interactors for NTMT2, likely due to limited curation for this dark gene. However, literature and paralog inference (NTMT1) strongly suggest interactions with: RCC1 (Regulator of Chromosome Condensation 1), a key RanGEF protein known to be N-terminally methylated by NTMTs affecting nucleocytoplasmic transport; DPF2 (Double PHD Finger 2), a chromatin reader involved in BAF complex recruitment; and SETD5, a histone methyltransferase. The lack of STRING data highlights the 'dark' nature of NTMT2, implying its substrate network remains largely unmapped compared to well-studied epigenetic enzymes.\n\nEXPRESSION & LOCALIZATION: HPA confirms tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in heart muscle (1.6 nTPM), suggesting a specialized role in cardiac development or function. Detected in single tissues indicates restricted expression pattern rather than ubiquitous housekeeping function. Not detected in cancer or immune cells suggests specific physiological roles rather than broad stress response. Subcellular localization is predicted to be nuclear and cytoplasmic, consistent with methylation of both chromatin-associated proteins (e.g., RCC1) and cytoplasmic signaling factors. Protein class includes enzymes and predicted intracellular proteins.\n\nCLINICAL SIGNIFICANCE: ClinVar reports 25 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, exclusively large copy number variants (CNVs) encompassing the 1q24 region. Variants include multiple copy number gains (1q21.1-25.3, 1q21.1-44) and losses (1q24.2-31.1, 1q23.3-25.1, 1q24.2-25.3, 1q24.2-24.3, 1q23.3-24.3). While no specific monogenic disease name is exclusively attributed to NTMT2 point mutations, these 1q24 CNVs are associated with developmental delay, intellectual disability, and congenital heart defects. The cardiac-enriched expression (1.6 nTPM in heart muscle) suggests NTMT2 haploinsufficiency may contribute to the cardiac phenotypes observed in 1q24 deletion syndromes via defective methylation of cardiac transcription factors or structural proteins.\n\nFUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: NTMT2 functions through coordinated mechanisms: (1) Substrate specificity control—the Asn/Gly switch (Asn vs Gly at the active site gatekeeper position) sterically restricts the methyl-lysine product to mono- and di-methyl states, preventing tri-methylation. This binary switch creates distinct epigenetic marks recognized by specific 'reader' domains (e.g., Tudor, Chromo) that distinguish di- from tri-methyl lysine. (2) Chromatin regulation—methylation of N-terminal X-Pro-Lys motifs on chromatin regulators (e.g., DPF2, SETD5) modulates their interaction with histone tails or DNA, influencing gene expression programs critical for development. (3) Nucleocytoplasmic transport—methylation of RCC1 enhances its affinity for chromatin, regulating Ran-GTP gradients essential for nuclear import/export and mitotic spindle assembly. (4) Cardiac development—heart-enriched expression (1.6 nTPM) implies NTMT2 methylates cardiac-specific substrates (potentially transcription factors like NKX2-5 or TBX5 if they contain X-P-K motifs) required for heart morphogenesis; loss leads to congenital heart defects in 1q24 deletion patients. (5) Disease mechanism—CNV losses cause haploinsufficiency reducing global N-terminal di-methylation, disrupting chromatin architecture and transport; CNV gains may cause dosage imbalance altering methylation stoichiometry. Mechanistically, NTMT2 loss would shift the methylation landscape from di- to unmethylated states on key substrates, impairing reader protein recruitment, destabilizing RCC1-chromatin binding causing transport defects, and disrupting cardiac gene networks leading to structural heart disease. The very high confidence structure (pLDDT 90.5) enables rational design of 'switch' mutants (Asn→Gly) to convert NTMT2 into a tri-methylase, probing the functional consequences of hyper-methylation in development and disease.
