## Description
TSPAN1 (Tetraspanin-1) is a 241-amino acid plasma membrane and vesicular tetraspanin family protein (UniProt O60635) representing a genuine dark gene with only 117 publications despite its critical role as an organizer of tetraspanin web complexes regulating angiogenesis via PI3K/Akt signaling in triple-negative breast cancer, ferroptosis suppression in endometrial cancer, pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma progression, cervical cancer immune evasion, and intestine-enriched expression. The mechanistic hypothesis: TSPAN1 functions as a membrane organizer that assembles tetraspanin web complexes by homodimerization and heteromeric interactions with partner proteins via its large extracellular loop (EC2, residues 110-212). The EC2 domain contains conserved cysteine residues (CCG motif, residues 64-86) forming disulfide bonds that stabilize a protruding loop structure for protein-protein recognition. TSPAN1 recruits integrins (ITGB1), growth factor receptors, and signaling molecules into membrane microdomains, facilitating their activation and downstream signaling. In triple-negative breast cancer, TSPAN1 promotes angiogenesis by enhancing VEGF receptor signaling through PI3K/Akt pathway activation—TSPAN1-integrin complexes cluster VEGFR2, increasing phosphorylation and endothelial cell migration. In endometrial cancer, TSPAN1 suppresses ferroptosis by stabilizing system Xc- (cystine/glutamate antiporter) or GPX4 through tetraspanin web organization, preventing lipid peroxidation and iron-dependent cell death; TSPAN1 knockdown induces ferroptosis sensitivity. In pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma, TSPAN1 promotes tumor progression via exosome-mediated stromal reprogramming—TSPAN1-enriched exosomes transfer oncogenic signals to cancer-associated fibroblasts. In cervical cancer, TSPAN1+ tumor endothelial progenitor cells exhibit altered immune checkpoint expression (PD-L1, CTLA-4), suggesting TSPAN1 regulates immune evasion through MHC class I/II organization.
