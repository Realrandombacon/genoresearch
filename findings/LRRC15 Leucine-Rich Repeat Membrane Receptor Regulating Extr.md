# LRRC15: Leucine-Rich Repeat Membrane Receptor Regulating Extracellular Matrix Remodeling and Cell Adhesion

## Description
LRRC15 (Gene ID: 84667, UniProt Q8TF66) is a 581 amino acid type I transmembrane protein located on chromosome 3q29 (NC_000003.12). InterPro domain analysis reveals a characteristic architecture of multiple Leucine-Rich Repeats (LRRs) (IPR001611/IPR032675/PF00560/PF13855, positions ~56-355) forming a curved solenoid structure ideal for protein-protein interactions, specifically classified within the Multifunctional Developmental and Immune Receptor family (IPR050328/PTHR24373, positions 223-486). The C-terminal region contains a cysteine-rich flanking domain (IPR000483/SM00082, positions 423-475) likely stabilizing the LRR cap or mediating dimerization. AlphaFold structure prediction (AF-Q8TF66-F1) yields a very high-confidence model (global pLDDT 83.6), depicting a classic horseshoe-shaped LRR ectodomain connected to a short transmembrane helix and cytoplasmic tail, consistent with a cell surface receptor or co-receptor function. Human Protein Atlas classifies LRRC15 as a predicted membrane protein and transporter involved in transport processes, localized to vesicles and the plasma membrane. Expression is tissue-enhanced, highest in skin (6.7 nTPM) and lymphoid tissue (4.6 nTPM), with broad detection in many tissues; it is cancer-enhanced, suggesting upregulation in tumor microenvironments. STRING database identifies 10 interaction partners heavily enriched for extracellular matrix (ECM) components and adhesion molecules: COL11A1 (score 0.525, collagen type XI alpha 1 chain), ADGRL1/2/3 (0.514 each, adhesion GPCRs), VSIG8 (0.503, V-set and immunoglobulin domain containing 8), LRRC18/59 (0.495/0.480, paralogs), FAP (0.477, fibroblast activation protein), BGN (0.467, biglycan), and THBS2 (0.460, thrombospondin 2). This network strongly implicates LRRC15 in ECM organization, cell-matrix adhesion, and potentially modulating growth factor availability or signaling via interactions with collagens and proteoglycans. ClinVar lists 39 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains and losses spanning 3q22-29 (multi-megabase regions affecting numerous genes), plus one 3' UTR variant (c.*120G>T); no specific monogenic disease is yet attributed solely to LRRC15, but dosage sensitivity of the 3q29 region is linked to developmental delays, intellectual disability, and susceptibility to schizophrenia. Conservation is evident across mammals (mouse Q80X72, rat Q8R5M3). Functional hypothesis: LRRC15 functions as a cell surface co-receptor or scaffold that binds specific ECM components (e.g., collagens, biglycan) via its LRR domain, regulating local matrix architecture, cell migration, and mechanotransduction signals. Interaction with FAP suggests a role in fibroblast activation and tissue remodeling, potentially influencing wound healing or tumor stroma formation. The link to adhesion GPCRs (ADGRLs) implies it may modulate their ligand binding or signaling output. Disease mechanism: Haploinsufficiency or overexpression due to 3q CNVs disrupts ECM homeostasis and cell adhesion dynamics, contributing to neurodevelopmental defects (via altered neuronal migration/glial scaffolding) and predisposing to malignancies by altering the tumor microenvironment. The 3' UTR variant may affect mRNA stability or localization. Priority experiments include co-IP/SPR to define specific ECM binding partners, CRISPR knockout in fibroblasts/cancer cells to assess effects on migration/invasion, and investigation of LRRC15's role in modulating ADGRL signaling.
