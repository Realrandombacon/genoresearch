## Description
IGFL3 (Insulin growth factor-like family member 3, UniProt Q6UXB1) is a 125-amino acid secreted protein located on chromosome 19q13.32. InterPro analysis reveals canonical insulin/IGF-like growth factor architecture: insulin growth factor-like family domain (IPR032744/PF14653, aa 1-120/34-115) forming the characteristic cysteine-rich fold with conserved disulfide bonds typical of IGF superfamily members. The protein belongs to the IGFL3-related subfamily (PTHR34827, aa 1-120), suggesting functional homology to insulin-like growth factors that regulate cell proliferation, differentiation, and survival. GO annotations indicate growth factor activity and potential roles in developmental processes. AlphaFold structure prediction achieves high confidence (pLDDT 76.0), indicating a well-folded stable secreted protein with defined secondary structure optimal for receptor binding and signal transduction. Human Protein Atlas shows tissue-enriched expression (detected in some tissues) with highest expression in skin (25.7 nTPM), cancer-enhanced pattern, and cell type-enhanced specificity — consistent with roles in epidermal homeostasis, wound healing, and potential oncogenic functions where IGF signaling is frequently dysregulated. STRING interaction network reveals 3 partners including IGFLR1 (0.702, IGF-like family receptor 1 — highest confidence interaction confirming ligand-receptor pairing within the IGFL signaling axis), PPP5D1 (0.583, PPPDE peptidase domain-containing protein), and PLGLB2 (0.448, plasminogen-like protein B2 involved in proteolysis). This interactome suggests IGFL3 functions within growth factor signaling networks potentially linked to extracellular matrix remodeling and proteolytic cascades. ClinVar contains 11 pathogenic variants consisting of large copy-number gains and losses across 19q13.31-q13.42 plus intronic variants (c.350+147A>G), demonstrating dosage sensitivity where genomic rearrangements disrupting chromosome 19q cause disease phenotypes potentially affecting skin development and growth regulation. Conservation across mammals (mouse Q6B9Z0, 140 aa; ferret M3Y1Z5, 144 aa; chimpanzee A0A6D2XTS1, 125 aa) indicates ancient evolutionary origin tied to fundamental growth regulatory mechanisms. IGFL3 likely functions as a secreted growth factor ligand, using its IGF-like fold to bind and activate IGFLR1 receptor tyrosine kinase while triggering downstream PI3K/AKT and MAPK signaling cascades that regulate keratinocyte proliferation, epidermal differentiation, and tissue repair — mechanisms critical for skin homeostasis and frequently co-opted in cancers where IGF pathway activation drives tumor progression.
