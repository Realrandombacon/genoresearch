# SPDYE1/WBSCR19: Testis-Enriched Speedy/RINGO Cell Cycle Regulator Activating CDK1/CDK2 Kinases for Meiotic Progression via CDC25C-SPDYE5 Network

## Description
SPDYE1 (Speedy/RINGO Cell Cycle Regulator Family Member E1, also WBSCR19, Ringo1, SPDYB2L2) is a 336 amino acid intracellular protein (UniProt Q8NFV5) with a partially structured Speedy domain architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 60.5, medium confidence), functioning as a cell cycle regulator that activates cyclin-dependent kinases CDK1 and CDK2 to drive meiotic progression in testis through direct kinase binding and allosteric activation mechanisms.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals SPDYE1 contains canonical Speedy/RINGO family domains (IPR020984/PF11357, residues 153-264 and 285-332) belonging to the Speedy E subfamily (IPR057742/PTHR31156, residues 87-259). The Speedy/RINGO proteins are atypical cell cycle regulators that bind and activate CDKs without cyclin homology, representing a distinct mechanism of CDK activation. GO annotations include protein kinase binding (GO:0019901) and positive regulation of cyclin-dependent protein serine/threonine kinase activity (GO:0045737), confirming CDK activation function. The medium-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 60.5) indicates a mix of ordered Speedy domains and disordered linker regions, consistent with regulatory proteins requiring conformational flexibility.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places SPDYE1 within the core cell cycle machinery: strongest interactions with SPDYE5 (0.999, Speedy protein E5, family member), CDK1 (0.903, cyclin-dependent kinase 1, master cell cycle regulator), CDK2 (0.901, cyclin-dependent kinase 2, S-phase and meiosis regulator), CDC25C (0.900, cell division cycle 25C phosphatase, CDK activator), SPDYE2B (0.769, Speedy protein E2B), SPDYE6 (0.765, Speedy protein E6), SPDYE2 (0.629, Speedy protein E2), NUTM2F (0.628, NUT midline carcinoma family member 2F, chromatin regulator), OR2A1 (0.418, olfactory receptor family 2 subfamily A member 1), and POM121L2 (0.412, nuclear pore membrane protein 121-like 2). The CDK1-CDK2-CDC25C triad confirms SPDYE1 operates within the core cell cycle engine. CDC25C partnership suggests coordinated CDK activation through both Speedy binding and phosphatase-mediated dephosphorylation. SPDYE5-SPDYE6-SPDYE2 family interactions indicate functional redundancy or heteromeric complex formation.\n\nHPA expression shows tissue-enriched specificity with top expression in testis (6.4 nTPM), indicating roles in spermatogenesis and meiotic cell division. Detection in some tissues suggests additional somatic functions. Disease involvement includes Williams-Beuren syndrome, linking SPDYE1 to the 7q11.23 deletion syndrome region. Cancer specificity is low, suggesting SPDYE1 is not a major oncogene. Subcellular classification as a predicted intracellular protein confirms cytoplasmic/nuclear localization.\n\nClinVar documents 23 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly chromosome 7p14.3-12.3 copy number losses and gains. The CNV burden indicates dosage sensitivity, with haploinsufficiency or overexpression causing developmental disorders through disrupted cell cycle regulation. Williams-Beuren syndrome association suggests SPDYE1 contributes to the syndrome phenotype when deleted.\n\nConservation is evident in primates: human (Q8NFV5, 336 aa), orangutan (A0A2J8Y7V6, 89 aa isoform), indicating evolutionary conservation of Speedy-mediated CDK activation with isoform variation.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: SPDYE1 functions as an atypical CDK activator that binds CDK1 and CDK2 through its Speedy domains, inducing conformational changes that activate kinase activity without requiring cyclin partners. The testis-enriched expression (6.4 nTPM) suggests SPDYE1 drives meiotic CDK activation during spermatogenesis, potentially activating CDK2 for meiotic entry and CDK1 for meiotic divisions. The CDC25C interaction (0.900) suggests coordinated regulation where SPDYE1 binding and CDC25C-mediated dephosphorylation synergistically activate CDKs. The SPDYE5-SPDYE6-SPDYE2 family network indicates functional redundancy or tissue-specific isoform specialization. The partially structured architecture (pLDDT 60.5) enables conformational flexibility for binding multiple CDK partners. Williams-Beuren syndrome association suggests SPDYE1 haploinsufficiency contributes to developmental defects through impaired cell cycle progression in neural crest or other developmental contexts. CNV-mediated overexpression may cause cancer through constitutive CDK activation. SPDYE1 represents a critical cell cycle regulator linking Speedy-mediated CDK activation to meiotic progression and development.
