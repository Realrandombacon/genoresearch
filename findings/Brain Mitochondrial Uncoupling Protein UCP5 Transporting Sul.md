## Description
SLC25A14 encodes a 325 aa brain mitochondrial carrier protein (UniProt O95258), also known as UCP5 or BMCP1 (brain mitochondrial carrier protein 1). InterPro analysis reveals canonical mitochondrial carrier architecture with three solcar repeats (IPR002067, IPR018108, IPR023395, IPR050391, PF00153; aa 41-323) forming the characteristic 6-transmembrane helix structure of the SLC25 family. AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (pLDDT 78.5), indicating a well-folded mitochondrial transporter. HPA shows ubiquitous expression across all tissues with mitochondrial localization, consistent with its role in cellular energy metabolism. STRING interactions reveal connections to vision-related proteins (OPN1MW 0.651, OPN1LW 0.593, NYX 0.647), cerebellar antigen CDR1 (0.716), oxidative stress regulators (GPX6/7/8 0.573-0.575, PARK7/DJ-1 0.530), and trafficking protein COG3 (0.674), suggesting roles in neuronal function and redox homeostasis. ClinVar contains 169 pathogenic variants (mostly copy number gains/losses on Xq23-28), though specific disease associations remain undefined, consistent with X-chromosome location. Literature (13 papers) reveals key mechanistic insights: SLC25A14/UCP5 transports sulfur oxyanions, phosphate, and dicarboxylates across the mitochondrial inner membrane (47 citations, 2019), distinguishing it from classical uncoupling proteins. The protein is linked to X-linked congenital nystagmus (NYS1), dopaminergic neuron oxidant stress attenuation via DJ-1 interaction, and Himalayan marmot hypoxic/cold adaptation. As a mitochondrial anion transporter, SLC25A14 likely regulates mitochondrial membrane potential, ROS production, and metabolic flux of sulfur compounds and organic acids. The vision-related STRING partners (opsins, nyctalopin) and nystagmus association suggest specific roles in retinal/cerebellar function. The PARK7 interaction links SLC25A14 to Parkinson's disease pathways through oxidative stress management. The 169 ClinVar variants indicate potential disease associations requiring phenotypic characterization, possibly involving neurodevelopmental, visual, or metabolic disorders. SLC25A14 represents an emerging therapeutic target for neurodegenerative disease, retinal disorders, and metabolic conditions, though precise substrate specificity, regulatory mechanisms, and disease-causing mutations require further characterization.
