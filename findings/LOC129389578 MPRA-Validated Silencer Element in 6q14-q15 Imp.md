# LOC129389578: MPRA-Validated Silencer Element in 6q14-q15 Imprinted Tumor Suppressor Critical Region

## Description
LOC129389578 (Gene ID: 129389578) is a functionally validated cis-regulatory silencer element mapping to chromosome 6q14.3 (89,449,365-89,449,565 bp, ~200 bp span). RefSeq annotation explicitly states: "MPRA-validated peak5952 silencer" - validated by STARR-seq massively parallel reporter assays in K562 erythroleukemia cells as repressing super core promoter SCP1 activity. Multiple evidence lines confirm non-coding regulatory function: (1) No UniProt accession (not a protein); (2) Genomic span of 200 bp is far too small for protein-coding gene; (3) ClinVar contains 6 pathogenic copy number variants spanning 6q14.1-16.1, 6q13-24.1, 6q14.3-15, 6q14.2-16.1, and 6q14.3-16.1 regions (deletions 6.7-17.8 Mb, duplication 67.7 Mb whole chromosome arm), associated with 6q rearrangement syndromes featuring intellectual disability, developmental delay, craniofacial dysmorphism, cardiac defects, and cancer predisposition. The 6q14-q15 region contains critical dosage-sensitive imprinted genes including PLAG1 (pleiomorphic adenoma gene 1, HMG box transcription factor, growth regulation, Wilms tumor), PARK2 (parkin RBR E3 ubiquitin protein ligase, mitophagy, Parkinson disease), BTBD9 (BTB domain containing 9, restless leg syndrome), and multiple tumor suppressor genes; (4) No HPA expression (non-coding - Ensembl ID could not be resolved); (5) No STRING interactions (non-coding). Mechanistic hypothesis: LOC129389578 functions as a transcriptional silencer that represses gene expression in hematopoietic or neural contexts. The element likely recruits co-repressor complexes (e.g., Polycomb repressive complex 2, NuRD, or HDAC-containing complexes) via sequence-specific DNA-binding proteins to establish repressive chromatin (H3K27me3, H3K9me3, DNA methylation). K562 erythroleukemia validation suggests activity in erythroid/myeloid lineage. The 6q14-q15 region contains imprinted domains with parent-of-origin-specific expression critical for growth regulation and tumor suppression. Pathogenic 6q CNVs disrupt silencer-enhancer balance, imprinting control, and TAD architecture, causing long-range dysregulation of PLAG1, PARK2, and BTBD9. PLAG1 overexpression causes salivary gland pleiomorphic adenoma and Wilms tumor. PARK2 loss causes mitochondrial dysfunction and Parkinson disease. 6q deletions are recurrent in myeloid malignancies. This represents a critical class of "dark genes" that are actually functional non-coding regulatory elements misannotated as genes.
