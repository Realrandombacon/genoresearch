## Description
ATAT1 (Alpha-tubulin N-acetyltransferase 1, also known as MEC-17 homolog) is a partially characterized dark gene with 91 publications, representing an emerging target in cytoskeletal biology and disease. UniProt accession Q5SQI0 identifies it as a 421-amino acid enzyme belonging to the GNAT (Gcn5-related N-acetyltransferase) superfamily that specifically catalyzes acetylation of alpha-tubulin at lysine 40 (K40), a well-studied post-translational modification associated with stable, long-lived microtubules. The gene is located on chromosome 6p21.33 and encodes the primary enzyme responsible for bulk alpha-tubulin acetylation in mammalian cells.\n\nInterPro scan reveals comprehensive domain architecture characteristic of the ATAT1/MEC-17 subfamily: (1) GNAT domain, ATAT-type (IPR007965, residues 1-201) defining the catalytic core with GO annotations for tubulin N-acetyltransferase activity (GO:0019799) and alpha-tubulin acetylation (GO:0071929); (2) Alpha-tubulin N-acetyltransferase family (IPR038746/PTHR12327, residues 1-343) encompassing the functional enzyme unit; (3) GNAT acetyltransferase, Mec-17 (PF05301/MF_03130, residues 3-190/4-201) showing conservation with C. elegans MEC-17; (4) Alpha-tubulin GNAT domain profile (PS51730, residues 1-190); (5) N-Acyltransferase superfamily (cd04301, residues 91-148) containing the catalytic residues; (6) CATH-Gene3D homologous superfamilies G3DSA:3.40.630.30 (residues 53-192) and G3DSA:6.20.370.120 (residues 1-52) indicating structural folds. GO annotations include microtubule localization (GO:0005874), consistent with substrate targeting.\n\nAlphaFold predicts a medium-confidence structure (global pLDDT 68.4), indicating a protein with both ordered catalytic domains and flexible/disordered regions.
