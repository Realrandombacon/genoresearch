## Description

AlphaFold structure (AF-Q5T7R7-F1) predicts a partially folded protein with medium confidence (global pLDDT 62.6), suggesting a mix of ordered domains and flexible/disordered regions typical of proteins involved in dynamic protein-protein interactions or regulatory functions. HPA expression reveals testis-enriched expression (23.7 nTPM) with tissue-specific RNA distribution, classified as predicted intracellular and membrane proteins, consistent with a role in spermatogenesis. STRING interactions reveal connections to nuclear envelope and transport machinery: NUP210L (0.511, nucleoporin Nup210-like protein involved in nuclear pore complex assembly), SPTY2D1OS (0.481, spt2 domain-containing 1 opposite strand), and SLC35E2A (0.445, solute carrier family 35 member E2A). ClinVar lists 7 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number gains/losses and inversions on chromosome 1p34.1-36.33, indicating clinical significance despite no specific monogenic disease annotation yet. Literature search reveals only 3 papers with a 2021 CRISPR/Cas9 study demonstrating C1orf185 is dispensable for fertility in mouse models despite testis-specific expression, suggesting functional redundancy or subtle phenotypes not captured in laboratory conditions. Functional hypothesis: C1orf185 functions as a testis-specific nuclear envelope-associated protein that facilitates nuclear-cytoplasmic transport or chromatin organization during spermatogenesis via DUF4718 domain-mediated interactions with nuclear pore components. Mechanistic proposal: C1orf185 localizes to nuclear envelope during meiotic prophase where it interacts with NUP210L to stabilize nuclear pore complex assembly in developing spermatocytes, potentially regulating mRNA export of spermatogenesis-specific transcripts; loss-of-function variants may contribute to non-obstructive azoospermia through subtle defects in nuclear transport efficiency that manifest only under physiological stress conditions.
