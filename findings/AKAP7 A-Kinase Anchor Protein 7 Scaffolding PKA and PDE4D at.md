## Description
AKAP7 (A-Kinase Anchor Protein 7, UniProt Q9P0M2/O43687) is a 348 amino acid (isoform gamma) vesicle-associated scaffolding protein with moderate characterization (54 papers) that functions as a critical organizer of cAMP signaling microdomains by tethering Protein Kinase A (PKA) and Phosphodiesterase 4D (PDE4D) to intracellular membranes, thereby regulating cardiac calcium handling, neuronal plasticity, and blood-brain barrier integrity. The protein contains a unique N-terminal cyclic phosphodiesterase-like domain (IPR009097/G3DSA:3.90.1140.10 residues 76-286, IPR019510/PF10469 residues 85-282) structurally homologous to RNA ligases but adapted for potential regulatory interactions, and a C-terminal PKA Regulatory Subunit Binding Domain (IPR019511/PF10470 residues 289-345) that anchors the PKA holoenzyme. STRING interactions reveal extremely high-confidence partnerships with core cAMP signaling components: PRKACA (0.965, catalytic subunit alpha), PRKACB (0.963, catalytic subunit beta), PRKACG (0.931, catalytic subunit gamma), AKAP1 (0.924, mitochondrial AKAP), PRKAR2A (0.905, regulatory subunit II alpha), AKAP5 (0.849, neuronal AKAP), YBEY (0.832, metallo-beta-lactamase), PDE4D (0.603, cAMP-specific phosphodiesterase), CACNA1C (0.474, L-type calcium channel), and PRKAR2B (0.434, regulatory subunit II beta), confirming its role as a central hub in the PKA-AKAP-PDE signalosome. HPA data shows tissue-enhanced expression detected in all tissues, with top expression in the adrenal gland (54.4 nTPM), low cancer specificity, low immune cell specificity, cell type-enhanced single cell specificity, and vesicular subcellular localization, consistent with its function in compartmentalized signaling. AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 76.7) indicates high confidence with a well-folded architecture featuring the distinct phosphodiesterase-like fold and an amphipathic helix for PKA binding.
