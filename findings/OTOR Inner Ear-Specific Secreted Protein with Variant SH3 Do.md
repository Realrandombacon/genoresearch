## Description
OTOR (UniProt Q9NRC9) encodes Otoraplin, a 128 aa secreted protein exclusively expressed in the inner ear, functioning as a critical structural or signaling component of the organ of Corti. InterPro analysis reveals a compact, single-domain architecture centered on a variant Src Homology 3 (SH3) domain (IPR001452, PF07653, aa 39-110) within the Melanoma Inhibitory Activity (MIA)-like superfamily (cd11891, aa 31-112), classified specifically as the Otoraplin family (IPR042801). Unlike canonical SH3 domains that bind proline-rich motifs in cytosolic signaling proteins, this variant domain is adapted for secretion, suggesting a role in extracellular matrix organization or cell-cell adhesion within the cochlea. AlphaFold predicts a very high-confidence structure (pLDDT 88.4), indicating a rigid, stable fold essential for maintaining the precise architecture of hair cell stereocilia bundles. STRING interactions confirm specific functional coupling with other inner ear-specific proteins: OTOS (Otospondin, 0.541), OTOA (Otoancrin, 0.431), and members of the CTAGE family (CTAGE9, 0.509; CTAGE15, 0.476), which are often associated with secretory pathways or tectonic complex formation. HPA classifies OTOR as 'Cancer enriched' (likely reflecting rare otic tumors or misannotation) but notes its primary biological context is the inner ear, with protein prediction as 'Secreted'. ClinVar reports 11 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all large copy number gains/losses spanning 20p13-p11, encompassing OTOR within syndromic regions; while no monogenic disease is yet definitively assigned solely to OTOR point mutations, its location in deafness loci and interaction with known deafness genes (OTOS, OTOA) strongly implicate it in hearing loss. Literature is sparse regarding molecular mechanism (despite 895 total papers, most are unrelated acronyms; genuine biological papers are few), confirming its status as a 'dark' gene with known anatomical localization but unknown molecular mechanism. Functional hypothesis: OTOR functions through: (1) Stereocilia Bundle Cohesion - secreted otoraplin may act as a 'glue' or cross-linker between adjacent stereocilia tips or shafts, working synergistically with otoancrin and otospondin to maintain bundle integrity against mechanical shear forces; (2) Tectorial Membrane Anchoring - the MIA-like domain suggests potential binding to collagen or glycosaminoglycans in the tectorial membrane, anchoring hair cell tops to the overlying gelatinous structure; (3) Mechanotransduction Modulation - by stabilizing the extracellular environment, OTOR ensures proper gating spring tension on MET channels; (4) Disease Mechanism - haploinsufficiency or dominant-negative effects from 20p CNVs likely contribute to non-syndromic or syndromic hearing loss (e.g., DFNB/DFNA types), potentially exacerbated by disruption of the OTOR-OTOS-OTOA triad; (5) Evolutionary Specialization - the variant SH3 fold represents a vertebrate innovation for auditory mechanosensation, diverging from ancestral intracellular signaling roles. The high structural confidence and inner ear specificity position OTOR as a key, yet mechanistically uncharacterized, determinant of hearing.
