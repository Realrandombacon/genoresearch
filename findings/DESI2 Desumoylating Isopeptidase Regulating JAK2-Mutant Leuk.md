# DESI2: Desumoylating Isopeptidase Regulating JAK2-Mutant Leukemia Survival, Pancreatic Cancer Apoptosis, and SUMO Homeostasis with Unmapped Clinical Phenotypes

## Description
DESI2 (Desumoylating Isopeptidase 2) is a 194-amino acid intracellular enzyme (UniProt Q9BSY9) representing a genuine dark gene with only 16 publications despite its critical roles in cancer biology, SUMO pathway regulation, and as a therapeutic target for myeloproliferative neoplasms. Structurally, DESI2 belongs to the PPPDE (Putative Protease Domain-containing E) family of deubiquitin-like enzymes with a canonical catalytic architecture: PPPDE peptidase domain (IPR008580/PF05903/SM01179/PS51858, residues 4-169/5-150/7-149) containing the catalytic cysteine-histidine-asparagine triad characteristic of cysteine proteases, PPPDE domain superfamily (IPR04266/G3DSA:3.90.1720.30, residues 2-168) defining the papain-like fold, and classification within the desumoylating isopeptidase family (PTHR12378, residues 4-169). The PPPDE domain mediates cleavage of SUMO (Small Ubiquitin-like Modifier) conjugates, functioning as a deSUMOylase. AlphaFold predicts high-confidence structure (pLDDT 85.9), consistent with a well-folded cysteine protease with stable catalytic core and defined substrate-binding cleft. Functionally, DESI2 localizes to vesicles with ubiquitous expression across all tissues (HPA: detected in all, low tissue specificity, low cancer specificity, cell type enhanced), classified as an enzyme/hydrolase/protease involved in the Ubl (ubiquitin-like) conjugation pathway. STRING interactions reveal exclusive connections to the SUMOylation machinery and related proteases: SENP5 (0.689, sentrin-specific protease 5), SENP1 (0.666), SENP3 (0.610), SENP6 (0.599), SENP7 (0.586), SUMO1 (0.585), SAE1 (0.581, SUMO-activating enzyme subunit 1), SUMO2 (0.564), COX20 (0.553, cytochrome c oxidase assembly protein), and USPL1 (0.526, ubiquitin-specific protease-like 1). This interaction network positions DESI2 within the dynamic SUMO cycle regulating protein stability, localization, and activity. Critically, ClinVar documents 96 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number losses spanning 1q43-44 (chr1:237818970-248924793/237958865-246428627/243204376-249224684/243364757-245372332/243453390-245467768/238681812-249224684), 1q44 deletions (chr1:244243574-245116806/244579324-246515944), large deletions (chr1:?_243668551_245027609_?), and copy number gains at 1q21.1-44 (chr1:143932350-249224684)x3 and 1q43-44 (chr1:238681812-249224684)x3, yet ZERO diseases are formally associated—a profound gap given the essential cancer and SUMO functions. Recent literature establishes emerging roles: DESI2 identified as a novel therapeutic target for JAK2-mutant leukemias (myeloproliferative neoplasms progressing to secondary AML) where DESI2 inhibition induces apoptosis in chemotherapy-resistant cells (1 citation, 2025); Phytophthora infestans RXLR effector AVR8 suppresses plant immunity by targeting DESI2 homolog, revealing conserved immune evasion mechanism (14 citations, 2023); combination of DESI2 and endostatin gene therapy significantly improves antitumor efficacy by accumulating DNA lesions, inducing apoptosis, and inhibiting angiogenesis (9 citations, 2018); DESI2 inhibits proliferation and promotes apoptosis of pancreatic cancer cells through regulating PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway (5 citations, 2018); and DESI2/IP10 combination therapy improves efficacy against murine carcinoma (2017). The mechanistic hypothesis: DESI2 functions as a deSUMOylating isopeptidase that removes SUMO modifications from target proteins, regulating their stability, nuclear-cytoplasmic shuttling, and transcriptional activity. In JAK2-mutant leukemias, DESI2 likely deSUMOylates JAK2 itself or downstream effectors (STATs), maintaining oncogenic signaling and preventing apoptosis—DESI2 inhibition would restore SUMO-mediated repression of JAK2-STAT pathway, triggering cell death. In pancreatic cancer, DESI2 may deSUMOylate negative regulators of PI3K/AKT/mTOR (e.g., PTEN, PDCD4), and its overexpression restores apoptotic sensitivity. The SENP interaction network suggests functional redundancy or substrate specificity partitioning among deSUMOylases. The vesicle localization indicates potential roles in secretory pathway SUMO regulation or exosomal SUMO cargo processing. The COX20 interaction suggests unexpected links to mitochondrial respiration regulation via SUMO. The absence of formal disease associations despite 96 pathogenic variants suggests DESI2 dysfunction contributes to myeloproliferative neoplasms, pancreatic cancer susceptibility, immunodeficiency (given plant pathogen targeting), or neurodevelopmental disorders (1q44 deletion syndrome features) not yet mapped to Mendelian phenotypes. Future work should identify DESI2 physiological SUMO substrates (JAK2, STATs, p53, PTEN), define mechanisms linking DESI2 to PI3K/AKT/mTOR regulation, investigate whether DESI2 mutations cause MPN predisposition or pancreatic cancer risk, and develop small-molecule DESI2 inhibitors for JAK2-mutant leukemia therapy.
