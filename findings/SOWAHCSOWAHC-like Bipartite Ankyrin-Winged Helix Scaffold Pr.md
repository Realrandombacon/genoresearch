## Description
SOWAHC (UniProt: Q53LP3, 525 aa) and its paralog LOC124907384 (SOWAHC-like) encode bipartite scaffold proteins with two distinct structural domains: an N-terminal winged helix-turn-helix (wHTH) DNA-binding fold (IPR058889, PF25877, residues 15-89) and a C-terminal ankyrin repeat protein-protein interaction array (IPR002110, PF12796, residues 272-372, 3 copies). This domain architecture is unique among ankyrin proteins, combining potential nucleic acid recognition with scaffold function.\n\nSTRING interaction network (10 partners, confidence 0.411-0.668) reveals SOWAHC connects to Rho GTPase signaling machinery: RHOA (0.596), RHOB (0.614), RHOF (0.411) - small GTPases regulating actin cytoskeleton; DIAPH1 (0.668, highest confidence) - formin homology protein nucleating actin filaments; CAPZB (0.542) - actin capping protein; SEPTIN10 (0.534) - GTP-binding cytoskeletal protein. This interaction profile positions SOWAHC as a Rho pathway scaffold coordinating actin polymerization and cytoskeletal remodeling.\n\nHPA data shows cytosolic localization with low tissue specificity (detected in many tissues), consistent with housekeeping cytoskeletal regulator. Classified as "essential protein" by HPA, indicating fundamental cellular role. Not detected in immune cells, suggesting non-immune functions.\n\nClinVar documents 24 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number losses and gains spanning 2q12-13 (10-50 Mb regions). Dosage sensitivity indicates SOWAHC haploinsufficiency contributes to chromosomal instability syndromes. The 2q12-13 region is associated with developmental phenotypes when disrupted.\n\nFunctional hypothesis: SOWAHC acts as a cytosolic scaffold that bridges Rho GTPase signaling (RHOA/RHOB) to actin nucleation machinery (DIAPH1, CAPZB) via ankyrin repeat-mediated protein-protein interactions. The N-terminal wHTH domain may provide regulatory autoinhibition or nucleic acid sensing. Dosage reduction impairs Rho-dependent cytoskeletal dynamics, affecting cell migration, cytokinesis, and tissue morphogenesis.
