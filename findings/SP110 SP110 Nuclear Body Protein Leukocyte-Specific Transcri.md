## Description
SP110 encodes a 689 aa nucleoplasmic protein (UniProt Q9HB58) that functions as a critical leukocyte-specific transcriptional coactivator and antiviral restriction factor, integrating interferon signaling with chromatin remodeling within promyelocytic leukemia (PML) nuclear bodies to regulate host-virus interactions and gene expression. This moderately studied gene (emerging clinical significance) has 98 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants though specific monogenic diseases await full characterization beyond susceptibility to viral infections and potential immunodeficiency. The protein features a multi-domain architecture enabling coordinated DNA binding, chromatin reading, and protein-protein interactions: N-terminal HSR domain (IPR004865, residues 1-108) mediating nuclear body targeting and homo/hetero-oligomerization; central SAND domain (IPR000770/G3DSA:3.10.390.10/cd09032-like, residues 454-535/448-544) providing DNA binding capacity; C-terminal PHD-type zinc finger (IPR001965/IPR019786/G3DSA:3.30.40.10, residues 536-578/534-579) recognizing histone modifications and mediating ubiquitin ligase activity; adjacent Bromodomain (IPR001487/G3DSA:1.20.920.10, residues 581-676/580-681) binding acetylated lysines on histones to recruit transcriptional machinery; classified within SP100/SP140 nuclear body family with GO terms for DNA binding, transcription regulation, host-virus interaction, and nucleus localization. STRING interactions reveal SP110 operates within high-confidence antiviral and nuclear body networks: SP100 (0.833), SP100 nuclear body protein forming heteromeric complexes; XAF1 (0.798), XIAP-associated factor 1 regulating apoptosis; DDX58 (0.784), RIG-I-like receptor detecting viral RNA; IFI35 (0.780), interferon-induced protein 35 restricting viral replication; SAMD9L (0.777), sterile alpha motif domain-containing protein 9-like antiviral factor; PARP9 (0.775), poly(ADP-ribose) polymerase family member 9 regulating interferon response; OAS1 (0.773)/OAS2 (0.766), 2-5 oligoadenylate synthetases degrading viral RNA; IFIT3 (0.768), interferon-induced protein with tetratricopeptide repeats 3; STAT1 (0.719), signal transducer and activator of transcription 1 mediating interferon signaling. HPA confirms low tissue specificity with detection in all tissues, low cancer specificity, cell type enhanced pattern, exclusive nucleoplasm localization consistent with nuclear body function, classified as disease-related gene/transcription factor involved in host-virus interaction and transcription regulation. ClinVar lists 98 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large-scale copy number gains (2q35-37.3 chr2:215591606-239217386 x3, 2q33.3-37.3 chr2:206965837-242783384 x3), splice site mutations (c.1447+1G>C, c.1447+1G>A, c.1448-2A>G, c.1279+1G>T), frameshift deletions/insertions (c.1359_1360del p.Lys453_Ser454insTer, c.708del p.Asp237fs, c.1493_1494insCAAC p.Pro499fs, c.1103_1109del p.Pro368fs) disrupting the SAND, PHD, or Bromodomain regions, demonstrating strong dosage sensitivity and functional disruption leading to potential immunodeficiency and viral susceptibility. AlphaFold structure prediction yields medium-low confidence (global pLDDT 58.0), revealing a partially structured protein with ordered domain cores (SAND, PHD, Bromodomain) connected by flexible linker regions consistent with intrinsically disordered regions enabling conformational plasticity during nuclear body assembly and chromatin engagement. Mechanistically, SP110 operates through integrated modes: (1) Antiviral restriction—functions as interferon-stimulated gene (ISG) product restricting viral replication via direct interaction with viral components or modulation of host factors; (2) Nuclear body assembly—HSR domain mediates recruitment to PML nuclear bodies where it complexes with SP100, PML, and other factors to create antiviral compartments; (3) Transcriptional coactivation—PHD and Bromodomains read histone marks (methylation/acetylation) recruiting transcriptional machinery to interferon-responsive genes; (4) STAT1 synergy—physical and functional interaction with STAT1 amplifies interferon signaling output; (5) DNA damage response—potential role in sensing/repairing DNA lesions within nuclear bodies; (6) Apoptosis regulation—XAF1 interaction suggests involvement in cell death pathways during infection. Loss-of-function causes defective PML nuclear body formation, impaired interferon responses, increased viral susceptibility, and potential dysregulation of hematopoietic gene expression.
