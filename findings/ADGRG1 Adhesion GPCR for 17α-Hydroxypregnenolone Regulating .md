## Description
ADGRG1 (Gene ID: 9289, UniProt: Q9Y653, aliases: GPR56, BFPP, TM7XN1) encodes a 693-amino acid adhesion G protein-coupled receptor (aGPCR) mapping to chromosome 16q21 (57,619,737-57,665,566 bp, ~45.8 kb genomic span). This is a well-characterized receptor with extensive functional data: (1) UniProt Q9Y653 confirmed (693 aa, ~77 kDa predicted molecular weight); (2) InterPro domains include: GPS motif (IPR000203, aa 342-394) for autoproteolytic cleavage, GPCR family 2 secretin-like (IPR000832, aa 403-661), GAIN domain superfamily (IPR046338, aa 198-382) for adhesion, PTX/LNS-like domain (IPR040679, aa 29-162) for extracellular matrix binding, and 7TM rhodopsin fold (G3DSA:1.20.1070.10, aa 390-668); (3) HPA expression shows thyroid gland enrichment at 202.1 nTPM with tissue-enhanced profile detected in all tissues, classified as G-protein coupled receptor, disease-related gene, and potential drug target; (4) STRING interactions identify 10 high/medium-confidence partners: GNG2 (G-protein γ2 subunit, score 0.943), TGM2 (transglutaminase 2, score 0.852 - known ligand), CD9 (tetraspanin, score 0.719), COL3A1 (collagen III, score 0.710 - ECM ligand), GNAQ (Gαq, score 0.680), ARRB2 (β-arrestin 2, score 0.664 - desensitization), SCT (secretin, score 0.652), GNA12/13 (Gα12/13, scores 0.650/0.538 - Rho signaling), GNB1 (Gβ1, score 0.503); (5) ClinVar contains 186 pathogenic variants including point mutations (c.938T>A p.Leu313Ter, c.101T>C p.Phe34Ser, c.847del p.Glu283fs) and copy number variants spanning 16q12.2-22.1, causing bilateral frontoparietal polymicrogyria (BFPP) - a brain malformation featuring intellectual disability, seizures, ataxia, and cobblestone-like cortical surface. Mechanistic hypothesis: ADGRG1 functions as a stereospecific receptor for the steroid hormone 17α-hydroxypregnenolone (17-OH), coupling to Gαq/11 and Gα12/13 signaling pathways. The GPS motif mediates autoproteolytic cleavage at the GPCR proteolytic site, generating N-terminal (NTF) and C-terminal (7TM) fragments that remain non-covalently associated. Ligand binding to the NTF PTX/LNS domain induces conformational changes transmitted to the 7TM domain, activating G-protein signaling. TGM2 binding (high-confidence STRING partner, score 0.852) anchors ADGRG1 to extracellular matrix, regulating cell adhesion and migration during cortical development. COL3A1 interaction suggests additional ECM ligands. Gαq/11 signaling activates phospholipase Cβ, generating IP3 and DAG for calcium mobilization and PKC activation. Gα12/13 signaling activates RhoGEFs for RhoA activation, regulating cytoskeletal dynamics critical for neuronal migration. ARRB2 recruitment mediates receptor desensitization and internalization. BFPP mutations disrupt receptor trafficking, ligand binding, or G-protein coupling, causing defective radial glial scaffolding and neuronal migration during corticogenesis. This represents a well-studied "dark gene" that has emerged from orphan receptor status to characterized aGPCR with defined ligand (17-OH), signaling partners, and disease mechanism.
