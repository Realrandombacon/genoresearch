## Description
TMEM179B encodes a 219 amino acid transmembrane protein (UniProt Q7Z7N9) with a claudin-like transmembrane domain (PF26158, IPR059010, residues 7-211) and high-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 85.9), indicating a well-folded four-transmembrane helix bundle characteristic of claudin family tight junction proteins. However, HPA subcellular localization reveals nuclear speckle and nucleolar targeting rather than plasma membrane localization, suggesting divergent function from classical barrier-forming claudins. STRING interaction network identifies strong connections to transcriptional coactivator complexes: SUPT3H (0.789, SAGA complex deubiquitinase module), SUPT20H (0.788, SAGA complex scaffold), TAF12 (0.784, TFIID general transcription factor), SGF29 (0.780, SAGA complex histone acetyltransferase module), TADA3 (0.759, ADA adaptor), and TRRAP (0.752, MYC coactivator), positioning TMEM179B within chromatin remodeling and transcriptional activation machinery. The protein shows ubiquitous expression across all tissues with low tissue specificity, consistent with a housekeeping role in basal transcription. ClinVar documents 11 pathogenic copy number variants at 11q12.3, including deletions and duplications, indicating dosage sensitivity. The mechanistic hypothesis reconciles structural and interaction data: TMEM179B retains the claudin fold but has evolved nuclear localization signals that redirect it to nuclear speckles, where it functions as a membrane-associated scaffold protein that modulates SAGA/TFIID complex assembly or recruitment to chromatin, potentially regulating transcriptional elongation or mRNA processing. The 11q12.3 CNV burden suggests haploinsufficiency disrupts transcriptional homeostasis, contributing to developmental or oncogenic phenotypes through altered expression of SAGA target genes.
