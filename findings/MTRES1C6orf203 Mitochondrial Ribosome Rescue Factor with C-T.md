## Description
MTRES1 (alias C6orf203/HSPC230/PRED31, mitochondrial transcription rescue factor 1) encodes a 240 amino acid partially structured mitochondrial matrix protein (AlphaFold pLDDT 66.9) containing a mitochondrial transcription rescue factor 1 C-terminal domain (IPR057896/PF25818, aa 133-235) and belonging to the PTHR13633 family (aa 2-238). HPA classifies the protein as predicted intracellular with mitochondrial matrix localization, ubiquitous tissue distribution (detected in all tissues), low tissue specificity, and Gene Ontology annotations for transcription regulation and RNA-binding activity. ClinVar contains 19 pathogenic variants predominantly consisting of 6q15-22.2 copy number losses and gains plus one intronic variant (c.544-14C>A) associated with chromosomal deletion/duplication syndromes. STRING interactions reveal high-confidence network with mitochondrial large ribosomal subunit proteins: MRPL58 (mitochondrial ribosomal protein L58, 0.914), MALSU1 (mitochondrial assembly of ribosomal large subunit 1, 0.913), C12orf65 (mitochondrial translation release factor, 0.893), MRPL16 (0.863), MRPL33 (0.855), MRPL13 (0.854), MRPL9 (0.854), MRPL39 (0.849), MRPL40 (0.841), and MRPL57 (0.831). Conservation across vertebrates (mouse Q9CQF4 240 aa) indicates strong functional constraint. The C-terminal domain architecture combined with mitochondrial matrix localization and extensive mitoribosomal protein interactions strongly suggests MTRES1 functions as a mitochondrial ribosome rescue factor that resolves stalled translation complexes on the mitochondrial large ribosomal subunit. The protein likely acts analogously to bacterial ribosome rescue factors (ArfA/ArfB) by recognizing stalled mitoribosomes and facilitating nascent peptide release or ribosome recycling. Interaction with MALSU1 (mitoribosome assembly factor) and C12orf65 (translation release factor) places MTRES1 within the mitochondrial translation quality control pathway. The RNA-binding annotation suggests direct interaction with mitochondrial mRNA or rRNA during rescue activity. The medium-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 66.9) indicates partial disorder consistent with ribosome rescue factors that require conformational flexibility for substrate recognition. Pathogenic 6q CNVs likely cause haploinsufficiency disrupting mitochondrial translation rescue, leading to accumulation of stalled ribosomes, mitochondrial proteotoxic stress, and impaired oxidative phosphorylation, potentially contributing to metabolic disease phenotypes and developmental defects in high-energy-demand tissues.
