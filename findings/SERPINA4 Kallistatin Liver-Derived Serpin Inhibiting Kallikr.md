## Description
SERPINA4 (UniProt P29622), known as Kallistatin, encodes a 427-amino acid secreted serine protease inhibitor containing a canonical serpin domain (IPR000215/PF00079, aa 54-424) with the conserved serpin signature motif (PS00284, aa 397-407). The protein folds into the characteristic serpin superfamily architecture (IPR036186/G3DSA:2.30.39.10 & 3.30.497.10), confirmed by a high-confidence AlphaFold prediction (pLDDT: 86.7). \n\nSTRING interactions define SERPINA4's dual regulatory role: it tightly binds tissue kallikrein KLK1 (0.905) and prostate kallikrein KLK2 (0.997) to inhibit their proteolytic activity, while also interacting with the Wnt co-receptor LRP6 (0.777), suggesting a mechanism for suppressing Wnt/β-catenin signaling. Additional links include elafin/PI3 (0.864) and KLK4 (0.848). HPA confirms liver-specific synthesis (514.2 nTPM), classifying it as a plasma protein secreted into circulation.\n\nWith only 65 papers, SERPINA4 is a transitional dark gene with emerging clinical relevance beyond its initial characterization. Recent studies highlight: (1) urinary SERPINA4 as a novel electrochemiluminescence biomarker for diabetic nephropathy (2025); (2) m⁶A-dependent stabilization of SERPINA4 driving colorectal cancer progression (2026); (3) association with idiopathic nephrotic syndrome and COVID-19 induced acute kidney injury. ClinVar lists 21 pathogenic variants, predominantly 14q32 copy number alterations. This positions SERPINA4 as a critical liver-derived checkpoint regulating protease cascades (kallikrein-kinin) and morphogen signaling (Wnt), with untapped potential in renal disease diagnostics and cancer therapeutics.
