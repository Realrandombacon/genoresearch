## Description
CYB5R1 (Cytochrome b5 Reductase 1) is a 305-amino acid mitochondrial/cytosolic flavoprotein (UniProt Q9UHQ9) that represents a genuine dark gene with only 38 publications despite critical emerging roles in ferroptosis suppression and lipid metabolism. Structurally, CYB5R1 contains canonical oxidoreductase domains including FAD/NAD(P)-binding domain (IPR001433/G3DSA:3.40.50.80, residues 181-288), flavoprotein pyridine nucleotide cytochrome reductase motifs (IPR001709, residues 96-199), FAD-binding ferredoxin reductase-type domain (IPR017927, residues 44-156), and belongs to the NADH:cytochrome b5 reductase family (IPR001834/IPR039261, residues 35-304). AlphaFold predicts very high-confidence structure (pLDDT 94.4), indicating a stable enzymatic architecture typical of NADH-dependent oxidoreductases. Functionally, CYB5R1 localizes to mitochondria and cytosol with tissue-enhanced expression, highest in skeletal muscle (591.7 nTPM, HPA), and participates in lipid biosynthesis, steroid metabolism, and sterol biosynthesis. STRING interactions confirm oxidoreductase network membership through strong associations with SUOX (0.948, sulfite oxidase), DPYD (0.877, dihydropyrimidine dehydrogenase), UMPS (0.848, UMP synthase), SCD (0.820, stearoyl-CoA desaturase), DHODH (0.803, dihydroorotate dehydrogenase), FDXR (0.726, ferredoxin reductase), POR (0.709, cytochrome P450 oxidoreductase), and cytochrome b5 paralogs CYB5B (0.487) and CYB5A (0.483). Critically, ClinVar documents 20 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants (large copy number gains/losses spanning 1q21-32, SNVs including c.166-37C>T, c.476-169C>G, c.552C>T p.Gly184=) yet ZERO formally associated Mendelian diseases—a significant gap. Recent literature definitively establishes CYB5R1 as a Coenzyme Q (CoQ) reductase that suppresses ferroptosis: endothelial CYB5R1 suppresses ferroptosis and atherosclerosis (1 citation, 2025); membrane damage during ferroptosis is caused by oxidation of phospholipids catalyzed by POR and CYB5R1 (530 citations, 2020); CYB5R1 functions in parallel to FSP1 (ferroptosis suppressor protein 1) to suppress ferroptosis via CoQ reduction (2024); and CYB5R1 correlates with stemness and drug resistance in gastric cancer (5 citations, 2023). The mechanistic hypothesis: CYB5R1 functions as an NADH-dependent oxidoreductase that reduces CoQ to ubiquinol in mitochondria and cytosol, thereby preventing lipid peroxidation and ferroptotic cell death through a mechanism parallel to but distinct from FSP1. In cancer contexts, CYB5R1 overexpression promotes drug resistance by protecting tumor cells from ferroptosis-inducing therapies; in cardiovascular disease, endothelial CYB5R1 deficiency predisposes to atherosclerosis via increased ferroptotic endothelial cell death. The skeletal muscle enrichment suggests metabolic specialization for lipid handling or redox homeostasis. The absence of formal disease nomenclature despite pathogenic variants implies phenotypic complexity, compensation by related reductases (CYB5R3 causes methemoglobinemia), or requirement for specific stressors (oxidative challenge, iron overload) to manifest pathology.
