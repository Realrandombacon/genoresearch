## Description
GPSM1 (UniProt Q86YR5), or G-protein signaling modulator 1 (also known as LGN/Leu-Gly-Asn-enriched protein), encodes a 675-amino acid intracellular scaffold protein belonging to the TPR repeat superfamily. The protein architecture features three canonical GoLoco/GPR motifs (IPR003109/PF02188/PS50877, aa 495-517/548-570/596-618) that bind inactive GDP-bound Gαi subunits and inhibit GDP dissociation, plus extensive tetratricopeptide repeat domains (IPR019734/IPR011990/PF13176/PF13424, aa 21-363/485-571/581-653) forming helical scaffolds for protein-protein interactions. AlphaFold predicts medium-confidence structure (global pLDDT: 67.9), indicating a partially structured protein with ordered TPR helical bundles connected by flexible linkers—consistent with scaffold proteins requiring conformational adaptability for multi-protein complex assembly.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals GPSM1's central role in G-protein signaling: GNAI1 (0.989), GNAI3 (0.952), GNAI2 (0.947)—all three Gαi subunits confirming direct GoLoco motif binding—plus PARD3B (0.792, polarity protein), GNAO1 (0.718, Gαo), FRMPD1 (0.716, FERM-PDZ scaffolding), NUMA1 (0.644, mitotic spindle orientation), INSC (0.545, inscuteable asymmetry regulator), and RASD1 (0.453, dexamethasone-induced Ras-related protein). This network positions GPSM1 at the intersection of G-protein signaling, cell polarity, and mitotic spindle orientation.\n\nWith only 54 papers, GPSM1 qualifies as a genuine dark gene with breakthrough disease mechanisms emerging in 2025. Recent studies reveal: (1) GPSM1/CSF1 signaling disruption reprograms tumor-associated macrophages to overcome anti-PD-1 resistance in colorectal cancer—linking G-protein modulation to immunotherapy response; (2) GPSM1-MMP19 cooperation promotes colorectal cancer proliferation and epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT); (3) myeloid GPSM1 regulates atherosclerosis progression by governing monocyte/macrophage activation and chemotaxis; (4) GPSM1 impairs adipose CD103+CD73+ Treg cell subsets during obesity, disrupting metabolic homeostasis. HPA data confirms brain-enriched expression (105.6 nTPM top tissue) with nucleoplasm/Golgi localization, classified in neurogenesis and differentiation pathways. ClinVar lists 74 pathogenic variants—all chromosome 9q34.3 copy number losses/gains—confirming dosage sensitivity and implicating GPSM1 in genomic disorders. We hypothesize GPSM1 functions as a molecular switch controlling Gαi protein cycling: the GoLoco motifs sequester GDP-bound Gαi, preventing Gβγ release and maintaining signaling quiescence until upstream signals trigger conformational changes via TPR-mediated partner recruitment; in macrophages, this regulates CSF1 receptor signaling output determining M1/M2 polarization states critical for tumor immunity and atherosclerotic plaque stability—the obesity-Treg connection suggests GPSM1 integrates metabolic cues with immune cell positioning through G-protein-dependent cytoskeletal remodeling.
