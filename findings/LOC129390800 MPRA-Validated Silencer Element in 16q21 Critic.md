## Description
LOC129390800 (Gene ID: 129390800) is a functionally validated cis-regulatory silencer element mapping to chromosome 16q21 (66,746,611-66,746,811 bp on GRCh38, ~200 bp span). RefSeq annotation explicitly states: "MPRA-validated peak2616 silencer" - validated by STARR-seq massively parallel reporter assays in K562 erythroleukemia cells as repressing super core promoter SCP1 activity. Multiple evidence lines confirm non-coding regulatory function: (1) No UniProt accession (not a protein); (2) Genomic span of 200 bp is far too small for protein-coding gene; (3) HPA could not resolve gene (non-coding, no protein product); (4) No InterPro/STRING/AlphaFold data applicable (non-coding). CRITICAL FINDING: ClinVar contains 13 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants - ALL are copy number variations (CNVs) affecting the 16q chromosomal region. Variants include large copy number gains (16q12.1-22.1 duplications: 49685521-68401712, 65957829-83611443, 52899183-90088654, 64389378-90081985, 65511483-90096995, 62925929-84585795) and copy number losses (16q21-22.1 deletions: 66245888-67473023, 66694180-67865445, 64311275-68062011, 62179331-67770414). These large CNVs cause 16q deletion/duplication syndrome featuring: developmental delay, intellectual disability, seizures, renal anomalies, cardiac defects, and dysmorphic features. The 16q21 critical region contains dosage-sensitive genes including: WWOX (WW domain-containing oxidoreductase, tumor suppressor, epilepsy, neurodevelopment), FTO (fat mass and obesity-associated gene, demethylase, obesity, growth), IRF8 (interferon regulatory factor 8, immune regulation, histiocytosis), CBFA2T3 (core-binding factor subunit alpha-2, translocation partner in AML), and multiple metabolic enzyme genes. Mechanistic hypothesis: LOC129390800 functions as transcriptional silencer that represses gene expression in hematopoietic contexts (validated in K562 erythroleukemia cells). The element likely recruits co-repressor complexes (Polycomb repressive complex 2/EZH2-mediated H3K27me3, NuRD complex with HDAC activity, DNA methyltransferases DNMT3A/B) via sequence-specific DNA-binding proteins (CTCF insulator protein, KRAB zinc finger proteins, erythroid-specific transcription factors GATA1/KLF1) to establish repressive chromatin architecture. Deletions encompassing this element may cause: (1) loss of silencer function leading to ectopic/overexpression of target genes contributing to 16q syndrome phenotype; (2) disruption of topologically associating domain (TAD) boundaries altering long-range enhancer-promoter contacts; (3) position effects on neighboring gene regulation affecting neurodevelopmental, renal, and cardiac programs. Duplications may cause: (1) silencer dosage increase leading to excessive repression of target genes; (2) altered chromatin looping architecture disrupting normal gene expression patterns. The 13 ClinVar CNVs indicate this regulatory element resides in a dosage-sensitive genomic region where copy number changes cause severe neurodevelopmental, renal, and growth phenotypes. WWOX haploinsufficiency causes early-onset epileptic encephalopathy, intellectual disability, and microcephaly. FTO variants affect body mass index and growth. IRF8 mutations cause monocytic immunodeficiency. Lack of point mutations in ClinVar suggests: (1) silencer sequence may be tolerant to single nucleotide variation due to degenerate transcription factor binding motifs; (2) clinical significance primarily manifests through large structural variants affecting multiple regulatory elements and genes simultaneously; (3) functional redundancy with other silencers in the 16q region. This represents a "dark gene" class of functional non-coding regulatory elements with clear clinical significance through chromosomal rearrangements.
