## Description
NATD1 (N-acetyltransferase domain containing 1, Gene ID: 256302, aliases C17orf103/Gtlf3b) encodes a 113 amino acid intracellular protein localized to chromosome 17p11.2. UniProt accession Q8N6N6 reveals extensive cross-species conservation with Xenopus laevis homolog (109 aa, ~96% identity) and Macaca mulatta ortholog (63 aa), indicating strong evolutionary constraint on the catalytic core. AlphaFold structure (AF-Q8N6N6-F1) predicts exceptionally high-confidence folding (pLDDT 88.1), with the entire 1-113 residue range forming a stable globular domain characteristic of GCN5-related N-acetyltransferases. InterPro analysis identifies multiple GNAT superfamily signatures: IPR016181 (acyl-CoA N-acyltransferase, residues 19-103), IPR031165 (Yjdj-type GNAT domain, 22-112), IPR045057 (GNAT family, 15-111), PF14542 (PFAM GNAT domain, 30-103), PS51729 (Yjdj-type GNAT profile, 22-112), PTHR31435 (NATD1 family, 15-111), and SSF55729 (acyl-CoA N-acyltransferase superfamily, 19-103), placing NATD1 in the G3DSA:3.40.630.30 homologous superfamily with canonical acetyl-CoA binding fold. HPA expression shows skeletal muscle enrichment (37.6 nTPM) with tissue-enhanced pattern detected across all tissues and immune cell enhancement, suggesting roles in muscle metabolism or chromatin regulation in myocytes. STRING interactions identify 7 partners including DHRS7B (0.517), DRC3 (0.514), TMEM11 (0.471), GID8 (0.422), DRG2 (0.414), NT5M (0.403), and FKBP7 (0.401), implicating NATD1 in protein complex assembly potentially involving nucleocytoplasmic transport or metabolic regulation. ClinVar contains 5 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants exclusively as chromosome 17 copy number gains/losses (17p13.3-11.2 and 17p11.2 regions), indicating dosage sensitivity and potential contribution to 17p deletion/duplication syndromes. The convergence of canonical GNAT catalytic domain architecture, high-confidence AlphaFold structure, skeletal muscle expression, protein interaction network, and CNV pathogenicity strongly supports NATD1 functioning as a protein N-acetyltransferase that modifies histone or metabolic enzyme substrates in muscle tissue, where precise gene dosage is critical for neuromuscular development and 17p11.2-linked phenotypes.
