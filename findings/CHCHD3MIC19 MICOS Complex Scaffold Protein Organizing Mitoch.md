## Description
CHCHD3 (227 aa, UniProt Q9NX63), also known as MIC19 or MINOS3, is an essential inner mitochondrial membrane scaffold protein that serves as a core component of the mitochondrial contact site and cristae organizing system (MICOS) complex, maintaining cristae junction integrity and coordinating mitochondrial bioenergetic competence. The protein architecture features a conserved MICOS complex subunit domain (IPR007964/PF05300, aa29-174) that mediates heterotypic interactions within the MICOS complex, plus a C-terminal coiled-coil-helix-coiled-coil-helix (CHCH) domain (PS51808, aa180-222) characteristic of mitochondrial intermembrane space proteins that utilize disulfide relay systems for import and folding via the MIA40/CHCHD4 pathway. The full-length MICOS19 family signature spans aa1-227 (IPR052632/PTHR21588). HPA confirms tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in skeletal muscle (329.6 nTPM) and tongue (227.3 nTPM), consistent with high metabolic demand tissues requiring robust cristae architecture, plus ubiquitous detection across all tissues with strict mitochondrial localization. Mechanistically, CHCHD3 forms the central hub of MICOS complex assembly through ultra-high-confidence interactions: MICOS13/MIC13 (STRING 0.998) forms a subcomplex regulating cristae junction width; IMMT/MICOS60 (0.994) serves as the scaffold backbone anchoring MICOS to inner membrane; CHCHD10 (0.978) links MICOS to mitochondrial dynamics and neuromuscular disease pathways; APOO/MICOS27 (0.967) and APOOL/MICOS25 (0.958) stabilize cristae curvature; MTX2/MICOS32 (0.936) and CHCHD6/MICOS25 (0.919) complete the hexameric core; MICOS10 (0.875) represents an additional regulatory subunit; connections to DNAJC11 (0.560) link MICOS to Charcot-Marie-Tooth neuropathy mechanisms, while SAMM50 (0.428) bridges MICOS to the sorting and assembly machinery at outer membrane contact sites. AlphaFold prediction (pLDDT 84.7) reveals high-confidence folded structure with extended coiled-coil regions forming dimerization interfaces consistent with crystallographic studies showing MIC19 adopts V-shaped homodimers that bridge adjacent cristae membranes. Clinical significance includes 33 pathogenic/likely pathogenic ClinVar variants: large copy number losses spanning 7q31.32-36.1 (chr7:122190535-149944340 deletions), 7q32.3-36.1 losses, intronic splice variants (c.82-134T>G), though no specific monogenic syndrome has been definitively established despite strong disease associations. Conservation across mammals (mouse Q9CRB9 227 aa) indicates essential mitochondrial function maintained over ~90 million years. Literature (71 papers) establishes CHCHD3 in cancer immunometabolism and neurodegeneration: 2025 studies demonstrate CHCHD3 regulates cGAS-STING axis to remodel lung adenocarcinoma immune microenvironment through mitochondrial DNA release modulation; 2024 work shows ASB1 E3 ubiquitin ligase inhibits prostate cancer progression by K48-linked ubiquitination and proteasomal degradation of CHCHD3; 2026 reviews define CHCHD3/MIC19 roles in cristae structure regulation across metabolic and neurodegenerative diseases. The functional hypothesis posits that CHCHD3 acts as a 'cristae junction architect': imported into intermembrane space via MIA40 oxidative folding pathway where CHCH domain cysteines form stabilizing disulfides; MICOS19 homodimers assemble with MICOS13 heterodimers forming a ~300 kDa subcomplex that oligomerizes into ring-like structures at cristae junctions; these rings maintain ~10-20 nm junction diameter preventing cristae vesiculation while allowing metabolite diffusion; upon cellular stress (hypoxia, oncogenic transformation), CHCHD3 undergoes post-translational modifications (ASB1-mediated ubiquitination, phosphorylation) triggering MICOS disassembly and cristae remodeling that releases mitochondrial DNA into cytosol activating cGAS-STING innate immune signaling; in cancer, this creates immunosuppressive microenvironment through chronic type I interferon exposure driving PD-L1 upregulation; in neurodegeneration, CHCHD10-CHCHD3 complex instability causes cristae fragmentation impairing oxidative phosphorylation contributing to motor neuron death; therapeutic strategies targeting CHCHD3 include ASB1 activators for prostate cancer and MICOS stabilizers for mitochondrial myopathies.
