# PIGP: GPI Anchor Biosynthesis Enzyme Subunit Regulating Epilepsy, Congenital Anomalies, and Neurodevelopment via Phosphatidylinositol Glycan Assembly and Vesicular Trafficking

## Description
PIGP (UniProt P57054), also known as Phosphatidylinositol N-acetylglucosaminyltransferase subunit P, encodes a 158 aa membrane-bound enzyme belonging to the evolutionarily conserved GPI-anchor biosynthesis superfamily, functioning as a critical regulator of cell surface protein anchoring, vesicular trafficking, and neurodevelopment through its specific role in the final steps of GPI-anchoring. InterPro analysis reveals a compact, modular architecture: an N-terminal catalytic domain (aa 24-153, IPR016542, PF08510) containing a conserved phosphatidylinositol N-acetylglucosaminyltransferase fold capable of transferring GlcNAc-phosphatidylinositol to the C-terminal GPI-anchored protein substrate; classification within the PIG-P family (IPR013717, IPR052263) links it functionally to the PIG-A, PIG-C, and PIG-L subunits in the multi-enzyme GPI biosynthesis complex. AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (global pLDDT 82.2), indicating a well-folded globular domain with precise geometry for substrate recognition and catalysis, consistent with stable enzymatic regulators of membrane protein topology. STRING interactions define PIGP as a core node of the GPI biosynthesis complex with near-perfect connectivity: PIGC (0.999), PIGA (0.999), PIGY (0.999), DPM2 (0.998, di-phosphatidylinositol phosphatase), PIGL (0.974, phosphatidylinositol glycan N-acetylglucosaminyltransferase), CDIPT (0.812, cytochrome diphosphatidylinositol phosphatase), PYURF (0.811), PIGH (0.803), and PIGQ (0.747), suggesting roles in coordinating the sequential enzymatic steps of GPI anchor assembly within the Golgi apparatus. HPA expression shows low tissue specificity with detection in all tissues, classified as a disease-related metabolic protein localized to vesicles and membranes, with cell type enhancement consistent with roles in secretory pathway dynamics; also classified as involved in epilepsy, linking GPI anchor defects to neuronal hyperexcitability. ClinVar reports 31 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains and losses on chromosome 21q11.2-22.3 (spanning the PIGP locus), indicating genomic instability at this region potentially linked to 22q11.2 deletion syndrome (DiGeorge/Velocardiofacial syndrome) phenotypes including cardiac and craniofacial defects, though no specific monogenic disease has been definitively assigned to PIGP alone. Literature (96 papers) highlights: (1) Epilepsy Driver—PIGP deficiency causes early-onset epileptic-dyskinetic encephalopathy via impaired GPI-anchoring of neuronal surface proteins (e.g., NMDA receptors, ion channels), leading to disrupted synaptic transmission and neuronal hyperexcitability; (2) Congenital Anomalies—PIGP mutations are associated with multiple congenital anomalies-hypotonia-seizures syndrome, suggesting a role in organogenesis via proper membrane protein localization; (3) GPI Anchor Deficiency—PIGP is a key component of the 'GPI-anchored protein deficiency' phenotype, where loss of functional GPI anchors leads to accumulation of free GPI precursors and reduced surface expression of GPI-anchored proteins. Functional hypothesis: PIGP functions as a: (1) GPI Anchor Assembly Catalyst—catalyzes the transfer of GlcNAc-phosphatidylinositol to the C-terminal amino acid of GPI-anchored proteins, completing the anchor assembly in the Golgi; (2) Neurodevelopmental Regulator—ensures proper localization of synaptic proteins (e.g., NMDA receptors, ion channels) to the neuronal membrane, with defects leading to epileptic seizures and cognitive impairment; (3) Vesicular Trafficking Modulator—coordinates the movement of GPI-anchored proteins through the secretory pathway, with dysfunction causing accumulation in the ER/Golgi and loss of surface expression. Mechanistic proposal: In the Golgi, the PIG complex (PIGA, PIGC, PIGP, PIGL, etc.) sequentially modifies the lipid carrier. PIGP specifically adds the GlcNAc moiety, a step essential for the stability and function of the final GPI anchor. Defects in PIGP (via CNVs or mutations) lead to incomplete GPI anchors, causing mislocalization of critical neuronal surface proteins. This mislocalization disrupts synaptic signaling, triggering epileptic seizures. The high-confidence structure (pLDDT 82.2) supports stable catalysis. The strong STRING interactions (0.999 with PIGC/PIGA/PIGY) confirm its role as an obligate subunit of the GPI biosynthesis complex. Chromosome 21q22 CNVs disrupt PIGP dosage, contributing to the pleiotropic effects of 22q11.2 deletion syndrome. The combination of a well-folded catalytic domain, essential role in GPI anchor biosynthesis, strong complex interactions, epilepsy association, and clinical variant burden positions PIGP as a genuine dark gene where intrinsic enzymatic function is critical for membrane protein topology and neuronal health.
