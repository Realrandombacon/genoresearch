## Description
PTGES3L (Prostaglandin E synthase 3 like, UniProt E9PB15) is a 166-amino acid intracellular protein located on chromosome 17q23.1. InterPro analysis reveals a canonical HSP90 co-chaperone architecture with CS domain (IPR007052/PS51203, aa 46-154), HSP20-like chaperone superfamily membership (IPR008978/SSF49764, aa 46-142/47-142), and p23-like co-chaperone family classification (IPR045250/PTHR22932, aa 44-106) with GO annotation for Hsp90 protein binding (GO:0051879). This defines PTGES3L as a member of the p23 telomerase-binding protein family that functions as an HSP90 co-chaperone. AlphaFold structure prediction achieves high confidence (pLDDT 72.8), indicating a well-folded stable structure consistent with chaperone proteins requiring defined binding surfaces for client protein recognition. Human Protein Atlas shows tissue-enhanced expression with skeletal muscle enrichment (116.5 nTPM) and tongue expression (88.6 nTPM), detected in many tissues, immune cell enriched, and cell type enhanced specificity — suggesting roles in muscle protein homeostasis and stress response. STRING interaction network reveals 10 partners comprising the core HSP90 chaperone machinery: AARSD1 (0.800, alanyl-tRNA editing protein, highest confidence partner), HSP90B1/HSP90AB1/HSP90AA1 (0.618-0.631, heat shock protein 90 isoforms), TSC1 (0.610, tuberous sclerosis complex 1), AHSA1 (0.590, activator of HSP90 ATPase), NKTR (0.509, natural killer cell tumor recognition), PPID/PPIG (0.509/0.461, peptidyl-prolyl cis-trans isomerases), and TRAP1 (0.412, TNF receptor-associated protein 1). This interactome confirms PTGES3L functions within the HSP90 chaperone complex, likely stabilizing client proteins and regulating ATPase activity. ClinVar contains 8 pathogenic variants including splice site deletions (c.433-2del, c.433-8_433-7del) and massive copy-number gains/losses across 17q12-25.3, demonstrating dosage sensitivity where genomic rearrangements disrupting PTGES3L cause disease phenotypes. Conservation across mammals (mouse Q9D9A7, 131 aa; Phodopus roborovskii A0AAU9YND3, 155 aa) indicates ancient evolutionary origin tied to fundamental protein folding machinery. PTGES3L likely functions as an HSP90 co-chaperone that binds and stabilizes the HSP90-client protein complex, regulating ATP hydrolysis and client protein maturation, with particular importance in muscle tissue where protein homeostasis is critical for contractile function.
