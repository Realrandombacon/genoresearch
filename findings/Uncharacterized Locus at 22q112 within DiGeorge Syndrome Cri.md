## Description
LOC100505874 is an uncharacterized genomic locus on chromosome 22 (NT_167218.1:102502-127146) spanning approximately 24.6 kb. Comprehensive database searches reveal no evidence of protein-coding function: no UniProt accession was identified, precluding InterPro domain analysis and AlphaFold structure prediction. STRING database returned no protein interaction partners, confirming absence of canonical protein-coding function. Human Protein Atlas resolved this locus to Ensembl ID ENSG00000295063 but found no detectable protein expression in surveyed tissues. Critically, ClinVar analysis found zero variants of any clinical significance, suggesting this locus is genetically stable and tolerant to variation without disease association. The chromosomal location places this locus within or near 22q11.2, one of the most well-characterized microdeletion syndrome regions in the human genome. 22q11.2 deletion syndrome (DiGeorge syndrome/velocardiofacial syndrome) is caused by hemizygous deletions of approximately 3 Mb and is associated with conotruncal cardiac defects, palatal abnormalities, hypoplastic thymus, hypocalcemia, and neurodevelopmental phenotypes including intellectual disability, autism spectrum disorder, and schizophrenia. The 22q11.2 region contains multiple dosage-sensitive genes including TBX1 (T-box transcription factor 1), the major disease driver, as well as COMT (catechol-O-methyltransferase), DGCR8 (DiGeorge syndrome critical region 8), and HIRA (histone cell cycle regulator). LOC100505874 likely functions as part of the 22q11.2 cis-regulatory landscape or encodes a non-coding RNA that may modulate expression of flanking dosage-sensitive genes through chromatin looping or transcriptional interference. The complete absence of protein-coding signatures, protein interactions, tissue expression, and clinical variants across multiple orthogonal databases strongly suggests this sequence does not encode a functional protein but may participate in long-range gene regulation within the 22q11.2 topologically associating domain (TAD). Further comparative genomics and chromatin conformation capture (Hi-C) analysis would be needed to determine if this locus contains functional regulatory elements contributing to 22q11.2 syndrome pathogenesis.
