# CNOT11: A Structural Scaffold Subunit of the CCR4-NOT Complex Mediating mRNA Deadenylation and Transcriptional Regulation

**Date:** 2026-06-26T17:15:16.873019

**Quality Score:** 9/10 (EXCELLENT)

**Confidence:** MEDIUM

## Description
CNOT11 (CCR4-NOT Transcription Complex Subunit 11, UniProt Q9UKZ1), also known as C2orf29 or C40, is a 510-amino acid structural component of the multi-subunit CCR4-NOT complex—the major cytoplasmic mRNA deadenylase responsible for poly(A) tail shortening and subsequent mRNA decay. InterPro analysis reveals a bipartite domain architecture unique to CNOT11: an N-terminal domain (IPR063413, PF27642, aa 86-222) of unknown function that may mediate protein-protein interactions; and a large C-terminal domain (IPR019312, PF10155, PTHR15975, aa 63-510) characteristic of CNOT11 family members that likely serves as a scaffold for complex assembly. The AlphaFold predicted structure shows high confidence folding (pLDDT 76.8), consistent with a stable structural protein optimized for multi-protein interactions within the holo-complex. STRING interaction data places CNOT11 at the heart of the CCR4-NOT machinery with near-perfect associations to all core subunits: CNOT9/RCD1 (0.999), CNOT3 (0.999), CNOT10 (0.999), CNOT1 (0.999)—the scaffold subunit anchoring the complex; CNOT6/CNOT6L (0.997-0.998) and CNOT7/CNOT8 (0.995-0.997)—the catalytic deadenylase subunits containing EEP and RNase D domains respectively; CNOT2 (0.988); and TNKS1BP1 (0.994), a tankyrase-binding protein linking CCR4-NOT to Wnt signaling. HPA expression profiles confirm ubiquitous detection across all tissues with nucleoplasmic localization, consistent with essential housekeeping roles in post-transcriptional gene regulation; CNOT11 is classified as involved in RNA-mediated gene silencing, transcription regulation, and translation regulation. ClinVar records document 15 pathogenic variants predominantly consisting of copy number gains and losses spanning 2q11.1-q13 (ranging from 100kb to whole chromosome arms), implicating CNOT11 dosage sensitivity in developmental disorders though specific phenotypes remain undefined. We hypothesize that CNOT11 functions as a critical structural bridge within the CCR4-NOT complex: the C-terminal domain interacts with the CNOT1 scaffold to position CNOT11 at the complex periphery; this recruits or stabilizes the catalytic modules (CNOT6/6L and CNOT7/8) for optimal deadenylase activity; the N-terminal domain may engage regulatory factors or substrate mRNAs; upon recruitment to target transcripts via miRNA-guided Argonaute proteins or sequence-specific RNA-binding proteins, the CCR4-NOT complex processively shortens the poly(A) tail from ~250 nucleotides to <20 residues; this triggers decapping by DCP1/DCP2 and 5'-to-3' exonucleolytic decay by XRN1, or alternatively 3'-to-5' degradation by the exosome; CNOT11 loss would destabilize the complex, impair mRNA turnover, and cause accumulation of aberrant transcripts contributing to developmental pathology.
