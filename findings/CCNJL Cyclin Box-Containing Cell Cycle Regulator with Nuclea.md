## Description
CCNJL (Cyclin-J-like protein, UniProt Q8IV13) is a 435-amino acid intracellular protein that functions as a member of the cyclin family of cell cycle regulators. InterPro analysis reveals canonical cyclin architecture: dual cyclin box domains consisting of N-terminal cyclin domain (IPR006671/PF00134/SM00385/G3DSA:1.10.472.10/SSF47954, aa 36-93/44-184/3-93) and C-terminal cyclin domain (IPR004367/PF02984/SM01332/cd20529, aa 193-321/193-299/202-296/193-294) forming the characteristic two-domain cyclin fold (IPR013763/IPR036915/IPR039361/PTHR10177, aa 12-303) that creates a hydrophobic groove for CDK binding and activation. GO molecular function confirms cyclin activity. AlphaFold structure prediction achieves medium confidence (pLDDT 67.2), indicating a partially structured protein with ordered cyclin core domains connected by flexible linkers — architecture consistent with cyclins where rigid CDK-binding surfaces are connected by regulatory regions subject to phosphorylation and degradation signals. Human Protein Atlas shows tissue-enhanced expression detected in many tissues with brain enrichment (16.1 nTPM top expression), low cancer specificity, group-enriched blood cell specificity, cell type-enhanced single cell specificity, and nuclear speckles subcellular localization — nuclear speckle positioning suggests involvement in transcriptional regulation or RNA processing coordination alongside cell cycle control. This interactome definitively places CCNJL within the CDK regulatory network, likely functioning as an activator of PCTAIRE-family kinases (CDK16/17/18) rather than canonical cell cycle CDKs. ClinVar contains 18 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants consisting of copy number variations (deletions, duplications, gains, losses) spanning chromosome 5q33-35 regions — these CNVs encompass multiple genes and are associated with developmental disorders, though no specific disease is attributed solely to CCNJL. Conservation across mammals (Mus musculus Q5SRT8, 387 aa) indicates ancient evolutionary origin tied to fundamental cell cycle regulation mechanisms. CCNJL likely functions as a regulatory cyclin that binds and activates specific CDK partners (particularly PCTAIRE-family kinases CDK16/17/18) through its cyclin box interface, coordinating phosphorylation events during cell cycle progression, neuronal development (explaining brain enrichment), or transcriptional regulation via nuclear speckle association — with pathogenic CNVs potentially disrupting dosage-sensitive cell cycle control contributing to developmental phenotypes through impaired CDK activation and downstream signaling cascades.
