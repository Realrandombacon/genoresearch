# LOC129389371: MPRA-Validated Silencer Element on 5q31.1 with Moderate Burden of 6 Recurrent Pathogenic CNVs

**Date:** 2026-03-20T20:31:21.663220

## Description
LOC129389371 is a 200bp cis-regulatory element (CRE) located on chromosome 5q31.1 (GRCh38: 136524066-136524266), functionally validated as a transcriptional silencer through STARR-seq massively parallel reporter assays (MPRAs) in K562 erythroleukemia cells. This element lacks canonical promoter, enhancer, or CTCF-bound insulator chromatin signatures but demonstrates robust repressive activity against the super core promoter SCP1. As a non-coding regulatory locus, it has no UniProt accession, protein domains, or STRING interaction partners. Human Protein Atlas could not resolve this locus to an Ensembl ID, consistent with its non-coding regulatory classification.\n\nClinVar analysis reveals 6 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variants disrupting this region—a moderate burden among characterized silencer elements. Variants include 3 duplications (copy number gains spanning 5q23.3-33.2, 5q31.1-31.3, covering ~1.5-23Mb regions) and 3 deletions (copy number losses spanning 5q21.3-33.2, 5q31.1-31.2, covering ~2-49Mb regions). These recurrent CNVs are associated with 5q deletion/duplication syndromes (often overlapping with Cri-du-chat syndrome regions or distinct 5q31 microdeletion syndromes) featuring developmental delay, intellectual disability, craniofacial dysmorphism, skeletal anomalies, and congenital heart defects. The 5q31.1 region is gene-dense, containing critical dosage-sensitive genes such as IL5, IL13, IL4 (cytokine cluster), and MEF2C (transcription factor critical for neurodevelopment).\n\nHYPOTHESIS: LOC129389371 functions as a critical distal silencer within the 5q31.1 topologically associating domain (TAD), regulating expression of dosage-sensitive immune and neurodevelopmental genes. Candidate targets include MEF2C (haploinsufficiency causes severe intellectual disability, stereotypic movements, and epilepsy) or the Th2 cytokine cluster (IL4/IL5/IL13). The silencer likely recruits co-repressor complexes (PRC2, NCoR/SMRT, HDACs) to deposit H3K27me3 marks at target promoters. The balanced CNV burden (3 duplications : 3 deletions) suggests strict dosage sensitivity where both loss of repression (deletions) and silencer titration/ectopic repression (duplications) lead to pathological gene expression states. Deletions may cause derepression of MEF2C or cytokines leading to neurodevelopmental or immune dysregulation, while duplications may cause excessive silencing of adjacent genes. The K562 erythroleukemia validation hints at potential roles in hematopoietic lineage specification or immune response modulation.
