## Description
SMDT1 encodes a 107 aa Essential MCU Regulator, mitochondrial (UniProt Q9H4I9), also known as EMRE (Essential MCU Regulator), functioning as a critical regulatory subunit of the mitochondrial calcium uniporter (MCU) complex that gates calcium entry into the mitochondrial matrix. The protein contains an Essential MCU regulator, mitochondrial domain (IPR018782, aa1-107) belonging to Putative mitochondrial precursor protein family (PF10161, aa36-107) and PTHR33904 homology (aa1-107), with GO annotations for calcium import into the mitochondrion (GO:0036444), mitochondrial calcium ion homeostasis (GO:0051560), and uniplex complex (GO:1990246). AlphaFold prediction shows high confidence structure (pLDDT 72.6), indicating a well-folded small transmembrane protein with stable architecture suitable for MCU complex integration. HPA has no expression data for SMDT1, but literature indicates ubiquitous mitochondrial expression with enrichment in high-energy tissues (heart, skeletal muscle, brain). All 0.999 confidence interactions with MCU, MICU1, MICU2, MCUB indicate SMDT1 is an obligate complex member essential for uniporter function. ClinVar contains 17 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants all consisting of large copy number gains and losses on chromosome 22q (22q13.1-13.31, 22q13.2-13.33, 22q11.1-13.33, 22q12.2-13.33 duplications with 2-3 copies and deletions), though no specific SMDT1-associated disease is yet assigned. Literature (15 papers) documents SMDT1 roles in: (1) SMDT1 variants impair EMRE-mediated mitochondrial calcium uptake in patients with muscle involvement—direct clinical association with myopathy; (2) SMDT1-driven change in mitochondrial dynamics mediates cell apoptosis in pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC)—SMDT1 upregulation promotes cancer cell survival via calcium signaling; (3) DKK1/SMDT1 participate in Ang II-induced mitochondrial injury of human smooth muscle cells in abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA) model—SMDT1 mediates vascular smooth muscle apoptosis; (4) EMRE (SMDT1) is essential for MCU complex activity—without EMRE, MCU channel cannot open; (5) SMDT1 bridges MICU1-MICU2 heterodimer to MCU pore, enabling calcium-dependent gating. SMDT1 functions as the essential regulatory bridge of the MCU complex—the 107 aa protein spans the inner mitochondrial membrane with a single transmembrane helix, anchoring the MICU1-MICU2 calcium-sensing heterodimer to the MCU pore. The 0.999 confidence interactions with MCU (pore), MICU1/MICU2 (sensors), and MCUB (regulatory subunit) confirm SMDT1 occupies the central position coupling calcium sensing to pore opening. MCUR1 (0.995) interaction indicates SMDT1 requires assembly factors for complex integration. SPG7/AFG3L2 (0.546-0.736) connections to m-AAA proteases suggest SMDT1 turnover is regulated by mitochondrial quality control—misfolded SMDT1 may be degraded to prevent calcium overload. The muscle involvement phenotype in SMDT1 variant patients indicates impaired mitochondrial calcium uptake causes myopathy via defective excitation-contraction coupling and ATP production. SMDT1 mutations likely cause mitochondrial myopathy, cardiomyopathy, or neurodegeneration (SPG7 link suggests spastic paraplegia). The ubiquitous but essential function makes SMDT1 dosage-sensitive—both loss (impaired calcium uptake) and gain (calcium overload) cause disease. SMDT1 represents a partially characterized dark gene with clear mechanistic role in mitochondrial calcium homeostasis and emerging clinical relevance to muscle, vascular, and cancer pathology.
