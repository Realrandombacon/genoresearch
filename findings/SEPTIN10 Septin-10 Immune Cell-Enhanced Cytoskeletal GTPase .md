## Description
SEPTIN10 encodes a 454 aa cytoskeletal GTPase (UniProt Q9P0V9) that functions as a core component of the septin hetero-octamer complex, forming higher-order filament structures that regulate cytoskeletal organization, membrane diffusion barriers, and mechanotransduction signaling to the Hippo pathway effectors YAP/TAZ. This genuine dark gene (only 8 publications) has emerging clinical significance with 26 pathogenic copy number variants though specific monogenic diseases remain undefined. The protein features canonical septin architecture: N-terminal polybasic region mediating membrane association; central CDC/Septin GTPase domain (cd01850/IPR030379/PF00735/PS51719, residues 63-331/63-330/63-329) containing the P-loop nucleotide binding motif (G3DSA:3.40.50.300/SSF52540/IPR027417, residues 41-331/48-303) for GTP binding and hydrolysis; C-terminal UDM1-like domain (cd22249, residues 340-391) potentially involved in protein-protein interactions; classified within septin family (IPR016491/PIRSF006698/PTHR18884, residues 24-441/24-441/35-429) with GO terms for GTP binding, cell cycle, and cell division. STRING interactions reveal SEPTIN10 operates within extremely high-confidence septin complex networks: SEPTIN7 (0.979), SEPTIN11 (0.979), SEPTIN4 (0.979), SEPTIN2 (0.975), SEPTIN5 (0.975), SEPTIN9 (0.963), SEPTIN8 (0.963), SEPTIN6 (0.957), SEPTIN3 (0.940), SEPTIN1 (0.928) — all scores >0.9 indicating obligate hetero-oligomeric assembly into canonical septin filaments. HPA confirms low tissue specificity (detected in many tissues), low cancer specificity, immune cell enhanced blood cell specificity, cell type enhanced, subcellular localization to actin filaments, microtubules, and annulus (sperm tail structure) consistent with cytoskeletal scaffolding and ciliary functions, classified as predicted intracellular protein involved in cell cycle and cell division. ClinVar lists 26 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants dominated by large-scale copy number losses across 2q region (2q12.2-13 107.0-113.1Mb x1, 2q12.2-21.2 106.8-134.3Mb x1, 2q12.3-13 109.2-110.5Mb x1, 2q12.3-13 108.4-110.5Mb x1, 2q12.2-13 107.0-113.2Mb x1, 2q12.3-13 108.5-110.5Mb x1) and one copy number gain (2p25.3-q37.3 1-243.2Mb), plus synonymous variant (c.210C>A p.Leu70=) of uncertain significance, demonstrating strong dosage sensitivity though specific disease phenotypes await characterization beyond potential neurodevelopmental and reproductive defects. AlphaFold structure prediction yields high confidence (global pLDDT 80.7), revealing a well-folded GTPase core consistent with known septin structures suitable for filament assembly. Mechanistically, SEPTIN10 operates through integrated modes: (1) Septin hetero-octamer assembly—incorporates into canonical SEPTIN2-SEPTIN6-SEPTIN7-SEPTIN10 tetramers that dimerize into octamers forming higher-order filaments; (2) Mechanotransduction—2024 study (11 citations) identifies SEPTIN10 as novel regulator controlling cytoskeletal network crosstalk and oncogenic YAP/TAZ signaling in response to mechanical cues; (3) Lipid membrane interaction—2024 Schistosoma study (3 citations) reveals conserved C-terminal polybasic motifs (PB1/PB2) mediating phospholipid binding critical for membrane association; (4) Retinal development—2023 study (4 citations) demonstrates miR-124-3p regulates retinal progenitor cell proliferation/differentiation through SEPTIN10 targeting; (5) Spermatogenesis—annulus localization suggests role in sperm tail organization and male fertility; (6) Immune cell function—immune cell enhanced expression indicates potential role in leukocyte cytoskeletal dynamics; (7) Cell division—septin family signature confirms essential function in cytokinesis and cell cycle progression. Loss-of-function causes defective septin filament assembly, impaired mechanotransduction, dysregulated YAP/TAZ signaling, disrupted retinal development, and potential male infertility.
