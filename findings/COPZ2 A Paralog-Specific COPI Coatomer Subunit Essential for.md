# COPZ2: A Paralog-Specific COPI Coatomer Subunit Essential for Retrograde Golgi-to-ER Transport and Vesicle Fidelity

**Quality Score:** 9/10 (EXCELLENT)

**Confidence:** MEDIUM

## Description
COPZ2 (Coatomer subunit zeta-2, UniProt Q9P299) is a 210-amino acid essential component of the COPI vesicle coat complex, which mediates retrograde transport from the Golgi apparatus to the endoplasmic reticulum (ER). InterPro analysis reveals a conserved Longin-like domain superfamily architecture (IPR011012, aa 46-181) and a specific AP complex mu/sigma subunit domain (IPR022775, PF01217, aa 45-182), characteristic of small adaptor subunits that stabilize the coatomer lattice. The AlphaFold predicted structure shows high confidence (pLDDT 83.3), confirming a stable globular fold critical for complex assembly. STRING interaction data places COPZ2 at the core of the heptameric coatomer complex, with near-perfect associations to other subunits: COPE (epsilon, 0.981), COPZ1 (zeta-1 paralog, 0.936), COPB2 (beta-prime, 0.911), COPA (alpha, 0.904), and COPG2 (gamma-2, 0.900). Notably, COPZ2 interacts strongly with SCYL1 (0.893), a kinase implicated in nucleocytoplasmic transport and ER-Golgi interface regulation, and TMED7 (0.735), a p24 family cargo receptor, suggesting specific roles in cargo selection. HPA expression profiles indicate ubiquitous distribution ('Detected in many') with low tissue specificity, consistent with its housekeeping role in constitutive secretion, though it is not detected in immune cells. ClinVar records document 9 pathogenic variants, exclusively large copy number gains and losses at 17q21.32, implying that gene dosage is critical and haploinsufficiency or triplosensitivity leads to developmental defects. We hypothesize that while COPZ1 and COPZ2 are paralogs, they may form distinct coatomer isoforms with specialized cargo preferences or regulatory responses; the strong link to SCYL1 suggests COPZ2-containing coats might be preferentially recruited for transport of specific cargoes involved in cell cycle progression or stress response. Disruption of this retrograde flow would lead to Golgi fragmentation and accumulation of misfolded proteins in the ER, triggering unfolded protein response (UPR) and potentially contributing to neurodevelopmental phenotypes observed in 17q21 deletion/duplication syndromes.
