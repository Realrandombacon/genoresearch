## Description
ATL3 encodes a 541 aa integral membrane GTPase (UniProt Q6DD88), also known as AT3, that functions as a dynamin-like enzyme essential for the homotypic fusion of endoplasmic reticulum (ER) tubules to form the characteristic three-way junctions of the ER network. The protein features a canonical atlastin architecture: an N-terminal GTPase domain (IPR015894/IPR030386/PF02263/cd01851/G3DSA:3.40.50.300, residues 24-305/37-305/56-303) containing the P-loop for GTP binding/hydrolysis; a central three-helix bundle domain (IPR003191/PF02841/G3DSA:1.20.58.420, residues 313-434/338-450) acting as a lever arm for conformational change; and C-terminal transmembrane domains anchoring the protein to the ER membrane. STRING interactions place ATL3 at the core of ER shaping and autophagy networks: REEP1 (0.951) and REEP5 (0.896), reticulon-like proteins stabilizing ER curvature; GABARAPL1 (0.869), GABARAP (0.797), and GABARAPL2 (0.746), ATG8-family proteins linking ATL3 to ER-phagy (selective ER autophagy); SPART (0.853), spartin involved in ER tubule dynamics and lipid droplet regulation; SPAST (0.568), spastin microtubule-severing enzyme coordinating ER-microtubule interactions; ARL6IP1 (0.533), an ER-shaping protein; RTN1 (0.480) and RTN3 (0.444), reticulons maintaining high ER curvature. HPA confirms ubiquitous expression with ER localization, annotated for hydrolase activity and neuropathy disease involvement. ClinVar lists 38 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including missense mutations (p.Met141Lys, p.Gln170His, p.Glu199Asp, p.Tyr509Cys/Ser), nonsense (p.Ser93Ter), in-frame deletions (p.Glu335del), duplications (p.Ala355_Ala356ins...), and copy number gains on 11q12.1-13.3—these cause Hereditary Sensory Neuropathy Type 1F (HSN1F), characterized by sensory loss, motor axonal neuropathy, and myoclonus. AlphaFold structure prediction yields high confidence (global pLDDT 86.8), revealing a well-folded GTPase domain poised for dimerization-driven fusion. Mechanistically, ATL3 functions through multiple integrated modes: (1) As a fusogen, ATL3 molecules on opposing ER tubules dimerize via their GTPase domains in a GTP-dependent manner; subsequent GTP hydrolysis triggers a dramatic conformational change in the three-helix bundle, pulling the membranes together to overcome repulsive forces and drive lipid mixing/fusion; (2) ER network maintenance—cooperation with REEPs, reticulons, and spastin ensures proper ER morphology and distribution along microtubules, critical for neuronal axon integrity; (3) ER-phagy regulation—interaction with GABARAP family members positions ATL3 as a potential regulator or substrate of selective ER degradation; recent studies (2023) demonstrate coronaviruses hijack ATL3 and the ER-phagy receptor FAM134B into p62 condensates to subvert ER-phagy, facilitating viral replication factory formation; (4) Neuropathy pathogenesis—HSN1F mutations likely impair GTPase activity or dimerization, leading to fragmented ER, defective cargo transport, and axonal degeneration specifically in sensory neurons; (5) Cancer biomarker potential—machine learning studies (2025) identify ATL3 as a predictor of drug response, possibly via altered ER stress signaling or autophagy flux. Disruption of ATL3 causes ER fragmentation, impaired protein/lipid trafficking, defective ER-phagy, and selective sensory neuron death—phenotypes directly observed in HSN1F patients.
