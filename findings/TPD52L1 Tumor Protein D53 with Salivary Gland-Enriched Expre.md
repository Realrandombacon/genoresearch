## Description
TPD52L1 encodes Tumor protein D53 (204 aa, Q16890), a member of the TPD52 family functioning in vesicular trafficking, protein secretion, and cancer progression. InterPro analysis reveals canonical TPD52 family architecture: tumour protein D52 domain (IPR007327, positions 8-189) spanning most of the protein; TPD52 family Pfam domain (PF04201, positions 31-151) forming coiled-coil interaction interface; PANTHER family (PTHR19307, positions 8-189) defining functional class. STRING interactions reveal TPD52 family oligomerization and trafficking network: TPD52 (0.889, highest confidence - forms hetero-oligomers), TPD52L3 (0.728), TPD52L2 (0.710) - indicating family member complex formation; CLTC (0.664, clathrin heavy chain - endocytosis), RNF217 (0.644, E3 ubiquitin ligase), RAB5C (0.607, early endosome GTPase), LRIG3 (0.570, leucine-rich repeat protein), PUM1 (0.541, RNA-binding protein), CLINT1 (0.532, clathrin interactor), KDELR2 (0.465, ER retention receptor) - positioning TPD52L1 in endocytic trafficking and secretory pathways. HPA shows tissue-enhanced expression with salivary gland enrichment (281.1 nTPM); plasma membrane, cell junctions, and cytosol localization consistent with trafficking roles; detected in many tissues; low cancer specificity. ClinVar documents 18 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly chromosome 6q CNVs including losses (6q22.1-22.33, 6q22.31-23.2, 6q22.31-23.3, 6q21-23.1, 6q22.31-22.33) and gains (6q22.31-25.3, 6q21-23.2, 6p25.3-q27) indicating dosage sensitivity at 6q22 locus, though specific disease phenotypes remain incompletely characterized. AlphaFold structure shows medium confidence (pLDDT 64.6) with partially structured coiled-coil domain and disordered regions typical of scaffold proteins requiring conformational flexibility. Literature (43 papers) reveals emerging roles: 2026 bone organ-on-chip studies establish TPD52L1 enhances osteogenic differentiation of mesenchymal stem cells contributing to osteoporosis repair; 2023 gastric cancer expression studies show clinical significance; 2020 lung adenocarcinoma research identifies TPD52L1-ROS1 rearrangement as acquired osimertinib resistance mechanism responsive to crizotinib combination therapy; comparative chicken breed studies link TPD52L1 expression to carcass traits. Functional hypothesis: TPD52L1 functions as a scaffold protein mediating vesicular trafficking through TPD52 family homo/hetero-oligomerization; coiled-coil domain enables complex formation with TPD52/TPD52L2/TPD52L3 regulating secretory pathway efficiency; salivary gland enrichment (281.1 nTPM) suggests specialized role in protein secretion relevant to saliva production; plasma membrane/cell junction localization positions TPD52L1 in receptor trafficking and cell adhesion regulation; cancer roles indicate oncogenic potential through enhanced secretion of growth factors or cytokines. Mechanistic proposal: TPD52 domain coiled-coil structure (positions 31-151) mediates oligomerization forming ring-like complexes that recruit trafficking machinery (clathrin/CLTC, RAB5C early endosomes); interaction with RNF217 suggests ubiquitination-dependent regulation of stability or cargo selection; KDELR2 interaction links TPD52L1 to ER-to-Golgi retrograde transport; CLINT1 binding positions complex in clathrin-mediated endocytosis; 6q22 CNVs (losses more frequent) suggest haploinsufficiency could disrupt secretory pathway contributing to disease; osteogenic differentiation role indicates TPD52L1 regulates secretion of bone matrix proteins (collagen, osteocalcin) or growth factors (BMPs); TPD52L1-ROS1 fusion in lung cancer creates oncogenic driver through constitutive kinase activation; family member redundancy (TPD52/TPD52L2/TPD52L3) may buffer loss-of-function explaining limited specific disease associations despite CNV presence; coiled-coil oligomerization enables avidity-based cargo capture and membrane curvature generation for vesicle budding. This gene represents a moderately characterized trafficking regulator (43 papers) with emerging roles in cancer, osteogenesis, and secretory pathway regulation.
