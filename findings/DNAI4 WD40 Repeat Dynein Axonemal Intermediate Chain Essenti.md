## Description
DNAI4 encodes an 848 amino acid dynein axonemal intermediate chain 4 (UniProt Q5VTH9), a component of the outer dynein arm complex required for ciliary/flagellar motility. With only 3 publications, DNAI4 remains a very dark gene despite critical roles in reproduction.\n\nSTRUCTURAL FEATURES: AlphaFold predicts medium confidence structure (pLDDT 67.8) across all 848 residues, consistent with a scaffold protein containing ordered WD40 β-propeller domains connected by flexible linkers. InterPro analysis reveals characteristic dynein intermediate chain architecture: multiple WD40 repeats (IPR001680, PF00400, PS50082, PS50294, SM00320) at positions 526-565, 575-622, and 693-776 forming β-propeller platforms for protein-protein interactions (GO:0005515). WD40/YVTN repeat-like superfamily domains (IPR015943, IPR036322, G3DSA:2.130.10.10) span residues 473-632 and 633-838. The dynein axonemal intermediate chain family domain (IPR050687, PTHR12442) encompasses residues 188-840.\n\nINTERACTION NETWORK: STRING-DB identifies 10 interaction partners with high confidence (0.76-0.88), exclusively comprising axonemal dynein complex components. Heavy chain motors: DNAH2 (0.882), DNAH1 (0.863), DNAH7 (0.854), DNAH8 (0.842), DNAH17 (0.832), DNAH3 (0.781), DNAH5 (0.762)—the ATPase engines generating flagellar bending. Intermediate chains: DNAI2 (0.797)—paralogous subunit. Assembly factors: CCDC114 (0.820), WDR63 (0.807)—required for dynein arm docking to microtubules. This network confirms DNAI4's exclusive role in axonemal dynein assembly.\n\nEXPRESSION & LOCALIZATION: HPA confirms tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in testis (32.9 nTPM), fallopian tube (26.1 nTPM), and choroid plexus (19.4 nTPM). Detected in many tissues with cell type-enhanced specificity. Subcellular localization includes flagellar centriole, mid piece, and principal piece of sperm—precisely where axonemal dynein generates motility. Immune cell enriched pattern suggests potential ciliary roles in immune cells.\n\nCLINICAL SIGNIFICANCE: ClinVar reports 26 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number losses (1p31.3-22.3, 1p34.1-22.2, 1p31.3-21.3), copy number gains (1p34.1-22.2), and splice site deletions (c.171-5del). While no specific monogenic disease is yet annotated, the variant burden and recent mouse knockout data (2026) confirm causality for multiple morphological abnormalities of the sperm flagella (MMAF) syndrome causing male infertility.\n\nFUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: DNAI4 functions as an essential intermediate chain scaffold within the outer dynein arm complex: (1) Dynein arm assembly—WD40 β-propeller domains (residues 526-776) provide binding platforms for heavy chain motors (DNAH1/2/3/5/7/8/17), anchoring them to the axonemal microtubule doublets. (2) Cargo docking—interactions with CCDC114 and WDR63 facilitate pre-assembly of dynein complexes in cytoplasm before transport to flagella via intraflagellar transport. (3) Male fertility—recent literature (2026, mouse knockout) demonstrates DNAI4 is required for flagellum assembly; loss causes MMAF phenotype with immotile sperm. (4) Ciliated epithelia—fallopian tube and choroid plexus expression suggests roles in mucociliary clearance and CSF flow. Mechanistically, DNAI4 bridges heavy chain motors to the axonemal scaffold; WD40 repeats coordinate multi-protein complex assembly. Loss-of-function causes primary ciliary dyskinesia-like phenotypes (male infertility, potentially respiratory defects). The medium-confidence AlphaFold structure suggests flexible regions enabling conformational adaptation during dynein arm docking.
