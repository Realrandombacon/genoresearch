## Description
ZNF724 (UniProt A8MTY0) encodes a 619 amino acid nuclear protein belonging to the Krüppel-associated box (KRAB) domain-containing zinc finger protein family. InterPro analysis reveals a canonical KRAB repressor domain (IPR001909, PF01352, residues 3-75) at the N-terminus, which recruits the corepressor TRIM28/KAP1 to establish heterochromatin via histone methyltransferase complexes. The protein contains three C2H2-type zinc finger motifs (IPR013087, PF00096) at residues 170-304, forming a DNA-binding module that recognizes specific GC-rich target sequences. STRING interaction data identifies TRIM28 as the primary binding partner (score 0.597), consistent with the canonical KRAB-ZFP repression mechanism where TRIM28 bridges KRAB domains to SETDB1 histone methyltransferase and HP1 heterochromatin proteins. A secondary interaction with DHX8 (0.493), an ATP-dependent RNA helicase, suggests potential coupling of transcriptional repression to RNA processing or export. Human Protein Atlas shows tissue-enhanced expression in bone marrow (2.6 nTPM) and esophagus (2.4 nTPM), indicating roles in hematopoietic and epithelial cell lineages. ClinVar documents 4 pathogenic copy number gains on chromosome 19 encompassing the ZNF724 locus, suggesting dosage sensitivity where gene duplication may cause aberrant transcriptional silencing of target genes. The mechanistic hypothesis proposes ZNF724 functions as a sequence-specific transcriptional repressor that recruits TRIM28-mediated heterochromatin machinery to silence developmental or cell cycle regulators in hematopoietic progenitors, with dysregulation contributing to myeloid malignancies or developmental disorders.
