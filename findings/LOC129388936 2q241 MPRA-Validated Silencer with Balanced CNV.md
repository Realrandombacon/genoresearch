# LOC129388936: 2q24.1 MPRA-Validated Silencer with Balanced CNV Burden (5 Variants, ~3:2 Duplication Bias) and 2q24 Microdeletion Syndrome Association

## Description
LOC129388936 is a 200bp cis-regulatory element (CRE) located on chromosome 2q24.1 (GRCh38: 158188002-158188202), functionally validated as a transcriptional silencer through STARR-seq massively parallel reporter assays (MPRAs) in K562 erythroleukemia cells. This locus lacks canonical promoter, enhancer, or CTCF-bound insulator chromatin signatures but demonstrates robust repressive activity against the super core promoter SCP1. As a non-coding regulatory sequence, it has no UniProt accession, protein domains, STRING interaction partners, or Human Protein Atlas expression data (query not applicable for non-coding locus).\n\nClinVar analysis reveals 5 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variants disrupting this region. The variant spectrum shows a BALANCED pattern with slight duplication bias: 3 duplications (copy number gains spanning 2q24.1-31.1 ~22 Mb, 2q22.3-24.1 ~14 Mb, 2q24.1-24.3 ~13 Mb) versus 2 deletions (copy number losses spanning 2q23.3-24.2 ~7.5 Mb, 2q23.1-24.3 ~14 Mb). This 3:2 ratio (1.5:1) indicates near-balanced dosage sensitivity where both haploinsufficiency and overexpression are pathogenic, similar to LOC129389754 (6:4) and LOC129390753 (~1:1). These CNVs are associated with 2q24.1 deletion/duplication syndromes, characterized by developmental delay, intellectual disability, craniofacial dysmorphisms, and skeletal abnormalities.\n\nThe genomic context is highly notable: 2q24.1 resides within the 2q24 microdeletion syndrome critical region, which contains the SCN gene cluster of voltage-gated sodium channels: SCN1A (sodium channel protein type 1 subunit alpha, Dravet syndrome/GEFS+), SCN2A (sodium channel protein type 2 subunit alpha, epilepsy/autism), SCN3A, SCN9A (pain perception), and SCN1B. This region is also associated with MLL2 (KMT2D, Kabuki syndrome) distal regulatory elements. Mutations in SCN1A cause Dravet syndrome (severe myoclonic epilepsy of infancy), while SCN2A mutations cause early infantile epileptic encephalopathy. The precise dosage of these sodium channels is critical for neuronal excitability.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: LOC129388936 functions as a critical distal silencer within the 2q24.1 topologically associating domain (TAD), specifically regulating voltage-gated sodium channel genes (SCN1A, SCN2A) in neuronal and potentially hematopoietic contexts. The balanced CNV pattern with slight duplication bias (3:2) indicates TRUE dosage sensitivity where both reduced silencing (haploinsufficiency, 50% activity causing channel overexpression/hyperexcitability) and excessive silencing (triplication, 150% activity causing channel underexpression/hypoexcitability) disrupt neuronal function. The mechanism likely involves recruitment of neuron-specific co-repressor complexes (REST/NRSF, CoREST, HDACs) that fine-tune SCN gene expression levels to prevent epileptogenesis. In K562 cells, this silencer may represent a cryptic regulatory element or a shared ancestral mechanism. The 2q24.1 location within the SCN cluster raises the possibility that LOC129388936 helps establish precise SCN1A/SCN2A expression thresholds required for normal action potential firing. The balanced bias suggests target genes are highly sensitive to both overexpression (seizures, hyperexcitability) and underexpression (developmental delay, hypoexcitability), consistent with the known dosage sensitivity of SCN1A (haploinsufficiency causes Dravet, triplication causes mild epilepsy). This silencer may be a key therapeutic target for modulating sodium channel expression in epilepsy.
