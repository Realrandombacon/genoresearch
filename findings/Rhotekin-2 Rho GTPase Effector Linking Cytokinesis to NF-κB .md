# Rhotekin-2: Rho GTPase Effector Linking Cytokinesis to NF-κB Signaling via PH Domain-Mediated Membrane Recruitment

## Description
RTKN2 (Rhotekin-2, UniProt Q8IZC4) is a 609-amino acid intracellular protein that functions as a Rho GTPase effector connecting cytoskeletal dynamics to transcriptional regulation. The protein contains three key structural domains: an N-terminal HR1 rho-binding domain (IPR011072, residues 5-82) that directly interacts with active RhoA/RhoC GTPases, a central Anillin homology domain (IPR012966/PF08174, residues 98-246) implicated in cytokinesis and actin organization, and a C-terminal Pleckstrin Homology domain (IPR001849/PF00169, residues 286-396) that mediates phospholipid binding and membrane recruitment. AlphaFold prediction (pLDDT 69.9) suggests a partially structured protein with ordered domains connected by flexible linkers.\n\nSTRING interaction network reveals RTKN2 associates with RHOC (score 0.563) and RHOA (score 0.556), confirming its role as a Rho effector, plus ARID5B (0.520) and SH3KBP1 (0.409) suggesting links to chromatin remodeling and endocytic trafficking. HPA expression shows lung-enriched expression (73.2 nTPM) with nucleoplasmic and mitochondrial localization, consistent with dual roles in cytoplasmic signaling and nuclear transcription regulation. RTKN2 positively regulates NF-κB transcription factor activity and non-canonical NF-κB signal transduction while negatively regulating intrinsic apoptotic signaling.\n\nClinVar contains 20 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including deletions and copy number variations, though specific disease associations remain undefined. This variant burden suggests RTKN2 dysfunction contributes to disease pathogenesis, potentially through disrupted Rho-NF-κB crosstalk affecting cell survival and inflammation. The combination of Rho-binding capacity, membrane targeting via PH domain, and nuclear localization positions RTKN2 as a signaling hub that translates extracellular cues into transcriptional programs governing cell proliferation, survival, and cytokinesis.
