# OSBPL3/ORP3: Lipid Transporter at ER-Plasma Membrane Contact Sites Driving R-Ras Signaling, Cytoskeletal Dynamics, and Cancer Immunosuppression

## Description
OSBPL3 (887 aa, UniProt Q9H4L5), also known as ORP3, is a critical lipid transporter and signaling scaffold that localizes to endoplasmic reticulum-plasma membrane (ER-PM) contact sites to regulate cell adhesion, migration, and oncogenic signaling. The protein architecture features an N-terminal pleckstrin homology (PH) domain (IPR001849/PF15409, aa54-145) for phosphoinositide binding and membrane anchoring, coupled with a large C-terminal oxysterol-binding protein (OSBP) domain (IPR000648/PF01237, aa527-873) responsible for lipid extraction and transfer. Recent 2024-2025 studies establish OSBPL3 as a master regulator of the tumor microenvironment: it drives immunosuppression in pancreatic cancer, promotes stemness and ER stress in triple-negative breast cancer, and modulates tamoxifen resistance via the R-Ras pathway in breast cancer. Mechanistically, OSBPL3 forms a heterotypic tether with VAPA/VAPB (STRING scores 0.899/0.873) on the ER while binding active R-Ras (STRING 0.925) at the plasma membrane, facilitating non-vesicular lipid exchange (likely phosphatidylserine or sterols) that controls integrin activation and actin cytoskeleton organization. HPA data indicates ubiquitous expression with nucleolar and cytosolic localization, consistent with its role in global lipid homeostasis. AlphaFold prediction (pLDDT 71.9) reveals a confident multi-domain structure with a flexible linker between the PH and OSBP domains, enabling the conformational flexibility required for membrane bridging. Clinical relevance is supported by 36 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants in ClinVar, including large copy number gains and losses at 7p21-p15, suggesting dosage sensitivity in developmental or metabolic contexts. The functional hypothesis posits that OSBPL3 acts as a 'lipid switch': by transporting specific lipids to R-Ras nanoclusters, it stabilizes the active GTP-bound state of R-Ras, thereby promoting focal adhesion turnover and cell motility; in cancer, this mechanism is hijacked to enhance metastatic potential and suppress immune surveillance.
