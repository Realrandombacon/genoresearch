## Description
RFX4 encodes regulatory factor X4, a 735 aa winged helix DNA-binding transcription factor (UniProt Q33E94) belonging to the RFX family that regulates ciliogenesis and neural development through X-box motif recognition. InterPro reveals characteristic RFX architecture: DNA-binding RFX-type winged-helix domain (IPR003150/PF02257/PS51526, residues 60-136) mediating sequence-specific DNA binding, RFX BCD domain (IPR057321/PF25340, residues 201-474) unique to RFX1-4/6/8 subfamily for protein-protein interactions, and RFX transcription factor family membership (IPR039779/PTHR12619, residues 19-582). AlphaFold predicts medium confidence structure (pLDDT 65.9) consistent with mixed ordered DNA-binding domain and intrinsically disordered transactivation regions typical of transcriptional regulators. STRING reveals functional clustering with RFX family members and cell cycle regulators: RFX7 (0.533), RFX3 (0.483), RFX2 (0.478), plus DNA damage response proteins ATR (0.555), CHEK2 (0.508), CCNK (0.534), DTX2 (0.552), GMNC (0.520, multicilin regulating ciliogenesis), PLAGL1 (0.484), and SS18L1 (0.447). HPA shows group-enriched expression in testis (70.2 nTPM) and brain (22.2 nTPM) with nucleoplasmic localization, classified as transcription factor/activator/DNA-binding protein. ClinVar lists 13 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number gains at 12q23.1-24.33, deletions at 12q23.3-24.12, frameshift Glu67fs, nonsense Arg122Ter, and missense His85Arg in the DNA-binding domain though specific monogenic disease associations remain undefined. Functional mechanism: RFX4 binds X-box promoter motifs (5\'-GTTYNNNNYAC-3\') as homo- or heterodimers with RFX2/RFX3 to regulate target gene transcription. Unlike other RFX family members that primarily control ciliogenesis genes, RFX4 exhibits dual functionality: (1) ciliogenesis-independent role in neural tube dorsal-ventral patterning and neuronal differentiation through induction of proneural genes POU3F2 and NEUROD1, and (2) potential ciliogenesis regulation via GMNC interaction. Recent literature reveals expanded disease relevance: intellectual disability risk through cortical neurogenesis regulation by restraining premature neuronal differentiation (2026/1 citation), glioblastoma progression and poor prognosis via glioma stem cell maintenance (2020/16 citations), zebrafish neural tube midline formation (2018/27 citations), and allele dosage effects on brain phenotype demonstrating haploinsufficiency sensitivity (2018/13 citations). Despite mechanistic understanding of DNA binding and dimerization, complete target gene repertoire across developmental stages, isoform-specific functions (multiple splice variants exist), regulatory mechanisms controlling nuclear localization and DNA binding affinity, precise contribution to human intellectual disability syndromes, and therapeutic potential for modulating RFX4 activity in glioblastoma remain incompletely characterized - representing genuine dark aspects of this critical neurodevelopmental transcription factor.
