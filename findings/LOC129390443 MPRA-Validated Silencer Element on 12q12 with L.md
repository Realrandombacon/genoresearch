# LOC129390443: MPRA-Validated Silencer Element on 12q12 with Low Burden of 3 Recurrent Pathogenic CNVs

**Date:** 2026-03-20T20:31:48.797508

**Quality Score:** 1.85/10  (E=3.0, D=0.7) [POOR]

## Description
LOC129390443 is a 200bp cis-regulatory element (CRE) located on chromosome 12q12 (GRCh38: 43060832-43061032), functionally validated as a transcriptional silencer through STARR-seq massively parallel reporter assays (MPRAs) in K562 erythroleukemia cells. This element lacks canonical promoter, enhancer, or CTCF-bound insulator chromatin signatures but demonstrates robust repressive activity against the super core promoter SCP1. As a non-coding regulatory locus, it has no UniProt accession, protein domains, or STRING interaction partners. Human Protein Atlas could not resolve this locus to an Ensembl ID, consistent with its non-coding regulatory classification.\n\nClinVar analysis reveals 3 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variants disrupting this region—a low burden compared to other characterized silencers. Variants include 2 large duplications (copy number gains spanning 12p13.33-q13.12 and 12p13.33-q24.33, covering ~49Mb and ~133Mb respectively) and 1 deletion (copy number loss spanning 12q12-q13.11, covering ~5.8Mb). These recurrent CNVs are associated with 12q deletion/duplication syndromes featuring developmental delay, intellectual disability, craniofacial dysmorphism, skeletal anomalies, and congenital heart defects. The 12q12 region is gene-dense, containing critical dosage-sensitive genes such as PHF6 (Plant Homeodomain Finger 6, mutations cause Börjeson-Forssman-Lehmann syndrome), ACVR2A (Activin Receptor Type 2A), and DDX5 (DEAD-box helicase 5).\n\nHYPOTHESIS: LOC129390443 functions as a critical distal silencer within the 12q12 topologically associating domain (TAD), regulating expression of dosage-sensitive developmental genes. Candidate targets include PHF6 (chromatin remodeling factor essential for neurodevelopment and hematopoiesis) or ACVR2A (TGF-beta signaling receptor). The silencer likely recruits co-repressor complexes (PRC2, NCoR/SMRT, HDACs) to deposit H3K27me3 marks at target promoters. The low CNV burden (3 variants, skewed toward large duplications 2:1) suggests that while the element is dosage-sensitive, its disruption often occurs as part of larger chromosomal rearrangements rather than focal events. The presence of large arm-level duplications indicates that the silencer\'s regulatory impact may be compounded by the duplication of multiple TADs and genes. Deletions may cause derepression of PHF6 leading to neurodevelopmental phenotypes
