# COX8C: Testis-Specific Cytochrome c Oxidase Subunit VIII Paralog Optimizing Mitochondrial Respiration for Spermatogenesis and Sperm Motility

## Description
COX8C (Cytochrome c Oxidase Subunit 8C, also COX8-3) is a 72 amino acid mitochondrial inner membrane protein (UniProt Q7Z4L0) with a well-folded transmembrane architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 79.2, high confidence), characteristic of small hydrophobic subunits of cytochrome c oxidase (Complex IV) that stabilize the holoenzyme complex and modulate respiratory chain efficiency.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals COX8C belongs to the cytochrome c oxidase subunit 8 family (IPR003205/IPR036548/PTHR16717, residues 1-72) with a conserved C-terminal Cytochrome_c_Oxidase_VIII domain (cd00930/G3DSA:4.10.81.10/SSF81431, residues 29-72/29-71/31-72) and PF02285 Cytochrome oxidase c subunit VIII domain (residues 30-71). The protein is involved in mitochondrial electron transport from cytochrome c to oxygen (GO:0006123) as part of respiratory chain complex IV (GO:0045277). The small size and hydrophobic nature indicate a transmembrane helix that anchors within the Complex IV structure near the proton channel.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals COX8C functions exclusively within the cytochrome c oxidase complex: strongest interactions with COX7A2 (0.833, cytochrome c oxidase subunit 7A2), COX4I2 (0.820, cytochrome c oxidase subunit 4I2, testis-specific isoform), COX4I1 (0.788, cytochrome c oxidase subunit 4I1, ubiquitous isoform), COX7B2 (0.759, cytochrome c oxidase subunit 7B2), COX6A2 (0.751, cytochrome c oxidase subunit 6A2, muscle-specific), COX6A1 (0.734, cytochrome c oxidase subunit 6A1), COX7C (0.729, cytochrome c oxidase subunit 7C), COX8A (0.728, cytochrome c oxidase subunit 8A, ubiquitous isoform), COX6B1 (0.727, cytochrome c oxidase subunit 6B1), and COX15 (0.588, cytochrome c oxidase assembly protein). These interactions confirm COX8C as an integral Complex IV subunit that co-assembles with other tissue-specific isoforms.\n\nHPA expression shows testis-enriched specificity (71.9 nTPM) with detection in single tissue only, consistent with a germ cell-specific respiratory chain isoform. The protein is classified as predicted membrane protein and transporter, reflecting its role in mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation.\n\nClinVar documents 23 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all large chromosome 14q31.1-32.2 copy number variations (gains and losses spanning 14q13.3-32.33 to 14q31.1-32.2 regions). These CNVs suggest dosage sensitivity potentially causing male infertility through impaired spermatogenesis and sperm motility defects.\n\nConservation is evident from mouse (A6H666, 72 aa), rat (Q7TNN2, 72 aa), and lemur (Q7YRF4, 71 aa) orthologs, indicating ancient evolutionary conservation of testis-specific respiratory chain optimization.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: COX8C acts as a testis-specific paralog of the ubiquitous COX8A subunit that optimizes cytochrome c oxidase activity for the unique bioenergetic demands of spermatogenesis and sperm motility. During spermatogenesis, germ cells undergo dramatic metabolic remodeling from glycolysis to oxidative phosphorylation; COX8C incorporation into Complex IV may enhance respiratory efficiency, reduce reactive oxygen species (ROS) production, or adapt proton pumping to the low-oxygen testicular environment. The co-expression with COX4I2 (another testis-specific Complex IV subunit) suggests coordinated isoform switching that creates a germ cell-specific respirasome. Sperm flagellar motility requires sustained ATP production from mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation in the midpiece; COX8C dysfunction impairs this process, causing asthenozoospermia (reduced sperm motility) and male infertility. CNV-mediated haploinsufficiency disrupts Complex IV assembly or stability specifically in germ cells, while somatic tissues compensate with COX8A. The clinical CNVs spanning 14q31-32 may cause syndromic infertility when combined with neighboring gene disruptions. COX8C represents a paradigm of tissue-specific respiratory chain adaptation where paralogs fine-tune oxidative phosphorylation for specialized cellular functions.
