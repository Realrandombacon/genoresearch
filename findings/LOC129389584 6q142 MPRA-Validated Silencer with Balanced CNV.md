## Description
LOC129389584 is a 200bp cis-regulatory element (CRE) located on chromosome 6q14.2 (GRCh38: 95544679-95544879), functionally validated as a transcriptional silencer through STARR-seq massively parallel reporter assays (MPRAs) in K562 erythroleukemia cells. This locus lacks canonical promoter, enhancer, or CTCF-bound insulator chromatin signatures but demonstrates robust repressive activity against the super core promoter SCP1. As a non-coding regulatory sequence, it has no UniProt accession, protein domains, STRING interaction partners, or Human Protein Atlas expression data (query not applicable for non-coding locus).\n\nClinVar analysis reveals only 2 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variants disrupting this region, representing a LOW CNV burden. The variant spectrum shows PERFECTLY BALANCED pattern: 1 duplication (copy number gain spanning 6q13-24.1 ~68 Mb large region) versus 1 deletion (copy number loss spanning 6q14.2-16.1 ~15 Mb). This 1:1 ratio indicates TRUE dosage sensitivity where both haploinsufficiency and overexpression are pathogenic, similar to LOC129390753 (~1:1) and LOC129389236 (~4:3). However, the low variant count (2 vs 5-29 in other silencers) suggests this element may have milder phenotypic effects or lower mutational target size. These CNVs are associated with 6q14.2 deletion/duplication syndromes, characterized by developmental delay, intellectual disability, and craniofacial dysmorphisms.\n\nThe genomic context is highly notable: 6q14.2 resides within the 6q deletion syndrome critical region. Nearby genes include PRDM1 (PR/SET domain 1, B lymphocyte induced maturation protein 1, transcriptional repressor essential for B-cell differentiation), SIM1 (single-minded homolog 1, Prader-Willi-like obesity syndrome, hypothalamic development), MAP3K7 (mitogen-activated protein kinase kinase kinase 7, CARD10 signaling), and TRIM22 (tripartite motif-containing 22, antiviral restriction factor). This region is also associated with 6q14.2 microdeletion syndrome and 6q14.2 microduplication syndrome.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: LOC129389584 functions as a critical distal silencer within the 6q14.2 topologically associating domain (TAD), specifically regulating immune and developmental genes (PRDM1, SIM1) in hematopoietic and neuronal contexts. The perfectly balanced 1:1 CNV pattern indicates TRUE dosage sensitivity where both reduced silencing (haploinsufficiency, 50% activity) and excessive silencing (triplication, 150% activity) disrupt target gene expression. The mechanism likely involves recruitment of B-cell-specific co-repressor complexes (PRDM1 itself may autoregulate, or recruit TLE/Groucho, HDACs) that fine-tune PRDM1 and SIM1 expression levels. In K562 erythroleukemia cells, this silencer may regulate PRDM1 to prevent premature B-cell differentiation or maintain erythroid lineage identity. The 6q14.2 location near PRDM1 (master B-cell regulator) and SIM1 (hypothalamic obesity regulator) raises the possibility that LOC129389584 helps establish precise expression thresholds for these dosage-sensitive transcription factors. PRDM1 haploinsufficiency causes lymphoproliferative disorders, while SIM1 haploinsufficiency causes severe obesity. The balanced bias suggests both genes require precise expression levels - too much PRDM1 causes B-cell malignancy, too little causes immunodeficiency; too much SIM1 causes hypothalamic dysfunction, too little causes obesity. The low CNV count (2 variants) may indicate this silencer operates in a narrower developmental window or has redundant regulatory elements.
