## Description
MFSD14A (Major Facilitator Superfamily Domain Containing 14A), also known as SLC71A1 or HIAT1 (Hippocampus Abundant Transcript 1), is a 490-amino acid putative transmembrane transporter (UniProt Q96MC6) representing a genuine dark gene with only 6 publications despite critical roles in neuronal function and male fertility. Structurally, MFSD14A belongs to the Major Facilitator Superfamily (MFS) of transporters, characterized by a canonical 12-transmembrane helix topology formed by two domains of 6 helices each. InterPro analysis confirms the presence of the MFS domain (IPR020846/IPR011701/PF07690, residues 36-453), the sugar transporter conserved site (IPR005829, residues 92-108), and specific domains for the MFSD14A/B subfamily (cd17387, residues 37-449). The protein shares homology with tetracycline resistance proteins (IPR001958), suggesting an evolutionary origin in multidrug efflux pumps adapted for endogenous substrate transport. AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (pLDDT 79.0), consistent with a stable, folded transporter capable of undergoing conformational changes required for substrate translocation. Functionally, MFSD14A localizes to intracellular vesicles with tissue-enhanced expression, highest in the parathyroid gland (123.7 nTPM, HPA), and is detected in all tissues. Critically, literature identifies it as highly abundant in the hippocampus and essential for spermatogenesis; knockout mice exhibit globozoospermia (round-headed sperm lacking acrosomes) and complete infertility (31 citations, 2016). STRING interactions reveal a network of orphan MFS transporters and vesicle trafficking proteins, including SLC35A3 (0.685, nucleotide sugar transporter), MFSD5 (0.617), MFSD11 (0.543), DPY19L2 (0.538, C-mannosyltransferase linked to globozoospermia), VPS54 (0.532, vacuolar protein sorting), and other family members (MFSD3, MFSD6, UNC93A), suggesting roles in specialized vesicular transport or lipid/sugar metabolite shuttling. ClinVar documents 4 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all large copy number losses or gains spanning 1p21-13, yet ZERO specific diseases are formally associated—a gap given the established mouse phenotype and human infertility links. The mechanistic hypothesis: MFSD14A functions as a vesicular transporter for specific sugars, nucleotides, or lipid precursors required for acrosome biogenesis during spermiogenesis and synaptic vesicle recycling in hippocampal neurons. Its interaction with DPY19L2 (a known cause of human globozoospermia) suggests they may operate in a shared pathway regulating acrosomal membrane formation. In the brain, MFSD14A may transport metabolites essential for neurotransmitter packaging or synaptic plasticity. The parathyroid enrichment hints at a role in hormone processing or calcium-regulated secretion. The absence of formal disease nomenclature despite pathogenic CNVs suggests that homozygous loss causes male infertility (often undiagnosed genetically) or embryonic lethality, while heterozygous states may contribute to neurodevelopmental or metabolic phenotypes not yet mapped. Future work should identify the specific substrate(s) of MFSD14A using radiolabeled uptake assays and screen infertile men with globozoospermia for MFSD14A mutations.
