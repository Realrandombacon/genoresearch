# MYL1: A Fast Skeletal Muscle Myosin Light Chain Modulating Cross-Bridge Cycling Kinetics via EF-Hand Calcium Sensing

**Quality Score:** 10/10 (EXCELLENT)

**Confidence:** HIGH

## Description
MYL1 (Myosin Light Chain 1, UniProt P05976), also known as MLC1F or MLC3F, is a 194-amino acid alkali myosin light chain specifically expressed in fast-twitch skeletal muscle fibers that modulates myosin motor function during rapid contraction cycles. InterPro analysis reveals a canonical EF-hand calcium-binding protein architecture: two paired EF-hand domains (IPR002048, IPR011992, G3DSA:1.10.238.10, aa 47-192) forming a helix-loop-helix motif that coordinates calcium ions via conserved acidic residues in the loop regions (IPR050230 family). The AlphaFold predicted structure shows high confidence (pLDDT 87.9), confirming a stable calmodulin-like fold optimized for regulatory protein-protein interactions. STRING interaction data places MYL1 at the core of the fast skeletal muscle contractile apparatus with near-perfect associations to myosin heavy chains MYH7 (0.998), MYH2 (0.994), and MYH13 (0.973)—the motor proteins that generate force; the homologous light chain MYLPF (0.995); thin filament regulators TNNT3 (0.977), TNNI1 (0.975), and TNNI2 (0.973); the giant scaffolding protein nebulin NEB (0.973); and non-muscle light chain MYL12B (0.979). HPA expression profiles confirm extreme tissue specificity with exceptional expression in skeletal muscle (20434.9 nTPM) and tongue (14550.6 nTPM)—both rich in fast-twitch fibers—consistent with its specialized role in rapid contraction. ClinVar records document 28 pathogenic variants including nonsense mutations (p.Arg80Ter) and large copy number gains/losses at 2q32-q37, implicating MYL1 dosage in myopathic disorders. We hypothesize that MYL1 functions as a kinetic tuner of the myosin cross-bridge cycle: unlike regulatory light chains that control activation via phosphorylation, alkali light chains like MYL1 bind constitutively to the myosin heavy chain neck region via their EF-hand domains, stabilizing the lever arm and modulating the rate of ADP release and phosphate dissociation; this determines shortening velocity and power output in fast fibers. The EF-hand domains may also provide calcium-sensitive modulation independent of troponin-mediated thin filament regulation, allowing fine-tuning of contraction dynamics during high-frequency stimulation. Loss of MYL1 would impair fast fiber contractility while sparing slow fibers, potentially causing exercise intolerance or congenital myopathy phenotypes. This positions MYL1 as a critical determinant of muscle performance specialization.
