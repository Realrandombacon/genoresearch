## Description
SERTM2 (serine rich and transmembrane domain containing 2, Gene ID: 401613) is a 90-amino acid micropeptide encoded on chromosome Xq23 that represents a very dark gene with emerging functional characterization in neurodevelopment and reproduction. This gene was recently recognized as a protein-coding micropeptide within what was previously annotated as lncRNA (LINC00890/CARDEL). Literature analysis reveals only 3 publications, with groundbreaking 2025 studies demonstrating that SERTM2 is a conserved micropeptide promoting GDNF-mediated motor neuron subtype specification (cited 2x) and establishing it as a neuroactive player in the micropeptide world (cited 1x). Custom antibodies confirmed protein production from this small ORF. InterPro analysis reveals a serine-rich and transmembrane domain-containing protein family architecture: IPR031741 (positions 1-85), PF15872 serine-rich transmembrane domain (positions 18-83), and PTHR35660 (positions 1-85), consistent with its name and predicted membrane localization. AlphaFold structure prediction shows medium confidence (global pLDDT 67.1), indicating a partially structured protein with both ordered transmembrane regions and potentially disordered serine-rich domains. STRING database returned no interaction partners, reflecting the extreme novelty of this micropeptide's functional characterization. HPA data reveals tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in male reproductive tissues: seminal vesicle (71.2 nTPM), epididymis (39.4 nTPM), and endometrium (25.6 nTPM), with cancer-enriched RNA specificity suggesting potential oncogenic roles. ClinVar contains 78 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all copy number variations spanning Xp22.33-q28 regions, though no specific monogenic disease is attributed solely to SERTM2; these CNVs are associated with X-linked developmental disorders. The 2025 literature establishing SERTM2's role in GDNF-mediated motor neuron specification, combined with its transmembrane domain architecture and high reproductive tissue expression, suggests SERTM2 functions as a membrane-associated signaling modulator that potentiates GDNF (glial cell-derived neurotrophic factor) receptor signaling during motor neuron development, potentially through direct interaction with RET/GFRα receptor complexes. The serine-rich regions may serve as phosphorylation sites for regulatory control. The cancer-enriched expression pattern indicates SERTM2 may be aberrantly upregulated in tumors, representing a potential oncogenic micropeptide. The X-chromosome location and high CNV burden place SERTM2 within regions associated with X-linked intellectual disability and developmental delay syndromes. This represents a very dark gene transitioning to functional characterization through recent micropeptide discovery efforts.
