## Description
CHMP2A (charged multivesicular body protein 2A, alias BC2, VPS2A) encodes a 222-amino acid cytosolic protein belonging to the Snf7/CHMP family (IPR005024/PF03357/PTHR10476, residues 5-220) that forms the core polymer of ESCRT-III (endosomal sorting complex required for transport III). AlphaFold predicts high confidence structure (pLDDT=75.2), indicating a well-folded protein with stable tertiary structure characteristic of membrane-remodeling proteins. The CATH-Gene3D superfamily (G3DSA:6.10.140.1230, residues 6-181) corresponds to the Snf7-like helical bundle fold that undergoes conformational changes during membrane scission. HPA classifies CHMP2A as an essential protein with ubiquitous expression (detected in all tissues) and dual nucleoplasmic/cytoplasmic localization, consistent with roles in both endosomal sorting and nuclear processes. STRING interactions reveal extremely strong coupling to ESCRT-III machinery: CHMP4A (0.999), CHMP3/RNF103-CHMP3 (0.998), CHMP6 (0.990), CHMP1A (0.983), CHMP1B (0.971), VPS4A (0.962, AAA ATPase for ESCRT-III disassembly), VPS4B (0.956), CHMP3 (0.942), CHMP4B (0.922), and CHMP5 (0.871). This interaction network positions CHMP2A as a central scaffold that nucleates ESCRT-III polymerization on endosomal membranes. ClinVar documents 8 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including 19q13.33-13.43 copy number gains, suggesting dosage sensitivity may contribute to genomic instability or developmental disorders. Conservation is strong across mammals: mouse (Q9DB34, 222 aa), chimpanzee. Mechanistically, CHMP2A functions as a membrane-remodeling subunit that oligomerizes into spiral filaments on intraluminal vesicle necks, recruiting VPS4 ATPase for membrane scission and ESCRT-III disassembly. The protein undergoes autoinhibitory conformational changes: closed cytosolic state prevents aggregation, while open membrane-bound state enables polymerization. CHMP2A is essential for MVB biogenesis, receptor downregulation (EGFR, cytokine receptors), viral budding (HIV-1, Ebola), cytokinetic abscission, and nuclear envelope repair. The nucleoplasmic localization suggests additional roles in chromatin organization or DNA damage response.
