## Description
PIRT (Phosphoinositide-interacting regulator of transient receptor potential channels, also known as phosphoinositide-interacting protein) is a 137 amino acid membrane-associated protein (UniProt P0C851) with medium-confidence partially-structured architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 64.0), characteristic of lipid-binding regulatory proteins that contain both ordered phosphoinositide-binding regions and intrinsically disordered segments for dynamic protein-protein interactions. The protein contains a phosphoinositide-interacting protein family domain (IPR028068/PF15099, residues 7-137) enabling phosphatidylinositol bisphosphate binding activity (GO:0035091), and belongs to the transmembrane and phosphoinositide-interacting regulators family (PTHR16100, residues 1-136) involved in regulation of sensory perception of pain (GO:0051930) and positive regulation of cation channel activity (GO:0043265). PIRT is located on chromosome 17p12 and is classified as a predicted membrane protein and transporter with subcellular localization to plasma membrane and cytosol, enabling transmembrane transporter binding activity.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals a network of ion channel, autophagy, and lipid signaling proteins: TRPV1 (0.712, Transient receptor potential cation channel subfamily V member 1, capsaicin/heat receptor), WIPI1 (0.666, WD repeat domain phosphoinositide-interacting protein 1, autophagy), STARD10 (0.627, StAR-related lipid transfer domain-containing protein 10, lipid transport), PIK3C3 (0.611, Phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase catalytic subunit type 3, PI3P synthesis), WIPI2 (0.607, WD repeat domain phosphoinositide-interacting protein 2, autophagosome formation), ATG14 (0.605, Autophagy related 14, autophagy initiation), ZFYVE1 (0.541, Zinc finger FYVE-type containing 1, endosomal trafficking), RASD2 (0.422, RAS dexamethasone-induced 2, GTPase signaling), SMCO3 (0.417, Single-pass membrane and coiled-coil domain-containing protein 3, membrane protein), and SQSTM1 (0.403, Sequestosome 1/p62, autophagy substrate). These interactions confirm PIRT functions within ion channel regulation and phosphoinositide signaling pathways, with TRPV1 as the highest-confidence partner (0.712) reflecting PIRT's direct role in potentiating TRPV1 channel activity for pain sensation.\n\nHPA expression reveals PIRT has tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in brain (11.9 nTPM) and intestine (4.1 nTPM), detected in some tissues, consistent with specialized roles in neuronal nociception and intestinal sensory function. This brain enrichment (11.9 nTPM) indicates PIRT is critical for TRPV1-mediated pain signaling in sensory neurons. Subcellular localization to plasma membrane and cytosol suggests PIRT functions at the membrane-cytosol interface for phosphoinositide-dependent TRPV1 regulation. Cancer-enriched RNA specificity suggests potential roles in tumor cell signaling through ion channel modulation. The protein is classified as a predicted membrane protein and transporter involved in cation channel regulation.\n\nClinVar documents 11 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all consisting of copy number gains (17p13.2-12, 17p13.3-11.2, 17p13.3-12, 17p13.3-q25.3 amplifications) and one copy number loss (17p13.1-12 deletion). The variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity with predominant gain-of-function through amplifications. While no specific Mendelian disease is annotated, 17p CNVs are associated with developmental delay, neurological disorders, and Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease type 1A. Given PIRT's TRPV1 regulatory function and brain-enriched expression, loss-of-function or overexpression may cause pain perception disorders, thermal sensitivity abnormalities, or neuropathic pain syndromes through altered TRPV1 channel gating.\n\nConservation is present across mammals: human (P0C851, 137 aa), mouse (Q8BFY0, 135 aa), indicating the phosphoinositide-binding architecture is conserved in mammals with nearly identical protein length.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: PIRT functions as a phosphoinositide-binding regulatory protein that potentiates TRPV1 channel activity for pain and heat sensation. The medium-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 64.0) indicates a partially structured architecture with an ordered phosphoinositide-binding domain (residues 7-137) that binds phosphatidylinositol 4,5-bisphosphate (PIP2) and phosphatidylinositol 3-phosphate (PI3P), embedded in disordered regions that enable dynamic TRPV1 interaction. Through TRPV1 interaction (0.712), PIRT directly binds and potentiates TRPV1 channel opening in response to capsaicin, heat, and protons. WIPI1 and WIPI2 interactions (0.666/0.607) link PIRT to autophagy through phosphoinositide binding. STARD10 interaction (0.627) connects PIRT to lipid transfer and membrane lipid composition. PIK3C3 interaction (0.611) links PIRT to PI3P synthesis for endosomal trafficking. ATG14 interaction (0.605) suggests roles in autophagosome formation. ZFYVE1 interaction (0.541) connects PIRT to endosomal FYVE domain proteins. SQSTM1 interaction (0.403) indicates potential autophagy substrate functions. Plasma membrane/cytosol localization positions PIRT at the interface for phosphoinositide-dependent TRPV1 regulation. Brain-enriched expression (11.9 nTPM) indicates PIRT functions in sensory neurons for pain perception. 17p CNVs cause PIRT haploinsufficiency or overexpression, potentially altering TRPV1 sensitivity and causing pain disorders or thermal sensitivity abnormalities. PIRT represents a candidate gene for congenital insensitivity to pain and may serve as a therapeutic target for pain modulation through TRPV1 sensitization.
