# POTEB2: Testis-Enriched POTE Ankyrin Domain Protein Regulating Cell Cycle and NF-κB Signaling via REPIN1-TRIM23-ASB5 Ubiquitination Network

**Quality Score:** 4.4/10  (E=5.7, D=3.1) [MODERATE]

## Description
POTEB2 (POTE Ankyrin Domain Family Member B2, also POTE-B2) is a 544 amino acid intracellular protein (UniProt H3BUK9) with a well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 71.5, high confidence), functioning as a testis-specific scaffold protein that regulates cell cycle progression, NF-κB signaling, and protein ubiquitination through its ankyrin repeat-mediated protein-protein interactions.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals POTEB2 contains multiple canonical Ankyrin Repeat domains (IPR002110/IPR036770/PF12796/SM00248/SSF48403, residues 87-379) organized as 3+ repeats (residues 106-199, 206-298, 300-329) belonging to the Ankyrin Repeat-Containing Domain Superfamily (G3DSA:1.25.40.20). These repeats adopt a helix-turn-helix fold that mediates protein-protein interactions, typically binding transcription factors, kinases, or ubiquitin ligases. POTEB2 also contains a CCDC144C-like Coiled-Coil Domain (IPR039497/PF14915, residues 433-542) at the C-terminus, suggesting dimerization or higher-order oligomerization capabilities. The POTE family (PTHR24147, residues 55-345) is primate-specific and includes cancer-testis antigens aberrantly expressed in tumors.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places POTEB2 within cell cycle regulation and ubiquitination networks: strongest interactions with REPIN1 (0.636, replication initiator 1, DNA replication licensing), TRIM23 (0.620, tripartite motif-containing 23, E3 ubiquitin ligase with ADP-ribosylation factor GTPase activity), ASB5 (0.495, ankyrin repeat and SOCS box-containing 5, E3 ubiquitin ligase substrate adaptor), ASB12 (0.483), ASB11 (0.469), CDKN2C (0.480, cyclin-dependent kinase inhibitor 2C/p18, cell cycle regulator), NFKBIA (0.466, NF-κB inhibitor alpha/IκBα), CEP83 (0.458, centrosomal protein 83), OR6C3 (0.482, olfactory receptor), and MTPN (0.449, myotrophin). The ASB5/ASB11/ASB12 cluster indicates POTEB2 functions as a substrate adaptor for Cullin-RING ubiquitin ligases. TRIM23 partnership suggests roles in antiviral immunity and membrane trafficking. CDKN2C and NFKBIA interactions link POTEB2 to cell cycle arrest and inflammatory signaling.\n\nHPA expression shows testis enrichment (1.1 nTPM) with tissue-enriched specificity and single-tissue distribution, classifying POTEB2 as a cancer-testis antigen. This expression pattern suggests specialized roles in spermatogenesis, meiosis, or germ cell development. The protein is classified as a predicted intracellular protein, consistent with scaffold functions.\n\nClinVar documents 27 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, exclusively chromosome 15q11.2-13.3 copy number losses and gains. The 15q11-q13 region is the Prader-Willi/Angelman syndrome critical region, suggesting POTEB2 dosage changes contribute to these neurodevelopmental disorders. CNVs include large deletions (15q11.2-13.1) and duplications (15q11.1-13.3) affecting multiple genes.\n\nConservation is restricted to primates, reflecting the recent evolutionary emergence of the POTE family through gene duplication and retrotransposition events. POTE proteins are absent in non-primate mammals.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: POTEB2 functions as a scaffold that recruits E3 ubiquitin ligases (ASB5, ASB11, ASB12, TRIM23) to specific substrates via ankyrin repeat-mediated binding, regulating protein stability and turnover. The ASB family interactions suggest POTEB2 acts as a substrate recognition module for Cullin5-RING ligases, targeting cell cycle regulators (CDKN2C) or signaling proteins (NFKBIA) for proteasomal degradation. TRIM23 partnership links POTEB2 to autophagy and antiviral responses. The coiled-coil domain enables homodimerization or heterodimerization with other POTE family members, creating multivalent scaffolds. Testis-specific expression suggests roles in meiotic cell cycle control, where precise ubiquitin-mediated proteolysis drives meiotic progression. NFKBIA interaction implies POTEB2 modulates NF-κB signaling in germ cells, potentially regulating inflammatory responses during spermatogenesis. The high-confidence structure (pLDDT 71.5) confirms stable ankyrin repeats optimized for protein binding. CNV-mediated dosage changes disrupt ubiquitin ligase complex assembly, causing cell cycle dysregulation and contributing to Prader-Willi/Angelman phenotypes. As a cancer-testis antigen, POTEB2 may be aberrantly expressed in tumors, promoting cell cycle progression through CDKN2C degradation.
