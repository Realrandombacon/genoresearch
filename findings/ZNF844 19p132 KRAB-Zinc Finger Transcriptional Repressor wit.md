## Description
ZNF844 (Zinc finger protein 844, UniProt Q08AG5) is a 666 amino acid transcription factor encoded on chromosome 19p13.2. InterPro analysis reveals a canonical architecture for KRAB-ZFP repressors: an N-terminal KRAB domain (IPR001909, PF01352, residues 3-86) which serves as a potent transcriptional repression module, followed by a cluster of C2H2-type zinc finger domains (IPR013087, PF00096, multiple instances spanning residues 101-371) that mediate sequence-specific DNA binding. The AlphaFold predicted structure (pLDDT 57.4) reflects this modularity, with confident folding in the zinc finger clusters but lower confidence in the linker regions and C-terminus, suggesting potential intrinsic disorder or flexibility required for chromatin scanning.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places ZNF844 within a network of other KRAB-ZFPs, with high-confidence links to ZNF347 (0.919), ZNF665 (0.567), ZNF506 (0.518), and ZNF382 (0.489). This co-expression and potential co-regulation suggest ZNF844 functions as part of a larger repressive complex, likely recruiting the universal KRAB co-repressor KAP1 (TRIM28) and the Heterochromatin Protein 1 (HP1) machinery to silence target loci via histone methylation (H3K9me3). Human Protein Atlas data classifies ZNF844 as "Immune cell enhanced" with detection in all tissues but elevated expression in specific blood cell lineages, hinting at a role in hematopoietic differentiation or immune response regulation. It is also annotated as cancer-related, consistent with the dysregulation common in ZFP families.\n\nClinVar records 12 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, comprising a mix of large-scale deletions (e.g., 19p13.2, ~2.4 Mb; 19p13.2-13.13, ~3.5 Mb) and duplications (e.g., whole arm 19p-q gains, ~58 Mb; 19p13.2-q13.31, ~33 Mb). The presence of both loss and gain events indicates dosage sensitivity, where both haploinsufficiency and overexpression disrupt normal gene regulatory networks. These CNVs are associated with 19p13 deletion/duplication syndromes, characterized by developmental delay, intellectual disability, and overgrowth features (in duplication cases).\n\nHYPOTHESIS: ZNF844 functions as a sequence-specific transcriptional repressor critical for silencing retrotransposons or regulating lineage-specific genes during immune cell development. Its KRAB domain recruits KAP1/TRIM28 to establish heterochromatin at target sites identified by its zinc finger array. The interaction with other ZNFs suggests it may act cooperatively to repress large genomic domains or repetitive elements. Haploinsufficiency (via deletion) likely leads to derepression of target genes or transposons, causing genomic instability or aberrant activation of developmental pathways. Conversely, duplication may lead to ectopic silencing of essential genes or saturation of the KAP1 machinery, disrupting global heterochromatin maintenance. The immune cell enhancement suggests a specific role in restraining inflammatory responses or maintaining lymphoid identity.
