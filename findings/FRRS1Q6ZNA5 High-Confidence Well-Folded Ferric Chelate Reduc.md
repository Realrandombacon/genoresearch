## Description
FRRS1 (Ferric chelate reductase 1, also known as SDFR2, SDR2, stromal cell-derived receptor 2) is a 592 amino acid membrane-associated oxidoreductase (UniProt Q6ZNA5) with high-confidence well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 88.3), characteristic of stable transmembrane enzymes that catalyze electron transfer reactions. The protein contains multiple defining domains: a cytochrome b561/ferric reductase transmembrane domain (IPR006593/PS50939, residues 335-534) with oxidoreductase activity, a Reeler domain (IPR002861/PF02014/G3DSA:2.60.40.4060/cd08544, residues 13-179/31-163) involved in protein-protein interactions, a DOMON domain (IPR005018/PF03351/PS50836/cd09628, residues 216-331/217-331/192-354) that binds heme or metal cofactors, and belongs to the cytochrome b561 family. FRRS1 is located on chromosome 1p21.2 and functions as a ferric reductase that reduces Fe3+ to Fe2+ before transport from endosome to cytoplasm.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals a network of cytochrome b561 family members and iron metabolism proteins with high to medium confidence scores: CYB561 (0.956, Cytochrome b561, the canonical family member involved in ascorbate-dependent ferric reduction), HPX (0.779, Hemopexin, heme-binding plasma protein), CYB561D2 (0.642, Cytochrome b561 domain-containing protein 2, paralogous family member), HSD17B14 (0.521, 17-beta-hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase 14, oxidoreductase), CYBRD1 (0.507, Cytochrome b reductase 1, duodenal ferric reductase Dcytb), ATP5PD (0.500, ATP synthase subunit d, mitochondrial), ATP5PB (0.463, ATP synthase subunit b), ATP5F1D (0.446, ATP synthase subunit delta), and ATP5F1C (0.436, ATP synthase subunit gamma). These interactions confirm FRRS1 functions within the cytochrome b561 ferric reductase family and may link iron metabolism with mitochondrial energy production.\n\nHPA expression reveals FRRS1 is tissue-enhanced with highest expression in liver (26.7 nTPM), detected in many tissues. The protein shows cancer-enhanced expression and immune cell-enriched expression in blood cells. Subcellular localization is predicted as membrane protein with oxidoreductase molecular function involved in electron transport and transport biological processes. The liver-enriched expression is consistent with the liver's central role in systemic iron homeostasis, storing iron in ferritin and producing hepcidin.\n\nClinVar documents 11 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including large copy number losses (1p22.1-21.1, 1p21.3-13.2 deletions), copy number gains (1p22.1-11.2, 1p36.33-q44, 1p31.1-21.1 amplifications), and intronic/splice region SNVs (c.197-129A>G, c.333+15T>G, c.759+2T>G). The variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity. While no specific Mendelian disease is annotated, 1p CNVs are associated with developmental delay, intellectual disability, and congenital anomalies. Given FRRS1's role in iron reduction and liver expression, dosage imbalance may affect cellular iron homeostasis, potentially causing iron deficiency or overload phenotypes.\n\nConservation is strong across vertebrates: human (Q6ZNA5, 592 aa), mouse (Q8K385, 592 aa), bovine (A2VE04, 591 aa), xenopus (Q6INU7, 590 aa), zebrafish (A4QP81, 573 aa), indicating an ancient and essential function in iron metabolism conserved from fish to mammals.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: FRRS1 functions as an endosomal ferric reductase that reduces Fe3+ to Fe2+ within acidified endosomes, enabling iron export to the cytoplasm via DMT1 (divalent metal transporter 1). The cytochrome b561 transmembrane domain forms the catalytic core with heme-binding sites for electron transfer from cytoplasmic donors (possibly ascorbate or NADH) to luminal ferric iron. The Reeler and DOMON domains may mediate protein-protein interactions with endosomal trafficking machinery or iron transporters. Through CYB561 and CYBRD1 interactions, FRRS1 may coordinate with other ferric reductases in different cellular compartments. The liver-enriched expression suggests FRRS1 plays a key role in hepatic iron handling, potentially in transferrin receptor-mediated iron uptake or ferritin iron mobilization. Immune cell expression implies roles in macrophage iron recycling from phagocytosed erythrocytes. Cancer-enhanced expression suggests FRRS1 may be upregulated in tumors to support iron-dependent proliferation. 1p CNVs cause FRRS1 dosage imbalance, disrupting cellular iron homeostasis and contributing to neurodevelopmental phenotypes through impaired iron delivery to the brain. FRRS1 represents a candidate gene for iron metabolism disorders and a potential therapeutic target for iron-related diseases.
