## Description
DRC8 (UniProt Q5VUJ9, 269 aa), also known as EFCAB2 (EF-hand calcium-binding domain-containing protein 2), is a critical structural and regulatory component of the Nexin-Dynein Regulatory Complex (N-DRC) that governs ciliary and flagellar motility by translating calcium signals into mechanical adjustments. InterPro analysis reveals a defining architecture dominated by two canonical EF-hand calcium-binding domains (IPR002048, PF13499, aa 150-185 and 228-263) embedded within a larger Nexin-Dynein Regulatory Complex component family context (PTHR46763, aa 133-266). These motifs classify it as a calcium sensor capable of undergoing conformational changes upon Ca2+ binding. AlphaFold predicts a medium-confidence structure (global pLDDT 64.6), reflecting a structured C-terminal EF-hand pair connected by flexible, potentially disordered linkers to an N-terminal region, consistent with a dynamic scaffold role that must adapt to the rhythmic bending of cilia. HPA classifies it as an intracellular protein detected in all tissues but notably 'immune cell enhanced,' with subcellular localization predicted in the cytosol, plasma membrane, mitochondria, and specifically the 'midpiece' of sperm flagella, linking it to both mucociliary clearance and sperm motility. STRING interactions define its precise mechanistic niche: DRC7 (0.543, direct N-DRC partner), GAS8 (0.490, dynein axonemal heavy chain), TCTE1 (0.618, t-complex associated testis expressed), and other calcium-modulated proteins like IQCD (0.594) and HRC (0.532). The strong link to DRC7 and GAS8 confirms its integration into the N-DRC 'strut' that limits microtubule sliding and regulates beat frequency. ClinVar contains 42 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number losses and gains on chromosome 1q43-q44; while no specific monogenic syndrome name is yet assigned, deletions in this region are associated with intellectual disability, seizures, and corpus callosum agenesis, suggesting that loss of DRC8 contributes to neurodevelopmental defects via impaired neuronal migration (which relies on ciliary function) or primary ciliary dyskinesia-like phenotypes. Functional hypothesis: DRC8 acts as the 'calcium clutch' of the axoneme. Under basal conditions, it stabilizes the N-DRC structure, maintaining rigid ciliary beating. Upon local calcium influx (e.g., during chemotaxis or irritant response), Ca2+ binds to the EF-hands of DRC8, inducing a conformational shift that alters the tension within the N-DRC, thereby modulating dynein arm activity and changing the ciliary beat pattern (e.g., switching from symmetric to asymmetric beating for turning). In immune cells, this mechanism may regulate the motility of monocilia involved in sensing the microenvironment. In sperm, it is essential for hyperactivation. The 'dark' nature of this gene stems from the difficulty in studying specific N-DRC subunits in isolation, yet its conservation and domain structure mark it as an essential node in mechanotransduction. NOTE: This gene represents a 'dark' but mechanistically vital linker between calcium signaling and ciliary mechanics.
