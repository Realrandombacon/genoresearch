# POMK: Protein O-Mannose Kinase Phosphorylating α-Dystroglycan O-Mannose Glycans Essential for Matriglycan Elongation by LARGE1, Causative Gene for Dystroglycanopathies Including Walker-Warburg Syndrome and Limb-Girdle Muscular Dystrophy with 105 Pathogenic Variants

## Description
POMK (Protein O-Mannose Kinase, UniProt Q9H5K3) is a 350 amino acid intracellular kinase with moderate characterization (38 papers) that functions as a critical enzyme in the dystroglycan glycosylation pathway, specifically phosphorylating the O-mannose glycan on α-dystroglycan to enable subsequent matriglycan elongation by LARGE1. The protein contains a protein kinase domain (IPR000719/PF07714/PS50011, residues 81-350/83-195/81-350) with protein kinase activity (GO:0004672), ATP binding (GO:0005524), and protein phosphorylation (GO:0006468), serine-threonine/tyrosine-protein kinase catalytic domain (IPR001245, residues 83-195), protein kinase-like superfamily (IPR011009/SSF56112, residues 81-330), and belongs to the Protein O-mannose kinase family (IPR039318/PTHR22618, residues 1-338/1-338) with carbohydrate kinase activity (GO:0019200) and protein O-linked glycosylation function (GO:0006493), placing it in the essential dystroglycanopathy machinery. STRING interactions reveal very high-confidence partnerships with dystroglycan glycosylation enzymes and substrates: B3GALNT2 (0.987, beta-1,3-N-acetylgalactosaminyltransferase 2), DAG1 (0.921, dystroglycan 1), POMGNT2 (0.892, protein O-mannose beta-1,2-N-acetylglucosaminyltransferase 2), POMGNT1 (0.890, protein O-mannose beta-1,2-N-acetylglucosaminyltransferase 1), POMT1 (0.855, protein O-mannosyl-transferase 1), FKRP (0.829, fukutin-related protein), FKTN (0.810, fukutin), POMT2 (0.755, protein O-mannosyl-transferase 2), RXYLT1 (0.720, ribitol xylosyltransferase 1), and B4GAT1 (0.720, beta-1,4-galactosyltransferase 1), confirming membership in the complete α-dystroglycan glycosylation cascade. HPA data shows low tissue specificity with detection in all tissues, low cancer specificity, low cell type specificity, and classification as disease-related gene/enzyme/kinase/transferase/potential drug target with disease involvement including congenital muscular dystrophy, dystroglycanopathy, limb-girdle muscular dystrophy, and lissencephaly. ClinVar contains 105 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including frameshift deletions (c.43del p.Arg15fs, c.61del p.Val21fs), duplications (c.91_98dup p.Leu34fs), missense mutations (c.812A>G p.Asp271Gly, c.580C>A p.His194Asn), and large copy number gains/deletions at 8p11.23-q21.3 (chr8:36650289-43776564/37551624-52934472/20136266-43786723/27024288-89410121), directly causing severe dystroglycanopathies. AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 91.6) indicates very high confidence with well-folded kinase architecture typical of atypical kinases specialized for carbohydrate substrate phosphorylation. Functional hypothesis: POMK acts as a specialized atypical protein kinase that phosphorylates the C6 position of O-mannose glycans on α-dystroglycan, creating a critical primer structure required for LARGE1-mediated synthesis of the repeating [-GlcA-β1,3-Xyl-α1,3-]n matriglycan chain that binds extracellular matrix proteins (laminin, agrin, perlecan), where research demonstrates: (1) POMK regulates dystroglycan function via LARGE1-mediated matriglycan elongation (2020, 20 citations)—POMK phosphorylation is an obligatory step preceding LARGE1 activity, (2) POMK as candidate gene for Walker-Warburg Syndrome (WWS) with meningoencephalocele (2020, 5 citations)—biallelic variants cause severe congenital muscular dystrophy with brain and eye malformations, (3) Homozygous POMK variant causes severe ventriculomegaly (2019, 7 citations)—prenatal detection of CNS defects linked to POMK deficiency, (4) 3D structural analysis of POMK as causative gene product of dystroglycanopathy (2017, 26 citations)—structural insights into kinase mechanism and disease mutations, (5) STRING partners (POMT1/2, POMGNT1/2, B3GALNT2, FKTN, FKRP, B4GAT1, RXYLT1, LARGE1 implied via DAG1) confirm integration into sequential glycosylation pathway where POMK acts after initial mannose transfer and GlcNAc addition but before matriglycan polymerization, (6) Disease involvement (congenital muscular dystrophy, WWS, limb-girdle MD, lissencephaly) reflects critical role in basement membrane integrity in muscle, brain, and eye through α-dystroglycan-ECM linkage, (7) 105 ClinVar variants indicate strong disease causality with loss-of-function mechanism leading to hypoglycosylated α-dystroglycan and disrupted ECM adhesion, (8) Very high confidence structure (pLDDT 91.6) indicates stable kinase fold with specialized active site for carbohydrate substrate recognition, making POMK a therapeutic target for dystroglycanopathies through glycosylation pathway restoration.
