## Description
RNF149 (Ring Finger Protein 149, alias DNAPTP2, UniProt Q8NC42) is a 400 amino acid integral membrane E3 ubiquitin-protein ligase containing a canonical RING-type zinc finger domain (IPR001841/PF13639/PS50089, residues 268-310/269-310) that defines the H2 subclass of RING fingers (CD16804/IPR042712, residues 268-315) essential for E2 ubiquitin-conjugating enzyme binding and ubiquitin transfer activity. The protein also contains a PA (protease-associated) domain (IPR003137/IPR046450/PF02225/CD02122, residues 108-171/89-192/43-185) typically involved in protein-protein interactions and substrate recognition. Additional structural annotations include CATH-Gene3D zinc/RING finger superfamily (G3DSA:3.30.40.10, residues 230-315) and PANTHER family PTHR46539 (RING-type zinc finger E3 ubiquitin-protein ligases, residues 199-325). HPA expression demonstrates ubiquitous detection across all tissues with low tissue specificity but immune cell enrichment at the single-cell level, classified as metabolic enzyme and transferase involved in Ubl (ubiquitin-like) conjugation pathway, with subcellular localization to vesicles and plasma membrane consistent with membrane-associated ubiquitination functions. ClinVar contains 15 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly copy number gains and losses spanning 2q11.1-13 regions, indicating dosage sensitivity though no specific disease phenotype is yet defined. Mechanistic hypothesis: RNF149 functions as a membrane-associated E3 ubiquitin ligase that regulates receptor turnover and signal transduction through ubiquitin-dependent proteasomal degradation or endocytic trafficking. The PA domain suggests substrate recognition capability, potentially targeting activated receptors (ACVR1, TNFRSF10B) for downregulation following ligand binding. Immune cell enrichment indicates specialized role in immune receptor regulation, possibly controlling cytokine receptor sensitivity or death receptor-mediated apoptosis thresholds. Vesicle and plasma membrane localization supports involvement in endocytic sorting decisions—determining whether ubiquitinated cargo undergoes lysosomal degradation or recycling. Interaction with UBE2O (an E2/E3 hybrid with broad substrate range) suggests potential cooperative ubiquitination or competition for substrates. SETDB2 interaction hints at chromatin-level coordination between ubiquitination and histone methylation pathways. Dosage sensitivity from ClinVar CNVs suggests precise RNF149 levels critical for proper receptor homeostasis, with potential implications for immune dysregulation or developmental disorders when disrupted.
