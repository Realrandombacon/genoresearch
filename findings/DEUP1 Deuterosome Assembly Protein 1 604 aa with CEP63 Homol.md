## Description
DEUP1 (Deuterosome Assembly Protein 1), also known as Deup1, is a 604-amino acid intracellular protein that functions as a critical scaffold for deuterosome formation—the non-membranous organelles that drive massive centriole amplification during multiciliated cell (MCC) differentiation. InterPro analysis reveals a specialized domain architecture homologous to centrosomal protein CEP63: an N-terminal CEP63/Deup1 domain (IPR031470, residues 11-280; PF17045, residues 11-280; PTHR18875, residues 29-312) essential for self-assembly and centriole recruitment, and a C-terminal CEP152-binding coiled-coil domain (IPR057656, residues 561-595; PF25771, residues 561-595) that anchors DEUP1 to the centrosomal scaffold. AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (pLDDT 77.6), indicating a well-folded coiled-coil protein capable of forming stable oligomeric assemblies—a feature consistent with its role as a structural platform for deuterosome biogenesis.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places DEUP1 in a network of centriole amplification and cell cycle regulators with medium-to-high confidence: SPAG5 (0.689, spindle-associated protein 5, mitotic spindle assembly), CDC20B (0.663, cell division cycle 20B, anaphase-promoting complex), TMEM135 (0.648, transmembrane protein 135, peroxisome-mitochondria regulation), STIL (0.643, SCL/TAL1 interrupting locus, centriole duplication), MCIDAS (0.637, Multicilin-associated protein, transcriptional coactivator), GMNC (0.622, Geminin coiled-coil domain-containing protein, multiciliogenesis master regulator), CCNO (0.459, cyclin-O, centriole amplification), PLK4 (0.425, polo-like kinase 4, master centriole biogenesis kinase), CCDC78 (0.411, coiled-coil domain-containing protein 78), and SASS6 (0.410, SAS-6 centriolar protein, cartwheel assembly). This network confirms DEUP1's position downstream of the GMNC-MCIDAS transcriptional program, where it physically assembles deuterosomes that nucleate hundreds of procentrioles via PLK4-STIL-SAS6 machinery.\n\nHuman Protein Atlas classifies DEUP1 as a group-enriched intracellular and membrane protein with highest expression in testis (15.3 nTPM) and fallopian tube (4.5 nTPM), reflecting its role in multiciliated epithelia of the reproductive tract (efferent ducts, oviduct) and potentially spermatogenesis. It has low cancer specificity but is detected in some tissues. Biological processes are strictly limited to cilium biogenesis and degradation.\n\nClinVar reports 16 pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants, including large copy number variations spanning 11q14-22 (coordinates include chr11:81041448-98151664x1 loss, chr11:81478509-104667040x1 loss, chr11:32799481-134938470x3 gain, chr11:84924674-93574799 loss, chr11:80318996-96116221 loss, chr11:77855209-98002445 loss, chr11:71588805-116680918x3 gain, chr11:230616-134938470 gain) and a specific missense mutation (c.820A>G, p.Ser274Gly) affecting the N-terminal CEP63-homology domain. While no single-gene Mendelian disorder is exclusively named in ClinVar, aberrant DEUP1 promoter methylation is clinically significant in hepatocellular carcinoma (2019, 5 citations), and recent 2026 studies identify DEUP1 as a scaffold for basal foot integrity and planar polarity in multiciliated cells.\n\nLiterature analysis (17 papers) reveals DEUP1 is partially characterized with emerging mechanistic insights. Key findings include its 2021 biophysical characterization showing self-assembly properties driving deuterosome formation, its 2021 identification as an E2F4 cytoplasmic binding partner (with SAS6) for centriole replication, its 2023 discovery that Deup1 loss does not abolish centriole amplification (suggesting deuterosome-independent pathways exist), and its 2026 role in basal foot anchoring for ciliary orientation. The 2019 hepatocellular carcinoma study demonstrates epigenetic silencing via promoter methylation.\n\nWe hypothesize that DEUP1 functions through a self-assembly scaffold mechanism: (1) Transcriptional activation—GMNC-MCIDAS complex induces DEUP1 expression during MCC differentiation; (2) Deuterosome nucleation—DEUP1 N-terminal domains self-oligomerize via coiled-coil interactions to form electron-dense deuterosome platforms; (3) CEP152 recruitment—C-terminal coiled-coil binds CEP152, linking deuterosomes to existing centrosomal material; (4) Procentriole assembly—DEUP1 scaffolds recruit PLK4 kinase, which phosphorylates STIL and SAS6 to initiate cartwheel formation and procentriole budding; (5) Basal foot anchoring—post-differentiation, DEUP1 maintains basal foot structures that anchor cilia to the apical membrane and establish planar polarity for coordinated beating; (6) Cancer evasion—hepatocellular carcinoma silences DEUP1 via promoter methylation, potentially disrupting ciliary tumor suppressor functions; (7) Reproductive specialization—testis and fallopian tube expression suggests roles in sperm flagella formation and oviductal ciliary transport. The 16 ClinVar CNVs represent a significant burden of 11q genomic instability potentially affecting multiciliogenesis, mucociliary clearance, and reproductive function. DEUP1 represents a validated deuterosome scaffold, an emerging basal foot integrity factor, a hepatocellular carcinoma epigenetic target, and a candidate gene for primary ciliary dyskinesia-like syndromes.
