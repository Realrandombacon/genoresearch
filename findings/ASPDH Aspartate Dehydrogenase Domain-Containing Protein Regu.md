## Description
ASPDH (Aspartate Dehydrogenase Domain-Containing Protein, UniProt A6ND91) is a 283 aa intracellular enzyme belonging to the aspartate/homoserine dehydrogenase family that functions in NAD+ biosynthesis and amino acid metabolism through NAD(P)-dependent oxidation of L-aspartate. InterPro analysis reveals canonical dehydrogenase architecture: NAD(P)-binding Rossmann-like domain (IPR005106/IPR036291, positions 8-157; PF03447, positions 13-123; G3DSA:3.40.50.720, positions 11-126) for cofactor binding, aspartate dehydrogenase catalytic domain (IPR002811, positions 173-261; PF01958, positions 173-261) for substrate recognition and catalysis, L-aspartate dehydrogenase family domain (IPR011182, positions 7-274; PIRSF005227, positions 7-274; PTHR31873, positions 3-283), and C-terminal dihydrodipicolinate reductase domain (G3DSA:3.30.360.10, positions 127-228). AlphaFold structure shows very high confidence (pLDDT 93.7), indicating an exceptionally well-folded two-domain dehydrogenase with stable Rossmann fold for NAD(P) binding and catalytic pocket. HPA data confirms liver-enriched expression (129.1 nTPM, tissue enhanced), also detected in brain (40.3 nTPM) and kidney (38.2 nTPM), nucleoplasm/cytosol localization, classified as predicted intracellular protein with cancer-enriched expression pattern. Literature (22 papers) establishes ASPDH roles in: (1) novel NAADP-binding protein identification linking ASPDH to calcium release from intracellular acidic stores (2022, 4 citations), (2) NAADP-binding proteins connecting NAADP signaling to cancer and immunity with ASPDH as less selective binder compared to HN1L/JPT2 and LSM12 (2023, 4 citations), (3) de novo nonsense variant causing ulcerative skin lesions in Holstein cattle (2020), and (4) bacterial aspartate dehydrogenase characterization for metabolic engineering applications. FUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: ASPDH serves as an NAD(P)-dependent L-aspartate dehydrogenase that catalyzes oxidation of L-aspartate to iminoaspartate using NAD+ or NADP+ as cofactor, contributing to NAD+ biosynthesis pathway and aspartate metabolism while functioning as an NAADP-binding protein that modulates calcium signaling from acidic intracellular stores. MECHANISTIC PROPOSAL: The N-terminal Rossmann fold domain (positions 11-126) binds NAD(P)+ cofactor through conserved glycine-rich motif, positioning the nicotinamide ring for hydride transfer. The C-terminal catalytic domain (positions 173-261) contains the aspartate binding pocket with catalytic residues for substrate dehydrogenation. ASPDH catalyzes: L-aspartate + NAD(P)+ → iminoaspartate + NAD(P)H + H+, with iminoaspartate spontaneously hydrolyzing to oxaloacetate and ammonia. This reaction connects to NAD+ biosynthesis from quinolinate precursor and aspartate family amino acid metabolism. The NAADP-binding function suggests ASPDH contains a distinct binding pocket for nicotinic acid adenine dinucleotide phosphate (NAADP), the most potent calcium-mobilizing second messenger. NAADP binding may allosterically regulate ASPDH enzymatic activity or recruit ASPDH to acidic calcium stores (lysosomes, endosomes) for localized calcium signaling. In cancer, ASPDH overexpression may enhance NAD+ production supporting tumor metabolism and Warburg effect, while NAADP-mediated calcium signaling promotes proliferation and metastasis. Liver enrichment (129.1 nTPM) reflects central role in hepatic amino acid metabolism and gluconeogenesis. Brain and kidney expression suggests neuronal and renal metabolic functions. The 14 ClinVar CNVs indicate ASPDH dosage sensitivity may contribute to metabolic disorders or cancer predisposition. Very high AlphaFold confidence (93.7) confirms stable two-domain dehydrogenase structure suitable for drug targeting.
