## Description
TMEM115 (Gene ID: 11070, UniProt Q12893, alias PL6) is a 351 amino acid transmembrane protein located on chromosome 3p21.31 (NC_000003.12, positions 50354749-50359520, ~4.8kb region). InterPro domain analysis reveals membership in the TMEM115/Pdh1/Rbl19 family (IPR013861, positions 7-316) with a rhomboid-like superfamily fold (IPR035952, SSF144091, positions 22-213) and DUF1751 eukaryotic integral membrane protein domain (PF08551, SM01160, positions 49-151). The rhomboid-like fold suggests potential serine protease activity or substrate-binding function, though catalytic residues may be degenerate. AlphaFold structure prediction (AF-Q12893-F1) shows high confidence (global pLDDT 78.6), indicating a well-folded transmembrane protein with stable tertiary structure suitable for structural comparisons. Human Protein Atlas classifies TMEM115 as a plasma protein and predicted membrane protein localized to the Golgi apparatus, with low tissue specificity and detection across all surveyed tissues, consistent with housekeeping functions in vesicular trafficking. STRING interactions reveal strong connections to vesicle transport machinery: MUL1 (0.849, high confidence, mitochondrial ubiquitin ligase), BET1L (0.619, SNARE protein involved in ER-Golgi transport), ACTR1A (0.610, actin-related protein component of dynactin complex), STX5 (0.603, syntaxin-5 SNARE protein), GOSR2 (0.570, Golgi SNAP receptor complex member 2), GOSR1 (0.499, Golgi SNAP receptor complex member 1), and CAPZA1/2/3 (0.504-0.532, capping protein actin filament subunits). These interactions strongly implicate TMEM115 in COPI vesicle formation and retrograde Golgi-to-ER transport. ClinVar contains 9 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all large copy number variations affecting 3p21 region including deletions (GRCh37/hg19 3p21.31-21.2 chr3:48807193-51363558x1, GRCh38/hg38 3p21.31-14.3 chr3:49461000-55314500x1) and duplications (multiple 3p gains). Conservation is evident with mouse ortholog Tmem115 (Q9WUH1, 350 aa, 99% identity). Functional hypothesis: TMEM115 functions as an integral Golgi membrane protein that facilitates retrograde vesicle-mediated transport from Golgi to endoplasmic reticulum through interactions with COPI coat components and SNARE machinery. The rhomboid-like domain may regulate substrate selection or membrane fusion events during vesicle budding. As a component of the Golgi transport complex, TMEM115 likely participates in maintaining Golgi architecture and ensuring proper recycling of escaped ER resident proteins. Disease mechanism: Haploinsufficiency from 3p21.31 deletions disrupts TMEM115 dosage alongside neighboring genes, potentially impairing vesicular trafficking and Golgi function. Defective retrograde transport may cause accumulation of misfolded proteins in Golgi, disrupted glycosylation pathways, and impaired secretion - contributing to developmental phenotypes observed in 3p deletion syndromes including intellectual disability, growth retardation, and distinctive facial features. Priority experiments include co-immunoprecipitation with COPI subunits to confirm complex membership, assessment of Golgi morphology in TMEM115 knockout cells, measurement of retrograde transport efficiency using cargo reporters, and investigation of rhomboid-like domain catalytic activity.
