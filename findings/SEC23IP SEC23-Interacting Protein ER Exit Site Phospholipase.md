# SEC23IP (SEC23-Interacting Protein): ER Exit Site Phospholipase A1 Regulating COPII Vesicle Biogenesis and ER-Golgi Transport via SAM-Mediated Protein Oligomerization and DDHD Catalytic Lipid Remodeling, with Pathogenic Variants Implicated in Neural Crest Developmental Disorders

## Description
SEC23IP encodes a 1000 aa multi-domain phospholipase A1 enzyme (UniProt Q9Y6Y8), also known as P125 or iPLA1beta, that functions as a critical regulator of COPII-coated vesicle formation at endoplasmic reticulum exit sites (ERES), coupling lipid remodeling to protein cargo selection during ER-Golgi transport. The protein features a sophisticated domain architecture: an N-terminal region of unknown function; two WWE domains (IPR004170/PF02825/PF23464, residues 272-342 and 347-430) mediating protein-protein interactions with ubiquitinated substrates or ADP-ribose polymers; a central sterile alpha motif (SAM) domain (IPR001660/PF00536, residues 641-706; IPR037603) enabling homo-oligomerization and heterotypic protein interactions essential for ERES scaffolding; and a C-terminal DDHD catalytic domain (IPR004177/PF02862/PS51043, residues 779-989) conferring phosphatidic acid-preferring phospholipase A1 activity for membrane lipid remodeling. STRING interactions place SEC23IP at the heart of the COPII vesicle machinery: SEC31A (0.959) and SEC13 (0.946) forming the outer COPII coat cage; SEC24A/B/C (0.582-0.869) cargo adaptors recognizing export signals; SEC23A (0.665) GTPase-activating protein for SAR1; SCFD1 (0.792) syntaxin-binding protein facilitating vesicle tethering; and SEC16A (0.763) large peripheral membrane protein organizing ERES architecture. Additional partners include WWOX (0.733), tumor suppressor potentially linking SEC23IP to cancer pathways, and PDCD6 (0.477), calcium-binding apoptosis regulator. HPA confirms ubiquitous expression (detected in all tissues) with low tissue specificity and vesicular subcellular localization, consistent with fundamental role in secretory pathway function across cell types. ClinVar lists 36 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number gains (10q24.1-26.3 spanning 98-131Mb, 10q25.1-26.3 spanning 107-135Mb, 10q23.33-26.3 spanning 95-135Mb) and losses (10q26.11-26.3 spanning 121-135Mb), plus splice site mutations (c.2313+1G>T, c.1873-5del), demonstrating dosage sensitivity though specific monogenic diseases remain undefined—gene info notes frog ortholog required for neural crest development suggesting potential Waardenburg syndrome involvement. AlphaFold structure prediction yields medium confidence (global pLDDT 64.7), revealing partially structured protein with well-folded SAM and DDHD domains interspersed with flexible/linker regions characteristic of dynamic membrane-associated scaffolds requiring conformational flexibility for vesicle budding cycles. Mechanistically, SEC23IP operates through integrated modes: (1) ERES recruitment—localizes to ER exit sites via SEC23/SEC24 interaction anchoring to nascent COPII coats; (2) Lipid remodeling—DDHD domain hydrolyzes phosphatidic acid generating lysophosphatidic acid and free fatty acids altering membrane curvature favoring vesicle budding; (3) Coat stabilization—SAM domain oligomerization crosslinks COPII components enhancing coat assembly efficiency; (4) Cargo selection coordination—WWE domains may recognize ubiquitinated cargo or regulate coat dynamics via ADP-ribosylation sensing; (5) Neural crest specification—developmental studies suggest SEC23IP-dependent secretion of neural crest migratory factors (e.g., Wnts, BMPs) requires precise COPII function. Disruption causes defective protein secretion, ER stress accumulation, impaired neural crest migration potentially leading to pigmentation defects, hearing loss, and craniofacial anomalies characteristic of Waardenburg spectrum disorders.
