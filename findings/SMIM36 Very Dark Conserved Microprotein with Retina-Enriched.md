## Description
SMIM36 (small integral membrane protein 36, Gene ID: 101927367) is a 93-amino acid predicted membrane protein encoded on chromosome 17q22 that represents a very dark gene with only 2 publications but groundbreaking functional characterization in retinal development. Literature analysis reveals a pivotal 2023 study demonstrating that SMIM36 is a novel and conserved microprotein involved in retinal lamination in zebrafish (cited 1x), establishing direct experimental evidence for developmental function. A 2025 GWAS study links SMIM36 variants to psoriasis treatment response with IL-17A pathway inhibitors. UniProt entry A0A1B0GVT2 exists for the 93-aa protein, with orthologs in mouse, rat, and birds indicating evolutionary conservation. InterPro scan failed, suggesting no conserved domains detectable by current methods—consistent with a lineage-specific microprotein. AlphaFold structure prediction shows medium confidence (global pLDDT 64.2), indicating a partially structured small membrane protein. STRING database reveals 10 protein interactions with notable partners including YARS1 (0.690, tyrosyl-tRNA synthetase), EPRS1 (0.650, glutamyl-prolyl-tRNA synthetase), and multiple SMIM family members (SMIM41: 0.677, SMIM38: 0.669), plus SCYGR5/7 (scavenger receptors), TMDD1, and EDDM13. This interaction network suggests potential roles in translation regulation or membrane trafficking. HPA reveals group-enriched expression with highest levels in retina (10.0 nTPM) and testis (2.6 nTPM), directly supporting the zebrafish retinal lamination findings and suggesting conserved function across vertebrates. ClinVar contains 3 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all copy number variations spanning 17q21-17q25 regions, though no specific monogenic disease is attributed solely to SMIM36; these CNVs are associated with 17q deletion/duplication syndromes featuring developmental delay, intellectual disability, and growth abnormalities. The retina-enriched expression, zebrafish retinal lamination phenotype, and conservation across vertebrates strongly suggest SMIM36 functions as a developmental microprotein essential for proper retinal layer formation, potentially through interactions with aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases (YARS1, EPRS1) that may regulate local protein synthesis in developing retinal neurons. The testis expression suggests additional roles in spermatogenesis. This represents a very dark gene with rare but high-quality functional evidence establishing a specific developmental role.
