## Description
DYNLT3 (Dynein Light Chain Tctex-Type 3) is a 116-amino acid X-linked dynein light chain protein with partial characterization (30 publications) but significant gaps in mechanistic understanding, particularly regarding its specific cargo recognition and disease etiology. The protein contains a canonical dynein light chain DLC-like domain (cd21463, residues 16-112) within the Tctex-1 family (PF03645, residues 18-110; IPR005334), forming a compact α-helical bundle structure predicted with very high confidence by AlphaFold (global pLDDT 91.1). This structural architecture is characteristic of dynein light chains that function as adaptor modules linking the dynein motor complex to diverse cellular cargoes.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals an exceptionally high-confidence network of dynein/dynactin complex components and ciliary trafficking regulators: DYNC1I2 (dynein intermediate chain 2, score 0.983), WDR60 (WD repeat protein 60, 0.978), DYNLL1 (dynein light chain 1, 0.976), DYNLRB2 (dynein light chain roadblock-type 2, 0.970), DYNC2LI1 (dynein-2 light intermediate chain 1, 0.965), DYNLL2 (0.962), DYNC1LI1 (0.881), DYNLRB1 (0.821), DYNC1I1 (0.760), and WDR34 (0.616). The strong WDR60 and WDR34 interactions specifically implicate DYNLT3 in intraflagellar transport (IFT) and ciliary function, as these WD-repeat proteins are essential for dynein-2 mediated retrograde transport in cilia.\n\nHuman Protein Atlas classifies DYNLT3 as a motor protein with ubiquitous expression (detected in all tissues, low tissue specificity) involved in cell cycle, cell division, mitosis, and transport. However, functional literature reveals tissue-specific roles: DYNLT3 regulates melanosome movement, distribution, acidity, and transfer to keratinocytes (critical for skin pigmentation), and functions as an oncogene/tumor suppressor depending on cancer context — promoting breast cancer proliferation via epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT) while inhibiting cervical cancer growth through Wnt pathway suppression. miR-1-3p targets DYNLT3 to suppress ovarian cancer metastasis.\n\nClinVar reports 150 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains and losses spanning chromosome Xp (the gene's locus), though no specific Mendelian disease is yet assigned. The X-linked location and high variant burden suggest potential roles in X-linked intellectual disability, male infertility (via sperm flagella defects), or ciliopathy syndromes that remain clinically unrecognized. The ubiquity of expression combined with specific melanosome and ciliary functions suggests DYNLT3 dosage sensitivity may cause pleiotropic phenotypes.\n\nWe hypothesize that DYNLT3 functions as a cargo-specific adaptor that recruits the dynein-2 motor complex to melanosomes and ciliary vesicles via interactions with WDR60/WDR34, regulating retrograde intraflagellar transport and pigment granule positioning. The high structural confidence (pLDDT 91.1) indicates evolutionarily conserved folding critical for dynein intermediate chain binding. X-linked dosage imbalance from CNVs may disrupt ciliary trafficking in neural development (causing intellectual disability) or sperm flagella assembly (causing male infertility), explaining the high pathogenic variant count without assigned diseases.
