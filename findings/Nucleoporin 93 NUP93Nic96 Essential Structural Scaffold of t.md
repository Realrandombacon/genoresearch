# Nucleoporin 93 (NUP93/Nic96): Essential Structural Scaffold of the Nuclear Pore Complex Inner Ring

**Confidence:** HIGH

## Description
NUP93 (also known as Nic96) encodes an 819 aa essential structural protein (UniProt Q8N1F7) that serves as the central scaffold of the nuclear pore complex (NPC) inner ring. The protein is defined by a conserved Nup93/Nic96 domain (IPR007231, PF04097) spanning residues 219-810, which mediates critical interactions within the NPC architecture. STRING interaction analysis places NUP93 at the absolute core of the nucleoporin network with ultra-high confidence scores (0.981-0.999) to nearly all major NPC subcomplexes: NUP98, NUP58, NUP188, NUP214, NUP155, NUP205, NUP85, NUP35, NUP107, and NUP54. This connectivity confirms its role as a linchpin holding the Y-complex and inner ring together. HPA data indicates ubiquitous expression across all tissues with localization to the nuclear envelope and nuclear pore, consistent with its housekeeping function in mRNA and protein translocation. The gene is flagged as essential and involved in cancer and developmental diseases. ClinVar lists 52 pathogenic variants including nonsense mutations (p.Glu136Ter, p.Arg802Ter, p.Arg578Ter), frameshifts (p.Cys487fs), and large copy number gains/losses, linking NUP93 dysfunction to steroid-resistant nephrotic syndrome and developmental delays. AlphaFold structure prediction yields a high-confidence model (global pLDDT 79.9), revealing a well-folded architecture composed of elongated alpha-helical bundles suitable for forming stable scaffolding interfaces. Mechanistically, NUP93 acts as the foundational assembly point for the NPC inner ring: it directly binds NUP188 and NUP205 to form the central scaffold, while simultaneously recruiting the NUP98-NUP62 channel complex via NUP58/NUP54 interactions. This arrangement creates the selective permeability barrier and transport channel. Disruption of NUP93 destabilizes the entire pore structure, leading to defective nucleocytoplasmic transport, accumulation of toxic nuclear/cytoplasmic aggregates, and cell death—explaining the severe phenotypes observed in patients with truncating variants.
