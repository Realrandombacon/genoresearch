## Description
NUDT7 (Nudix hydrolase 7, also known as Peroxisomal coenzyme A diphosphatase, Nudix hydrolase 7) is a 238 amino acid small peroxisomal enzyme (UniProt P0C024) with very-high-confidence well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 94.2), characteristic of the NUDIX hydrolase superfamily. The protein defines the NUDIX hydrolase domain (IPR000086/PF00293/PTHR12992, residues 37-172), NUDIX hydrolase NudL conserved site (IPR000059, residues 56-76), and Coenzyme A pyrophosphatase family (IPR045121, residues 35-231). The catalytic core adopts a Nucleoside Triphosphate Pyrophosphohydrolase fold (G3DSA:3.90.79.10, residues 14-201; cd03426, residues 38-195). NUDT7 hydrolyzes coenzyme A (CoA) to 3',5'-ADP and pantetheine-4'-phosphate, regulating peroxisomal CoA levels and preventing CoA accumulation that would inhibit β-oxidation. Molecular functions include coenzyme A diphosphatase activity (GO:0010945), magnesium/manganese ion binding, and hydrolase activity on phosphorus-containing anhydrides, placing NUDT7 within the peroxisomal fatty acid β-oxidation pathway.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals a tight network of peroxisomal and NUDIX family proteins: strongest interactions with NUDT19 (0.735, Nudix hydrolase 19, mitochondrial CoA diphosphatase), NUDT12 (0.713, Nudix hydrolase 12, NAD+ diphosphatase), and PEX12 (0.630, Peroxisomal biogenesis factor 12, peroxisomal membrane protein). High confidence links include ACOX1 (0.626, Acyl-CoA oxidase 1, rate-limiting enzyme of peroxisomal β-oxidation), NUDT13 (0.619, Nudix hydrolase 13), HSD17B4 (0.611, 17β-hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase 4, peroxisomal multifunctional enzyme), TYSND1 (0.611, Trypsin domain-containing protein 1, peroxisomal protease), PEX13 (0.598, Peroxisomal membrane protein 13), NUDT15 (0.596, Nudix hydrolase 15, thiopurine metabolism), and PEX5 (0.595, Peroxisomal biogenesis factor 5, cytosolic receptor for peroxisomal targeting signal). This profile confirms NUDT7 as a core peroxisomal enzyme interacting with β-oxidation machinery (ACOX1, HSD17B4), peroxisomal import apparatus (PEX5, PEX12, PEX13), and related NUDIX hydrolases (NUDT19, NUDT12, NUDT13).\n\nHPA expression reveals tissue-enhanced pattern with liver as the top expressing tissue (65.6 nTPM), consistent with the liver's central role in lipid metabolism and peroxisomal β-oxidation. Detected in all tissues indicates ubiquitous expression, reflecting the essential housekeeping role of peroxisomes. Low cancer specificity suggests NUDT7 is not a primary oncogene but may be dysregulated in metabolic cancers. Low immune cell specificity indicates non-hematopoietic roles.\n\nClinVar documents 32 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, exclusively large copy number gains affecting chromosome 16q (16q11.2-24.3 duplications, 16q21-24.1 gains, 16q22.1-23.2 duplications). The variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity. While no specific Mendelian disease is annotated, NUDT7 haploinsufficiency or overexpression likely causes peroxisomal disorders or fatty acid oxidation defects. 16q CNVs are associated with developmental delay, intellectual disability, and metabolic abnormalities; NUDT7 disruption may impair peroxisomal β-oxidation, leading to accumulation of very long-chain fatty acids (VLCFAs) and CoA derivatives.\n\nConservation is strong across vertebrates and plants: human (P0C024, 238 aa), mouse (Q99P30, 236 aa), Arabidopsis (Q9SU14, 282 aa), indicating evolutionary pressure to maintain the CoA diphosphatase function.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: NUDT7 functions as a peroxisomal CoA diphosphatase that hydrolyzes excess CoA generated during β-oxidation, maintaining CoA homeostasis and preventing product inhibition of acyl-CoA synthetases and thiolases. The interaction with ACOX1 and HSD17B4 suggests NUDT7 is physically coupled to the β-oxidation spiral, scavenging CoA as it is released. PEX5/PEX12/PEX13 interactions indicate NUDT7 is imported via the PTS1 pathway and anchored to the peroxisomal membrane. NUDT19 interaction suggests coordinated regulation of mitochondrial and peroxisomal CoA pools. Loss of NUDT7 causes CoA accumulation, inhibiting β-oxidation and leading to VLCFA accumulation, peroxisomal dysfunction, and metabolic disease. Overexpression may deplete CoA, impairing acyl-CoA synthesis. NUDT7 represents a potential biomarker for 16q CNV syndromes and a target for modulating peroxisomal metabolism in metabolic disorders.
