## Description
CCNYL1 (Cyclin Y Like 1) is a 359 amino acid intracellular regulatory protein (UniProt Q8N7R7) with a high-confidence folded structure (AlphaFold pLDDT 73.8) containing a canonical cyclin box domain (residues 186-282) and N-terminal cyclin domain (residues 180-285), functioning as a cyclin-dependent kinase regulatory subunit that modulates Wnt signaling, spermatogenesis, and neurogenesis through activation of CDK14/CDK16/CDK17 kinases at the plasma membrane.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals CCNYL1 contains a classic cyclin architecture: a cyclin box domain found in cyclin-Y family (cd20540, residues 186-282) that mediates CDK binding and activation, an N-terminal cyclin domain (IPR006671/PF00134/SM00385, residues 180-285/193-278) forming the characteristic cyclin fold, and a cyclin-like domain (IPR013763, residues 193-278). The protein belongs to the Cyclin Y family (IPR012399, residues 1-359) and cyclin-like superfamily (IPR036915/G3DSA:1.10.472.10/SSF47954, residues 191-285/129-331). An N-terminal aspartate-rich disordered region (PF27989, residues 1-93) may mediate membrane association or partner recruitment. GO annotations include protein kinase binding (GO:0019901) and regulation of cyclin-dependent protein serine/threonine kinase activity (GO:0000079). The CCNY partnership (0.702) confirms membership in the cyclin Y family. CDK14-CDK17-CDK18-CDK16 interactions (0.612/0.487/0.466/0.442) identify CCNYL1 as a regulatory subunit for PCTAIRE kinases, which phosphorylate substrates involved in Wnt signaling, neurite outgrowth, and spermatogenesis. METTL21A partnership suggests potential methylation regulation.\n\nHPA expression shows low tissue specificity with detection in all tissues, consistent with fundamental cell cycle and signaling functions. Subcellular localization at plasma membrane is distinctive for a cyclin, suggesting CCNYL1 activates CDKs at membrane-proximal signaling complexes rather than in the nucleus. Biological process annotations include differentiation, neurogenesis, and spermatogenesis, indicating specialized roles in developmental contexts. Classification as cyclin confirms regulatory function. Low cancer and immune cell specificity suggest housekeeping roles.\n\nClinVar documents 27 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large chromosome 2q copy number gains and losses (2q33.3-37.3, 2q32.3-34, 2q33.2-34, 2q32.1-36.1, 2q32.2-34, 2q33.1-34, 2q31.1-35) and one intronic deletion (c.432-11del). The cyclin box domain (residues 186-282) binds and activates CDKs through the characteristic cyclin-CDK interface.
