# ZNF765: KRAB-Zinc Finger Transcriptional Repressor with Disease-Associated Copy Number Variants

**Date:** 2026-03-23T06:21:38.571461

**Quality Score:** 1.85/10  (E=3.4, D=0.3) [POOR]

## Description
ZNF765 encodes a 523 amino acid Krüppel-associated box (KRAB) domain-containing zinc finger protein localized to the nucleus. The protein contains a canonical KRAB repression domain (IPR001909, PF01352, positions 8-81) that recruits KAP1/TRIM28 corepressors to silence target genes through histone deacetylation and H3K9 trimethylation. Three C2H2-type zinc fingers (IPR013087, PF00096, positions 215-321) provide sequence-specific DNA binding to GC-rich promoter elements. Human Protein Atlas shows ubiquitous expression across all tissues with low tissue specificity (nTPM data available in HPA), consistent with housekeeping transcriptional regulation roles. ClinVar documents 16 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly copy number gains (CNVs) spanning 19q13.33-13.43, suggesting dosage sensitivity where gene duplication disrupts transcriptional balance. The KRAB-ZFP architecture places ZNF765 within the largest family of mammalian transcriptional repressors, with evolutionary conservation of the dual-domain structure across vertebrates. Mechanistically, ZNF765 likely functions as a context-dependent silencer of proliferation or differentiation genes, with CNV-mediated overexpression potentially contributing to developmental disorders through aberrant target gene repression. The combination of repressor domain, DNA-binding specificity, ubiquitous expression, and dosage-sensitive pathogenic variants supports a model where ZNF765 maintains transcriptional homeostasis in dividing cells.
