## Description
NRBP2 (Nuclear Receptor-Binding Protein 2) encodes a 501 aa cytosolic pseudokinase (UniProt Q9NSY0) with opposing regulatory roles in L1 retrotransposition and neurogenesis. InterPro analysis reveals a catalytically inactive protein kinase architecture: IPR042697 (Nuclear receptor-binding protein 2 pseudokinase domain, aa 46-307), IPR000719 (Protein kinase domain, aa 38-306, GO:0004672/GO:0005524/GO:0006468), IPR011009 (Protein kinase-like domain superfamily, aa 54-316), IPR050588/PTHR13902 (With No Lysine (K) Ser/Thr Protein Kinase family, aa 18-493), PF00069 (Protein kinase domain, aa 85-304), PS50011 (Protein kinase domain profile, aa 38-306), cd14035 (MLF1-ADaptor Molecule-Like pseudokinase, aa 46-307), G3DSA:1.10.510.10 (Transferase/Phosphotransferase domain 1, aa 129-313), G3DSA:3.30.200.20 (Phosphorylase Kinase domain 1, aa 21-124), and SSF56112 (PK-like superfamily, aa 54-316). ClinVar contains 66 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number gains (8q24.13-24.3, 8q23.2-24.3), copy number losses (8q24.3), and splice site variants (c.605-4A>T, c.1077-4C>T, c.1382-45C>T), suggesting genomic instability at 8q24 contributes to disease. AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 79.2) indicates high confidence with well-folded pseudokinase domain. Literature (30 papers) documents opposing roles of NRBP1/NRBP2 paralogs in regulating L1 retrotransposition (2024-2025), cancer-associated fibroblast-mediated chemotherapeutic sensitivity in breast cancer via NRBP2 regulation, KLF4/MLL3 Compass Complex regulation of NRBP2 in acute myeloid leukemia elimination, and neurogenesis functions. FUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: NRBP2 functions as a catalytically inactive pseudokinase that regulates L1 retrotransposon activity through protein-protein interactions rather than phosphorylation, acting as a molecular scaffold or competitive inhibitor of NRBP1. The brain-enriched expression (95.2 nTPM) and neurogenesis annotation suggest NRBP2 maintains genomic stability in neural progenitor cells by suppressing L1 retrotransposition, which is critical for preventing insertional mutagenesis during neurodevelopment. The TSC22D family interactions (TSC22D1-4) indicate involvement in transcriptional repression and stress response pathways, potentially linking retrotransposon control to cellular stress signaling. In cancer contexts, NRBP2 expression in cancer-associated fibroblasts modulates tumor cell chemotherapeutic sensitivity, suggesting stromal-epithelial crosstalk functions. The 66 ClinVar variants including large copy number alterations at 8q24 suggest NRBP2 haploinsufficiency or dosage effects contribute to neurodevelopmental disorders and cancer predisposition through dysregulated retrotransposon control and neurogenesis defects.
