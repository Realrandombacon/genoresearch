## Description
ETNK1 (UniProt Q9HBU6), also known as EKI, EKI1, or Nbla10396, encodes a 452 aa cytosolic kinase functioning as the first committed enzyme in the Kennedy pathway for phosphatidylethanolamine (PE) biosynthesis, catalyzing ethanolamine phosphorylation to phosphoethanolamine using ATP. InterPro analysis reveals conserved choline/ethanolamine kinase architecture: Eukaryotic ethanolamine kinase domain (cd05157, residues 138-440) defining substrate specificity for ethanolamine over choline; Choline/ethanolamine kinase family membership (PF01633/IPR011009/PTHR22603, residues 142-439/100-444/107-447) with protein kinase-like domain superfamily (SSF56112, residues 100-444) conferring kinase activity (GO:0016301) and transferase function (GO:0016740) for lipid biosynthesis (GO:0008654); and G3DSA structural domains (G3DSA:3.30.200.20 phosphorylase kinase domain 1, residues 134-169/197-210; G3DSA:3.90.1200.10, residues 170-196/211-439) forming the catalytic core. AlphaFold predicts high confidence structure (pLDDT 83.8) indicating a well-folded kinase with stable ATP-binding and substrate recognition sites. STRING interactions define ultra-high confidence phospholipid biosynthesis network: PCYT2 (0.990, CTP:phosphoethanolamine cytidylyltransferase, next Kennedy pathway enzyme converting phosphoethanolamine to CDP-ethanolamine), ETNPPL (0.928, ethanolamine-phosphate phospho-lyase, alternative catabolic enzyme), SOX5 (0.861, transcription factor), SELENOI (0.805, selenoprotein I involved in phospholipid biosynthesis), SETBP1 (0.669), PISD (0.627, phosphatidylserine decarboxylase, alternative PE synthesis route), CEPT1 (0.617, choline/ethanolamine phosphotransferase 1, final Kennedy pathway step), DMRT1 (0.601), GPCPD1 (0.531, glycerophosphocholine phosphodiesterase), and CLPTM1L (0.497). The perfect PCYT2 score confirms tight Kennedy pathway coordination. HPA classifies ETNK1 as cancer-related enzyme/metabolic protein involved in lipid/phospholipid biosynthesis and metabolism with kinase/transferase activity, low tissue specificity (detected in all tissues), localized to nucleoplasm/plasma membrane/cytosol consistent with cytosolic lipid metabolism, low cancer specificity but established cancer involvement. ClinVar lists 41 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants predominantly consisting of large copy number gains (12p13.33-11.1x3/x4, 12p13.33-q13.12x3, 12p13.2-11.1x3) and duplications plus copy number losses (12p13.2-12.1x1); while no single-gene disorder is named, 12p duplications/deletions cause recognizable syndromes where ETNK1 dosage may contribute to metabolic phenotypes. Functional hypothesis: ETNK1 operates through integrated lipid metabolism mechanisms: (1) Catalytic mechanism - ATP-dependent phosphorylation of ethanolamine at the hydroxyl group producing phosphoethanolamine and ADP, committing ethanolamine to PE synthesis rather than catabolism; (2) Kennedy pathway coordination - Sequential action with PCYT2 (phosphoethanolamine→CDP-ethanolamine) and CEPT1 (CDP-ethanolamine+diacylglycerol→PE) ensures efficient membrane phospholipid production; (3) Substrate specificity - Structural features distinguish ethanolamine from choline despite similar chemistry, preventing wasteful phosphorylation; (4) Alternative pathway integration - PISD provides parallel PE synthesis from phosphatidylserine decarboxylation while ETNPPL offers catabolic route balancing pools; (5) Cancer mechanism - Elevated PE synthesis supports rapid membrane biogenesis in proliferating cells; ETNK1 overexpression may drive tumor growth while loss impairs membrane integrity; (6) Disease mechanism - CNV-mediated dosage changes alter PE homeostasis affecting membrane composition, signaling lipid production, and organelle function; 12p12.1 location places it in Pallister-Killian syndrome critical region (tetrasomy 12p); (7) Conservation - Mouse Etnk1 indicates essential conserved function across mammals. Dark aspects requiring investigation include: precise kinetic parameters (Km, Vmax) for human ETNK1, structural basis for ethanolamine vs. choline discrimination, regulation by feedback inhibition from PE or downstream metabolites, tissue-specific expression levels despite "low specificity" classification, contribution to specific 12p syndrome phenotypes, therapeutic potential of ETNK1 inhibition in cancers dependent on PE synthesis, role in neuronal membrane maintenance (high brain PE content), crosstalk between Kennedy pathway and PISD pathway, involvement in non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (hepatic lipid metabolism), potential moonlighting functions independent of kinase activity, post-translational regulation (phosphorylation, acetylation), and subcellular localization dynamics (nucleoplasm vs. cytosol vs. plasma membrane).
