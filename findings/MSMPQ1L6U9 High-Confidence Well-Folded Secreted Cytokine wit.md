## Description
MSMP (Microseminoprotein, prostate associated, also known as PSMP) is a 139 amino acid secreted cytokine localized to vesicles and cytosol (UniProt Q1L6U9) with high-confidence well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 77.9). The protein defines the beta-microseminoprotein family (IPR008735/PTHR10500, residues 19-116) and contains a characteristic PSP94 immunoglobulin-like beta-sandwich domain (PF05825/G3DSA:2.60.40.1900, residues 35-118, ~84 aa, ~60% of protein). This domain is stabilized by ten conserved cysteine residues forming five intra-molecular disulfide bonds, creating a rigid scaffold resistant to proteolysis in extracellular environments. Molecular functions include cytokine activity, chemotaxis, and inflammatory response, placing MSMP within immune modulation and cell migration pathways. The gene is implicated in prostate cancer tumorigenesis.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals a network linking MSMP to its paralog and chemokine signaling: strongest interaction with MSMB (0.777, high confidence, Beta-microseminoprotein, the canonical prostate secretory protein), indicating functional redundancy or heterodimerization. High confidence link to CCR2 (0.666, C-C motif chemokine receptor 2, a GPCR mediating monocyte chemotaxis) suggests MSMP functions as a chemokine-like ligand recruiting immune cells. Connections to GBA2 (0.440, Glucosylceramidase beta 2, involved in glycosphingolipid metabolism), SEC63 (0.422, Sec63 homolog, protein translocation across ER membrane), and ST6GALNAC5 (0.417, ST6 N-acetylgalactosaminide alpha-2,6-sialyltransferase 5, glycosylation enzyme) suggest roles in protein secretion, membrane trafficking, and post-translational modification.\n\nHPA expression reveals a tissue-enhanced pattern with highest levels in choroid plexus (3.1 nTPM), pituitary gland (1.3 nTPM), and placenta (1.1 nTPM). This unexpected distribution beyond prostate suggests broader physiological roles in cerebrospinal fluid production, neuroendocrine regulation, and maternal-fetal interface immunity. The 'predicted secreted protein' classification and 'cancer enhanced' status indicate dysregulation in tumors. Subcellular localization in vesicles confirms secretory pathway trafficking.\n\nClinVar documents 74 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains and losses affecting chromosome 9p: CNV gains (9p24.3-q21.13, 9p24.3-13.1, 9p24.3-q34.3 duplications) and CNV losses (9p13.3-13.1 deletions). The variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity. While no specific Mendelian disease is associated, 9p deletions cause 9p deletion syndrome (craniofacial dysmorphism, intellectual disability, cardiac defects). MSMP haploinsufficiency may contribute to immune dysregulation and impaired chemotaxis. In prostate cancer, MSMP overexpression promotes tumor cell migration, invasion, and metastasis via CCR2-mediated signaling.\n\nConservation is strong across mammals: human (Q1L6U9, 139 aa), mouse (B1AWI6, 139 aa), guinea pig (A0A286XI43, 139 aa), bushbaby (H0XUF8, 139 aa), naked mole rat (G5BKZ4, 139 aa), indicating evolutionary pressure to maintain the cysteine-rich PSP94 fold.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: MSMP functions as a secreted chemokine-like cytokine that modulates immune cell recruitment and inflammation. The rigid PSP94 beta-sandwich scaffold, stabilized by five disulfide bonds, presents specific surface epitopes for receptor binding (likely CCR2 or related GPCRs). In normal physiology, MSMP produced by choroid plexus may regulate leukocyte trafficking into the CNS, while placental MSMP modulates maternal immune tolerance. In prostate cancer, tumor-derived MSMP creates a chemotactic gradient recruiting CCR2+ monocytes/macrophages that promote tumor progression through cytokine release and matrix remodeling. Interaction with MSMB suggests potential heterodimerization expanding receptor specificity. The cancer-enhanced expression pattern makes MSMP a potential biomarker or therapeutic target. Disulfide bond integrity is critical for function; reduction would abolish activity. 9p CNVs disrupt MSMP dosage, altering chemokine homeostasis and contributing to 9p deletion syndrome phenotypes.
