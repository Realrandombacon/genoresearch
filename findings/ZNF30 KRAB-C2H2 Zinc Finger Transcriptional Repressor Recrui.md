# ZNF30: KRAB-C2H2 Zinc Finger Transcriptional Repressor Recruiting TRIM28/KAP1 Heterochromatin Machinery

**Quality Score:** 1.85/10  (E=2.7, D=1.0) [POOR]

## Description
ZNF30 encodes a 623 amino acid nuclear transcription factor (UniProt P17039) with a canonical KRAB-ZNF repressor architecture: an N-terminal KRAB domain (IPR001909/PF01352, residues 13-85) followed by multiple C2H2-type zinc finger arrays (IPR013087/PF00096, 10+ fingers spanning residues 111-619). The KRAB domain mediates heterochromatic gene silencing by recruiting TRIM28/KAP1 (STRING score 0.651), which scaffolds the SETDB1 histone methyltransferase complex and HP1α to deposit H3K9me3 marks at target promoters. HPA data shows tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in retina (14.4 nTPM) and brain (12.5 nTPM), consistent with neuronal transcriptional regulation. Subcellular localization is nucleoplasmic with cytosolic presence. ClinVar documents 18 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number losses and gains on chromosome 19q13.11, promoter variants (c.-11A>G), and intronic variants (c.257-549G>T), suggesting dosage sensitivity and regulatory disruption. The tandem C2H2 zinc finger array confers sequence-specific DNA recognition, with each finger contacting 3-4 base pairs in the major groove. ZNF30 likely functions as a sequence-specific transcriptional repressor silencing neuronal genes during development or maintaining heterochromatin boundaries in retinal photoreceptors. TRIM28 recruitment enables epigenetic silencing via histone methylation and DNA methylation crosstalk. Disruption of ZNF30 copy number or DNA-binding specificity could derepress target genes, causing neurodevelopmental or retinal pathology.
