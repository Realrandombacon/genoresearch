## Description
PAPOLG (Poly(A) Polymerase Gamma) is a 736-amino acid nuclear poly(A) polymerase (UniProt Q9BWT3) that represents a partially characterized dark gene with only 25 publications despite clear enzymatic function and emerging roles in oncology and virology. Structurally, PAPOLG contains a canonical nucleotidyltransferase catalytic domain (IPR048840/cd05402, residues 20-213) with the characteristic Dxh motif for ATP binding and poly(A) synthesis, a central poly(A) polymerase domain (IPR007012, residues 218-362), and an RNA-binding domain (IPR007010/PF04926, residues 365-430) for substrate recognition. AlphaFold predicts a confident structure (pLDDT 74.8) consistent with the canonical two-lobe nucleotidyltransferase fold. Functionally, PAPOLG operates within the mRNA 3'-end processing machinery: STRING interactions reveal tight association with canonical cleavage/polyadenylation factors including CPSF1 (0.961), FIP1L1 (0.958), WDR33 (0.943), and SNRNP70 (0.988), as well as related poly(A) polymerases MTPAP (0.941) and TUT1 (0.938). HPA confirms nucleoplasmic and nuclear body localization with ubiquitous expression across tissues. Despite 21 ClinVar pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants (including copy number gains/losses at 2p16.1 and splice variants c.1028-6A>T, c.*380G>T), no specific Mendelian disease has been defined—suggesting either embryonic lethality, functional redundancy with other poly(A) polymerases, or subclinical phenotypes. Critically, PAPOLG has emerged as an oncogenic driver: it is frequently amplified at 2p15-p16.1 in transformed follicular lymphoma (2019), drives hepatocellular carcinoma progression via the GSEC/miR-101-3p/SNX16/PAPOLG ceRNA axis (2022), shows differential expression in brain-metastatic breast cancer (2021), and contributes to SARS-CoV-2-induced inflammation by stabilizing NF-κB mRNA (2025). The mechanistic hypothesis: PAPOLG provides non-canonical polyadenylation activity that selectively stabilizes inflammatory and oncogenic transcripts (e.g., NF-κB pathway mRNAs) under stress conditions, distinguishing it from housekeeping poly(A) polymerases like PAPOLA/PAPOLB. This substrate selectivity, combined with its integration into the cleavage/polyadenylation complex, positions PAPOLG as a regulatory node linking RNA processing to immune activation and tumor progression.
