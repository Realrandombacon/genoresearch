## Description
LOC646736 is an uncharacterized non-coding genetic locus on chromosome 2q36.3 (NC_000002.12, positions 226142793-226180061, ~37 kb region) with moderate clinical significance despite lacking protein-coding features. No UniProt accession exists, preventing InterPro domain analysis and AlphaFold structure prediction. No mRNA transcript was identified in NCBI nucleotide databases. Human Protein Atlas could not resolve the gene to an Ensembl ID, indicating no detectable protein expression. STRING-DB detected no protein-protein interactions. However, ClinVar contains 7 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all representing large-scale copy number variations spanning the 2q35-37.3 region (variants include 4510139, 155149, 154206, 146683, 59159, 59158, 57419). These CNVs are predominantly copy number gains (trisomy) ranging from focal alterations (~5-10 Mb) to whole-arm changes (24+ Mb). The 2q36-37 chromosomal location is clinically notable as this region is associated with 2q37 deletion syndrome (characterized by brachydactyly, intellectual disability, short stature, and distinctive facial features) and Albright hereditary osteodystrophy-like phenotypes. The region contains multiple dosage-sensitive genes including HDAC4 (histone deacetylase 4, chromatin remodeling, brachydactyly mental retardation syndrome, haploinsufficient), PAX3 (paired box 3, neural crest development, Waardenburg syndrome), GLI2 (GLI family zinc finger 2, hedgehog signaling, holoprosencephaly), SATB2 (special AT-rich sequence binding protein 2, craniofacial development, cleft palate), and GNAS (guanine nucleotide binding protein alpha stimulating, pseudohypoparathyroidism). Functional hypothesis: LOC646736 likely represents a cis-regulatory element or chromatin boundary controlling expression of neighboring dosage-sensitive genes in the 2q36-37 critical region, particularly HDAC4 which is the primary driver of 2q37 deletion syndrome through histone deacetylation and neuronal differentiation regulation. The 7 pathogenic CNVs indicate dosage sensitivity where copy number alterations disrupt normal transcriptional regulation of chromatin modification and developmental genes. Disease mechanism likely involves triplosensitivity of HDAC4 (leading to altered bone development and cognitive function through defective histone deacetylation), dysregulation of PAX3/SATB2 (affecting neural crest and craniofacial development), or altered GLI2 expression affecting hedgehog signaling and forebrain development. The absence of protein-coding features combined with ClinVar evidence (7 variants) and neurodevelopmental/craniofacial disease association suggests this locus functions as a regulatory hub controlling skeletal and neurological development through long-range gene regulation.
