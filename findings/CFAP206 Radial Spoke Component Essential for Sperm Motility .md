## Description
CFAP206 (Cilia and Flagella Associated Protein 206, alias C6orf165, SPGF102) is a 622-amino acid structural component of motile cilia and flagella, specifically localized to the radial spoke apparatus of the axoneme. InterPro analysis confirms membership in the CFAP206 family (IPR021897, residues 5-620) containing a Domain of Unknown Function (PF12018/DUF3685, residues 215-491) that likely mediates protein-protein interactions within the ciliary scaffold. AlphaFold predicts high-confidence structure (pLDDT 85.4), indicating a well-folded protein with stable secondary and tertiary structure suitable for incorporation into the multi-protein axonemal complex.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals CFAP206 operates within a dedicated ciliogenesis network with highest confidence for CCDC113 (0.707), a coiled-coil domain protein involved in ciliary assembly; SMIM8 (0.551), a small integral membrane protein; EFCAB10 (0.530), an EF-hand calcium-binding protein suggesting calcium-dependent regulation of ciliary beating; and other radial spoke/axonemal components including CFAP61 (0.508), RSPH4A (0.495, radial spoke head protein 4A), DRC1 (0.445, dynein regulatory complex subunit 1), and CFAP58 (0.493). This interaction profile confirms CFAP206's role as part of the radial spoke-dynein regulatory complex interface that controls axonemal beat frequency and waveform.\n\nHuman Protein Atlas classifies CFAP206 as 'group enriched' with top expression in testis (55.2 nTPM) and fallopian tube (21.0 nTPM), consistent with its function in motile cilia of sperm flagella and oviduct epithelium. Cancer-enhanced expression suggests potential roles in tumor cell motility or cilia-dependent signaling pathways. ClinVar reports 20 pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants including truncating mutations (c.1430del/c.1430dup p.Asn477fs), missense variants (c.353A>G p.Asp118Gly), splice site mutations (c.192+4A>T), and large copy number losses/gains spanning 6q14.3-16.1, indicating dosage sensitivity and clear disease causality.\n\nLiterature analysis (15 papers) establishes CFAP206 as a FOXJ1 transcriptional target essential for sperm motility, mucociliary clearance of airways, and brain development. A landmark 2021 study demonstrated that bi-allelic truncating variants in CFAP206 cause male infertility in both human and mouse models, with affected individuals exhibiting asthenozoospermia (reduced sperm motility) due to axonemal defects. A 2020 study showed Cfap206 knockout mice display impaired mucociliary clearance leading to respiratory complications and hydrocephalus from defective ependymal cilia. A 2022 bivalve study using RNAi and CRISPR/Cas9 confirmed evolutionary conservation of CFAP206 function in ciliogenesis across metazoans.\n\nWe hypothesize that CFAP206 functions as a radial spoke scaffold protein that physically links the central pair microtubules to outer dynein arms, transmitting mechanical signals that regulate dynein activity and coordinate axonemal beating. The DUF3685 domain (residues 215-491) likely mediates specific interactions with RSPH4A and DRC1 to form the mechanochemical signaling hub. Calcium binding via EFCAB10 interaction (0.530) may provide feedback regulation of beat frequency in response to intracellular calcium fluctuations. In sperm, CFAP206 deficiency disrupts flagellar waveform generation, causing infertility. In airways, defective mucociliary clearance predisposes to chronic respiratory infections. In the brain, ependymal cilia dysfunction impairs cerebrospinal fluid flow, causing hydrocephalus. CFAP206 represents a clinically actionable target for male infertility diagnosis and a model for understanding radial spoke assembly and ciliary mechanotransduction.
