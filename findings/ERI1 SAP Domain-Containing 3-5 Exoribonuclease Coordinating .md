## Description
ERI1 encodes a 349 aa 3'-5' exoribonuclease 1 (UniProt Q8IV48) located at 8p23.1, functioning as a multifunctional RNA metabolism enzyme with critical roles in histone mRNA 3'-end processing, RNA interference, and GPI anchor biosynthesis. InterPro analysis reveals a bipartite domain architecture: N-terminal SAP domain (IPR003034/PF02037, aa 51-120) for nucleic acid binding and DNA/RNA recognition, and C-terminal DEDDh 3'-5' exonuclease domain (IPR047201/cd06133/PF00929, aa 130-310) within the ribonuclease H-like superfamily (IPR036397, aa 121-349) conferring 3'-5'-RNA exonuclease activity (GO:0000175). AlphaFold predicts high-confidence structure (pLDDT 85.2), indicating a well-folded enzyme with stable catalytic core suitable for substrate recognition. STRING interactions reveal dual functional networks: (1) RNA metabolism - SLBP (0.999, histone mRNA hairpin-binding protein), DICER1 (0.967, miRNA biogenesis), SYNCRIP (0.937, hnRNP Q), XRN1 (0.891, 5'-3' exoribonuclease), DEDD (0.882, related exonuclease), LSM11 (0.802, histone mRNA processing); (2) GPI anchor biosynthesis - PIGC (0.833), PIGA (0.804), PIGQ (0.785), essential for glycosylphosphatidylinositol anchor assembly. HPA shows ubiquitous expression (detected in all tissues) with low tissue specificity, vesicle and microtubule localization, consistent with housekeeping RNA processing functions. ClinVar reports 85 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including frameshifts (c.627dup p.Val210fs), nonsense mutations (c.949C>T p.Arg317Ter), missense variants (c.869G>A p.Gly290Glu), and 8p23 copy number losses, causing recessive phenotypic dichotomy: spondylo-epi-metaphyseal dysplasia (SEMD) Guo-Campeau type with skeletal abnormalities, dwarfism, and intellectual disability with distal limb anomalies. Literature (62 papers) details emerging mechanisms: (1) Congenital bone disorders from ERI1-mediated RNA metabolism dysfunction (0 citations, 2025); (2) Null and missense mutations causing recessive phenotypic dichotomy in humans (5 citations, 2023); (3) Influenza A virus co-opting ERI1 bound to histone mRNA for viral transcription (7 citations, 2020); (4) Established roles in 5.8S rRNA 3'-end maturation and histone mRNA turnover. Functional hypothesis: ERI1 likely functions through: (1) Histone mRNA 3'-end Processing - SAP domain recognizing histone mRNA stem-loop via SLBP interaction, exonuclease trimming 3' extensions for proper maturation; (2) RNAi Regulation - DEDDh domain degrading siRNAs to prevent off-target effects and maintain RNAi homeostasis; (3) GPI Anchor Biosynthesis - PIG complex interaction suggesting unexpected role in glycolipid anchor assembly, potentially linking RNA metabolism to membrane protein anchoring; (4) Viral Susceptibility - histone mRNA-bound ERI1 recruited by influenza for viral transcription, representing host-pathogen interface. The SAP-exonuclease architecture enables coordinated nucleic acid recognition and catalysis. Disease implications include: skeletal dysplasia (SEMD), neurodevelopmental disorders, potential viral susceptibility variants, GPI anchor deficiency syndromes. Despite 62 papers, ERI1's GPI biosynthesis role and tissue-specific functions remain underexplored.
