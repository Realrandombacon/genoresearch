## Description
CCDC92B (Coiled-coil domain containing 92B, UniProt A0A8I5KY20) is a 255 amino acid intracellular protein encoded on chromosome 17p13.3. InterPro analysis reveals a conserved coiled-coil architecture: an N-terminal coiled-coil domain of unknown function (IPR039496, PF14916, residues 7-62) and membership in the CCDC92/74/92 protein family (IPR040370, PTHR14882, residues 1-248), encompassing nearly the entire protein. This domain organization suggests oligomerization capability and scaffolding function typical of coiled-coil proteins. The gene is a paralog of CCDC92 and CCDC74 family members, indicating functional redundancy or specialization within this protein family.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis returned no results, indicating CCDC92B is not well-characterized in protein interaction databases. This absence may reflect its recent annotation, tissue-specific expression, or transient/weak interactions not captured in large-scale studies. Human Protein Atlas data classifies CCDC92B as "Group enriched" with expression detected in some tissues, showing peak expression in brain (4.6 nTPM) and retina (3.9 nTPM). It is not detected in immune cells but shows cell type enhancement in single-cell data, suggesting specialized neuronal or glial functions.\n\nClinVar records 25 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, comprising a MIXED pattern of both copy number gains (duplications, e.g., 17p13.3-12 ~11 Mb, 17p13.3 ~0.4-3 Mb) and losses (deletions, e.g., 17p13.3-13.2 ~4-6 Mb, 17p13.3 ~0.2-3 Mb, plus a focal intragenic deletion c.2688360_2784321del). This balanced variant spectrum indicates TRUE dosage sensitivity where both haploinsufficiency and overexpression are pathogenic. These CNVs are associated with 17p13.3 deletion/duplication syndromes, including Miller-Dieker syndrome (lissencephaly) and 17p13.3 microduplication syndrome, characterized by severe intellectual disability, neuronal migration defects, and dysmorphic features.\n\nThe genomic context is critical: 17p13.3 contains TP53 (tumor suppressor), PAFAH1B1/LIS1 (lissencephaly-1, neuronal migration), YWHAE (14-3-3 epsilon, neurodevelopmental), and the Miller-Dieker critical region. CCDC92B resides within this neurodevelopmentally essential locus.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: CCDC92B functions as a coiled-coil scaffolding protein that mediates protein complex assembly in neuronal tissues, potentially interacting with neuronal migration machinery (LIS1/PAFAH1B1 complex), cytoskeletal regulators, or synaptic scaffolding proteins. The brain and retina enrichment suggests roles in neurodevelopment, photoreceptor maintenance, or neuronal cytoskeleton organization. Coiled-coil domains typically mediate homooligomerization or heterooligomerization with partner coiled-coil proteins, creating higher-order scaffolds that concentrate signaling complexes. The mixed CNV pattern indicates CCDC92B operates within a narrow dosage range: haploinsufficiency disrupts scaffold integrity, causing mislocalization of neuronal migration or synaptic proteins, while overexpression may saturate binding partners or form aberrant aggregates. The 17p13.3 location within the Miller-Dieker critical region suggests CCDC92B may contribute to lissencephaly phenotypes when deleted, potentially acting as a modifier of LIS1 function. The absence of STRING partners may reflect brain-specific interactions not captured in general databases. Future studies should test interactions with LIS1, 14-3-3 proteins, or neuronal coiled-coil scaffolds.
