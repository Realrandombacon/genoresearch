## Description
SLC25A16 (P16260, 332 aa), also known as the Mitochondrial Coenzyme A Transporter, is a critical inner mitochondrial membrane protein responsible for importing free Coenzyme A (CoA) and potentially specific acyl-CoA species into the mitochondrial matrix, thereby fueling the TCA cycle and fatty acid oxidation. InterPro identifies a canonical mitochondrial carrier architecture with three repeated solute carrier domains (IPR002067/PF00153, aa 35-124, 131-216, 240-330) forming the transmembrane pore, belonging to the Solute Carrier Family 25 (IPR018108, PTHR24089). AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (global pLDDT 83.8, residues 1-332), revealing a compact bundle of six transmembrane helices characteristic of the mitochondrial carrier fold, optimized for the alternating access mechanism of anion transport. HPA classifies it as a predicted mitochondrial membrane protein with low tissue specificity but detected in all tissues; notably, it is 'cancer enhanced,' suggesting upregulation in malignancies to support heightened metabolic demand. STRING reveals 10 interactions defining its metabolic and regulatory network: RPL18 (0.909, ribosomal protein linking translation to metabolism), FAU (0.821, ubiquitin-like protein), TRIM13 (0.791, E3 ligase), MTCH2 (0.786) and MTCH1 (0.781) (mitochondrial carrier homologs involved in apoptosis and lipid metabolism), TMEM192 (0.630, lysosomal membrane protein), and immune checkpoints CD274 (0.576, PD-L1), PDCD1 (0.574, PD-1), and PAEP (0.576). The unexpected links to PD-L1 and PD-1 suggest a role in modulating tumor immunity via metabolic reprogramming. ClinVar contains 19 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including missense mutations (e.g., c.92G>T p.Arg31Leu) and large copy number gains/losses on chromosome 10q; homozygous missense mutations cause autosomal recessive isolated fingernail dysplasia, highlighting a unique tissue-specific vulnerability to CoA deficiency. Functional hypothesis: SLC25A16 acts as the primary gatekeeper for cytosolic CoA entry into mitochondria. Since CoA cannot be synthesized within the matrix, this transport is rate-limiting for acetyl-CoA formation, citrate synthesis, and energy production. In nails, which have high keratin synthesis demands requiring precise lipid and sulfur metabolism, partial loss of function disrupts matrix formation leading to dysplasia. In cancer, overexpression likely drives the 'Warburg effect' reversal or supports oxidative phosphorylation in aggressive tumors, while simultaneously interacting with immune checkpoint pathways (PD-1/PD-L1) to create an immunosuppressive microenvironment. The interaction with MTCH2 suggests a secondary role in regulating mitochondrial outer membrane permeability and apoptosis. Conservation across vertebrates confirms its essential evolutionary role in cellular bioenergetics.
