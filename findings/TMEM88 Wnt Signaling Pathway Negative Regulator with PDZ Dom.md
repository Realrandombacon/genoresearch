# TMEM88: Wnt Signaling Pathway Negative Regulator with PDZ Domain Binding and Cardiac Expression

**Date:** 2026-03-23T04:14:36.463273

**Quality Score:** 3.65/10  (E=4.8, D=2.5) [MODERATE]

## Description
TMEM88 encodes a 159-amino acid single-pass transmembrane protein (UniProt Q6PEY1) that functions as a negative regulator of the canonical Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway. The protein contains a conserved TMEM88 domain (PF27917, IPR060563, residues 42-129) embedded within the larger TMEM88 family signature (IPR033355, residues 14-159), suggesting a specialized structural fold for membrane protein-protein interactions. HPA expression profiling reveals tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in heart muscle (97.8 nTPM), and subcellular localization to plasma membrane and cytosol, consistent with its role as a transmembrane signaling modulator. STRING interaction network identifies high-confidence binding to DVL1 (Dishevelled-1, score 0.893), a critical cytoplasmic phosphoprotein that transduces Wnt signals from Frizzled receptors to downstream effectors. Additional medium-confidence interactions include KDM6B (histone demethylase, 0.584), TMEM45A (0.541), and TMEM94 (0.528), suggesting potential roles in chromatin regulation and membrane complex assembly. The gene enables PDZ domain binding activity per Gene Ontology annotation, likely mediating direct interaction with the PDZ domains of DVL1 to sequester or inhibit Dishevelled function. ClinVar documents 22 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains and deletions spanning 17p13.1, indicating dosage sensitivity where altered TMEM88 expression may contribute to developmental disorders or cardiomyopathy given the cardiac-enriched expression pattern. Mechanistically, TMEM88 likely acts as a membrane-tethered inhibitor that binds DVL1 via PDZ interactions, preventing DVL1 polymerization or membrane recruitment required for Wnt signalosome assembly, thereby dampening β-catenin stabilization and TCF/LEF transcriptional activation. The specific structural determinants of DVL1 binding, regulatory post-translational modifications, and tissue-specific Wnt pathway contexts remain uncharacterized.
