## Description
LRRC4 (Leucine-rich repeat-containing protein 4, UniProt Q9HBW1), also known as NGL-2 or Netrin-G2 ligand, is a 653 amino acid transmembrane protein encoded on chromosome 7q32.1. It plays a critical role in the modulation of chemical synaptic transmission and synapse organization, specifically within excitatory synapses and dendritic spines. InterPro analysis reveals a canonical domain architecture for synaptic adhesion molecules: an N-terminal Leucine-Rich Repeat (LRR) domain (IPR001611, IPR003591, residues ~45-300) responsible for protein-protein interactions, flanked by cysteine-rich regions (IPR000483), followed by a C-terminal Immunoglobulin I-set domain (IPR013098, residues ~355-441) that mediates homophilic or heterophilic binding. The AlphaFold predicted structure confirms this modular arrangement with high confidence (global pLDDT 76.5), indicating a stable, well-folded extracellular region poised for specific receptor engagement.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places LRRC4 at the heart of a synaptic organizing network, with extremely high-confidence links to its cognate ligands NTNG2 (Netrin-G2, score 0.999) and NTNG1 (Netrin-G1, score 0.924). It also interacts strongly with postsynaptic scaffolding proteins like DLG4 (PSD-95, score 0.699) and signaling molecules such as PTPRS (score 0.643) and ADGRL2/3 (Latrophilins). This network underscores its function as a bridge between presynaptic netrin-Gs and the postsynaptic density, facilitating synapse formation and maintenance. Human Protein Atlas data confirms its brain-specific function, showing "Tissue enhanced" expression with a peak in the brain (28.6 nTPM), and subcellular localization to the Golgi apparatus (consistent with secretory pathway trafficking) and plasma membrane.\n\nClinVar records 24 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large-scale copy number losses (deletions spanning 7q31-36, e.g., ~27 Mb, ~17 Mb) and gains (duplications spanning 7q22-36, e.g., ~50 Mb, whole arm). These CNVs are associated with 7q deletion/duplication syndromes, characterized by severe intellectual disability, autism spectrum disorders, language delays, and distinctive facial features. The loss of LRRC4 likely contributes significantly to the synaptic deficits observed in these patients.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: LRRC4 functions as a critical postsynaptic adhesion molecule that binds presynaptic Netrin-G2 (NTNG2) to specify excitatory synapse formation in distinct neuronal circuits. Upon binding, LRRC4 recruits the PSD-95 (DLG4) complex via its cytoplasmic tail (potentially through PDZ-domain interactions or adaptors), anchoring glutamate receptors and organizing the postsynaptic density. This interaction triggers downstream signaling cascades involving MAPK1/3 to stabilize synaptic connections. Haploinsufficiency of LRRC4 (via 7q deletions) disrupts this specific synaptic wiring, leading to a reduction in excitatory synapse density or function, which manifests as the cognitive and behavioral phenotypes of 7q syndrome. Its high structural confidence and specific interaction profile make it a prime candidate for therapeutic targeting in neurodevelopmental disorders.
