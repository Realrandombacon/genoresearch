## Description
TMEM44 (Gene ID: 55576, UniProt Q2T9K0) is a 475 amino acid transmembrane protein located on chromosome 3q29 (NC_000003.12). InterPro domain analysis reveals membership in the Lysosomal/Vacuolar Amino Acid Transporter 1 family (IPR051415/PTHR16201, positions 5-339), strongly suggesting TMEM44 functions as an amino acid permease facilitating nutrient transport across lysosomal membranes. This family includes proteins critical for mTORC1 signaling by exporting amino acids from lysosomes to the cytosol. AlphaFold structure prediction (AF-Q2T9K0-F1) yields a medium-confidence model (global pLDDT 59.1), consistent with a multi-pass transmembrane topology typical of solute carriers, with structured transmembrane helices connected by flexible loops. Human Protein Atlas classifies TMEM44 as a predicted intracellular and membrane protein localized to the cytosol (likely reflecting cytosolic domains of the transmembrane protein), with expression detected in many tissues showing low tissue and cancer specificity; notably, it is group-enriched in blood cells and cell type-enhanced at single-cell resolution, suggesting specialized roles in hematopoietic lineages. STRING database identifies 10 interaction partners revealing connections to membrane trafficking and signaling: PPP1R18 (score 0.518, protein phosphatase 1 regulator linking to cytoskeletal dynamics), FAM43A (0.509) and FAM86B1 (0.484) are uncharacterized membrane-associated proteins, ATP13A3 (0.480, P5B-type ATPase transporting polyamines across endo-lysosomal membranes), ANO7 (0.466, anoctamin calcium-activated chloride channel implicated in prostate cancer), CALHM2 (0.416, calcium homeostasis modulator forming ion channels), C1orf226 (0.416), MCTP1 (0.415, multiple C2 domain transmembrane protein), SNRNP48 (0.413, spliceosomal component), and LSG1 (0.406, large subunit GTPase involved in ribosome biogenesis). The ATP13A3 interaction is particularly informative—both proteins localize to endo-lysosomal compartments and may coordinate polyamine and amino acid transport. ClinVar lists 38 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all large copy number gains or losses spanning 3q26-29 (multi-megabase regions affecting many genes); no specific monogenic disease is yet attributed solely to TMEM44, but 3q29 deletion/duplication syndromes cause neurodevelopmental disorders, autism, and schizophrenia. Conservation is evident across vertebrates (teleost fish Astatotilapia calliptera A0A3P8N7G2). Functional hypothesis: TMEM44 functions as a lysosomal amino acid exporter, releasing essential amino acids (potentially leucine, arginine, or glutamine) from lysosomal lumen to cytosol following autophagic degradation or endocytic uptake. This transport activity directly regulates mTORC1 activation on the lysosomal surface, coupling nutrient availability to cell growth and proliferation. In blood cells, TMEM44 may support rapid proliferation during immune responses or erythropoiesis. The interaction with ATP13A3 suggests coordinated regulation of lysosomal metabolite pools. Disease mechanism: Haploinsufficiency within 3q29 deletions may impair lysosomal amino acid efflux, disrupting mTORC1 signaling and contributing to neurodevelopmental phenotypes through defective neuronal growth and synaptic plasticity. In blood cells, reduced TMEM44 may impair proliferation. Priority experiments include lysosomal localization validation, radiolabeled amino acid transport assays in proteoliposomes, assessment of mTORC1 activity upon TMEM44 knockout, and investigation of TMEM44 variants in 3q29 syndrome patients.
