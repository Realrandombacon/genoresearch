## Description
TBC1D3I (TBC1 domain family member 3I, UniProt A0A087WXS9) is a 549-amino acid intracellular protein located on chromosome 17q12 that functions as a Rab GTPase-activating protein (Rab-GAP). InterPro analysis reveals canonical TBC domain architecture: central Rab-GAP-TBC domain (IPR000195/SM00164/PF00566/PS50086, aa 98-316/105-312/101-293) with characteristic tre-2/bub2/cdc16 fold (G3DSA:1.10.10.750/G3DSA:1.10.472.80/G3DSA:1.10.8.270/SSF47923, aa 76-115/224-356/116-223/74-253) that forms the catalytic core for accelerating GTP hydrolysis on Rab GTPases. GO annotations confirm GTPase activation molecular function. AlphaFold structure prediction achieves medium confidence (pLDDT 58.9), indicating a partially structured protein with mixed ordered catalytic domains and disordered regulatory regions — architecture consistent with Rab-GAPs where the TBC domain core maintains rigid geometry for substrate binding while flanking regions provide flexibility for membrane recruitment and partner regulation. Human Protein Atlas shows tissue-enriched expression restricted to testis (6.6 nTPM), detected in some tissues, not detected in cancers or immune cells, vesicles subcellular localization, predicted intracellular and membrane protein — suggesting specialized roles in spermatogenesis where vesicle trafficking drives acrosome biogenesis, sperm maturation, and flagellar assembly. STRING interaction network reveals no known interactors — absence of detected partners reflects either technical challenges capturing transient enzyme-substrate interactions typical of GAPs, genuine functional isolation as a lineage-specific paralog, or that TBC1D3I operates in testis-specific complexes not yet mapped. ClinVar contains 10 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all copy number variations (gains and losses) spanning the 17q12 region — these large CNVs encompass multiple genes including HNF1B and are associated with 17q12 deletion/duplication syndrome featuring renal cysts, diabetes, and neurodevelopmental disorders, though TBC1D3I's individual contribution remains undefined. Conservation across primates indicates recent evolutionary expansion of the TBC1D3 family (TBC1D3A-I are primate-specific paralogs), suggesting neofunctionalization for species-specific vesicle trafficking regulation. TBC1D3I likely functions as a Rab-GAP that binds specific Rab GTPases through its TBC domain, inserting an arginine finger into the Rab active site to stabilize the transition state and accelerate GTP hydrolysis by 10^5-fold, thereby switching Rabs from active GTP-bound to inactive GDP-bound states and terminating vesicle docking/fusion events — critical for coordinating membrane trafficking steps in developing spermatids where precise spatiotemporal control of vesicle transport determines acrosome formation and sperm differentiation, with copy number sensitivity suggesting dosage-dependent functions where altered expression disrupts vesicle homeostasis contributing to 17q12 syndrome phenotypes.
