## Description
BROX (BRO1 Domain and CAAX Motif Containing) encodes a 411 amino acid cytoplasmic/nuclear protein (UniProt Q5VW32) functioning as an ESCRT (Endosomal Sorting Complex Required for Transport) pathway component that bridges ESCRT-III subunits to the VPS4 ATPase machinery for membrane remodeling events including multivesicular body formation, nuclear envelope repair, cytokinesis, and autophagosome closure. Despite 2812 total papers in literature search (largely contaminated by computer vision 'box' detection papers), the gene-specific literature remains limited, establishing BROX as a partially characterized dark gene with emerging roles in developmental disorders linked to 1q41 deletion syndrome. Recent discoveries implicate BROX in nuclear envelope integrity maintenance, chromatin organization, and membrane repair pathways critical for cell division and neuronal development.\n\nSTRUCTURAL FEATURES: AlphaFold predicts a very high confidence structure (pLDDT 92.1) across all 411 residues, indicating an exceptionally well-folded stable protein optimized for protein-protein interactions within the ESCRT machinery. BROX haploinsufficiency likely contributes to neurodevelopmental phenotypes through impaired nuclear envelope repair in dividing neural progenitors.\n\nFUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: BROX functions through coordinated mechanisms: (1) ESCRT-III recruitment—BRO1 domain (residues 4-411, pLDDT 92.1) binds CHMP4A/B/C (0.825/0.736/0.731) via conserved hydrophobic pockets, stabilizing ESCRT-III filaments on endosomal membranes for ILV formation. (5) Autophagosome closure—ESCRT-III recruitment enables autophagosome sealing, critical for autophagic flux and cellular homeostasis. (6) Developmental neurobiology—1q41 deletion syndrome phenotypes (developmental delay, corpus callosum agenesis) suggest BROX haploinsufficiency impairs neural progenitor division via defective nuclear envelope repair and cytokinesis. (7) Disease mechanism—splice-site mutation (c.102-173G>C) would cause exon skipping producing truncated protein lacking CHMP4-binding surface; CNV losses cause haploinsufficiency reducing ESCRT capacity; CNV gains may cause dominant-negative effects or dosage imbalance. Mechanistically, BROX loss would destabilize ESCRT-III filaments reducing membrane scission efficiency, impair nuclear envelope repair causing DNA damage and genomic instability, block cytokinesis causing binucleation, disrupt autophagosome closure impairing proteostasis, and cause neurodevelopmental defects via mitotic errors in neural progenitors. The very high confidence structure (pLDDT 92.1) enables rational design of CHMP4-binding interface mutants to dissect ESCRT-III versus VPS4 recruitment functions, and development of small molecules stabilizing BROX-CHMP4 interactions for treating 1q41 deletion syndrome and related ESCRTopathies.
