## Description
TM9SF3 (Transmembrane 9 Superfamily Member 3, UniProt Q9HD45) is a 589 amino acid Golgi-resident transmembrane protein functioning as an essential receptor for Golgi-selective autophagy (Golgiphagy). With 24 literature references (partially characterized dark gene), TM9SF3 has emerged in 2025 as a critical quality control factor safeguarding Golgi integrity and glycosylation fidelity. The protein contains a Nonaspanin/TM9SF family domain (IPR004240, aa 29-589) and Endomembrane protein 70 domain (PF02990, aa 57-545), characteristic of 9-transmembrane proteins localized to endomembrane compartments. AlphaFold predicts a well-folded structure (pLDDT 86.6, high confidence) across all 589 residues, indicating stable transmembrane architecture. STRING interactions reveal partnerships with Golgi/vesicle trafficking proteins: TMED7 (0.600 - transmembrane emp24 domain-containing protein 7), ZFPL1 (0.500 - zinc finger PLAP-like 1), TMED4 (0.490), TMEM41B (0.461 - ER membrane protein), SPATA46 (0.440), HSPA13 (0.433), FBXO8 (0.426), SSMEM1 (0.420), PGAP2 (0.419 - GPI anchor remodeling), and GRAMD1C (0.416). HPA confirms Golgi apparatus and vesicle localization with ubiquitous expression across all tissues. ClinVar documents 19 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variants (gains/losses across 10q regions), though specific TM9SF3-associated diseases require further characterization. Landmark 2025 studies established TM9SF3 as: (1) ATG8-binding Golgiphagy receptor essential for sequestering Golgi fragments into autophagosomes during stress, (2) Guardian of glycosylation fidelity by removing damaged Golgi cisternae, (3) Mediator of PI(4,5)P2 phospholipid flop in response to extracellular acidic pH, enabling cellular adaptation to acidosis. Earlier 2017 work showed miR-1193 suppresses T-cell leukemia proliferation by directly targeting TM9SF3. Functional hypothesis: TM9SF3 functions as a Golgi-resident selective autophagy receptor that binds ATG8-family proteins (LC3/GABARAP) via a putative LIR (LC3-interacting region) motif, recruiting damaged Golgi membranes to forming phagophores for lysosomal degradation. The 9-transmembrane topology positions TM9SF3 as an integral membrane scaffold that may oligomerize to cluster ATG8 and cargo. The TMED/ZFPL1 interactions suggest coordination with vesicle coat machinery. The 2025 PI(4,5)P2 flop discovery reveals an unexpected lipid transport function, potentially linking Golgiphagy to plasma membrane lipid homeostasis during acidosis. Disease mechanism: TM9SF3 dysfunction may cause Golgi fragmentation, glycosylation defects, and impaired stress adaptation, contributing to cancer (T-cell leukemia) and potentially neurodegenerative disorders where Golgi quality control is critical.
