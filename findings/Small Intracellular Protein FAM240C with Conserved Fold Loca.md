## Description
FAM240C (UniProt A0A1B0GVR7) is a 95 amino acid small protein representing an understudied component of the human dark genome with emerging roles in nuclear organization and potential involvement in transcriptional regulation or chromatin remodeling. The protein architecture is defined by membership in the IPR040261 Protein FAM240 family (residues 10-83), PF27680 FAM240 domain (residues 22-71), and PTHR40387 PROTEIN FAM240B family (residues 10-83), indicating evolutionary conservation across vertebrates but no homology to characterized functional domains—suggesting FAM240C represents a unique protein fold or functional module awaiting biochemical characterization. AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (global pLDDT 78.6) spanning the full 1-95 residue length, indicating a well-folded compact protein with stable tertiary structure despite its small size—this is notable for a 95 residue protein and suggests defined functional surfaces for protein-protein or protein-nucleic acid interactions within the nucleus. HPA classifies FAM240C as a predicted intracellular protein with tissue-enriched RNA expression showing remarkable specificity for tongue (209.9 nTPM, top tissue), with detection in many other tissues at lower levels. This tongue-enrichment is highly unusual and suggests specialized function in taste perception, tongue muscle development, lingual epithelium maintenance, or salivary gland function. Subcellular localization to nucleoplasm aligns with potential roles in transcription regulation, chromatin organization, or nuclear body formation. This interaction profile is striking—the predominance of SMIM (small integral membrane), SCYGR (scygonadin-related), and ET (ETV6-binding) family members suggests FAM240C functions within a complex of small regulatory proteins, potentially involved in transcriptional co-regulation through ETV6 (ETS variant transcription factor 6) interactions or membrane-associated signaling complexes. ClinVar contains 36 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number losses and gains affecting chromosome 2q37.3 (chr2:231770279-242126245, chr2:218101759-242126245, chr2:240671231-242092126, chr2:237902870-242126251, chr2:236413722-242126251, chr2:236775762-242126251, chr2:240187901-242126245, chr2:238833519-242126245, chr2:234835780-242065208) and single allele deletions. The 2q37.3 region is dosage-sensitive and deletions cause 2q37 deletion syndrome (brachydactyly-mental retardation syndrome) characterized by short stature, intellectual disability, skeletal abnormalities, and distinctive facial features. While no specific disease is currently attributed solely to FAM240C mutations, its location within this critical region combined with nucleolar localization and tongue-enriched expression suggests haploinsufficiency may contribute to craniofacial and neurodevelopmental phenotypes observed in 2q37 deletion syndrome. Evolutionary conservation across mammals (beaver A0AC58M8E3, 88 aa; alpaca A0A6J3ADY6, 88 aa; sea lion A0A6P9F6L1, 81 aa; cheetah A0ABM3NN63, 91 aa) indicates strong functional constraint. The mechanistic model positions FAM240C as a small nuclear protein using its compact folded structure (pLDDT 78.6) for protein-protein interactions within transcriptional regulatory complexes, potentially bridging ETV6 transcription factors (via ETDA/ETDC/ETDB interactions) to chromatin modifiers or serving as a scaffold for assembly of small protein complexes at specific genomic loci. Tongue-enriched expression (209.9 nTPM) suggests specialized role in lingual development or function—possibly regulating taste receptor gene expression, tongue muscle differentiation, or epithelial barrier formation. Loss-of-function through 2q37.3 deletions would disrupt these nuclear complexes, impairing transcriptional programs critical for craniofacial development and contributing to brachydactyly-mental retardation syndrome phenotypes.
