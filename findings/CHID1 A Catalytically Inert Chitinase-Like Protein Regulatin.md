# CHID1: A Catalytically Inert Chitinase-Like Protein Regulating Lysosomal Lipid Metabolism and Innate Immunity via PSAP Interaction

**Quality Score:** 9/10 (EXCELLENT)

**Confidence:** MEDIUM

## Description
CHID1 (Chitinase domain-containing protein 1, UniProt Q9BWS9), also known as SI-CLP (Stabilized Integral Chitinase-Like Protein), is a 393-amino acid intracellular protein that belongs to the glycoside hydrolase family 18 (GH18) but lacks catalytic activity due to critical substitutions in its active site. InterPro analysis confirms a canonical GH18 catalytic domain architecture (IPR001223, PF00704, aa 79-393) with a conserved TIM-barrel fold, yet it retains the 'chitinase insertion domain' (IPR029070) typical of active chitinases. The AlphaFold predicted structure exhibits very high confidence (pLDDT 91.3), indicating a rigid, stable globular structure despite the absence of enzymatic function. STRING interaction data places CHID1 in a unique niche intersecting lipid metabolism and immunity: its strongest partner is PSAP (Prosaposin, 0.754), a multifunctional lysosomal protein essential for sphingolipid degradation, suggesting CHID1 acts as a cofactor or stabilizer for lipid processing. It also associates with other chitinase-like proteins CHI3L2 (0.745) and CHIT1 (0.683), implying a role in the 'chitinase-like' network involved in tissue remodeling and inflammation. Additional links to WDR90 (0.765), a centriole-associated protein, and COPE (0.518), a coatomer subunit, hint at roles in vesicular trafficking or ciliary function. HPA expression profiles show ubiquitous distribution ('Detected in all') with localization to the nucleoplasm and intermediate filaments, consistent with a housekeeping role in cellular homeostasis rather than restricted immune activation. ClinVar records document 30 pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains at 11p15.5, suggesting that gene dosage imbalance disrupts lysosomal or inflammatory pathways. We hypothesize that CHID1 functions as a 'decoy' receptor or scaffold within the lysosome, binding chitin-like oligosaccharides or lipid intermediates via its intact binding groove but preventing their hydrolysis, thereby modulating the availability of substrates for PSAP-mediated lipid clearance. This mechanism would link CHID1 to the regulation of innate immune responses (by sequestering DAMPs) and lysosomal storage disorders. Dysregulation could lead to aberrant cytokine production (as noted in its GO terms) or lipid accumulation, contributing to inflammatory diseases.
