## Description
ATP5MJ encodes a 58-amino acid mitochondrial proteolipid subunit j of the F(0) membrane sector of ATP synthase (Complex V), the terminal enzyme of oxidative phosphorylation. This VERY DARK gene has only 3 literature references despite clear clinical significance. InterPro analysis reveals membership in the mitochondrial proteolipid family (IPR012574, PF08039, PTHR15233) spanning residues 1-57. AlphaFold predicts a well-folded structure with high confidence (pLDDT 86.5), consistent with its role as an integral membrane protein. Human Protein Atlas shows ubiquitous expression across all tissues with low tissue specificity, localized to mitochondria and nucleoli fibrillar centers—dual localization suggesting potential roles beyond canonical OXPHOS function. Critically, ClinVar documents 57 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly copy number losses and gains at 14q32, indicating dosage sensitivity. Recent literature (2024) links Complex V alterations to cardiac dysfunction in human heart failure, suggesting ATP5MJ variants may contribute to mitochondrial cardiomyopathy. The 2025 selenoproteome study and 2026 TNBC chemotherapy response biomarker validation indicate emerging relevance in redox biology and cancer treatment response. Functional hypothesis: ATP5MJ stabilizes the F(0) proton channel assembly, and its loss disrupts proton motive force coupling to ATP synthesis, causing bioenergetic failure in high-energy tissues (heart, muscle, brain). Mechanistic proposal: As a small transmembrane proteolipid, ATP5MJ likely interacts with ATP5MG (subunit g), ATP5ME (subunit e), and ATP5MK (subunit k) to form the F(0) rotor-stator interface; pathogenic CNVs cause haploinsufficiency or triplosensitivity disrupting Complex V holoenzyme assembly.
