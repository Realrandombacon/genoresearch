# FAM13A: Cytosolic RhoGAP Regulating Small GTPase Signaling in Lung Epithelial Integrity and COPD Susceptibility

**Date:** 2026-03-19T10:48:31.389354

**Quality Score:** 3.75/10  (E=7.2, D=0.3) [MODERATE]

## Description
FAM13A (family with sequence similarity 13 member A, alias ARHGAP48, FAM13A1) encodes a 1023-amino acid cytosolic protein belonging to the FAM13 family (IPR039102/PTHR15904, residues 21-1023) that functions as a Rho GTPase-activating protein (RhoGAP) regulating small GTPase-mediated signal transduction. The protein contains an N-terminal RhoGAP catalytic domain (IPR000198/PF00620/PS50238/SM00324, residues 43-231) characteristic of GTPase acceleration machinery, with the catalytic arginine finger motif essential for stimulating GTP hydrolysis in Rho family GTPases (RhoA, Rac1, Cdc42). A C-terminal FAM13A-like domain (IPR059029/PF26116, residues 947-1016) may mediate protein-protein interactions or regulatory functions. AlphaFold predicts medium confidence structure (pLDDT=60.4), indicating a partially folded protein with structured RhoGAP domain and flexible linker/C-terminal regions typical of dynamic signaling scaffolds. HPA classifies FAM13A as a plasma protein with ubiquitous expression (detected in all tissues), low tissue specificity, and triple nucleolar/cell junction/cytosolic localization, consistent with roles in cytoskeletal organization and cell-cell adhesion. Gene ontology annotations include GTPase activation and small GTPase-mediated signal transduction, positioning FAM13A within Rho signaling pathways. STRING interactions reveal strong coupling to lung disease and epithelial integrity genes: HHIP (0.854, hedgehog-interacting protein, COPD susceptibility), CHRNA3 (0.720, nicotinic acetylcholine receptor subunit alpha-3, nicotine dependence/COPD), GSTCD (0.708, glutathione S-transferase, lung function), CHRNA5 (0.641, nicotinic receptor subunit alpha-5, smoking behavior/COPD), TRPV4 (0.640, transient receptor potential vanilloid-4, calcium channel, epithelial barrier), MMP12 (0.626, matrix metalloproteinase-12, macrophage elastase, emphysema), SERPINA1 (0.615, alpha-1-antitrypsin, protease inhibitor, COPD), THSD4 (0.601, thrombospondin domain protein, extracellular matrix), IREB2 (0.426, iron-responsive element binding protein-2, iron metabolism), and RIN3 (0.410, Ras-interacting protein, endocytosis). The HHIP-CHRNA3-CHRNA5-GSTCD cluster represents the major COPD GWAS locus on 4q22.1, establishing FAM13A as a causal gene for chronic obstructive pulmonary disease. ClinVar documents 29 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including 4q21.21-22.3 copy number losses/gains, splice site variants (c.1271+5G>A), missense mutations (p.Pro822Leu), deletions, and translocations, indicating dosage sensitivity that may contribute to lung development disorders or COPD susceptibility. Conservation is evident across mammals: mouse (Q8BGI4, 693 aa), bovine (Q8HYW0, 697 aa). Mechanistically, FAM13A functions as a RhoGAP that accelerates GTP hydrolysis of RhoA/Rac1/Cdc42, converting active GTP-bound states to inactive GDP-bound states, thereby terminating Rho signaling cascades. In lung epithelial cells, FAM13A likely regulates actin cytoskeleton dynamics, adherens junction assembly, and epithelial barrier integrity through RhoA inhibition. The nucleolar localization suggests additional roles in ribosome biogenesis or stress response. Cell junction localization indicates direct involvement in maintaining epithelial adhesion complexes. Loss of FAM13A RhoGAP activity causes constitutive Rho activation, leading to cytoskeletal hypercontractility, junctional disruption, increased epithelial permeability, and enhanced inflammatory responses—hallmarks of COPD pathogenesis. The protein may integrate smoking-induced signals (via CHRNA3/CHRNA5 nicotinic receptors) with cytoskeletal remodeling, making it a therapeutic target for smoking-related lung disease.
