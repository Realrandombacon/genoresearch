ClinVar documents 15 pathogenic variants, predominantly chromosome 16p11.2 copy number gains/losses (16p11.2 duplication/deletion syndromes) associated with neurodevelopmental disorders including autism spectrum disorder, schizophrenia, and intellectual disability. The 16p11.2 region is a well-established genomic hotspot for neuropsychiatric disease.

FUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: ZNF771 operates as a KRAB-domain zinc finger transcriptional repressor that recruits TRIM28/KAP1 chromatin silencing machinery to specific genomic targets. The C2H2 zinc finger array (3 fingers, 63-146aa) recognizes 9-10bp DNA sequences, while the N-terminal region (1-62aa) likely contains a KRAB repression domain that binds TRIM28. TRIM28 recruitment establishes H3K9me3 heterochromatin marks via SETDB1, silencing target genes. Nucleolar localization suggests additional roles in ribosomal DNA (rDNA) silencing or nucleolar stress response, potentially coupling chromatin repression to ribosome biogenesis regulation. Interaction with RCOR3 indicates potential crosstalk with REST neuronal gene silencing pathway. High-confidence AlphaFold structure (81.2 pLDDT) supports stable zinc finger folding required for sequence-specific DNA recognition. Pathogenic 16p11.2 CNVs disrupt ZNF771 dosage, causing haploinsufficiency or overexpression that dysregulates neurodevelopmental gene expression programs, contributing to autism and schizophrenia phenotypes.
