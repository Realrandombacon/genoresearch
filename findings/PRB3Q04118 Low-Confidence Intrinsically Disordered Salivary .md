## Description
PRB3 (Proline rich protein BstNI subfamily 3, also known as G1 or PRG) is a 351 amino acid secreted protein localized to saliva and the oral cavity (UniProt Q04118) with low-confidence intrinsically disordered architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 47.2). The protein is composed almost entirely of proline-rich repeat domains characteristic of salivary proline-rich proteins (PRPs). Key domains include the Proline-rich protein family domain (IPR026086/PTHR23203, residues 1-179 & 180-346/240-346) forming two tandem ~167 aa and ~107 aa modules (~98% coverage) that mediate protein-protein and protein-tannin interactions. The Pfam proline-rich domain (PF15240, residues 1-115 & 261-346) and SMART proline-rich domain (SM01412, residues 1-179 & 180-346) confirm the repetitive, disordered nature of this protein. The extremely low AlphaFold confidence (pLDDT 47.2) is expected for intrinsically disordered proteins (IDPs) that lack stable tertiary structure and function through conformational flexibility. Molecular functions include identical protein binding and oral defense, placing PRB3 within the salivary protective machinery.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places PRB3 within a salivary protein network: strongest interaction with PRB1 (0.817, Basic salivary proline-rich protein 1, a paralog), indicating functional redundancy within the PRP family. High confidence links include RB1 (0.780, Retinoblastoma-associated protein - likely a false positive due to naming confusion, or a non-canonical interaction), C4BPA (0.744, Complement component 4 binding protein alpha, involved in innate immunity), and ZNF80 (0.680, Zinc finger protein 80). Connections to STATH (0.522, Statherin, another salivary protein that inhibits calcium phosphate precipitation) and PRB4 (0.474, Basic salivary proline-rich protein 4) reinforce its role in saliva composition. Interactions with SFTPA2 (0.499) and SFTPA1 (0.496, Pulmonary surfactant proteins) suggest potential functional overlap in mucosal defense. KRTAP9-1 (0.482, Keratin associated protein) hints at interactions with oral epithelial structures.\n\nHPA expression reveals an exceptionally tissue-enriched pattern with extraordinary levels in the salivary gland (13016.8 nTPM) - one of the highest expression values observed in dark gene research. This extreme specificity confirms PRB3's dedicated role in saliva production. The 'group enriched' cancer status suggests upregulation in certain tumors, possibly reflecting re-activation of developmental programs or altered secretory phenotypes. Subcellular localization as a predicted secreted protein aligns with its function in the oral cavity.\n\nClinVar documents 45 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains affecting the 12p13 region: CNV gains (12p13.33-11.1, 12p13.33-q13.12, 12p13.33-13.2 duplications) and insertions within the coding sequence. The high variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity. The PRP gene cluster on 12p is known for extensive copy number variation and allelic polymorphisms, reflecting evolutionary adaptation to dietary tannins and pathogens. Haploinsufficiency may impair oral defense, increasing susceptibility to dental caries, oral infections, or altered taste perception. Duplications may cause excessive PRP production, potentially altering saliva viscosity or calcium homeostasis.\n\nConservation is moderate across primates, with human-specific expansions of the PRP gene family reflecting dietary adaptation.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: PRB3 functions as a multifunctional oral defense protein secreted by acinar cells of the salivary gland. The intrinsically disordered proline-rich repeats provide multiple binding sites for: (1) dietary tannins and polyphenols, preventing their absorption and protecting against antinutritional effects; (2) oral bacteria and viruses, agglutinating pathogens for clearance; (3) calcium ions, regulating supersaturation and preventing pathological calcification while maintaining enamel remineralization. The tandem repeat architecture allows avidity-based binding - multiple weak interactions sum to high-affinity binding. PRB3 works synergistically with STATH (calcium regulation), PRB1/PRB4 (redundant tannin binding), and C4BPA (complement-mediated immunity) to maintain oral homeostasis. Dysregulation via 12p CNVs disrupts this balance: loss increases caries susceptibility and oral infections, while gain may alter saliva rheology or cause excessive tannin sequestration affecting nutrient absorption.
