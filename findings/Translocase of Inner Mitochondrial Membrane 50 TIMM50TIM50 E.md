# Translocase of Inner Mitochondrial Membrane 50 (TIMM50/TIM50): Essential Receptor Subunit of TIM23 Complex Governing Mitochondrial Protein Import and Membrane Permeability

**Date:** 2026-06-17T10:33:47.146229

**Quality Score:** 7/10 (GOOD)

**Confidence:** MEDIUM-HIGH

## Description
TIMM50 encodes a 353 aa integral membrane protein (UniProt Q3ZCQ8) that serves as the critical receptor subunit of the TIM23 translocase complex in the mitochondrial inner membrane. The protein contains a conserved FCP1 homology domain (IPR004274, PS50969, residues 143-286) belonging to the haloacid dehalogenase (HAD) superfamily (IPR023214, G3DSA:3.40.50.1000, residues 133-317), though in TIMM50 this domain has evolved for protein-protein interactions rather than phosphatase activity. TIMM50 belongs to the mitochondrial import inner membrane translocase subunit TIM50 family (IPR050365, PTHR12210, residues 124-308). STRING interactions place TIMM50 at the absolute core of the mitochondrial protein import machinery with ultra-high confidence partnerships: TIMM23 (0.999), the central channel-forming subunit; TOMM40 (0.965), the outer membrane translocase pore; TIMM44 (0.931), the membrane anchor recruiting mtHsp70 motor; TIMM21 (0.860), a peripheral membrane component; PAM16 (0.829) and DNAJC19 (0.822), components of the presequence translocase-associated motor (PAM); and TIMM17A/TIMM17B (0.823/0.809), regulatory subunits controlling channel gating. HPA confirms ubiquitous expression across all tissues with dual localization to mitochondria and nucleoplasm, and explicitly annotates involvement in epilepsy and intellectual disability. ClinVar lists 29 pathogenic variants including missense mutations affecting critical residues (p.Arg113Cys, p.Ser9Leu, p.Arg243Gly, p.Arg295Gln, p.Gln313Glu), a nonsense mutation (p.Gln110Ter), and large copy number gains on chromosome 19q, linking TIMM50 dysfunction to mitochondrial disease phenotypes including MGCA9 (mitochondrial complex V deficiency). AlphaFold structure prediction yields high confidence (global pLDDT 79.9), revealing a well-folded architecture with structured domains suitable for stable integration into the TIM23 complex. Mechanistically, TIMM50 functions through a multi-step import cycle: (1) Its intermembrane space domain recognizes and binds presequence-bearing precursor proteins emerging from the TOM40 channel; (2) TIMM50 maintains the permeability barrier of the inner membrane by keeping the TIM23 channel closed in the absence of substrate, preventing proton leak and loss of membrane potential; (3) Upon precursor binding, TIMM50 undergoes conformational changes that open the TIMM23 channel and recruit the PAM motor complex (TIMM44-PAM16-DNAJC19-mtHsp70) to drive ATP-dependent translocation into the matrix. The FCP1-like domain likely mediates interactions with TIMM23 and TIMM44 rather than catalyzing dephosphorylation. Disruption of TIMM50 would cause catastrophic failure of mitochondrial protein import, leading to accumulation of unfolded precursors in the cytosol, loss of membrane potential, defective oxidative phosphorylation, and cell death—particularly affecting high-energy tissues like brain (explaining epilepsy/intellectual disability) and muscle.
