# WDR3: 943 aa Nuclear Utp12 Component of Small-Subunit Processome with High-Confidence Double Beta-Propeller Structure (pLDDT 84.8) and Ubiquitous Expression in 18S rRNA Biogenesis

## Description
WDR3 (Q9UNX4, 943 aa), also known as UTP12 or DIP2, is an essential nuclear protein belonging to the WD repeat family and serving as a core component of the small-subunit (SSU) processome complex involved in 18S ribosomal RNA biogenesis. InterPro identifies a distinctive double beta-propeller architecture: two WD40/YVTN repeat-like domains (IPR015943, G3DSA:2.130.10.10, aa 13-222, 279-456, 457-648) forming stable seven-bladed propellers, multiple WD40 repeats (IPR001680, PF00400, aa 54-228, 400-482, 485-526), and a specific Dip2/Utp12 family C-terminal domain (IPR007148, PF04003, aa 805-907). The conserved WD40 signature sites (IPR019775, aa 122-136, 206-220) confirm structural integrity for protein-protein interactions. AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (pLDDT 84.8, residues 1-943) consistent with a large, well-folded protein featuring two distinct beta-propeller domains connected by linkers, typical of SSU processome scaffolding factors. HPA shows ubiquitous expression with low tissue specificity (detected in all tissues); localized to nucleoplasm and nucleoli; classified as an essential protein and predicted intracellular protein involved in ribosome biogenesis. STRING reveals 10 extremely high-confidence interactions (scores 0.995-0.999) forming the core SSU processome network: UTP15 (0.999, U3 small nucleolar RNA-associated protein 15), BYSL (0.998, Bystin-like protein), UTP20 (0.998, U3 small nucleolar RNA-associated protein 20), UTP6 (0.998, U3 small nucleolar RNA-associated protein 6), NOP58 (0.998, Nucleolar protein 58), UTP18 (0.997, U3 small nucleolar RNA-associated protein 18), MPHOSPH10 (0.997, M-phase phosphoprotein 10), UTP3 (0.996, U3 small nucleolar RNA-associated protein 3), PWP2 (0.996, Periodic tryptophan protein 2 homolog), and KRR1 (0.995, Small subunit processome component 19). ClinVar contains 14 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number losses across chromosome 1p (chr1:116675753-121116815, chr1:102021465-119737478) and gains (chr1:111647582-121343783, chr1:849467-249224684), plus intronic/splice variants (c.2582+58C>G, c.2152-14C>G, c.1097+3G>T), though no specific monogenic diseases are yet assigned. Functional hypothesis: WDR3/Utp12 functions as a critical structural scaffold within the 90S pre-ribosome (SSU processome) that facilitates the early cleavage events of 35S/47S pre-rRNA to generate 18S rRNA. The double beta-propeller structure provides extensive surface area for simultaneous binding to multiple SSU processome components (UTP-A, UTP-B, and UTP-C modules), stabilizing the massive ribonucleoprotein assembly. The C-terminal Dip2/Utp12 domain (aa 805-907) likely mediates specific RNA or protein contacts essential for processome integrity. Ubiquitous expression and nucleolar localization reflect its housekeeping role in ribosome production required for cell proliferation in all tissues. Disruption via CNVs or mutations would impair 18S rRNA synthesis, leading to nucleolar stress, p53 activation, and potentially developmental defects or ribosomopathies. Conservation across eukaryotes (mouse 942 aa, Dictyostelium 942 aa) confirms ancient and essential role in translation machinery assembly. NOTE: This gene is a well-characterized but under-studied essential factor in ribosome biogenesis.
