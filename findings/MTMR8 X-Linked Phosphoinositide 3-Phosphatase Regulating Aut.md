## Description
MTMR8 (UniProt Q96EF0) encodes a 704 aa intracellular lipid phosphatase belonging to the myotubularin family, functioning as a critical regulator of phosphoinositide metabolism, autophagy, and muscle development through heterodimerization with its catalytically inactive partner MTMR9. InterPro analysis reveals a specialized multi-domain architecture characteristic of myotubularins: an N-terminal GRAM domain (aa 8-101, IPR048994, cd13345) mediating membrane association and lipid binding; a central protein tyrosine phosphatase-like catalytic domain (aa 126-433, IPR030591, cd14584) containing the active site motif (HCXXGXXR, aa 336-346, IPR016130) responsible for dephosphorylating phosphatidylinositol 3-phosphate [PI(3)P] and phosphatidylinositol 3,5-bisphosphate [PI(3,5)P2]; and classification within the Myotubularin family (IPR030564) and Protein-tyrosine phosphatase superfamily (IPR029021). AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (global pLDDT 81.9), indicating a well-folded, stable enzyme with distinct domains poised for membrane recruitment and catalytic activity. STRING interactions define MTMR8 as a central node in phosphoinositide signaling and autophagy networks: FIG4 (0.919, PI(3,5)P2 5-phosphatase), PIKFYVE (0.915, PI(3)P 5-kinase), INPP4A (0.905), INPP5F (0.904), INPP4B (0.903), CDIPT (0.900), PIP4P2 (0.900), PIP4P1 (0.900), PIK3C3 (0.806, VPS34), and crucially MTMR9 (0.428), its obligate heterodimeric partner that lacks catalytic activity but stabilizes MTMR8 and determines substrate specificity. HPA expression shows low tissue specificity with detection in multiple tissues including muscle and nervous system, classified as an intracellular metabolic enzyme involved in lipid metabolism; absence in cancer suggests a housekeeping role in membrane trafficking rather than oncogenic drive. ClinVar reports 46 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including frameshift mutations (c.21del, p.Lys8fs) and large copy number gains/losses spanning the X chromosome (Xp22.33-q28), indicating genomic instability at this locus potentially linked to X-linked neuromuscular disorders, though no specific monogenic disease has been definitively assigned to MTMR8 alone (unlike MTM1 which causes X-linked myotubular myopathy). Literature (19 papers) highlights: (1) Autophagy Regulation—MTMR8/MTMR9 heterodimers control autophagosome formation by regulating PI(3)P levels on early endosomes, with loss leading to autophagic defects; (2) Muscle Development—cooperation with PI3K regulates actin filament modeling and muscle fiber integrity in zebrafish, suggesting conserved roles in vertebrate myogenesis; (3) Substrate Specificity—MTMR9 binding alters MTMR8 substrate preference from PI(3)P to PI(3,5)P2, demonstrating allosteric regulation within the heterodimer; (4) Reversible Oxidation—catalytic cysteine can be reversibly oxidized, providing a redox-sensing mechanism for regulating phosphatase activity under oxidative stress. Functional hypothesis: MTMR8 functions as a: (1) Phosphoinositide Homeostat—dephosphorylates PI(3)P and PI(3,5)P2 to maintain endosomal membrane identity and prevent aberrant fusion/fission events; (2) Autophagy Gatekeeper—regulates the initiation and maturation of autophagosomes by controlling PI(3)P pools required for WIPI/ATG18 recruitment; (3) Muscle Integrity Factor—maintains sarcomeric structure and T-tubule organization in skeletal muscle through localized lipid signaling; (4) Redox Sensor—modulates activity in response to cellular oxidative state, linking metabolic stress to membrane trafficking. Mechanistic proposal: In the cytosol, MTMR8 exists in an autoinhibited state or bound to MTMR9. Upon membrane recruitment via its GRAM domain binding to specific lipid motifs, the MTMR8-MTMR9 heterodimer engages PI(3)P or PI(3,5)P2 substrates. The catalytic domain hydrolyzes the 3-phosphate group, converting PI(3)P to PI and PI(3,5)P2 to PI(5)P, thereby terminating signals for endosomal sorting and autophagosome formation. MTMR9 acts as a scaffold that stabilizes MTMR8 against degradation and shifts its substrate specificity toward PI(3,5)P2, essential for late endosomal/lysosomal function. Disruption of this balance (via mutation or CNV) leads to accumulation of PI(3)P, causing enlarged endosomes, defective autophagy, and muscle weakness reminiscent of myotubular myopathy phenotypes. The high-confidence structure (pLDDT 81.9) supports a rigid catalytic core capable of precise lipid headgroup recognition. X-chromosome location implies hemizygous males are particularly vulnerable to loss-of-function variants. The combination of a well-folded phosphatase architecture, exclusive interaction with phosphoinositide regulators and MTMR9, ubiquitous metabolic expression, high variant burden (46 ClinVar entries), and demonstrated roles in autophagy and muscle development positions MTMR8 as a genuine dark gene requiring mechanistic dissection of its contribution to X-linked neuromuscular diseases and autophagy-related disorders.
