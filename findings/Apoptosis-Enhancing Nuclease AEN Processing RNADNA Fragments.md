## Description
AEN (Apoptosis-Enhancing Nuclease) is a 325 aa metal-dependent exonuclease functioning as a DNA/RNA degradation enzyme that accelerates apoptotic cell death by processing nucleic acid fragments. InterPro analysis reveals a canonical ribonuclease H-like catalytic core (G3DSA:3.30.420.10, aa 107-288) with an EXOIII exonuclease domain (SM00479, aa 109-275) and a Pfam exonuclease domain (PF00929, aa 111-266), placing it in the REXO1/REXO3/REXO4 family (IPR047021, aa 47-305) of RNA exonucleases. AlphaFold predicts a confident structure (pLDDT 74.3) consistent with a stable α/β fold characteristic of RNase H superfamily enzymes. HPA expression shows low tissue specificity with detection across many tissues, cancer-enhanced expression, and subcellular localization to nucleoplasm and nucleoli, consistent with a nuclear nuclease role. ClinVar contains 32 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains and losses on chromosome 15q24-26, though specific AEN-associated diseases remain undefined. Literature is confounded by non-gene abbreviations, but the gene name 'Apoptosis-enhancing nuclease' indicates its discovered function in accelerating cell death.
