# ZNF611: KRAB Domain C2H2 Zinc Finger Transcriptional Repressor with Potential Roles in Sensory Neuron Gene Regulation via TRIM28/KAP1 Recruitment

## Description
ZNF611 (Gene ID: 124534, UniProt Q8N823) is a 705 amino acid Krüppel-associated box (KRAB) domain-containing zinc finger protein located on chromosome 19q13.42 (NC_000019.10). InterPro domain analysis reveals a canonical KRAB repressor domain (IPR001909/PF01352/IPR036051, positions 24-97) at the N-terminus, followed by an extensive array of C2H2-type zinc finger domains (IPR013087/PF00096/PS00028, positions 231-314+ with additional fingers extending through position 421 per IPR050758) responsible for sequence-specific DNA binding. The protein belongs to the broader C2H2 zinc finger protein family (IPR036236). AlphaFold structure prediction (AF-Q8N823-F1) yields a medium-confidence model (global pLDDT 64.1), consistent with structured KRAB and zinc finger domains connected by flexible linkers, typical for transcriptional regulators requiring dynamic chromatin engagement. Human Protein Atlas classifies ZNF611 as a predicted intracellular transcription factor involved in transcription and its regulation, with DNA-binding molecular function localized to the nucleoplasm. Expression is ubiquitous (detected in all tissues) with low tissue and cancer specificity, but cell type-enhanced single-cell specificity indicates regulated expression in specific lineages. STRING database identifies 10 interaction partners revealing intriguing functional connections: TRIM28 (score 0.580, KAP1 co-repressor critical for KRAB-ZFP mediated silencing) strongly supports transcriptional repression function; ZNF44 (0.513) and ZNF675 (0.486) are paralogous KRAB-ZFPs suggesting functional redundancy or cooperation; RPP38 (0.497) is an RNase P/MRP subunit linking to RNA processing; FOXD4L6 (0.479) is a forkhead transcription factor involved in neuroectoderm development; notably, three olfactory receptors appear as interactors (OR5AP2: 0.475, OR1L8: 0.445, OR8B4: 0.432), suggesting potential roles in sensory neuron gene regulation or chromatin organization of olfactory receptor clusters; NXPE3 (0.459) is a neurexophilin/PC-esterase domain protein; and HORMAD1 (0.427) is involved in meiotic recombination. ClinVar lists 16 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains spanning 19q13.33-13.43 (multi-megabase regions affecting many genes); no specific monogenic disease is yet attributed solely to ZNF611, but dosage sensitivity of the 19q13 region is linked to developmental delays and neurological phenotypes. Conservation is evident across primates (chimpanzee A0A2I3RYX7, gorilla G3S7M3, gibbon G1R298, Cercocebus A0A2K5MK00). Functional hypothesis: ZNF611 acts as a sequence-specific transcriptional repressor silencing distinct sets of target genes during neurodevelopment and potentially in sensory neurons. By recruiting TRIM28/KAP1/SETDB1 complex, it establishes H3K9me3 heterochromatin marks to silence target genes. The olfactory receptor interactions suggest ZNF611 may participate in the epigenetic regulation of olfactory receptor gene clusters, which require monoallelic expression control. Interaction with FOXD4L6 hints at roles in early neural development. Disease mechanism: Haploinsufficiency or overexpression due to 19q13 CNVs disrupts the balance of repressive chromatin marks, leading to derepression of target genes involved in neurodevelopment and potentially olfactory system function. Priority experiments include ChIP-seq to define genomic binding sites (particularly at olfactory receptor clusters), SELEX for motif determination, co-IP validation of TRIM28/RPP38 interactions, and CRISPR knockout in neuronal progenitors and olfactory sensory neurons.
