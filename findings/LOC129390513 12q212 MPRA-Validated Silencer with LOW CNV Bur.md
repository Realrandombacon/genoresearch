## Description
LOC129390513 is a 200bp cis-regulatory element (CRE) located on chromosome 12q21.2 (GRCh38: 79370115-79370315), functionally validated as a transcriptional silencer through STARR-seq massively parallel reporter assays (MPRAs) in K562 erythroleukemia cells. This locus lacks canonical promoter, enhancer, or CTCF-bound insulator chromatin signatures but demonstrates repressive activity against the super core promoter SCP1. As a non-coding regulatory sequence, it has no UniProt accession, protein domains, STRING interaction partners, or Human Protein Atlas expression data (query not applicable for non-coding locus).\n\nClinVar analysis reveals 6 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variants disrupting this region, representing LOW CNV burden (compared to RNF115: 134 variants, LOC101928389: 82 variants, LOC129389193: 19 variants, LOC129389757: 16 variants, ZNF716: 9 variants, LOC129390592: 9 variants). The variant spectrum shows MODERATE DELETION BIAS: 4 copy number losses (deletions spanning 12q21.2-21.31 through 12q15-21.31, ~5-11 Mb genomic spans) versus 2 copy number gains (duplications spanning 12q21.2-21.31 ~8 Mb and whole-arm 12p13.33-q24.33 ~133 Mb). This ~2:1 ratio (67% deletions, 33% duplications) indicates moderate loss-of-function dosage sensitivity where reduced silencing activity is more pathogenic than increased silencing, contrasting with duplication-biased elements like LOC129389193 (100% duplications) and LOC132205950 (~86% duplications).\n\nThe genomic context places LOC129390513 within 12q21.2, a region associated with 12q deletion/duplication syndromes (characterized by intellectual disability, growth retardation, cardiac defects, and craniofacial dysmorphism). Nearby 12q21 genes include FZD10 (frizzled-10, Wnt receptor, developmental signaling), PAK1IP1 (PAK1 interacting protein 1, ribosome biogenesis), and TBC1D3 (TBC1 domain family member 3, Rab GTPase regulation). The 12q21 region is critical for neurodevelopment, Wnt signaling, and vesicular trafficking.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: LOC129390513 functions as a distal silencer within the 12q21.2 topologically associating domain (TAD), potentially regulating FZD10, PAK1IP1, or TBC1D3 in hematopoietic and developmental contexts. The moderate ~2:1 deletion bias suggests that LOSS-of-silencing (haploinsufficiency, 50% activity) is moderately more pathogenic than GAIN-of-silencing (triplication, 150% activity). This indicates the target gene(s) require MAXIMAL repression - reduced silencing causes ectopic overexpression leading to developmental defects. The mechanism likely involves recruitment of co-repressor complexes (possibly involving Polycomb repressive complexes or HDACs) that maintain tight repression of Wnt signaling components (FZD10) or cell cycle regulators. In K562 cells (erythroleukemia), this silencer may regulate Wnt pathway genes critical for hematopoietic differentiation. The 12q21.2 location near FZD10 (Wnt receptor) raises the possibility that LOC129390513 prevents ectopic Wnt signaling activation. FZD10 overexpression is associated with colorectal cancer and developmental defects. The LOW CNV burden (6 variants) indicates this element has modest mutational target size, with moderate loss-of-function dosage sensitivity suggesting 12q21 deletion phenotypes may involve silencer-mediated Wnt pathway dysregulation.
