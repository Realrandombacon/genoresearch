## Description
PIP4P2 (Phosphatidylinositol-4,5-Bisphosphate 4-Phosphatase 2, also known as Type 2 PI(4,5)P2 4-Phosphatase or TMEM55B homolog, UniProt Q8N4L2) is a 257 aa intracellular/membrane-associated enzyme belonging to the phosphoinositide phosphatase family that functions as a PI(4,5)P2 4-phosphatase converting phosphatidylinositol 4,5-bisphosphate to phosphatidylinositol 5-phosphate (PI5P), regulating phosphoinositide homeostasis, alpha cell actin depolymerization and glucagon secretion, macrophage phagocytosis downregulation, and autophagy positive regulation. FUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: PIP4P2 serves as a 4-position phosphatase that dephosphorylates PI(4,5)P2 to generate PI5P, acting in opposition to PIP4K2A/B/C kinases that phosphorylate PI5P back to PI(4,5)P2, thereby controlling the PI5P:PI(4,5)P2 ratio that regulates actin cytoskeleton dynamics, membrane trafficking, autophagy initiation, and immune cell function. MECHANISTIC PROPOSAL: The catalytic domain (positions 4-256) contains the active site that specifically removes the 4-position phosphate from PI(4,5)P2, generating PI5P. In pancreatic alpha cells, PIP4P2-mediated PI5P production triggers actin depolymerization through PI5P effectors (potentially MORN4, WIPI proteins), enabling glucagon granule exocytosis - dysfunction contributes to diabetes through impaired glucagon secretion. In macrophages, PIP4P2 localizes to phagosomes where PI5P production downregulates phagocytic activity, potentially through PI5P-mediated inhibition of PI(4,5)P2-dependent actin polymerization required for phagosome formation. The PIP4P2-PIP4K2A opposing enzyme pair creates a phosphoinositide cycle: PIP4K2A produces PI(4,5)P2 from PI5P (ATP-dependent), PIP4P2 converts PI(4,5)P2 back to PI5P, allowing dynamic regulation of both lipids. INPP5E/INPP5B/OCRL 5-phosphatases provide additional regulatory nodes by removing 5-position phosphates. The 37 ClinVar variants at 8p/q suggest PIP4P2 dosage sensitivity contributes to metabolic and immune disorders.
