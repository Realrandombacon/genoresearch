## Description
PGA3 (Pepsinogen A3) is a 388-amino acid secreted aspartic peptidase zymogen belonging to the peptidase family A1 that functions as a precursor to pepsin A, the primary proteolytic enzyme in gastric juice responsible for protein digestion. InterPro analysis reveals a canonical two-lobed aspartic protease structure: an N-terminal propeptide domain (IPR012848, residues 19-45; PF07966) that maintains the enzyme in an inactive state at neutral pH, and a catalytic pepsin domain (IPR001461, residues 58-385; IPR033121, residues 75-387; IPR034162, residues 66-386; PF00026, residues 75-387) containing the conserved active site motifs (IPR001969, residues 91-102 and 274-285) with the critical DTG catalytic triad essential for acid-dependent proteolysis. The protein belongs to the acid protease superfamily (G3DSA:2.40.70.10) with GO annotations for aspartic-type endopeptidase activity (GO:0004190) and proteolysis (GO:0006508). AlphaFold predicts a very high-confidence structure (pLDDT 91.2), consistent with a stable, well-folded digestive enzyme optimized for the harsh acidic environment of the stomach.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places PGA3 in a tight functional cluster of gastric digestive enzymes with exceptionally high confidence: PGA4 (0.980, pepsinogen A4, the closest paralog), LIPF (0.704, gastric lipase), PGA5 (0.497, pepsinogen A5), and moderate connections to AMY2B/AMY1B/AMY2A (amylases), F5 (coagulation factor V), PROC (protein C), PLG (plasminogen), and MS4A1 (CD20). This network confirms PGA3's exclusive role in the gastric digestive machinery, co-expressed and potentially co-regulated with other luminal hydrolases. The strong PGA4 interaction suggests functional redundancy or cooperative activation in the stomach lumen.\n\nHuman Protein Atlas classifies PGA3 as a tissue-enriched enzyme with extreme specificity for the stomach, showing massive expression levels (63,357.7 nTPM) in gastric mucosa, consistent with its role as a major secretory product of chief cells. It is also classified as cancer-enriched, likely reflecting its use as a biomarker for gastric cancer or tumors of gastric origin. Subcellular localization predictions indicate secretion, matching its function as a luminal digestive enzyme.\n\nClinVar reports 10 pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants, all large copy number variations spanning chromosome 11 (coordinates include 11p11.12-q12.2 duplication, 11q12.1-13.3 duplication, 11p13-q25 duplication). While no specific monogenic disease is named solely for PGA3, these CNVs overlap with regions associated with developmental disorders and potential gastric dysfunction. The lack of point mutations suggests that complete loss of PGA3 may be compensated by other pepsinogens (PGA4, PGA5), or that gastric proteolysis defects are subclinical due to dietary factors or redundant enzymatic activity.\n\nLiterature analysis (47 papers) indicates PGA3 is a moderately studied digestive enzyme, primarily investigated in the context of gastric physiology, pepsinogen isoform diversity, and as a serum biomarker for gastric mucosal integrity (pepsinogen I/II ratio). Unlike the extensively characterized PGA1 or PGA5, PGA3 has fewer dedicated mechanistic studies, though its sequence and structure are well-conserved among mammals. Recent work focuses on its potential role in gastric cancer prognosis and Helicobacter pylori-associated gastritis.\n\nWe hypothesize that PGA3 functions as a specialized gastric protease with subtle substrate preferences distinct from PGA4/PGA5, potentially optimizing digestion of specific dietary proteins or resisting specific bacterial protease inhibitors. Its extreme stomach enrichment (63k+ nTPM) suggests it is a dominant contributor to gastric proteolytic capacity. The CNVs on 11p/q may cause dosage effects altering the pepsinogen pool, potentially influencing susceptibility to gastric ulcers, protein malabsorption, or gastric cancer progression. PGA3 represents a candidate biomarker for gastric mucosal atrophy, a potential target for modulating gastric digestion in metabolic disorders, and a component of the 'pepsinogen code' that determines individual digestive efficiency.
