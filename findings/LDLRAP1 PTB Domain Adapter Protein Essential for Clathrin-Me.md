## Description
LDLRAP1 encodes a 308 amino acid phosphotyrosine-binding (PTB) domain adapter protein (UniProt Q5SW96), also known as ARH (Autosomal Recessive Hypercholesterolemia protein), that bridges the LDL receptor to the clathrin endocytic machinery. With 284 publications, LDLRAP1 is moderately characterized—its disease mechanism is established but structural nuances remain incompletely understood.\n\nSTRUCTURAL FEATURES: AlphaFold predicts a medium-confidence structure (pLDDT 69.4) across all 308 residues, suggesting a partially structured protein with both ordered domains and flexible regions. InterPro analysis reveals a canonical PTB domain architecture: Phosphotyrosine-binding domain (cd13159, IPR006020, PF00640, PS01179, SM00462, G3DSA:2.30.29.30, SSF50729, residues 43-178) forming the core NPXY motif recognition fold. The PTB/PI domain (IPR011993) and Adapter and Engulfment Domain-Containing Protein family signatures (IPR051133, PTHR11232, residues 23-303) span most of the protein. The medium pLDDT suggests the C-terminal region (~residues 180-308) may be intrinsically disordered, potentially facilitating dynamic interactions with multiple partners.\n\nINTERACTION NETWORK: STRING-DB identifies 10 interaction partners with medium-high confidence (0.448-0.993), defining LDLRAP1's role in LDLR endocytosis. Core endocytic machinery: AP2B1 (0.993)—clathrin adapter complex subunit linking LDLRAP1 to clathrin coats; CLTC (0.853)—clathrin heavy chain forming the vesicle cage. Receptor partners: LDLR (0.993)—primary substrate, binds NPXY motif in cytoplasmic tail; LRP2 (0.980)—megalin, related LDLR family member. Functional paralog: DAB2 (0.814)—disabled homolog 2, alternative LDLR adapter. Ligand carriers: GC (0.739)—vitamin D-binding protein; CUBN (0.727)—cubilin, endocytic receptor. Regulatory factors: PCSK9 (0.633)—promotes LDLR degradation; APOB (0.523), APOE (0.448)—LDL ligands. This network positions LDLRAP1 as the critical bridge between LDLR cytoplasmic tails and clathrin coats.\n\nEXPRESSION & LOCALIZATION: HPA confirms ubiquitous expression detected in all tissues with enhancement in brain (79.7 nTPM). Subcellular localization includes microtubules—consistent with vesicle trafficking along cytoskeletal tracks. Biological processes include cholesterol metabolism, endocytosis, lipid/sterol/steroid metabolism. Disease involvement includes atherosclerosis, hyperlipidemia, and disease variants. Classified as disease-related gene with cell type enriched expression.\n\nCLINICAL SIGNIFICANCE: ClinVar reports 554 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants—the highest burden among genes analyzed—including frameshift deletions (c.19del p.Ala7fs, c.583del p.Ala195fs, c.509_510del p.Glu170fs, c.272_276delinsTAAT p.Lys91fs, c.246del p.Lys83fs, c.17_20del p.Ser6fs), nonsense mutations (c.808C>T p.Gln270Ter), missense variants (c.854A>G p.Gln285Arg, c.488A>C p.Gln163Pro), and splice variants (c.89-5C>G). These cause Autosomal Recessive Hypercholesterolemia (ARH, MIM# 603813), characterized by severe LDL-C elevation, premature atherosclerosis, and xanthomas. The extraordinary variant burden reflects strong purifying selection and clinical screening intensity.\n\nFUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: LDLRAP1 functions as an essential endocytic adapter through a coordinated mechanism: (1) Receptor recognition—the PTB domain (residues 43-178) binds the NPXY internalization motif in the LDLR cytoplasmic tail, recognizing the tyrosine in a specific sequence context. (2) Clathrin recruitment—the C-terminal region (residues 180-308), likely disordered (pLDDT 69.4), contains clathrin-box motifs that recruit AP2B1 and CLTC to form the coated pit. (3) Vesicle scission—interaction with microtubules facilitates transport of internalized LDLR vesicles toward early endosomes. Mechanistically, LDLRAP1 dimerization may enhance avidity for clustered LDLRs. Loss-of-function causes ARH because LDLR remains stranded at the plasma membrane, unable to internalize despite normal surface expression (distinguishing ARH from familial hypercholesterolemia caused by LDLR mutations). Recent literature (2025) reveals prognostic significance in triple-negative breast cancer and methylation status associations with cardiovascular events in FH patients. The brain-enriched expression (79.7 nTPM) suggests potential roles in neuronal cholesterol homeostasis, relevant to neurodegenerative disease.
