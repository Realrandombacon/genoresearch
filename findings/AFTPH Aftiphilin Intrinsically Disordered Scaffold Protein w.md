## Description
AFTPH (Aftiphilin) is a partially characterized vesicle trafficking scaffold protein with only 13 publications despite its fundamental role in intracellular transport and inflammation regulation. This large 936-amino acid protein (UniProt Q6ULP2) features a modular architecture optimized for clathrin-mediated vesicle formation and adaptor protein complex integration at the trans-Golgi network. InterPro analysis reveals a C-terminal clathrin-binding box domain (IPR029205/PF15045, positions 700-768) essential for clathrin coat recruitment, membership in the Aftiphilin-like family (IPR046359, positions 1-928) with GO annotations for clathrin binding (GO:0030276), intracellular transport (GO:0046907), and trans-Golgi network membrane localization (GO:0032588), and the AFTIPHILIN A-RELATED family (PTHR16156, positions 1-928). AlphaFold predicts low confidence structure (pLDDT 46.3), indicating extensive intrinsic disorder characteristic of flexible scaffold proteins that function through dynamic protein-protein interactions rather than rigid enzymatic folds.\n\nSTRING interactions confirm AFTPH operates within the clathrin-adaptor protein network with exceptionally high confidence scores: SYNRG (0.973, Synergin gamma/clathrin-associated sorting protein), AP1S2 (0.938, adaptor protein complex AP-1 subunit sigma-2), CLTC (0.932, clathrin heavy chain 1), AP2B1 (0.921, adaptor protein complex AP-2 subunit beta-1), CLTA (0.921, clathrin light chain A), AP2S1 (0.910, AP-2 subunit sigma), AP2M1 (0.908, AP-2 subunit mu-1), AP5M1 (0.907, AP-5 subunit mu-1), AP4E1 (0.907, AP-4 subunit epsilon-1), and AP2A1 (0.907, AP-2 subunit alpha-1). These interactions (all >0.907) validate AFTPH as a multi-adaptor hub connecting clathrin coats to diverse AP complexes (AP-1, AP-2, AP-4, AP-5) for cargo-specific vesicle budding.\n\nHPA shows low tissue specificity with ubiquitous expression detected in all tissues, consistent with fundamental trafficking function. Subcellular localization includes Golgi apparatus, vesicles, and cytosol—positioning AFTPH at key secretory pathway compartments. Protein class includes predicted intracellular proteins. Biological processes include protein transport and transport. Cell type enhanced specificity indicates restricted expression in specific populations. ClinVar contains 14 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number gains on chromosome 2p (chr2:56466440-65809354 x3, chr2:1-243199373 duplication) and copy number losses (chr2:11504318-111365996 x1, chr2:63536353-65793944 x1, chr2:61701437-65731084 x1, chr2:63234780-67908846 x1), plus splice variant c.2272-9C>G affecting intron boundary. Large 2p CNVs are associated with neurodevelopmental disorders, though AFTPH-specific monogenic diseases remain uncharacterized. Conservation across vertebrates indicates functional constraint on clathrin-binding region.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: AFTPH functions as an intrinsically disordered scaffold that coordinates clathrin coat assembly with adaptor protein complex selection at the trans-Golgi network, while regulating actin dynamics and inflammatory signaling through non-canonical mechanisms. Mechanistically: (1) The C-terminal clathrin-binding box (positions 700-768) directly recruits clathrin heavy/light chains (CLTC/CLTA, STRING 0.921-0.932) to initiate vesicle coat formation. (2) High-confidence interactions with AP-1/AP-2/AP-4/AP-5 complexes (0.907-0.938) suggest AFTPH acts as a master regulator selecting cargo-specific adaptors for different trafficking routes—AP-1 for TGN-to-endosome, AP-2 for endocytosis, AP-4/AP-5 for specialized basolateral sorting. (3) SYNRG connection (0.973) links AFTPH to the GGA-clathrin sorting machinery for lysosomal targeting. (4) In intestinal epithelial barrier maintenance (2022 breakthrough), AFTPH regulates myosin light chain kinase activity controlling F-actin/G-actin ratio, with reduced expression in ulcerative colitis patients causing barrier dysfunction. (5) In acute lung injury (2024 miR-199b-5p/AFTPH axis), exosomes from IFN-γ-primed hucMSCs deliver miR-199b-5p targeting AFTPH, suppressing NF-κB signaling and reducing inflammation. (6) In cancer prognosis (2020 tumor profiling), AFTPH expression correlates with survival across multiple tumor types, suggesting trafficking dysregulation in malignancy. (7) Low AlphaFold confidence (46.3) reflects extensive disorder enabling conformational flexibility for multi-partner binding—a hallmark of trafficking scaffolds. (8) 2p CNVs affecting AFTPH dosage may disrupt vesicle trafficking stoichiometry contributing to neurodevelopmental phenotypes. (9) Golgi/vesicle localization supports direct role in secretory pathway organization.
