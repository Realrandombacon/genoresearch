## Description
PCP2 (Purkinje cell protein 2, also known as GPSM4, PCD5, Lgn2) is a 136 amino acid intracellular protein (UniProt Q8IVA1) with medium-confidence partially-structured architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 69.9), characteristic of GoLoco/GPR motif-containing G protein regulators. The protein contains two conserved GoLoco/GPR motifs (IPR003109/PF02188/PS50877/SM00390, residues 23-45 and 63-85) that function as Gα-GDP dissociation inhibitors (GDIs), preventing GDP-GTP exchange and locking Gα subunits in their inactive GDP-bound state. A tetratricopeptide repeat (TPR) domain (G3DSA:1.25.40.10/IPR011990, residues 6-86) provides a helical scaffold for protein-protein interactions. The protein belongs to the Purkinje cell protein 2 family (IPR042168/PTHR47503, residues 1-136) with molecular functions in guanyl-nucleotide exchange factor activity (GO:0005085) and GTPase regulator activity (GO:0030695), placing PCP2 within G protein-coupled receptor (GPCR) signaling pathways.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals a network of G protein signaling components: strongest interactions with GNAI3 (0.794, Guanine nucleotide-binding protein G(i) subunit alpha-3), GNAI2 (0.708, G(i) subunit alpha-2), GABRA6 (0.680, Gamma-aminobutyric acid type A receptor subunit alpha-6), GNAI1 (0.673, G(i) subunit alpha-1), RIC8A (0.657, Resistance to inhibitors of cholinesterase 8 homolog A, non-canonical GEF), GRM6 (0.633, Metabotropic glutamate receptor 6), RIC8B (0.602, Resistance to inhibitors of cholinesterase 8 homolog B), TMEM38A (0.591, Trimeric intracellular cation channel type A), GNAT3 (0.587, Guanine nucleotide-binding protein G(t) subunit alpha-3), and GNAO1 (0.554, Guanine nucleotide-binding protein G(o) subunit alpha). This profile confirms PCP2 functions as a Gα-GDI, specifically regulating G(i/o) family members in neuronal and sensory signaling contexts. The interaction with GRM6 (metabotropic glutamate receptor) is particularly relevant given PCP2's retinal expression.\n\nHPA expression reveals group enrichment in brain (111.4 nTPM), testis (110.8 nTPM), and retina (31.1 nTPM), detected in many tissues. Subcellular localization includes nuclear speckles and vesicles, consistent with roles in signal transduction and potentially transcriptional regulation. The protein is classified as predicted intracellular.\n\nClinVar documents 9 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including a critical splice site mutation (c.292-1G>A) that likely causes exon skipping or aberrant splicing, large deletions affecting 19p13.3-13.2, and copy number gains (19p13.3-13.2 duplications, 19p13.3-q13.43 amplifications). The variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity. While no specific Mendelian disease is annotated, 19p CNVs are associated with developmental delay, intellectual disability, autism spectrum disorders, and epilepsy. Given PCP2's role in G protein signaling, dosage imbalance may cause dysregulated GPCR signaling in neurons, affecting synaptic transmission, neuronal development, and sensory processing.\n\nConservation is strong across vertebrates: human (Q8IVA1, 136 aa), mouse (P12660, 120 aa), indicating an ancient function in neuronal G protein regulation.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: PCP2 functions as a Gα-GDI that binds inactive Gα(i/o)-GDP complexes via its GoLoco motifs, preventing GDP dissociation and maintaining G proteins in their off state. This regulates the amplitude and duration of GPCR signaling, particularly in Purkinje cells, photoreceptors, and other neurons. The TPR domain may recruit PCP2 to specific subcellular compartments or scaffold additional signaling components. In the retina, PCP2 likely modulates GRM6 signaling in bipolar cells, affecting visual signal transduction. In the brain, PCP2 regulates G(i/o)-coupled receptors involved in neurotransmission, synaptic plasticity, and neuronal migration. Loss of PCP2 causes constitutive G protein activation or dysregulated signaling, leading to neuronal hyperexcitability, impaired synaptic transmission, and neurodevelopmental defects. Dosage sensitivity explains the association with 19p CNV syndromes. PCP2 represents a critical modulator of neuronal GPCR signaling and a candidate gene for neurodevelopmental disorders.
