## Description
STRN3 (Striatin-3) is a partially characterized scaffolding protein with 58 publications, emerging as a core component of the STRIPAK (Striatin-Interacting Phosphatase and Kinase) complex that integrates phosphatase signaling with cytoskeletal dynamics and cell polarity. This 797-amino acid protein (UniProt Q13033) features a bipartite architecture optimized for multi-protein complex assembly. InterPro analysis reveals an N-terminal striatin domain (IPR013258, positions 64-193) likely mediating coiled-coil oligomerization, and a C-terminal WD40 repeat β-propeller scaffold (IPR001680/PF00400, positions 469-796 with 7 WD40 repeats at 469-517/522-562/575-614/697-796 plus conserved sites IPR019775 at 495-509/738-752) forming a platform for protein-protein interactions. Additional domains include YVTN repeat-like quinoprotein amine dehydrogenase superfamily (G3DSA:2.130.10.10, positions 415-568/569-696/697-796) and PAC1/LIS1-like WD40 repeats (IPR020472, positions 495-509/601-615/738-752). The universal 0.999 confidence scores for core STRIPAK components (PPP2CA, PDCD10, STRIP1, STRN4, SIKE1) validate STRN3 as an essential complex scaffold. PPP2CA/PPP2R1A interactions position STRN3 as a PP2A phosphatase recruiter.\n\nHPA shows ubiquitous expression across all tissues with low specificity, consistent with housekeeping scaffolding function. Subcellular localization includes plasma membrane, actin filaments, primary cilium, and cytosol—positioning STRN3 at cell polarity and motility hubs. Calmodulin-binding molecular function suggests calcium-dependent regulation. Protein class includes plasma proteins and predicted intracellular proteins. ClinVar contains 29 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants predominantly consisting of large copy number losses on chromosome 14q12-21.2 (e.g., chr14:30935698-44461013 deletion, chr14:29190489-45325177 deletion, chr14:24959823-33415359 deletion) and copy number gains (chr14:20511673-61826023 triplication, chr14:20511672-47481203 duplication), though specific monogenic diseases remain incompletely characterized—large 14q CNVs are associated with neurodevelopmental disorders. Conservation across vertebrates indicates functional constraint.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: STRN3 functions as a modular scaffold that organizes the STRIPAK complex to coordinate PP2A phosphatase activity with kinase signaling for cell polarity, ciliogenesis, and growth control. Mechanistically: (1) The N-terminal striatin domain mediates homo/hetero-oligomerization with STRN4, forming dimeric/tetrameric platforms. (2) The C-terminal WD40 β-propeller binds PPP2CA catalytic subunit (STRING 0.999), recruiting PP2A holoenzyme to specific substrates. (3) PDCD10/Ccm1 interaction (0.999) links STRIPAK to cerebral cavernous malformation pathway and vascular integrity. (4) MOB4 binding (0.998) connects STRIPAK to NDR/LATS kinase regulation—STRN3 may dephosphorylate LATS1/2 to inhibit Hippo pathway, promoting YAP/TAZ nuclear translocation and proliferation (2025 pancreatic cancer autophagy study). (5) Primary cilium localization positions STRN3 for ciliogenesis regulation—loss causes ciliary defects affecting Hedgehog signaling. (6) Actin filament association suggests roles in cytoskeletal remodeling during cell migration. (7) Chromosomal rearrangements create oncogenic STRN3::NTRK2 fusions in pilocytic astrocytoma (2025 case report—larotrectinib responsive) and STRN3-ALK fusions in lung adenocarcinoma (2025 case reports—brigatinib/osimertinib responsive), where STRN3 coiled-coil domains drive constitutive kinase dimerization and activation. (8) SIKE1 interaction suggests TBK1/IKKε regulation linking STRIPAK to innate immunity. The medium AlphaFold confidence reflects flexible scaffold architecture required for dynamic complex assembly.
