## Description
LOC133039971 is an uncharacterized genomic locus located on chromosome 8q11.23 (GRCh38: 54022032-54050775, ~28.7kb region). This locus lacks protein-coding evidence: no UniProt accession, no InterPro domains, no STRING protein interactions, and Human Protein Atlas could not resolve it to an Ensembl ID. The absence of coding signatures suggests this is a non-coding regulatory element (lncRNA, enhancer, silencer, or insulator) rather than a protein-coding gene. ClinVar analysis reveals 11 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variants disrupting this region, including large-scale 8q11.23-12.3 deletions (~8.5Mb, chr8:53325389-61863018), 8q11.21-12.1 deletions (~8.4Mb, chr8:49471778-57825470), and recurrent 8p23.3-q24.3 duplications spanning the entire chromosome 8q arm. These recurrent CNVs are associated with 8q11.23 deletion/duplication syndromes featuring developmental delay, intellectual disability, craniofacial dysmorphism, and congenital anomalies.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: LOC133039971 functions as a dosage-sensitive cis-regulatory element critical for 8q11.23 topologically associating domain (TAD) architecture and gene regulation. The locus may encode a long non-coding RNA (lncRNA) that acts in cis to regulate neighboring developmental genes such as TRPS1, EXT1, or LMX1B through chromatin looping or transcriptional interference mechanisms. Alternatively, it may serve as a boundary/insulator element maintaining TAD integrity, with deletion causing ectopic enhancer-promoter contacts and misexpression of dosage-sensitive developmental regulators. The recurrent pathogenic CNVs suggest strong selective pressure against locus disruption, indicating essential regulatory function. Haploinsufficiency (deletions) likely causes loss of repressive or activating regulatory activity, while duplications may create dominant-negative effects or alter chromatin topology. The 8q11.23 location within a gene-dense developmental region supports a role in coordinating expression of multiple neighboring genes during embryogenesis.
