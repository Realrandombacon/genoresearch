## Description
ZMYM6 (Zinc Finger MYM-Type Protein 6) is a massive 1,325-amino acid nuclear protein (UniProt O95789) representing a genuine dark gene with only 5 publications despite its complex domain architecture and clinical significance. Structurally, ZMYM6 contains multiple distinctive domains: three MYM-type zinc finger motifs (IPR010507/PF06467, residues 133-174/316-350/466-500) featuring FCS sequence motifs for zinc coordination and protein-protein interactions; three TRASH domains (IPR011017/SM00746/PF24900, residues 80-228) originally identified in chromatin-associated proteins and implicated in nucleic acid binding; a C-terminal RNase H-like catalytic domain superfamily (IPR012337/SSF53098, residues 897-1291) suggesting potential nuclease activity; a FAM200/GTF2IRD2 helical domain (PF27046, residues 1009-1157); and a FAM200/ZBED5 RNase-like domain (PF27073, residues 825-999). The protein belongs to the zinc finger MYM-type/QRICH1 family (IPR051284/PTHR45736). AlphaFold predicts medium-confidence structure (pLDDT 68.6), consistent with a large multi-domain protein containing both structured catalytic regions and intrinsically disordered segments typical of transcriptional/chromatin regulators that undergo conformational flexibility during complex assembly. Functionally, ZMYM6 localizes to the nucleoplasm with ubiquitous expression across all tissues (HPA: detected in all, low tissue specificity) and cell type-enhanced patterns, suggesting housekeeping roles in nuclear processes. STRING interactions reveal connections to chromatin modifiers and nuclear proteins: ZNF841 (0.580, zinc finger protein), SETDB2 (0.510, histone methyltransferase for H3K9), UCHL3 (0.457, ubiquitin carboxyl-terminal hydrolase), POLK (0.437, DNA polymerase kappa for translesion synthesis), plus ZNF354A, NECAB3, AP4S1, and CACHD1—suggesting roles in epigenetic regulation, DNA repair, and protein homeostasis. Critically, ClinVar documents 10 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number losses/gains spanning 1p36-34 (multiple entries affecting megabase regions), an intronic SNV (c.94-5T>A), and inversions, yet ZERO diseases are formally associated—a significant gap given the genomic instability implications. Recent literature is sparse but includes associations with zygote developmental potential (2026), breast cancer CNV signatures (81 citations, 2013), bladder cancer models (2024), coronary artery disease miRNA regulation (2024), and HPV-mediated gene hypermethylation in cervical cancer (2020). The mechanistic hypothesis: ZMYM6 functions as a multi-functional chromatin regulator combining DNA/RNA binding (via MYM zinc fingers and TRASH domains) with potential catalytic activity (via RNase H-like domain) to coordinate transcriptional programs, RNA processing, or DNA damage responses. The TRASH domain homology to glucocorticoid receptor DNA-binding domains suggests direct DNA recognition, while the RNase H-like fold may process RNA-DNA hybrids (R-loops) or participate in RNA surveillance. In cancer contexts, ZMYM6 dysregulation through CNVs or HPV-mediated hypermethylation may disrupt chromatin architecture and genome stability. The 1p36 deletion syndrome region overlaps ZMYM6, suggesting haploinsufficiency could contribute to neurodevelopmental phenotypes (developmental delay, intellectual disability) characteristic of this common microdeletion syndrome. Future work should define ZMYM6's enzymatic activity (if any), identify binding partners in chromatin complexes, and screen 1p36 deletion patients for ZMYM6-specific phenotypes.
