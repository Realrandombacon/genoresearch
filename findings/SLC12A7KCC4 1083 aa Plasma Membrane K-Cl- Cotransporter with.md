## Description
SLC12A7 (Q9Y666, 1083 aa), also known as KCC4 (Potassium-Chloride Cotransporter 4), is a critical electroneutral cation-chloride cotransporter belonging to the SLC12 family. InterPro identifies a canonical architecture featuring two large homologous domains forming the transport core (IPR004841, PF00324, G3DSA:1.20.1740.10, aa 124-295, 418-695) characteristic of amino acid/polyamine transporters, flanked by specific K/Cl co-transporter signatures (IPR000076, PR01081, aa 47-75, 265-277) and extensive C-terminal regulatory domains (IPR018491, PF03522, aa 709-1083) essential for kinase binding and oligomerization. The protein belongs to the broader SLC12A transporter family (IPR004842, PTHR11827). AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (pLDDT 80.8, residues 1-1083), indicating a stable 12-transmembrane helix bundle with ordered cytoplasmic termini required for regulatory complex assembly. Although HPA lacks specific nTPM data, literature confirms expression in kidney distal tubule, inner ear supporting cells, and osteoclasts. STRING reveals 10 interactions defining the WNK-SPAK regulatory axis: STK39 (0.674, SPAK kinase), WNK3 (0.646), WNK1 (0.568), OXSR1 (0.565, OSR1 kinase), WNK4 (0.503), SLC12A4 (0.496, KCC1), SLC4A5 (0.486, Na+/HCO3- exchanger), EZR (0.557, ezrin cytoskeletal linker), LGALS9C (0.684), and OPRD1 (0.533). The strong links to WNK kinases and SPAK/OSR1 confirm tight phosphorylation-dependent regulation of transport activity. ClinVar contains 140 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including splice site mutations (c.918-9C>G, c.918-3C>T, c.1130-96G>A, c.1630-100G>T), large copy number losses (chr5:113462-5578808, chr5:113577-26369454), and gains, causing autosomal recessive deafness (DFNB16) and proximal renal tubular acidosis (pRTA) with osteopetrosis. Functional hypothesis: SLC12A7/KCC4 functions as an electroneutral K+:Cl- cotransporter (1:1 stoichiometry) responsible for Cl- efflux and cell volume regulation in specialized epithelia. In the kidney, it mediates basolateral Cl- exit in type A intercalated cells, working in concert with apical H+-ATPase and AE1 to secrete acid; loss causes pRTA. In the inner ear, it maintains low intracellular Cl- in supporting cells, essential for K+ recycling and endolymph homeostasis; disruption leads to sensorineural deafness. The long C-terminus serves as a scaffold for WNK1/3/4 kinases and SPAK/OSR1, which phosphorylate N-terminal threonine residues to inhibit transport under high Cl- conditions, preventing excessive ion loss. Interaction with ezrin (EZR) links the transporter to the actin cytoskeleton for polarized membrane targeting. Conservation across vertebrates (mouse 1083 aa, rat 1083 aa, rabbit 1106 aa) confirms essential physiological role.
