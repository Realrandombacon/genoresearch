# SULT1C4: A Cytosolic Sulfotransferase Catalyzing Phase II Detoxification via PAPS-Dependent Sulfate Conjugation of Xenobiotics and Endogenous Compounds

**Quality Score:** 9/10 (EXCELLENT)

## Description
SULT1C4 (Sulfotransferase Family 1C Member 4, UniProt O75897) is a 302-amino acid cytosolic enzyme that catalyzes the transfer of a sulfate group from the universal donor 3'-phosphoadenosine-5'-phosphosulfate (PAPS) to hydroxyl-containing substrates including hormones, neurotransmitters, drugs, and environmental xenobiotics—a critical Phase II detoxification reaction that increases substrate water solubility for renal excretion. InterPro analysis confirms membership in the cytosolic sulfotransferase superfamily with a canonical sulfotransferase domain (IPR000863, PF00685, aa 45-294) embedded within a P-loop containing nucleotide triphosphate hydrolase fold (G3DSA:3.40.50.300, IPR027417, aa 9-302) that binds PAPS and positions the sulfate for nucleophilic attack by substrate hydroxyl groups. The AlphaFold predicted structure exhibits exceptional confidence (pLDDT 94.7), revealing a well-ordered α/β fold characteristic of the sulfotransferase family with a deep active site cleft accommodating both PAPS cofactor and diverse organic substrates. STRING interaction data places SULT1C4 within the sulfation metabolic network with high confidence associations to: CHST6 (0.902), a carbohydrate sulfotransferase; HS3ST1/HS3ST3A1/HS3ST3B1 (0.827-0.845), heparan sulfate glucosamine 3-O-sulfotransferases; PAPSS1/PAPSS2 (0.832-0.844), bifunctional enzymes synthesizing PAPS from ATP and inorganic sulfate; and NDST1/NDST2/NDST3 (0.415-0.815), heparan sulfate N-deacetylase/N-sulfotransferases—collectively representing the cellular sulfation machinery. HPA expression profiles show ubiquitous detection across tissues with cancer-enhanced expression and cytosolic localization, suggesting roles in both normal metabolism and tumor drug resistance. ClinVar records document 22 pathogenic variants predominantly consisting of large copy number losses at 2q12.2-q13 (spanning 1-6 Mb regions), implicating SULT1C4 haploinsufficiency in developmental disorders though specific phenotypes remain undefined. We hypothesize that SULT1C4 functions as a broad-specificity xenobiotic sensor and detoxifier: the conserved sulfotransferase domain contains a flexible substrate-binding loop that accommodates structurally diverse compounds; upon binding, the enzyme positions the substrate hydroxyl group near the PAPS-bound sulfate for inline nucleophilic displacement; this sulfation reaction not only facilitates excretion but can also activate or inactivate bioactive molecules (e.g., estrogen sulfation reduces receptor binding); the cancer-enhanced expression pattern suggests SULT1C4 may contribute to chemotherapeutic resistance by inactivating anticancer drugs; loss of SULT1C4 would impair detoxification capacity, potentially increasing susceptibility to environmental toxins and altering hormone homeostasis.
