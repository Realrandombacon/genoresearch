## Description
CHERP (Calcium Homeostasis Endoplasmic Reticulum Protein, also known as C19orf44) is a 916 aa multifunctional protein functioning as a critical bridge between intracellular calcium signaling and pre-mRNA splicing. Despite its name suggesting an ER localization, CHERP is predominantly nuclear and acts as an essential component of the U2 small nuclear ribonucleoprotein (snRNP) complex, required for spliceosome assembly and cell viability. With 220 literature references, CHERP represents a 'partially characterized' dark gene where its dual role in calcium regulation and splicing is established but mechanistically distinct from canonical splicing factors. InterPro analysis reveals a unique domain architecture: an N-terminal SURP module (IPR000061/PF01805, aa 13-65) involved in RNA binding and protein-protein interactions, a central CID domain (IPR006569/PF04818, aa 149-289) often associated with transcription elongation, a large region of unknown function containing a DUF7819 domain (IPR056721/PF25127, aa 637-743) specific to CHERP, and a C-terminal G-patch domain (IPR000467/PF01585, aa 839-891) known to recruit RNA helicases. AlphaFold predicts a medium-confidence structure (pLDDT 63.2), consistent with a large scaffold containing structured domains connected by flexible linkers, potentially allowing conformational changes upon calcium binding or RNA engagement. HPA confirms ubiquitous expression with low tissue specificity, detected in all tissues, and classifies it as an essential protein, underscoring its fundamental role in cellular physiology. STRING interactions place CHERP firmly within the spliceosomal machinery: it binds strongly to U2SURP (0.886), PUF60 (0.881), RBM17 (0.874), the RNA helicase DHX15 (0.851), and core U2 snRNP components SNRPA1 (0.828) and SF1 (0.812). This network confirms CHERP's role in stabilizing the U2 snRNP-pre-mRNA interaction during early spliceosome assembly. ClinVar lists 12 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, primarily large copy number losses and gains on chromosome 19p, implicating CHERP dosage sensitivity in developmental disorders, although specific monogenic diseases are not yet fully defined. Recent literature highlights CHERP's necessity for cell cycle progression and its potential role in coupling calcium fluxes to alternative splicing decisions, though the exact mechanism of calcium sensing remains elusive.
