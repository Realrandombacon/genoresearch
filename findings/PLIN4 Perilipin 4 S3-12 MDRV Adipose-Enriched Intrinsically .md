## Description
PLIN4 encodes a 1371 aa intrinsically disordered scaffold protein (UniProt Q96Q06), also known as S3-12 or MDRV, that functions as a critical structural component of intracellular lipid droplets (LDs) in adipose tissue, regulating lipid storage, lipolysis, and ferroptotic cell death responses. This well-studied gene (168 publications) has emerging clinical significance for autosomal dominant vacuolar myopathy caused by repeat expansions, male infertility via testicular ferroptosis, and neurodegeneration. The protein features a unique architecture dominated by intrinsic disorder: Perilipin family domain (IPR004279/PF03036/PTHR47538, residues 1156-1365/470-1371) located at the C-terminus providing the conserved PAT (Perilipin/ADRP/TIP47) domain fold; Flagellar protein FliS-like superfamily (G3DSA:1.20.120.340, residues 1198-1353) structurally related to bacterial chaperones; Mannose-6-phosphate receptor binding protein 1 C-terminal domain superfamily (SSF109775, residues 1163-1366) indicating evolutionary relationship to lipid-binding proteins; classified within metabolic proteins with GO terms for lipid droplet organization and lipid storage. STRING interactions reveal PLIN4 operates within high-confidence lipid metabolism networks: LIPE (0.705), hormone-sensitive lipase mediating triglyceride hydrolysis; ADIPOQ (0.691), adiponectin in adipose signaling; ABHD5 (0.652), alpha/beta hydrolase domain containing 5 (CGI-58) activating lipases; CIDEC (0.631)/CIDEA (0.419), cell death-inducing DFFA-like effectors promoting LD fusion; PLIN2 (0.567)/PLIN1 (0.528), paralogous perilipins coating LDs; RAB18 (0.536), small GTPase regulating LD-ER contact; PNPLA2 (0.470), adipose triglyceride lipase (ATGL); TRARG1 (0.454), trafficking regulator. HPA confirms tissue-enhanced expression with massive adipose enrichment (715.6 nTPM), breast (303.4 nTPM), skeletal muscle (235.5 nTPM), detected in many tissues, cancer-enhanced expression, not detected in immune cells, lipid droplet/cytosol/plasma membrane subcellular localization. ClinVar lists 20 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including splice site mutation (c.258+2T>C), synonymous variant (c.1419C>G p.Val473=), extensive copy number gains (19p13.3 3.1-5.5Mb x3, 19p13.3-13.2 0.26-7.2Mb x3, 19p13.3 0.26-4.8Mb x3, 19p13.3-13.2 3.1-9.7Mb x3, 19p13.3-q13.43 0.26-58.9Mb x3), copy number losses (19p13.3 3.5-5.4Mb x1, 19p13.3 3.1-4.8Mb), and critically, recent 2025 studies identify repeat expansions in PLIN4 causing autosomal dominant vacuolar myopathy with sarcolemmal features, establishing a novel pathogenic mechanism. AlphaFold structure prediction yields very low confidence (global pLDDT 34.7), confirming PLIN4 is a highly intrinsically disordered protein (IDP) lacking stable tertiary structure, consistent with its function as a flexible scaffold coating lipid droplet surfaces through amphipathic helix formation upon lipid binding. Mechanistically, PLIN4 operates through integrated modes: (1) Lipid droplet scaffolding—disordered N-terminal region forms protective coat around neutral lipid core, preventing uncontrolled lipase access; (2) Ferroptosis regulation—2024 study (35 citations) demonstrates PLIN4 exacerbates cadmium-induced testosterone deficiency by promoting ferroptosis in testicular Leydig cells; 2025 study shows PLIN4 modulates benzo[a]pyrene-induced neuronal ferroptosis and cognitive decline; (3) Vacuolar myopathy—2025 studies identify repeat expansions causing autophagic vacuolar myopathy, suggesting toxic gain-of-function or aggregation propensity; (4) Lipolysis control—interacts with ABHD5 to regulate PNPLA2/ATGL and LIPE activity, balancing lipid storage vs. mobilization; (5) Adipose inflammation—2025 knockout mouse study reveals elevated adipose inflammation but reduced hepatic triacylglycerol storage; (6) Disorder-mediated function—intrinsically disordered nature allows conformational plasticity for dynamic LD coating and protein-protein interactions. Loss-of-function causes defective lipid storage, increased basal lipolysis, reduced adipose inflammation protection, altered ferroptosis sensitivity, and repeat expansion-associated vacuolar myopathy.
