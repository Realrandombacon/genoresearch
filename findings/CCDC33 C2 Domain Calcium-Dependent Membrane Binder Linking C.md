# CCDC33: C2 Domain Calcium-Dependent Membrane Binder Linking Ciliary Architecture to SWI/SNF Chromatin Remodeling and TGF-β Signaling with Testis-Specific Expression

## Description
CCDC33 (Gene ID: 84277, UniProt Q8N5R6) is a 958 amino acid coiled-coil domain-containing protein located on chromosome 15q24.1 (NC_000015.10). This gene encodes a large scaffolding protein with dual functional modules: an N-terminal coiled-coil domain for protein-protein interactions and a central C2 domain for calcium-dependent membrane binding. InterPro domain analysis reveals modular architecture: C2 domain (IPR000008/PF00168/cd00030/G3DSA:2.60.40.150/SSF49562/PS50004, positions 214-353, ~140 residues forming calcium/lipid-binding module), CCDC33 family (IPR039889/PTHR21623, positions 22-935, ~913 residues spanning nearly the entire protein), CCDC33 N-terminal domain (IPR060125/PF26735, positions 19-194, ~175 residues). The C2 domain is a conserved calcium-dependent membrane-targeting module found in diverse proteins including protein kinase C, phospholipases, synaptotagmins, and rabphilin—mediating transient membrane association upon calcium influx. The extensive coiled-coil region (913 residues) forms elongated α-helical bundles ideal for scaffolding multi-protein complexes. STRING interaction network identifies 10 medium-confidence partners revealing connections to ciliary biology, chromatin remodeling, and TGF-β signaling: UBL7 (0.661 medium confidence, ubiquitin-like protein 7, proteasome regulator), FBF1 (0.617 medium confidence, Fas-binding factor 1, centriolar satellite and ciliary gating protein), SMARCB1 (0.593 medium confidence, SWI/SNF related matrix-associated actin-dependent regulator of chromatin subfamily B member 1, core component of BAF chromatin remodeling complex), DRC7 (0.575 medium confidence, dynein regulatory complex subunit 7, axonemal dynein regulator), C11orf88 (0.550 medium confidence, chromosome 11 open reading frame 88), RIBC2 (0.509 medium confidence, RIB43A domain containing 2, ciliary protein), CFAP157 (0.497 medium confidence, cilia and flagella associated protein 157, axonemal component), CCDC96 (0.473 medium confidence, another coiled-coil ciliary protein), SMAD3 (0.473 medium confidence, SMAD family member 3, TGF-β receptor signal transducer), and ISLR2 (0.470 medium confidence, immunoglobulin superfamily containing leucine-rich repeat 2). The enrichment for ciliary proteins (FBF1, DRC7, RIBC2, CFAP157, CCDC96 = 5 of 10 partners) strongly suggests CCDC33 functions in cilia/flagella assembly or maintenance. FBF1 localizes to centriolar satellites and regulates ciliary gate permeability. DRC7 is part of the nexin-dynein regulatory complex controlling axonemal dynein activity for ciliary beating. Human Protein Atlas classifies CCDC33 (ENSG00000140481) as plasma protein and predicted intracellular protein with group-enriched RNA expression pattern detected in some tissues. Top tissue expression shows testis-enrichment (19.3 nTPM) and fallopian tube expression (17.9 nTPM)—both ciliated epithelia where CCDC33 likely supports ciliogenesis or ciliary function. Cancer-enhanced specificity suggests potential oncogenic role through dysregulated ciliary signaling or chromatin remodeling. Cell type enhanced single cell specificity indicates specialized cellular functions. AlphaFold structure prediction (AF-Q8N5R6-F1) yields HIGH-confidence model (global pLDDT 71.8), indicating a well-folded stable protein across all 958 residues—the high confidence score supports genuine structured coiled-coil bundle with properly folded C2 domain. High-confidence structure enables rational mapping of calcium-binding loops in C2 domain and coiled-coil heptad repeats for protein interaction interfaces. ClinVar lists 36 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large chromosomal copy number variations spanning chromosome 15q regions (chr15:73506509-102429112x3 gain, chr15:72943184-76085232 loss, chr15:67358491-91644328x3 gain, chr15:74353736-77884397x1 deletion, chr15:74398068-76054094 loss, chr15:74398162-76054094x1 deletion, chr15:72943184-75567198x1 deletion, chr15:72943184-75544524x1 deletion); one SNV in neighboring STRA6 gene (NM_001199040.2:c.45G>A/p.Trp15Ter) suggesting 15q24 microdeletion syndrome region; no specific monogenic disease is attributed solely to CCDC33, suggesting dosage sensitivity within larger 15q chromosomal syndromes affecting development and cognition. Conservation is evidenced by orthologs across vertebrates: Mus musculus Ccdc33 (Q3ULW6, 985 aa)—slightly longer mouse ortholog suggests conserved function with minor sequence divergence. Functional hypothesis: CCDC33 functions as a calcium-regulated scaffolding protein coordinating ciliary assembly, chromatin remodeling, and TGF-β signal transduction through modular domain architecture. The C2 domain (residues 214-353) binds calcium ions through conserved aspartate residues in three loops, triggering conformational change that exposes hydrophobic residues for membrane insertion—enabling rapid recruitment to ciliary base, centriolar satellites, or plasma membrane upon calcium signaling. The extensive coiled-coil region (residues 22-935) forms parallel or antiparallel α-helical bundles providing multiple interaction surfaces for partner proteins. FBF1 interaction (0.617) suggests CCDC33 localizes to centriolar satellites and regulates ciliary gate composition—critical for controlling protein entry into ciliary compartment. DRC7 (0.575), CFAP157 (0.497), and CCDC96 (0.473) interactions place CCDC33 within axonemal dynein regulatory complex, potentially modulating ciliary beating frequency or waveform. SMARCB1 interaction (0.593) unexpectedly links CCDC33 to SWI/SNF (BAF) chromatin remodeling complex—suggesting nuclear function in transcriptional regulation, possibly shuttling between cytoplasm and nucleus like many ciliary proteins (inverse relationship between ciliogenesis and cell cycle). SMAD3 interaction (0.473) connects CCDC33 to TGF-β signaling pathway—SMAD3 translocates to nucleus upon TGF-β receptor activation to regulate transcription of target genes controlling cell proliferation, differentiation, and fibrosis. UBL7 interaction (0.661) suggests proteasomal regulation or ubiquitin-like modification. RIBC2 interaction (0.509) extends ciliary connections—RIBC2 is involved in ciliary vesicle trafficking. Mechanistically, CCDC33 may function as: (1) Calcium-sensitive ciliary scaffold recruiting dynein regulatory components to axoneme upon calcium influx; (2) Centriolar satellite organizer controlling ciliary gate permeability and cargo selection; (3) Nuclear-cytoplasmic shuttle coupling ciliary status to chromatin remodeling via SMARCB1; (4) TGF-β pathway modulator through SMAD3 interaction, potentially regulating fibrotic responses; (5) Testis-specific ciliary regulator supporting spermatogenesis (sperm flagella are modified cilia). Testis (19.3 nTPM) and fallopian tube (17.9 nTPM) expression aligns perfectly with ciliary function hypothesis—both tissues contain abundant motile cilia/flagella. Disease mechanism: CCDC33 haploinsufficiency or overexpression from chromosome 15q24 CNVs may contribute to 15q24 microdeletion/duplication syndrome phenotypes including intellectual disability, growth abnormalities, craniofacial defects, and genital anomalies. The 36 ClinVar variants suggest dosage sensitivity; 15q24 deletions cause developmental delay, immune deficiency, and recurrent infections. CCDC33 may contribute through disrupted ciliogenesis (causing ciliopathy features like situs inversus, hydrocephalus, retinal degeneration), aberrant chromatin remodeling (altering neurodevelopmental gene expression via SMARCB1), or impaired TGF-β signaling (causing fibrotic or proliferative disorders). Cancer-enhanced expression suggests oncogenic potential through dysregulated ciliary signaling (cilia suppress Hedgehog signaling in many cancers) or chromatin remodeling. Testis-specific expression suggests male infertility phenotype if disrupted. Priority experiments include immunofluorescence colocalization with ciliary markers (acetylated tubulin, ARL13B, γ-tubulin), centriolar satellite markers (PCM1, CEP290), and nuclear markers; co-IP validation of FBF1, DRC7, SMARCB1, SMAD3 interactions under calcium-replete vs calcium-depleted conditions; investigation of CCDC33 role in ciliogenesis using serum starvation assays and ciliary length measurements; assessment of CCDC33 effect on ciliary beating frequency using high-speed video microscopy; CRISPR knockout to assess ciliary, chromatin, and TGF-β signaling phenotypes; structural characterization leveraging AlphaFold model (pLDDT 71.8) to map calcium-binding loops and coiled-coil interaction interfaces; analysis of CCDC33 expression in ciliopathy patient cells and testis/fallopian tube tumors; investigation of CCDC33 nucleocytoplasmic shuttling in response to calcium signals and TGF-β stimulation.
