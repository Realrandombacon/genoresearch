## Description
DCAF6 (DDB1- and CUL4-associated factor 6, UniProt Q58WW2) encodes an 860 aa nuclear protein functioning as a substrate recognition receptor for the CUL4-DDB1 E3 ubiquitin ligase complex, linking ubiquitination machinery to nuclear receptor signaling and calcium-dependent regulation. InterPro analysis reveals a complex domain architecture dominated by WD40 repeats forming a beta-propeller structure for protein-protein interactions: three canonical WD40 repeats (IPR001680/PF00400/SM00320 at residues 40-79, 82-124, 130-170) providing the DDB1-binding interface, extensive WD40/YVTN repeat-like superfamily coverage (IPR015943/IPR036322/G3DSA:2.130.10.10 spanning residues 34-314 and 623-807) creating the substrate-binding surface, and a distinctive IQ motif (PS50096, residues 676-703) suggesting calmodulin binding and calcium-dependent regulation unique among DCAF family members. The DCAF8-like family assignment (IPR045151, residues 8-853) confirms its role as a specialized adaptor. AlphaFold predicts a medium confidence structure (pLDDT 63.4) consistent with a rigid WD40 beta-propeller core surrounded by flexible linkers and disordered regions typical of dynamic E3 ligase adaptors. STRING interactions define DCAF6 as an integral component of the CUL4-RING ligase (CRL4) network with maximum confidence links to scaffold proteins CUL4A (0.989), CUL4B (0.969), and DDB1 (0.973), plus strong associations with other DCAF receptors (DCAF4 0.943, DCAF10 0.936, DCAF12 0.932, DCAF1 0.932, DCAF5 0.925) and DNA damage sensor DDB2 (0.925), indicating functional redundancy and shared substrate pools within the CRL4 system; the link to TRPC4AP (0.900) suggests potential crosstalk with calcium signaling pathways. HPA classifies DCAF6 as group-enriched with highest expression in skeletal muscle (585.8 nTPM) and tongue (429.2 nTPM), localized to nucleoplasm and cytosol, involved in Ubl conjugation pathway, reflecting dual roles in nuclear transcription regulation and cytoplasmic signaling. ClinVar lists 40 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number gains (1q21.1-25.3 chr1:143550435-185515177x3, 1q21.1-44 chr1:143932350-249224684x3) and losses (1q24.2-31.1 chr1:167994071-187711459x1) alongside specific mutations like p.Gly374Glu and splice variants, though no single-gene disorder is definitively assigned suggesting contribution to 1q duplication/deletion syndromes or complex traits. Functional hypothesis: DCAF6 operates through a multi-step mechanism: (1) Complex assembly - WD40 propeller binds DDB1 anchoring DCAF6 to CUL4A/B scaffolds forming active CRL4(DCAF6) E3 ligase; (2) Substrate recruitment - Variable loops on WD40 blade surfaces recognize specific degrons on target proteins, potentially including nuclear receptors (GR, AR) as suggested by gene info coactivator function; (3) Calcium modulation - IQ motif (676-703) binds calmodulin in response to Ca2+ signals, potentially altering substrate affinity or complex stability linking cellular calcium status to ubiquitination rates; (4) Ubiquitin transfer - CUL4-bound E2 enzyme transfers ubiquitin to recruited substrates marking them for proteasomal degradation; (5) Transcriptional control - By regulating turnover of nuclear receptors or co-regulators, DCAF6 modulates glucocorticoid and androgen signaling pathways critical for stress response, metabolism, and development; (6) Tissue specificity - High muscle expression suggests specialized role in myogenesis, muscle maintenance, or response to exercise-induced calcium fluxes. Disease mechanisms may involve: (a) Haploinsufficiency from deletions reducing CRL4 activity leading to accumulation of toxic substrates or dysregulated nuclear receptor signaling; (b) Dosage effects from duplications causing hyper-ubiquitination and depletion of essential factors; (c) Disrupted calcium sensing via IQ motif mutations uncoupling ubiquitination from physiological signals; (d) Contribution to 1q syndromes featuring developmental delay, growth defects, and potential endocrine abnormalities. Conservation extends to mouse (Q9DC22, 876 aa) and orangutan (Q5R9B8, 860 aa) indicating preserved function across mammals. Dark aspects requiring investigation include: specific substrate identity beyond hypothetical nuclear receptors, structural basis of IQ motif-calmodulin interaction and its allosteric effect on WD40 substrate binding, tissue-specific interactome differences between muscle and other tissues, role in muscle wasting diseases or cachexia, potential involvement in prostate cancer via androgen receptor regulation, crosstalk with DNA damage response through DDB2 interaction, and therapeutic potential of targeting DCAF6-substrate interface in hormone-dependent cancers.
