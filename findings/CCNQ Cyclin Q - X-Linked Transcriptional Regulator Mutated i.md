## Description
CCNQ (Cyclin Q, UniProt Q8N1B3) is a 248 amino acid nuclear/cytosolic protein functioning as a cyclin-family transcriptional regulator. With only 12 literature references (genuine dark gene status), CCNQ has emerging roles in X-linked STAR syndrome (syndactyly, telecanthus, anogenital and renal malformations), a rare ciliopathy. The protein contains two canonical cyclin box domains: first cyclin box (IPR048055, cd20534, aa 26-135) and second cyclin box (IPR048055, cd20535, aa 140-243), characteristic of cyclin-fold proteins that regulate cyclin-dependent kinases (CDKs). AlphaFold predicts a well-folded structure (pLDDT 87.9, high confidence) across all 248 residues, indicating stable cyclin-fold architecture. STRING interactions reveal high-confidence partnerships with CDK10 (0.841 - cyclin-dependent kinase 10), SALL1 (0.815 - spalt-like transcription factor 1), SALL4 (0.784 - spalt-like transcription factor 4), CDK13 (0.672), CDK12 (0.670), and CDK9 (0.553), confirming integration into transcriptional regulation networks. HPA shows ubiquitous expression across all tissues with nucleoplasm and cytosol localization, classified as an essential protein with cyclin molecular function. ClinVar documents 221 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number losses/gains across Xq26.3-q28, splice site mutations (c.429+1G>T), and in-frame deletions (c.296+9_296+20del), strongly implicating CCNQ in human disease. The 2022 discovery identified CCNQ tail extension variants causing STAR syndrome, establishing CCNQ as an X-linked dominant ciliopathy gene. 2025 literature broadens the STAR syndrome phenotypic spectrum to include toe polydactyly and supernumerary nipple. Functional hypothesis: CCNQ functions as a non-canonical cyclin that partners with CDK10/CDK12/CDK13 to regulate RNA polymerase II-dependent transcription, potentially through interaction with SALL1/SALL4 transcription factors during development. Unlike canonical cell cycle cyclins, CCNQ's essential status and ciliopathy phenotype suggest roles in transcriptional programs governing ciliogenesis, renal development, and limb patterning. The cyclin box domains likely mediate CDK binding and activation, while the C-terminal region may provide specificity for developmental transcription complexes. The 221 ClinVar variants underscore CCNQ's critical role in human development, with loss-of-function mutations disrupting transcriptional regulation of ciliary and developmental genes.
