# Histone H4 Variant H4C16: Replication-Dependent Core Histone with Intellectual Disability Associations and Immune Cell-Enhanced Expression

## Description
H4C16 (Histone H4 Cluster 16) is a 103-amino acid core histone protein with only 2 publications confirming its very dark gene status despite encoding a fundamental chromatin component. The protein contains highly conserved histone H4 domains including histone H4 family domain (IPR001951, positions 1-102) that mediates DNA binding and structural constituent of chromatin functions, histone-fold domain (IPR009072, G3DSA:1.10.20.10, positions 2-103) enabling protein heterodimerization activity and nucleosome assembly, TATA box binding protein associated factor (TAF) histone-like fold domain (IPR004823, positions 28-93), CENP-T/Histone H4 histone fold domain (IPR035425, PF15511, positions 44-96), and conserved histone H4 signature (IPR019809, PS00047, positions 15-19). AlphaFold predicts a well-folded structure (pLDDT 89.8), consistent with the canonical histone fold architecture required for nucleosome core particle assembly. HPA reveals nucleoplasm localization, DNA-binding molecular function, disease involvement (disease variant, intellectual disability), low tissue specificity with detected-in-some distribution, immune cell-enhanced blood cell specificity, and cell type-enhanced single cell specificity. ClinVar documents 40 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants (primarily chromosome 12p13.33-11.1/12p13.2-12.1 copy number gains and losses), though specific disease phenotypes beyond intellectual disability remain uncharacterized. STRING interactions returned no partners, typical for core histones where interaction networks are defined by nucleosome structure rather than dynamic protein-protein interactions. The 2 literature papers (2025) focus on 6PPD-Quinone toxicogenomics in Caco-2/HepG2 cells and nafamostat mesylate in exertional heat stroke - neither directly characterizes H4C16 function. We hypothesize H4C16 functions as a replication-dependent core histone H4 variant that incorporates into nucleosomes during S-phase DNA replication, forming the (H3-H4)2 tetramer that serves as the foundation for nucleosome assembly with H2A-H2B dimers. The histone-fold domain mediates H4-H3 heterodimerization and subsequent tetramerization, while the N-terminal tail (positions 1-21, not captured in structured domains) undergoes post-translational modifications (acetylation, methylation) regulating chromatin accessibility. Immune cell-enhanced expression suggests H4C16 may have specialized roles in rapidly proliferating immune cells during clonal expansion. The 40 ClinVar variants and intellectual disability association indicate H4C16 dosage sensitivity - copy number alterations disrupting histone stoichiometry likely cause chromatin assembly defects affecting neurodevelopment. H4C16 represents one of ~15-20 human H4 variants that are nearly identical at the protein level but differ in genomic location and expression timing, with most variants remaining functionally uncharacterized despite their essential role in epigenetic inheritance.
