## Description
MARCHF8 encodes a 291 aa membrane-associated RING-CH-type E3 ubiquitin-protein ligase (UniProt Q5T0T0) located at 10q11.21, functioning as a critical regulator of immune surface protein turnover and antiviral defense. InterPro analysis reveals extensive RING-CH finger domain architecture: (1) RING-CH finger H2 subclass C4HC3-type (cd16807, aa 73-136); (2) RING-type zinc finger (IPR001841, aa 80-127); (3) RING-CH-type zinc finger (IPR011016, aa 72-133) with zinc ion binding (GO:0008270); (4) RING/FYVE/PHD-type superfamily (IPR013083, aa 64-142); (5) RING-variant domain (PF12906, aa 80-126); (6) MARCHF family (PTHR45981, aa 79-264); (7) SMART RING-variant domain (SM00744, aa 79-127) with demonstrated E3 ligase activity. AlphaFold predicts a confident structure (pLDDT 70.1), indicating a well-folded E3 ligase with stable RING-CH catalytic core. STRING interactions reveal immune-focused network: STAP2 (0.653, signal transducing adaptor), HLA-DPA1 (0.548, MHC class II antigen), UBE2H (0.491, E2 ubiquitin-conjugating enzyme), TFRC (0.448, transferrin receptor), UBE2D1 (0.433, E2 enzyme), CDC34 (0.426, cell division cycle 34), BST2 (0.426, bone marrow stromal cell antigen 2/tetherin), CD86 (0.424, T-cell costimulatory molecule), TNFRSF10A (0.414, TRAIL receptor). HPA has no expression data, suggesting low abundance or tissue-restricted expression in immune cells. ClinVar reports 9 pathogenic variants, all chromosome 10q copy number variations (gains/losses/inversions), though specific disease phenotypes remain undefined. Literature (25 papers) reveals: (1) miR-34a-5p/MARCHF8/ADAM10 axis regulating vascular endothelial dysfunction and senescence (2025, 6 citations)—linking MARCHF8 to vascular aging; (2) MARCHF8-mediated TGFBI ubiquitination regulating NF-κB inflammatory responses and ECM degradation in intervertebral disc degeneration (2025, 3 citations); (3) MARCHF8 restricts HSV-1 infection by inhibiting viral genome replication (2025); (4) BST-2 promotes viral N protein degradation through MARCHF8/NDP52 autophagy pathway in SADS-CoV infection. Mechanistically, MARCHF8 functions through: (1) E3 ubiquitin ligase activity—transferring ubiquitin from E2 enzymes (UBE2H, UBE2D1) to target membrane proteins; (2) MHC-II downregulation—ubiquitinating HLA-DPA1/HLA-DQA2 to modulate antigen presentation; (3) Viral restriction—targeting viral proteins for autophagic degradation via BST2/NDP52 pathway; (4) Immune checkpoint regulation—modulating CD86 costimulatory molecule surface expression; (5) Receptor trafficking—ubiquitinating TFRC and TNFRSF10A for endosomal sorting. In disease, MARCHF8 dysfunction contributes to: vascular aging and endothelial dysfunction via ADAM10 dysregulation; intervertebral disc degeneration through TGFBI/NF-κB pathway; viral susceptibility (HSV-1, coronaviruses) when restriction activity is impaired; potential autoimmune disorders via MHC-II dysregulation. The 10q CNVs suggest dosage sensitivity, with both gains and losses potentially pathogenic.
