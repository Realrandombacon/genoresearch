## Description
POFUT4 (UniProt Q495W5), also known as FUT11 or FUCTXI, encodes a 492-amino acid type II transmembrane glycosyltransferase belonging to the glycosyltransferase family 10 (IPR001503/PTHR11929, aa 65-446) with metazoan alpha-(1,3)-fucosyltransferase specificity (IPR017176/PIRSF037332, aa 10-490). The enzyme contains an N-terminal fucosyltransferase domain (IPR031481/PF17039, aa 77-178) and C-terminal catalytic GT10 domain (IPR038577/IPR055270/PF00852/G3DSA:3.40.50.11660, aa 149-360) within the UDP-glycosyltransferase/glycogen phosphorylase superfamily (SSF53756). AlphaFold predicts a well-folded structure (pLDDT: 85.3), consistent with stable Golgi enzyme architecture required for GDP-fucose binding and substrate recognition.\n\nSTRING interactions returned no results, suggesting POFUT4 remains poorly characterized in interaction databases. HPA confirms ubiquitous expression (low tissue specificity, detected in all tissues) with dual Golgi apparatus and nuclear membrane localization, classified as metabolic protein with glycosyltransferase activity (GO:0008417, GO:0046920).\n\nPOFUT4 catalyzes the transfer of fucose from GDP-fucose to specific acceptor substrates in the Golgi, contributing to N-glycan core fucosylation—a critical post-translational modification affecting protein stability, cell adhesion, and immune recognition. ClinVar contains 13 pathogenic variants (10q22.2 CNVs including losses and gains at 10q21.3-23.32). Gene annotations implicate POFUT4 in stomach cancer pathogenesis as a diagnostic biomarker, suggesting dysregulated fucosylation contributes to gastric tumor progression. This positions POFUT4 as an understudied fucosyltransferase with potential oncogenic function through aberrant glycosylation patterns.
