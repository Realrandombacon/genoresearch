## Description
PTRH2 (Peptidyl-tRNA Hydrolase 2, also known as Bit1 - Bcl-2 Interacting killer) is a 179 amino acid mitochondrial enzyme that hydrolyzes peptidyl-tRNAs released during premature translation termination, serving as a critical quality control mechanism linking mitochondrial protein synthesis to apoptotic signaling. InterPro analysis identifies a canonical PTH2 catalytic domain (IPR002833, PF01981, residues 65-179; PTHR12649, residues 5-179) with peptidyl-tRNA hydrolase activity (GO:0004045), classified within the Bit1 homologous superfamily (G3DSA:3.40.1490.10, SSF102462, residues 63-179) and featuring a prokaryotic membrane lipoprotein lipid attachment site (PS51257, residues 1-25) consistent with mitochondrial import. AlphaFold structure (AF-Q9Y3E5-F1) predicts high confidence (pLDDT 83.9) across the entire 179 residue sequence, indicating a stable folded enzyme suitable for substrate binding and catalysis. HPA data classifies PTRH2 as a mitochondrial enzyme with low tissue specificity (detected in all tissues), involved in apoptosis, and categorized as a disease-related gene with potential as a drug target. STRING interactions reveal a functional network centered on mitochondrial integrity and cell death regulation: strong association with TLE5 (Transducin-Like Enhancer 5, 0.893), TMEM109 (0.793), and GATB (Glutamyl-tRNA Amidotransferase subunit B, 0.793) linking to mitochondrial translation; connections to USP30 (0.582), a mitochondrial deubiquitinase regulating mitophagy; UBQLN1/2 (0.492-0.553), ubiquitin-like proteins mediating proteasomal degradation; FKBP8 (0.529) and BCL2 (0.527), anti-apoptotic regulators; and VDAC2 (0.488), a voltage-dependent anion channel controlling mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilization. ClinVar lists 26 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including nonsense mutations (p.Arg34Ter, p.Trp31Ter), frameshift (p.Ser43fs), missense (p.Lys81Arg, p.Val23Ala), and large copy number losses/deletions on chromosome 17q22-23.2, causing Infantile-onset Multisystem Neurologic, Endocrine, and Pancreatic Disease (IMNEPD), a rare autosomal recessive disorder featuring microcephaly, intellectual disability, exocrine pancreatic dysfunction, and endocrine abnormalities. Literature (50 papers) establishes PTRH2/Bit1 as a dual-function protein: canonically recycling stalled peptidyl-tRNAs in mitochondria to maintain translation fidelity, and alternatively functioning as an apoptotic sensor that translocates to the nucleus upon anoikis (loss of cell adhesion) to activate caspase-independent cell death. Recent work (2025) demonstrates PTRH2 controls the deubiquitinase TRABID to regulate mt-ND5 stability and oxidative metabolism, positioning it as a master regulator of mitochondrial respiratory chain integrity. As a dark gene with established disease causality but incompletely characterized mechanisms, PTRH2 represents a critical node connecting translation quality control, metabolic homeostasis, and programmed cell death with therapeutic potential for neurodevelopmental disorders and cancer.
