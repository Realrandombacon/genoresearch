## Description
SNX11 (Sorting Nexin 11) is a 270 amino acid endosomal protein critical for lysosomal degradation, endosome homeostasis, and viral entry mechanisms. InterPro analysis identifies a canonical Phox Homology (PX) domain (IPR001683, PF00787, SM00312) spanning residues 14-132, which mediates specific binding to phosphatidylinositol 3,5-bisphosphate [PI(3,5)P2], a lipid essential for late endosome function. The protein belongs to the SNX10/11 family (IPR043544) and contains a PX domain superfamily fold (G3DSA:3.30.1520.10, SSF64268). AlphaFold structure (AF-Q9Y5W9-F1) predicts a confident fold (pLDDT 75.1) across the full 270 residues, consistent with a stable PX domain architecture. HPA data shows ubiquitous expression with enrichment in specific cell types, localizing to the endoplasmic reticulum and vesicles. STRING interactions reveal strong functional coupling to the Retromer complex (VPS35 score 0.641, VPS26B 0.584, VPS29 0.557, VPS26A 0.418) and other sorting nexins (SNX21 0.532, SNX32 0.503, SNX22 0.472), suggesting a role in retrograde transport from endosomes to the Golgi or plasma membrane. Recent literature (20 papers total) highlights SNX11 as an essential host factor for Dabie bandavirus (SFTS virus) infection, where it facilitates V-ATPase assembly required for viral entry. ClinVar lists 10 pathogenic variants, primarily large copy number losses and gains on chromosome 17, indicating potential haploinsufficiency or dosage sensitivity, though specific monogenic diseases remain undefined. Mechanistically, SNX11 likely acts as a membrane tether that recruits PI(3,5)P2-rich endosomal domains to assemble V-ATPase complexes, thereby regulating endosomal acidification and cargo sorting.
