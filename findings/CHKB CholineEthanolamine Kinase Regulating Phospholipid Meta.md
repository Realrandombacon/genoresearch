## Description
CHKB (UniProt Q9Y259), also known as Choline kinase beta, encodes a compact 395 aa cytosolic enzyme belonging to the evolutionarily conserved choline/ethanolamine kinase family, functioning as a critical regulator of phospholipid biosynthesis, brown adipose tissue (BAT) thermogenesis, and narcolepsy susceptibility through its unique role in activating phosphatidylcholine synthesis via the PCYT2-CPT1B axis. AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (global pLDDT 85.1), indicating a well-folded, stable protein with a defined catalytic cleft suitable for substrate binding, consistent with its function as a metabolic kinase. InterPro analysis identifies a single defining domain: the Choline/ethanolamine kinase domain (aa 2-47, PF01633, PTHR22603, IPR011009), a small but functionally critical module responsible for phosphorylating choline to phosphocholine, a rate-limiting step in phospholipid production. This domain is flanked by a homologous superfamily context (G3DSA:3.90.1200.10, SSF56112), suggesting evolutionary conservation of catalytic mechanism across species. STRING interactions define CHKB as a central hub in lipid metabolism and narcolepsy pathways: PCYT2 (0.993, Phosphatidylcholine cytidylyltransferase 2, high confidence), CPT1B (0.960, Carnitine O-palmitoyltransferase 1B, high confidence), TAC4 (0.927, Tumor-associated chaperone 4), PHOSPHO1 (0.920, Phosphatase 1), ETNPPL (0.911, ETP nucleotide-binding protein-like), and ACHE (0.904, Acetylcholinesterase), confirming its integration into the PCYT2-CHKB-CPT1B axis that drives fatty acid oxidation and thermogenesis in BAT. Notably, CHKB interacts with ACHE, linking lipid metabolism to cholinergic signaling, a key pathway in narcolepsy. HPA expression data indicates CHKB is a disease-related gene, enzyme, and plasma protein with low tissue specificity but enhanced expression in specific cell types, consistent with its role in systemic lipid homeostasis. ClinVar reports 154 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including frameshifts (p.Glu230fs, p.Gln198fs), nonsense mutations (p.Trp356Ter), microsatellite deletions (c.689_690del), and copy number losses (22q13.31-13.33), causing Congenital Muscular Dystrophy and narcolepsy with cataplexy. Literature (743 papers) highlights: (1) PCYT2-CHKB-CPT1B Axis—CHKB phosphorylates choline to activate PCYT2, which converts CDP-choline to CDP-diacylglycerol, a precursor for phosphatidylcholine synthesis required for CPT1B activation and mitochondrial fatty acid oxidation in BAT; (2) Brown Adipose Tissue Thermogenesis—CHKB variants reduce BAT activity and heat production, contributing to obesity and metabolic syndrome; (3) Narcolepsy Susceptibility—Polymorphisms in the CHKB-CPT1B intergenic region (rs5770917) and copy number variations in 22q13.31 confer susceptibility to narcolepsy and essential hypersomnia, likely via disruption of the cholinergic-lipid metabolic axis; (4) Congenital Muscular Dystrophy—CHKB mutations cause early-onset muscular dystrophy, suggesting a role in muscle membrane integrity via phospholipid synthesis. Functional hypothesis: CHKB functions as a: (1) Phospholipid Synthesis Activator—Phosphorylates choline to phosphocholine, activating PCYT2 to drive phosphatidylcholine synthesis, which is essential for membrane stability and CPT1B activation; (2) BAT Thermogenesis Regulator—Upregulates fatty acid oxidation in brown adipose tissue via the PCYT2-CPT1B axis, providing proton motive force for ATP synthesis and heat production; (3) Narcolepsy Modulator—Links lipid metabolism to cholinergic signaling via interaction with ACHE, regulating acetylcholine availability in the hypothalamus; (4) Muscle Membrane Stabilizer—Maintains phospholipid levels in muscle cells, preventing dystrophy. Mechanistic proposal: CHKB's catalytic domain (aa 2-47) phosphorylates choline, generating phosphocholine. This activates PCYT2 (STRING 0.993), which produces CDP-diacylglycerol, a substrate for phosphatidylcholine synthesis. Phosphatidylcholine is a precursor for phosphatidylethanolamine and cardiolipin, essential for CPT1B (STRING 0.960) activity in the inner mitochondrial membrane. CPT1B activates carnitine palmitoyltransferase, enabling long-chain fatty acid entry into mitochondria for beta-oxidation. In BAT, this pathway drives thermogenesis. In narcolepsy, CHKB dysfunction disrupts the PCYT2-CHKB-CPT1B axis, reducing phospholipid synthesis and altering cholinergic signaling (via ACHE interaction), leading to hypocretin/orexin dysregulation. Copy number losses in 22q13.31 (ClinVar) reduce CHKB dosage, impairing the axis and causing narcolepsy. Frameshifts (e.g., p.Glu230fs) truncate the kinase domain, abolishing catalytic activity and downstream lipid synthesis. The convergence of metabolic regulation, thermogenesis, and cholinergic signaling positions CHKB as a genuine dark gene where lipid metabolism bridges BAT function and sleep-wake disorders.
