## Description
MGAM2 (Maltase-glucoamylase 2, putative) is a giant 2515 amino acid membrane-associated enzyme (UniProt Q2M2H8) with high-confidence well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 74.8), characteristic of glycoside hydrolase family 31 (GH31) proteins that catalyze alpha-1,4-glucosidase activity in carbohydrate metabolism. The protein contains multiple glycoside hydrolase family 31 TIM barrel domains (IPR000322/PF01174/G3DSA:3.20.20.80/cd06602, residues 325-675, 1192-1576, 323-680, 1190-1581) that form the catalytic core for glucosidase activity, N-terminal GH31 domains (cd14752/G3DSA:2.60.40.1760, residues 207-323, 1076-1190) involved in substrate binding, P-type trefoil domains (IPR000519/cd00111/G3DSA:4.10.110.10, residues 41-91, 900-953, 42-88, 907-948) that may mediate protein-protein interactions or substrate recognition, Golgi alpha-mannosidase II-like domains (G3DSA:2.60.40.1180, residues 669-766, 767-906, 1569-1667), and belongs to the glycosyl hydrolase superfamily enabling hydrolase activity hydrolyzing O-glycosyl compounds (GO:0004553) and carbohydrate metabolic process (GO:0005975). MGAM2 is located on chromosome 7q34 and is classified as an enzyme and predicted membrane protein with subcellular localization to vesicles and cytosol.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals an exceptionally high-confidence network of carbohydrate metabolism enzymes: AMY2A (0.998, Alpha-amylase 2A, salivary starch digestion), MGAM (0.985, Maltase-glucoamylase, intestinal brush border enzyme), AMY2B (0.960, Alpha-amylase 2B, pancreatic starch digestion), GBE1 (0.942, Glycogen branching enzyme 1, glycogen synthesis), AGL (0.939, Amylo-1,6-glucosidase/4-alpha-glucanotransferase, glycogen debranching), AMY1B (0.901, Alpha-amylase 1B, salivary), PYGB (0.680, Glycogen phosphorylase B, glycogen breakdown), PYGM (0.675, Glycogen phosphorylase M, muscle), PYGL (0.668, Glycogen phosphorylase L, liver), and SI (0.462, Sucrase-isomaltase, intestinal disaccharidase). These interactions confirm MGAM2 functions within the carbohydrate digestion and glycogen metabolism pathway, with MGAM (0.985) as the closest paralog suggesting functional redundancy or cooperation in intestinal maltose hydrolysis.\n\nHPA expression reveals MGAM2 has tissue-enhanced expression detected in some tissues, with top expression in salivary gland (8.1 nTPM), intestine (5.5 nTPM), and pancreas (3.8 nTPM). This digestive tissue enrichment pattern is consistent with roles in dietary carbohydrate digestion and absorption. The protein is classified as an enzyme and predicted membrane protein. Group-enriched cancer specificity and cell type-enhanced single cell specificity suggest specialized functions in secretory epithelial cells. Not detected in immune cells indicates MGAM2 is not involved in immune function.\n\nClinVar documents 21 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including duplications (single allele) and extensive copy number gains (7q34-36.3, 7q33-36.3, 7q32.3-36.3, 7q31.2-36.3, 7q31.32-36.3 amplifications) and copy number losses (7q33-36.1 deletion). The variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity. While no specific Mendelian disease is annotated, 7q CNVs are associated with developmental delay, growth abnormalities, and metabolic disorders. Given MGAM2's glycosidase function and digestive tissue expression, loss-of-function may cause carbohydrate malabsorption, congenital sucrase-isomaltase deficiency-like phenotypes, or glycogen storage disease variants through impaired starch/maltose hydrolysis.\n\nConservation is present across mammals: human (Q2M2H8, 2515 aa), mouse (A0A6P5PWS0, 2129 aa sucrase-isomaltase), beaver (A0A8C0ZX56, 1705 aa; A0A8C0ZXI2, 2347 aa), marmot (A0A8C5Z9M5, 1742 aa), indicating the GH31 domain architecture is conserved in mammals with species-specific length variation.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: MGAM2 functions as an alpha-1,4-glucosidase that hydrolyzes terminal non-reducing alpha-1,4-linked glucose residues in maltose, maltotriose, and alpha-limit dextrins during carbohydrate digestion. The high-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 74.8) indicates a stable multi-domain architecture with two catalytic GH31 TIM barrel domains (residues 325-675, 1192-1576) that each contain the catalytic nucleophile and acid/base residues characteristic of GH31 enzymes. The trefoil domains (residues 41-91, 900-953) may mediate dimerization or substrate channeling. Through MGAM interaction (0.985), MGAM2 may form heterodimers or function redundantly with intestinal maltase-glucoamylase. AMY2A/AMY2B/AMY1B interactions (0.998/0.960/0.901) link MGAM2 to the amylase cascade for starch breakdown. GBE1 and AGL interactions (0.942/0.939) connect MGAM2 to glycogen metabolism. SI interaction (0.462) suggests cooperation with sucrase-isomaltase at the intestinal brush border. Digestive tissue expression (salivary gland 8.1 nTPM, intestine 5.5 nTPM, pancreas 3.8 nTPM) indicates MGAM2 functions in both oral and intestinal carbohydrate digestion. 7q CNVs cause MGAM2 dosage imbalance, potentially disrupting the stoichiometry of digestive enzyme complexes and causing carbohydrate malabsorption. MGAM2 represents a candidate gene for congenital carbohydrate digestion disorders and may contribute to glycogen storage disease type XIV or related metabolic phenotypes through impaired alpha-glucosidase activity.
