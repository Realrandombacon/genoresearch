## Description
SARM1 (Sterile Alpha and TIR Motif Containing 1; UniProt Q6SZW1) is a 724 amino acid mitochondrial and cytosolic protein with emerging but still limited mechanistic characterization in specific disease contexts, representing a PARTIALLY CHARACTERIZED gene encoding the central executioner of axon degeneration (Wallerian degeneration) and a potent regulator of innate immunity. Unlike canonical Toll-like receptor adaptors that signal via NF-kappaB activation, SARM1 uniquely functions as an NAD+ hydrolase (NADase) whose TIR domain catalyzes the cleavage of NAD+ into nicotinamide and ADP-ribose, triggering catastrophic metabolic collapse in neurons and modulating inflammatory responses in immune cells through a tightly regulated autoinhibition mechanism involving its N-terminal Armadillo repeats and central SAM domains.\n\nSTRUCTURAL ARCHITECTURE: InterPro analysis reveals SARM1 contains a sophisticated multi-domain architecture designed for autoinhibition and rapid activation: (1) N-terminal Armadillo-type fold (IPR016024/IPR011989/G3DSA:1.25.10.10/cd24153, residues 94-399/97-305, ~300 aa) — extended solenoid structure mediating autoinhibitory interactions with the C-terminal TIR domain; (2) Central tandem Sterile Alpha Motif (SAM) domains (IPR001660/IPR013761/G3DSA:1.10.150.50, Repeat 1: residues 409-477; Repeat 2: residues 478-547; ~140 aa total) — protein-protein interaction modules facilitating oligomerization and signal propagation; (3) C-terminal Toll/Interleukin-1 Receptor (TIR) domain (IPR000157/G3DSA:3.40.50.10140, residues 553-703/560-703, ~140-150 aa) — catalytic NADase domain responsible for enzymatic activity and downstream signaling; (4) Full-length family (PTHR11358) defines SARM1-like proteins across metazoans.
