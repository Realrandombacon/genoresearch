## Description
LOC101929767 is an uncharacterized genetic locus on chromosome 17q21.31 (NC_000017.11, positions 43170402-43148365, ~22 kb region) with clinical significance despite lacking protein-coding features. No UniProt accession exists, preventing InterPro domain analysis and AlphaFold structure prediction. No mRNA transcript was identified in NCBI nucleotide databases, and Human Protein Atlas could not resolve the gene to an Ensembl ID, indicating no detectable protein expression. STRING-DB detected no protein-protein interactions. However, ClinVar contains 3 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all representing large-scale copy number gains spanning the 17q region (variants include 148363, 58696, 57282). These duplications range from focal amplifications (~30 Mb) to whole-arm changes (38+ Mb). The 17q21.31 chromosomal location is clinically notable as this region is associated with 17q21.31 microdeletion syndrome (Koolen-de Vries syndrome, characterized by intellectual disability, hypotonia, friendly behavior, and dysmorphic features) and 17q21.31 duplication syndrome. The region contains multiple dosage-sensitive genes including KANSL1 (KAT8 regulatory NSL complex subunit 1, chromatin modification, haploinsufficient), MAPT (microtubule-associated protein tau, neurodegeneration), CRHR1 (corticotropin-releasing hormone receptor 1, stress response), and STH (KCNJ9, G-protein-activated inward rectifier potassium channel). Functional hypothesis: LOC101929767 likely represents a cis-regulatory element or chromatin boundary controlling expression of neighboring dosage-sensitive genes in the 17q21.31 critical region, particularly KANSL1 which is the primary driver of Koolen-de Vries syndrome. The 3 pathogenic CNVs (all duplications) indicate triplosensitivity where copy number gains disrupt normal transcriptional regulation through gene dosage effects. Disease mechanism likely involves overexpression of KANSL1 or other 17q21.31 genes through enhancer amplification or topological domain disruption leading to neurodevelopmental phenotypes distinct from the deletion syndrome. The 17q21.31 region is also notable for a common inversion polymorphism (H1/H2 haplotypes) affecting ~900 kb, which may influence susceptibility to structural variants. The absence of protein-coding features combined with ClinVar evidence and neurodevelopmental disease association suggests this locus functions as a regulatory hub controlling chromatin architecture and gene expression in brain development.
