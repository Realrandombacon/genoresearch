# SLCO1B7: OATP Family Organic Anion Transporter with Unique Kazal Domain Regulating Hepatic Bile Acid and Drug Transport

**Date:** 2026-04-06T13:07:29.376894

**Quality Score:** 4.6/10  (E=5.9, D=3.3) [MODERATE]

## Description
SLCO1B7 (Solute Carrier Organic Anion Transporter Family Member 1B7, also OATP1B7, LST-3, SLC21A21) is a 640 amino acid plasma membrane transporter protein (UniProt G3V0H7) with a well-folded transmembrane architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 82.2, high confidence), characteristic of the organic anion transporting polypeptide (OATP/SLCO) family with a unique Kazal domain insertion.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals SLCO1B7 contains canonical OATP family features: an Organic Anion Transporter Polypeptide domain (IPR004156/PF03137/PTHR11388/TIGR00805, residues 1-611/1-412/460-573/1-601) conferring transmembrane transport (GO:0055085) and membrane localization (GO:0016020), plus a Major Facilitator Superfamily (MFS) transporter architecture (IPR020846/IPR036259/PS50850/SSF103473, residues 1-603) with multiple transmembrane helices (G3DSA:1.20.1250.20, residues 1-82/126-398). Uniquely, SLCO1B7 contains an inserted Kazal-type serine protease inhibitor domain (IPR002350/IPR036058/PF07648/PS51465/SSF100895, residues 406-461/410-459/413-459) conferring protein binding (GO:0005515) activity, distinguishing it from other OATP family members.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals SLCO1B7 functions within a transport and protease regulation network: strongest interactions with LECT2 (0.606, leukocyte cell-derived chemotaxin-2, a hepatokine with chemotactic activity), HRG (0.529, histidine-rich glycoprotein, plasma protein regulating coagulation and fibrinolysis), SLC35G4 (0.505, solute carrier family 35 member G4), OR10S1 (0.447, olfactory receptor 10S1), SLC35G3 (0.433), STARD9 (0.424, START domain-containing protein 9), ZNF772 (0.416, zinc finger protein 772), MSANTD1 (0.405, Myb/SANT-like DNA-binding domain-containing protein 1), UBAP2 (0.400, ubiquitin-associated protein 2), and CLPSL1 (0.400, carboxypeptidase-like protein 1). The LECT2 and HRG interactions suggest hepatic plasma protein regulation, while CLPSL1 indicates potential protease pathway connections consistent with the Kazal domain.\n\nHPA expression data is unavailable, consistent with SLCO1B7 being a putative/poorly annotated gene. However, based on OATP family homology and interaction partners, liver-specific expression is predicted.\n\nClinVar documents 43 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all large chromosome 12p12.2-p13.33 copy number variations (gains and losses spanning 12p13.33-11.1 and 12p12.2-12.1 regions). These CNVs suggest dosage sensitivity potentially causing developmental disorders through impaired organic anion transport and bile acid homeostasis.\n\nConservation is evident from macaque orthologs (F6YTD7, 747 aa; A0A5F7ZR91, 686 aa), indicating functional importance in primates.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: SLCO1B7 acts as a sodium-independent organic anion transporter mediating hepatic uptake of bile acids, bilirubin, and xenobiotic drugs (statins, antibiotics, anticancer agents) from portal blood into hepatocytes. The MFS transporter core forms 12 transmembrane helices creating a substrate-binding pocket for amphipathic organic anions. The unique Kazal domain insertion (residues 406-461) in the large extracellular loop distinguishes SLCO1B7 from other OATP1B subfamily members (OATP1B1, OATP1B3) and may serve regulatory functions: (1) binding plasma proteases to protect the transporter from cleavage, (2) interacting with LECT2/HRG to coordinate transport with inflammatory states, or (3) mediating protein-protein interactions for transporter clustering or endocytosis. The Kazal domain's protein binding capability suggests SLCO1B7 integrates transport activity with extracellular signaling. CNV-mediated dosage imbalance disrupts hepatic organic anion clearance, potentially causing drug toxicity, cholestasis, or developmental phenotypes through altered bile acid signaling. The 12p12.2 location near other SLCO genes suggests evolutionary expansion for substrate specificity diversification.
