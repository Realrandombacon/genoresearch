## Description
NPIPA5 (Nuclear pore complex-interacting protein family member A5, UniProt E9PKD4) is a 350-amino acid intracellular protein that functions as a nuclear pore complex (NPC) interacting protein potentially involved in nucleocytoplasmic transport or NPC assembly. InterPro analysis reveals defining NPIP architecture: Nuclear pore complex interacting protein domain (IPR009443/PTHR15438, aa 9-335/9-335) spanning most of the protein, with an N-terminal NPIP domain (IPR054697/PF06409, aa 22-243/22-243) that likely mediates protein-protein interactions with nucleoporins or transport factors. GO annotations are limited but the domain architecture strongly suggests roles in nuclear transport. AlphaFold structure prediction achieves medium confidence (pLDDT 51.7), indicating a partially structured protein with mixed ordered and disordered regions — this is characteristic of nucleocytoplasmic transport proteins that require conformational flexibility to interact with multiple binding partners and pass through the NPC central channel. Human Protein Atlas shows tissue-enhanced expression for NPIPA5 (ENSG00000183793) with top expression in brain at 160.4 nTPM, detected in all tissues, low cancer specificity, not detected in immune cells, cell type enhanced single cell specificity, and nucleoplasm subcellular localization — this brain-enriched pattern suggests potential roles in neuronal gene regulation or nuclear transport specific to neural development and function. NPIPA5 likely functions as an NPC-associated protein that uses its NPIP domains to bind nucleoporins (potentially NUP62 or other FG-nups) and facilitate cargo transport through the nuclear pore, or participates in NPC assembly/maintenance — the medium structural confidence (pLDDT 51.7) reflecting intrinsically disordered regions common in nucleocytoplasmic transport proteins that enable dynamic interactions with multiple partners, with brain-enriched expression (160.4 nTPM) suggesting specialized roles in neuronal nuclear transport contributing to 16p13.11 syndrome phenotypes when disrupted.
