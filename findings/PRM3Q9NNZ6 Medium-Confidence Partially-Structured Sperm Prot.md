## Description
PRM3 (Protamine 3, also known as PRM3) is a 103 amino acid nuclear protein (UniProt Q9NNZ6) with medium-confidence partially-structured architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 57.9), characteristic of intrinsically disordered DNA-binding proteins that undergo conformational changes during chromatin condensation. The protein belongs to the protamine-P3 family (IPR026077/PTHR14317, residues 1-103), enabling DNA binding activity and involved in DNA condensation, spermatogenesis, and differentiation biological processes. PRM3 is located on chromosome 16p13.13 and functions as a sperm-specific chromatin protein that replaces histones during spermatid differentiation to compact paternal DNA into a highly condensed state required for fertilization.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals a high-confidence network of sperm chromatin and fertilization proteins: PRM2 (0.837, Protamine-2, major sperm chromatin protein), TNP2 (0.825, Transition nuclear protein 2, histone-to-protamine transition), PRM1 (0.548, Protamine-1, major sperm chromatin protein), TNP1 (0.532, Transition nuclear protein 1, histone replacement), ROMO1 (0.489, Reactive oxygen species modulator 1, mitochondrial function), BRDT (0.453, Bromodomain testis-specific protein, chromatin reader), TMEM225 (0.451, Transmembrane protein 225, sperm membrane), IZUMO3 (0.448, Izumo family member 3, sperm-egg fusion), TSSK6 (0.447, Testis-specific serine/threonine kinase 6, sperm signaling), and SPACA3 (0.441, Sperm acrosome associated 3, acrosome reaction). These interactions confirm PRM3 functions within the sperm chromatin remodeling pathway, with PRM1/PRM2 as core protamine partners, TNP1/TNP2 mediating the histone-to-protamine transition, and BRDT recognizing acetylated histones during chromatin reorganization.\n\nHPA expression reveals PRM3 has tissue-enriched expression specifically in testis (80.6 nTPM), detected in some tissues, consistent with exclusive roles in spermatogenesis and sperm maturation. This extreme testis enrichment (80.6 nTPM) indicates PRM3 is critical for sperm chromatin compaction and male fertility. Subcellular localization includes nucleus and chromosome, consistent with DNA-binding chromatin protein function. The protein is classified as a predicted intracellular protein involved in differentiation, DNA condensation, and spermatogenesis. Cell type-enriched single cell specificity suggests expression in spermatids and mature sperm. Not detected in immune cells and not detected in cancer indicates PRM3 is strictly germ cell-specific.\n\nClinVar documents 17 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including copy number gains (16p13.3-13.13, 16p13.3-11.2, 16p13.3-12.2, 16p13.3-q24.3, 16p13.3-12.3, 16p13.3-q24.3 amplifications) and deletions (16p13.3-11.2). The variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity. While no specific Mendelian disease is annotated, 16p CNVs are associated with male infertility, azoospermia, and spermatogenic failure. Given PRM3's testis-enriched expression and chromatin compaction function, loss-of-function or dosage imbalance may cause male infertility through defective sperm chromatin condensation, leading to DNA fragmentation, failed fertilization, or early embryonic arrest.\n\nConservation is present across mammals: human (Q9NNZ6, 103 aa), mouse (Q62100, 101 aa), rat (Q64256, 104 aa), cow (Q32PA2, 93 aa), indicating an ancient and essential function in sperm chromatin compaction conserved in mammals.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: PRM3 functions as a sperm-specific chromatin protein that compacts paternal DNA during spermiogenesis through arginine/lysine-rich DNA binding. The medium-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 57.9) indicates intrinsic disorder characteristic of protamines that undergo conformational flexibility to wrap around DNA. PRM3 cooperates with PRM1 (0.548) and PRM2 (0.837) to replace histones and transition proteins (TNP1 0.532, TNP2 0.825) during chromatin remodeling. BRDT (0.453) recognizes acetylated histones and recruits chromatin remodeling complexes. ROMO1 (0.489) links PRM3 to mitochondrial ROS regulation during sperm maturation. TMEM225, IZUMO3, TSSK6, and SPACA3 interactions connect chromatin compaction to sperm membrane organization and fertilization capacity. 16p CNVs cause PRM3 dosage imbalance, potentially disrupting the stoichiometry of protamine complexes and causing incomplete chromatin condensation. This leads to sperm DNA fragmentation, reduced fertilization efficiency, and male infertility. PRM3 represents a candidate gene for non-obstructive azoospermia and may serve as a biomarker for sperm chromatin quality in assisted reproduction.
