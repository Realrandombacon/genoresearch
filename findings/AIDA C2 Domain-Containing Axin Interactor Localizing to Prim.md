# AIDA: C2 Domain-Containing Axin Interactor Localizing to Primary Cilium and Cytokinetic Bridge in Wnt Signaling Regulation

## Description
AIDA encodes a 306-amino acid axin interactor, dorsalization-associated protein (UniProt Q96BJ3), a C2 domain-containing scaffold protein functioning in Wnt signaling pathway regulation and ciliary biology. The protein contains an N-terminal AIDA domain (IPR023421, PF08910, positions 9-112) unique to this protein family, plus a C-terminal C2 domain (IPR025939, PF14186, PS51911, positions 157-304/152-306) characteristic of calcium-dependent membrane-targeting modules, classified within the axin interactor dorsalization-associated protein family (PTHR28654, positions 27-306). AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (pLDDT 85.8, residues 1-306) indicating stable folding with distinct N-terminal and C2 domain lobes. STRING interaction analysis reveals AIDA forms part of the Wnt signaling network with medium-confidence interactions: AXIN1 (0.468, Wnt pathway scaffold), PAGE2B (0.447, cancer/testis antigen), FAM118A (0.446, uncharacterized protein), UBE2Q1 (0.436, ubiquitin-conjugating enzyme), NFKBIA (0.431, NF-kappaB inhibitor), and NAT14 (0.412, N-alpha-acetyltransferase), confirming its role as axin-binding partner in Wnt pathway modulation. HPA data shows ubiquitous expression across all tissues with low tissue specificity, with striking subcellular localization to microtubules, cytokinetic bridge, primary cilium, and basal body - structures critical for cell division and ciliary signaling. ClinVar contains 28 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number gains (1q21.1-44, 1q31.3-44, 1q41-42.13) and losses (1q41, 1q41-42.12, 1q32.2-44), plus single nucleotide variants (c.111-146G>T), though specific Mendelian disorders remain undefined. Despite 67,914 papers matching 'AIDA' in literature searches, nearly all reference the AIDA acronym in unrelated contexts (marketing models, AI algorithms, autoinflammatory disease networks) - the gene itself remains genuinely understudied with minimal direct characterization. The functional hypothesis: AIDA serves as a C2 domain-containing adapter that bridges axin (Wnt pathway destruction complex scaffold) with ciliary and cytoskeletal structures, potentially regulating Wnt/beta-catenin signaling spatially at the primary cilium where Wnt receptors localize; the C2 domain may mediate calcium-dependent membrane association at ciliary base or cytokinetic bridge during cell division; cytokinetic bridge localization suggests roles in abscission checkpoint or midbody signaling coordinating cell cycle progression with Wnt pathway activity; basal body/primary cilium targeting implies functions in ciliopathy-related pathways; disease-associated 1q CNVs may disrupt AIDA dosage, altering Wnt signaling output and contributing to developmental defects or cancer through dysregulated beta-catenin stability; interaction with NFKBIA suggests potential crosstalk between Wnt and NF-kappaB inflammatory pathways.
