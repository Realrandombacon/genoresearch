## Description
TMEM150C (also known as TTN3/tentin-3) encodes a 249 amino acid transmembrane protein that functions as an auxiliary subunit of mechanosensitive ion channels. The protein contains a conserved N-terminal CWH43-like domain (IPR019402, PF10277, residues 9-217) and belongs to the DRAM/TMEM150 autophagy modulator family (IPR050911, PTHR21324, residues 10-241), suggesting dual functionality in mechanotransduction and lysosomal autophagy regulation.

Expression profiling (HPA) reveals tissue-enhanced expression with peak levels in epididymis (127.4 nTPM), detected across all tissues, cancer-enhanced expression pattern, and immune cell enrichment. This expression profile suggests roles in reproductive tissue function and immune cell mechanosensation.

Protein interaction network (STRING) shows strong associations with mechanosensitive channel components: PIEZO2 (score 0.622), PIEZO1 (0.435), TMEM120A (0.541, another mechanosensitive channel subunit), TRPV4 (0.425, mechanosensitive TRP channel), and KCNK2 (0.435, mechanosensitive potassium channel). Additional interactions with TMEM221 (0.483), GPRC5C (0.458), PHACTR2 (0.423), TMEM211 (0.420), and TMEM87A (0.402) suggest membership in a transmembrane protein complex involved in mechanical signal transduction.

Clinical significance is demonstrated by 37 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants in ClinVar, predominantly copy number losses and gains on chromosome 4q21.21-22.3, indicating dosage sensitivity and disease association.

Evolutionary conservation is strong: orthologs in mouse (Q8C8S3), rat (B5DFH9), cow (A5D7C9), and fish (A0A3B5BCR6) all maintain identical 249 aa length, indicating functional constraint across vertebrates.

Mechanistic hypothesis: TMEM150C serves as a regulatory auxiliary subunit that modulates gating kinetics and mechanical sensitivity of PIEZO channel complexes in dorsal root ganglion neurons and other mechanosensitive tissues. The DRAM family domain suggests potential lysosomal localization or autophagy regulation function, possibly linking mechanical stress to autophagic responses. In epididymis, high expression may regulate sperm maturation through mechanosensitive signaling. Disease variants likely disrupt channel assembly, trafficking, or mechanogating properties.
