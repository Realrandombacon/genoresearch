## Description
SPATA33 encodes a 139 amino acid spermatogenesis-associated protein (UniProt Q96N06) characterized by a domain of unknown function DUF4609 (InterPro IPR027930, PF15382, residues 70-136) that spans the majority of the C-terminal region—this conserved domain family is exclusively found in spermatogenesis-associated proteins across vertebrates. HPA data reveals striking testis-specific expression pattern with 86.5 nTPM in testis tissue (tissue enriched specificity), while detected at low levels in all other tissues, consistent with a specialized role in male germ cell development. Subcellular localization shows nucleoplasmic and vesicular distribution, and the gene is annotated with autophagy biological process—suggesting involvement in autophagic vesicle formation or cargo recognition during spermatogenesis. STRING interactions identify 10 protein partners including DEF8 (0.577, differentially expressed in FDC-P.8 cells), TEX43 (0.560, testis-expressed protein 43), DBNDD1 (0.560, dysbindin domain-containing protein), PPP3CC (0.531, calcineurin catalytic subunit gamma—calcium-dependent phosphatase), SPIRE2 (0.487, actin nucleation factor), VDAC2 (0.479, voltage-dependent anion channel 2—mitochondrial outer membrane), and AP1G1 (0.451, adaptor protein complex 1 gamma subunit—clathrin-mediated vesicle trafficking)—this interaction network places SPATA33 at the intersection of vesicle trafficking, mitochondrial function, and cytoskeletal remodeling. ClinVar documents 42 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number gains spanning 16q11.2-24.3 (chr16:46432879-90294753) and deletions affecting 16q23.3-24.3 and 16q24.3 terminal regions, indicating strong dosage sensitivity and disease association through chromosomal rearrangement mechanisms. The combination of testis-enriched expression, DUF4609 domain architecture, autophagy annotation, vesicular localization, and vesicle trafficking/cytoskeletal interaction partners suggests SPATA33 functions as a testis-specific adaptor protein coordinating autophagic vesicle formation with cytoskeletal remodeling during spermatid differentiation, with haploinsufficiency via 16q24.3 deletions disrupting this process contributing to male infertility phenotypes observed in 16q terminal deletion syndromes.
