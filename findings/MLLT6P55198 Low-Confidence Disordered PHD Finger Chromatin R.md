## Description
MLLT6 (MLLT6, PHD finger containing, also known as AF-17) is a 1093 amino acid intracellular protein (UniProt P55198) with low-confidence disordered architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 49.9), characteristic of intrinsically disordered chromatin regulators that function as transcriptional scaffolds through extensive protein-protein interactions rather than enzymatic activity. The protein contains multiple PHD-type zinc finger domains (IPR001965/IPR019786/IPR019787/IPR049781/PF13831/PF13832/PS01359, residues 7-55/5-57/119-178/117-180) that recognize histone modifications, an extended PHD (ePHD) domain (IPR034732/cd15709, residues 62-181/61-185) found in AF-17 and similar proteins that mediates chromatin binding, belongs to the zinc/RING finger domain superfamily (G3DSA:3.30.40.10, residues 2-60/61-182), and is classified as a histone modification regulator (IPR050701, residues 8-453). MLLT6 is located on chromosome 17q12 and enables histone binding activity and nucleosome binding activity, functioning in regulation of transcription by RNA polymerase II, renal potassium/sodium excretion, and renal water absorption as a nuclear chromatin regulator.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals a high-confidence network of MLL/KMT2A fusion partners and transcriptional elongation factors: MLLT3 (0.980, MLLT3 super elongation complex subunit, AF9), DOT1L (0.894, Histone-lysine N-methyltransferase DOT1L, H3K79 methyltransferase), AFF1 (0.862, AF4/FMR2 family member 1, super elongation complex), KMT2A (0.791, Histone-lysine N-methyltransferase 2A/MLL, H3K4 methyltransferase), AFDN (0.778, Afadin, cell junction), SEPTIN6 (0.554, Septin-6, cytoskeleton), ELL (0.543, Elongation factor for RNA polymerase II), MLLT10 (0.517, MLLT10 histone lysine methyltransferase DOT1L cofactor), MLLT1 (0.484, MLLT1 super elongation complex subunit, ENL), and LASP1 (0.413, LIM and SH3 domain protein 1, actin cytoskeleton). These interactions confirm MLLT6 functions within the MLL/KMT2A super elongation complex (SEC) that regulates transcriptional elongation through H3K79 methylation by DOT1L.\n\nHPA expression reveals MLLT6 has low tissue specificity and is detected in many tissues, consistent with a housekeeping role in transcriptional regulation. Subcellular localization is nucleoplasm, consistent with chromatin-associated transcriptional regulation. The protein is classified as a cancer-related gene, disease-related gene, and proto-oncogene. Cancer-enhanced expression indicates MLLT6 is upregulated in tumors, consistent with its role as an MLL fusion partner in leukemia. Cell type-enhanced single cell specificity suggests specialized functions in specific cell lineages. Not detected in immune cells indicates MLLT6 is not primarily involved in immune function.\n\nClinVar documents 8 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including copy number gains (17q12-21.2, 17p11.2-q21.2, 17p13.3-q25.3, 17q23.1-25.1, 17q21.33-24.2 amplifications), copy number loss (17q12-22 deletion), and critically a t(11;17)(q23;q12-q21) translocation. The translocation is particularly significant as it fuses MLLT6/AF-17 with KMT2A/MLL, creating an oncogenic fusion protein that drives acute leukemia through aberrant transcriptional activation of HOX genes. The variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity and oncogenic potential. 17q CNVs are associated with developmental delay and cancer predisposition.\n\nConservation is strong across vertebrates: human (P55198, 1093 aa), cow (F1MLY5, 1193 aa), indicating an ancient and essential function in chromatin regulation conserved in mammals.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: MLLT6/AF-17 functions as a PHD finger chromatin reader and scaffold protein within the MLL/KMT2A super elongation complex that regulates transcriptional elongation. The disordered architecture (pLDDT 49.9) provides flexible interaction surfaces for assembling multi-protein complexes. PHD finger domains (residues 7-55, 119-178) recognize methylated histone H3 tails, anchoring MLLT6 to active chromatin. The extended PHD domain (residues 62-181) mediates additional chromatin interactions. Through MLLT3 (0.980) and MLLT1 (0.484) interactions, MLLT6 recruits the super elongation complex to chromatin. DOT1L interaction (0.894) links MLLT6 to H3K79 methylation, a mark of transcriptional elongation. KMT2A interaction (0.791) connects MLLT6 to H3K4 methylation machinery. AFF1 and ELL interactions link MLLT6 to RNA polymerase II elongation. The t(11;17) translocation creates MLL-MLLT6 fusion proteins that constitutively recruit DOT1L and SEC to HOX gene loci, causing leukemogenesis through aberrant transcriptional activation. Cancer-enhanced expression suggests MLLT6 overexpression contributes to tumor progression. MLLT6 represents a candidate gene for leukemia and may contribute to renal phenotypes through transcriptional regulation of kidney-specific genes.
