## Description
ERVV-1 (Endogenous retrovirus group V member 1, envelope, also known as ENVV1, HERV-V1) is a 477 amino acid predicted membrane protein (UniProt B6SEH8) with low-confidence disordered architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 42.2), characteristic of retroviral envelope proteins that function through membrane fusion and protein-protein interactions rather than structured enzymatic activity. The protein contains a TLV/ENV coat polyprotein family domain (IPR018154, residues 7-465) enabling viral envelope function, belongs to the ENV polyprotein family (PF00429, residues 308-428), contains a heptad repeat 1-heptad repeat 2 region of the transmembrane subunit (cd09950, residues 355-426) characteristic of retroviral fusion machinery, and is classified as a viral envelope protein family member (PTHR10424, residues 7-465). ERVV-1 is located on chromosome 19q13.41 as part of an HERV provirus with inactivating mutations in gag and pol genes, while the envelope gene remains expressed. The protein is classified as a predicted membrane protein with subcellular localization to centrosome and cytosol.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals a network of endogenous retrovirus elements and placental/developmental regulators: ERVMER34-1 (0.639, Endogenous retrovirus group member 34-1, HERV envelope), ITIH4 (0.551, Inter-alpha-trypsin inhibitor heavy chain 4, inflammation/placenta), FANCM (0.532, Fanconi anemia complementation group M, DNA repair), CCDC7 (0.496, Coiled-coil domain-containing protein 7, centrosome), ZNF681 (0.476, Zinc finger protein 681, transcription), XAGE2 (0.448, X antigen family member 2, cancer/testis antigen), ZNF273 (0.438, Zinc finger protein 273, transcription), ZSCAN1 (0.421, Zinc finger and SCAN domain-containing protein 1, transcription), GCM1 (0.414, Glial cells missing homolog 1, placental development), and ERVW-1 (0.403, Endogenous retrovirus group W member 1/syncytin-1, placental fusion). These interactions confirm ERVV-1 functions within the endogenous retrovirus network, with ERVMER34-1 as the highest-confidence partner (0.639) reflecting coordinated HERV expression. The ERVW-1 interaction (0.403) is particularly significant as ERVW-1 encodes syncytin-1, the well-characterized placental fusogen.\n\nHPA expression reveals ERVV-1 has tissue-enriched expression with highest levels in placenta (15.1 nTPM), detected in some tissues, consistent with specialized roles in placental development and trophoblast fusion. This placental enrichment (15.1 nTPM) indicates ERVV-1 functions in reproductive biology, similar to other HERV envelope proteins like syncytin-1. Cancer-enhanced RNA specificity suggests potential reactivation in malignancies, a known feature of HERV elements. Subcellular localization to centrosome and cytosol suggests roles in cell division or intracellular trafficking. The protein is classified as a predicted membrane protein, consistent with envelope protein topology.\n\nClinVar documents 16 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all consisting of extensive copy number gains (19q13.33-13.43, 19q13.32-13.42, 19q13.31-13.42, 19p13.3-q13.43 amplifications spanning various coordinates). The variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity with predominant gain-of-function through duplications. While no specific Mendelian disease is annotated, 19q CNVs are associated with developmental delay, neurological disorders, and growth abnormalities. Given ERVV-1's placental expression and retroviral envelope function, overexpression may cause placental abnormalities, trophoblast fusion defects, or developmental disorders through altered cell-cell fusion mechanisms.\n\nConservation is restricted to primates as an endogenous retrovirus element: human (B6SEH8, 477 aa), with related ERV elements in other primates. ERVV-1 represents a human-specific or primate-specific HERV integration that was co-opted for placental function, similar to syncytin-1 (ERVW-1) and syncytin-2 (ERVFRD-1).\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: ERVV-1 functions as a placental envelope protein derived from an ancient retroviral infection that was co-opted (exapted) for mammalian placental development, mediating trophoblast cell-cell fusion similar to syncytin-1. The low-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 42.2) indicates intrinsic disorder consistent with flexible envelope proteins that undergo conformational changes during membrane fusion. The heptad repeat region (residues 355-426) is characteristic of class I viral fusion proteins that form six-helix bundles during fusion. Through ERVMER34-1 interaction (0.639), ERVV-1 may coordinate with other HERV elements for synchronized placental expression. ERVW-1/syncytin-1 interaction (0.403) suggests functional cooperation in trophoblast fusion. GCM1 interaction (0.414) links ERVV-1 to placental development transcriptional regulation. ITIH4 interaction (0.551) connects ERVV-1 to inflammatory and placental processes. FANCM interaction (0.532) suggests potential DNA damage response roles. Centrosomal localization indicates possible roles in cell division. Placenta-enriched expression (15.1 nTPM) positions ERVV-1 in trophoblast differentiation. 19q CNVs cause ERVV-1 overexpression, potentially disrupting placental architecture through aberrant cell fusion. ERVV-1 represents a domesticated HERV envelope protein contributing to human placental evolution and may serve as a biomarker for placental disorders or cancers with HERV reactivation.
