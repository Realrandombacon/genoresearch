## Description
PRAMEF20 (PRAME family member 20, UniProt Q5VT98) is a 475-amino acid intracellular protein that functions as a member of the PRAME (preferentially expressed antigen in melanoma) family of leucine-rich repeat-containing proteins. InterPro analysis reveals defining PRAME architecture: N-terminal PRAME-like domain (IPR060378/PF27091, aa 6-113) connected to extensive C-terminal leucine-rich repeat region (IPR060377/PF27089, aa 147-475/158-412) forming a ribonuclease inhibitor-like fold (G3DSA:3.80.10.10/SSF52047, aa 158-412/178-409) characteristic of curved solenoid structures that mediate protein-protein interactions. GO annotations confirm roles in positive regulation of cell population proliferation (GO:0008284), negative regulation of apoptotic process (GO:0043066), and negative regulation of cell differentiation (GO:0045596). AlphaFold structure prediction achieves high confidence (pLDDT 85.2), indicating a well-folded protein with stable LRR solenoid architecture — essential for maintaining the concave binding surface required for specific partner recognition. Human Protein Atlas shows no detected expression in standard tissue panels — this absence reflects extreme tissue restriction to testis/germ cells characteristic of cancer-testis antigen families where PRAME proteins are normally expressed only in spermatogenic cells but reactivated in diverse malignancies. STRING interaction network reveals 10 partners consisting almost exclusively of ribosomal proteins with exceptionally high confidence scores: FAU (0.961, ubiquitin-like protein S30 fusion), RPL23 (0.720, ribosomal protein L23), MRPL36 (0.719), MRPL17 (0.717), and MRPL12 (0.716, mitochondrial ribosomal large subunit proteins), TUFM (0.717, elongation factor Tu mitochondrial), RPS11 (0.716), RPS3 (0.716), RPS18 (0.716), and RPS15 (0.716, cytoplasmic ribosomal small subunit proteins). This interactome definitively places PRAMEF20 within ribosome biogenesis and/or translation regulation pathways, potentially bridging cytoplasmic and mitochondrial translation systems. ClinVar contains 25 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants consisting of copy number variations (deletions, duplications, gains, losses) spanning chromosome 1p regions (1p36.33-36.12) — these large CNVs encompass multiple genes and are associated with developmental disorders, though no specific disease is attributed solely to PRAMEF20. Conservation in primates (Pan troglodytes H2R9H1, 439 aa; Gorilla gorilla G3QHX9/G3SG43, 462-475 aa) indicates recent evolutionary expansion tied to reproductive function. PRAMEF20 likely functions as a protein-protein interaction scaffold through its LRR solenoid that binds specific ribosomal subunits or assembly factors, coordinating ribosome biogenesis during spermatogenesis where massive protein synthesis demands occur, or regulating translation efficiency in germ cells — with cancer-testis antigen properties suggesting potential roles in tumor cell proliferation where reactivation supports rapid growth through enhanced ribosome production and apoptosis suppression, explaining the anti-apoptotic and pro-proliferative GO annotations.
