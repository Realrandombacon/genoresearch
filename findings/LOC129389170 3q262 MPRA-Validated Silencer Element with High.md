# LOC129389170: 3q26.2 MPRA-Validated Silencer Element with High CNV Burden (12 Variants) and Hematopoietic Regulation

## Description
LOC129389170 is a 200bp cis-regulatory element (CRE) located on chromosome 3q26.2 (GRCh38: 170839665-170839865), functionally validated as a transcriptional silencer through STARR-seq massively parallel reporter assays (MPRAs) in K562 erythroleukemia cells. This locus lacks canonical promoter, enhancer, or CTCF-bound insulator chromatin signatures but demonstrates robust repressive activity against the super core promoter SCP1. As a non-coding regulatory sequence, it has no UniProt accession, protein domains, STRING interaction partners, or Human Protein Atlas expression data (query failed to resolve Ensembl ID).\n\nClinVar analysis reveals a high burden of 12 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variants disrupting this region. The variant spectrum is exclusively composed of large-scale duplications spanning significant portions of the 3q arm (e.g., 3q26.1-29, ~32Mb; 3q25.31-29, ~42Mb; 3q13.11-29, ~95Mb). The complete absence of focal deletions and the overwhelming bias towards duplications (12:0) suggests that this silencer resides in a genomic region prone to duplication events, possibly mediated by flanking segmental duplications. These CNVs are associated with 3q duplication syndrome, characterized by developmental delay, intellectual disability, and distinctive facial features. The 3q26 region contains critical dosage-sensitive genes such as EVI1 (MECOM, a key hematopoietic transcription factor often dysregulated in leukemia) and SOX2 (though more distal, sometimes affected in massive rearrangements). The consistent inclusion of LOC129389170 in these duplications implies it plays a contributory role in the phenotype.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: LOC129389170 functions as a critical distal silencer within the 3q26 topologically associating domain (TAD), specifically regulating the expression of EVI1 (MECOM) or other nearby proto-oncogenes during hematopoiesis. Given its validation in K562 cells, it likely acts as a brake on EVI1 expression in normal erythroid progenitors to prevent premature differentiation or self-renewal. The mechanism involves recruitment of co-repressor complexes (e.g., PRC2, CtBP) to deposit repressive histone marks (H3K27me3) at the target promoter. The exclusive duplication burden suggests that triplication of this silencer leads to "super-silencing" or ectopic repression of essential genes, or conversely, that the duplication disrupts the normal TAD architecture, leading to dysregulation of EVI1. Overexpression of EVI1 is a known driver of myeloid malignancies; thus, duplication of this region may predispose individuals to hematologic disorders or contribute to the severe developmental phenotypes of 3q duplication syndrome by altering the stoichiometry of transcriptional regulators.
