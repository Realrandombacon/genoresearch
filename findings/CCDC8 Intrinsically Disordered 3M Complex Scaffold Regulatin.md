## Description
CCDC8 (coiled-coil domain containing 8, alias 3M3, PPP1R20, p90) encodes a 538-amino acid intrinsically disordered scaffold protein belonging to the CCDC8 family (IPR026526/PTHR47741, residues 470-517) that functions as an essential regulatory subunit of the 3M E3 ubiquitin ligase complex (CUL7-FBXW8-CCDC8-OBSL1). The protein lacks canonical catalytic domains but contains a C-terminal CCDC8 family region characteristic of protein-protein interaction scaffolds. AlphaFold predicts low confidence structure (pLDDT=46.4), indicating a predominantly intrinsically disordered protein (IDP) that functions through dynamic protein-protein interactions rather than stable tertiary folding—typical of signaling scaffolds and ubiquitin ligase adaptors. HPA classifies CCDC8 as a disease-related gene with tissue-enhanced expression (ovary 35.3 nTPM top, detected in many tissues), triple nucleoplasm/plasma membrane/cytosol localization, and dwarfism disease involvement, consistent with roles in growth regulation and developmental signaling. Gene ontology annotations include p53-mediated apoptosis following DNA damage, positioning CCDC8 within DNA damage response and cell cycle control pathways. STRING interactions reveal tight coupling to the 3M ubiquitin ligase machinery: OBSL1 (0.999, obscurin-like 1, cytoskeletal adaptor, 3M complex anchor), CUL7 (0.997, cullin-7, E3 ubiquitin ligase scaffold), FBXW8 (0.795, F-box/WD repeat protein, substrate recognition), TP53 (0.734, tumor protein p53, DNA damage response), KAT5 (0.655, histone acetyltransferase, chromatin remodeling), MAPK14 (0.645, p38 alpha MAP kinase, stress response), RBX1 (0.641, RING box protein, E3 ligase catalytic core), UBC (0.595, ubiquitin-conjugating enzyme), CUL9 (0.583, cullin-9, ubiquitin ligase), and ANKRA2 (0.422, ankyrin repeat protein, transcriptional corepressor). The OBSL1-CUL7-FBXW8 triad represents the canonical 3M complex, with CCDC8 serving as a critical bridging scaffold that stabilizes complex assembly and substrate recognition. ClinVar documents 37 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including nonsense mutations (p.Trp203Ter, p.Trp497Ter, p.Gln343Ter), frameshift deletions (p.Lys29delinsAsnTer, p.Ala353fs, p.Gln327fs, p.Ser108fs), and missense changes (p.Ala191Val, p.Met1Ile, p.Ala527Val), causing 3M syndrome-3 (growth retardation disorder with skeletal dysplasia, characteristic facial features, and normal intelligence), demonstrating strict requirement for postnatal growth regulation. Conservation is evident across mammals: mouse (D3YZV8, 685 aa), rat (P62521, 643 aa). Mechanistically, CCDC8 functions as an intrinsically disordered scaffold that bridges OBSL1 (cytoskeletal membrane anchor) to the CUL7-FBXW8 catalytic core, enabling substrate recruitment and ubiquitin transfer. The 3M complex targets growth-regulating substrates (IGF signaling components, cell cycle regulators, p53 effectors) for proteasomal degradation, thereby controlling cell proliferation and somatic growth. Loss of CCDC8 destabilizes the 3M complex, causing substrate accumulation, dysregulated growth signaling, and 3M syndrome pathogenesis. The disordered nature enables conformational plasticity for binding multiple partners, while p53 interaction suggests roles in DNA damage-induced apoptosis. CCDC8 represents a paradigm of IDP-mediated ubiquitin ligase regulation.
