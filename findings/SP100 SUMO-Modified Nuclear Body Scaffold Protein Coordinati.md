# SP100: SUMO-Modified Nuclear Body Scaffold Protein Coordinating PML-NB Antiviral Defense, Chromatin Regulation, and Autoimmune Pathogenesis in Primary Biliary Cholangitis

## Description
SP100 (879 aa, UniProt P23497), also known as nuclear autoantigen Sp-100 or speckled protein 100, is a major component of PML (promyelocytic leukemia) nuclear bodies that functions as a SUMO-modified scaffold protein coordinating antiviral innate immunity, chromatin organization, and transcriptional regulation within discrete subnuclear compartments. The protein architecture features multiple functional domains: an N-terminal HSR domain (IPR004865, aa33-149) mediating nuclear body targeting and protein-protein interactions; a central SAND domain (IPR000770/PF01342, aa595-676) providing DNA-binding capability for chromatin association; and two C-terminal HMG-box domains (IPR009071/PF00505, aa676-754 and aa768-838) that recognize distorted DNA structures and facilitate chromatin remodeling. The full SP100/SP110/SP140 family signature spans aa42-872 (IPR043563). HPA confirms tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in bone marrow (177.8 nTPM), ubiquitous detection across all tissues, strict nucleoplasmic and nuclear body localization, and classification as a transcription factor involved in host-virus interaction and transcriptional regulation. Mechanistically, SP100 forms ultra-high-confidence interactions within the PML nuclear body proteome: SUMO1 (STRING 0.997) and SUMO2 (0.933) represent the covalent modifiers that conjugate to SP100 lysine residues regulating nuclear body dynamics; UBE2I (0.952) is the E2 SUMO-conjugating enzyme catalyzing SP100 sumoylation; ATRX (0.991) links SP100 to chromatin remodeling and alpha-thalassemia mental retardation syndrome pathways; DAXX (0.918) connects to death-domain associated protein and histone chaperone functions; PML (0.782) is the core nuclear body scaffold forming heteromeric complexes with SP100; H3C12 (0.929), H3-3B (0.442), and H3C13 (0.431) represent histone H3 variants linking SP100 to nucleosome organization; HSPA4 (0.479) provides chaperone activity for nuclear body assembly. AlphaFold prediction (pLDDT 56.9) reveals partially structured architecture with well-folded SAND and HMG-box domains connected by intrinsically disordered regions consistent with scaffold proteins that undergo liquid-liquid phase separation to form membraneless organelles; the low confidence score reflects dynamic conformational flexibility required for nuclear body assembly/disassembly. Clinical significance includes 35 pathogenic/likely pathogenic ClinVar variants: large copy number gains spanning 2q35-37.3 (chr2:215591606-239217386 amplifications), 2q33.3-37.3 gains, splice site mutations (c.1546+1G>A, c.1345+1G>A, c.32+2T>G), though no specific monogenic syndrome has been definitively established; anti-SP100 autoantibodies serve as diagnostic biomarkers for primary biliary cholangitis (PBC) with high specificity. Conservation across mammals indicates essential nuclear function maintained over ~90 million years. Literature (747 papers) establishes SP100 in autoimmunity, cancer, and antiviral defense: 2025 systematic reviews characterize SP100 family protein expression patterns across various cancers correlating with prognosis; anti-SP100 antibodies combined with anti-gp210 and AMA-M2 improve PBC diagnostic accuracy in multiplex bead-based assays; SP100 restricts herpesvirus replication through PML-NB mediated epigenetic silencing of viral genomes; interferon-inducible SP100 isoforms enhance antiviral state in infected cells. The functional hypothesis posits that SP100 acts as a 'nuclear body phase separation coordinator': constitutive sumoylation by UBE2I-SUMO1/SUMO2 system targets SP100 to PML nuclear bodies where multivalent interactions drive liquid-liquid phase separation forming membraneless compartments; the SAND domain anchors SP100 to specific chromatin loci while HMG-box domains recognize DNA structural distortions during transcription or damage; upon viral infection, interferon signaling induces SP100 expression and alternative splicing generating isoforms that recruit epigenetic silencing machinery (ATRX, DAXX, histone deacetylases) to viral episomes establishing heterochromatic repression; in autoimmune pathogenesis, aberrant SP100 exposure during hepatocyte apoptosis generates autoantigens triggering anti-SP100 antibody production in genetically susceptible individuals developing PBC; in cancer, SP100 dysregulation alters nuclear body integrity affecting tumor suppressor PML function and DNA damage response; therapeutic strategies include SP100-targeted immunotherapy for PBC monitoring and SP100 modulators enhancing antiviral nuclear body function.
