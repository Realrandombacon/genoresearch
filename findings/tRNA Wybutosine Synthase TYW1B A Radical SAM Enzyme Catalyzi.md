# tRNA Wybutosine Synthase TYW1B: A Radical SAM Enzyme Catalyzing Hypermodified tRNA Phe Maturation with Links to Williams-Beuren Syndrome Region and Translational Fidelity Control

## Description
TYW1B (tRNA-yW Synthesizing Protein 1 Homolog B) is a 668-amino acid S-adenosyl-L-methionine-dependent enzyme belonging to the Radical SAM superfamily, characterized by multiple catalytic domains including an N-terminal flavodoxin domain (IPR001094/PF00258, positions 46-171; IPR008254, 37-191) for FMN binding and electron transfer, a central Radical SAM domain (IPR007197/PF04055, 336-580; IPR058240, 349-532) containing the canonical CxxxCxxC motif for [4Fe-4S] cluster coordination and reductive cleavage of S-adenosylmethionine, a tRNA wybutosine-synthesis domain (IPR013917, 531-593), and a C-terminal aldolase-type TIM barrel fold (G3DSA:3.20.20.70/IPR013785, 338-603) classified under the tRNA 4-demethylwyosine synthase family (IPR034556, 339-668). STRING interactions reveal functional coupling to tRNA modification machinery: TYW3 (0.556, direct pathway partner in wybutosine biosynthesis), TRMT5 (0.442, tRNA methyltransferase), TRMT12 (0.466, tRNA modification enzyme), alongside metabolic enzymes UROD (0.619, uroporphyrinogen decarboxylase in heme biosynthesis), ENDOV (0.615, endonuclease V for DNA repair), OSBPL7 (0.576, oxysterol-binding protein), MARC1 (0.519, molybdenum cofactor metabolism), ENTPD4 (0.477, nucleotidase), COBLL1 (0.451), and NBPF9 (0.431, neuroblastoma breakpoint family). HPA data shows tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in choroid plexus (12.3 nTPM), low cancer and immune cell specificity, cell type-enhanced single cell specificity, and precise subcellular localization to nucleoplasm, nucleoli, and nucleoli rim—consistent with its role in tRNA processing within the ribosome biogenesis compartment; classified as an enzyme and predicted membrane protein with lyase activity. ClinVar contains 25 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains and losses spanning 7q11.21-23 regions (the Williams-Beuren syndrome critical region) and missense variants (p.Ala54Val, p.Gly398Val), though specific monogenic disease phenotypes remain under characterization (significance marked '?' for most); the genomic location strongly implicates TYW1B as a contributor to Williams-Beuren syndrome developmental phenotypes when deleted. AlphaFold structure (AF-Q6NUM6-F1) predicts high confidence folding (pLDDT 76.2) consistent with a well-structured multi-domain enzyme typical of Radical SAM superfamily members. With only 8 papers, TYW1B represents a genuine DARK gene: despite its clear enzymatic identity, its specific physiological substrates, regulation, and contribution to human disease remain poorly characterized beyond its genomic location in the Williams-Beuren critical region. The mechanistic hypothesis posits that TYW1B catalyzes the radical SAM-dependent formation of wybutosine (yW), a hypermodified guanosine derivative at position 37 adjacent to the anticodon of tRNA-Phe, which stabilizes codon-anticodon interactions during translation and prevents frameshifting; loss of TYW1B function leads to reduced translational fidelity particularly for phenylalanine-rich proteins, potentially contributing to the neurodevelopmental and cardiovascular defects observed in Williams-Beuren syndrome through proteotoxic stress and misregulation of specific client proteins requiring high-fidelity translation.
