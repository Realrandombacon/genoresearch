# RRP15: Nucleolar Ribosome Biogenesis Factor with High-Confidence Pre-60S Assembly Network and 40 ClinVar Variants

## Description
RRP15 (Q9Y3B9) encodes a 282 amino acid nucleolar protein essential for early maturation steps of the 60S ribosomal subunit. With only 24 publications, RRP15 remains partially characterized despite its fundamental role in ribosome biogenesis and emerging cancer relevance.\n\nSTRUCTURAL FEATURES: InterPro analysis reveals a conserved RRP15 domain architecture (IPR012459, residues 1-281; PF07890/Rrp15p, 92-215; PTHR13245, 1-281) with GO annotation for rRNA processing (GO:0006364). AlphaFold prediction shows medium confidence folding (pLDDT 66.6) across all 282 residues, suggesting a partially structured protein with both ordered domains and flexible regions typical of nucleolar assembly factors that undergo conformational changes during ribosome maturation.\n\nINTERACTION NETWORK: STRING-DB reveals RRP15 operates within an exceptionally tight pre-60S ribosomal assembly complex through ultra-high-confidence interactions: RPF1 (0.989), RPF2 (0.985), SURF6 (0.982), NIFK (0.978), EBNA1BP2 (0.977), MAK16 (0.972), WDR74 (0.968), GTPBP4 (0.964), BRIX1 (0.960), and PPAN (0.815). All partners are established 60S biogenesis factors, positioning RRP15 as a central scaffold coordinating early nucleolar assembly steps.\n\nEXPRESSION & DISEASE: HPA shows ubiquitous expression across all tissues with nucleoplasm, nucleoli rim, mitotic chromosome, and mitochondrial localization—consistent with dual roles in ribosome production and cell division. ClinVar contains 40 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including multiple copy number losses at 1q41 and copy number gains, yet no specific disease phenotype is defined. This suggests RRP15 haploinsufficiency may cause undiagnosed ribosomopathies or contribute to cancer susceptibility.\n\nFUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: RRP15 likely functions as a molecular scaffold that bridges early pre-60S assembly factors during nucleolar ribosome maturation. The Rrp15p domain (92-215) creates a structured core while flanking regions provide flexibility for conformational transitions. Recent cancer literature (2023-2024) demonstrates RRP15 deficiency induces ribosome stress that suppresses colorectal cancer proliferation via LZTS2-mediated beta-catenin inhibition, and RRP15 is a therapeutic target in hepatocellular carcinoma and pancreatic cancer. The 40 ClinVar variants without defined phenotypes suggest systematic screening for RRP15 mutations in patients with unexplained developmental disorders, bone marrow failure, or cancer predisposition could reveal novel ribosomopathy syndromes.
