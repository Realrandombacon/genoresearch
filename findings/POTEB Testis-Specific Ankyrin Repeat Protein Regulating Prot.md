# POTEB: Testis-Specific Ankyrin Repeat Protein Regulating Protein-Protein Interactions in Spermatogenesis

## Description
POTEB (POTE Ankyrin Domain Family Member B, also A26B1, CT104.5, POTE-15, POTE15) is a 544 amino acid intracellular protein (UniProt A0A0A6YYL3) with a well-folded ankyrin repeat architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 71.1, high confidence), characteristic of scaffold proteins mediating protein-protein interactions in specialized cellular contexts.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals POTEB contains multiple ankyrin repeats (IPR002110/PF12796/SM00248, residues 106-299) organized in three copies within an ankyrin repeat-containing domain superfamily (IPR036770/G3DSA:1.25.40.20/SSF48403, residues 87-379), conferring protein binding activity (GO:0005515). The C-terminal region contains a CCDC144C-like coiled-coil domain (IPR039497/PF14915, residues 433-542) for oligomerization. The protein belongs to the ankyrin repeat domain-containing protein family (PTHR24147).\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals POTEB functions within an ankyrin repeat protein network: strongest interactions with LRRC37A (0.645, leucine-rich repeat protein 37A), ARL17B (0.638, ADP-ribosylation factor-like protein 17B), ANKRD46 (0.492, ankyrin repeat domain-containing protein 46), NFKBIA (0.436, NF-kappa-B inhibitor alpha), MTPN (0.436, myotrophin), ASB16 (0.428), ASB11 (0.427), ASB5 (0.414), CDKN2C (0.410, cyclin-dependent kinase inhibitor 2C), and ASB12 (0.402). The ASB family interactions suggest roles in ubiquitin-mediated protein degradation.\n\nHPA expression shows exceptional testis specificity (1.1 nTPM) with tissue-enriched classification and detection in single tissue type, consistent with specialized reproductive function. The protein is classified as intracellular with no cancer or immune cell detection.\n\nClinVar documents 31 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large chromosome 15q11.2 copy number variations (deletions and duplications spanning 15q11.1-q13.3 regions). These CNVs overlap the Prader-Willi/Angelman syndrome critical region, suggesting POTEB dosage sensitivity may contribute to neurodevelopmental phenotypes through genomic imprinting effects.\n\nConservation is evident from the POTE family expansion in primates, with human paralogs POTE A, B, C, D, E, F, G, H, and J, indicating recent evolutionary expansion for reproductive functions.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: POTEB acts as a testis-specific scaffold protein that coordinates protein complex assembly during spermatogenesis through its ankyrin repeat-mediated protein interactions. The ankyrin repeats bind client proteins including cell cycle regulators (CDKN2C) and ubiquitin ligase adaptors (ASB family), potentially regulating protein stability during meiotic progression. The coiled-coil domain enables homodimerization or heterodimerization with other POTE family members. Testis-restricted expression suggests roles in germ cell development, acrosome formation, or sperm maturation. CNV-mediated dosage imbalance in the 15q11.2 region disrupts imprinted gene expression, contributing to Prader-Willi/Angelman syndrome phenotypes through POTEB haploinsufficiency or overexpression effects on protein interaction networks.
