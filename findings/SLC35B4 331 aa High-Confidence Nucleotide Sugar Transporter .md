## Description
SLC35B4 (Q969S0, 331 aa), also known as the UDP-GlcNAc/UDP-xylose transporter, is a critical metabolic gatekeeper that transports activated sugar donors from the cytoplasm into the lumen of the endoplasmic reticulum (ER) and Golgi apparatus, fueling essential glycosylation pathways. InterPro identifies it as a member of the HUT1 family (IPR013657, aa 4-321) and the UAA transporter superfamily (PF08449, aa 4-314), characterized by multiple transmembrane helices forming a specific pore for nucleotide-sugar translocation. AlphaFold predicts a very high-confidence structure (global pLDDT 86.6, residues 1-331), revealing a compact, rigid bundle of transmembrane domains consistent with a facilitated diffusion or antiport mechanism. HPA classifies it as a predicted membrane protein with low tissue specificity, detected in all tissues but enhanced in specific cell types, reflecting its fundamental housekeeping role in protein maturation. STRING reveals a tight functional cluster of 10 interactions dominated by other nucleotide sugar transporters (SLC35A3 score 0.919, SLC35A5 0.675, SLC35D3 0.597, SLC35D1 0.575) and AMP deaminases (AMPD2 0.821, AMPD3 0.821, AMPD1 0.791); the strong link to AMPD enzymes suggests a coordinated regulation of nucleotide pools and energy status alongside glycosylation flux. ClinVar contains 33 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number losses and gains on chromosome 7q (e.g., chr7:122190535-149944340 x1, chr7:133851002-159119707 x3); while no specific monogenic syndrome name is yet assigned, the variant burden implies potential developmental disorders or metabolic vulnerabilities. Functional hypothesis: SLC35B4 acts as the rate-limiting supply line for UDP-N-acetylglucosamine (UDP-GlcNAc), the substrate for O-GlcNAcylation. By controlling nuclear and cytoplasmic O-GlcNAc levels, it directly stabilizes the c-MYC oncoprotein (preventing its degradation) and modulates the YAP1 signaling axis, driving proliferation in gastric and liver cancers. Furthermore, recent literature indicates SLC35B4 is hijacked by Influenza A virus (H5N1) to facilitate viral internalization and replication, likely by modifying host surface receptors or endosomal trafficking machinery. The interaction with AMPD suggests a feedback loop where purine metabolism influences glycosylation capacity. Conservation across vertebrates confirms its essential role.
