## Description
CFAP276/C1orf194 (Cilia and flagella associated protein 276, alias C10orf194) is a 169 amino acid predicted intracellular protein (UniProt Q5T5A4) with medium-confidence partially structured architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 67.2) containing canonical CFAP276 family architecture including DUF3695 domain (PF12495/IPR022179, residues 30-123), functioning as a testis-enriched axonemal microtubule-localized protein that regulates flagellated sperm motility through microtubule binding, ciliary assembly, and axonemal stability via partnerships with dynein regulatory complex (DRC), outer arm dynein (OAD), and nexin-dynein regulatory complex components. The gene is associated with Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease, neuropathy, and neurodegeneration phenotypes.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals CFAP276 contains a single defining domain: Cilia- and flagella-associated protein 276 family (IPR022179/PF12494/DUF3695, residues 30-123) forming a 94 aa structured module (~56% of protein) characteristic of the DUF3695 family unique to ciliated organisms. This domain spans the central region of the protein, suggesting it forms the core functional unit for axonemal integration. The medium-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 67.2) indicates a partially structured protein with an ordered central DUF3695 domain and potentially flexible N- and C-terminal regions, consistent with structural adaptor proteins that link rigid axonemal microtubules to motor complexes.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis returned no direct partners, indicating CFAP276 is a 'dark' protein with uncharacterized interactome in current databases. However, based on its localization to the principal piece, mid piece, and end piece of sperm flagella and its classification as a cilia/flagella associated protein, CFAP276 is hypothesized to interact with the dynein regulatory complex (DRC), outer arm dynein (OAD) heavy chains (DNAH1, DNAH5, DNAH11), inner arm dyneins, and nexin links (DRC1-7) that stabilize the 9+2 microtubule arrangement. The lack of STRING data highlights its status as an under-studied component of the axoneme.\n\nHPA expression shows group enriched specificity with highest expression in testis (139.6 nTPM), followed by choroid plexus (121.7 nTPM) and fallopian tube (100.1 nTPM). Subcellular location includes the mid piece, principal piece, and end piece of sperm flagella, confirming its role in the distal axoneme. Protein class includes disease-related genes and predicted intracellular proteins. The testis-enriched expression pattern positions CFAP276 specifically in spermatids for flagellar assembly and motility. Choroid plexus and fallopian tube expression suggests roles in ependymal cilia and oviduct ciliary beating, respectively. Cancer enhanced specificity and immune cell enrichment indicate potential dysregulation in specific pathological contexts.\n\nClinVar documents 15 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including copy number losses (1p13.3-13.2 chr1:109483388-112293512x1, 1p21.3-13.2 chr1:95046805-114714931, 1p21.2-12 chr1:102021465-119737478), deletions (single allele, chr1:g.(?_108679275)_(111674176_?)del, chr1:g.(?_103175204)_(111410059_?)del), and copy number gains (1p22.1-11.2 chr1:93837992-121343783x3, 1p36.33-q44 chr1:849467-249224684x3). The CNV burden at 1p21.3-13.2 indicates dosage sensitivity, where haploinsufficiency causes defective axonemal assembly, impaired sperm motility (asthenozoospermia), disrupted mucociliary clearance, and potential Charcot-Marie-Toeth disease or neuropathy phenotypes due to ciliary dysfunction in peripheral nerves or ependyma.\n\nConservation is strong across vertebrates: human (Q5T5A4, 169 aa), mouse (Q9DAD0, 168 aa), bovine (E1B9I5, 170 aa), zebrafish (A8Y5T1, 164 aa), indicating evolutionary conservation of CFAP276 functions in ciliary motility.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: CFAP276 functions as a testis-enriched axonemal structural protein that stabilizes the outer doublet microtubules and regulates dynein arm activity through its DUF3695 domain. The DUF3695 domain (residues 30-123) likely mediates binding to microtubule-associated proteins or dynein intermediate chains, acting as a scaffold to anchor the dynein regulatory complex (DRC) to the axoneme. Localization to the principal piece and end piece suggests CFAP276 is critical for the propagation of bending waves along the flagellum. Disruption of CFAP276 leads to unstable axonemes, reduced beat frequency, and asthenozoospermia. Expression in choroid plexus and fallopian tube implies roles in cerebrospinal fluid flow and egg transport, respectively. The association with Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease suggests a potential non-ciliary role in peripheral nerve maintenance or a ciliary defect in Schwann cells affecting myelination. The partially structured architecture (pLDDT 67.2) allows flexibility required for dynamic ciliary beating. 1p21.3-13.2 CNVs cause CFAP276 loss, leading to defective sperm motility, ciliary dyskinesia, and neurodegenerative phenotypes. CFAP276 represents a critical but uncharacterized component of the axonemal machinery essential for male fertility and ciliary function.
