## Description
LOC129390134 is a 200bp cis-regulatory element (CRE) located on chromosome 10p15.3 (GRCh38: 15166335-15166535), functionally validated as a transcriptional silencer through STARR-seq massively parallel reporter assays (MPRAs) in K562 erythroleukemia cells. This locus lacks canonical promoter, enhancer, or CTCF-bound insulator chromatin signatures but demonstrates robust repressive activity against the super core promoter SCP1. As a non-coding regulatory sequence, it has no UniProt accession, protein domains, STRING interaction partners, or Human Protein Atlas expression data (query failed to resolve Ensembl ID).\n\nClinVar analysis reveals 5 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variants disrupting this region. The variant spectrum includes massive whole-arm alterations spanning 10p: large deletions (10p15.3-12.31, ~19Mb; 10p14-12.31, ~14Mb; 10p15.1-13, ~12Mb) and duplications (10p15.3-12.1, ~26Mb; 10p15.3-12.31, ~19Mb). The exclusive presence of large-scale CNVs suggests that while the silencer itself is small, its disruption is a consistent feature of 10p deletion/duplication syndromes. These CNVs are associated with severe phenotypes including intellectual disability, hypogonadism, and cardiac defects. The 10p15 region contains critical dosage-sensitive genes such as DMRT1 (Doublesex and Mab-3 Related Transcription Factor 1, sex determination) and GATA3 (GATA Binding Protein 3, though more distal, often affected in large deletions), as well as KIF1B. The silencer likely regulates one of these key developmental regulators or a nearby hematopoietic factor.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: LOC129390134 functions as a critical distal silencer within the 10p15 topologically associating domain (TAD), specifically regulating genes involved in early hematopoietic differentiation or sex determination. Given its validation in K562 cells, it likely represses proto-oncogenes or lineage-inappropriate transcription factors in erythroid progenitors to maintain proper differentiation trajectories. The mechanism involves recruitment of co-repressor complexes (e.g., PRC2, Sin3A) to deposit repressive histone marks (H3K27me3) at target promoters. Loss of this silencer (via 10p deletions) leads to aberrant overexpression of target genes, potentially contributing to the hematologic abnormalities (anemia, thrombocytopenia) and developmental defects seen in 10p deletion syndrome (DiGeorge-like phenotype). The location near the telomere suggests it may be part of a sub-telomeric regulatory cluster controlling multiple genes. The duplication burden implies that increased copy number may lead to ectopic silencing of essential genes, exacerbating the phenotype.
