# Oxysterol-Binding Protein 2 (OSBP2): Retina-Enriched Lipid Transporter at ER-Golgi Contact Sites with Chromosome 22q Amplification in Cancer

**Confidence:** MEDIUM

## Description
OSBP2 (Oxysterol-Binding Protein 2) is a 916-amino acid cytosolic lipid-binding protein with 38 publications confirming its partially characterized dark gene status. The protein contains conserved oxysterol-binding protein domains including N-terminal Pleckstrin Homology (PH) domain (IPR001849, PF00169, positions 182-276; cd13284, positions 185-280) that mediates phosphoinositide binding and membrane targeting, plus C-terminal oxysterol-binding protein lipid-binding domain (IPR000648, PF01237, positions 522-905) with conserved oxysterol-binding signature (IPR018494, PS01013, positions 622-632) that binds oxysterols and other sterol lipids. AlphaFold predicts a well-folded structure (pLDDT 70.2), consistent with stable lipid transfer protein architecture. HPA reveals extreme retina-enriched expression (149.3 nTPM) and bone marrow expression (49.6 nTPM), tissue-enhanced distribution, cytosol localization, and biological processes including lipid transport, spermatogenesis, differentiation, and transport. ClinVar documents 18 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants (primarily chromosome 22q11.1-13.33 copy number gains), though specific disease phenotypes beyond cancer remain uncharacterized. STRING interactions reveal OSBP2 functions within ER-Golgi membrane contact site machinery with very high confidence connections to VAPA (0.938) and VAPB (0.785) - integral ER membrane proteins that recruit lipid transfer proteins to membrane contact sites - plus interactions with HSCB (0.810), UQCR10 (0.810), DMTN (0.768), EPB42 (0.747), OSBPL9 (0.696), SLC4A1 (0.661), PLEK2 (0.543), and PLEK (0.511). Recent studies (2020-2023) established OSBP2 as a histone lysine lactylation (Kla)-specific gene and novel therapeutic target for hepatocellular carcinoma, original characterization showing OSBP2 highly expressed in retina (2001), and OSBP2 expression in chronic myeloid leukemia (2003). We hypothesize OSBP2 functions as a non-vesicular lipid transporter that localizes to ER-Golgi membrane contact sites via PH domain binding to Golgi phosphoinositides and VAPA/VAPB recruitment, transferring oxysterols and other sterol lipids between ER and Golgi membranes to maintain lipid homeostasis critical for retinal photoreceptor membrane renewal and spermatogenesis. The VAPA/VAPB interactions confirm membrane contact site localization mechanism shared with other oxysterol-binding proteins, while OSBPL9 connection suggests functional redundancy within the OSBP-related protein (ORP) family. Retina enrichment reflects high demand for lipid transport in photoreceptor outer segment disc membrane biogenesis, and chromosome 22q amplifications in cancer suggest OSBP2 overexpression may contribute to tumor lipid metabolism reprogramming—a mechanism exploitable for hepatocellular carcinoma therapy via Kla-specific gene targeting.
