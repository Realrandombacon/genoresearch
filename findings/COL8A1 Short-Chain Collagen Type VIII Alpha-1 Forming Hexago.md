# COL8A1: Short-Chain Collagen Type VIII Alpha-1 Forming Hexagonal Lattice in Corneal Endothelium Basement Membrane with Vascular Expression and FECD Association

**Quality Score:** 9/10 (EXCELLENT)

**Confidence:** MEDIUM

## Description
COL8A1 (Collagen type VIII alpha-1 chain, UniProt P27658) is a 744-amino acid short-chain collagen that serves as a major structural component of Descemet's membrane, the specialized basement membrane of the corneal endothelium. Unlike fibrillar collagens (types I, II, III), COL8A1 belongs to the non-fibrillar collagen family characterized by interrupted Gly-X-Y repeats and the capacity to form hexagonal lattice structures rather than rope-like fibrils. The protein architecture includes: an N-terminal signal peptide for secretion; multiple collagen triple helix repeat domains (IPR008160, PF01391, 20 copies spanning aa 158-213 and 474-529) forming the characteristic triple-helical rod domain through Gly-X-Y motifs where every third residue is glycine enabling tight packing; and a C-terminal C1q domain (IPR001073, PF00386, SM00110, aa 609-744) belonging to the tumor necrosis factor-like superfamily (IPR008983, SSF49842) that mediates trimerization and higher-order assembly into hexagonal arrays. The AlphaFold predicted structure shows medium confidence (pLDDT 56.7), consistent with collagen's repetitive structure presenting modeling challenges and suggesting flexible regions between structured triple-helical segments. STRING interaction network reveals COL8A1 functions within a collagen interactome with high-confidence associations to: COL8A2 (0.927), its paralog forming heterotrimeric type VIII collagen; GP6 (0.945), glycoprotein VI platelet collagen receptor suggesting vascular roles; COL15A1 (0.801), basement membrane collagen; COL4A3/4/5 (0.773-0.790), type IV collagen network components of basement membranes; COL10A1 (0.781), another short-chain collagen; COL1A1/2 (0.511-0.771), fibrillar collagens; and DR1 (0.762), negative regulator of transcription potentially linking to gene regulation. HPA expression profiles demonstrate tissue-enriched expression with blood vessels showing highest levels (226.1 nTPM), vesicular subcellular localization consistent with secretory pathway trafficking, classification under angiogenesis and cell adhesion biological processes, cancer-enhanced expression pattern suggesting upregulation in tumors, and absence in immune cells indicating specificity to structural/vascular tissues. ClinVar records document 10 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants predominantly consisting of copy number alterations on chromosome 3q11.1-q21.2 (deletions spanning 93.8-124.8 Mb and duplications), though specific point mutations associated with Fuchs endothelial corneal dystrophy (FECD) are not captured in this dataset; FECD is an age-related corneal dystrophy where COL8A1 mutations (particularly Q455K in the triple helical domain) cause abnormal Descemet's membrane thickening, corneal edema, and vision loss requiring transplantation. We hypothesize that COL8A1 functions through a multi-step assembly mechanism: (1) co-translational translocation into ER lumen via signal peptide; (2) prolyl and lysyl hydroxylation establishing triple helix stability; (3) C-terminal C1q domain-mediated trimerization nucleating assembly of three alpha chains (either COL8A1 homotrimers or COL8A1/COL8A2 heterotrimers); (4) secretion and extracellular processing removing N- and C-propeptides; (5) self-assembly into hexagonal lattice structures through lateral interactions between triple helices; (6) integration with other basement membrane components (type IV collagen, laminins, perlecan) forming Descemet's membrane; (7) in vascular endothelium, similar lattice formation contributing to vessel wall integrity and angiogenesis regulation. The blood vessel-enriched expression (226.1 nTPM) suggests underappreciated vascular functions beyond cornea, potentially explaining cancer-enhanced expression through tumor angiogenesis. Medium-confidence AlphaFold structure indicates conformational flexibility enabling adaptation to mechanical stress in corneal tissue. Compared to extensively studied fibrillar collagens, COL8A1 represents a specialized short-chain collagen with distinct assembly properties and tissue-specific functions worthy of investigation in corneal dystrophies, vascular diseases, and tumor microenvironments.
