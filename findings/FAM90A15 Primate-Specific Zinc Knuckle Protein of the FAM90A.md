## Description
FAM90A15 (Gene ID: 389630, UniProt P0C7V4) is a 464 amino acid protein located on chromosome 8p23.1 (NC_000008.11, positions 7256903-7259913, ~3kb region). This gene belongs to subfamily II of the primate-specific FAM90A gene family, which originated from multiple duplications and rearrangements unique to primates (Bosch et al., 2007). InterPro domain analysis reveals membership in the FAM90 family (IPR039213, positions 2-462) with a CCHC-type zinc knuckle domain (IPR041670, PF15288, positions 41-82) characteristic of nucleic acid-binding proteins. AlphaFold structure prediction (AF-P0C7V4-F1) shows low global confidence (pLDDT 49.9), indicating extensive intrinsically disordered regions typical of proteins functioning through dynamic protein-protein or protein-nucleic acid interactions rather than rigid enzymatic scaffolds. Human Protein Atlas classifies this as a pseudogene with no detectable RNA expression across tissues, but notes potential nucleoplasmic and nucleolar localization if expressed - consistent with zinc knuckle proteins that typically bind RNA/DNA in nuclear compartments. STRING database returns no interaction partners, reflecting limited characterization of this primate-specific protein. ClinVar contains 34 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number variations affecting the 8p23 region including deletions (GRCh38/hg38 8p23.3-21.2 chr8:449893-23854904)x1 and duplications (multiple chr8:241530-12698554 gains). The 8p23.1 region is a well-known hotspot for structural variation associated with congenital heart defects, developmental delay, intellectual disability, and behavioral abnormalities. Conservation is limited to primates - no orthologs exist in mouse or zebrafish, consistent with recent evolutionary emergence. Functional hypothesis: FAM90A15 encodes a primate-specific zinc knuckle protein that functions in nucleolar RNA processing or ribosome biogenesis pathways. Zinc knuckle domains typically recognize specific RNA sequences or structures, suggesting roles in post-transcriptional regulation, RNA stability, or ribosomal RNA maturation. The nucleolar localization prediction supports involvement in ribosome assembly or rRNA processing. As a primate-specific gene, FAM90A15 may contribute to human-specific aspects of neurodevelopment or cognition. Disease mechanism: Copy number alterations at 8p23.1 disrupt FAM90A15 dosage alongside neighboring genes, potentially contributing to 8p23.1 deletion/duplication syndrome phenotypes. Haploinsufficiency may impair nucleolar function and ribosome biogenesis while duplication may cause aberrant RNA processing. Given the primate-specific nature, phenotypic consequences may be subtle or context-dependent. Priority experiments include verification of protein expression via targeted proteomics, determination of actual subcellular localization via immunofluorescence, identification of RNA binding partners via CLIP-seq, investigation of nucleolar function in FAM90A15 knockout models, and careful genotype-phenotype correlation in patients with focal 8p23.1 rearrangements to determine independent contribution to developmental phenotypes.
