## Description
EXOSC8 encodes a 276 aa exosome complex component 8 (UniProt Q96B26), also known as RRP43 or CIP3, functioning as an essential core component of the RNA exosome complex that catalyzes 3'-5' exoribonucleolytic degradation of AU-rich element (ARE)-containing mRNAs, rRNA processing, and RNA quality control. The protein contains dual phosphorolytic exoribonuclease domains: N-terminal RNase PH domain 1 (IPR001247/PF01138, aa31-166) and C-terminal RNase PH domain 2 (IPR015847/PF03725, aa193-256) belonging to the PNPase/RNase PH superfamily (IPR027408/G3DSA:3.30.230.70), forming the RRP43 subunit architecture (cd11369/IPR033196, aa6-266) characteristic of exosome core components. AlphaFold prediction shows high confidence (pLDDT 85.9), indicating a well-folded bilobed RNase PH fold typical of phosphorolytic exonucleases. HPA shows ubiquitous expression across all tissues with low tissue specificity, localizing to nucleoplasm, nucleoli fibrillar center, mitotic chromosomes, vesicles, and cytosol—consistent with essential RNA processing roles, with disease involvement in neurodegeneration. STRING interactions reveal EXOSC8 forms the exosome core complex with all exosome components: EXOSC1/2/3/4/5/7 (all 0.997-0.999), EXOSC10 (0.999, catalytic subunit), C1D (0.999, nuclear exosome targeting), DIS3L (0.996, exoribonuclease), and MPHOSPH6 (0.977, M-phase phosphoprotein). ClinVar contains 32 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including 13q13.2-21.32/13q12.3-14.3 copy number losses, 13q11-31.3/13q13.2-21.1 copy number gains, splice site mutations (c.192+1G>A, c.17+5del), and deletions. Literature (49 papers) demonstrates EXOSC8 mutations cause pontocerebellar hypoplasia type 1C (PCH1C) featuring cerebellar/spinal muscular atrophy, spasticity, psychomotor retardation, nystagmus, ophthalmoparesis, epilepsy, and hypomyelination; EXOSC8 promotes colorectal cancer tumorigenesis through ribosome biogenesis regulation; EXOSC8 depletion causes exosome complex destabilization and ARE-mRNA accumulation; EXOSC8 variants expand PCH1C phenotypic spectrum to include mitochondrial respiratory chain dysfunction; and EXOSC8 is essential for embryonic development. EXOSC8 functions as a structural and catalytic core of the 9-subunit exosome—its RNase PH domains form the central channel through which RNA substrates are threaded for processive 3'-5' degradation. The exosome complex requires EXOSC8 for structural integrity; mutations disrupt exosome assembly or catalytic activity, causing toxic accumulation of unstable transcripts (ARE-mRNAs, aberrant rRNAs, cryptic unstable transcripts). PCH1C pathogenesis reflects neuronal vulnerability to RNA metabolism defects: cerebellar Purkinje cells and spinal motor neurons require precise mRNA turnover for synaptic plasticity and survival, making them selectively vulnerable to exosome dysfunction. Cancer-associated overexpression enhances ribosome biogenesis, supporting rapid proliferation in colorectal tumors. Ubiquitous expression underscores EXOSC8's essential role in cellular RNA homeostasis across all tissues.
