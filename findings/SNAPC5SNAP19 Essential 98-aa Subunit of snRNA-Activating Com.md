## Description
SNAPC5 (UniProt O75971), also known as SNAP19, encodes a compact 98-amino acid essential subunit of the snRNA-activating protein complex (SNAPc) belonging to the SNAPc subunit 5 family (IPR029138/PF15497/PTHR15333, aa 1-97). Despite its small size, AlphaFold predicts a well-folded structure (pLDDT: 76.6) consistent with stable protein-protein interaction platform required for complex assembly. The protein localizes to the nucleoplasm and functions in transcription initiation at both RNA polymerase II and III promoters (GO:0006366, GO:0006384).\n\nSTRING interactions define SNAPC5's position within the core transcription machinery: strongest links to SNAPc complex subunits SNAPC3 (0.999), SNAPC4 (0.719), SNAPC1 (0.713), SNAPC2 (0.499), plus general transcription factors GTF2B/TFIIB (0.959), GTF2A2 (0.906), GTF2A1 (0.895), TATA-box binding protein TBP (0.875), RNA polymerase III subunit POLR3D (0.713), and Pol III-specific factor BRF2 (0.547). HPA confirms ubiquitous expression (low tissue specificity, detected in all tissues) with nucleoplasmic localization and classification as essential protein.\n\nWith only 6 papers, SNAPC5 represents a genuinely dark gene despite its fundamental role in snRNA biogenesis. SNAPc binds proximal sequence elements (PSE) of snRNA gene promoters and recruits either Pol II (for U1, U2, U4, U5 snRNAs) or Pol III (for U6 snRNA and 7SK RNA). Emerging 2022-2024 literature reveals translational potential: plasma exosomal lnc-SNAPC5-3:4 serves as biomarker for monitoring anlotinib efficacy in advanced non-small cell lung cancer (20 citations), while lnc-SNAPC5-3:4 inhibits malignancy via miR-224-3p upregulation. ClinVar contains 23 pathogenic variants including promoter variant c.-21T>G. This positions SNAPC5 as an understudied but essential transcription factor with emerging oncological applications.
