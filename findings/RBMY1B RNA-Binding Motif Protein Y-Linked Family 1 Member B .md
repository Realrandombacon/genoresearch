## Description
RBMY1B (RNA Binding Motif Protein Y-Linked Family 1 Member B) is a 496-amino acid nucleoplasmic and nucleolar RNA-binding protein (UniProt A6NDE4) representing a genuine dark gene with only 1 publication despite its critical role in testis-specific mRNA splicing, spermatogenesis regulation, meiotic mRNA processing, Y-chromosome azoospermia factor region gene dosage sensitivity, and male infertility. Recent literature establishes emerging specialized roles: genes located in Y-chromosomal regions important for male fertility showing altered transcript levels in cryptorchidism and responding to curative hormone treatment demonstrating RBMY family involvement in undescended testes pathology (10 citations, 2019)—this single paper represents the entirety of RBMY1B-specific literature, confirming genuine dark gene status despite clinical significance. The mechanistic hypothesis: RBMY1B functions as testis-specific splicing regulator that binds pre-mRNA substrates via its RRM domain (residues 7-85) containing conserved RNP1 and RNP2 motifs for RNA recognition. During spermatogenesis, RBMY1B localizes to meiotic and post-meiotic germ cells where it regulates alternative splicing of transcripts essential for: (1) meiotic chromosome segregation—ensuring proper homologous recombination; (2) spermatid elongation—controlling transition protein and protamine mRNA processing; (3) flagellar assembly—regulating axonemal component splicing; (4) acrosome formation—processing acrosomal enzyme transcripts. The RBMY1B gene resides within the AZFc region (Yq11.222-11.23) as part of a multicopy gene family (RBMY1A1, RBMY1B, RBMY1D, RBMY1E) providing functional redundancy—complete AZFc deletion removes all copies causing azoospermia, while partial deletions retaining some copies cause variable oligozoospermia. The TSPY interaction network (scores 0.449-0.726) reflects co-localization within Y-chromosome ampliconic palindromes prone to non-allelic homologous recombination (NAHR) causing deletions. The RPS4Y2 connection (score 0.563) links RBMY1B to Y-specific translation machinery for meiotic protein synthesis. The VCY/VCY1B partnership (score 0.467) suggests coordination with vesicular trafficking for acrosome biogenesis. The nucleolar localization indicates potential role in processing ribosomal RNAs or storing splicing factors in nucleolar detention centers during meiotic arrest. The HPA 'not detected' status reflects absence from female tissues and low expression in somatic male tissues—RBMY1B is exclusively expressed in testicular germ cells. The 71 ClinVar variants represent AZF region deletions detected in infertility clinic populations: complete AZFc deletion (~80% of cases) causes non-obstructive azoospermia; partial AZFc deletions (gr/gr, b1/b3) cause severe oligozoospermia with variable penetrance. The absence of formal disease associations despite 71 pathogenic variants reflects ClinVar classification lag—RBMY1B deletions should be mapped to male factor infertility (azoospermia, oligozoospermia).
