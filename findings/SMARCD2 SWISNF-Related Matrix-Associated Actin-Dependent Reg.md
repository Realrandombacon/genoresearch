## Description
SMARCD2 encodes a 531 aa intracellular chromatin regulatory protein (UniProt Q92925), also known as BAF60B or SGD2, that functions as an essential subunit of the BAF (BRG1/BRM-associated factor) SWI/SNF ATP-dependent chromatin remodeling complex required for lineage-specific transcriptional programs during hematopoietic differentiation, particularly neutrophil maturation. This well-studied gene (53 publications) has critical clinical significance for Specific Granule Deficiency type 2 and cardiovascular disease. The protein features specialized chromatin remodeling architecture: SWIB/MDM2 domain (IPR003121/IPR019835/IPR030090/IPR036885/PF02201/PS51925/SM00151/cd17675, residues 301-391/306-383/307-386) mediating protein-protein interactions within the BAF complex and potential MDM2-related regulatory functions; SWIFT domain (IPR063302/PF29592, residues 190-302) providing additional interaction surfaces for complex assembly; classified within SMARCD/SWI-SNF chromatin remodeling family (PTHR13844, residues 128-480) with GO terms for transcription regulation and chromatin regulation. STRING interactions reveal SMARCD2 operates within extremely high-confidence BAF chromatin remodeling complex networks: SMARCC1 (0.999), SMARCE1 (0.999), SMARCB1 (0.999), SMARCA4 (0.999), SMARCA2 (0.999), SMARCC2 (0.999) forming the core BAF complex scaffold; ARID1A (0.997)/ARID1B (0.995), AT-rich interaction domain proteins conferring DNA targeting specificity; ACTL6A (0.997), actin-like protein 6A in chromatin remodeling; SMARCD1 (0.995), paralogous BAF60A subunit. HPA confirms ubiquitous expression with low tissue specificity (detected in all tissues), low cancer and immune cell specificity, nucleoplasm subcellular localization, classified as disease-related chromatin regulator with transcription regulation function. ClinVar lists 41 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including nonsense mutations causing premature termination (c.1099C>T p.Arg367Ter within SWIB domain, c.1217dup p.Tyr406Ter, c.310C>T p.Arg104Ter), frameshift deletion (c.572del p.Lys191fs), splice site mutations disrupting mRNA processing (c.444+1G>A, c.216+1G>C), large insertion (c.763_764insAGAAGATGTTGTTATACTGTATTATGGCTGATTTTCTTTTTAATATATTTAGCCAAGTGATGCA), start codon variant (c.4T>G p.Ser2Ala), intronic variant (c.1317+49G>A), and copy number gain (17q23.2-24.1 58-62Mb x3), demonstrating extreme mutational sensitivity causing Specific Granule Deficiency type 2 though specific disease mechanisms remain under investigation. AlphaFold structure prediction yields high confidence (global pLDDT 76.1), revealing a well-folded protein with structured SWIB and SWIFT domains characteristic of stable BAF complex subunits. Mechanistically, SMARCD2 operates through integrated modes: (1) Neutrophil maturation—2023-2025 studies demonstrate SMARCD2 mutations disrupt neutrophil differentiation causing Specific Granule Deficiency type 2 characterized by neutropenia, recurrent life-threatening infections, absent specific granules, and defective chemotaxis; (2) BAF complex assembly—SWIB domain mediates interactions with catalytic subunits SMARCA4/SMARCA2 (BRG1/BRM ATPases) and scaffold subunits SMARCC1/SMARCC2 (BAF170/BAF155) enabling ATP-dependent nucleosome sliding and chromatin accessibility changes; (3) Myeloid lineage specification—2025 study describes novel nonsense mutation causing dysplasia of all myeloid cell lines, indicating broad role in hematopoietic differentiation beyond neutrophils; (4) Hematopoietic stem cell transplantation—2024 case report demonstrates curative HSCT in child with homozygous SMARCD2 variant, establishing treatment paradigm; (5) Aortic aneurysm/dissection—2025 study shows endothelial SMARCD2 exacerbates AAD through inflammatory signaling pathways, suggesting vascular remodeling role; (6) Chromatin accessibility regulation—BAF complex recruitment to lineage-specific enhancers enables transcription factor binding and gene activation during differentiation; (7) Paralog specificity—SMARCD1/BAF60A and SMARCD3/BAF60C represent tissue-specific paralogs with distinct developmental roles. Loss-of-function causes defective BAF complex assembly, impaired chromatin remodeling at myeloid-specific genes, blocked neutrophil maturation, absent specific granule formation, Specific Granule Deficiency type 2 with severe infections, myeloid dysplasia, and increased aortic aneurysm susceptibility.
