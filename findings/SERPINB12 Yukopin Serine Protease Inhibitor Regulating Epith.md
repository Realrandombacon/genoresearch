## Description
SERPINB12 (also known as Yukopin) encodes a 405 aa serine protease inhibitor (UniProt Q96P63) located at 18q21.33, functioning as a critical regulator of epithelial differentiation, skin barrier formation, and cancer progression. InterPro analysis confirms its canonical serpin architecture: N-terminal domain (G3DSA:3.30.497.10, aa 13-198/302-352) and C-terminal domain (G3DSA:2.30.39.10, aa 199-301/353-398) forming the characteristic alpha-1-antitrypsin fold (IPR000215/PF00079), with a conserved reactive center loop signature (PS00284, aa 378-388) essential for protease inhibition. AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (pLDDT 88.7), indicating a stable, well-folded inhibitory conformation typical of functional serpins. STRING interactions reveal a specialized network in epithelial differentiation and skin barrier formation: CASP14 (0.731, caspase-14, cornification), KPRP (0.659, keratinocyte protein, barrier), NCBP2 (0.591, nuclear cap-binding), FLG2 (0.527, filaggrin-2, skin barrier), SPRR4 (0.516, small proline-rich protein), TGM3 (0.508, transglutaminase-3, crosslinking), SBSN (0.508, suprabasin), DSG1 (0.484, desmoglein-1, adhesion), and KRTDAP (0.416, keratinocyte differentiation). HPA expression shows strong group enrichment in stratified epithelia: skin (55.5 nTPM), vagina (37.0 nTPM), cervix (24.1 nTPM), consistent with roles in barrier maintenance. ClinVar reports 54 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large 18q copy number gains (x3, x4) and losses (x1), suggesting dosage sensitivity in developmental syndromes. Literature (31 papers) details emerging mechanistic roles: (1) NSCLC Progression - smoking-induced SERPINB12 upregulation activating WNT signaling to promote proliferation and metastasis; (2) Enterocyte Differentiation - SLFN12-SERPINB12-deubiquitylase complex driving human enterocyte maturation; (3) EoE Steroid Dependency - SERPINB12 expression predicting steroid response in eosinophilic esophagitis; (4) Skin Barrier - interaction with CASP14 and filaggrin family members regulating cornification and desquamation. Functional hypothesis: SERPINB12 likely functions through: (1) Protease Inhibition - binding and inhibiting CASP14 or related proteases to modulate terminal differentiation and cornification; (2) WNT Pathway Activation - stabilizing β-catenin or upstream regulators to drive proliferation in cancer contexts; (3) SLFN12 Complex Formation - partnering with schlafen-12 and deubiquitylases to regulate protein stability during enterocyte differentiation; (4) Barrier Maintenance - cooperating with filaggrin, SPRR, and transglutaminase networks to establish epithelial integrity; (5) Dosage Sensitivity - 18q amplification causing SERPINB12 overexpression, potentially driving WNT-mediated tumor growth, while deletion impairs barrier function. Disease implications include NSCLC prognosis, EoE treatment response, skin barrier disorders, and potential roles in gastrointestinal epithelial diseases. Despite 31 papers, the precise native protease targets, structural mechanism of WNT activation, and therapeutic potential of SERPINB12 inhibition in smoking-related cancers remain underexplored.
