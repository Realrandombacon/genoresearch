## Description
RABGGTB (Rab Geranylgeranyl Transferase Type-2 Subunit Beta) is a 331-amino acid catalytic subunit of the Rab geranylgeranyltransferase complex (UniProt P53611) that represents a genuine dark gene with only 29 publications despite essential cellular function and clear disease relevance. Structurally, RABGGTB contains a canonical prenyltransferase alpha-alpha toroid domain (IPR001330/PF00432, residues 18-315) and belongs to the geranylgeranyl transferase type-2 beta subunit family (IPR026873/PTHR11774, residues 18-324), placing it within the terpenoid cyclase/protein prenyltransferase superfamily (IPR008930/G3DSA:1.50.10.20). AlphaFold predicts an exceptionally high-confidence structure (pLDDT 96.7), indicating a stable, well-folded enzymatic architecture typical of prenyltransferases. Functionally, RABGGTB localizes to vesicles with ubiquitous expression across all tissues (HPA: detected in all, low tissue specificity) and is classified as an essential protein. The enzyme forms a heterodimeric complex with RABGGTA (alpha subunit) to catalyze post-translational geranylgeranylation of Rab GTPases—adding two 20-carbon geranylgeranyl lipid groups to C-terminal cysteines, enabling membrane association and vesicular trafficking. STRING interactions confirm this mechanism through near-perfect associations with PTAR1 (0.999, prenyltransferase alpha subunit), RABGGTA (0.999, catalytic partner), CHM/CHML (0.923-0.961, Rab escort proteins that deliver substrates), and multiple Rab GTPases (RAB1A: 0.931, RAB3A: 0.808). Critically, ClinVar documents 26 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants (copy number losses/gains, splice variants, SNVs) yet ZERO formally associated Mendelian diseases—a striking gap. Recent literature definitively links RABGGTB to amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS): AAV9-RABGGTB-GFP+ injection protects SOD1G93A mice (6 citations, 2023), and RABGGTB plays a critical role in ALS pathogenesis (3 citations, 2023). Mouse models show skeletal muscle-specific deficiency induces myopathy and exacerbates HMG-CoA reductase deficiency symptoms (2026). The mechanistic hypothesis: RABGGTB functions as the catalytic beta subunit that, together with RABGGTA and CHM/CHML escort proteins, geranylgeranylates Rab GTPases critical for motor neuron vesicular trafficking, autophagy, and synaptic vesicle recycling. Defective Rab prenylation impairs membrane trafficking in long axons, leading to motor neuron degeneration in ALS. The absence of formal disease nomenclature despite pathogenic variants suggests recent discovery (ALS link only 2023), phenotypic complexity requiring specific genetic backgrounds, or compensation by related prenyltransferases (RABGGTA paralogs). Future work should define RABGGTB-specific Rab substrates in motor neurons and investigate whether heterozygous variants cause milder neuromuscular phenotypes.
