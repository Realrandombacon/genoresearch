## Description
SP140 (UniProt Q13342), also known as LYSP100-A/LYSP100-B, encodes an 867 aa interferon-inducible nuclear body protein functioning as a chromatin reader and transcriptional regulator restricted to immune cells. InterPro analysis reveals a characteristic SP100 family multi-domain architecture: an N-terminal Homogeneously Staining Region (HSR) domain (IPR004865, residues 22-138) mediating nuclear body targeting and protein-protein interactions, a central SAND domain (IPR000770/G3DSA:3.10.390.10, residues 580-661) providing DNA-binding capability for sequence-specific chromatin recognition, a Plant Homeodomain (PHD) zinc finger (IPR001965/IPR011011/cd15626, residues 692-734) reading histone methylation marks (likely H3K4me3), and a C-terminal Bromodomain (IPR001487/cd05501/G3DSA:1.20.920.10, residues 754-859) recognizing acetylated lysines on histones and transcription factors. AlphaFold predicts medium-low confidence structure (pLDDT 57.0) indicating a partially disordered protein where the four domains likely form structured modules connected by flexible linkers enabling conformational adaptability for chromatin scanning and partner recruitment. STRING interactions define an immune-chromatin network: SP140L (0.903, paralogous nuclear body protein), IGKV1-33/IGKV1D-33 (0.900, immunoglobulin kappa variable genes suggesting B-cell specific functions), AIM2 (0.689, absent in melanoma 2 inflammasome sensor linking to innate immunity), PPIE (0.638, peptidyl-prolyl isomerase E), TENT5C (0.592, terminal nucleotidyltransferase), PML (0.574, promyelocytic leukemia protein forming nuclear bodies), ISG20 (0.563, interferon-stimulated gene 20 exonuclease), DIS3 (0.540, exosome complex exonuclease), and CBX5 (0.539, chromobox homolog 5/HP1α heterochromatin protein). HPA classifies SP140 as group-enriched with highest expression in lymphoid tissue (52.3 nTPM) and intestine (14.5 nTPM), immune cell enhanced, detected in many tissues, low cancer specificity, localized to nucleoli fibrillar center and mitochondria though primarily nuclear, reflecting specialized role in immune cell chromatin regulation and interferon responses. ClinVar lists 69 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number gains on 2q (2q35-37.3x3, 2q33.3-37.3x3), splice site mutations (c.2059-15T>G, c.1058-1G>A, c.406+4A>T), intronic variants (c.1565-6C>G, c.1445-10T>A, c.1159+15C>A), and synonymous changes (c.1158A>G) though no single-gene disorder is definitively assigned suggesting contribution to 2q37 deletion/duplication syndromes (Albright hereditary osteodystrophy-like phenotypes) or immune dysregulation disorders. Functional hypothesis: SP140 operates as an interferon-inducible chromatin reader coordinating immune gene expression through multi-layered mechanisms: (1) Nuclear body assembly - HSR domain mediates recruitment to PML nuclear bodies where SP140 concentrates with partner proteins forming transcriptional hubs; (2) Chromatin reading - SAND domain binds specific DNA sequences while PHD finger recognizes H3K4me3 marks at active promoters and bromodomain binds acetylated histones (H3K27ac, H4Kac) at enhancers, integrating multiple epigenetic signals; (3) Interferon response - As an interferon-stimulated gene, SP140 amplifies antiviral transcriptional programs by recruiting chromatin modifiers to ISG promoters; (4) B-cell regulation - Strong association with immunoglobulin kappa genes (IGKV1-33) suggests specific role in B-cell development or antibody gene regulation; (5) Inflammasome modulation - AIM2 interaction links SP140 to cytosolic DNA sensing and IL-1β production pathways; (6) Disease mechanisms - CNV gains cause SP140 overexpression potentially disrupting chromatin homeostasis contributing to 2q37 syndromes; loss may impair interferon responses increasing viral susceptibility; immune cell restriction suggests phenotypes manifest primarily in hematopoietic lineages; (7) Therapeutic implications - Bromodomain inhibitors (BET inhibitors) may indirectly affect SP140 function; SP140 expression levels could serve as biomarker for interferon pathway activation in autoimmune diseases. Conservation extends to mouse and other mammals indicating preserved immune regulatory function. Dark aspects requiring investigation include: specific DNA binding sequence motif for SAND domain, histone mark specificity of PHD/bromodomains, complete interactome in different immune cell types (B cells vs T cells vs macrophages), role in viral infections beyond interferon induction, contribution to immunoglobulin class switching or somatic hypermutation, genotype-phenotype correlations in 2q37 syndromes, potential SUMOylation or other PTMs regulating nuclear body dynamics, crosstalk with SP140L paralog, involvement in autoimmune diseases (given immune restriction and interferon inducibility), and therapeutic targeting strategies for modulating SP140 in immune disorders.
