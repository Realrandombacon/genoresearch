## Description
MYCBP2 encodes a massive 4678 aa E3 ubiquitin-protein ligase (UniProt O75592), also known as PAM (Protein Associated with Myc) or PHR1, functioning as a central signaling hub in neuronal development, axon guidance, and synapse formation. As a member of the conserved PHR family (Pam/Highwire/RPM-1), MYCBP2 contains multiple functional domains: C-terminal RING finger domain (IPR001841/PF13639, aa4428-4479) for E3 ligase activity, B-box type 2 zinc finger (cd19799, aa4259-4307), RCC1 repeats (IPR000408, aa601-718) for guanine nucleotide exchange, APC10/DOC domain (IPR004939, aa3718-3897) for substrate recognition, and multiple PHR domains (G3DSA:2.60.120.820) characteristic of this family. HPA shows ubiquitous expression (detected in all tissues) with nucleoplasmic, vesicular, and cytosolic localization, consistent with roles in ubiquitin-mediated protein turnover (Ubl conjugation pathway). STRING interactions reveal MYCBP2 functions in multi-protein complexes: FBXO45 (0.999, F-box substrate adaptor), E2 conjugating enzyme UBE2D2 (0.935), MYC oncoprotein (0.923), ubiquitin chains (UBC/UBB/UBA52 all >0.900), LRPAP1 (0.851, LDL receptor chaperone), NMNAT2 (0.690, NAD+ biosynthesis enzyme critical for axon maintenance), and SKP1 (0.454, SCF complex component). ClinVar contains 267 pathogenic variants including frameshifts (p.Lys1722fs), missense mutations across the protein length, and inversions, associated with emerging MYCBP2-related neurodevelopmental disorder (NDD) featuring intellectual disability, autism spectrum features, and hypotonia. Literature (94 papers) demonstrates MYCBP2 regulates axon termination through NMNAT2 ubiquitination and degradation, controls presynaptic active zone formation, modulates cAMP signaling via adenylyl cyclase regulation, inhibits mTOR pathway affecting protein synthesis and neuronal growth, and mediates spinal cord injury recovery through extracellular vesicle delivery. MYCBP2 likely functions as a molecular brake on axon growth—ubiquitinating growth-promoting proteins (NMNAT2, DLK/MAP3K12) to terminate axon extension and promote synapse stabilization. Loss-of-function variants cause hyperactive axon growth and defective synaptogenesis, explaining neurodevelopmental phenotypes. The giant protein size (4678 aa) enables simultaneous coordination of multiple signaling pathways (cAMP, mTOR, ubiquitin-proteasome) through distinct domain modules, positioning MYCBP2 as a master regulator of neuronal connectivity.
