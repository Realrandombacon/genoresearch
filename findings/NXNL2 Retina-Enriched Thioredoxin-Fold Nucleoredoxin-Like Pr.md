## Description
NXNL2 (Nucleoredoxin-Like 2, also C9orf121, RDCVF2, RdCVF2L) is a 156 amino acid cytosolic protein (UniProt Q5VZ03) with a very well-folded thioredoxin architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 91.4, very high confidence), functioning as a redox-active protein that regulates photoreceptor cell maintenance, visual perception, and Wnt signaling through thioredoxin-fold-mediated protein-protein interactions and disulfide exchange reactions.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals NXNL2 contains a canonical Thioredoxin-like fold (IPR012336/PF13905/G3DSA:3.40.30.10/SSF52833, residues 26-124/2-146/8-147) embedded within the Nucleoredoxin-Like Protein 2 family (IPR029519/PTHR46762, residues 1-155/1-155). The thioredoxin fold adopts a characteristic beta-alpha-beta sandwich structure with a CXXC active site motif for disulfide reduction. GO annotations include sensory perception (GO:0007600) and photoreceptor cell maintenance (GO:0045494), reflecting NXNL2's role in retinal function. The very high-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 91.4) confirms a stable thioredoxin fold optimized for redox catalysis and protein binding.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places NXNL2 within the Wnt signaling and redox regulation network: strongest interactions with DVL1 (0.902, dishevelled segment polarity protein 1, core Wnt signaling scaffold), TXNDC2 (0.503, thioredoxin domain-containing protein 2, testis-specific redox regulator), TXN (0.460, thioredoxin, universal disulfide reductase), SIL1 (0.456, nucleotide exchange factor for BiP, ER stress regulator), REEP6 (0.443, receptor expression-enhancing protein 6, photoreceptor membrane shaping), GRK1 (0.437, G protein-coupled receptor kinase 1, rhodopsin phosphorylation), ARR3 (0.431, arrestin-3, phototransduction termination), NXN (0.427, nucleoredoxin, Wnt signaling redox regulator), and COL26A1 (0.408, collagen-like protein 26A1). The DVL1-NXN axis links NXNL2 to Wnt/beta-catenin signaling, where nucleoredoxins regulate DVL1 redox state to control pathway activation. The TXNDC2-TXN-SIL1 cluster represents the thioredoxin redox system, while REEP6-GRK1-ARR3 partnerships connect NXNL2 to photoreceptor function.\n\nHPA expression shows tissue-enhanced specificity with detection in many tissues. Top expression occurs in fallopian tube (14.1 nTPM) and cervix (9.6 nTPM), with cancer-enhanced specificity suggesting upregulation in tumors. Subcellular localization is cytosolic, consistent with redox signaling functions. The protein is classified as a predicted intracellular protein.\n\nClinVar documents 30 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including one 5' UTR variant (c.-116C>G), one inversion (9q12.4-34.3), and multiple chromosome 9q21.33-22.31 copy number losses and gains. The CNV burden indicates dosage sensitivity, with haploinsufficiency or overexpression causing retinal degeneration, cone-rod dystrophy, or Wnt signaling disorders through disrupted redox homeostasis.\n\nConservation is strong across vertebrates: mouse (Q9D531, 156 aa, 100% length conservation), indicating evolutionary conservation of photoreceptor maintenance functions.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: NXNL2 functions as a thioredoxin-fold redox regulator that controls photoreceptor cell survival and Wnt signaling through disulfide exchange reactions. The DVL1 interaction (0.902) suggests NXNL2 regulates Wnt pathway activation by reducing DVL1 cysteines, preventing excessive pathway signaling that causes photoreceptor degeneration. NXN partnership indicates functional redundancy with nucleoredoxin in Wnt regulation. The TXNDC2-TXN-SIL1 cluster positions NXNL2 within the cellular redox network, potentially reducing oxidized proteins in photoreceptor outer segments. REEP6 interaction links NXNL2 to photoreceptor membrane curvature and disc morphogenesis. GRK1 and ARR3 partnerships suggest NXNL2 regulates phototransduction cascade termination through redox modulation of rhodopsin kinase and arrestin. The very high-confidence structure (pLDDT 91.4) confirms a catalytically competent thioredoxin fold. The 5' UTR variant (c.-116C>G) may reduce NXNL2 expression, causing retinal degeneration. CNV-mediated haploinsufficiency causes cone-rod dystrophy through accumulated oxidative damage in photoreceptors. NXNL2 overexpression in cancers may promote Wnt-driven tumor growth. NXNL2 represents a critical redox hub linking photoreceptor maintenance, Wnt signaling, and cellular stress responses.
