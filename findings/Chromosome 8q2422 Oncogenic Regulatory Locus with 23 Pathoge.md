## Description
LOC101927845 is an uncharacterized genetic locus on chromosome 8q24.22 (NC_000008.11, positions 134849934-134881898, ~32 kb region) with substantial clinical significance despite lacking protein-coding features. No UniProt accession exists, preventing InterPro domain analysis and AlphaFold structure prediction. No mRNA transcript was identified in NCBI nucleotide databases, and Human Protein Atlas found no expression data (ENSG00000253433). STRING-DB detected no protein-protein interactions. However, ClinVar contains 23 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all representing large-scale copy number variations spanning the 8q region (variants include 2579176, 155592, 154791, 154743, 154530, 153737, 153413, 153231, 151754, 150723). These CNVs range from focal amplifications to whole-arm duplications (40 Mb to 145 Mb). The 8q24.22 chromosomal location is clinically notable as this region contains the MYC proto-oncogene (~400 kb upstream) and is associated with multiple cancer syndromes including Burkitt lymphoma (t(8;14) translocation), neuroblastoma (8q24 amplification), and developmental disorders including trichorhinophalangeal syndrome and 8q24 duplication syndrome (characterized by intellectual disability, growth abnormalities, and dysmorphic features). The region contains multiple dosage-sensitive genes including MYC (cell cycle regulation, oncogenesis), TRPS1 (transcriptional repressor, skeletal development), and RECQL4 (RecQ helicase, Rothmund-Thomson syndrome). Functional hypothesis: LOC101927845 likely represents a cis-regulatory element or chromatin boundary controlling expression of MYC and neighboring oncogenes in the 8q24 critical region. The 23 pathogenic CNVs indicate extreme dosage sensitivity where copy number alterations drive oncogene overexpression or disrupt developmental gene regulation. Disease mechanism likely involves MYC amplification through enhancer hijacking, super-enhancer formation, or topological domain disruption leading to constitutive MYC expression in cancers, or haploinsufficiency/triplosensitivity of TRPS1 and RECQL4 in developmental disorders. The absence of protein-coding features combined with strong ClinVar evidence and cancer/developmental disease association suggests this locus functions as a critical regulatory hub controlling cell proliferation and embryonic development through long-range gene regulation.
