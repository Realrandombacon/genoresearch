# DNAH9: Axonemal Dynein Heavy Chain 9 — Ciliary Motor Protein with Ciliopathy Associations

## Description
DNAH9 encodes a 4,486 amino acid axonemal dynein heavy chain (UniProt Q9NYC9) that functions as an ATP-dependent microtubule motor protein essential for ciliary and flagellar beating. The protein contains multiple conserved dynein motor domains including P-loop NTP hydrolase (G3DSA:3.40.50.300), dynein heavy chain N-terminal domains (G3DSA:1.20.140.100, 3.20.180.20), and the D6 dynein motor region (G3DSA:1.10.8.720, residues 4018-4180). AlphaFold predicts a well-folded N-terminal structure (pLDDT 92.4, residues 1-798). DNAH9 shows tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in fallopian tube (33.1 nTPM), brain (13.5 nTPM), and choroid plexus (11.9 nTPM). STRING analysis reveals 10 high-confidence interaction partners (all scores >0.72): DNAI2 (0.942), DNAI1 (0.931), CCDC114 (0.804), DNAH12 (0.795), DNALI1 (0.765), WDR78 (0.759), DNAAF1 (0.750), NME8 (0.749), CCDC103 (0.748), and CCDC65 (0.722) — forming the outer dynein arm complex. ClinVar documents 260 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including nonsense mutations (p.Tyr2509Ter, p.Glu1071Ter, p.Gln1266Ter, p.Arg524Ter) and frameshifts causing Primary Ciliary Dyskinesia and Kartagener syndrome. This gene localizes to cytosol, flagellar centriole, and end piece, functioning in cilium biogenesis. The mechanistic model: DNAH9 hydrolyzes ATP via its AAA+ motor domain to generate conformational changes that slide adjacent microtubule doublets, producing the bending motion of cilia/flagella. Disruption abolishes mucociliary clearance and sperm motility.
