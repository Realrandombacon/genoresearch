# CCDC177: DUF4659 Coiled-Coil Scaffold Protein with Neural/Retinal Expression and 14q CNV Disease Association

## Description
CCDC177 (Gene ID: 56936, UniProt Q9NQR7) encodes a 707-amino acid coiled-coil domain protein containing DUF4659 (Domain of Unknown Function 4659, Pfam PF15558, InterPro IPR029090, residues 334-705). This domain architecture suggests a scaffolding function where the N-terminal coiled-coil region mediates oligomerization or protein-protein interactions while the C-terminal DUF4659 domain provides binding specificity. Multiple evidence lines support functional importance: (1) HPA shows enriched expression in brain (4.3 nTPM) and retina (4.1 nTPM) with cytosolic localization, indicating neural/visual system roles; (2) STRING interactions reveal 10 medium-confidence partners including LENG1 (0.562), MYO15B (0.537), TPGS1 (0.493), HRCT1 (0.475), OOSP1 (0.469), and FOXN4 (0.446) — LENG1 is a leukocyte-derived chemotaxin, MYO15B is an unconventional myosin involved in stereocilia elongation (hearing), FOXN4 is a forkhead transcription factor critical for retinal development; (3) ClinVar contains 10 pathogenic copy number variants spanning 14q24.1, including deletions and duplications associated with chromosome 14q syndromes featuring intellectual disability, developmental delay, and retinal dystrophy; (4) Strong evolutionary conservation evidenced by mouse ortholog Ccdc177 (Q3UHB8, 706 aa, ~99% identity) and zebrafish/pan troglodytes orthologs. Functional hypothesis: CCDC177 functions as a cytosolic scaffolding protein that organizes multi-protein complexes in neural and retinal cells. The coiled-coil domain mediates homodimerization or heterodimerization while DUF4659 recruits client proteins. Interaction with FOXN4 suggests involvement in retinal transcriptional regulation; MYO15B interaction suggests potential roles in cytoskeletal organization or intracellular transport. Pathogenic 14q CNVs disrupt CCDC177 dosage, causing haploinsufficiency or overexpression that perturbs scaffolding complex stoichiometry, leading to neurodevelopmental phenotypes (intellectual disability, developmental delay) and retinal abnormalities (dystrophy, vision loss). The 14q24.1 region is a known neurodevelopmental critical region — CCDC177 may contribute to 14q deletion syndrome phenotypes alongside neighboring genes. This represents a classic "dark gene" with clear domain architecture, tissue-specific expression, disease association, and interaction partners but no experimentally validated molecular function.
