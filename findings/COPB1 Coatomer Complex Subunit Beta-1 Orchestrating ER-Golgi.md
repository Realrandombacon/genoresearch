## Description
COPB1 (Coatomer Subunit Beta 1) is a 953 amino acid essential component of the COPI (Coat Protein Complex I) vesicular coat machinery that mediates retrograde transport from the Golgi apparatus to the endoplasmic reticulum, serving as a critical regulator of protein trafficking, Golgi integrity, and cellular homeostasis. InterPro analysis identifies characteristic coatomer architecture including adaptin-like N-terminal domain (IPR002553, PF01602, residues 23-530) for vesicle coat assembly and intracellular protein transport (GO:0006886), coatomer beta C-terminal domain (IPR011710, PF07718, residues 667-806) for structural scaffolding, coatomer beta subunit appendage platform domain (IPR029446, PF14806, residues 813-944) for cargo recognition, armadillo-type fold superfamily (IPR011989, IPR016024, SSF48371, residues 24-697) providing leucine-rich repeat variant structural homology (G3DSA:1.25.10.10), and coatomer beta subunit family signature (IPR016460, PIRSF005727, PTHR10635, residues 4-951). AlphaFold structure (AF-P53618-F1) predicts high confidence (pLDDT 82.3) across the 953 residue sequence, indicating a stable multi-domain scaffold protein suitable for COPI coat assembly and membrane curvature generation. HPA data classifies COPB1 as an essential disease-related protein involved in ER-Golgi transport, host-virus interaction, and protein transport, with Golgi apparatus, vesicle, and cytosol localization, low tissue specificity (detected in all tissues), and implications in cataract and intellectual disability. STRING interactions reveal exceptionally tight coupling to the complete COPI complex: COPZ1 (Coatomer subunit zeta-1, 0.999), COPG1 (0.999), COPA (Coatomer subunit alpha, 0.999), COPE (0.999), COPB2 (Coatomer subunit beta-2, 0.999), ARCN1 (Delta-coat protein, 0.999), COPG2 (0.998) as the seven COPI subunits; ARF1 (ADP-ribosylation factor 1, 0.992) as the GTPase that recruits COPI to membranes; COPZ2 (0.977) as zeta isoform; and ARFGAP3 (0.961) as the GTPase activating protein regulating ARF1 cycling. ClinVar lists 18 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including splice site mutations (c.-57-2A>G, c.957+1G>T), missense changes (p.Gln701Arg, p.Phe551Val), large copy number gains on 11p15.5-14.2, 11p15.2-14.1, 11p15.3-13, and 11p15.5-13 (multiple independent CNV events spanning 2-34 Mb), and deletions, causing Baralle-Macken syndrome characterized by neurological dysfunction, intellectual disability, and developmental delay. Literature (45 papers) establishes COPB1 as a multifunctional trafficking regulator: biallelic COPB1 variants cause Baralle-Macken syndrome with neurological phenotypes; COPB1 deficiency triggers osteoporosis with elevated iron stores by inducing osteoblast ferroptosis linking trafficking to bone metabolism; COPB1-knockdown induced type I interferon signaling activation inhibits Chlamydia psittaci intracellular proliferation revealing antiviral roles; expanding clinical and immunological phenotypes of COPB1 deficiency include immune dysregulation; and COPB1 functions in Golgi-to-ER retrieval of escaped ER residents maintaining organelle identity. As a well-studied essential trafficking component with defined monogenic disease, COPB1 represents a critical node connecting vesicular transport to neurodevelopment, bone homeostasis, and host-pathogen interactions through COPI-mediated retrograde cargo sorting.
