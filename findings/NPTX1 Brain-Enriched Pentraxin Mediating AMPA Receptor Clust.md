## Description
NPTX1 (Neuronal Pentraxin 1, UniProt Q15818) is a 432 aa secreted neuronal protein that functions as an activity-dependent regulator of excitatory synapse formation and AMPA receptor clustering. Structurally, AlphaFold predicts a confident fold (pLDDT=75.8) dominated by the C-terminal pentraxin domain (IPR001759, PF00354, aa 222-428) belonging to the Concanavalin A-like lectin superfamily (IPR013320), enabling calcium-dependent ligand binding. The N-terminal region (aa 1-221) contains a coiled-coil domain mediating oligomerization into pentameric structures typical of pentraxins. Functionally, STRING interactions reveal specific connections to AMPA receptor subunits GRIA1 (0.673) and GRIA4 (0.522), the neuronal pentraxin receptor NPTXR (0.655), and synaptic organizers SLITRK1 (0.634) and SLITRK2 (0.806), positioning NPTX1 as a hub for excitatory synapse assembly. HPA shows strong brain enrichment (188.4 nTPM) with Golgi/vesicle localization, consistent with regulated secretion. ClinVar identifies 25 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including missense mutations (p.Gly190Asp, p.Gln370Pro, p.Ala279Gly) and copy number gains, with recent literature linking NPTX1 to spinocerebellar ataxia type 50 (SCA50), dystonia with brain iron accumulation, and neuropathic pain via SIGMAR1-mediated AMPA receptor trafficking. Literature (148 papers) reveals emerging roles in adrenocortical carcinoma suppression, osteogenic differentiation, and neuroglial proliferation via β-catenin regulation. The gene represents a 'semi-dark' synaptic organizer where canonical pentraxin structure is established, but specific disease mechanisms in ataxia, pain processing, and cancer remain underexplored.
