## Description
LOC113960610 is a Sharpr-MPRA regulatory region 13245 located on chromosome 20q13 (genomic coordinates: 46040909-46041203 on NC_000020.11). This 295 bp genomic sequence was predicted as a transcriptional regulatory region based on ENCODE chromatin state analysis and experimentally validated as a functional repressive element (silencer) using Sharpr-MPRA in HepG2 liver carcinoma cells. As a non-coding regulatory element, it lacks protein-coding potential (no UniProt entry, no InterPro domains, no AlphaFold structure, no STRING interactions, no HPA protein expression). ClinVar analysis identified 3 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants: 2 copy number gains (triplifications) and 1 copy number loss (deletion), indicating balanced dosage sensitivity where both under-dosage and over-dosage disrupt cellular homeostasis. The moderate variant count (3 vs 5 for LOC114004388, 21 for LOC114004412) suggests intermediate clinical penetrance. The 20q chromosomal region is oncogene-rich and contains AURKA (Aurora kinase A at 20q13.2, amplified in multiple cancers), SRC (Src proto-oncogene at 20q11.22, non-receptor tyrosine kinase), ZNF217 (zinc finger protein at 20q13.2, amplified in breast/ovarian cancer), and TPX2 (microtubule organizing factor at 20q11.21, overexpressed in cancers). As a repressive enhancer/silencer, this element likely suppresses expression of one or more target genes in cis within the 20q domain. The balanced pathogenicity pattern (2 gains, 1 loss) suggests this silencer regulates oncogenes or cell cycle genes requiring precise expression levels - both haploinsufficiency and overexpression cause disease. This element may contribute to 20q amplification syndromes in cancer (particularly breast, ovarian, colorectal), myeloproliferative disorders, or developmental anomalies through dysregulation of critical 20q oncogenes requiring tight transcriptional repression.
