## Description
PMIS2 (PMIS2 Transmembrane Protein, Gene ID: 111216276, alias IFITMD9) is a very dark gene with only 3 publications on Semantic Scholar, representing an understudied sperm membrane protein located at chromosome 19q13.12. The gene encodes a 150 amino acid transmembrane protein (UniProt A0A1W2PS18) with predicted membrane localization. A landmark 2012 mouse knockout study demonstrated that PMIS2-knockout males produce normal-looking spermatozoa but are sterile due to severe fertilization defects, establishing PMIS2 as essential for sperm-egg interaction. A 2024 study characterized PMIS2 (SMA20) as a rapidly evolving sperm membrane alloantigen with species-divergent function in fertilization. InterPro analysis reveals conserved CD225/Dispanin family domains: IPR007593 (CD225/Dispanin family, positions 68-145), PF04505 (Interferon-induced transmembrane protein, 68-145), and PTHR14768 (CD225/Dispanin domain-containing protein, 34-149). GO annotation includes membrane localization (GO:0016020). AlphaFold predicts medium-confidence structure (global pLDDT 62.6), indicating a partially structured transmembrane protein with both ordered domains and flexible regions—characteristic of membrane proteins with extracellular interaction domains. STRING interactions reveal 10 partners (scores 0.400-0.506) all related to sperm function and fertilization: PRSS37 (0.506, serine protease), RNASE10 (0.479, ribonuclease), PRSS55 (0.472, sperm serine protease), SYNDIG1L (0.471, sperm differentiation), TEX101 (0.456, testis-expressed protein), CATSPERD (0.446, cation channel sperm-associated), CFAP54 (0.434, cilia/flagella protein), SUN5 (0.426, sperm head-tail coupling), PDILT (0.416, protein disulfide isomerase), and CASC4 (0.400). HPA shows RNA not detected in bulk tissues, cancer, or blood cells, but group-enriched in specific cell types—consistent with highly restricted testis/sperm expression. ClinVar contains 3 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all copy number variations on 19q12-13.13 (2 losses, 1 gain), indicating genomic instability at this locus may contribute to disease susceptibility, potentially including male infertility. The CD225/Dispanin family includes interferon-induced transmembrane proteins (IFITMs) involved in viral restriction and membrane fusion. PMIS2's unique combination of Dispanin domain, sperm-specific expression, knockout sterility phenotype, and fertilization protein interactions suggests it functions as: (1) a sperm membrane fusion protein mediating sperm-egg recognition and binding during fertilization, analogous to IFITM-mediated viral-cell fusion, (2) a rapidly evolving alloantigen (per 2024 paper) contributing to reproductive isolation between species through divergent sperm-egg compatibility, (3) a component of the sperm acrosome reaction machinery facilitating zona pellucida penetration, or (4) a sperm membrane stability protein maintaining structural integrity during epididymal maturation and capacitation. The very dark literature status (3 papers), definitive mouse knockout sterility phenotype, CD225/Dispanin domain membership, and high-confidence sperm protein interaction network make PMIS2 a compelling candidate for functional characterization as a novel fertilization factor with potential applications in contraception and infertility treatment.
