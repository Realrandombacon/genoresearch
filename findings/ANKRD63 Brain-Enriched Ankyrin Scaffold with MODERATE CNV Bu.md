## Description
ANKRD63 encodes a 380 amino acid ankyrin repeat domain-containing protein (UniProt: C9JTQ0) located on chromosome 15q15.1. InterPro analysis reveals canonical ankyrin repeat architecture (IPR002110, PF12796, positions 46-145) with 3 ankyrin repeat copies forming protein-protein interaction scaffolds. Notably, ANKRD63 belongs to the Dendritic Spine Regulation and Scaffold family (IPR050889, PTHR24166, positions 19-173), implicating roles in postsynaptic density organization and excitatory synapse maintenance. Ankyrin repeats mediate binding to diverse partners including cytoskeletal proteins, signaling molecules, and transcription factors.\n\nSTRING-DB analysis identifies 3 medium-confidence interaction partners: LSM12 (score 0.467, LSm12 homolog, mRNA decapping complex, P-body localization), DNAH14 (score 0.460, dynein heavy chain 14, axonemal dynein, ciliary motility), and SNX31 (score 0.427, sorting nexin 31, endosomal trafficking). These interactions suggest ANKRD63 functions in mRNA metabolism (LSM12), cytoskeletal organization (DNAH14), and vesicular trafficking (SNX31) - consistent with ankyrin scaffold protein roles.\n\nHuman Protein Atlas reveals BRAIN-ENRICHED expression (17.7 nTPM in brain, tissue enriched, detected in single tissue distribution) with MITOCHONDRIAL subcellular localization. This brain-specific expression pattern aligns with the dendritic spine scaffold family classification, suggesting ANKRD63 functions in neuronal synapse organization and plasticity. Mitochondrial localization is unexpected for ankyrin scaffolds and may indicate roles in mitochondrial-cytoskeletal coupling or mitochondrial trafficking in neurons.\n\nClinVar analysis reveals 7 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variants, representing MODERATE CNV burden. The variant spectrum shows GAIN BIAS: 71% gains (variants 3063379 x3, 560049 duplication, 442893 x3, 442892 gain, 395145 x4 - spanning 15q11.2-26.3, 15q11.2-21.2, ~20-80Mb genomic spans), 14% losses (564194 x1 15q14-15.1), and 14% complex (585332 15q14-15.1). This 71:29 GAIN:LOSS ratio indicates gain-of-function dosage sensitivity where increased ANKRD63 dosage is predominantly pathogenic while haploinsufficiency is partially tolerated.\n\nThe genomic context places ANKRD63 within 15q15.1, adjacent to the clinically important 15q11.2 BP1-BP2 microdeletion region (Burnside-Butler syndrome, neurodevelopmental disorder with cognitive impairment, behavioral problems, motor delays). Chromosome 15q11.2-15.1 contains genes involved in neurodevelopment including NIPA1, NIPA2, CYFIP1, TUBGCP5 (15q11.2 BP1-BP2 region) and various neuronal development genes.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: ANKRD63 functions as a neuronal ankyrin scaffold protein regulating dendritic spine morphology and synaptic plasticity through protein-protein interaction networks. The brain-enriched expression (17.7 nTPM) and dendritic spine scaffold family classification strongly support neuronal synaptic roles. The mechanism likely involves: (1) ankyrin repeat-mediated scaffolding of postsynaptic density proteins (PSD-95, Shank, Homer family) where ANKRD63 organizes excitatory synapse protein complexes; (2) mitochondrial-cytoskeletal coupling in neurons where mitochondrial localization facilitates ATP delivery to active synapses or mitochondrial trafficking along dendrites; (3) mRNA metabolism regulation through LSM12 interaction where ANKRD63 anchors mRNA processing complexes at dendritic spines for local translation; (4) vesicular trafficking through SNX31 interaction where ANKRD63 regulates AMPA receptor endocytosis/recycling for synaptic plasticity. The GAIN BIAS (71% duplications) suggests that ANKRD63 OVEREXPRESSION causes synaptic dysfunction - potentially through: excessive scaffold assembly disrupting PSD organization, dominant-negative sequestration of binding partners, or aberrant mitochondrial anchoring impairing energy distribution. 15q CNVs cause Burnside-Butler syndrome (15q11.2 BP1-BP2 microdeletion), neurodevelopmental disorders, and cognitive impairment. ANKRD63 dosage alterations may contribute to 15q11.2-15.1 phenotypes through dendritic spine dysregulation affecting excitatory synapse density, morphology, and plasticity. The mitochondrial localization suggests additional roles in neuronal energy metabolism. MODERATE 7 variant burden indicates intermediate mutational target size with gain-of-function dosage sensitivity.
