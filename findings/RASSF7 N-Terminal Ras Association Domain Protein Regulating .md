## Description
RASSF7 encodes a 373 aa Ras association domain-containing protein 7 (UniProt Q02833), also known as HRC1, located at 11p15.5, functioning as a non-enzymatic RAS effector that regulates centrosome duplication, microtubule stability, and cell cycle progression. InterPro analysis reveals a specialized N-terminal RASSF architecture: N-terminal Ras-associating (RA) domain (IPR000159/PF00788/SM00314/cd16135, aa 6-90) adopting a ubiquitin-like fold (G3DSA:3.10.20.90/SSF54236/IPR029071) essential for protein-protein interactions rather than direct RAS binding, full-length N-terminal RASSF family membership (IPR033593/PTHR15286, aa 8-325), and specific RA domain profile (PS50200, aa 10-89). AlphaFold predicts high-confidence structure (pLDDT 73.8), indicating a well-folded protein with stable RA domain and potentially flexible C-terminal regions involved in partner recruitment. STRING interactions reveal a focused network linking cytoskeleton, apoptosis, and RASSF paralogs: DISC1 (0.837, disrupted in schizophrenia 1, critical for centrosome/microtubule function), RASSF10 (0.836, closest paralog), RASSF5 (0.783, NORE1A), RASSF3 (0.771), RASSF1 (0.660, tumor suppressor), RASSF2 (0.627), PPP1R13B (0.517, apoptosis regulator), RASSF9 (0.448), RASSF6 (0.439), TP53BP2 (0.400, p53-binding protein). HPA expression shows centriolar satellite localization with tissue-enhanced RNA expression, highest in kidney (121.5 nTPM), detected in many tissues with low cancer and immune cell specificity, consistent with housekeeping roles in cell division. ClinVar reports 20 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly 11p15.5 copy number gains (x3, x4) and losses, suggesting dosage sensitivity in chromosomal disorder syndromes (e.g., Beckwith-Wiedemann spectrum) affecting growth and development. Literature (61 papers) details emerging roles in cancer and cell biology: (1) Prostate cancer regulation - LncRNA-AP006284.1 promoting cell growth/motility by forming RNA-DNA triplexes to recruit GNL3/SFPQ complex facilitating RASSF7 transcription (4 citations, 2022); (2) c-Myc suppression - RASSF7 inhibiting oncogenic c-Myc function through non-enzymatic mechanisms, potentially competing for transcriptional co-factors or stabilizing repressors (7 citations, 2018); (3) Apoptosis modulation - Sonapatha (Oroxylum indicum) inducing HeLa cell cytotoxicity by suppressing RASSF7 alongside NF-κB, COX-2, and NRF2 (8 citations, 2021); (4) Centrosome dynamics - RASSF7 localizing to centriolar satellites, regulating centrosome cohesion and microtubule nucleation via DISC1 interaction. Functional hypothesis: RASSF7 likely functions through: (1) Centrosome/Microtubule Regulator - anchoring to centriolar satellites via DISC1 interaction, stabilizing microtubule minus-ends and ensuring proper spindle assembly during mitosis; (2) c-Myc Antagonist - acting as a brake on c-Myc-driven proliferation, possibly by sequestering transcriptional activators or promoting c-Myc degradation, with loss contributing to uncontrolled growth in prostate/cervical cancers; (3) LncRNA Target - serving as downstream effector for oncogenic lncRNAs (e.g., AP006284.1) that enhance its transcription via RNA-DNA triplex formation, creating a feed-forward loop in cancer progression; (4) Apoptosis Modulator - interacting with PPP1R13B/TP53BP2 to fine-tune apoptotic thresholds, balancing cell survival vs. death signals in response to stress; (5) RASSF Network Hub - coordinating with N-terminal paralogs (RASSF10, RASSF5) to integrate RAS signaling outputs with cytoskeletal organization. The ubiquitin-like RA fold distinguishes RASSF7 from C-terminal RASSFs (RASSF1-6) that contain SARAH domains, suggesting unique interaction partners and mechanisms. Disease implications include: prostate cancer progression, cervical carcinoma, Beckwith-Wiedemann syndrome (11p15 duplication), potential links to schizophrenia (via DISC1 axis), and microtubule-related ciliopathies. Despite 61 papers, RASSF7's specific binding partners beyond DISC1, structural basis for c-Myc inhibition, and therapeutic potential in lncRNA-driven cancers remain underexplored.
