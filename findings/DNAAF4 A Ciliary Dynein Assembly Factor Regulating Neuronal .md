## Description
DNAAF4 (UniProt Q8WXU2) encodes a 420 amino acid protein belonging to the Axonemal Dynein Assembly Factor 4 family (IPR052004, PTHR46492). InterPro analysis reveals a unique domain architecture including a CS domain (IPR007052, PF04969, aa 3-87), a Tetratricopeptide repeat (TPR) domain (IPR011990, G3DSA:1.25.40.10, aa 276-397), and HSP20-like chaperone motifs (IPR008978, aa 4-154), consistent with roles in protein binding and chaperone-mediated assembly. AlphaFold structure (AF-Q8WXU2-F1) predicts a highly confident 3D fold (global pLDDT 83.9), indicating a stable, well-defined structure suitable for ciliary assembly. HPA expression shows tissue-enhanced expression with peak levels in retina (27.4 nTPM), detected in many tissues, classified as disease-related genes involved in neurogenesis and ciliopathies, with subcellular localization to plasma membrane, cytosol, and ciliary structures (connecting piece, mid piece, principal piece). Literature (28 papers) confirms DNAAF4 as a critical factor for ciliary motility and neuronal migration, with splice site mutations causing Primary Ciliary Dyskinesia (PCD) and infertility, and associations with developmental dyslexia via brain function networks. ClinVar lists 92 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants (including deletions, duplications, and missense mutations like c.584del and c.884A>G), predominantly on chromosome 15q21.3, confirming strong clinical significance despite no single monogenic disease annotation, suggesting contributions to ciliopathy syndromes and neurodevelopmental disorders. STRING interactions identify high-confidence partners including DCDC2 (0.978), KIAA0319 (0.972), PIH1D3 (0.961), DNAAF3 (0.901), DNAAF1 (0.861), and ROBO1 (0.844), placing DNAAF4 within the core ciliary dynein assembly complex. Functional hypothesis: DNAAF4 functions as a ciliary dynein assembly factor that regulates neuronal migration during cerebral neocortex development and maintains ciliary motility; its TPR domain mediates complex formation with DCDC2 and other dynein assembly factors to ensure proper ciliary structure and function. Mechanistic proposal: DNAAF4 recruits and stabilizes axonemal dynein complexes via its CS and TPR domains; loss-of-function mutations disrupt ciliary assembly leading to PCD phenotypes (infertility, respiratory defects) and impaired neuronal migration causing dyslexia; its expression in the retina suggests a role in photoreceptor development, while its association with developmental dyslexia implies a broader role in cortical layering via ciliary signaling pathways.
