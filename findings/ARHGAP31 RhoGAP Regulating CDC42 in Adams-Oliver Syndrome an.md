## Description
ARHGAP31 (UniProt Q2M1Z3), also known as CdGAP, encodes a 1444-amino acid Rho GTPase-activating protein containing a canonical RhoGAP domain (IPR000198/PF00620, aa 21-216/35-182) within the Rho GTPase activation protein superfamily (IPR008936/G3DSA:1.10.555.10, aa 18-220). AlphaFold predicts a largely disordered protein (pLDDT: 42.9), consistent with scaffold proteins that function through flexible protein-protein interactions rather than stable globular domains.\n\nSTRING interactions confirm ARHGAP31's role as a CDC42/RAC1 regulator: strongest links to CDC42 (0.855) and RHOA (0.805), with connections to transcriptional repressor ZEB2 (0.839), myosin light chain kinase MYLK (0.816), and endocytic scaffolds ITSN1/ITSN2 (0.747/0.664). HPA shows ubiquitous expression with immune cell enhancement. ClinVar contains 76 pathogenic variants including nonsense mutations (p.Gln883Ter), frameshifts (p.Gln1056fs), and missense variants across the RhoGAP domain.\n\nWith 45 papers, ARHGAP31 is a transitional dark gene—initially identified as an Adams-Oliver Syndrome (AOS) causative gene, but with expanding functional understanding. Recent work reveals a DLG1-ARHGAP31-CDC42 axis essential for intestinal stem cell responses to fluctuating Wnt niche signals (2023, 17 citations), establishing its role beyond developmental limb defects. Additional studies show ARHGAP31 regulation by RSK phosphorylation and 14-3-3β binding (2018, 13 citations), and novel frameshift variants in neurodevelopmental disorders (2025). This positions ARHGAP31 as a multifunctional RhoGAP bridging cytoskeletal dynamics to transcriptional programs in development, stem cell maintenance, and disease.
