## Description
ARHGAP39 (Rho GTPase-Activating Protein 39, also known as KIAA1688) is a 1083 aa multi-domain cytoskeletal regulator functioning as a critical switch for Rho GTPase signaling during neurodevelopment and cancer progression. With only 23 literature references, ARHGAP39 represents a 'partially characterized' dark gene where the integration of its diverse domain architecture into coherent cellular functions is still being elucidated. InterPro analysis reveals a sophisticated modular organization: an N-terminal tandem WW domain pair (IPR001202, aa 25-58 and 63-97) for proline-rich motif recognition and protein-protein interactions, a central MyTH4 domain (IPR000857/PF00784, aa 722-879) for microtubule binding and cytoskeletal crosslinking, and a C-terminal RhoGAP catalytic domain (IPR000198/PF00620, aa 890-1079) that accelerates GTP hydrolysis on Rho family GTPases to switch them from active to inactive states. AlphaFold predicts a low-confidence global structure (pLDDT 58.6), indicating extensive intrinsically disordered regions connecting the structured domains—this flexibility likely enables ARHGAP39 to function as a dynamic scaffold that adapts to multiple binding partners. HPA shows tissue-enhanced expression with enrichment in testis (20.3 nTPM) and brain (16.1 nTPM), localizing to the nucleoplasm, suggesting dual roles in neuronal development and germ cell maturation. STRING interactions place ARHGAP39 at the intersection of cytoskeletal dynamics and axon guidance: CDC42 (0.755, primary RhoGAP substrate), RAC1 (0.613, alternative substrate), ROBO1 (0.709, Slit receptor for axon guidance), ROBO4 (0.614, vascular guidance), CNKSR2 (0.716, synaptic scaffold), and FAM83H (0.725, keratinocyte differentiation). ClinVar lists 58 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number gains/losses on chromosome 8q and missense mutations, with a 2024 study identifying homozygous ARHGAP39 variants causing lethal cerebellar vermis hypoplasia in a consanguineous Saudi family, definitively linking ARHGAP39 to human neurodevelopmental disease. Recent literature (2023-2025) establishes ARHGAP39 as: (1) Essential for cerebellar development where loss causes vermis hypoplasia and facial dysmorphism; (2) Prognostic biomarker in breast cancer and hepatocellular carcinoma where expression correlates with immune infiltration and survival; (3) Suppressor of cell migration and invasion in hepatocellular cancer, contrasting with its oncogenic role in breast cancer context.
