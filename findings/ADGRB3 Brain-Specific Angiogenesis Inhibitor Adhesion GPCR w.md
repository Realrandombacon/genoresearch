## Description
ADGRB3 (BAI3) encodes a 1522 amino acid adhesion G protein-coupled receptor expressed predominantly in brain tissue (28.2 nTPM) and pituitary gland (21.3 nTPM). The protein architecture features an N-terminal CUB domain (IPR000859, residues 30-159) for protein-protein interactions, three thrombospondin type-1 repeats (IPR000884, residues 291-452) conferring anti-angiogenic activity, a GPCR proteolytic site (GPS motif, IPR000203, residues 815-868) that mediates autocleavage, and a 7-transmembrane serpentine domain (IPR000832, residues 878-1150) belonging to the secretin-like GPCR family. AlphaFold predicts the full-length structure with medium confidence (pLDDT 63.8), reflecting the challenging topology of multi-domain adhesion GPCRs. STRING interactions reveal high-confidence partnerships with C1QL3 (0.833) and C1QL1 (0.803) - complement C1q-like proteins that function as synaptic organizers in cerebellar circuitry - plus GRM8 (metabotropic glutamate receptor, 0.730), ELMO1 (engulfment and cell motility protein, 0.637), and RANBP2 (0.808). This interaction network positions ADGRB3 at the intersection of synaptic organization, angiogenesis inhibition, and phagocytic signaling. The thrombospondin repeats mediate anti-angiogenic function by binding integrins and matrix components, while the C1QL interactions suggest roles in cerebellar synapse formation and maintenance. ClinVar documents 14 pathogenic variants including copy number losses/gains across 6q12-13 and missense mutations (p.Val765Ala, p.Arg1433Lys), implicating ADGRB3 dosage sensitivity in neurodevelopmental disorders. Conservation analysis shows the adhesion GPCR architecture is maintained across vertebrates. The mechanistic model proposes that ADGRB3 functions as a bifunctional receptor: extracellular thrombospondin repeats inhibit angiogenesis by sequestering pro-angiogenic factors, while GPCR signaling through Gα12/13 and RhoA pathways regulates cytoskeletal dynamics in neuronal development. Cleavage at the GPS motif generates NTF/CTF fragments that may signal independently, a hallmark of adhesion GPCR activation.
