# PDCD6IP: Programmed Cell Death 6-Interacting Protein (ALIX) Orchestrating ESCRT-Mediated Membrane Scission, Cytokinesis, and Neurodevelopment via BRO1 Domain Scaffolding of CHMP4-VPS4 Complexes in Primary Microcephaly and Intellectual Disability

## Description
PDCD6IP (Programmed Cell Death 6-Interacting Protein), also known as ALIX (Apoptosis-Linked Gene 2-Interacting Protein X), is an 868 amino acid multifunctional scaffolding protein that serves as a central coordinator of the ESCRT (Endosomal Sorting Complex Required for Transport) machinery, mediating membrane scission events critical for cytokinesis, multivesicular body biogenesis, viral budding, apoptotic body formation, and neuronal development through BRO1 domain-dependent recruitment of CHMP4 proteins and VPS4 ATPases. InterPro analysis identifies characteristic tripartite architecture including N-terminal BRO1 domain (IPR004328, IPR038499, PF03097, PS51180, G3DSA:1.25.40.280, residues 3-392) for CHMP4 binding and endosomal sorting, central V-shaped domain (IPR025304, PF13949, G3DSA:1.20.120.560/G3DSA:1.20.140.50/G3DSA:1.25.40.280, residues 413-701) mediating protein-protein interactions (GO:0005515) and HIV late domain binding, middle V-domain dimerization module (cd09235, residues 360-698) for oligomerization, N-terminal Bro1-like domain (cd09240, residues 2-345) for PDCD6/apoptosis regulation, and PDCD6 interacting protein-related family signature (PTHR23030, residues 3-865). AlphaFold structure (AF-Q8WUM4-F1) predicts high confidence (pLDDT 83.6) across the 868 residue sequence, indicating a stable V-shaped scaffolding protein with distinct functional modules suitable for coordinating multi-protein ESCRT assemblies. HPA data classifies PDCD6IP as a disease-related protein involved in apoptosis, cell cycle, cell division, host-virus interaction, and protein transport, with vesicular and centrosomal localization reflecting ESCRT and cytokinetic functions, ubiquitous expression (detected in all tissues), low tissue/cancer specificity, and disease involvement in intellectual disability and primary microcephaly. STRING interactions reveal exceptionally tight coupling to the ESCRT machinery: CHMP4A (Charged Multivesicular Body Protein 4A, 0.999) as the primary ESCRT-III partner; SDCBP (Syndecan Binding Protein/Syntenin-1, 0.998) for cargo recognition and exosome biogenesis; TSG101 (Tumor Susceptibility Gene 101, 0.859) as the ESCRT-I component; MVB12A (0.751), CHMP4C (0.744), and CHMP4B (0.549) as ESCRT-III paralogs; CEP55 (Centrosomal Protein 55 kDa, 0.498) for cytokinetic abscission; VPS4B (0.452) and VPS4A (0.403) as AAA+ ATPases driving membrane scission; and PDCD6 (0.445) as the apoptosis regulator. ClinVar lists 21 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including frameshift duplication (c.154_158dup p.Val54fs), splice variants (c.1058-196T>A, c.835-23G>T), large copy number gains on 3p26.3-14.3, 3p26.3-22.3 (multiple independent CNV events spanning 17-49 Mb), and deletions on 3p26.3-22.2, causing primary microcephaly with intellectual disability through disrupted neural progenitor cytokinesis. Literature (93 papers) establishes PDCD6IP as a well-characterized ESCRT regulator: biallelic variants cause neurodevelopmental disorder with microcephaly confirming essential role in brain development; miR-363-3p targets PDCD6IP to attenuate oxygen-glucose deprivation/reoxygenation-induced neuronal injury linking to ischemic stroke protection; PDCD6IP functions in HIV budding through V-domain late domain interactions; and comprehensive reviews detail PDCD6IP roles in cytokinetic abscission, exosome biogenesis, and apoptotic body formation. As a well-studied ESCRT scaffold with defined monogenic disease, PDCD6IP represents a critical node connecting membrane remodeling to neurodevelopment, cell division, and vesicular trafficking through BRO1-mediated CHMP4 recruitment and V-domain-dependent protein interactions disrupted in primary microcephaly.
