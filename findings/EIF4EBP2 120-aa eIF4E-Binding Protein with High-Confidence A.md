## Description
EIF4EBP2 (UniProt Q13542), or eukaryotic translation initiation factor 4E-binding protein 2 (4E-BP2), encodes a compact 120-amino acid intrinsically disordered regulator that adopts a structured conformation upon binding eIF4E. The protein contains a canonical eIF4E-binding domain (IPR008606/PF05456/PTHR12669, aa 7-120) featuring the conserved YXXXXLΦ motif essential for competitive inhibition of eIF4E-eIF4G complex assembly. AlphaFold predicts a confident structure (global pLDDT: 76.0), though the protein likely remains disordered until phosphorylation-dependent conformational changes occur.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis confirms EIF4EBP2 as a central node in the mTOR signaling network: EIF4E (0.999, direct binding partner), EIF4G1 (0.731), MTOR (0.719, upstream kinase), RPTOR (0.665, mTORC1 component), RPS6KB1 (0.630, parallel mTOR effector), and paralogs EIF4EBP1 (0.517) and EIF4EBP3 (0.451). HPA data shows ubiquitous expression with nucleoplasmic and mitochondrial localization, consistent with dual roles in nuclear mRNA export regulation and mitochondrial translation control.\n\nWith 51 papers, EIF4EBP2 occupies an intermediate space between dark and characterized genes. Its paralog 4E-BP1 is far better studied, but 4E-BP2 has unique neuronal functions. Knockout mice demonstrate epilepsy susceptibility via dysregulated eIF4E-dependent translational control. Recent 2025 studies show 4E-BP2 deletion protects against type 1 diabetes by modulating autoreactive T cell function. In breast cancer, EIF4EBP2 variants show causal associations via Mendelian randomization. ClinVar lists 14 pathogenic variants including chromosome 10q CNVs. We hypothesize EIF4EBP2 functions as a phosphorylation-regulated molecular switch: when hypophosphorylated (low mTOR activity), it binds eIF4E to repress cap-dependent translation of proliferation/survival mRNAs; when hyperphosphorylated (high mTOR activity), it releases eIF4E to permit translation, making it a critical checkpoint in neuronal plasticity, immune tolerance, and oncogenic transformation.
