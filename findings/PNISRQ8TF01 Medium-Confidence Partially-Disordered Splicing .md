## Description
PNISR (PNN interacting serine and arginine rich protein, also known as SRrp130, SFRS18, or C6orf111) is an 805 amino acid intracellular protein localized to nuclear speckles, cytosol, and plasma membrane (UniProt Q8TF01) with medium-confidence partially-disordered architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 53.8). The protein defines the PNISR family (IPR031937/PTHR31518, residues 1-805) and contains a central Arginine/Serine-rich domain (PF15996, residues 223-391) forming a ~169 aa module (~21%). This RS-rich region is characteristic of splicing factors and is likely intrinsically disordered, facilitating dynamic protein-RNA and protein-protein interactions. Molecular functions include RNA binding (GO:0003723) and regulation of mRNA splicing via spliceosome, placing PNISR within the core pre-mRNA processing machinery. Its classification as an 'Essential protein' underscores its critical role in cell viability.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals a specialized network within the spliceosome and nuclear speckles: strongest interaction with SRSF11 (0.782, Serine and arginine rich splicing factor 11), indicating co-regulation of alternative splicing events. High confidence links include ARGLU1 (0.779, Arginine and glutamate rich protein 1, a transcriptional coactivator and splicing factor) and LUC7L3 (0.728, Luc7 like 3, a component of the U1 snRNP). Connections to U2AF2 (0.688, U2 small nuclear RNA auxiliary factor 2, essential for 3' splice site recognition), SRSF2 (0.608), PRPF39 (0.557), and SCAF11 (0.529) confirm its integration into the early spliceosome assembly. Interaction with PNN (0.518, Pinin, a desmosomal protein also found in nuclear speckles) validates its namesake partnership. RBM39 (0.432) and NKTR (0.411) complete the network, suggesting roles in RNA export or stability.\n\nHPA expression reveals low tissue specificity with detection in all tissues, consistent with a housekeeping role in gene expression. Subcellular localization in nuclear speckles (sites of splicing factor storage and assembly) confirms its function. The presence at the plasma membrane suggests potential non-canonical roles or trafficking intermediates.\n\nClinVar documents 20 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number losses affecting the 6q16 region: CNV losses (6q16.1-16.2, 6q15-21, 6q16.1-21 deletions) and a missense variant (p.Asp438Gly). The variant burden indicates haploinsufficiency sensitivity. Loss of PNISR would disrupt spliceosome assembly or fidelity, leading to widespread splicing errors, accumulation of unspliced transcripts, and cell death. This is particularly detrimental in developing tissues, contributing to syndromes associated with 6q deletions (developmental delay, microcephaly, cardiac defects).\n\nConservation is strong across vertebrates: human (Q8TF01, 805 aa), mouse (A2AJT4, 805 aa), indicating evolutionary pressure to maintain the RS-rich domain and interaction interfaces.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: PNISR functions as a regulatory scaffold within the spliceosome, using its disordered RS-rich domain to recruit or stabilize specific splicing factors (SRSF11, U2AF2) at pre-mRNA substrates. The low AlphaFold confidence reflects the functional necessity of disorder for conformational flexibility and rapid binding kinetics. Interaction with PNN suggests a link between desmosomal integrity and nuclear splicing, possibly coordinating cell adhesion status with gene expression programs. In nuclear speckles, PNISR may act as a reservoir or chaperone for splicing components. Haploinsufficiency leads to insufficient spliceosome activity, causing global splicing defects and triggering apoptosis or developmental failure.
