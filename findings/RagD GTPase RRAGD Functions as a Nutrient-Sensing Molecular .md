## Description
RRAGD (Ras-related GTP-binding protein D) is a 400 aa small GTPase functioning as a critical nutrient sensor that regulates the mechanistic target of rapamycin complex 1 (mTORC1) in response to amino acid availability. With 61 literature references, RRAGD represents a 'partially characterized' dark gene where its core role in mTOR signaling is established, but specific disease mechanisms (e.g., kidney hypomagnesemia) and regulatory nuances are emerging. InterPro analysis reveals a canonical Rag GTPase architecture: a P-loop containing nucleoside triphosphate hydrolase domain (IPR027417/G3DSA:3.40.50.300, aa 59-243) responsible for GTP binding/hydrolysis, embedded within a larger Gtr1/RagA family region (IPR006762/PF04670, aa 61-290/373) characteristic of the Rag subfamily. AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (pLDDT 75.1), indicating a well-folded G-domain capable of conformational switching between GDP/GTP states. HPA shows tissue-enhanced expression, highest in skeletal muscle (79.1 nTPM) and tongue (56.3 nTPM), with localization to nucleoplasm, vesicles, and centrosomes—consistent with dynamic shuttling between cytosol and lysosomes upon stimulation. STRING interactions reveal an extremely tight functional cluster dedicated to mTORC1 regulation: RRAGD binds almost exclusively to its obligate heterodimer partner RRAGC (0.999), the opposing pair RRAGA/RRAGB (0.999), and the Ragulator complex components (LAMTOR1/3/4/5: 0.952-0.999) that anchor the heterodimer to the lysosomal surface. It also interacts strongly with the amino acid transporter SLC38A9 (0.998) which acts as the upstream sensor, and the mTORC1 core (MTOR: 0.852, RPTOR: 0.778). ClinVar lists 25 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including missense mutations in the G-domain (p.Leu25Val, p.Pro35Leu, p.Thr91Ile, p.Ser77Phe, p.Ile100Arg, p.Thr97Pro, p.Ser76Trp) and splice site defects, definitively linking RRAGD to autosomal dominant kidney hypomagnesemia (ADKH) characterized by hypokalemia, nephrocalcinosis, and dilated cardiomyopathy. Recent literature (2024-2025) establishes RRAGD as: (1) Target of STAT6 mutations in follicular lymphoma driving hyperactive mTOR signaling; (2) Mediator of autophagic cell death repression in melanoma via the LINC00622/BTF3 axis; (3) Key node in atherosclerosis where miR-125b-1-3p targets RRAGD to modulate autophagy.
