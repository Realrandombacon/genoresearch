## Description

The protein contains a SPEC3/Stum family domain (IPR026673, residues 6-129) and an ectodermal ciliogenesis protein domain (PF15795, residues 44-127), indicating roles in cilia formation and sensory signal transduction. HPA data shows group-enriched expression with highest levels in brain (47.5 nTPM) and intestine (15.2 nTPM), consistent with mechanosensory functions in neuronal and enteric nervous systems. ClinVar contains 35 pathogenic copy number variants (gains, losses, deletions, duplications) at 1q21.1-44, though no specific diseases are yet assigned—suggesting potential neurodevelopmental or gastrointestinal phenotypes. AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 59.6) indicates a partially structured protein with potential intrinsically disordered regions, typical of signaling adaptors. Functional hypothesis: STUM acts as a mechanosensory adaptor protein that transduces mechanical stimuli into cellular signals in neurons and intestinal epithelial cells, potentially through cilia-mediated sensing and ubiquitin-dependent protein turnover, with loss-of-function causing neurodevelopmental disorders or intestinal motility defects through disrupted mechanotransduction pathways.
