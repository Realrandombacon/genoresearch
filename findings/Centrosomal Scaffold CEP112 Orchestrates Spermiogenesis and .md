## Description
CEP112 (Centrosomal Protein of 112 kDa, also known as CCDC46) is a 955 aa large intracellular scaffold protein essential for centrosome integrity, primary cilia formation, and the translational regulation of fertility genes during spermiogenesis. Despite 25 literature references, its molecular mechanism remains partially obscure, classifying it as a 'partially characterized' dark gene requiring deeper functional synthesis. InterPro analysis reveals a unique domain architecture dominated by a large Domain of Unknown Function (DUF4485/PF14846, aa 13-98) at the N-terminus and a family-specific region spanning almost the entire length (IPR055310, aa 5-955), suggesting it acts as a massive structural platform rather than an enzyme. AlphaFold predicts a confident global structure (pLDDT 75.8), indicating a stable, elongated coiled-coil rich scaffold capable of bridging large distances within the centrosomal matrix. HPA shows ubiquitous expression with strong enhancement in testis (43.8 nTPM), localizing to the centrosome, midbody ring, and nucleoplasm, consistent with roles in cell division and sperm tail assembly. STRING interactions place CEP112 in a network of ciliary and centrosomal components: UBA52 (0.627, ubiquitin fusion), ERC1 (0.617, active zone protein), TSGA10 (0.592, testis-specific centrosomal protein), SUN5 (0.571, LINC complex component critical for sperm head-tail coupling), CDK5RAP2 (0.518, centrosomal kinase regulator), and TTC29 (0.517, ciliary trafficking). ClinVar lists 16 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including nonsense mutations (p.Gln418Ter, p.Arg166Ter) and missense changes (p.Arg702Cys, p.Arg692Trp), alongside large 17q copy number gains, implicating CEP112 haploinsufficiency or dosage sensitivity in developmental disorders, potentially affecting cilia-dependent pathways. Recent literature (2024) explicitly links CEP112 to phase separation mechanisms that coordinate the translational regulation of essential fertility genes during the haploid phase of spermatogenesis, a novel function for a centrosomal protein.
