## Description
NAT8B (Gene ID: 51471), also known as ATase1, encodes a 227 amino acid N-acetyltransferase (UniProt Q9UHF3) located at chromosome 2p13.1. Despite historical annotation as a 'putative pseudogene' with two common polymorphic nonsense mutations, recent literature (13 papers, including 2020-2025 studies) demonstrates NAT8B is a functional ER membrane-bound acetyltransferase that regulates the secretory pathway through Nε-lysine acetylation of nascent glycoproteins.\n\nInterPro analysis confirms canonical GNAT (Gcn5-related N-acetyltransferase) superfamily architecture: IPR000182 (GNAT domain, aa 61-214), IPR016181 (Acyl-CoA N-acyltransferase, aa 4-203), IPR050769 (camello-type N-acetyltransferase, aa 2-202), PF00583 (GNAT family, aa 91-193), PTHR13947, cd04301 (N-Acyltransferase superfamily, aa 109-176), G3DSA:3.40.630.30, and SSF55729. These domains define the acetyl-CoA binding pocket and catalytic machinery.\n\nAlphaFold predicts exceptional structural confidence (pLDDT 91.5), indicating a well-folded enzyme with stable tertiary structure. This high confidence contradicts pseudogene status and supports functional protein production.\n\nSTRING found no interaction partners (likely due to database annotation lag), and HPA has no expression data. However, literature reveals NAT8B forms a heteromeric complex with NAT8 (ATase2) in the ER membrane, with differential regulation adjusting secretory pathway engagement. Mouse overexpression studies demonstrate that NAT8B/NAT8 dysregulation causes autistic-like phenotypes with altered dendritic branching and spine formation, as well as progeria-like phenotypes through secretome disruption.\n\nClinVar reports 10 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all copy number variations (deletions/duplications) at 2p13, though no specific disease associations are established. These CNVs may affect dosage-sensitive secretory pathway regulation.\n\nFunctional hypothesis: NAT8B/ATase1 is a functional ER acetyltransferase that acetylates nascent glycoproteins entering the secretory pathway, modulating protein folding efficiency and secretion rates. Together with NAT8/ATase2, it forms a regulatory switch controlling secretory flux in neurons and other secretory cells. Dysregulation contributes to neurodevelopmental disorders (autism spectrum) and accelerated aging phenotypes through proteostasis disruption.
