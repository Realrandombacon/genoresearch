## Description
YPEL5 encodes a 121 amino acid protein yippee-like 5 (UniProt P62699), an essential component of the evolutionarily conserved GID/CTLH E3 ubiquitin ligase complex that regulates substrate ubiquitination and proteasomal degradation. The protein contains a canonical Yippee/Mis18/Cereblon zinc-binding domain (IPR004910/PF03226 at positions 14-107) characteristic of the Yippee family (IPR039058 at positions 1-111), with a defined Yippee domain structure (IPR034751/PS51792 at positions 13-110). This zinc-binding domain mediates protein-protein interactions within multi-subunit E3 ligase complexes and is structurally related to Mis18 centromere assembly factors and Cereblon ubiquitin ligase adaptors.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals YPEL5 forms an exceptionally tight complex with GID/CTLH E3 ubiquitin ligase subunits: WDR26 (score 0.939—scaffold subunit), ARMC8 (0.921—armadillo repeat protein 8, substrate receptor), RMND5A (0.901—required for meiotic division 5A, catalytic RING component), GID8 (0.885—GID complex subunit 8), MAEA (0.866—macrophage erythroblast attacher, substrate receptor), RANBP10 (0.854—RAN binding protein 10), RMND5B (0.832—paralog of RMND5A), RANBP9 (0.794—RAN binding protein 9), MKLN1 (0.722—muskelin), and GID4 (0.696—proline-recognition subunit). This positions YPEL5 at the heart of regulated protein degradation controlling cell cycle progression, metabolic enzyme turnover, and developmental signaling.\n\nHPA expression data classifies YPEL5 as tissue-enriched in bone marrow (413.6 nTPM—highest expression), detected in all tissues with cell type-enhanced patterns. Subcellular localization is nucleoplasmic and cytosolic, consistent with roles in both nuclear substrate recognition (transcription factors, cell cycle regulators) and cytoplasmic protein quality control. Protein class includes essential proteins, indicating fundamental cellular requirement.\n\nClinVar documents 21 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number gains (2p25.3-23.1 duplications spanning 30 Mb; 2p24.1-22.2 gains; 2p25.3-22.3 duplications), copy number losses (2p25.1-q13 deletions spanning 100 Mb; 2p23.2-21 losses), promoter variants (NM_016061.3:c.-24-9T>A), and complex rearrangements. Critically, ClinVar lists 0 associated diseases—a profound knowledge gap given essential GID/CTLH complex function and substantial variant burden. This suggests YPEL5 dysfunction may cause undiagnosed hematopoietic disorders (bone marrow enrichment), developmental defects (GID complex regulates embryonic patterning), metabolic syndromes (substrate turnover defects), or cancer predisposition.\n\nAlphaFold structure prediction (AF-P62699-F1) yields a global pLDDT score of 93.1 (very high confidence) across all 121 residues, indicating an exceptionally well-folded zinc-binding domain structure suitable for stable GID/CTLH complex assembly.\n\nLiterature analysis (35 papers) reveals emerging functional understanding with critical knowledge gaps. Landmark 2021 study (cited 75x) demonstrates METTL3/YTHDF2 m6A axis accelerates colorectal carcinogenesis through epigenetic YPEL5 suppression, establishing YPEL5 as tumor suppressor. Recent 2026 studies define roles in circadian period regulation via PERIOD protein ubiquitination/stability control and female fertility through folliculogenesis/oocyte maturation regulation. 2023 zebrafish studies confirm liver development requirements. 2017 yeast complementation assays demonstrate pro-apoptotic activity. However, no Mendelian disease phenotype has been defined despite 21 pathogenic variants. Key unknowns include: specific disease phenotypes from germline loss-of-function mutations, contribution to hematopoietic disorders (bone marrow enrichment), precise substrate specificity within GID/CTLH complex, role in circadian disorder pathogenesis, and therapeutic potential for cancers with YPEL5-dependent ubiquitination dysregulation.
