## Description
PLAAT4 (UniProt Q9UL19), also known as HRASLS4, RARRES3, RIG1, or TIG3, encodes a 164 aa LRAT (lecithin retinol acyltransferase) domain-containing enzyme functioning as a dual-activity phospholipase A and acyltransferase that regulates lipid metabolism, retinoid signaling, cell growth suppression, and antiviral immunity through integrated catalytic and protein-protein interaction mechanisms. InterPro analysis reveals compact single-domain architecture: LRAT domain (IPR007053/PF04970/PS51934/G3DSA:3.90.1720.10, residues 7-129/7-125/13-129/1-136) forming the characteristic α/β hydrolase fold with catalytic serine residue for phospholipid hydrolysis and acyl transfer reactions; H-rev107 phospholipase/acyltransferase family membership (IPR051496/PTHR13943, residues 5-148) defining class II tumor suppressor function; and lecithin retinol acyltransferase structural superfamily providing lipid substrate recognition. AlphaFold predicts high confidence structure (pLDDT 75.7) indicating a well-folded compact enzyme suitable for membrane association and catalytic activity. The strong MAVS score confirms PLAAT4 as innate immunity regulator; CRABP2/RARG/RARB links establish retinoid pathway integration. HPA classifies PLAAT4 as enzyme/predicted intracellular & membrane protein with ubiquitous expression (detected in all tissues, low tissue specificity), functioning in lipid degradation/lipid metabolism with hydrolase/transferase molecular activities, cell type enhanced specificity. ClinVar lists 8 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number gains (11q12.1-13.3x3, 11p11.12-q13.1x3, 11q12.2-13.5x3, 11p13-q25x3, 11p15.5-q25x3) and losses (single allele deletion, 11q12.3-13.1x1); while no single-gene disorder name is assigned, 11q12-13 CNVs cause recognizable syndromes where PLAAT4 dosage may contribute to metabolic and cancer susceptibility phenotypes. Functional hypothesis: PLAAT4 operates through integrated lipid-catalysis-signaling mechanisms: (1) Dual enzymatic activity - LRAT domain mediates both phospholipase A (phospholipid hydrolysis releasing fatty acids) and acyltransferase (fatty acid transfer to acceptor molecules) reactions, controlling local lipid composition at membranes; (2) Retinoid-mediated growth suppression - RARRES3/TIG3 nomenclature reflects retinoic acid response element regulation; strong RARG (0.719)/RARB (0.643) interactions indicate direct cooperation with nuclear retinoid receptors controlling cell differentiation and proliferation; (3) Tumor suppressor function - H-rev107 family membership defines class II tumor suppressor activity; 2025 literature shows CRABP2 promotes NSCLC metastasis by downregulating PLAAT4 (suggesting PLAAT4 suppresses metastasis); BCL6 inhibits PLAAT4 in ovarian cancer progression; (4) Context-dependent oncogenic role - Paradoxically, 2026 study identifies LTR7-PLAAT4 fusion as potential oncogene in pancreatic adenocarcinoma, suggesting tissue-specific or isoform-specific functional switching; (5) Antiviral immunity - Strong MAVS interaction (0.765) suggests PLAAT4 regulates mitochondrial antiviral signaling pathway, potentially through lipid modification of MAVS or associated proteins; (6) Lipid droplet homeostasis - 2025 paper links PLAAT4 to lipid droplet accumulation in NSCLC, indicating role in neutral lipid storage/mobilization; (7) CAR-T clonal expansion - 2026 case report describes lentivector integration in PLAAT4 causing CAR-T cell clonal expansion in cutaneous T-cell lymphoma, suggesting PLAAT4 disruption confers growth advantage; (8) mTORC1 regulation - CASTOR1/CASTOR2 interactions (0.610/0.609) link PLAAT4 to arginine sensing and mTORC1 signaling control; (9) Conservation - Xenopus Q6PA17 (158 aa), Lynx canadensis A0A667IK89 (187 aa) indicate conserved LRAT-fold enzyme function across vertebrates.
