## Description
FAM184A (family with sequence similarity 184 member A, alias C6orf60) encodes a 1140-amino acid intracellular scaffold protein belonging to the TAG-278-related family (IPR039478/PTHR18870, residues 16-1140). The protein contains an N-terminal coiled-coil domain (IPR039478/PF15665, residues 54-264) characteristic of oligomerization scaffolds that mediate protein complex assembly. AlphaFold predicts high confidence structure (pLDDT=74.1), indicating a well-folded protein with stable tertiary structure suitable for sustained protein-protein interactions. HPA classifies FAM184A as a predicted intracellular protein with subcellular localization to vesicles, centriolar satellites, basal bodies, and cytosol, strongly implicating roles in centrosome/cilium biology and vesicular trafficking. The cancer-enhanced expression pattern suggests potential oncogenic function through dysregulated cell cycle control. STRING interactions reveal medium-confidence partnerships with LRRC61 (0.613, leucine-rich repeat protein), DNAJC27 (0.601, DnaJ heat shock protein co-chaperone), SHISAL1 (0.543, uncharacterized protein), PLEKHO2 (0.469, pleckstrin homology domain protein), and PCM1 (0.469, pericentriolar material 1, centrosome scaffolding), positioning FAM184A within the centrosomal proteome and ciliogenesis machinery. The PCM1 interaction is particularly significant as PCM1 anchors proteins to centriolar satellites for ciliary assembly. ClinVar documents 27 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including 6q copy number losses/gains (6q21-23.2), deletions, and insertions, indicating strong dosage sensitivity and potential contribution to developmental disorders. Conservation is evident across vertebrates. Mechanistically, FAM184A likely functions as a coiled-coil-mediated oligomerization scaffold that recruits client proteins to centriolar satellites via PCM1-dependent trafficking, regulating ciliogenesis and centrosome duplication. The DNAJC27 interaction suggests chaperone-mediated folding or client protein delivery, while vesicular localization indicates potential roles in endosomal trafficking. Cancer-enhanced expression suggests FAM184A may promote proliferation through centrosome amplification or cilia-dependent signaling pathways (Hedgehog, PDGFRα).
