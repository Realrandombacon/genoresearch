## Description
GK5 (Glycerol Kinase 5) is a 529-amino acid intracellular kinase belonging to the FGGY family of carbohydrate kinases that catalyzes the ATP-dependent phosphorylation of glycerol to glycerol-3-phosphate, a critical step in glycerol metabolism linking lipid breakdown to glycolysis and gluconeogenesis. InterPro analysis reveals a characteristic two-domain FGGY kinase architecture: an N-terminal domain (IPR018484, residues 21-282; PF00370) and a C-terminal domain (IPR018485, residues 292-479; PF02795) that together form the nucleotide-binding domain (NBD, cd07793, residues 20-520; IPR037444, residues 20-520) with a conserved active site motif (IPR018483, residues 391-411) essential for phosphotransferase activity. The protein belongs to the carbohydrate kinase FGGY family (IPR000577, residues 21-528) with GO annotations for kinase activity (GO:0016301), phosphotransferase activity with alcohol group as acceptor (GO:0016773), and carbohydrate metabolic process (GO:0005975). AlphaFold predicts an exceptionally high-confidence structure (pLDDT 93.9), indicating a stable, well-folded metabolic enzyme with defined catalytic architecture.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places GK5 in a metabolic network centered on glycerol/lipid metabolism and protein degradation: GPD2 (0.807, mitochondrial glycerol-3-phosphate dehydrogenase, the enzyme that converts glycerol-3-phosphate to dihydroxyacetone phosphate, directly downstream of GK5), DBT (0.694, dihydrolipoamide branched-chain transacylase E2, involved in branched-chain amino acid metabolism), and multiple proteasome regulatory subunits (PSMD4/0.603, PSMD6/0.598, PSMD12/0.565, PSMD2/0.562, PSMD11/0.560, all components of the 19S proteasome cap). The strong GPD2 interaction confirms GK5's direct role in the glycerol-3-phosphate shuttle, while proteasome connections suggest potential regulation through protein turnover or complex formation. Additional partners include CEACAM4 (0.570, carcinoembryonic antigen-related cell adhesion molecule 4), UNC80 (0.563, NALCN channel regulator), and RSPH3 (0.513, radial spoke head protein 3).\n\nHuman Protein Atlas classifies GK5 as a metabolic enzyme with low tissue specificity, detected in all tissues, consistent with a housekeeping metabolic function. Subcellular localization includes nucleoplasm and cytosol, suggesting potential nuclear roles beyond cytoplasmic glycerol metabolism. The cell type-enhanced single cell classification indicates elevated expression in specific cell types, potentially those with high lipid turnover.\n\nClinVar reports 19 pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants, all large copy number variations spanning chromosome 3q (coordinates include 3q22.1-29 duplication, 3q22.1-25.1 deletion, 3q13.31-26.31 duplication, 3q22.3-26.1 duplication, 3q23 deletion, 3q22.2-24 duplication, 3p26.3-q29 duplication). While no specific monogenic disease is named solely for GK5, these CNVs overlap with regions associated with developmental disorders and metabolic phenotypes. The absence of point mutations suggests GK5 loss may be compensated by other glycerol kinases (GK) or that glycerol metabolism defects are subclinical.\n\nLiterature analysis (53 papers) reveals that GK5 is surprisingly understudied in humans—most search results refer to Honda GK5 engines or C. elegans gk5 strains. Only one relevant human study (2017, 16 citations) demonstrated that microRNA-645 promotes renal clear cell carcinoma metastasis and proliferation by targeting GK5, suggesting tumor suppressor function. This indicates GK5 is a genuine dark gene despite the 'well-studied' classification.\n\nWe hypothesize that GK5 functions as a metabolic gatekeeper for glycerol utilization with roles beyond simple glycerol phosphorylation: (1) Glycerol-3-phosphate shuttle regulation—GK5 may coordinate with GPD2 to control NADH/NAD+ balance and mitochondrial respiration; (2) Proteasome coupling—PSMD interactions suggest GK5 may be regulated by ubiquitin-proteasome degradation or form complexes with proteasome subunits for metabolic coordination; (3) Tumor suppression—miR-645 targeting in renal cancer suggests GK5 downregulation promotes metastasis, potentially through metabolic reprogramming favoring Warburg effect; (4) Nuclear metabolic signaling—nucleoplasmic localization suggests potential roles in nuclear glycerol metabolism or transcriptional regulation of metabolic genes. The 3q CNVs may cause dosage effects altering glycerol metabolism, potentially contributing to metabolic disorders, cancer susceptibility, or developmental phenotypes. GK5 represents a candidate metabolic tumor suppressor, a potential biomarker for renal cell carcinoma, and an uncharacterized component of the glycerol-3-phosphate shuttle with therapeutic implications for metabolic diseases and cancer.
