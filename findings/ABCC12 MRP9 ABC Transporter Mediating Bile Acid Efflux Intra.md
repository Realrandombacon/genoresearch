## Description
ABCC12 (ATP-Binding Cassette Subfamily C Member 12/MRP9, UniProt Q96J65) is a 1359 aa full-length ABC transporter that functions in bile acid conjugate efflux and intrahepatic bile duct development through ATP-dependent substrate translocation across canalicular membranes. Structurally, AlphaFold predicts a well-folded membrane protein (pLDDT=77.8) featuring canonical ABC transporter architecture with two ATP-binding cassette domains (IPR003439/IPR003593, aa 467-701 and 1120-1354), two six-transmembrane helical domains (cd18592/cd18599, aa 125-411 and 791-1094), and P-loop containing NTP hydrolase folds (G3DSA:3.40.50.300, aa 444-712 and 1106-1356) enabling ATP hydrolysis-coupled transport. Functionally, STRING interactions reveal coupling to related ABC transporters: ABCC11/MRP8 (0.663) sharing substrate specificity, ABCF2 (0.524), ABCE1 (0.465), ABCF3 (0.464), and ABCB1/MDR1 (0.452), consistent with MRP subfamily functional redundancy. HPA shows testis-enriched expression (2.2 nTPM) and skeletal muscle expression (1.3 nTPM) with predicted membrane localization. ClinVar identifies 16 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number gains/losses at 16q11.2-q24.3 and splice variants (c.3715-5T>G), though no specific Mendelian disease is annotated - these overlap with chronic cholestasis syndrome regions. Literature (49 papers) reveals emerging mechanistic insights: deleterious ABCC12 variants cause idiopathic chronic cholestasis with intrahepatic bile duct loss in children (2021, 20 citations), ABCC12 deficiency causes bile duct paucity in zebrafish and mouse models (2019), and the transporter is evaluated in breast cancer diagnostic profiles (2019). The gene represents a 'semi-dark' ABC transporter where canonical MRP function is established, but specific substrate identity, mechanisms linking ABCC12-mediated efflux to bile duct morphogenesis, and tissue-specific vulnerability in cholestatic disease remain underexplored therapeutic targets.
