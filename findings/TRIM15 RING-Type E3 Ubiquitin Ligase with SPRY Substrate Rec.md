# TRIM15: RING-Type E3 Ubiquitin Ligase with SPRY Substrate Recognition Domain and 6p CNV Disease Association

## Description
TRIM15 (Gene ID: 89870, UniProt Q9C019) encodes a 465-amino acid tripartite motif (TRIM) family E3 ubiquitin-protein ligase. Domain architecture follows canonical TRIM organization: N-terminal RING finger domain (residues 16-61, IPR001841, PF00097) confers E3 ubiquitin ligase catalytic activity via E2 ubiquitin-conjugating enzyme recruitment; tandem B-box type zinc fingers (residues 78-119, IPR000315, PF00643) mediate protein-protein interactions and higher-order oligomerization; C-terminal SPRY/B30.2 domain (residues 276-465, IPR001870, IPR003877, PF00622) serves as substrate recognition module for specific protein targets. Multiple evidence lines support E3 ligase function: (1) InterPro confirms canonical TRIM architecture with GO terms "zinc ion binding" and "protein binding"; (2) STRING interactions reveal PXN (paxillin, score 0.630) as top partner - paxillin is a focal adhesion scaffold protein regulating cell migration and integrin signaling; GPC5 (glypican-5, 0.517) is a heparan sulfate proteoglycan involved in Wnt/Hedgehog signaling; MAVS (mitochondrial antiviral signaling protein, 0.495) is a key innate immunity adaptor for RIG-I-like receptor antiviral responses; PRYP3/PRY/PRY2 (SPRY domain-containing proteins, 0.405-0.507) suggest homotypic SPRY-SPRY interactions; (3) ClinVar contains 6 pathogenic copy number variants spanning 6p22.1-6q27 region (deletions 28-32 Mb, duplications 156 kb-171 Mb), associated with 6p duplication/deletion syndromes featuring developmental delay, intellectual disability, craniofacial dysmorphism, and cardiac defects. The 6p22.1 region contains TRIM15 within a gene-dense cluster including ID4 (inhibitor of DNA binding), TEF (transcription factor), and multiple olfactory receptors; (4) HPA data unavailable but RefSeq indicates cytoplasmic localization consistent with TRIM family members. Functional hypothesis: TRIM15 functions as a substrate-specific E3 ubiquitin ligase that catalyzes K48-linked polyubiquitination targeting proteins for proteasomal degradation, or K63-linked chains for signaling modulation. The SPRY domain recognizes specific degron motifs in substrate proteins involved in focal adhesion turnover (paxillin), growth factor signaling (glypicans), or antiviral immunity (MAVS). TRIM15 may regulate cell migration, epithelial-mesenchymal transition, or innate immune responses through targeted ubiquitination. B-box domains mediate TRIM15 oligomerization (higher-order assemblies typical of TRIM proteins), enhancing E3 ligase processivity. Haploinsufficiency or overexpression from 6p CNVs alters TRIM15 dosage, causing dysregulated ubiquitination of focal adhesion and signaling substrates contributing to neurodevelopmental and craniofacial phenotypes. This represents a "dark gene" with clear domain architecture and disease association but no experimentally validated substrate specificity or E2 partner preference.
