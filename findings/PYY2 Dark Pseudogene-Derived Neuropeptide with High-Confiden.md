## Description
PYY2 (Peptide YY 2 Pseudogene, Gene ID: 23615) is a dark gene with only 5 publications on Semantic Scholar, representing an understudied neuropeptide family member located at chromosome 17q11.2. Despite its pseudogene annotation, PYY2 encodes a 33 amino acid putative peptide (UniProt Q9NRI6) with predicted extracellular localization. A 2000 evolutionary study specifically characterized PYY2 and PPY2 as species-specific novel members of the neuropeptide Y gene family, suggesting functional divergence from canonical PYY. STRING interactions returned no partners, and HPA has no expression data, consistent with low/restricted expression or pseudogene status. ClinVar contains no variants, indicating no known disease associations. The canonical Peptide YY (PYY) is a 36 amino acid gut hormone produced by L-cells in the ileum and colon that inhibits gastric motility, reduces appetite via Y2 receptors in the hypothalamus, and regulates energy homeostasis. PYY belongs to the neuropeptide Y (NPY) family which includes NPY, PYY, and pancreatic polypeptide (PP), all sharing a characteristic PP-fold structure. PYY2's high-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 83.6) and membership in the NPY family suggest it functions as: (1) a processed pseudogene/retrogene producing a truncated but functional neuropeptide with altered receptor specificity compared to canonical PYY, (2) a species-specific neuropeptide variant (the 2000 paper notes species-specific evolution) with specialized physiological roles in certain lineages, (3) a competitive antagonist at PYY receptors (Y1, Y2, Y4, Y5) modulating appetite and gut motility, or (4) a bovine caltrin homolog (UniProt P06833, 80aa) suggesting potential roles in reproductive biology—caltrin proteins are involved in sperm capacitation and calcium regulation. The 33aa length matches the active PYY3-36 form (the DPP4-cleaved active metabolite), suggesting PYY2 may represent a constitutively active neuropeptide bypassing proteolytic processing. The combination of pseudogene annotation, high-confidence structure, NPY family membership, and species-specific evolution literature make PYY2 a compelling candidate for functional investigation as a novel neuropeptide hormone with potential roles in appetite regulation, gut-brain signaling, or reproductive physiology.
