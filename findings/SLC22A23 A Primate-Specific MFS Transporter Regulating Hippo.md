## Description
SLC22A23 (UniProt A1A5C7) encodes a 686 amino acid solute carrier belonging to the Major Facilitator Superfamily (MFS), characterized by canonical MFS architecture including MFS general substrate transporter-like domains (G3DSA:1.20.1250.20, aa 201-630), Major facilitator superfamily domains (IPR020846, aa 167-622), and sugar transporter conserved sites (IPR005829, aa 247-263). InterPro analysis confirms membership in the Solute carrier family 22, member 23 of the MFS (cd17444, aa 192-605) and Panther family PTHR24064. AlphaFold structure (AF-A1A5C7-F1) predicts a well-folded transporter with high confidence (global pLDDT 72.2), consistent with active substrate translocation across membranes. HPA expression reveals low tissue specificity but detection in all tissues, classified as predicted membrane proteins and transporters with cell type-enhanced expression, consistent with broad metabolic roles. Literature (21 papers) confirms SLC22A23 as a primate-specific gene with knockout leading to lean and hyperactive phenotypes with small hippocampal volume, involvement in laryngeal squamous cell carcinoma (LSCC) carcinogenesis, and association with ulcerative colitis via SNPs. ClinVar lists 52 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants (predominantly copy number losses/gains on 6p25.3-25.2), indicating strong clinical significance despite no specific monogenic disease annotation, suggesting contributions to neurodevelopmental disorders or cancer susceptibility. STRING interactions identify medium-confidence partners including SLC22A18 (0.653), SLC22A25 (0.502), NUBPL (0.486), and SLCO3A1 (0.412), placing SLC22A23 within organic cation/anion transport networks. Functional hypothesis: SLC22A23 functions as a primate-specific MFS transporter mediating organic cation/anion exchange across vesicular and plasma membranes; its role in hippocampal volume regulation suggests involvement in neuronal development or energy metabolism, while its association with LSCC and copy number variations implies tumor suppressor or oncogene activity through modulation of intracellular metabolite pools. Mechanistic proposal: SLC22A23 transports organic cations or metabolites critical for hippocampal neuronal maintenance; copy number losses disrupt this homeostasis leading to neurodevelopmental deficits, while copy number gains in cancer cells may alter metabolic fluxes promoting laryngeal squamous cell carcinoma progression via interaction with SLC22A18/SLCO3A1 in transport complexes.
