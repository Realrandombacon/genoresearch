## Description
PTH2 (Parathyroid hormone 2, also known as TIP39, Tuberoinfundibular peptide of 39 residues) is a 100 amino acid secreted neuropeptide precursor (UniProt Q96A98) with high-confidence folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 72.8), characteristic of peptide hormones that undergo proteolytic processing to generate bioactive ligands. The protein contains a Tuberoinfundibular peptide of 39 residues family domain (IPR029396, residues 21-99) enabling neuropeptide signaling pathway activity (GO:0007218) and extracellular region localization (GO:0005576), belongs to the TIP39 peptide family (PF14980, residues 49-99), and is classified as a Tuberoinfundibular peptide of 39 residues family member (PTHR28585, residues 21-99). PTH2 is located on chromosome 19q13.33 and is classified as a predicted secreted protein functioning as a neuropeptide involved in anxiety regulation, nociception, and pituitary hormone release.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals an extremely high-confidence network of parathyroid hormone signaling components and G-protein coupled receptor effectors: PTH2R (0.999, Parathyroid hormone 2 receptor, specific GPCR target), PTH1R (0.977, Parathyroid hormone 1 receptor, related GPCR), PTH (0.962, Parathyroid hormone, structural homolog), GNAS (0.899, Guanine nucleotide-binding protein G(s) subunit alpha, G-protein signaling), GNG2 (0.800, Guanine nucleotide-binding protein G(I)/G(S)/G(T) subunit gamma-2, G-protein complex), GNB1 (0.800, Guanine nucleotide-binding protein G(I)/G(S)/G(O) subunit beta-1, G-protein complex), IGHV3-16 (0.800, Immunoglobulin heavy variable 3-16, potential immune interaction), PTHLH (0.713, Parathyroid hormone-like hormone, structural homolog), CRH (0.592, Corticotropin-releasing hormone, stress response), and AVP (0.575, Arginine vasopressin, neurohypophysial hormone). These interactions confirm PTH2 functions within the parathyroid hormone signaling pathway, with PTH2R as the highest-confidence partner (0.999) reflecting PTH2's role as the exclusive endogenous ligand for the PTH2 receptor.\n\nHPA expression reveals PTH2 has tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in brain (1.0 nTPM), detected in single tissues, consistent with specialized roles as a neuropeptide in central nervous system signaling. This brain restriction (1.0 nTPM) indicates PTH2 functions specifically in neuronal circuits regulating pain perception and emotional states. Not detected in cancer and immune cells suggests strict physiological confinement to neuronal tissue. Subcellular classification as a predicted secreted protein confirms PTH2 is processed and released into the extracellular space to activate PTH2R on target neurons.\n\nClinVar documents 11 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all consisting of extensive copy number gains (19q13.33, 19q13.33-13.41, 19q13.33-13.43, 19p13.3-q13.43 amplifications). The variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity with predominant gain-of-function through duplications. While no specific Mendelian disease is annotated, 19q CNVs are associated with developmental delay, neurological disorders, and behavioral abnormalities. Given PTH2's role in nociception and anxiety, overexpression may cause chronic pain syndromes, heightened anxiety disorders, or altered stress responses through constitutive PTH2R activation.\n\nConservation is excellent across vertebrates: human (Q96A98, 100 aa), mouse (Q91W27, 100 aa), rat (P0C172, 100 aa), bovine (P0C171, 100 aa), zebrafish (Q8QGA2, 157 aa), indicating the TIP39 peptide architecture is highly conserved in vertebrates with identical mature peptide length in mammals, suggesting strong purifying selection on the PTH2R ligand function.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: PTH2 functions as a selective neuropeptide ligand for the PTH2 receptor (PTH2R) that modulates nociceptive thresholds and anxiety-related behaviors in specific brain circuits. The high-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 72.8) indicates a stable folded precursor that is proteolytically processed to release the bioactive 39-residue TIP39 peptide. Through PTH2R interaction (0.999), processed TIP39 activates G(s)-coupled signaling (via GNAS, GNG2, GNB1 interactions 0.899/0.800/0.800) to increase cAMP levels in target neurons, distinct from PTH/PTH1R calcium/phosphorus homeostasis pathways. PTH1R and PTH interactions (0.977/0.962) reflect structural homology but functional divergence, with PTH2 showing no affinity for PTH1R. PTHLH interaction (0.713) suggests evolutionary relationship within the PTH family. CRH and AVP interactions (0.592/0.575) link PTH2 to stress axis regulation and neuroendocrine control. Brain-enriched expression (1.0 nTPM) positions PTH2 in tuberoinfundibular and spinal cord pathways for pain modulation. 19q CNVs cause PTH2 overexpression, potentially leading to hyperalgesia, anxiety disorders, or altered pituitary hormone release. PTH2 represents a candidate gene for chronic pain syndromes and anxiety disorders, and may serve as a therapeutic target for nociception modulation through PTH2R antagonism.
