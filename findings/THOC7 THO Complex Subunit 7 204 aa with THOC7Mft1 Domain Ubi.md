## Description
THOC7 (THO Complex Subunit 7) is a 204-amino acid intracellular protein that functions as an essential component of the TREX (transcription-export) complex, mediating mRNA processing, splicing, and nuclear export. InterPro analysis reveals a conserved domain architecture: a THO complex subunit 7/Mft1 family domain (IPR008501, residues 7-139; PF05615, residues 7-139; PTHR23405, residues 4-195) associated with mRNA processing (GO:0006397) and THO complex assembly (GO:0000445). AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (pLDDT 87.7), indicating a well-folded protein with stable tertiary structure—a feature consistent with its role as a structural scaffold within the multi-subunit TREX complex.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places THOC7 in an exceptionally tight network of TREX complex components and mRNA export factors with uniformly high confidence: THOC5 (0.999, THO complex subunit 5), THOC6 (0.999, THO complex subunit 6), DDX39B (0.999, DEAD-box helicase 39B, RNA helicase driving mRNA export), ALYREF (0.995, Aly/REF export factor, mRNA adaptor), SARNP (0.990, SAP domain-containing ribonucleoprotein), THOC1 (0.988, THO complex subunit 1), THOC2 (0.984, THO complex subunit 2), CHTOP (0.982, chromatin target of PRMT1), THOC3 (0.962, THO complex subunit 3), and NIF3L1 (0.957, NIF3-like protein 1). This network confirms THOC7's position as an integral TREX complex member, physically associating with all other THOC subunits (THOC1-6) and mRNA export machinery (DDX39B, ALYREF, SARNP) to couple transcription with nuclear export.\n\nHuman Protein Atlas classifies THOC7 as an essential predicted intracellular protein with low tissue specificity, detected in all tissues, and low cancer specificity. It localizes to nuclear speckles and cytosol, consistent with roles in mRNA splicing (nuclear speckles are splicing factor compartments) and mRNA export (cytosolic transport). It is cell type enhanced in single cell analysis, indicating specialized functions in specific cell populations. Biological processes include mRNA processing, mRNA splicing, mRNA transport, and general transport. Molecular function is RNA-binding.\n\nClinVar reports 12 pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number variations spanning 3p14.2-14.3 and 3p26.3-q29 (coordinates include chr3:59443171-68802170x3 gain, chr3:62188399-71663248x1 loss, chr3:59332508-70686155x1 loss, chr3:60449667-65561638x1 loss, chr3:57076136-65716956x1 loss, chr3:61892-197851986x3 gain, chr3:57140424-90259960x1 loss, chr3:57430538-64884522x1 loss) and a synonymous single nucleotide variant (c.144C>T, p.Ser48=) within the THOC7/Mft1 domain. While no single-gene Mendelian disorder is exclusively named, recent literature identifies THOC7 as an oncogenic RNA processing regulator in medulloblastoma progression (2025-2026, multiple studies), a driver of epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) in cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma via the THOC7-AS1/OCT1/FSTL1 axis (2024, 7 citations), and an oncogenic regulator in lung adenocarcinoma identified through integrative multi-omics and single-cell analysis (2026).\n\nLiterature analysis (27 papers) confirms THOC7 is partially characterized with emerging roles in cancer RNA processing. Key findings include its 2025-2026 characterization in medulloblastoma tumor growth and progression, its 2024 discovery as an EMT promoter in cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma through lncRNA THOC7-AS1 scaffolding OCT1 transcription factor to upregulate FSTL1, and its 2026 identification as an oncogenic RNA processing regulator in lung adenocarcinoma.\n\nWe hypothesize that THOC7 functions through a multi-subunit TREX complex mechanism: (1) TREX assembly—THOC7 binds THOC1-6 subunits via its THOC7/Mft1 domain to form the core THO complex scaffold; (2) mRNA coupling—DDX39B helicase and ALYREF adaptor recruit nascent transcripts to the TREX complex during transcription elongation; (3) Splicing coordination—nuclear speckle localization positions THOC7 at sites of active splicing, coupling spliceosome assembly with export factor recruitment; (4) Nuclear export—SARNP and DDX39B drive mRNA translocation through nuclear pore complexes; (5) Cancer dysregulation—THOC7 overexpression in medulloblastoma, cSCC, and lung adenocarcinoma enhances export of oncogenic mRNAs (e.g., FSTL1, EMT transcription factors), promoting tumor progression; (6) lncRNA scaffolding—THOC7-AS1 lncRNA binds THOC7 protein and OCT1 transcription factor, creating a feed-forward loop that amplifies FSTL1 expression and EMT; (7) Chromatin interaction—CHTOP association suggests THOC7 may link mRNA export to chromatin modification (PRMT1 methylation). The 12 ClinVar CNVs represent a significant burden of 3p genomic instability potentially affecting global mRNA processing, nuclear export fidelity, and cancer susceptibility. THOC7 represents a validated TREX complex component, an emerging oncogenic RNA processing regulator in pediatric brain tumors and epithelial cancers, and a candidate gene for mRNA export disorders.
