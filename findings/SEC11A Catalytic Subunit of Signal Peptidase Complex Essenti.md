# SEC11A: Catalytic Subunit of Signal Peptidase Complex Essential for Secretory Protein Maturation with Roles in Cancer Progression and Viral Replication

**Date:** 2026-06-14T18:52:18.024810

**Quality Score:** 9/10 (EXCELLENT)

**Confidence:** MEDIUM

## Description
SEC11A (P67812) encodes a 179 amino acid catalytic subunit of the signal peptidase complex (SPC) that functions as a serine endopeptidase responsible for cleaving N-terminal signal peptides from nascent secretory and membrane proteins during their translocation into the endoplasmic reticulum. InterPro analysis reveals a compact peptidase architecture: a peptidase S26B family domain (IPR001733/PF00717, positions 14-179/37-127) containing the characteristic serine protease catalytic machinery; a peptidase S24/S26A/S26B/S26C domain (IPR015927, positions 37-127) shared among signal peptidases; a peptidase S26 domain (IPR019533, positions 48-142) with serine-type endopeptidase activity (GO:0004252); conserved active site residues (IPR019756, positions 54-61) and conserved site (IPR019758, positions 111-124) essential for catalysis; classified within the LexA/signal peptidase-like superfamily (IPR036286, positions 34-129) and SEC11 C-terminal short helix domain (IPR063090, positions 151-176). AlphaFold predicts an exceptionally well-folded structure (pLDDT 90.8, very high confidence) consistent with stable enzymatic architecture required for precise substrate recognition and cleavage. STRING interactions demonstrate exclusive partnerships with secretory pathway machinery: SEC11C (0.946), the paralogous catalytic subunit forming alternative SPC complexes; SPCS1/2/3 (0.403-0.668), signal peptidase complex non-catalytic subunits providing structural scaffolding; SEC61A1/G (0.601-0.645), the translocon channel mediating ER protein import; SSR3 (0.676), signal sequence receptor subunit; POLR2G (0.770), RNA polymerase II subunit suggesting transcriptional coupling; RPL26/RPS25 (0.665-0.677), ribosomal proteins indicating co-translational processing. HPA shows ubiquitous expression across all tissues with low specificity, nuclear membrane and Golgi apparatus localization consistent with ER-associated secretory protein processing, classified under hydrolase/protease molecular functions and metabolic/enzymatic proteins. ClinVar contains 33 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants predominantly large copy number gains and losses spanning chromosome 15q24.1-26.3 regions (e.g., chr15:80776252-92767345 duplications, chr15:84908070-85681134 deletions), though specific monogenic disease associations remain undefined. Literature (37 papers) demonstrates SEC11A contributes to tumor progression in head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC) through growth factor secretion; SEC11A upregulation is a biomarker of poor prognosis in HNSCC and locally advanced gastric cancer; SEC11A identified as critical host factor for human metapneumovirus (HMPV) replication through virus-induced alternative splicing events. The mechanistic hypothesis: SEC11A functions as the catalytic engine of the signal peptidase complex positioned at the ER membrane lumen where it recognizes and cleaves hydrophobic signal peptides from nascent polypeptides emerging through the SEC61 translocon; the serine active site (positions 54-61) performs nucleophilic attack on the peptide bond at the signal peptide cleavage site enabling mature protein release into the ER lumen for folding and glycosylation; SEC11C paralog may provide functional redundancy or tissue-specific specialization; SPCS subunits position SEC11A optimally relative to the translocon exit portal; ubiquitous expression reflects fundamental role in secretory pathway essential for all cells producing secreted/membrane proteins; cancer progression via SEC11A overexpression suggests enhanced secretory capacity enables elevated growth factor/cytokine secretion promoting tumor microenvironment remodeling and metastasis; viral exploitation through HMPV-induced alternative splicing indicates pathogens hijack host secretory machinery for viral protein maturation; copy number variations on 15q may alter SEC11A dosage affecting global secretory protein flux with developmental consequences; very high-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 90.8) indicates rigid active site geometry enabling precise substrate positioning for efficient signal peptide cleavage.
