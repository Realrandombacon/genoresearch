## Description
PRAM1 (PML-RARA-regulated adapter molecule 1, UniProt Q96QH2) is a 670 aa intracellular adaptor protein with a distinctive domain architecture centered on a C-terminal SH3 domain. InterPro analysis identifies a canonical SH3 domain (IPR001452, PF00059, positions 571-649) within a helically-extended SH3 domain superfamily (IPR029294, PF14603, positions 576-663), classified within the FYN-binding protein 1/2-like family (IPR043443, positions 220-659) associated with integrin-mediated signaling and plasma membrane localization. AlphaFold structure shows medium confidence (pLDDT 54.0), indicating PRAM1 is partially intrinsically disordered with a structured C-terminal SH3 domain embedded in flexible linker regions — characteristic of scaffold/adaptor proteins that function through dynamic protein-protein interactions. HPA data reveals bone marrow-enriched expression (91.5 nTPM, tissue enriched), Golgi apparatus localization, and blood cell group enrichment. STRING interactions confirm functional clustering with transcriptional regulators and signaling adaptors: NCOR2 (0.845, nuclear receptor co-repressor 2), NCOR1 (0.746, nuclear receptor co-repressor 1), SKAP1 (0.733, Src kinase-associated phosphoprotein 1), ZBTB16 (0.728, PLZF transcription factor), RARA (0.715, retinoic acid receptor alpha), LCP2 (0.670, SLP-76 adaptor), PML (0.660, promyelocytic leukemia protein), DNMT3A (0.641, DNA methyltransferase 3A), DNMT1 (0.418). ClinVar contains 7 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number gains at 19p13.3-13.2 and missense variant p.Glu626Val. Literature (21 papers) establishes PRAM1 as a target gene of the PML-RARA fusion oncoprotein in acute promyelocytic leukemia (APL), with expression features investigated in AML via microarray databases. FUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: PRAM1 acts as a molecular bridge between transcriptional co-repressor complexes (NCOR1/2) and signaling adaptors (SKAP1, LCP2) during hematopoietic differentiation, modulating retinoic acid receptor signaling. MECHANISTIC PROPOSAL: The C-terminal SH3 domain binds proline-rich motifs in signaling proteins (SKAP1, LCP2), while the N-terminal disordered region recruits transcriptional regulators (NCOR1/2, ZBTB16/PLZF). In APL, PML-RARA fusion aberrantly upregulates PRAM1, disrupting normal differentiation programs. PRAM1 may coordinate epigenetic remodeling via DNMT3A interactions and integrin signaling via its FYN-binding protein family classification. The Golgi localization suggests potential roles in vesicle trafficking or protein sorting during differentiation. The 7 ClinVar variants indicate PRAM1 dysfunction contributes to hematologic disorders, potentially through altered co-repressor complex assembly or signaling adaptor recruitment.
