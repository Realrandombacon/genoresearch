## Description
ANKS1A encodes a 1134 aa cytosolic/nucleoplasmic adapter protein (UniProt Q92625), also known as ANKS1, that functions as a multi-domain scaffolding molecule regulating ephrin receptor signaling, cerebrovascular clearance, and ciliary protein transport through protein-protein interaction networks. The protein features a sophisticated multi-domain architecture: multiple N-terminal ankyrin repeats (IPR002110/G3DSA:1.25.40.20, residues 65-144/140-213/211-278/56-142) for protein binding; dual sterile alpha motif (SAM) domains (IPR001660/G3DSA:1.10.150.50, residues 693-762/767-835) mediating protein-protein interactions and potential polymerization; and a C-terminal phosphotyrosine-binding (PTB/PI) domain (IPR006020/G3DSA:2.30.29.30/cd01274, residues 922-1070/926-1071/937-1071) for recognizing phosphorylated tyrosine motifs in signaling proteins. STRING interactions place ANKS1A in receptor tyrosine kinase signaling and trafficking networks: RIN1 (0.790), a Ras/Rap guanine nucleotide exchange factor linking to EGFR endocytosis; EGFR (0.715), epidermal growth factor receptor; TCP11 (0.629), a tetratricopeptide repeat protein; EPHA8 (0.603), an ephrin type-A receptor confirming predicted ephrin receptor binding; TAF11 (0.579), a transcription factor IID subunit; SCUBE3 (0.567), a secreted glycoprotein; NT5C2 (0.544), a nucleotidase; BAG3 (0.542), a co-chaperone regulating autophagy; DAB2IP (0.433), a Ras GTPase-activating protein; and FANCE (0.405), a Fanconi anemia core complex protein. HPA confirms ubiquitous expression across all tissues with subcellular localization to nucleoplasm and cytosol, annotated as immune cell enhanced and cell type enhanced—consistent with roles in diverse cellular contexts. ClinVar lists 7 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number losses/gains spanning chromosome 6p21.31-6p25.3-q27 (ranging from 2.2Mb to 170Mb) and a missense variant (c.1423G>A/p.Asp475Asn), demonstrating dosage sensitivity though specific disease associations remain undefined. AlphaFold structure prediction yields medium confidence (global pLDDT 60.2), revealing a partially structured architecture with ordered ankyrin/SAM/PTB domains connected by flexible linkers—consistent with scaffold proteins requiring conformational adaptability. Mechanistically, ANKS1A functions through multiple integrated modes: (1) As a PTB domain adapter, ANKS1A recognizes phosphorylated tyrosine motifs in activated receptors (EPHA8, EGFR), recruiting downstream effectors for signal transduction; (2) Recent studies (2023) demonstrate ANKS1A regulates LRP1-mediated cerebrovascular clearance in brain endothelial cells—ANKS1A deficiency impairs Aβ peptide clearance across the blood-brain barrier, linking to Alzheimer's disease pathology; (3) ANKS1A controls intraflagellar transport in ependymal cilia (2023)—deficiency causes aberrant entry of protein transport machinery (FOP) into cilia
