# TRIM32: E3 Ubiquitin Ligase with TRIM/RING/B-box/NHL Domains Mediating Protein Degradation in Muscular Dystrophy and Bardet-Biedl Syndrome

## Description
TRIM32 (Gene ID: 22954, UniProt Q13049) is a 653 amino acid E3 ubiquitin-protein ligase mapping to chromosome 9q33.1 (116,687,304-116,701,298 bp). The protein contains canonical TRIM family architecture: N-terminal RING finger domain (aa 18-67, IPR001841) conferring E3 ubiquitin ligase activity, two B-box zinc fingers (aa 95-138, IPR000315) for protein-protein interactions, coiled-coil region for dimerization, and C-terminal NHL repeats (aa 358-499, IPR01258) for substrate recognition. InterPro analysis confirms: RING-type zinc finger (C3HC4), B-box-type zinc finger, 3 NHL repeats, and TolB-like beta-propeller fold (G3DSA:2.120.10.30). STRING interactions (10 partners, scores 0.567-0.888) reveal functional networks: AGO1 (0.888, RNAi machinery), STING1 (0.888, innate immunity), UBE2D1 (0.869, E2 ubiquitin conjugating enzyme), TRIM56 (0.863, paralogous E3 ligase), FKRP (0.863, Fukutin-related protein, muscular dystrophy), ABI2 (0.860, actin regulation), CAPN3 (0.858, calpain-3 protease, limb-girdle muscular dystrophy), DTNBP1 (0.842, dystrobrevin-binding protein), UBE2V1/UBE2N (0.841/0.567, E2 complex). HPA shows low tissue specificity with detection in many tissues, intermediate filament localization, and classification as disease-related enzyme in Ubl conjugation pathway. ClinVar contains 143 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including frameshifts (c.357dup p.Cys120fs, c.1270dup p.Ala424fs), nonsense (c.1569dup p.Glu524Ter, c.1498C>T p.Arg500Ter), and missense (c.1388C>T p.Ala463Val) mutations. Disease associations: Limb-girdle muscular dystrophy type 2H/R8 (LGMD2H/LGMDR8), Bardet-Biedl syndrome type 11 (BBS11), intellectual disability, ciliopathy. Mechanistic hypothesis: TRIM32 functions as substrate-specific E3 ubiquitin ligase recruiting E2 conjugating enzymes (UBE2D1, UBE2N/UBE2V1) to polyubiquitinate target proteins for proteasomal degradation. NHL repeats recognize specific substrates including: (1) Actin thin filament proteins (desmin, actin) - mutation causes muscular dystrophy via impaired sarcomere protein turnover; (2) Ciliary proteins (BBS complex components) - mutation causes Bardet-Biedl syndrome via defective ciliogenesis; (3) Innate immunity adaptors (STING1) - regulates antiviral responses; (4) Transcription factors (Tat, PIASy) - modulates HIV-1 transactivation and SUMOylation pathways. TRIM32 dimerization via coiled-coil enables avidity for substrates; RING domain coordinates catalytic ubiquitin transfer. FKRP and CAPN3 interactions suggest TRIM32 regulates glycosylation and proteolysis in muscle maintenance. This represents a well-characterized "dark gene" that has emerged as clinically important through human genetics.
