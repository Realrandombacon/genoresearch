# LOC129388898: MPRA-Validated Silencer Element on 2q12.3 with Recurrent Dosage-Sensitive Pathogenic CNVs

**Date:** 2026-03-20T20:20:54.425623

**Quality Score:** 1.97/10  (E=3.65, D=0.3) [POOR]

## Description
LOC129388898 is a 200bp cis-regulatory element (CRE) located on chromosome 2q12.3 (GRCh38: 108870098-108870298), functionally validated as a transcriptional silencer through STARR-seq massively parallel reporter assays (MPRAs) in K562 erythroleukemia cells. This element lacks canonical promoter, enhancer, or CTCF-bound insulator chromatin signatures but demonstrates robust repressive activity against the super core promoter SCP1. As a non-coding regulatory locus, it has no UniProt accession, protein domains, or STRING interaction partners. Human Protein Atlas could not resolve this locus to an Ensembl ID, consistent with its classification as a non-coding regulatory element rather than a protein-coding gene.\n\nClinVar analysis reveals 5 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variants disrupting this region: 3 duplications (2q11.1-13, 2q11.2-13, 2q11.2-13 spanning ~16-16Mb) and 2 deletions (2q12.3-13 spanning ~1.7-1.2Mb). These recurrent CNVs encompass the 2q12.3-13 region and are associated with chromosomal duplication/deletion syndromes featuring developmental delay, intellectual disability, craniofacial dysmorphism, and hematologic abnormalities. The recurrence of both gain and loss variants suggests strong selective pressure against silencer dosage disruption.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: LOC129388898 functions as a critical distal silencer within the 2q12.3 topologically associating domain (TAD), likely regulating expression of neighboring genes through long-range chromatin interactions. Candidate target genes include IL1RN (interleukin-1 receptor antagonist), SATB2 (special AT-rich sequence-binding protein 2, critical for craniofacial development), or BCL2L1 (anti-apoptotic regulator). The silencer recruits co-repressor complexes (e.g., Polycomb Repressive Complex 2, NCoR/SMRT, HDACs) to deposit repressive histone marks (H3K27me3) at target promoters. Duplication may titrate co-repressor complexes or alter TAD architecture, causing haploinsufficiency-like effects. Deletion may derepress proto-oncogenes or developmental regulators. The K562 erythroleukemia validation context supports hematopoietic regulatory function, potentially linking silencer dysfunction to myeloid differentiation defects observed in 2q duplication syndromes.
