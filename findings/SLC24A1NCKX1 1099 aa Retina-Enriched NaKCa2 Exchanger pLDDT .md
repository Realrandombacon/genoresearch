## Description
SLC24A1 (UniProt O60721, 1099 aa), also known as NCKX1 (Na+/Ca2+-K+ Exchanger 1), is a critical retinal membrane transporter responsible for extruding calcium from photoreceptor outer segments during the recovery phase of phototransduction, enabling vision adaptation in low-light conditions. InterPro analysis reveals a complex multi-domain architecture characteristic of the SLC24/NCKX family: sodium/potassium/calcium exchanger family (IPR004481/PF01699, aa 24-1099/459-600 & 938-1086), NCKX1-specific family signature (IPR004817, aa 1-827), and dual ion-binding regions (G3DSA:1.20.1420.30, aa 490-620 & 895-1087) that coordinate the alternating access mechanism for 4Na+:1Ca2+:1K+ exchange. AlphaFold predicts a medium-confidence structure (global pLDDT 54.8), reflecting the challenge of modeling large multi-pass membrane proteins with flexible intracellular loops, though the transmembrane core domains show higher local confidence consistent with the conserved ion-binding pockets. HPA classifies it as a tissue-enriched metabolic protein with extraordinary retina-specific expression (179.2 nTPM), undetectable in most other tissues, confirming its specialized role in photoreceptor physiology; subcellular localization is predicted to the plasma membrane of rod and cone outer segments. STRING interactions reveal a tightly integrated phototransduction network: CNGA1 (0.773, cyclic nucleotide-gated channel alpha subunit), CABP4 (0.704, calcium-binding protein 4), PDE6B (0.681, phosphodiesterase 6B), LRIT3 (0.666, leucine-rich repeat protein), CNGB3 (0.627, cyclic nucleotide-gated channel beta subunit), SLC8A1 (0.623, NCX1 sodium/calcium exchanger paralog), GNAT1 (0.621, transducin alpha subunit), RCVRN (0.533, recoverin), PDE6A (0.522), and CNGB1 (0.521), placing SLC24A1 at the heart of the visual cascade. ClinVar contains 60 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including frameshifts (c.60del p.His21fs, c.2186_2187del p.Pro729fs), nonsense mutations (c.28C>T p.Gln10Ter, c.1749G>A p.Trp583Ter), and splice variants (c.1945-2A>G), all causing autosomal recessive congenital stationary night blindness (CSNB) characterized by impaired rod function and non-progressive night vision loss from birth. Functional hypothesis: SLC24A1 operates as the terminal calcium clearance mechanism in photoreceptors. Upon light exposure, cGMP-gated channels close, reducing intracellular Ca2+; SLC24A1 then extrudes residual Ca2+ in exchange for Na+ and K+ to reset the photoreceptor for subsequent photon detection. The dual ion-binding domains (aa 490-620, 895-1087) likely undergo conformational changes during the transport cycle, with the medium-confidence AlphaFold structure suggesting flexible linkers between rigid transmembrane bundles. Loss-of-function mutations cause Ca2+ accumulation in outer segments, preventing channel reopening and causing sustained hyperpolarization—manifesting clinically as night blindness. The retina-specific expression (179.2 nTPM) explains the tissue-restricted phenotype despite ubiquitous calcium signaling needs. The interaction with CABP4 (0.704) suggests calcium-dependent regulation, while SLC8A1 (0.623) represents a potential compensatory paralog in non-retinal tissues. Conservation across vertebrates (mouse Q91WD8, rat Q9QZM6, bovine Q28139) confirms essential function. NOTE: This gene represents a 'dark' but disease-defined transporter where retinal specialization masks potential roles in other calcium-regulated processes.
