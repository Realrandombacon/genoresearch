## Description
RNF39 (Q9H2S5, 420 aa) is a RING-HC finger E3 ubiquitin-protein ligase with bipartite domain architecture: N-terminal RING-HC finger domain (cd16592, aa 82-139; IPR001841, aa 88-134) for E2 binding and catalytic activity, and C-terminal PRY/SPRY domain (cd12888, aa 229-415; IPR001870, aa 210-420; IPR003877, aa 281-419) for substrate recognition. AlphaFold predicts medium-confidence structure (pLDDT 66.3, residues 1-420), consistent with ordered RING and SPRY domains connected by flexible linker regions. STRING reveals 10 interactions with strongest partners being HLA-F (0.796), ZNRD1 (0.707), and HLA-A (0.687) - all within or near the MHC class I region on chromosome 6p22.1. Additional interactions include C10orf105 (0.578), TCF19 (0.560), TP53 (0.557), CDSN (0.539), KIAA1257 (0.523), TRIM31 (0.523), and CCHCR1 (0.491). ClinVar contains 7 pathogenic variants, predominantly copy number gains and deletions spanning 6p regions, though no specific diseases are yet assigned. HPA data unavailable. Functional hypothesis: RNF39 functions as an MHC-associated E3 ubiquitin ligase that regulates antigen presentation and immune signaling through ubiquitination of HLA class I molecules (HLA-F, HLA-A interactions). The SPRY domain likely recognizes specific substrates while the RING-HC finger catalyzes ubiquitin transfer. The strong interaction with ZNRD1 (zinc finger RNA polymerase I subunit, 0.707) suggests potential roles in transcriptional regulation. Rat studies indicate involvement in early-phase synaptic plasticity, possibly through activity-dependent ubiquitination of synaptic proteins. The MHC class I localization and HLA interactions position RNF39 at the intersection of immune regulation and neuronal function, potentially explaining associations with neurodevelopmental and autoimmune conditions via 6p CNVs. TRIM31 interaction (0.523) suggests functional redundancy or cooperation with related TRIM family E3 ligases in MHC-mediated processes.
