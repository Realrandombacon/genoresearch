# SLC48A1 (HRG1): A High-Confidence Heme Transporter Regulating Myelin Integrity and Ferroptosis via Endosomal Recycling and Iron Homeostasis

**Date:** 2026-04-16T11:03:16.547525

**Quality Score:** 3.98/10  (E=6.15, D=1.8) [MODERATE]

## Description
SLC48A1 (UniProt Q6P1K1) encodes a 146 amino acid heme transporter (HRG1) localized to endosomes and lysosomes, critical for intracellular heme availability and iron recycling. InterPro analysis confirms a canonical HRG family fold (IPR026218, PF16954) spanning the entire protein (aa 4-145), with specific heme-binding motifs (IPR026218) essential for substrate transport. AlphaFold structure (AF-Q6P1K1-F1) predicts a highly stable, well-folded transporter with high confidence (global pLDDT 86.9), consistent with its role in maintaining structural integrity of heme-containing proteins. HPA expression reveals exceptional enrichment in the brain (131.3 nTPM), significantly higher than other tissues, with predicted vesicular localization, aligning with its role in oligodendrocyte myelination. Literature search (21 papers) confirms its essential role in normal iron homeostasis and myelin integrity, with biallelic loss-of-function variants causing hypomyelinating leukodystrophy and developmental delay. ClinVar lists 12 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants (mostly copy number gains/losses on chr12 and single nucleotide variants), though no specific monogenic disease is yet annotated, suggesting contributions to complex neurodevelopmental disorders or ferroptosis susceptibility. STRING interactions identify high-confidence partners including FLVCR1 (0.614), SLC46A1 (0.599), and FECH (0.533), placing HRG1 within the iron/heme transport network and linking it to ferroptosis regulation via SLC7A11 (0.444). Functional hypothesis: SLC48A1 functions as a specialized endosomal heme transporter that regulates intracellular heme availability for proteolipid biosynthesis in oligodendrocytes; its interaction with FLVCR1 and SLC46A1 facilitates iron-heme recycling, where dysregulation contributes to hypomyelination and potential ferroptosis susceptibility via modulation of lipid peroxidation.
