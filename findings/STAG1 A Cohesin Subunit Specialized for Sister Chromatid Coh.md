# STAG1: A Cohesin Subunit Specialized for Sister Chromatid Cohesion and DNA Repair via HEAT Repeat-Mediated Scaffolding

## Description
STAG1 (Stromal Antigen 1, UniProt Q8WVM7) is a 1,258-amino acid nuclear protein that serves as an essential subunit of the cohesin complex, responsible for holding sister chromatids together from DNA replication until anaphase. InterPro analysis reveals a characteristic architecture dominated by HEAT repeats: a central Armadillo-type fold (IPR016024, aa 177-805) and specific HEAT-repeat domains (IPR056396, PF24571, aa 483-747) that form a curved solenoid structure ideal for protein-protein interactions. It also contains a conserved STAG domain (IPR013721, PF08514, aa 161-268) and a Stromalin Conservative Domain (IPR020839, PS51425, aa 296-381) critical for complex stability. The AlphaFold predicted structure shows high confidence (pLDDT 78.6), confirming a rigid, elongated scaffold capable of bridging large molecular distances. STRING interaction data places STAG1 at the core of the cohesin ring, with perfect associations to the structural maintenance of chromosomes proteins SMC3 (0.999) and SMC1B (0.998), and the kleisin subunit RAD21 (0.965). It also interacts strongly with the paralog STAG2 (0.982) and the loader NIPBL (0.942), suggesting distinct but overlapping roles in loop extrusion and cohesion establishment. HPA expression profiles indicate ubiquitous presence ('Detected in all') with specific localization to the nucleoplasm, nuclear bodies, and notably the primary cilium, hinting at non-canonical roles in ciliary signaling or cell cycle coordination. ClinVar records document 170 pathogenic variants, including frameshifts (p.Thr152fs), nonsense mutations (p.Arg1206Ter), and missense changes affecting the HEAT repeats, which cause Mendelian Recurrent Developmental Disorder 47 (MRD47) characterized by intellectual disability and growth retardation. We hypothesize that STAG1 confers specificity to the cohesin complex for certain genomic loci or repair pathways; its unique HEAT repeat arrangement may recruit specific regulators (like PDS5A/B or WAPL) to modulate the 'cohesion-release' cycle differently than STAG2. Disruption of STAG1 likely leads to premature sister chromatid separation, genomic instability, and defective DNA double-strand break repair, underlying the neurodevelopmental phenotypes observed in patients.
