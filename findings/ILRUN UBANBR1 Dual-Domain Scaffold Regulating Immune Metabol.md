# ILRUN: UBA/NBR1 Dual-Domain Scaffold Regulating Immune Metabolism and Viral Entry

**Date:** 2026-04-16T12:30:28.798802

**Quality Score:** 2.85/10  (E=4.6, D=1.1) [WEAK]

## Description
ILRUN encodes a 298-amino acid protein characterized by a conserved UBA-like domain (IPR009060, PF14555, residues 23-65) and an Ig-like fold (IPR013783, PF16158, residues 70-181) adjacent to an NBR1-like domain (IPR032350, residues 71-180). AlphaFold predicts a well-folded structure (pLDDT 71.2), suggesting a stable scaffold capable of mediating protein-protein interactions. STRING interactions identify partners in lipid metabolism and autophagy pathways: SPDEF (0.597), SMIM29 (0.577), NUDT3 (0.551), and HSPBP1 (0.451), supporting a role in nuclear speckle organization and cytoskeletal dynamics. HPA data reveals high expression in skeletal muscle (202.1 nTPM) with ubiquitous detection, localizing to nuclear speckles, centrosomes, and cytosol—consistent with a role in RNA processing and organelle tethering. ClinVar reports 5 pathogenic copy number variants (gains/losses at 6p21.31-21.2), indicating dosage sensitivity despite the lack of a defined Mendelian disease phenotype. Literature (18 papers) confirms ILRUN as an inhibitor of proinflammatory cytokines (IFN-alpha/beta) and a regulator of RAAS, with recent findings linking it to SARS-CoV-2 entry blockade via ACE2 downregulation. The mechanistic hypothesis is that ILRUN functions as a phase-separated scaffold that sequesters UBA/NBR1 effectors to modulate lipid droplet dynamics and cytokine signaling; its structural integrity allows it to act as a negative regulator of innate immunity and viral entry, while copy number alterations disrupt this balance, potentially contributing to autoimmune susceptibility or viral susceptibility.
