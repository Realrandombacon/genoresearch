HPA data localizes ZNF665 to the nuclear membrane with low tissue specificity and expression detected in some tissues, consistent with cell-type-specific transcriptional regulation. The protein is classified as a transcription factor with DNA-binding molecular function. ClinVar documents 17 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including multiple chromosome 19q copy number gains (chr19:49625130-57647352x3, chr19:50152520-58581203x3) and point variants (c.15G>A p.Gln5=, c.1423A>G p.Ser475Gly), indicating dosage sensitivity and disease relevance. Conservation across primates is evident (orangutan Q5R8X1 613 aa, rhinopithecus A0A2K6MMC5 629 aa). Mechanistic hypothesis: ZNF665 functions as a sequence-specific transcriptional repressor that binds genomic DNA through its C2H2 zinc finger array and recruits the TRIM28/KAP1 corepressor complex via the KRAB domain, leading to heterochromatin formation through HP1 recruitment and H3K9me3 histone methylation. This silences target genes involved in cell differentiation or proliferation. Dysregulation through copy number gain would cause aberrant repression of tumor suppressor or developmental genes, potentially contributing to disease phenotypes.
