# NIM1K: Prenylated Serine/Threonine Kinase Trafficked to Primary Cilia via PDE6D — Potential Regulator of Ciliary Signaling and Cell Cycle in Brain and Retina

## Description
NIM1K (NIM1 serine/threonine protein kinase) encodes a 436 amino acid cytosolic kinase (UniProt Q8IY84) belonging to the NIM1 kinase family. InterPro analysis reveals a canonical serine/threonine protein kinase domain (IPR000719, PF00069, cd14075, IPR049571, residues 71-325) with conserved ATP-binding site (IPR017441, PS00107, residues 80-103) and catalytic active site (IPR008271, PS00108, residues 192-204). The kinase domain adopts a protein kinase-like fold superfamily (IPR011009, G3DSA:1.10.510.10, residues 71-366) with transferase phosphotransferase domain architecture. NIM1K is classified as a predicted membrane protein, consistent with potential prenylation-mediated membrane association.\n\nAlphaFold prediction shows high global confidence (pLDDT 82.4) across all 436 aa, indicating a well-folded stable kinase structure. The high confidence suggests NIM1K maintains a rigid catalytic core optimized for ATP binding and substrate phosphorylation, typical of active serine/threonine kinases.\n\nSTRING interactions reveal NIM1K operates in cell cycle and calcium signaling networks with medium confidence scores: PCP4L1 (0.487) is a calmodulin-binding protein involved in calcium signaling; TPCN1 (0.453) is two-pore calcium channel protein 1 mediating endolysosomal calcium release; ZNF131 (0.452) is a zinc finger transcription factor; WEE1 (0.447) is a critical cell cycle checkpoint kinase that phosphorylates CDK1 to inhibit G2/M transition; PLA2G4D (0.434) is phospholipase A2; CCDC152 (0.429) is a coiled-coil domain protein; MYBPC2 (0.421) is myosin-binding protein C; AMPD3 (0.419) is AMP deaminase; GPR26 (0.416) is a G-protein coupled receptor; TNK2 (0.408) is a non-receptor tyrosine kinase. The WEE1 interaction strongly suggests NIM1K functions in cell cycle regulation, potentially as a checkpoint kinase or WEE1 regulator. TPCN1 and PCP4L1 interactions link NIM1K to calcium signaling pathways.\n\nHPA expression shows NIM1K is brain-enriched (12.1 nTPM) with group-enriched RNA specificity, also detected in retina (9.5 nTPM), pituitary gland (6.3 nTPM), and choroid plexus (4.2 nTPM). It shows cancer-enriched RNA specificity and is not detected in immune cells. Subcellular localization is cytosolic. NIM1K is classified as an enzyme, predicted membrane protein with kinase, serine/threonine-protein kinase, and transferase molecular functions.\n\nClinVar reports 8 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all 5p13.3-11 copy number gains. While no specific monogenic disease is definitively assigned, the 5p13 locus overlaps with developmental disorder regions. The CNV sensitivity suggests NIM1K dosage affects neurodevelopment.\n\nLiterature (only 5 papers) reveals a critical but understudied role: The landmark 2023 study (7 citations) demonstrated that NIM1K is a prenylated protein trafficked by PDE6D (phosphodiesterase 6D) to primary cilia. PDE6D mutations impair NIM1K ciliary localization, causing Joubert syndrome — a ciliopathy characterized by cerebellar vermis hypoplasia, intellectual disability, and retinal degeneration. This directly links NIM1K dysfunction to human ciliopathy pathogenesis. NIM1K requires prenylation (likely geranylgeranylation) for PDE6D recognition and ciliary membrane targeting.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: NIM1K functions as a ciliary serine/threonine kinase that regulates ciliary signaling cascades critical for neurodevelopment and photoreceptor function. The prenylation-PDE6D trafficking mechanism positions NIM1K at the ciliary membrane where it likely phosphorylates ciliary substrates involved in Hedgehog signaling, Wnt signaling, or calcium-dependent pathways. The brain and retina expression pattern matches Joubert syndrome phenotypes — NIM1K loss from cilia in these tissues would disrupt developmental signaling. The WEE1 interaction suggests NIM1K may coordinate ciliary signaling with cell cycle progression, potentially phosphorylating WEE1 or shared substrates to couple ciliary status to G2/M checkpoint control. This is particularly relevant in proliferating neural progenitors where cilia disassembly precedes mitosis. The TPCN1 interaction suggests NIM1K may regulate endolysosomal calcium release, which is critical for ciliary function and autophagy. In cancer, NIM1K overexpression may drive proliferation through WEE1-mediated checkpoint bypass or enhanced ciliary signaling. The 5p13 CNV gains suggest NIM1K dosage affects neurodevelopment — overexpression may be as deleterious as loss. Therapeutic strategies targeting NIM1K prenylation or PDE6D interaction could restore ciliary localization in Joubert syndrome.
