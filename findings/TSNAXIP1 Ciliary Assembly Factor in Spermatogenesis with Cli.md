## Description
TSNAXIP1 (Translin-associated factor X-interacting protein 1) is a 658 amino acid protein (UniProt Q2TAA8) with a well-defined 3D structure (AlphaFold pLDDT 84.1, high confidence). The protein contains a conserved N-terminal domain (IPR032755/PF15739, residues 52-161) characteristic of the translin-associated factor X-interacting family (PTHR16306). \n\nTSNAXIP1 shows testis-enriched expression (19.7 nTPM) with secondary expression in fallopian tube (8.1 nTPM), consistent with its predicted role in spermatogenesis and cell differentiation. The protein localizes to nucleoplasm and cytosol, with predicted perinuclear cytoplasmic activity.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals TSNAXIP1 functions in a ciliary/flagellar protein network: strongest interaction with TSNAX (score 0.884), its binding partner, plus connections to DNAI1 (0.686), TEKT3 (0.627), CFAP74 (0.615), and LRRC71 (0.612) - all established ciliary axoneme or flagellar assembly proteins. This interaction pattern strongly suggests TSNAXIP1 participates in sperm flagellum formation during spermatogenesis.\n\nClinVar documents 28 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including nonsense mutations (p.Tyr145Ter) and multiple copy number variants (losses and gains at 16q22.1), indicating clinical relevance though specific disease associations remain undefined. Conservation is evident from mouse ortholog (Q99P25, 704 aa), suggesting functional importance across mammals.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: TSNAXIP1 acts as a scaffold protein that bridges TSNAX (a DNA/RNA-binding complex) with ciliary assembly machinery, coordinating gene expression programs required for flagellar biogenesis during spermatid differentiation. Disruption causes male infertility through defective sperm motility.
