## Description
CCDC17 (UniProt Q96LX7) is a 622 amino acid coiled-coil domain-containing protein representing an understudied component of the human dark genome with emerging roles in transcriptional regulation and reproductive tissue function. The protein contains extensive coiled-coil architecture with three defining domains: (1) full-length CCDC17 family domain (IPR038800/PTHR33820) spanning residues 8-620, encompassing 98% of the protein sequence, (2) middle coiled-coil domain (IPR060405/PF28454) at residues 240-316 forming a central structural module, (3) C-terminal domain (IPR060404/PF28455) at residues 399-565 potentially mediating specific protein-protein interactions. AlphaFold predicts a medium-confidence structure (global pLDDT 67.9) spanning the full 1-622 residue length, indicating CCDC17 is a partially structured protein with ordered coiled-coil regions interspersed with flexible linkers—this architecture is characteristic of scaffold proteins that assemble multi-protein complexes through conformational plasticity. STRING interaction network reveals high-to-medium confidence associations with functionally coherent partners centered on transcriptional machinery: GPBP1L1 (0.898, GC-rich promoter-binding protein 1-like 1, highest confidence partner, serine/threonine kinase involved in transcriptional activation), ZNF684 (0.650, zinc finger protein 684, transcriptional regulator), and strikingly, seven RNA polymerase II subunits including POLR2D (0.585), POLR2G (0.567), CCDC173 (0.567), POLR2K (0.563), POLR2F (0.563), POLR2E (0.563), POLR2L (0.562), POLR2H (0.558). This interaction profile provides compelling evidence that CCDC17 functions as a transcriptional co-regulator associating with the RNA polymerase II holoenzyme complex, potentially serving as: (1) scaffold protein stabilizing Pol II assembly through coiled-coil mediated oligomerization, (2) regulatory subunit modulating Pol II activity in response to signaling cues via GPBP1L1 kinase interaction, (3) tissue-specific transcription factor recruiter via ZNF684 association, (4) chromatin accessibility regulator facilitating Pol II recruitment to reproductive tissue-specific promoters. HPA classifies CCDC17 as a predicted intracellular protein with tissue-enriched RNA expression pattern, showing highest expression in fallopian tube (33.9 nTPM), with detection in some but not all tissues and group-enriched cancer specificity, suggesting specialized function in female reproductive biology. Blood cell expression is absent, indicating CCDC17 is not involved in immune function. ClinVar contains 7 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including missense mutation (c.1669A>G p.Ile557Val within C-terminal domain), large copy number gains affecting chromosome 1p regions (chr1:44475302-89585894 1p34.1-22.2, chr1:33285582-47891811 1p35.1-33, chr1:849467-249224684 1p36.33-q44), copy number loss (chr1:45303358-52157856 1p34.1-32.3), and inversion, indicating CCDC17 resides in dosage-sensitive genomic regions where alterations cause disease phenotypes, potentially affecting reproductive development and fertility given fallopian tube enrichment. Evolutionary conservation is demonstrated by orthologs across vertebrates (mouse, rat, bovine), confirming functional constraint. The mechanistic model positions CCDC17 as a nuclear coiled-coil scaffold protein functioning through: (1) homo-oligomerization via middle coiled-coil domain forming dimeric/tetrameric assemblies, (2) hetero-oligomerization with RNA Pol II subunits through C-terminal domain mediating stable complex formation, (3) recruitment of GPBP1L1 kinase for phosphorylation-dependent regulation of transcriptional activity, (4) tissue-specific gene expression control in fallopian tube and reproductive tissues via ZNF684 cooperation. Dosage alterations from 1p CNVs would disrupt stoichiometry of transcriptional complexes, potentially causing developmental anomalies or reproductive disorders observed in chromosome 1p duplication/deletion syndromes.
