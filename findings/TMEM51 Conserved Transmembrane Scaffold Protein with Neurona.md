## Description
TMEM51 (C1orf72) encodes a 253 amino acid single-pass transmembrane protein belonging to the TMEM51 family (IPR029265, PF15345, PTHR16015, residues 1-253). The protein is predicted to localize to nucleoplasm and cytosol despite its transmembrane domain classification, suggesting potential dual membrane-associated and nuclear functions.

Protein interaction network (STRING) reveals associations with neuronal and ciliary proteins: CCDC183 (0.535, coiled-coil protein), FRMPD1 (0.502, FERM domain protein implicated in neuronal polarity), LHFPL2 (0.484, tetraspanin-like protein in hair cell stereocilia), CCDC9B (0.480, ciliary basal body protein), CDC42EP3 (0.471, CDC42 effector regulating actin cytoskeleton), SPATS2L (0.469), TMEM86A (0.449), PTER (0.449), CPNE5 (0.433, calcium-dependent phospholipid binding), and MCTP2 (0.423, multiple C2 domain transmembrane protein). This interaction profile suggests TMEM51 functions as a scaffold protein organizing multiprotein complexes at membrane-cytoskeleton interfaces, particularly in neuronal tissues.

HPA expression shows low tissue specificity with detection across many tissues and cell type-enhanced expression pattern, consistent with a ubiquitous scaffold function. Subcellular localization in nucleoplasm and cytosol indicates potential roles in nucleocytoplasmic transport or transcriptional regulation.

Clinical significance is demonstrated by 24 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants in ClinVar, predominantly large copy number variations on chromosome 1p36, indicating dosage sensitivity. The 1p36 deletion syndrome region encompasses TMEM51, suggesting haploinsufficiency contributes to neurodevelopmental phenotypes.

Mechanistic hypothesis: TMEM51 serves as a membrane-associated scaffold that anchors cytoskeletal regulators (CDC42EP3) and neuronal polarity determinants (FRMPD1, LHFPL2) to intracellular membranes. The conserved transmembrane domain anchors the protein while cytoplasmic regions recruit interaction partners. In neuronal development, TMEM51 may organize protein complexes at growth cones or synaptic membranes. Nuclear localization suggests potential chromatin-associated functions. Disease variants likely disrupt scaffold assembly, causing neurodevelopmental defects through impaired cytoskeletal organization and neuronal migration.
