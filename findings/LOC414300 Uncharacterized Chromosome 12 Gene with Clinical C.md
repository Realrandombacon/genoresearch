## Description
LOC414300 is a poorly characterized locus on chromosome 12q23.3 (genomic coordinates 105,102,471-105,107,641) that remains profoundly unannotated in major protein databases. No UniProt accession exists, precluding InterPro domain analysis, AlphaFold structure prediction, and detailed sequence characterization. STRING database found no protein interaction partners, and the Human Protein Atlas could not resolve the gene symbol to an Ensembl ID, indicating absence of validated antibodies or detectable expression in standard assays. Despite this molecular obscurity, ClinVar reports 2 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variants encompassing the LOC414300 region: large-scale duplications (CNV gains) on chromosome 12 spanning 12q21.33-q24.11 (91,044,318-109,133,210) and 12p13.33-q24.33 (121,271-133,196,807). These megabase-scale CNVs suggest the locus resides in a dosage-sensitive genomic region, though the variants are too large to attribute phenotype specifically to LOC414300. The "LOC" nomenclature indicates this is an uncharacterized open reading frame with predicted coding potential but no experimentally verified protein product. Given the complete absence of molecular characterization combined with location in a clinically relevant CNV region, LOC414300 may represent a false-positive gene prediction, a non-coding RNA misannotated as protein-coding, or a genuinely novel primate-specific gene requiring experimental validation. Priority for functional characterization should focus on confirming transcription and translation before mechanistic studies.
