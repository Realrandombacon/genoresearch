## Description
ZNF18 is a 549 amino acid Krüppel-associated box (KRAB) domain-containing zinc finger protein located on chromosome 17p12. The protein contains three key functional domains: a SCAN oligomerization domain (aa 37-149, IPR003309) enabling protein-protein interactions and homodimerization, a KRAB-A repression domain (aa 211-283, IPR001909) that recruits KAP1/TRIM28 co-repressors for transcriptional silencing, and three C2H2-type zinc finger DNA-binding domains (aa 408-491, IPR013087) that recognize specific GC-rich DNA sequences. AlphaFold structure prediction shows medium confidence (pLDDT 54.4), consistent with modular domain architecture connected by flexible linkers typical of transcription factors. STRING interaction network reveals strong associations with other zinc finger proteins including ZNF24 (score 0.790), ZNF446 (0.726), and SCAND1 (0.704), suggesting participation in a coordinated transcriptional regulatory complex. HPA data shows ubiquitous expression across all tissues with low tissue specificity and nucleoplasmic localization, consistent with housekeeping transcriptional regulation roles. ClinVar documents 12 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly copy number gains spanning 17p13.3-12, indicating dosage sensitivity and potential disease mechanisms through overexpression. Conservation is evident with mouse (Q810A1, 556 aa) and rat (Q642B9, 556 aa) orthologs. Functional hypothesis: ZNF18 acts as a sequence-specific transcriptional repressor that silences target genes through KRAB-mediated recruitment of heterochromatin-forming complexes, with SCAN domain enabling cooperative binding through oligomerization. Dysregulation via copy number variation may contribute to developmental disorders through aberrant gene silencing programs.
