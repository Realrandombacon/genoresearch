# Uncharacterized Locus at 17p11.2 within Smith-Magenis/Potocki-Lupski Syndrome Critical Region with Pathogenic Copy Number Gain

## Description
LOC339260 is an uncharacterized genomic locus on chromosome 17p11.2 (NC_000017.11:20938565-20982356) spanning approximately 43.8 kb. Comprehensive database searches reveal no evidence of canonical protein-coding function: no UniProt accession was identified, precluding InterPro domain analysis and AlphaFold structure prediction. STRING database returned no protein interaction partners, confirming absence of characterized interactome. Human Protein Atlas could not resolve this locus to an Ensembl ID, indicating no detectable protein expression in surveyed tissues. However, ClinVar analysis reveals 1 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variant in this region: a copy number gain (trisomy) spanning 17p11.2 (chr17:16426633-20998588, ~4.6 Mb). This structural variant is associated with Potocki-Lupski syndrome (PTLS), a reciprocal duplication syndrome to Smith-Magenis syndrome (SMS). The 17p11.2 region contains RAI1 (retinoic acid induced 1), the dosage-sensitive gene responsible for both SMS (haploinsufficiency) and PTLS (trisomy). PTLS features include developmental delay, intellectual disability, infantile hypotonia, failure to thrive, autism spectrum disorder, and cardiovascular anomalies. LOC339260 likely functions as: (1) part of the RAI1 cis-regulatory landscape (enhancer/silencer elements), (2) a long non-coding RNA (lncRNA) modulating RAI1 expression, (3) a chromatin boundary element within the 17p11.2 topologically associating domain (TAD), or (4) an alternative transcript isoform of RAI1 or neighboring genes. The pathogenic copy number gain despite absence of protein evidence indicates this locus resides within the critical 17p11.2 dosage-sensitive region where trisomy disrupts neurodevelopmental processes via RAI1 overexpression and 3D genome reorganization affecting multiple 17p11.2 genes.
