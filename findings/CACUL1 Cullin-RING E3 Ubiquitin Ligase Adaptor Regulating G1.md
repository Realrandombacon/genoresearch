## Description
CACUL1 (Q86Y37) is a 369-aa CDK2-associated and cullin domain-containing protein with confident AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 72.6), indicating a stable well-folded protein with characteristic cullin architecture. InterPro analysis reveals cullin N-terminal domain (IPR001373/PF00888: positions 137-293), cullin repeat-like superfamily (IPR016159/SSF74788: positions 131-334), and CACUL1-specific family signatures (IPR042652/PTHR46636: positions 1-368), characteristic of cullin scaffold proteins that assemble multi-subunit E3 ubiquitin ligase complexes. HPA expression shows ubiquitous tissue distribution with low specificity, localized to nucleoplasm, functioning in cell cycle regulation. STRING interactions demonstrate medium-confidence binding to cullin-RING ligase components RNF7 (0.645), CAND1 (0.631), CAND2 (0.563), RBX1 (0.594), DCUN1D1 (0.544), SKP1 (0.429), plus substrate adaptors KBTBD4 (0.616), SPOP (0.606), and SPOPL (0.597). Literature (17 papers) establishes CACUL1 as positive regulator of G1/S transition and cell proliferation through CDK2 binding and activation. Recent 2024-2025 studies reveal CACUL1 forms complex with ARMC5 in adrenal cortical tumorigenesis (macronodular bilateral adrenal hyperplasia), promotes gallbladder cancer lymphatic metastasis via CACUL1/NRF2/ITGBL1/ITGA9/VEGF-C inflammatory pathway, and serves as target of circular RNA sponges in colorectal cancer (circZFR/miR-147a/CACUL1 axis) and gastric cancer (circ-ERBB2/miR-503/CACUL1 axis). ClinVar contains 32 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including splice site variants (c.886+78T>A) and copy number gains/losses at 10q25.1-26.3, though specific monogenic disease associations remain undefined. The confident structure (pLDDT 72.6) and cullin domain architecture suggest CACUL1 functions as scaffold adaptor for Cullin-RING E3 ubiquitin ligase complex, recruiting specific substrates for ubiquitin-mediated degradation while simultaneously binding/activating CDK2 to drive G1/S cell cycle progression, explaining its oncogenic role in multiple cancer types through dual regulation of protein turnover and cell cycle kinases.
