## Description
DYRK4 (Dual Specificity Tyrosine-Phosphorylation-Regulated Kinase 4) is a 520-amino acid intracellular kinase with 36 publications confirming its partially characterized dark gene status as an emerging member of the DYRK kinase family. The protein belongs to the protein kinase family (IPR000719, positions 104-400; IPR011009, positions 95-420; IPR017441, positions 110-133) containing a canonical catalytic kinase domain (cd14225, positions 60-400) with serine/threonine-protein kinase active site (IPR008271, positions 226-238) and N-terminal DYRK-specific domain (IPR042521, positions 16-76; G3DSA:3.30.10.30, positions 16-76) characteristic of dual-specificity tyrosine-phosphorylation-regulated kinases. AlphaFold predicts a well-folded structure (pLDDT 77.7, high confidence), consistent with stable kinase architecture typical of the CMGC kinase group. HPA reveals tissue-enhanced expression with testis (34.3 nTPM) as the top tissue, detected in all tissue distribution, low cancer specificity, low immune cell specificity, group enriched single cell type specificity, with annotated functions as kinase, serine/threonine-protein kinase, transferase, tyrosine-protein kinase, and subcellular localization to vesicles. ClinVar documents 58 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants (predominantly chromosome 12p13.33-11.1 and 12p13.33-q13.12 copy number gains and losses, all with uncertain clinical significance), indicating strong dosage sensitivity potentially affecting kinase signaling and developmental programs. STRING interactions reveal DYRK4 functions within the kinase regulatory network with high confidence connection to DCAF7 (0.856) - DDB1 and CUL4 associated factor 7, known DYRK kinase regulator and substrate adaptor, and medium confidence interactions with SLC13A5 (0.586) - solute carrier family 13 member 5 sodium-citrate transporter, and NRIP2 (0.435) - nuclear receptor interacting protein 2. Recent studies (2020-2024) established DYRK4 as upregulator of antiviral innate immunity by promoting IRF3 activation during viral infection, DYRK4 hypomethylation in peripheral blood associated with increased lung cancer risk, DYRK4 hypomethylation associated with coronary heart disease, and RAD51AP1-DYRK4 neoplastic fusion transcript conferring sensitivity to MEK inhibitor trametinib in aggressive luminal B breast cancers. We hypothesize DYRK4 functions as a dual-specificity kinase that phosphorylates both serine/threonine and tyrosine residues on substrate proteins through the canonical kinase domain (positions 60-400) with the active site (positions 226-238) catalyzing phosphate transfer from ATP (binding site positions 110-133). The DCAF7 interaction (0.856) suggests DYRK4 functions within a CUL4-DDB1 E3 ubiquitin ligase complex analogous to other DYRK kinases, where DCAF7 acts as substrate adaptor regulating kinase activity and substrate recognition. The IRF3 activation mechanism (2024 study) indicates DYRK4 phosphorylates IRF3 transcription factor promoting type I interferon production and antiviral responses. The testis-enriched expression (34.3 nTPM) suggests specialized function in spermatogenesis or germ cell development. The vesicle localization indicates potential role in membrane trafficking or secretory pathways. The epigenetic regulation through DNA methylation (2023 lung cancer and CHD studies) suggests DYRK4 expression is controlled by methylation status affecting disease susceptibility. The RAD51AP1-DYRK4 fusion (2020 study) creates oncogenic driver sensitizing tumors to MEK inhibition, indicating cross-talk with MAPK signaling. The 58 ClinVar chromosome 12p CNVs reflect dosage sensitivity affecting this genomic region. DYRK4 represents an uncharacterized DYRK kinase linking antiviral immunity, cancer susceptibility, cardiovascular disease, and kinase complex assembly through DCAF7-mediated regulation and IRF3 phosphorylation mechanisms.
