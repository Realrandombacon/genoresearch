## Description
LINGO1 encodes a 620 aa leucine-rich repeat and immunoglobulin-like domain-containing protein 1 (UniProt Q96FE5), also known as LERN1 or nogo receptor-interacting protein 1, functioning as a transmembrane regulator of CNS myelination, axon regeneration, and neuronal development through modulation of Nogo receptor signaling complexes. The protein contains an N-terminal leucine-rich repeat (LRR) domain architecture (IPR000372/IPR001611/IPR003591, aa41-421) forming a curved solenoid structure for protein-protein interactions, followed by a C-terminal immunoglobulin I-set domain (IPR003598/IPR007110/IPR013098/cd20969, aa422-516) mediating receptor complex assembly, belonging to the immunoglobulin-like fold superfamily (IPR013783/G3DSA:2.60.40.10). AlphaFold prediction shows high confidence (pLDDT 86.1), indicating a well-folded extracellular domain structure typical of LRR-Ig membrane receptors. HPA shows brain-enriched expression (109.9 nTPM) with tissue-enriched specificity, plasma membrane localization, cancer-enhanced pattern, and disease involvement in intellectual disability. STRING interactions reveal LINGO1 associates with myelination and axon guidance regulators: TNFRSF19 (0.996, TROY receptor), MAG (0.949, myelin-associated glycoprotein), RHOA (0.906, small GTPase), OMG (0.735, oligodendrocyte myelin glycoprotein), MYOC (0.716, myocilin), RTN4/Nogo (0.639, neurite outgrowth inhibitor), ARHGDIA (0.635, Rho GDP dissociation inhibitor), NGFR (0.625, p75 neurotrophin receptor), and RTN4R (0.510, Nogo receptor). ClinVar contains 18 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including 15q24.1-26.3/15q22.33-26.1 copy number gains, 15q24.1-25.2 copy number losses, and missense mutations in the LRR domain (p.Arg290Cys, p.Tyr288Cys, p.Arg290His). Literature (159 papers) demonstrates LINGO1 inhibits oligodendrocyte differentiation and CNS myelination through Nogo-66/NgR1/p75/LINGO1 receptor complex formation; LINGO1 antagonists (anti-LINGO1 antibodies, LINGO1-Fc) promote remyelination in multiple sclerosis and spinal cord injury models; LINGO1 regulates BK channel surface expression and biophysical properties affecting neuronal excitability; LINGO1 variants associate with essential tremor, Parkinson's disease susceptibility, and infantile hypotonia with epilepsy; LINGO1 contributes to cognitive dysfunction after anesthesia in aged mice; LINGO1 modulates RhoA-ROCK pathway activation inhibiting axon regeneration; and LINGO1 serves as potential drug target in Alzheimer's disease microglia. LINGO1 functions as a co-receptor in the Nogo signaling complex—its LRR domain binds Nogo-66, MAG, and OMG while the Ig domain recruits downstream signaling components, activating RhoA GTPase through ARHGDIA displacement. RhoA activation triggers ROCK kinase, causing growth cone collapse, cytoskeletal destabilization, and transcriptional repression of myelination genes. In adult CNS, LINGO1 maintains myelin stability but prevents regeneration after injury. Intellectual disability pathogenesis reflects disrupted neuronal development: LINGO1 mutations alter receptor complex assembly or RhoA signaling balance, affecting dendritic arborization, synapse formation, and cortical circuit maturation. BK channel modulation represents a novel ion channel regulatory function distinct from myelination roles, potentially explaining neurological phenotypes beyond axon guidance. Cancer-enhanced expression suggests oncogenic potential through EGFR binding and proliferation signaling.
