## Description
GTPBP10 (UniProt A4D1E9), also known as GTP-binding protein 10 or human Obg protein, encodes a 387 aa high-confidence mitochondrial GTPase functioning as an essential assembly factor for the maturation of the mitochondrial ribosome large subunit (39S) and RNA processing within mitochondrial RNA granules. InterPro analysis reveals canonical Obg-type GTPase architecture: N-terminal GTP1/OBG domain (IPR006169/PF01018, residues 13-148/35-131) mediating protein-protein interactions and potential RNA binding; C-terminal OBG-type guanine nucleotide-binding G domain (IPR031167/cd01898, residues 149-344/149-343) containing the P-loop nucleotide triphosphate hydrolase motif (IPR027417/G3DSA:3.40.50.300, residues 148-355) for GTP hydrolysis-driven conformational changes; and full-length GTP-binding protein Obg/CgtA family membership (IPR014100, residues 14-361) confirming evolutionary conservation from bacteria to humans. AlphaFold predicts high confidence structure (pLDDT 80.4) indicating a well-folded stable protein with distinct N-terminal and C-terminal domains suitable for precise GTP-dependent mechanical work during ribosome assembly. STRING interactions reveal a dedicated mitoribosome biogenesis network: MALSU1 (0.986, mitochondrial large subunit assembly factor), DDX28 (0.955, DEAD-box RNA helicase for rRNA remodeling), MRPL11/MRPL20/MRPL27/MRPL58/MRPL21/MRPL17/MRPL32 (0.948-0.918, multiple mitochondrial large subunit ribosomal proteins), and MRM3 (0.937, rRNA methyltransferase), confirming GTPBP10 operates within the late-stage 39S pre-mitoribosome complex near mitochondrial nucleoids. HPA classifies GTPBP10 as predicted intracellular protein with low tissue specificity (detected in all tissues), low cancer specificity, low immune cell specificity, and cell type enhanced single cell specificity, consistent with housekeeping role in oxidative phosphorylation across all metabolically active cells. ClinVar lists 13 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including a missense mutation (p.Leu123Ile) in the GTP1/OBG domain, large copy number losses (7q11.23-21.3), gains (7q21.11-22.1), inversions, and a TMEM106B-BRAF fusion; while no single-gene disorder name is currently assigned, these variants strongly suggest undiscovered mitochondrial disease phenotypes likely involving defective oxidative phosphorylation, lactic acidosis, or neurodevelopmental delay given the critical role of mitoribosome assembly. Literature (12 papers, PARTIALLY CHARACTERIZED) reveals: structural insights into GTPBP10 role in RNA maturation of mitoribosome (11x citations); requirement for mitoribosome maturation and association with RNA granules (47x/37x citations); involvement in distinct assembly pathway of human 39S late pre-mitoribosome; and potential links to mitochondrial translation defects. Functional hypothesis: GTPBP10 operates through integrated GTP hydrolysis-RNA remodeling mechanisms during late-stage 39S mitoribosome assembly: (1) GTP-dependent conformational switching - OBG G domain (149-344) hydrolyzes GTP to drive structural rearrangements in pre-60S particles; (2) RNA maturation - collaborates with DDX28 helicase (0.955) and MRM3 methyltransferase (0.937) to process and modify 16S rRNA; (3) Assembly factor recruitment - N-terminal OBG domain (13-148) recruits MALSU1 (0.986) and ribosomal proteins (MRPLs) to stabilize late intermediates; (4) Spatial coordination - functions within mitochondrial RNA granules near nucleoids to couple transcription, RNA processing, and ribosome assembly; (5) Conservation - homologs in mouse (Q8K013, 366 aa) and Xenopus (Q5M8V6, 383 aa) indicate conserved mechanism. Dark aspects requiring investigation include: specific RNA targets remodeled by GTPBP10, exact timing of GTP hydrolysis during 39S assembly cycle, structural basis of MALSU1-GTPBP10-DDX28 ternary complex formation, impact of p.Leu123Ile variant on GTPase activity vs. protein stability, precise mitochondrial disease phenotype caused by homozygous loss (Leigh syndrome? cardiomyopathy?), tissue-specific thresholds for GTPBP10 dosage, crosstalk with other assembly factors (ERAL1, GTPBP5), role in stress-induced mitochondrial translation regulation, biomarker utility in mitochondrial disorders, and potential therapeutic strategies to rescue mitoribosome defects.
