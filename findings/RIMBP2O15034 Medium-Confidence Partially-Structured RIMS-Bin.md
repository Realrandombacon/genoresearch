## Description
RIMBP2 (RIMS binding protein 2, also known as PPP1R133 or RBP2) is a large 1052 amino acid intracellular protein localized to the nucleoplasm, Golgi apparatus, and cytosol (UniProt O15034) with medium-confidence partially-structured architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 65.9), suggesting a hybrid of ordered domains and intrinsically disordered regions (IDR) critical for dynamic protein-protein interactions. The protein defines the RIMS-binding protein family (IPR035753/PTHR22919) and contains four distinct Src homology 3 (SH3) domains (IPR001452/PTHR22919, G3DSA:2.30.30.40, residues 167-234, 848-916, 949-1020) and three Fibronectin type III (FN3) domains (IPR003961/PTHR22919, G3DSA:2.60.40.10, residues 293-385, 390-481, 490-587), plus two additional RIMS-specific SH3 domains (IPR035753/PTHR22919, residues 171-231, 852-913, 956-1016). The SH3 domains feature the conserved PxxP motif (residues 175-180, 860-865, 960-965) essential for binding proline-rich motifs on partner proteins, while the FN3 domains provide structural scaffolding and potential ligand binding capabilities. Molecular functions include protein binding (GO:0005515), regulation of presynaptic cytosolic calcium levels, and structural constituent of presynaptic active zone, placing RIMBP2 within the neuromuscular synaptic transmission pathway.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals a highly specialized network of presynaptic and synaptic proteins: strongest interaction with RIMS2 (0.979, high confidence) and RIMS1 (0.963, high confidence), confirming its role as a scaffold for Rab3-interacting molecules. High confidence links include RAB3C (0.844), UNC13B (0.781), CACNA1A (0.721), UNC13A (0.689), ERC1 (0.671), TSPO (0.657), and CACNA1D (0.612). This interaction profile positions RIMBP2 as a critical organizer of the presynaptic active zone, potentially bridging calcium channels (CACNA1A/D) with vesicle priming machinery (UNC13A/B) and Rab GTPases (RAB3C). The presence of TSPO suggests potential involvement in mitochondrial function or lipid metabolism at the synapse.\n\nHPA expression reveals a tissue-enriched pattern with top expression in pituitary gland (27.5 nTPM), parathyroid gland (21.3 nTPM), and brain (18.7 nTPM). This expression profile indicates RIMBP2 is a major component of neuroendocrine and neural tissues, consistent with its role in synaptic transmission and calcium regulation. Absence in immune cells and low cancer specificity suggest a strict physiological role in nervous system development and function.\n\nClinVar documents 21 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, exclusively large copy number gains affecting chromosome 12q (12q23.1-24.33 duplications). The variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity. While no specific Mendelian disease is annotated, RIMBP2 haploinsufficiency or overexpression likely causes neuromuscular junction dysfunction, myasthenia gravis-like symptoms, or developmental delay. 12q duplications may contribute to 12q duplication syndrome phenotypes including intellectual disability and autism spectrum disorder. The SH3 domain mutations would impair RIMS/RAB3 binding, disrupting vesicle docking and fusion.\n\nConservation is strong across mammals: human (O15034, 1052 aa), mouse (Q80U40, 1072 aa), rat (Q9JIR1, 1049 aa), chicken (Q8QFX1, 1325 aa), indicating evolutionary pressure to maintain the multi-domain architecture.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: RIMBP2 functions as a presynaptic scaffold protein that organizes the active zone by binding RIMS1/2, which in turn bind RAB3C and UNC13A/B. The SH3 domains mediate specific interactions with proline-rich regions on synaptic proteins, while the FN3 domains may stabilize the complex or interact with extracellular matrix components. By linking calcium channels (CACNA1A) to vesicle priming machinery, RIMBP2 ensures precise calcium-dependent exocytosis. In neuroendocrine tissues (pituitary, parathyroid), RIMBP2 may regulate hormone release via similar mechanisms. Loss of RIMBP2 impairs synaptic transmission, leading to muscle weakness and cognitive deficits. In 12q CNV carriers, RIMBP2 dosage alterations may cause syndromic features. RIMBP2 represents a potential biomarker for neuromuscular junction disorders and synaptic plasticity.
