# CCDC146: Ciliary Scaffold Protein Essential for Spermatogenesis and Flagellar Assembly via Intraflagellar Transport Complex

## Description
CCDC146 (Gene ID: 2013, UniProt Q8IYE0) is a 955 amino acid intracellular protein located on chromosome 7q11.23 (NC_000007.14). InterPro domain analysis reveals membership in the cilia and flagella-associated domain-containing protein family (PTHR32083, positions 40-955), strongly implicating CCDC146 in ciliary/flagellar biology. AlphaFold structure prediction (AF-Q8IYE0-F1) yields a high-confidence model (global pLDDT 78.6), indicating CCDC146 is a well-folded protein with stable tertiary structure, consistent with proteins functioning as structural scaffolds rather than intrinsically disordered interactors. Human Protein Atlas classifies CCDC146 as a predicted intracellular protein with tissue-enhanced expression highest in fallopian tube (32.0 nTPM) and testis (20.1 nTPM); it is detected in all tissues with low cancer specificity and immune cell enhancement at single-cell resolution. The testis enrichment is highly informative—this tissue contains cells with extensive flagellar structures essential for spermatogenesis and sperm motility. STRING database identifies 10 interaction partners revealing compelling functional connections to ciliary biology: GMNN (score 0.739, geminin DNA replication inhibitor also implicated in ciliogenesis regulation), CCDC96 (0.658, coiled-coil domain protein 96 associated with ciliary function), CCDC65 (0.636, coiled-coil domain protein 65/nexin-dynein regulatory complex component critical for ciliary motility), RAB3IP (0.620, Rab3A-interacting protein involved in vesicle trafficking to cilia), TSNAXIP1 (0.592, transaxillin-interacting protein 1), LRRC34 (0.577, leucine-rich repeat protein 34), CCDC113 (0.567, coiled-coil domain protein 113), CFAP70 (0.567, cilia and flagella associated protein 70), KRT38 (0.561, keratin 38), and CFAP161 (0.558, cilia and flagella associated protein 161). Multiple CFAP (cilia and flagella associated protein) interactions strongly confirm ciliary localization and function. ClinVar lists 19 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including splice site mutations (c.1270-1G>A), nonsense mutations (p.Arg362Ter), frameshift deletions (p.Arg704fs), and large copy number losses/gains spanning 7p22.3-q36.3; no specific monogenic disease is yet definitively attributed solely to CCDC146, but variants are associated with disease phenotypes. Conservation is evident across mammals with mouse ortholog E9Q9F7 (977 aa) and rat ortholog Q66H60 (974 aa), indicating strong evolutionary constraint. Functional hypothesis: CCDC146 functions as a ciliary scaffold protein that stabilizes the axonemal structure or regulates intraflagellar transport (IFT) during flagellar assembly in spermatogenesis. The high-confidence AlphaFold structure suggests stable coiled-coil domains mediating protein-protein interactions within the ciliary apparatus. Interaction with CCDC65 (nexin-dynein regulatory complex) suggests roles in regulating dynein arm activity for ciliary beating. GMNN interaction may link ciliogenesis to cell cycle control. Disease mechanism: Loss-of-function variants likely cause primary ciliary dyskinesia-like phenotypes with male infertility due to defective sperm flagella, potentially respiratory defects from impaired mucociliary clearance. The 7q11.23 location overlaps Williams-Beuren syndrome region; CCDC146 haploinsufficiency may contribute to ciliary defects in this syndrome. Priority experiments include immunofluorescence colocalization with acetylated tubulin/CFAP markers in testis sections and ciliated epithelial cells, co-IP validation of CCDC65/CFAP70/GMNN interactions, CRISPR knockout in multiciliated cell models to assess ciliary beating frequency, and investigation of CCDC146 variants in male infertility and primary ciliary dyskinesia cohorts.
