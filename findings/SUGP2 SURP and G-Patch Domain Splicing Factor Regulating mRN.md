## Description
SUGP2 (SURP and G-patch Domain Containing 2, also SFRS14, SRFS14) is a 1082 amino acid nuclear protein (UniProt Q8IX01) with a partially structured conformation (AlphaFold pLDDT 52.9, medium confidence), characteristic of splicing factors containing both ordered RNA-binding domains and intrinsically disordered regions for flexible protein-RNA interactions.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals SUGP2 contains two SURP (SWAP/Surp) domains (IPR000061/PF01805, residues 587-639 and 785-839; G3DSA:1.10.10.790) conferring RNA binding (GO:0003723) and RNA processing (GO:0006396) functions, plus a C-terminal G-patch domain (IPR000467/PF01585/SM00443, residues 1009-1057) for nucleic acid binding. The protein belongs to the arginine/serine-rich splicing factor SF4/14 family (PTHR23340) and the SUGP1/2 family (IPR040169).\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals SUGP2 functions within the spliceosomal machinery: strongest interactions with MATR3-2 (0.777, matrin-3 isoform, nuclear matrix RNA-binding protein), HNRNPA1 (0.663, heterogeneous nuclear ribonucleoprotein A1), SFSWAP (0.560, SWAP splicing factor), SCAF11 (0.528, SR-related CTD-associated factor 11), ZC3H18 (0.498, zinc finger protein), TRIM28 (0.473, transcriptional regulator), RBM39 (0.445, RNA-binding motif protein 39), HNRNPM (0.435), and MATR3 (0.410). This interaction pattern positions SUGP2 within the spliceosome and nuclear matrix.\n\nHPA expression shows ubiquitous detection across all tissues with low tissue specificity and nucleoplasmic/nuclear bodies subcellular localization, consistent with housekeeping mRNA splicing functions. The protein is classified as intracellular with mRNA processing and splicing molecular functions.\n\nClinVar documents 10 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large chromosome 19p copy number variations (gains spanning 19p13.12-q13.43 regions). While specific SUGP2 point mutations are not yet catalogued, these CNVs suggest dosage sensitivity potentially causing developmental disorders through impaired splicing regulation.\n\nConservation is evident from mouse ortholog (Q8CH09, 1067 aa) and chicken ortholog (A0A8V1ADT8, 1058 aa), indicating functional importance across vertebrates.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: SUGP2 acts as a multi-domain splicing regulator that binds pre-mRNA through its SURP domains while recruiting spliceosomal components via its G-patch domain and disordered regions. The protein likely functions in alternative splicing regulation by interacting with matrin-3 at the nuclear matrix, where it coordinates splice site selection for specific transcript subsets. Nuclear body localization suggests roles in splicing factor storage or assembly. Ubiquitous expression indicates housekeeping splicing functions essential for cellular viability. CNV-mediated dosage imbalance disrupts spliceosomal stoichiometry, causing widespread splicing defects that manifest as developmental disorders through aberrant isoform production.
