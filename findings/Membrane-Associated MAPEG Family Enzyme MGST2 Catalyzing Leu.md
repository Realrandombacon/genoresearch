## Description
MGST2 encodes microsomal glutathione S-transferase 2 (MGST-II/GST2), a 147 aa membrane-associated enzyme (UniProt Q99735) belonging to the MAPEG (Membrane Associated Proteins in Eicosanoid and Glutathione metabolism) family that catalyzes leukotriene C4 synthesis and glutathione conjugation reactions. InterPro reveals characteristic MAPEG architecture: membrane-associated eicosanoid/glutathione metabolism domain (IPR001129/PF01124/G3DSA:1.20.120.550/IPR023352, residues 1-146) forming the trimeric membrane protein structure, 5-lipoxygenase-activating protein family motifs (IPR001446/PR00488, residues 2-23/44-63/64-83) mediating substrate binding, FLAP/GST2/LTC4S conserved signature (IPR018295/PS01297, residues 42-56) essential for catalytic activity, and comprehensive MAPEG family membership (IPR050997/PTHR10250, residues 5-136). AlphaFold predicts very high confidence structure (pLDDT 94.6) consistent with well-folded homotrimeric membrane enzyme typical of MAPEG superfamily with four transmembrane helices per monomer forming a central catalytic chamber. STRING reveals functional clustering with detoxification and lipid metabolism enzymes: EPHX1 (0.936, epoxide hydrolase-1 xenobiotic metabolism), GSS (0.917, glutathione synthetase producing GSH substrate), MGST1 (0.915, microsomal GST-1 paralog), CYP3A4 (0.913), CYP1A2 (0.912), CYP1A1 (0.912), CYP1B1 (0.706, cytochrome P450s generating reactive metabolites requiring conjugation), GPX4 (0.912, glutathione peroxidase-4 lipid peroxide detoxification), PRDX6 (0.911, peroxiredoxin-6 antioxidant defense), GSR (0.450, glutathione reductase regenerating reduced GSH). HPA shows tissue-enhanced expression with liver predominance (366.8 nTPM), detected in all tissues, immune cell-enhanced pattern, endoplasmic reticulum and cytosolic localization, classified as enzyme/metabolic protein/predicted membrane protein involved in leukotriene biosynthesis and lipid metabolism with lyase/oxidoreductase/transferase molecular function. ClinVar lists 35 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants consisting of extensive copy number gains (4q28.3-35.2, 4q21.21-31.21, 4q27-35.2, 4q24-35.2, 4p12-q35.2) and losses (4q28.3-31.21, 4q28.1-31.21) plus splice site variant c.230-11C>T though specific monogenic disease phenotypes remain undefined suggesting dosage sensitivity affecting inflammatory response and detoxification capacity. Functional mechanism: MGST2 operates through dual catalytic activities within the ER membrane: (1) leukotriene C4 synthase activity conjugating leukotriene A4 (epoxyeicosatrienoic acid) with reduced glutathione to produce LTC4, the precursor for inflammatory mediators LTD4 and LTE4 driving bronchoconstriction and vascular permeability in asthma/allergic responses, and (2) glutathione S-transferase activity conjugating electrophilic xenobiotics, carcinogens, and oxidative stress products with GSH for detoxification and export via multidrug resistance proteins. The enzyme functions as homotrimer with each 147 aa monomer contributing four transmembrane helices, creating three catalytic sites at subunit interfaces where GSH binds in extended conformation and electrophilic substrates access the active site from the membrane bilayer. Recent research suggests expanded disease relevance: asthma severity modulation through LTC4 production levels, cancer chemoresistance via enhanced xenobiotic detoxification, neurodegenerative disease protection through oxidative stress mitigation, and potential metabolic syndrome associations given liver-enriched expression. Despite mechanistic understanding of LTC4 synthesis and GSH conjugation, complete substrate specificity profile beyond canonical substrates, regulatory mechanisms controlling trimer assembly and catalytic activation, precise contribution of 4q CNVs to inflammatory/metabolic phenotypes, genotype-phenotype correlations for splice variants affecting membrane insertion, cell-type specific expression patterns across hepatocyte subpopulations and immune cells, isoform-specific functions from alternative splicing, and therapeutic potential for MGST2 inhibition in inflammatory diseases versus activation for chemoprotection remain incompletely characterized - representing genuine dark aspects of this critical membrane enzyme bridging inflammation and detoxification pathways.
