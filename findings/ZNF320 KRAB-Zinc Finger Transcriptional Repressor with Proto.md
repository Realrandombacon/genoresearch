# ZNF320: KRAB-Zinc Finger Transcriptional Repressor with Proto-Oncogenic Activity and Dosage Sensitivity

**Date:** 2026-03-20T09:34:39.990849

## Description
ZNF320 encodes a 509 amino acid C2H2 zinc finger protein (UniProt A2RRD8) containing an N-terminal KRAB repression domain (PF01352, IPR001909, residues 8-80) that recruits the corepressor TRIM28/KAP1 complex to silence target genes through heterochromatin formation. The protein possesses multiple C2H2-type zinc finger domains (PF00096, IPR013087) at positions 161-244 and additional zinc finger regions extending to residue 384, enabling sequence-specific DNA binding. AlphaFold structure prediction (pLDDT 68.8) indicates medium confidence with structured zinc finger regions and potentially flexible linker segments characteristic of Krüppel-associated box zinc finger proteins. HPA data reveals nucleoplasmic and cytoplasmic body localization with ubiquitous expression across all tissues (low tissue specificity), consistent with broad transcriptional regulatory function. The protein is classified as a transcription factor with proto-oncogenic disease involvement. STRING interactions show high-confidence association with DMBT1 (0.825), a tumor suppressor frequently deleted in cancers, and ZNF468 (0.772), plus medium-confidence interaction with TRIM28 (0.522), the canonical KRAB coreceptor, supporting the transcriptional repression mechanism. Additional interactions with ZNF paralogs (ZNF347, ZNF600, ZNF813, ZNF506) suggest functional redundancy within the ZNF gene cluster on chromosome 19q13.41. ClinVar documents 16 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number gains on 19q and a missense variant c.257G>C (p.Cys86Ser) affecting the zinc finger region, indicating dosage sensitivity where altered ZNF320 copy number disrupts transcriptional networks and may contribute to oncogenesis. The KRAB-TRIM28 interaction proposes a mechanistic role in epigenetic silencing through H3K9me3 deposition, while zinc finger-mediated DNA recognition provides target gene specificity.
