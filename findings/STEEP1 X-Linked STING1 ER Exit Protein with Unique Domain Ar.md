# STEEP1: X-Linked STING1 ER Exit Protein with Unique Domain Architecture Linking Viral Trafficking, Centrosomal Function, and Intellectual Disability

## Description
STEEP1 (STING1 ER exit protein 1, alias C10orf56) is a 222-amino acid X-linked protein (Xq23-28) that functions as an essential host dependency factor for Epstein-Barr virus (EBV) latent membrane protein 1 (LMP1) trafficking and signaling, enabling B-cell transformation into lymphoblastoid cell lines. InterPro analysis reveals STEEP1 belongs to the STEEP-like family (IPR029704, residues 1-216) with a unique STEEP1 domain (IPR057965/PF25809, residues 22-122) of unknown molecular function, classified under the UPF0428 protein family (PTHR46355). AlphaFold predicts a well-folded structure with high confidence (pLDDT 76.1), indicating a stable globular core despite the absence of homology to characterized protein folds.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis returned no partners, consistent with STEEP1's dark gene status and suggesting it may operate as a solitary factor or in transient complexes not captured by affinity purification methods. Human Protein Atlas classifies STEEP1 as detected in all tissues with low tissue specificity, localizing to the nucleoplasm and centrosome—two functionally distinct compartments suggesting dual roles in transcriptional regulation and cell division. Critically, HPA notes disease involvement in intellectual disability, aligning with its X-chromosomal location. ClinVar reports 181 pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number losses and gains spanning Xq21-q28 (encompassing STEEP1), plus specific missense mutations including p.Arg55Gln, p.Cys96Tyr, and p.Arg198Gln within or near the conserved STEEP1 domain.\n\nLiterature analysis (5 papers) reveals a groundbreaking 2026 CRISPR-Cas9 screen identifying STEEP1 as essential for EBV LMP1 plasma membrane trafficking and oncogenic signaling. LMP1 is the primary EBV oncoprotein that mimics constitutively active CD40, driving NF-κB, MAPK, and JAK/STAT pathways in infected B-cells. We hypothesize that STEEP1 functions as a specialized ER export adaptor that recognizes LMP1's transmembrane topology and facilitates its packaging into COPII vesicles for Golgi-to-plasma membrane transport. The centrosomal localization suggests a secondary role in mitotic spindle organization or ciliogenesis, potentially explaining the intellectual disability phenotype when disrupted—centrosomal defects cause microcephaly and neurodevelopmental disorders. The X-linked inheritance pattern and high variant burden suggest STEEP1 is dosage-sensitive, where haploinsufficiency (in females) or hemizygous loss (in males) causes neurodevelopmental phenotypes. The conserved STEEP1 domain (residues 22-122) likely mediates protein-protein interactions with ER exit machinery (e.g., SEC23/24, SAR1) or LMP1's cytoplasmic tail. STEEP1 represents a novel class of virus-host interface proteins where cellular ER trafficking factors are hijacked by viral oncoproteins, making it a potential antiviral target.
