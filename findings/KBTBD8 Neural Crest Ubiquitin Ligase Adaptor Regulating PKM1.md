## Description
KBTBD8 (Kelch repeat and BTB domain containing 8) is a 601-amino acid protein functioning as a substrate-specific adaptor for the CUL3-RING ubiquitin ligase (CRL3) complex. Structural analysis reveals a canonical architecture: an N-terminal BTB/POZ domain (IPR000210, residues 27-155) mediating CUL3 binding, followed by a BACK domain (IPR011705), and a C-terminal Kelch-type beta-propeller (IPR015915, residues 274-599) responsible for substrate recognition. The AlphaFold model (AF-Q8NFY9-F1) predicts a highly stable structure with a global pLDDT of 87.8, confirming the integrity of these domains.\n\nFunctionally, KBTBD8 is critical for neural crest cell development and oocyte quality. Mechanistically, it targets specific substrates for monoubiquitination; notably, it regulates Pyruvate Kinase M1 (PKM1) levels via the Erk1/2 and Aurora A signaling axis, thereby controlling energy metabolism in oocytes. Dysregulation leads to reduced oocyte quality and embryonic defects. In somatic cells, KBTBD8 localizes to the Golgi apparatus during interphase but translocates to the spindle apparatus during mitosis, suggesting a role in cell division fidelity.\n\nExpression data (HPA) shows KBTBD8 is ubiquitous but enriched in lymphoid tissue (19.4 nTPM) and bone marrow (13.1 nTPM). ClinVar records 9 pathogenic copy number variants (losses and gains) spanning the 3p14.1 locus, implicating dosage sensitivity in developmental disorders, though specific syndromic associations remain under-characterized. STRING interactions confirm a tight functional cluster with CUL3 (score 0.982), RBX1, and other Kelch-family adaptors (KLHL9, KLHL12, KBTBD7), reinforcing its role within the cullin-RING ligase network. With only 18 papers, primarily focused on cancer (ovarian, hepatocellular) and fertility, KBTBD8 represents a 'partially characterized' dark gene where the full spectrum of substrates and developmental pathways remains to be elucidated.
