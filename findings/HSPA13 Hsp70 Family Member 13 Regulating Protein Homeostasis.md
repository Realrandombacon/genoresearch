Functional hypothesis: HSPA13 functions through: (1) ATPase-driven Chaperone Cycle—binding to exposed hydrophobic regions of client proteins (e.g., ubiquilins, transcription factors), preventing aggregation, and facilitating folding or degradation; (2) Stress Response—upregulation during cellular stress to maintain proteostasis; (3) Chromatin Interaction—modulation of chromatin structure via interaction with transcriptional machinery; (4) Cancer Immune Evasion—facilitation of tumor cell survival and immune evasion mechanisms. Mechanistic proposal: In cancer, HSPA13 overexpression or dysregulation promotes protein stability of oncogenic clients and facilitates immune evasion via YTHDF3-mediated phase separation. In developmental disorders, dosage sensitivity (CNVs) leads to proteostasis collapse and cellular dysfunction. The combination of a well-folded Hsp70 NBD, high STRING confidence in ubiquilin partners, ubiquitous expression, extensive pathogenic variant burden (27 CNVs), and emerging literature (confirming genuine dark gene status despite functional importance) positions HSPA13 as a high-priority target for understanding proteostasis regulation and cancer immunology.
