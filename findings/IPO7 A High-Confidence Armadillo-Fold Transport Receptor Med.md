## Description
IPO7 (Importin-7, UniProt O95373) is a 1038-amino acid nuclear transport receptor belonging to the importin-beta superfamily. It functions as a RanGTP-dependent carrier that mediates the nuclear import of a broad spectrum of cargos, including histones (H1, H2A, H2B), ribosomal proteins, and viral components. InterPro analysis reveals a characteristic architecture composed of an N-terminal Importin-beta domain (IPR001494, aa 22-101) followed by an extensive array of Armadillo-like helical repeats (IPR011989, IPR016024) spanning nearly the entire protein length (aa 3-996), forming a flexible superhelical structure ideal for wrapping around diverse cargo proteins. Additional TPR-like repeats (IPR058669, aa 616-887) further stabilize cargo interactions. The AlphaFold predicted structure exhibits high confidence (pLDDT 87.9), confirming a stable, well-folded solenoid architecture typical of karyopherins. STRING interaction data highlights strong associations with the small GTPase RAN (score 0.910), which regulates the transport cycle, and other transport receptors like TNPO1 (0.868) and XPO1 (0.828). Notably, IPO7 interacts with FAU (0.931), a ubiquitin-like protein, suggesting a role in coordinating protein quality control or degradation during transit. HPA expression profiles indicate ubiquitous presence in the nucleoplasm and cytosol across all tissues, consistent with its essential housekeeping role in nucleocytoplasmic trafficking. ClinVar records list 17 pathogenic variants, primarily splice-site mutations (e.g., c.2490-2A>T) and large copy number gains at 11p15, although specific monogenic disease phenotypes remain to be fully elucidated. We hypothesize that IPO7 acts as a versatile 'generalist' transporter that utilizes its extended armadillo repeat surface to recognize non-classical nuclear localization signals on structurally diverse cargos, potentially serving as a critical bottleneck for cell cycle progression (via histone import) and host defense against viral invasion.
