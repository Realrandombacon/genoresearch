# CTDNEP1/DULLARD: HAD-Fold Phosphatase Regulating ER Membrane Morphology, Lipid Metabolism, and MYC-Driven Oncogenesis

**Date:** 2026-06-26T15:21:36.554010

**Quality Score:** 7/10 (GOOD)

**Confidence:** MEDIUM-HIGH

## Description
CTDNEP1 (244 aa, UniProt O95476), also known as DULLARD, is a noncanonical membrane-associated protein phosphatase that forms a heterodimeric complex with its regulatory subunit CNEP1R1/NEP1R1 to maintain endoplasmic reticulum membrane morphology and regulate lipid droplet dynamics. The enzyme belongs to the haloacid dehalogenase (HAD) superfamily (IPR023214, IPR036412) with a conserved FCP1 homology domain (IPR004274/PF03031, aa57-231) containing the catalytic phosphatase active site. Recent 2024 structural work elucidated the CTDNEP1-CNEP1R1 complex mechanism for ER membrane homeostasis. Functional studies reveal three critical biological roles: (1) negative regulation of RANKL-induced osteoclast differentiation, implicating CTDNEP1 in bone resorption disorders; (2) suppression of MYC amplification and genomic instability in Group 3 medulloblastoma, where loss-of-function mutations drive aggressive tumor phenotypes; (3) prognostic biomarker in pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma where low expression correlates with poor survival and immune modulation. STRING interactions confirm functional coupling with lipin phosphatidate phosphatases LPIN1 (0.702), LPIN2 (0.604), LPIN3 (0.886) for triglyceride biosynthesis regulation, plus BMP/BMPR2 signaling components. HPA shows tissue-enhanced expression peaking in skeletal muscle (395.2 nTPM) with lipid droplet localization. AlphaFold prediction (pLDDT 90.5) reveals a very high-confidence HAD-fold catalytic core with characteristic α/β topology. Clinical significance is underscored by 30 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants in ClinVar, predominantly large CNVs at 17p13.1, suggesting haploinsufficiency mechanisms. Mechanistically, CTDNEP1 dephosphorylates membrane-associated substrates to control lipid packing and ER curvature; dysregulation causes membrane stress, altered lipin activity, and in cancer contexts, MYC-driven proliferation through unchecked phosphorylation cascades.
