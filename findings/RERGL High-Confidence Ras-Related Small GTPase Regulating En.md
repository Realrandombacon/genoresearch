## Description
RERGL (RERG Like, alias RASL11B) is a 205 amino acid intracellular enzyme (UniProt Q9H628) with a high-confidence folded structure (AlphaFold pLDDT 76.9) containing a canonical Ras-family small GTPase domain (residues 19-173) and P-loop nucleotide triphosphate hydrolase motif, functioning as a molecular switch that regulates endocytic trafficking, vesicle transport, and vascular signaling through interactions with EH-domain scaffolding proteins and lipid kinases.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals RERGL contains a classic small GTPase architecture: a Ras family domain (IPR001806/PF00071/SM00173/PS51421, residues 19-173/1-205) characterized by the conserved G-box motifs (G1-G5) for GTP binding and hydrolysis, and a Rab subfamily profile (SM00175/PS51419, residues 20-175) suggesting potential functional overlap with Rab GTPases in membrane trafficking. The protein belongs to the Ras-related small GTPase family (IPR051065/PTHR45704, residues 17-203) and the RERG/RasL11-like superfamily (cd04146, residues 6-174). The CATH-Gene3D classification confirms a P-loop containing nucleotide triphosphate hydrolase fold (IPR027417/G3DSA:3.40.50.300/SSF52540, residues 13-189). GO annotations include GTPase activity (GO:0003924) and GTP binding (GO:0005525). Nuclear localization hints at non-canonical roles in gene regulation. 12p CNVs cause RERGL dosage imbalance, leading to defective receptor internalization, hyperactive growth signaling, and vascular malformations. RERGL represents a specialized Ras-family GTPase linking endocytic trafficking to vascular physiology.
