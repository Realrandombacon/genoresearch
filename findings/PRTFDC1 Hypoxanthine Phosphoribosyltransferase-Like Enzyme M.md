# PRTFDC1: Hypoxanthine Phosphoribosyltransferase-Like Enzyme Modulating Purine Salvage, PTSD Susceptibility, and Neuropathic Pain

## Description
PRTFDC1 (Q9NRG1) is a 225-aa phosphoribosyltransferase domain-containing protein with very high-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 90.8), indicating a stable well-folded enzyme with canonical PRTase fold. InterPro analysis reveals phosphoribosyltransferase domain (IPR000836/PF00156: positions 47-202), hypoxanthine phosphoribosyl transferase family signatures (IPR005904: positions 42-215, IPR050408: positions 10-224), and PRTase-like superfamily fold (IPR029057/SSF53271: positions 1-225/15-221), characteristic of purine salvage enzymes. HPA expression shows low tissue specificity with immune cell enrichment, localized to nucleoplasm, Golgi apparatus, and cytosol. STRING interactions demonstrate high-confidence binding to purine metabolism enzymes HPRT1 (0.794), ADSL (0.870), MTAP (0.731), PNP (0.730), plus ATP synthase subunits ATP5F1C (0.837), ATP5F1A (0.825), MT-ATP6 (0.816), and epigenetic regulator EEF2KMT (0.792). Literature (28 papers) identifies PRTFDC1 as genetic modifier of HPRT-deficiency (Lesch-Nyhan disease) in mouse models, genome-wide significant locus for combat stress vulnerability/PTSD in U.S. Marines (rs12411980), and regulator of myogenic differentiation in satellite cells. Recent 2024-2025 studies link PRTFDC1 expression to phantom tooth pain (neuropathic pain) and lung adenocarcinoma prognosis. ClinVar contains 13 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including splice site mutations (c.339+1G>A, c.156-139C>A, c.424-21T>G) and copy number gains/losses at 10p15.3-q26.3, though specific monogenic disease associations remain undefined. The very high-confidence structure (pLDDT 90.8) and HPRT1 interaction suggest PRTFDC1 functions as phosphoribosyltransferase in purine salvage pathway, potentially converting hypoxanthine/guanine to IMP/GMP using PRPP, while ADSL/MTAP/PNP interactions position it within broader nucleotide metabolism network affecting neurotransmitter synthesis and stress response pathways implicated in PTSD and neuropathic pain.
