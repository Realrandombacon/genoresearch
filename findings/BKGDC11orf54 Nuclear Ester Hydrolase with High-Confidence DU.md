## Description
BKGD (alias C11orf54/MEEP/PTD012, beta-keto-L-gulonate decarboxylase) encodes a 315 amino acid exceptionally well-folded protein (AlphaFold pLDDT 97.6) containing an ester hydrolase DUF1907 domain (IPR015021/PF08925, aa 20-303) with GO annotations for hydrolase activity acting on ester bonds (GO:0016788) and zinc ion binding (GO:0008270). HPA shows kidney-enriched expression (530.1 nTPM) with liver secondary expression (241.6 nTPM), nucleoplasm and nuclear body localization, and cancer-enhanced expression pattern. ClinVar contains 4 pathogenic variants consisting of 11q13.5-22.3 copy number losses and one intronic splice variant (c.507+61A>G) associated with chromosomal deletion syndromes. STRING interactions were not detected, suggesting the protein may function as a metabolic enzyme rather than a protein complex component. Conservation across vertebrates (mouse Q91V76 315 aa, rat Q5U2Q3 315 aa, Xenopus Q6GME2 316 aa) indicates strong functional constraint. The DUF1907 ester hydrolase domain architecture combined with nuclear body localization and hydrolase activity annotations strongly suggests BKGD functions as a nuclear metabolic enzyme involved in ester bond hydrolysis, potentially processing nuclear metabolites or modifying nuclear proteins through de-esterification. The kidney-enriched expression pattern suggests a role in renal metabolism, possibly involved in vitamin C biosynthesis pathway (beta-keto-L-gulonate is an ascorbate precursor) or detoxification of ester-containing xenobiotics. Nuclear body localization indicates potential involvement in transcriptional regulation through metabolite signaling or chromatin modification. Pathogenic 11q CNVs likely cause haploinsufficiency disrupting nuclear ester hydrolase activity, affecting renal metabolic homeostasis and potentially contributing to genomic instability through altered chromatin modification.
