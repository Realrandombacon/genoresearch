## Description
ARHGEF16 (UniProt Q5VV41), also known as GEF16 or NBR, encodes a 709 aa Rho guanine nucleotide exchange factor (RhoGEF) with unknown specific function but predicted roles in protein-protein and protein-lipid interactions. InterPro analysis reveals a canonical triple-domain RhoGEF architecture: an N-terminal Dbl homology (DH) domain (IPR000219/cd00160/G3DSA:1.20.900.10, residues 262-487/284-468/285-466) containing the catalytic core for guanyl-nucleotide exchange factor activity (GO:0005085) that activates Rho GTPases by promoting GDP-to-GTP exchange; a central Pleckstrin homology (PH) domain (IPR001849/cd01221/G3DSA:2.30.29.30/IPR011993, residues 489-622/501-622/494-617) providing membrane localization through phosphoinositide binding and potential allosteric regulation of DH domain activity; and a C-terminal Src homology 3 (SH3) domain (IPR001452/cd11938/G3DSA:2.30.30.40/IPR035797, residues 629-690/633-686/630-690) mediating protein-protein interactions with proline-rich motif-containing partners. AlphaFold predicts medium confidence structure (pLDDT 68.5) indicating a partially structured protein where the three domains are well-folded but connected by flexible linkers enabling conformational flexibility for substrate recognition and membrane recruitment. The CDC42/RHOA connections confirm canonical RhoGEF function while EPHA2/SCRIB links suggest roles in epithelial polarity and forward signaling. HPA classifies ARHGEF16 as tissue-enhanced with highest expression in intestine (41.4 nTPM), detected in many tissues, low cancer specificity, not detected in immune cells, localized intracellularly consistent with cytoplasmic signaling roles. ClinVar lists 100 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants consisting entirely of large copy number losses (1p36.32-36.31x1, 1p36.33-36.23x1, 1p36.32-36.21x1, 1p36.33-36.22x1, 1p36.32-36.23x1) and gains (1p36.33-36.31x3, 1p36.33-36.32x3) though no single-gene disorder is definitively assigned suggesting contribution to 1p36 deletion syndrome phenotypes including developmental delay, intellectual disability, and epilepsy. Functional hypothesis: ARHGEF16 operates through integrated Rho GTPase activation mechanisms: (1) Catalytic GEF activity - DH domain catalyzes GDP release from CDC42/RHOA/RHOG enabling GTP binding and conformational switch to active state; (2) Membrane recruitment - PH domain binds PIP2/PIP3 at plasma membrane bringing DH domain proximity to membrane-anchored Rho GTPase substrates; (3) Polarity complex assembly - SH3 domain binds proline-rich motifs in SCRIB, ELMO1/2 recruiting ARHGEF16 to apical-basal polarity complexes and phagocytic cups; (4) Ephrin signaling - EPHA2 interaction suggests ARHGEF16 acts downstream of Eph receptor activation controlling repulsive guidance and cell positioning in epithelia; (5) Intestinal homeostasis - High intestinal expression suggests specific role in epithelial cell migration, crypt-villus axis maintenance, or barrier function; (6) Disease mechanism - CNV losses cause haploinsufficiency disrupting Rho GTPase cycling leading to polarity defects, impaired cell migration, and neurodevelopmental anomalies; 1p36 location places it in critical region for developmental disorders; (7) Conservation - Mouse Arhgef16 (Q3U5C8, 713 aa) indicates essential conserved function. Dark aspects requiring investigation include: identification of specific Rho GTPase substrate preference (CDC42 vs RHOA vs RHOG), structural basis for autoinhibition and activation mechanism, precise subcellular localization in intestinal epithelium, contribution to specific 1p36 deletion syndrome phenotypes, genotype-phenotype correlations for CNV variants, functional divergence from paralogs (ARHGEF38), role in cancer progression (low cancer specificity but 1p36 frequently deleted in tumors), post-translational modifications regulating GEF activity, crosstalk with EPHA2 forward/reverse signaling, and therapeutic potential in polarity-related disorders.
