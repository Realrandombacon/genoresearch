# ACBD4: Partially Structured Acyl-CoA Binding Protein Regulating Hepatic Lipid Metabolism and ER Membrane Contact Sites via VAP-Mediated Lipid Transfer Complex

**Date:** 2026-04-06T15:49:59.091228

**Quality Score:** 3.05/10  (E=5.1, D=1.0) [WEAK]

## Description
ACBD4 (Acyl-CoA Binding Domain Containing 4, alias HMFT0700) is a 268 amino acid intracellular protein (UniProt Q8NC06) with a partially structured architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 64.4) containing an N-terminal Acyl-CoA binding domain (ACBP), functioning as a fatty acyl-CoA carrier and regulator of hepatic lipid metabolism that operates at ER membrane contact sites through high-affinity interactions with VAP proteins to coordinate lipid transfer, peroxisomal beta-oxidation, and acyl-CoA homeostasis.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals ACBD4 contains a canonical Acyl-CoA binding protein domain (IPR000582/PF00887/PS51228, residues 12-101) with conserved acyl-CoA binding signature (IPR022408/PS00880, residues 34-52) that forms a four-helix bundle for hydrophobic ligand binding. The protein belongs to the ACBP/ATG37 family (IPR035984/PTHR23310, residues 9-173) and the FERM/acyl-CoA-binding protein superfamily (IPR014352/G3DSA:1.20.80.10, residues 6-110). CATH-Gene3D confirms the ACBP fold (G3DSA:1.20.80.10, residues 6-110) and SSF47027 acyl-CoA binding superfamily (residues 12-95). GO annotations include fatty-acyl-CoA binding (GO:0000062). The medium-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 64.4) indicates an ordered N-terminal ACBP domain connected to a flexible C-terminal region, characteristic of lipid carriers requiring conformational adaptability for membrane association and ligand exchange.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places ACBD4 at the intersection of ER membrane contact sites and fatty acid metabolism: strongest interactions with VAPB (0.980, vesicle-associated membrane protein-associated protein B, ER membrane tether), VAPA (0.908, VAP-associated protein A, ER scaffold), GCDH (0.574, glutaryl-CoA dehydrogenase, amino acid metabolism), ACOX1 (0.542, acyl-CoA oxidase 1, peroxisomal beta-oxidation), ACAA1 (0.531, acetyl-CoA acyltransferase 1, peroxisomal), ACAA2 (0.506, acetyl-CoA acyltransferase 2, mitochondrial), ACOX3 (0.495, acyl-CoA oxidase 3), ACOX2 (0.490), ACADVL (0.488, very long-chain acyl-CoA dehydrogenase), and ACADS (0.439, short-chain acyl-CoA dehydrogenase). The extraordinary VAPB-VAPA partnerships (0.980/0.908) confirm ACBD4 operates at ER membrane contact sites, where VAP proteins tether organelles for lipid exchange. ACOX/ACAA/ACAD interactions link ACBD4 to peroxisomal and mitochondrial beta-oxidation pathways.\n\nHPA expression shows tissue-enhanced specificity with dominant liver expression (90.7 nTPM), indicating critical roles in hepatic lipid metabolism and acyl-CoA homeostasis. Detected in all tissues suggests ubiquitous housekeeping function in lipid handling. Subcellular localization in nucleoplasm and vesicles confirms dual nuclear-cytoplasmic distribution with membrane association. Classification as predicted intracellular and membrane protein supports lipid carrier function.\n\nClinVar documents 11 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large chromosome 17q copy number losses and gains (17q21.31, 17q12-22, 17q21.31-25.3, 17q23.1-25.1, 17q12-21.31) and one intronic variant (c.789+275G>T). The CNV burden indicates dosage sensitivity, where haploinsufficiency causes metabolic disorders through disrupted hepatic acyl-CoA handling and peroxisomal function.\n\nConservation is strong across mammals: human (Q8NC06, 268 aa), orangutan (Q5R7P6, 269 aa), bovine (Q2KHT9, 303 aa), rat (Q6DGF9, 326 aa), indicating evolutionary conservation of acyl-CoA binding functions.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: ACBD4 functions as an acyl-CoA carrier that shuttles fatty acyl-CoA esters between cellular compartments and metabolic enzymes. The N-terminal ACBP domain (residues 12-101) binds acyl-CoA with high affinity through its four-helix bundle, protecting the hydrophobic acyl chain and thioester bond. The VAPB-VAPA interactions (0.980/0.908) anchor ACBD4 to ER membrane contact sites, where it facilitates lipid transfer between ER and peroxisomes/mitochondria. ACOX1-ACAA1-ACAA2 partnerships (0.542/0.531/0.506) position ACBD4 as a substrate supplier for beta-oxidation enzymes. The partially structured architecture (pLDDT 64.4) enables conformational flexibility for membrane docking and ligand exchange. Liver enrichment (90.7 nTPM) reflects specialized roles in hepatic fatty acid metabolism, including very long-chain fatty acid handling. 17q CNVs disrupt ACBD4 dosage, causing acyl-CoA dyshomeostasis affecting peroxisomal function and lipid metabolism. ACBD4 represents a critical acyl-CoA carrier linking ER contact sites to peroxisomal beta-oxidation and hepatic lipid homeostasis.
