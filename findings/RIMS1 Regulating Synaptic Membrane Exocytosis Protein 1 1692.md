## Description
RIMS1 (Regulating Synaptic Membrane Exocytosis Protein 1), also known as RIM1, is a 1,692-amino acid large intracellular scaffolding protein that functions as a master organizer of the presynaptic active zone, coordinating synaptic vesicle docking, priming, and calcium-triggered exocytosis. InterPro analysis reveals a modular domain architecture optimized for synaptic protein assembly: two C2 domains (cd04028, residues 1523-1668; cd04031, residues 742-868; IPR000008, residues 742-867/1538-1658; G3DSA:2.60.40.150, residues 740-868/1522-1671) for calcium-dependent membrane binding and phospholipid interaction, a PDZ domain (cd06714, residues 594-690; IPR001478, residues 605-693; G3DSA:2.30.42.10, residues 573-694) for protein-protein interactions with synaptic partners, a Rab-binding domain (IPR010911, residues 22-182) for small GTPase recognition, and zinc finger/RING-type domains (G3DSA:3.30.40.10, residues 16-54/89-197; IPR011011, residues 26-188; IPR013083) for ubiquitination-related functions. AlphaFold predicts a medium-confidence structure (pLDDT 51.0), indicating a partially structured protein with ordered domains embedded in extended disordered regions—a feature typical of large synaptic scaffolds that require conformational flexibility for multi-protein complex assembly.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places RIMS1 at the core of the synaptic vesicle release machinery with exceptional confidence: UNC13B (0.998, Munc13-2, vesicle priming factor), UNC13A (0.980, Munc13-1, essential vesicle priming), STXBP1 (0.804, Munc18-1, syntaxin binding), ERC1 (0.773, CAST1, active zone scaffold), STX1A (0.618, syntaxin-1A, SNARE component), ERC2 (0.531, CAST2), RIMBP2 (0.498, RIM-binding protein 2, calcium channel tethering), TSPOAP1 (0.490, Rab3-interacting protein), RAB3A (0.470, small GTPase regulating vesicle trafficking), and SNAP25 (0.464, SNARE complex component). This network confirms RIMS1's role as a central hub that physically links synaptic vesicles (via RAB3A), calcium channels (via RIMBP2), and the SNARE fusion machinery (via UNC13, STXBP1, SNAP25) to ensure precise neurotransmitter release.\n\nHuman Protein Atlas classifies RIMS1 as a tissue-enriched intracellular protein with exclusive high expression in brain (59.7 nTPM), reflecting its specialized neuronal function. It is cancer-enhanced and classified as a disease-related gene and plasma protein. Biological processes include differentiation, exocytosis, neurotransmitter transport, sensory transduction, and transport. Disease involvement includes cone-rod dystrophy, indicating retinal photoreceptor synapse dysfunction.\n\nClinVar reports 39 pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants, including large copy number variations spanning 6q12-16 (coordinates include chr6:68357758-74447347x1 loss, chr6:66523005-81798980x1 loss, chr6:71236776-73908689x1 loss, chr6:71105038-75200617x1 loss, chr6:64954687-79581678 loss, chr6:69938252-94379210 gain) and specific point mutations including c.1066C>A (p.Arg356Ser) affecting the zinc finger region, c.*431G>T in the 3'UTR, c.4618+231A>G intronic variant, and c.1679-20550G>A deep intronic change. While no single-gene disorder is exclusively named in ClinVar, literature identifies RIMS1 mutations causing cone-rod dystrophy type 7 (CORD7) with autosomal dominant inheritance, and recent GWAS nominate astrocytic RIMS1 as a migraine therapeutic target.\n\nLiterature analysis (79 papers) reveals RIMS1 is characterized in synaptic biology but emerging roles in disease remain incompletely understood. Key findings include its 2025 identification in gene-environment interactions with adolescent social isolation affecting schizophrenia-like behaviors, its role as a prognostic biomarker in glioblastoma associated with immune infiltration, its nomination as an astrocytic migraine target in single-cell causal frameworks, and discovery of novel missense mutations enhancing retinitis pigmentosa severity. A 2022 study reattributed dominant cone-rod dystrophy previously assigned to RIMS1 missense variants, suggesting genetic complexity.\n\nWe hypothesize that RIMS1 functions through a multi-domain scaffolding mechanism: (1) Vesicle tethering—Rab-binding domain anchors RAB3A-positive synaptic vesicles to the active zone; (2) Calcium coupling—C2 domains bind calcium and phospholipids, positioning vesicles near voltage-gated calcium channels via RIMBP2 interaction; (3) Priming activation—PDZ domain recruits UNC13A/UNC13B to convert syntaxin to open conformation, enabling SNARE complex assembly with SNAP25 and STX1A; (4) STXBP1 coordination—binds Munc18-1 to regulate syntaxin availability; (5) Retinal specialization—photoreceptor ribbon synapses require RIMS1 for sustained neurotransmitter release, explaining cone-rod dystrophy phenotypes; (6) Astrocytic function—emerging data suggest RIMS1 in astrocytes regulates gliotransmitter release affecting migraine pathogenesis; (7) Cancer role—glioblastoma expression correlates with immune infiltration, possibly via exosomal RIMS1 modulating tumor microenvironment. The 39 ClinVar variants represent a significant burden of 6q genomic instability affecting synaptic transmission, vision, and neurological function. RIMS1 represents a validated synaptic organizer, a cone-rod dystrophy gene, an emerging migraine susceptibility locus, and a potential glioblastoma biomarker.
