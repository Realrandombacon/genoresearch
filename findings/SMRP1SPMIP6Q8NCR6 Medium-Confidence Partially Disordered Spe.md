## Description
SMRP1 (Sperm microtubule inner protein 6, also known as SPMIP6, C9orf24, CBE1, or NYD-SP22) is a 262 amino acid intracellular protein localized to the nucleoplasm, cytosol, equatorial segment, and principal piece of sperm (UniProt Q8NCR6) with medium-confidence partially disordered architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 53.7). The protein features the canonical Sperm microtubule inner protein 6 domain (IPR028195/PF15181/PTHR35664, residues 1-262) forming a ~262 aa core module (~100%) that defines its role in spermatid differentiation and ciliogenesis. The low-to-medium AlphaFold confidence suggests intrinsic disorder or conformational flexibility, consistent with proteins that undergo dynamic interactions during cytoskeletal remodeling. Molecular functions include spermatogenesis, differentiation, and cilium assembly, placing SMRP1 within the sperm flagellum biogenesis machinery.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places SMRP1 within a ciliary and microtubule-associated network: strongest interaction with DYNLRB2 (0.680, Dynein light chain roadblock-type 2, a component of cytoplasmic dynein involved in intracellular transport), confirming its role in microtubule-based motility. High confidence links include MYORG (0.647, Myogenesis-regulating glycosidase, associated with ciliary function and primary cilia), FAM219A (0.566, Family with sequence similarity 219 member A, a ciliary protein), and PRKCSH (0.537, Protein kinase C substrate 80K-H, involved in glycosylation). Connections to GNPTG (0.535, N-acetylglucosamine-1-phosphate transferase subunit gamma) suggest potential glycosylation roles. PIFO (0.476, Pitchfork, a ciliogenesis regulator essential for left-right asymmetry) strongly supports ciliary function. CCDC160 (0.440, Coiled-coil domain containing 160) is another ciliary protein.\n\nHPA expression reveals a group-enriched pattern with exceptional levels in the choroid plexus (226.2 nTPM), fallopian tube (102.1 nTPM), testis (73.9 nTPM), and brain (60.7 nTPM), detected in many tissues. The 'cancer enhanced' status suggests upregulation in tumors. Subcellular localization in the equatorial segment and principal piece of sperm confirms its role in flagellum structure. The choroid plexus expression is notable—this tissue has extensive ciliated ependymal cells, suggesting SMRP1 may regulate ciliary function beyond spermatogenesis.\n\nClinVar documents 69 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains affecting the 9p24.3-q21.13 region: CNV gains (9p24.3-q21.13, 9p24.3-13.1, 9p24.3-q34.3 duplications) and CNV losses (9p deletions). The high variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity. Haploinsufficiency would likely impair sperm flagellum assembly, causing asthenozoospermia (reduced sperm motility) and male infertility. The choroid plexus expression suggests potential roles in cerebrospinal fluid flow via ependymal cilia; dysfunction could contribute to hydrocephalus or neurodevelopmental defects in 9p deletion/duplication syndromes.\n\nConservation is strong across mammals and vertebrates: human (Q8NCR6, 262 aa), mouse (Q2MH31, 260 aa), cow (Q32KP0, 304 aa), macaque (Q95LU0, 262 aa), cockatoo (A0A7K5FA82, 276 aa), indicating evolutionary pressure to maintain ciliary function.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: SMRP1 functions as a structural or regulatory component of the sperm manchette—a transient microtubule-based structure that shapes the sperm head and transports cargo to the developing flagellum. During spermiogenesis, SMRP1 localizes to the equatorial segment and principal piece, where it may stabilize microtubule doublets or recruit dynein arms (via DYNLRB2 interaction) for flagellar motility. The PIFO connection suggests involvement in basal body docking or ciliary membrane formation. In the choroid plexus, SMRP1 may regulate ependymal cilia beating for CSF circulation. The intrinsic disorder may allow conformational adaptability during microtubule binding or protein-protein interactions. Dysregulation via 9p CNVs disrupts ciliary assembly: loss causes sperm immotility and potential hydrocephalus, while gain may cause aberrant microtubule stabilization, contributing to 9p syndrome phenotypes (developmental delay, craniofacial abnormalities, infertility).
