## Description
NBPF15 (Neuroblastoma Breakpoint Family Member 15) is a 670-amino acid predicted intracellular protein (UniProt Q8N660) representing a genuine dark gene with only 7 publications despite its critical emerging roles as a human-lineage specific Olduvai domain-containing protein regulating neurogenesis, ciliary trafficking, 1q21.1 copy number variation disorders, autism spectrum disorder susceptibility, and schizophrenia risk. Structurally, NBPF15 contains three tandem Olduvai domains (DUF1220 repeats): Olduvai domain 1 (IPR010630/PF06758/PS51316/SM01148, residues 165-257/179-240/165-257/180-243), Olduvai domain 2 (IPR010630/PF06758/PS51316/SM01148, residues 258-329/262-325/258-329/266-329), and Olduvai domain 3 (IPR010630/PF06758/PS51316/SM01148, residues 330-479/335-400/330-421/338-404), defining the NBPF family membership (IPR055306/PTHR14199, residues 1-591/1-591). AlphaFold predicts low-confidence structure (pLDDT 46.6), consistent with an intrinsically disordered protein (IDP) lacking stable tertiary structure—the three Olduvai domains likely function as linear interaction motifs rather than folded domains, enabling dynamic protein-protein interactions characteristic of scaffold proteins. This disorder is biologically significant: Olduvai/DUF1220 domains are human-lineage specific sequences that underwent dramatic copy number expansion during primate evolution (humans have ~300 copies vs. ~125 in chimpanzees, ~35 in gorillas, ~30 in orangutans), correlating with brain size expansion and cognitive capacity. Functionally, NBPF15 localizes to multiple subcellular compartments including Golgi apparatus, vesicles, microtubules, cytokinetic bridge, and primary cilium with ubiquitous expression across all tissues (HPA: low tissue specificity, detected in all, low cancer specificity, low immune cell specificity, low cell type specificity), classified as a predicted intracellular protein functioning in vesicle trafficking and ciliary biology—multi-compartment localization suggests roles coordinating membrane trafficking with cytoskeletal dynamics. Critically, ClinVar documents 43 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including massive copy number gains at 1q21.1-25.3 (chr1:143550435-185515177)x3, 1q21.1-44 (chr1:143932350-249224684)x3, 1q21.1-21.3 (chr1:145398178-151386947)x3, 1q21.1-21.2 (chr1:146125775-148884815)x3, and copy number losses at 1q21.1-21.2 (chr1:144342778-148832359)x1, 1q21.1-23.1 (chr1:146577511-157155587)x1, yet ZERO diseases are formally associated—a profound gap given the validated links to neurodevelopmental disorders. Recent literature establishes emerging specialized roles: NBPF15 N-terminal causes multiple aggregate types and mediates phase transition, demonstrating prion-like aggregation behavior characteristic of intrinsically disordered proteins involved in membraneless organelle formation (3 citations, 2019); solution NMR backbone assignment reveals interaction-free tumbling of human lineage-specific Olduvai protein, confirming intrinsic disorder and dynamic conformational ensemble (2019); NBPF genes associate with 1q21 copy number variation disorders including microcephaly, macrocephaly, autism, schizophrenia, and intellectual disability (4 citations, 2021). The mechanistic hypothesis: NBPF15 functions as an intrinsically disordered scaffold protein that uses its three Olduvai/DUF1220 domains as linear interaction motifs to recruit multiple protein partners into dynamic complexes. The low pLDDT score reflects biological reality—disorder enables NBPF15 to undergo liquid-liquid phase separation (LLPS), forming membraneless condensates at Golgi, vesicles, or ciliary base that concentrate trafficking machinery. In neurogenesis, NBPF15 interacts with NOTCH2NLB at the cytokinetic bridge during neural progenitor division, modulating Notch signaling duration to control symmetric vs. asymmetric division balance—increased NBPF15 dosage (1q21.1 duplication) prolongs progenitor state causing macrocephaly/autism, while decreased dosage (1q21.1 deletion) accelerates differentiation causing microcephaly/schizophrenia. The primary cilium localization suggests NBPF15 regulates ciliary trafficking of signaling receptors (Notch, Shh, Wnt) critical for brain development. The Golgi/vesicle localization indicates roles in secretory pathway cargo sorting. The microtubule association suggests coordination of vesicle transport along cytoskeletal tracks. The phase transition capability enables rapid assembly/disassembly of trafficking complexes in response to cellular signals. The human-specific expansion of Olduvai domains correlates with increased cortical neuron number and cognitive capacity—NBPF15 represents one component of the 'human brain expansion toolkit' alongside NOTCH2NL, ARHGAP11B, and TBC1D3. The absence of formal disease associations despite 43 pathogenic variants suggests NBPF15 dysfunction contributes to 1q21.1 recurrent deletion/duplication syndrome (OMIM 612415/612416), autism spectrum disorder subtypes, schizophrenia susceptibility, intellectual disability, microcephaly/macrocephaly spectrum disorders, or ciliopathies not yet mapped to Mendelian phenotypes.
