## Description
SPADH (Spermadhesin-like protein) is a genuine dark gene with only 3 publications, representing a human homolog of porcine spermadhesins with undefined physiological function. UniProt accession A0A494C103 identifies it as a 137-amino acid protein described as 'Novel protein similar to AQN-1 protein AQN-1 (Sus scrofa)'. InterPro scan reveals a canonical CUB domain architecture: cd00041 (CUB, 30-131), G3DSA:2.60.120.290 (Spermadhesin CUB superfamily, 22-133), IPR000859 (CUB domain, 30-132), IPR035914 (Spermadhesin CUB superfamily, 22-133), PF00431 (CUB domain, 30-129), PS01180 (CUB profile, 30-132), SM00042 (C1r/C1s/uEGF/BMP domain, 30-132), and SSF49854 (Spermadhesin CUB, 27-131). The structural similarity to porcine spermadhesin AQN-1 suggests conserved function in reproductive biology.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis returned no partners, reflecting the gene's 'dark' status and lack of curated interactome data. This absence of known interactions highlights the opportunity for discovery.\n\nHuman Protein Atlas classifies SPADH as a predicted intracellular protein with striking tissue specificity: 'Tissue enriched' expression exclusively in the epididymis (111.6 nTPM), with 'Not detected' status in all other tissues, cancers, and immune cells. Single-cell analysis confirms 'Cell type enriched' status. The epididymis is the site of sperm maturation, storage, and acquisition of motility/fertilization capacity. This expression pattern strongly implicates SPADH in post-testicular sperm modification.\n\nClinVar reports 13 pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants, all large copy number variations (CNVs) on chromosome 10q26 (e.g., chr10:121918547-133620674)x1 loss, chr10:119707856-133613639)x3 gain, chr10:100600492-133622588)x3 gain). While no specific disease phenotype is named, 10q26 deletions/duplications are associated with developmental delay, intellectual disability, and male infertility syndromes. The presence of these CNVs overlapping SPADH suggests dosage sensitivity, potentially affecting sperm function or epididymal integrity.\n\nLiterature reveals only 3 papers, none directly characterizing human SPADH function: (1) Molecular docking of bovine semen extenders (2025, indirect reference); (2) Brassica napus GWAS (irrelevant homolog); (3) Wheat drought tolerance (irrelevant). The complete absence of direct functional studies confirms SPADH as a true dark gene.\n\nWe hypothesize that SPADH functions as an epididymis-specific sperm-coating protein mediating sperm maturation via CUB domain-dependent interactions: (1) Sperm surface binding—secreted by epididymal epithelium, SPADH coats spermatozoa during transit, modifying surface properties for fertilization competence; (2) Glycan recognition—CUB domains in spermadhesins bind carbohydrate moieties (e.g., sulfated glycans) on sperm membrane or oviductal epithelium, facilitating sperm-egg interaction or sperm reservoir formation; (3) Protein scaffolding—SPADH may bridge sperm surface receptors with epididymal secretory proteins, stabilizing membrane domains required for motility; (4) Immune evasion—CUB domains resemble complement regulatory regions; SPADH may protect sperm from female reproductive tract immune surveillance; (5) Dosage sensitivity—13 CNVs suggest strict stoichiometric requirement; haploinsufficiency or overexpression may disrupt sperm coating, contributing to male infertility in 10q26 syndrome patients; (6) Evolutionary conservation—homology to porcine AQN-1 (fertility factor) implies conserved mammalian mechanism; (7) Structural stability—high pLDDT (87.1) indicates rigid binding pocket for specific ligand (glycan/protein); (8) Therapeutic potential—SPADH dysfunction may underlie unexplained male infertility; recombinant SPADH could improve assisted reproduction outcomes; (9) Biomarker utility—epididymis-specific expression makes SPADH a candidate biomarker for epididymal pathology or obstructive azoospermia; (10) Orphan function—while porcine homologs bind heparin and egg coat proteins, human SPADH ligands remain unidentified. The combination of CUB domain structure, exclusive epididymal expression (111.6 nTPM), high-confidence AlphaFold model, 10q26 clinical CNVs, and porcine spermadhesin homology positions SPADH as a high-priority candidate for functional characterization in male reproduction and infertility.
