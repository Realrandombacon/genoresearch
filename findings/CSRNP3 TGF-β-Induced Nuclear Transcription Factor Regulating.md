## Description
CSRNP3 (Cysteine and Serine Rich Nuclear Protein 3, also FAM130A2, MBU1, PPP1R73, TAIP-2) is a 585 amino acid nuclear protein (UniProt Q8WYN3) with a partially structured conformation (AlphaFold pLDDT 59.8, medium confidence), characteristic of transcription factors containing both ordered DNA-binding regions and intrinsically disordered activation domains for flexible protein-protein interactions.\n\nInterPro analysis identifies CSRNP3 as a member of the cysteine/serine-rich nuclear protein family (IPR023260/PTHR13580, residues 1-583) with a conserved N-terminal domain (IPR031972/PF16019, residues 62-279) and PRINTS signature motifs (PR02031 at residues 180-193, 205-216, 220-236). The protein belongs to the TGF-beta induced apoptosis protein family, indicating roles in programmed cell death regulation.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals connections to diverse regulatory proteins: ENPP5 (0.495, ectonucleotide pyrophosphatase), GPD2 (0.460, mitochondrial glycerol-3-phosphate dehydrogenase), LMNTD1 (0.453, laminin tail domain), SPHKAP (0.441, sphingosine kinase anchor protein), SCGB3A2 (0.429, secretoglobin), SCN3A (0.412, voltage-gated sodium channel), and PDGFD (0.410, platelet-derived growth factor D). This interaction pattern suggests roles in signal transduction and transcriptional regulation.\n\nHPA expression shows brain-enriched pattern (9.3 nTPM) with tissue-enhanced specificity and nucleoplasmic/nucleoli rim subcellular localization, consistent with transcriptional regulatory functions in neuronal cells. The protein is classified as a transcription factor with DNA-binding, activator, apoptosis, and transcription regulation molecular functions.\n\nClinVar documents 50 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large chromosome 2q copy number variations (gains and losses spanning 2q24.2-q37.3 regions). While specific CSRNP3 point mutations are not yet catalogued, these CNVs suggest dosage sensitivity potentially causing neurodevelopmental disorders through impaired apoptosis regulation.\n\nConservation is evident from mouse ortholog (P59055, 597 aa) and additional mouse isoforms (Q8BGQ2/CSRNP2, Q7TNU4), indicating functional importance across mammals with family member diversification.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: CSRNP3 acts as a TGF-β-induced transcription factor that binds specific DNA sequences through its cysteine/serine-rich N-terminal domain, recruiting chromatin remodeling complexes to regulate pro-apoptotic gene expression. The protein likely responds to TGF-β signaling by translocating to the nucleoli rim where it modulates RNA polymerase II transcription of apoptosis regulators. Brain-enriched expression suggests roles in neuronal development, synaptic plasticity, or neurodegenerative disease pathways. CNV-mediated dosage imbalance disrupts apoptotic homeostasis, potentially causing neurodevelopmental disorders or contributing to neurodegeneration through impaired cell death regulation.
