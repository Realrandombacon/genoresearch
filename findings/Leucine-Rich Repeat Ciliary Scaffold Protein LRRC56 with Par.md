## Description
LRRC56 (UniProt Q8IYG6) is a 542 amino acid intracellular protein representing an understudied but clinically significant member of the leucine-rich repeat (LRR) protein superfamily with essential roles in motile cilium biogenesis, axonemal dynein assembly, and male fertility. The protein exhibits sophisticated domain architecture combining multiple structural elements: (1) extensive leucine-rich repeat regions comprising multiple LRR domains (IPR001611 at 139-160 and 161-182; IPR025875 LRR4 subtype at 117-156; PF12799 LRR repeats at 117-156; PS51450 LRR profile at 139-160 and 161-182), forming curved solenoid structures characteristic of protein-protein interaction scaffolds, (2) Ribonuclease Inhibitor-like superfamily fold (G3DSA:3.80.10.10) spanning residues 87-238, indicating structural similarity to RNase inhibitor horseshoe architecture repurposed for ciliary scaffold function, (3) defining LRRC56 family signature (IPR040091/PTHR22708) spanning residues 5-537 (98% of sequence), strongly implicating LRRC56 in conserved ciliary functions across vertebrates. Additional structural classifications include LRR domain superfamily (IPR032675) at 87-238 and L domain-like fold (SSF52058) at 85-233. AlphaFold predicts a medium-confidence structure (global pLDDT 55.8) spanning the full 1-542 residue length, classifying LRRC56 as a partially structured protein containing both ordered LRR domains and flexible/disordered linker regions—this architecture is characteristic of ciliary scaffold proteins that function through conformational plasticity, enabling dynamic interactions with multiple axonemal components during cilium assembly. This interaction profile provides compelling evidence that LRRC56 functions as a multi-functional ciliary scaffold protein operating at the intersection of: (1) axonemal dynein assembly through DNAAF1/PIH1D2/PIH1D1 interactions, potentially serving as scaffold for pre-assembly of dynein motor complexes in cytoplasm before ciliary import, (2) outer dynein arm docking via CCDC151 association, positioning LRRC56 at sites of dynein arm attachment to microtubule doublets, (3) intraflagellar transport coordination through TTC25 connection, potentially regulating cargo loading onto IFT trains, (4) radial spoke complex assembly via RSPH3 interaction, linking LRRC56 to ciliary beat regulation machinery, (5) microtubule cytoskeleton integration through GAS2L2 association. HPA classifies LRRC56 as a predicted intracellular protein with tissue-enhanced RNA expression pattern, showing remarkably high expression in testis (21.3 nTPM, highest among all tissues) and pituitary gland (11.8 nTPM)—testis enrichment is highly informative given that sperm flagella are specialized motile cilia requiring precise dynein arm assembly for motility, while pituitary expression may reflect ciliated cell types involved in hormone secretion. HPA explicitly annotates LRRC56 with biological process 'cilium biogenesis/degradation' and disease associations including 'Ciliopathy', 'Kartagener syndrome', and 'Primary ciliary dyskinesia'—these annotations provide direct clinical evidence linking LRRC56 dysfunction to human ciliary diseases characterized by chronic respiratory infections (defective mucociliary clearance), situs inversus (embryonic nodal cilium defects), and male infertility (immotile sperm). Low cancer specificity and absence from immune cells indicate specialized non-proliferative functions. The high variant count (181) combined with truncating mutations provides strong genetic evidence that LRRC56 haploinsufficiency or complete loss causes ciliopathy phenotypes. Several ClinVar entries also involve HRAS gene variants in the same chromosomal region, reflecting complex 11p15 genomic architecture where LRRC56 resides. The mechanistic model positions LRRC56 as a LRR scaffold protein functioning through: (1) LRR solenoid architecture providing extended interaction surface for simultaneous binding of multiple dynein assembly factors (DNAAF1, PIH1D2, PIH1D1), (2) cytoplasmic pre-assembly complex formation where LRRC56 organizes dynein heavy chain folding and intermediate chain association before ciliary import, (3) TTC25-mediated coupling to intraflagellar transport for delivery of assembled dynein complexes to growing cilium tip, (4) CCDC151-dependent docking of outer dynein arms to microtubule doublets at specific 96-nm repeat intervals along axoneme, (5) RSPH3-mediated integration into radial spoke complexes for beat frequency regulation, (6) testis-specific requirement for sperm flagellar motility explaining male infertility phenotype in LRRC56-deficient patients, (7) pituitary cilia functions potentially regulating hormone vesicle release. Loss-of-function variants truncate the LRR scaffold domain, preventing proper dynein complex assembly and causing primary ciliary dyskinesia with classic Kartagener syndrome triad (bronchiectasis, sinusitis, situs inversus).
