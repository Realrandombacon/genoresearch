## Description
NAA16 (NARG1L) encodes an 864 aa auxiliary subunit of the NatA N-terminal acetyltransferase complex (UniProt Q6N069), essential for co-translational protein modification and stability regulation. The protein contains multiple tetratricopeptide repeat (TPR) domains (IPR019734, PF07719, aa46-79/80-113/224-257) forming a helical scaffold (IPR011990, aa53-470) and the NatA auxiliary subunit domain (IPR021183/PF12569, aa5-806). AlphaFold prediction shows high confidence (pLDDT 89.2), indicating a well-folded TPR-rich scaffold structure enabling multiple protein-protein interactions. HPA shows tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in bone marrow (22.1 nTPM) and pancreas (19.3 nTPM), with cytosolic localization consistent with ribosome-associated NatA function. STRING interactions reveal tight NatA complex membership: NAA10 (catalytic subunit, 0.999), NAA15 (scaffold, 0.952), HYPK (regulatory, 0.947), NAA50 (0.876), plus connections to other NAT complexes (NAA30 0.966, NAA20 0.692, NAA11 0.675). ClinVar contains 23 pathogenic variants (primarily 13q copy number losses/gains), though specific disease phenotypes remain undefined. Literature (10 papers) indicates roles in heart development (Drosophila models), N-terminal acetylation regulation, and apoptosis inhibition. NAA16 likely functions as a structural scaffold bridging NAA10 catalytic activity to ribosomal substrates via TPR-mediated protein interactions, with the well-folded architecture enabling stable NatA complex assembly for co-translational acetylation of ~40% of human proteome, regulating protein stability, localization, and turnover.
