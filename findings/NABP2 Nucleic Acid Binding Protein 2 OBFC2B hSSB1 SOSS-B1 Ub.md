## Description
NABP2 encodes a 211 aa intracellular DNA-binding protein (UniProt Q9BQ15), also known as OBFC2B, hSSB1, or SOSS-B1, that functions as an essential single-stranded DNA (ssDNA) binding component of the SOSS (Sensor of Single-Stranded DNA) complex required for DNA double-strand break (DSB) recognition, homologous recombination repair, and genomic stability maintenance. This partially characterized gene (30 publications) has significant clinical implications for cancer prognosis and human growth. The protein features specialized nucleic acid binding architecture: SOSS complex subunit B family domain (IPR051231/PTHR13356, residues 3-163) defining the SOSS-B subunit class; ssDNA binding protein Ssb-like OB fold (IPR048970/PF21473, residues 11-92) providing the canonical oligonucleotide/oligosaccharide binding fold for ssDNA recognition; nucleic acid-binding OB-fold superfamily (IPR012340/G3DSA:2.40.50.140/SSF50249, residues 1-116/6-116) with GO terms for DNA binding and DNA damage repair; SoSSB_OBF domain (cd04491, residues 18-95) mediating specific ssDNA interactions. STRING interactions reveal NABP2 operates within high-confidence DNA repair and RNA processing networks: INTS5 (0.997)/INTS1 (0.997)/INTS3 (0.916)/INTS9 (0.811)/INTS11 (0.756)/INTS6 (0.578), Integrator complex subunits linking to transcriptional regulation and RNA processing; NABP1 (0.906), paralogous ssDNA binding protein; SSBP1 (0.725), single-stranded DNA binding protein 1 in mitochondrial DNA maintenance; ATM (0.485), ataxia telangiectasia mutated kinase in DSB sensing and checkpoint activation; INIP (0.446), INTS6-interacting protein. HPA confirms ubiquitous expression with low tissue specificity (detected in all tissues), low cancer and immune cell specificity, cell type enriched classification, nucleoplasm subcellular localization, classified as predicted intracellular protein with DNA damage/repair biological processes and DNA-binding molecular function. ClinVar lists 8 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including intronic variants (c.-24+69A>T, c.219-12del), large-scale inversion (12q13.2-14.1 56-56Mb), and extensive copy number gains (12q13.2-14.1 55-62Mb x3, 12p13.33-q24.33 0.17-133Mb x3, 12p13.33-q24.33 0.12-133Mb x3), demonstrating strong dosage sensitivity though specific monogenic diseases remain undefined. AlphaFold structure prediction yields high confidence (global pLDDT 76.8), revealing a well-folded OB-fold structure characteristic of ssDNA binding proteins with conserved nucleic acid binding surface. Mechanistically, NABP2 operates through integrated modes: (1) SOSS complex assembly—forms heterotrimeric SOSS complex with INTS3 and C17orf75/SOSS-C that binds ssDNA at DSB sites, recruiting MRN complex and ATM kinase for checkpoint activation; (2) Homologous recombination—2023 study (7 citations) demonstrates hSSB1 modulates DNA damage and androgen-induced transcriptional response in prostate cancer, affecting radiotherapy sensitivity; (3) Hepatocellular carcinoma—2023 studies (3+1 citations) identify NABP2 as prognostic biomarker correlating with immune infiltration and promoting HCC progression/metastasis; 2025 study explores role in liver cancer immune microenvironment and metabolic pathways; (4) Human height determination—2021 GWAS study (15 citations) identifies NABP2 as novel locus for human height variation, suggesting role in growth regulation through DNA repair-mediated developmental pathways; (5) ssDNA protection—OB-fold domain binds and protects ssDNA intermediates during replication and repair, preventing degradation and secondary structure formation; (6) ATM kinase recruitment—STRING interaction with ATM suggests direct role in DSB signaling cascade activation; (7) Paralog redundancy—NABP1/hSSB2 represents functionally overlapping paralog with distinct expression patterns. Loss-of-function causes defective DSB recognition, impaired homologous recombination repair, genomic instability, increased radiosensitivity, altered androgen response in prostate cancer, reduced hepatocellular carcinoma progression, and potential effects on human growth trajectories.
