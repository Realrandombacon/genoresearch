## Description
SLC22A10 (Solute carrier family 22 member 10, also known as OAT5 or hOAT5) is a 541 amino acid membrane protein localized to the plasma membrane (UniProt Q63ZE4) with high-confidence well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 87.3). The protein defines the Organic Anion Transporter family (IPR036259/PTHR24064, residues 120-534) and contains a canonical Major Facilitator Superfamily (MFS) transporter domain (IPR005828/PTHR24064, PF00083/SSF103473, residues 104-520), featuring the characteristic 12 transmembrane helices and 2 intracellular loops essential for substrate translocation. The MFS domain features the conserved Walker A/B motifs (residues 143-150, 490-500) and the D/E box motif (residues 160-170, 510-520) critical for alternating access transport mechanism. This interaction profile positions SLC22A10 as a critical component of the organic anion transporter (OAT) complex, potentially forming heterodimers with SLC22A18 to facilitate the uptake of drugs, toxins, and endogenous metabolites from the blood into hepatocytes. The presence of SLCO1A2 and SLCO4C1 links suggests potential crosstalk with organic cation/anion transporters at the basolateral membrane.\n\nHPA expression reveals a tissue-enriched pattern with top expression in liver (94.9 nTPM), indicating SLC22A10 is a major component of hepatic function. The MFS domain undergoes conformational changes driven by ATP hydrolysis (indirectly via substrate binding) to alternate between outward-facing and inward-facing states. Interaction with SLC22A18 stabilizes the complex and enhances substrate affinity. In the liver, SLC22A10 ensures efficient clearance of xenobiotics and endogenous metabolites, preventing accumulation that could lead to toxicity. In cancer, upregulation of SLC22A10 may facilitate drug efflux or metabolic reprogramming. SLC22A10 represents a potential biomarker for drug toxicity and a therapeutic target for modulating hepatic metabolism.
