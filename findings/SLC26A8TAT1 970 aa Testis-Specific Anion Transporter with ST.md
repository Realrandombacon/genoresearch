## Description
SLC26A8 (Q96RN1, 970 aa), also known as TAT1 (Testis Anion Transporter 1) or AZON, is a member of the SLC26 family of anion transporters with expression strictly restricted to the testis, specifically in spermatocytes and developing sperm. InterPro identifies a canonical architecture featuring a large N-terminal transmembrane transport core (IPR001902, IPR011547, PF00916, PTHR11814, aa 59-809) characteristic of sulfate permease antiporters, followed by a prominent C-terminal cytoplasmic STAS domain (IPR002645, IPR036513, PF01740, PS50801, G3DSA:3.30.750.24, cd07042, aa 523-799). The STAS domain (Sulfate Transporter and Anti-Sigma factor antagonist) is critical for protein-protein interactions, regulatory phosphorylation, and potentially nucleotide binding, distinguishing it from simple channels. AlphaFold predicts a medium-confidence structure (pLDDT 68.6, residues 1-970), reflecting a well-folded transmembrane core but significant flexibility or disorder in the long N- and C-terminal extensions, consistent with a dynamic regulatory role during meiosis. HPA shows tissue-enriched expression exclusively in the testis (41.2 nTPM); localized to the acrosome and annulus of sperm; classified as a disease-related gene involved in anion exchange, differentiation, ion transport, meiosis, and spermatogenesis. STRING reveals 10 high-confidence interactions linking it to cytokinesis and chromosomal segregation: RACGAP1 (0.950, Rac GTPase-activating protein 1, essential for central spindle formation), ANLN (0.767, anillin, scaffold for contractile ring), ECT2 (0.684, RhoGEF for cytokinesis), KIF23 (0.587, kinesin for midbody formation), CFTR (0.725, cystic fibrosis transporter, functional partner), SLC26A9 (0.570), SLC26A4 (0.546), SLC26A3 (0.518), DNAJB13 (0.515, testis-specific chaperone), and MT-ND4 (0.506). The exceptionally strong link to RACGAP1 and ANLN suggests SLC26A8 is not merely a transporter but a structural component of the cytokinetic machinery or the sperm annulus, coordinating ion flux with cell division or morphological changes. ClinVar contains 15 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including frameshifts (c.1664del/p.Ile555fs), missense mutations (c.212G>T/p.Arg71Leu, c.290T>C/p.Leu97Pro), and large copy number losses/gains (chr6:35562152-42003452 deletion, chr6:29455465-81447367 duplication) affecting the 6p21 region; although no specific monogenic disease name is explicitly assigned in ClinVar, the variant burden and testis-specificity strongly implicate it in autosomal recessive male infertility (azoospermia or oligozoospermia) and defects in sperm maturation (SPGF3 locus). Functional hypothesis: SLC26A8/TAT1 functions as a chloride/bicarbonate or chloride/formate exchanger that regulates intracellular pH and volume during the dramatic morphological remodeling of spermiogenesis. Its interaction with RACGAP1 suggests a dual role: transporting anions to modulate local osmolarity at the cleavage furrow during meiotic divisions, and/or stabilizing the septin-based annulus structure in mature sperm to maintain membrane integrity and motility. The STAS domain likely serves as a hub for kinases (e.g., PKA) to regulate transport activity in response to cAMP signaling during capacitation. Disruption leads to failed cytokinesis, aberrant sperm head/tail formation, or inability to undergo the acrosome reaction, resulting in infertility. Conservation across vertebrates (mouse 999 aa, bovine 960 aa) confirms essential reproductive role. NOTE: This gene represents a 'dark' but mechanistically plausible link between ion transport and the mechanical events of sperm development.
