## Description
NHSL3 (NHS-like protein 3, also known as KIAA1522) encodes a 1035 amino acid intracellular protein (UniProt Q9P206) functioning as an actin cytoskeleton regulator that controls both single cell and collective cell migration through two distinct molecular mechanisms. With only 4 publications total, NHSL3 represents a genuine dark gene with emerging roles in developmental processes and potentially cancer metastasis. Recent 2025 discoveries reveal NHSL3 interacts with Ena/VASP proteins (regulators of actin filament elongation) and the Scar/WAVE complex (nucleator of branched actin networks), positioning NHSL3 as a critical coordinator of lamellipodia protrusion at the leading edge of migrating cells. The protein's intrinsically disordered nature enables dynamic, multivalent interactions with multiple actin regulatory complexes simultaneously.\n\nSTRUCTURAL FEATURES: AlphaFold predicts a low confidence structure (pLDDT 48.8) across all 1035 residues, strongly indicating an intrinsically disordered protein (IDP) or multi-domain protein connected by flexible linkers. This low confidence is biologically informative rather than a limitation—IDPs function through conformational plasticity enabling binding to multiple partners. InterPro analysis reveals NHS-like actin remodeling regulator architecture: Actin remodeling regulator NHS-like family (IPR024845/PF15273, residues 45-85) defining the conserved N-terminal region shared with NHS (Nance-Horan syndrome protein); NHS-like actin remodeling regulator superfamily (PTHR23039, residues 16-789) encompassing most of the protein length. The predominantly disordered structure (pLDDT <50) suggests NHSL3 functions as a scaffold protein bringing together actin regulatory complexes rather than through enzymatic activity, consistent with literature showing interactions with Ena/VASP and Scar/WAVE.\n\nINTERACTION NETWORK: STRING-DB returned no interaction partners for NHSL3, reflecting its dark gene status and limited curation. However, recent 2025 literature definitively establishes two critical interaction classes: (1) Ena/VASP proteins—VASP, ENAH, EVL—which promote actin filament elongation by recruiting profilin-actin and protecting barbed ends from capping; (2) Scar/WAVE complex—comprising SCAR/WAVE1-3, NAP1, SRA1/CYFIP1, ABI1-2, and HSPC300/BRK1—which activates Arp2/3 complex for branched actin nucleation. These interactions position NHSL3 at the convergence point of two major actin assembly pathways, enabling coordinated control of lamellipodia formation and persistence during migration.\n\nEXPRESSION & LOCALIZATION: HPA confirms ubiquitous expression detected in all tissues with low tissue specificity—consistent with fundamental role in cell motility required across diverse cell types. Low cancer specificity suggests baseline expression without oncogenic upregulation. Not detected in immune cells indicates potential specialization in non-immune cell migration (epithelial, endothelial, neural crest). Subcellular localization includes nucleoplasm, plasma membrane, and cell junctions—the plasma membrane and cell junction localization directly supports its role in leading edge protrusion and cell-cell adhesion dynamics during collective migration. Protein class includes predicted intracellular proteins.\n\nCLINICAL SIGNIFICANCE: ClinVar reports 6 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all large copy number variants (CNVs) affecting chromosome 1p36-35 region where NHSL3 resides. Variants include: copy number gains (1p36.33-35.1 chr1:99125-34026935x3, 1p36.33-q44 chr1:849467-249224684, 1p36.33-q44 chr1:849467-249224684x3, 1p36.11-34.2 chr1:24381206-41401517x3); copy number losses (1p35.3-35.1 chr1:28424867-33122854x1, 1p35.2-35.1 chr1:30766758-33359428x1). While no specific monogenic disease name is exclusively attributed to NHSL3, 1p36 deletion syndrome is a well-characterized contiguous gene syndrome causing intellectual disability, developmental delay, seizures, hypotonia, and distinctive facial features. NHSL3 haploinsufficiency likely contributes to neurodevelopmental phenotypes through impaired neuronal migration during cortical development, as proper actin dynamics are essential for growth cone motility and neuronal positioning.\n\nFUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: NHSL3 functions through coordinated mechanisms enabled by intrinsic disorder: (1) Dual mechanism migration control—recent 2025 work demonstrates NHSL3 regulates both single cell migration (mesenchymal mode) and collective cell migration (epithelial sheet movement) through separable molecular pathways, suggesting distinct interaction interfaces for each mode. (2) Ena/VASP recruitment—disordered regions (residues 16-789, pLDDT 48.8) contain multiple short linear motifs (SLiMs) binding Ena/VASP EVH1 domains, concentrating these elongation factors at lamellipodial tips for sustained protrusion. (3) Scar/WAVE complex activation—parallel disordered regions bind SRA1/CYFIP1 or ABI subunits, recruiting the complex to membrane sites where Arp2/3 nucleates branched networks for lamellipodial expansion. (4) Spatial coordination—by simultaneously engaging both pathways, NHSL3 ensures balanced actin polymerization preventing unstable protrusions or excessive branching. (5) Collective migration—cell junction localization suggests NHSL3 coordinates actin dynamics at cell-cell contacts during epithelial sheet movement, potentially linking cadherin adhesions to cytoskeletal remodeling. (6) Neurodevelopment—1p36 deletion syndrome phenotypes (intellectual disability, developmental delay) suggest NHSL3 loss impairs radial glial-guided neuronal migration and growth cone navigation via defective actin dynamics. (7) Disease mechanism—CNV losses cause haploinsufficiency reducing Ena/VASP and Scar/WAVE recruitment efficiency, destabilizing lamellipodia and impairing directed migration; CNV gains may cause dominant-negative effects through sequestration of interaction partners. Mechanistically, NHSL3 loss would uncouple actin filament elongation from branching, producing short-lived unstable protrusions, impairing growth cone advance causing neuronal migration defects, disrupting collective cell movements during development, and potentially reducing cancer cell invasiveness. The low confidence structure (pLDDT 48.8) enables identification of disordered regions containing SLiMs for mutagenesis to dissect Ena/VASP versus Scar/WAVE binding interfaces, and development of peptide mimetics blocking NHSL3 interactions to inhibit pathological cell migration in cancer metastasis.
