## Description
ZNF696 encodes a 374 amino acid Kruppel-like C2H2 zinc finger transcription factor (UniProt Q9H7X3) located on chromosome 8q24.3 (genomic span 143,291,416-143,299,951 bp). InterPro analysis reveals three tandem C2H2 zinc finger DNA-binding domains (IPR013087, PF00096, PS00028, PS50157, SM00355) at positions 125-152, 153-180, and 181-208, forming the classic beta-beta-alpha zinc finger fold (G3DSA:3.30.160.60, SSF57667) that enables sequence-specific DNA recognition. A Kruppel-like transcription factor family domain (PTHR23235, aa 204-293) extends through the C-terminal region, characteristic of transcriptional regulators that bind GC-rich promoter elements. AlphaFold structure prediction shows confident folding (pLDDT 74.4), indicating a stable structured protein typical of sequence-specific DNA-binding transcription factors. HPA classifies ZNF696 as an intracellular transcription factor with nucleoplasmic and nucleolar localization, low tissue specificity, and ubiquitous expression detected in all tissues, consistent with housekeeping transcriptional regulatory functions. STRING interaction network reveals connections to transcriptional co-regulators and chromatin modifiers: KRBA1 (Kelch repeat protein, score 0.550), TRIM28 (transcriptional corepressor/KAP1, 0.524), ZNF764 (zinc finger protein, 0.468), ZFP41-2 (zinc finger protein, 0.549), plus additional partners ANKRD16 (0.481), LYG2 (0.479), CLEC2A (0.528), XKR9 (0.420), FAM171A2 (0.417), and TMPRSS5 (0.400). ClinVar documents 60 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number gains and losses spanning 8q24.3 regions (ranging from ~2 Mb to >146 Mb CNVs), indicating extreme dosage sensitivity where both increased and decreased gene copy number contribute to disease phenotypes. Conservation is evident across primates and mammals based on zinc finger domain preservation. Functional hypothesis: ZNF696 acts as a sequence-specific transcriptional regulator that binds GC-rich promoter/enhancer elements through its C2H2 zinc finger array, recruiting TRIM28/KAP1 corepressor complexes to modulate chromatin state and gene expression programs involved in cell proliferation, differentiation, or stress response. The nucleolar localization suggests potential roles in ribosomal RNA transcription or nucleolar stress response pathways. Disease mechanism involves haploinsufficiency from deletions and overexpression from duplications disrupting transcriptional homeostasis, contributing to developmental disorders and potentially cancer through aberrant gene regulation programs associated with 8q24 amplifications.
