## Description
RHNO1 (Q9BSD3) encodes a 238 amino acid nuclear protein that functions as a critical adaptor in the ATR-CHK1 DNA damage checkpoint signaling pathway, bridging the 9-1-1 (RAD9-HUS1-RAD1) clamp complex loaded at sites of DNA damage to the TOPBP1 activator that stimulates ATR kinase activity, thereby coordinating cell cycle arrest, DNA repair, and replication stress responses essential for genome stability. InterPro analysis reveals a compact architecture specific to this checkpoint regulator: a Rad9/Hus1/Rad1-interacting nuclear orphan protein 1 family domain (IPR029293/PF15319/PTHR35541, positions 1-238/1-236/1-238) defining the entire protein as a unique functional unit with DNA damage checkpoint signaling (GO:0000077), cellular response to ionizing radiation (GO:0071479), and nucleus localization (GO:0005634). AlphaFold predicts a medium confidence structure (pLDDT 64.8) consistent with a partially structured protein containing both ordered domains enabling specific partner recognition and flexible linkers facilitating conformational adaptation during checkpoint complex assembly typical of signaling adaptors. STRING interactions demonstrate exclusive high-confidence partnerships with core DNA damage response machinery: RAD9A (0.895), RAD1 (0.775), and HUS1 (0.710) forming the heterotrimeric 9-1-1 clamp complex that RHNO1 directly binds; CLSPN (0.867), claspin mediating CHK1 activation downstream of ATR; CHEK1 (0.866), checkpoint kinase 1 executing cell cycle arrest; TOPBP1 (0.862), DNA topoisomerase II-binding protein 1 serving as the ATR activator that RHNO1 recruits to damaged DNA; BRCA1 (0.802), breast cancer type 1 susceptibility protein in homologous recombination repair; RAD17 (0.667), replication factor C-like clamp loader loading the 9-1-1 complex; ATRIP (0.642), ATR-interacting protein stabilizing ATR kinase; BRIP1 (0.608), BRCA1-interacting helicase in DNA repair. HPA shows ubiquitous expression with low tissue specificity detected in all tissues reflecting fundamental role in genome maintenance, classified under predicted intracellular proteins with cell cycle/DNA damage/DNA repair biological processes confirming essential checkpoint function. Literature (only 23 papers, confirming genuine dark gene status) defines RHNO1 as an emerging oncology target: RHNO1 disruption inhibits cell proliferation and induces mitochondrial apoptosis via PI3K/Akt pathway inactivation in hepatocellular carcinoma demonstrating oncogenic driver activity; microbial carrier-based delivery of RHNO1 mediates NF-κB signaling inhibition regulating proliferation and apoptosis in nonsmall cell lung cancer revealing dual context-dependent functions; CRISPR targeting the 5' regulatory region of FOXM1 inadvertently disrupts RHNO1 due to overlapping genomic organization complicating functional studies; mouse model of Rhno1 deletion reveals defects in DNA damage signaling and cell cycle checkpoints confirming essential in vivo function; recent reviews position RHNO1 at the crossroads of DNA replication stress, DNA repair, and cancer highlighting therapeutic potential. The mechanistic hypothesis: RHNO1 functions as a molecular bridge where its partially structured fold (pLDDT 64.8) enables simultaneous binding to the 9-1-1 clamp complex (via RAD9A/RAD1/HUS1 partnerships, scores 0.710-0.895) loaded at DNA damage sites by the RAD17-RFC clamp loader and to TOPBP1 (score 0.862) which recruits and activates ATR-ATRIP kinase complex; this bridging function positions ATR in proximity to its substrates including CHK1 (score 0.866) and CLSPN (score 0.867) enabling phosphorylation cascade that arrests cell cycle at G2/M checkpoint allowing time for DNA repair; BRCA1 partnership (score 0.802) links RHNO1 to homologous recombination repair coordinating checkpoint activation with repair pathway choice; PI3K/Akt pathway activation in hepatocellular carcinoma operates through RHNO1-dependent survival signaling where RHNO1 prevents excessive checkpoint-induced apoptosis enabling tumor cell proliferation under replication stress; NF-κB modulation in lung cancer suggests RHNO1 influences inflammatory signaling potentially through ATM/ATR-mediated IκB regulation; ubiquitous expression reflects essential genome surveillance role in all dividing cells; pathogenic CNVs cause dosage imbalance where amplification enhances checkpoint tolerance potentially enabling cancer cell survival under genotoxic stress while deletion impairs DNA damage responses causing genomic instability and neurodevelopmental defects; FOXM1 overlap complicates therapeutic targeting requiring isoform-specific approaches.
