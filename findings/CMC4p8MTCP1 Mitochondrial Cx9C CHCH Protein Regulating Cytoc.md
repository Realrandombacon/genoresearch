## Description
CMC4 (UniProt P56277), also known as p8 or p8MTCP1, encodes a compact 68-amino acid mitochondrial protein containing the characteristic Cx9C motif (IPR027179/PF08991, aa 6-59) within the coiled-coil-helix-coiled-coil-helix (CHCH) domain superfamily (PS51808, aa 4-46). The protein belongs to the cysteine alpha-hairpin motif superfamily (IPR009069/SSF47072, aa 1-64) and shares structural homology with cytochrome c oxidase copper chaperones (G3DSA:1.10.287.1130, aa 1-68). AlphaFold predicts a well-folded structure (pLDDT: 86.3), consistent with stable mitochondrial protein architecture despite its small size.\n\nSTRING interactions position CMC4 within the mitochondrial respiratory chain assembly network: strongest link to cytochrome c oxidase assembly factor COA4 (0.797), with connections to mitochondrial inner membrane proteins FUNDC2 (0.594), COA6 (0.566), copper chaperone COX17 (0.562), mitochondrial import oxidoreductases CHCHD4 (0.536)/CHCHD7 (0.535)/CHCHD5 (0.486), ATP synthase subunit ATP5F1B (0.499), adenine nucleotide translocator SLC25A4 (0.499), and paralogue CMC2 (0.400). HPA confirms ubiquitous expression with mitochondrial localization and proto-oncogene classification.\n\nOriginally identified through t(X;14) translocations in mature T-cell proliferations (MTCP1 locus at Xq28), CMC4 represents a transitional dark gene with emerging mitochondrial functions. ClinVar contains 246 pathogenic variants (Xq28 CNVs and promoter variants like c.-4G>C). The Cx9C-CHCH architecture suggests a role in cytochrome c oxidase biogenesis through disulfide relay mechanisms analogous to COA4/COA6, potentially linking mitochondrial dysfunction to T-cell leukemogenesis when dysregulated via chromosomal translocation.
