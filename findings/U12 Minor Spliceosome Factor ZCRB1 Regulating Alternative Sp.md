## Description
ZCRB1 (Zinc Finger CCHC-Type and RNA-Binding Motif-Containing Protein 1, UniProt Q8TBF4) is a 217-amino acid RNA-binding protein that functions as a core component of the U11/U12 minor spliceosome, responsible for splicing the rare ~0.5% of human introns with non-canonical splice sites. The protein contains an N-terminal RNA recognition motif (RRM, IPR000504, PF00076, positions 10-88) for RNA substrate binding and a C-terminal CCHC-type zinc finger (IPR001878, positions 106-122) for protein-protein interactions within the spliceosomal complex. AlphaFold predicts a confident structure (pLDDT 74.4) with two distinct folded domains connected by a flexible linker. ZCRB1 is ubiquitously expressed (HPA: detected in all tissues) with nuclear localization consistent with splicing function. STRING interactions reveal exclusive partnerships with spliceosome components: RNPC3 (0.975), SNRNP48 (0.968), ZMAT5 (0.961), SNRNP35 (0.958), SF3B2 (0.928), SF3B1 (0.924), SNRNP25 (0.898), and YBX1 (0.897). Mechanistically, ZCRB1 regulates: (1) USP21 minor intron retention suppressing hepatocellular carcinoma progression; (2) ciliogenesis and WNT signaling; (3) glycolysis via PKM2-HIF1α axis in nasopharyngeal carcinoma; (4) circHEATR5B-encoded protein production suppressing glioblastoma aerobic glycolysis through JMJD5 phosphorylation. ClinVar documents 11 pathogenic variants (mostly 12p/12q CNVs). With only 14 literature references despite critical roles in minor splicing, cancer metabolism, and ciliopathy, ZCRB1 represents an underexplored spliceosomal factor with therapeutic potential in oncology.
