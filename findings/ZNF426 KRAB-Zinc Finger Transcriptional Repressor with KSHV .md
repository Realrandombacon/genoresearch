## Description
ZNF426 (Zinc Finger Protein 426), also known as K-RBP (KSHV RTA-Binding Protein), is a 554 amino acid transcriptional repressor located on chromosome 19p13.2 that functions as a host restriction factor controlling Kaposi sarcoma-associated herpesvirus (KSHV) latency. The protein architecture features an N-terminal KRAB domain (IPR001909/PF01352, residues 41-112) which recruits the co-repressor TRIM28/KAP1, followed by three C2H2-type zinc finger domains (IPR013087/PF00096, residues 146-307) that mediate sequence-specific DNA recognition. AlphaFold prediction yields medium global confidence (pLDDT 66.1), reflecting the characteristic structural organization of KRAB-ZNF proteins with well-folded zinc finger clusters connected by flexible linker regions. Human Protein Atlas classifies ZNF426 as a nucleoplasmic transcription factor with ubiquitous expression across all tissues (low tissue specificity), consistent with a broad role in transcriptional regulation and antiviral defense. STRING interaction analysis identifies TRIM28 as the primary high-confidence partner (score 0.632), confirming the canonical KRAB-mediated recruitment of heterochromatin-forming complexes; additional partners include MAGOHB (mRNA splicing), PNMA5/PNMA3 (paraneoplastic antigens), ZNF268 (related zinc finger), and notably RBPJ (score 0.413), the canonical Notch signaling transcription factor that also serves as a KSHV latency control hub. ClinVar contains 6 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all copy number gains/duplications spanning 19p13, indicating dosage sensitivity. The gene summary reveals a critical virus-host interaction mechanism: ZNF426 binds the KSHV lytic transactivator RTA (ORF50) to repress viral reactivation from latency, while RTA counteracts this restriction by inducing ZNF426 degradation through the ubiquitin-proteasome pathway. This molecular arms race positions ZNF426 as an intrinsic immunity factor that epigenetically silences KSHV lytic genes, while the virus has evolved to neutralize this defense. The RBPJ interaction further suggests ZNF426 may compete with or modulate Notch signaling at KSHV latency control regions.
