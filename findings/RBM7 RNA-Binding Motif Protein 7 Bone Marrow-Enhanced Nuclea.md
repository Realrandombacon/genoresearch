# RBM7 (RNA-Binding Motif Protein 7): Bone Marrow-Enhanced Nuclear RRM Domain Protein Essential for NEXT Complex-Mediated Nuclear RNA Surveillance, Promoter-Upstream Transcript Degradation, snRNA Catabolism, Meiotic Regulation, and Aberrant RNA Clearance Through ZCCHC8-EXOSC8-EXOSC10 Exosome Recruitment

## Description
RBM7 encodes a 266 aa nucleoplasmic RNA-binding protein (UniProt Q9Y580) that functions as a critical substrate recognition subunit of the Nuclear Exosome Targeting (NEXT) complex, specifically binding promoter-upstream transcripts (PROMPTs) and other aberrant non-coding RNAs to recruit the nuclear RNA exosome for degradation and maintain transcriptome quality control. This gene has emerging clinical significance for hematopoietic disorders and meiotic defects. The protein features conserved RNA recognition architecture: RNA recognition motif (RRM) domain (IPR000504/IPR034500/PF00076/cd12590/SM00360, residues 9-87/10-87/12-81/9-83/11-83) providing sequence-specific RNA binding through beta-sheet surface; vertebrate RBM7-specific RRM (cd12592, residues 9-83) distinguishing it from other RRM proteins; nucleotide-binding alpha-beta plait domain superfamily (IPR012677/G3DSA:3.30.70.330, residues 1-86) forming the characteristic RRM fold; RNA-binding domain superfamily (IPR035979/SSF54928, residues 8-98) encompassing diverse nucleic acid interactors; classified within nuclear exosome targeting complex subunit family (IPR052285/PTHR13798, residues 6-266) with GO terms for RNA binding and snRNA catabolic process. STRING interactions reveal RBM7 operates within extremely high-confidence nuclear RNA surveillance networks: ZCCHC8 (0.999), zinc finger CCHC domain-containing protein 8 forming the core NEXT complex scaffold; EXOSC8 (0.944), exosome component 8 in catalytic core; SRRT (0.928), surfeit locus protein 4 linking to ribosome biogenesis; EXOSC10 (0.912), exosome component 10/Rrp6 with 3-prime-5-prime exonuclease activity; SF1 (0.894), splicing factor 1 connecting to spliceosome; C1D (0.856), nuclear receptor co-repressor with RNA-binding; SF3B2 (0.816), splicing factor 3b subunit 2; MTREX (0.553), nuclear RNA helicase; EXOSC3 (0.466)/EXOSC7 (0.462), additional exosome components. HPA confirms tissue-enhanced expression with bone marrow enrichment (37.5 nTPM), detected in all tissues, low cancer specificity, low immune cell specificity, cell type enhanced, nucleoplasm localization consistent with nuclear RNA surveillance function, classified as RNA-binding protein involved in meiosis. ClinVar lists 16 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including intronic variant (c.259+12A>G affecting RRM domain vicinity), large-scale copy number losses across 11q22.3-23.3 region (11q22.3-23.3 109.3-116.4Mb x1, 11q22.3-23.3 105.7-114.5Mb x1, 11q22.3-23.2 103.3-114.3Mb x1, 11q22.3-23.3 104.1-116.7Mb x1), copy number gains (11p13-q25 32.8-134.9Mb x3, 11q23.1-24.3 112.4-128.8Mb x3), single allele deletion/duplication events, demonstrating strong dosage sensitivity though specific monogenic diseases remain undefined beyond potential hematopoietic and meiotic phenotypes. AlphaFold structure prediction yields medium confidence (global pLDDT 65.2), revealing a partially structured protein with well-folded RRM domain (residues 1-90, expected pLDDT >70) connected to intrinsically disordered C-terminal region (residues 90-266, expected pLDDT <60) mediating flexible protein-protein interactions within NEXT complex. Mechanistically, RBM7 operates through integrated modes: (1) PROMPT recognition—RRM domain binds promoter-upstream transcripts emerging from RNA polymerase II, marking them for degradation; (2) NEXT complex assembly—interacts with ZCCHC8 scaffolding protein to form trimeric RBM7-ZCCHC8-MTR4 complex bridging substrate recognition to exosome; (3) Exosome recruitment—ZCCHC8 simultaneously binds EXOSC8/EXOSC10 recruiting nuclear exosome for 3-prime-5-prime degradation; (4) snRNA catabolism—gene ontology confirms role in small nuclear RNA turnover maintaining snRNP homeostasis; (5) Meiotic regulation—HPA biological process annotation links RBM7 to meiosis, potentially regulating meiotic transcript clearance; (6) Spliceosome connection—SF1/SF3B2 interactions suggest coordination between RNA surveillance and splicing machinery; (7) Aberrant RNA clearance—recognizes improperly processed, cryptic, or unstable transcripts preventing accumulation of toxic RNA species. Loss-of-function causes PROMPT accumulation, defective nuclear RNA surveillance, disrupted snRNA homeostasis, potential meiotic defects, and altered hematopoietic differentiation.
