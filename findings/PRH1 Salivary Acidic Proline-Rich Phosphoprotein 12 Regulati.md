## Description
PRH1 encodes a 166 aa salivary acidic proline-rich phosphoprotein 1/2 (UniProt P02810) located at 12p13.33, functioning as a major component of acquired enamel pellicle that regulates oral microbiome composition, calcium homeostasis, and bacterial adhesion. InterPro analysis reveals a characteristic intrinsically disordered architecture: Proline-rich protein family (IPR026086/PTHR23203, aa 1-166/1-71) with extensive proline-rich domains (PF15240/SM01412, aa 1-161/1-166) lacking stable secondary structure, consistent with proteins functioning through conformational flexibility and multivalent interactions. AlphaFold predicts medium-confidence structure (pLDDT 67.2), indicating a partially structured protein with significant intrinsic disorder, typical for salivary proline-rich proteins that adopt extended conformations for surface coating and ligand binding. Literature (46 papers) details emerging roles in oral biology and cancer: (1) Dental caries susceptibility - Human PRH1/PRH2 polymorphisms interacting with Streptococcus mutans virulence phenotypes to specify distinct microbial profiles in caries-active vs. caries-free individuals (4 citations, 2024); (2) NSCLC progression - Circular RNA circPRH1-PRR4 (readthrough transcript) stimulating RAB3D expression by sponging miR-877-5p, driving malignant progression in non-small cell lung cancer (14 citations, 2022); (3) Plant homolog function - Arabidopsis PRH1 mediating ARF7-LBD dependent auxin signaling for lateral root development (36 citations, 2019), highlighting conserved roles in growth regulation despite sequence divergence; (4) Pellicle formation - Acidic PRPs binding hydroxyapatite via phosphorylated serine residues, creating a conditioning film for selective bacterial colonization. Functional hypothesis: PRH1 likely functions through: (1) Enamel Pellicle Scaffold - intrinsically disordered structure enabling high-density coating of tooth surface, with proline-rich repeats providing flexible spacers for displaying phosphorylated serine clusters that bind calcium and hydroxyapatite; (2) Microbiome Modulator - selective adhesion platform promoting beneficial commensals (e.g., Streptococcus sanguinis) while restricting pathogens (e.g., S. mutans) based on genetic variants affecting charge distribution and binding affinity; (3) Calcium Reservoir - high-affinity calcium binding buffering supersaturated saliva, preventing spontaneous precipitation while facilitating remineralization; (4) circRNA Oncogene - readthrough transcription generating circPRH1-PRR4 that acts as competitive endogenous RNA (ceRNA) sequestering miR-877-5p, derepressing RAB3D (vesicle trafficking GTPase) to enhance NSCLC invasion/metastasis; (5) Circadian/Metabolic Link - potential interaction with cryptochromes (CRY1/2) suggesting roles in coordinating salivary secretion rhythms with metabolic status. The disordered proline-rich architecture parallels other salivary PRPs (PRB4, PRH2) but differs from structured globular proteins, enabling unique 'fuzzy' interactions. Disease implications include: dental caries susceptibility/resistance, periodontal disease, non-small cell lung cancer progression, 12p duplication syndromes (Pallister-Killian), potential Sjögren's syndrome biomarkers. Despite 46 papers, PRH1's specific strain-level bacterial binding preferences, structural dynamics of pellicle assembly, circRNA biogenesis regulation, and therapeutic targeting in NSCLC remain underexplored.
