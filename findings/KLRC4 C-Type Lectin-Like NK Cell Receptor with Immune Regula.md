## Description
KLRC4 (Killer Cell Lectin Like Receptor C4 / NKG2-F) encodes a 158 aa type II integral membrane protein (UniProt O43908) functioning as a C-type lectin-like receptor on natural killer (NK) cells. InterPro analysis reveals characteristic NK cell receptor domains: IPR016186 (C-type lectin-like/link domain superfamily, aa 104-145), IPR016187 (C-type lectin fold, aa 80-143), and IPR050919/PTHR22800 (NKG2/CD94 Natural Killer Receptors family, aa 1-143), plus G3DSA:1.10.287.770 (YojJ-like, aa 68-103) and G3DSA:3.10.100.10 (Mannose-Binding Protein A, aa 104-145). STRING interactions show high-confidence partnerships with KLRC2/NKG2C (0.977), KLRD1/CD94 (0.954), KIR3DL1 (0.939), KIR2DL3 (0.934), KIR2DL1 (0.931), HLA-E (0.921), and KLRC1/NKG2A (0.920), indicating KLRC4 functions within the NKG2 receptor network recognizing MHC class I ligands. HPA shows lymphoid tissue-enriched expression (8.9 nTPM), cancer-enhanced expression, and blood cell group enrichment, consistent with NK cell lineage specificity. ClinVar contains 38 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly copy number gains/duplications across 12p13.33-11.1, suggesting genomic instability at the NK receptor complex locus contributes to disease. AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 60.2) indicates medium confidence with partially structured domains, typical of immune receptors with flexible ligand-binding regions. Literature (49 papers) links KLRC4 to Behçet's disease susceptibility (44 citations), antithyroid drug-induced agranulocytosis, and breast cancer lymph node metastasis. FUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: KLRC4 likely functions as an activating or modulatory NK cell receptor that fine-tunes immune surveillance through heterodimerization with CD94 (KLRD1) and recognition of HLA-E or related MHC class I molecules, with copy number variations predisposing to autoimmune dysregulation (Behçet's, agranulocytosis) and altered cancer immune surveillance. The medium-confidence AlphaFold structure suggests conformational flexibility important for ligand discrimination between healthy and stressed/infected cells.
