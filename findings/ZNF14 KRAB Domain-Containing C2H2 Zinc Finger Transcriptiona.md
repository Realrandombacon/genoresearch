## Description
ZNF14 (Gene ID: 7561, UniProt P17017, aliases GIOT-4, KOX6) is a 642 amino acid Krüppel-associated box (KRAB) domain-containing zinc finger protein located on chromosome 19p13.11 (NC_000019.10, positions 19733111-19710471, ~22.6kb region). InterPro domain analysis reveals a canonical KRAB-A box domain (cd07765/IPR001909/PF01352, positions 3-76) that mediates transcriptional repression, three C2H2-type zinc finger domains (IPR013087/PF00096 at positions 103-130, 141-168, 169-196; PF13912 at positions 197-215, 223-245) for sequence-specific DNA binding, and membership in the C2H2-type zinc finger protein family (IPR050636, positions 214-446). AlphaFold structure prediction (AF-P17017-F1) shows high confidence (global pLDDT 78.5), indicating a well-folded stable protein with defined secondary and tertiary structure. Human Protein Atlas classifies ZNF14 as a transcription factor localized to nucleoplasm and cytosol, functioning in transcription regulation through DNA-binding. Expression shows low tissue specificity with detection in many tissues and cell type-enhanced patterns, suggesting ubiquitous but regulated expression. STRING database identifies 5 medium-confidence protein-protein interactions: TRIM28 (score 0.605), C8orf48 (0.447), USP49 (0.431), BORCS8 (0.408), and ZNF223 (0.408). TRIM28 (KAP1/TIF1β) is a critical co-repressor that specifically binds KRAB domains and recruits heterochromatin-forming complexes including SETDB1 histone methyltransferase and HP1 proteins. ClinVar contains 8 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all large copy number gains affecting chromosome 19p/q regions including duplications (GRCh37/hg19 19p13.11-q13.2 chr19:19546923-41313229x3, 19p13.12-12 chr19:14286624-20956753x3, 19p13.3-q13.43 chr19:260912-58956888/x3; GRCh38/hg38 19p13.12-12 chr19:15133594-24193591x3, 19p13.2-q13.31 chr19:11227942-44626354x3, 19p13.12-q11 chr19:13974677-27839676x3, 19p13.11-q13.11 chr19:17176767-34924150x3). Conservation across primates is evidenced by orthologs in crab-eating macaque (Q4R4C7, 642 aa), Sumatran orangutan (Q5R9F0, 642 aa), and spotted hyena (A0A6G1BEQ8, 455 aa). Functional hypothesis: ZNF14 functions as a sequence-specific DNA-binding transcriptional repressor through a two-module mechanism: (1) C-terminal C2H2 zinc fingers recognize specific DNA sequences at target gene promoters/enhancers, while (2) N-terminal KRAB domain recruits TRIM28/KAP1 co-repressor complex. TRIM28 then scaffolds assembly of heterochromatin-forming machinery including SETDB1 (H3K9 methyltransferase), HP1 proteins, and NuRD complex components, leading to H3K9me3 deposition, chromatin compaction, and stable gene silencing. This KRAB-ZFP/TRIM28 axis represents a major mammalian transcriptional repression system controlling development, retroelement silencing, and cell identity. Disease mechanism: Dosage sensitivity from chromosome 19 copy number gains causes ZNF14 overexpression, potentially leading to excessive transcriptional repression of developmental genes. The 19p13 region contains numerous dosage-sensitive genes, and ZNF14 dysregulation may contribute to phenotypes including developmental delay, intellectual disability, growth abnormalities, and congenital anomalies observed in 19p duplication syndromes. Priority experiments include identification of DNA binding targets via ChIP-seq, characterization of TRIM28-dependent repression mechanisms, assessment of H3K9me3 changes at target loci upon ZNF14 manipulation, and investigation of ZNF14's role in specific developmental contexts.
