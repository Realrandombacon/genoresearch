# MBLAC1: Metallo-Beta-Lactamase Fold RNA Endonuclease Regulating Histone mRNA 3'-End Processing and Cell Cycle Progression

**Date:** 2026-04-06T13:18:28.729387

**Quality Score:** 4.05/10  (E=7.0, D=1.1) [MODERATE]

## Description
MBLAC1 (Metallo-Beta-Lactamase Domain Containing 1, also C7orf44) is a 266 amino acid nuclear and cytoplasmic protein (UniProt A4D2B0) with a well-folded metallo-hydrolase architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 89.1, high confidence), characteristic of the MBL-fold superfamily that has been repurposed from antibiotic resistance enzymes to RNA processing endonucleases in eukaryotes.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals MBLAC1 contains a canonical metallo-beta-lactamase domain (IPR001279/IPR036866/IPR039344/PF00753/SM00849/PTHR23200, residues 66-231/78-129/2-243) belonging to the Ribonuclease Z/Hydroxyacylglutathione hydrolase-like superfamily (G3DSA:3.60.15.10/SSF56281, residues 1-239/73-237). The MBL-fold adopts a characteristic alpha-beta-beta-alpha sandwich with a central beta-sheet coordinating zinc ions for catalytic activity, conferring RNA endonuclease (GO:0004521) and metal ion binding (GO:0046872) activities. This places MBLAC1 in the same structural superfamily as ELAC1/ELAC2 (tRNA 3'-processing endonucleases) and RNase Z.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals MBLAC1 functions within RNA processing and metal-dependent hydrolase networks: strongest interactions with HAGHL (0.515, hydroxyacylglutathione hydrolase-like, metal-dependent hydrolase), MBLAC2 (0.507, metallo-beta-lactamase domain-containing protein 2, paralog), FAM214A (0.488), ELAC2 (0.486, elastase 2 homolog, tRNA 3'-processing endonuclease), DCLRE1A (0.481, DNA cross-link repair 1A, SNM1A nuclease), ZC3H6 (0.479, zinc finger CCCH-type-containing protein 6, RNA-binding), HAGH (0.478, hydroxyacylglutathione hydrolase), INTS9 (0.464, integrator complex subunit 9, RNA polymerase II transcription machinery), LACTB2 (0.447, lactamase beta 2, mitochondrial serine hydrolase), and ELAC1 (0.410, elastase 1 homolog, tRNA-splicing endonuclease). The ELAC1/ELAC2 and INTS9 interactions strongly suggest roles in RNA 3'-end processing and transcription-coupled RNA maturation.\n\nHPA expression shows low tissue specificity with detection in many tissues and predicted intracellular localization with hydrolase molecular function, consistent with housekeeping RNA processing roles. Single cell type specificity is cell type enhanced.\n\nClinVar documents 23 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including splice site mutations (c.-28-1G>A), deletions, and large chromosome 7q22.1 copy number variations (losses spanning 7q22.1-31.1 to 7q21.12-22.1 regions). These CNVs suggest dosage sensitivity potentially causing developmental disorders through impaired histone mRNA processing and cell cycle dysregulation.\n\nConservation is evident from mouse (Q8BWY4, 260 aa), rat (Q6AYD1, 251 aa), cow (Q2HJB0, 263 aa), and Xenopus (Q498J9, 233 aa) orthologs, indicating ancient evolutionary conservation of the MBL-fold RNA processing machinery.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: MBLAC1 acts as a zinc-dependent RNA endonuclease that processes histone mRNA 3'-ends during S-phase, coupling histone synthesis to DNA replication. Unlike canonical histone mRNAs that terminate in a stem-loop without poly(A) tails, replication-dependent histone mRNAs require specialized 3'-end processing by the U7 snRNP complex and endonucleases. MBLAC1's MBL-fold coordinates zinc ions to cleave pre-histone mRNA downstream of the stem-loop, generating mature 3'-ends. The ELAC1/ELAC2 interactions suggest functional similarity to tRNA 3'-processing, while INTS9 interaction links MBLAC1 to the Integrator complex that processes snRNA 3'-ends. The MBLAC2 paralog may provide redundancy or substrate specificity. DCLRE1A interaction suggests potential roles in DNA damage response through RNA-DNA hybrid resolution. Cell cycle regulation (G1/S transition) occurs because histone mRNA availability limits S-phase entry; MBLAC1 dysfunction causes histone mRNA accumulation defects, triggering cell cycle arrest or genomic instability. CNV-mediated haploinsufficiency disrupts histone mRNA metabolism, causing developmental defects through impaired chromatin assembly during rapid cell division.
