# USP43: Multi-Substrate Deubiquitinase Coordinating Oncogenic c-Myc Stability, Ferroptosis Suppression, and Antiviral Immunity via TBK1 Regulation

## Description
USP43 (Ubiquitin Specific Peptidase 43) is an emerging deubiquitinating enzyme with 44 publications, recently characterized (2024-2025) as a critical oncogenic driver in multiple cancers through stabilization of c-Myc, FASN, and TAZ, while simultaneously suppressing antiviral innate immunity via TBK1 degradation. This large 1123-amino acid nuclear protein (UniProt Q70EL4) contains a canonical USP catalytic domain (IPR001394/PF00443, positions 102-707) with conserved active site signatures (PS00972 positions 102-117; PS00973 positions 652-669) characteristic of cysteine-type deubiquitinases. The catalytic domain belongs to the peptidase C19 family (cd02674, positions 540-708) within the papain-like cysteine peptidase superfamily (IPR038765). AlphaFold predicts medium-confidence structure (pLDDT 60.3), indicating a structured catalytic core surrounded by flexible regulatory regions typical of substrate-selective DUBs.\n\nSTRING interactions reveal USP43 operates within ubiquitin signaling and DNA replication networks: YWHAB/14-3-3β (0.636, phospho-serine binding regulator), PAN2 (0.627, poly(A)-specific ribonuclease), POLD1 (0.582, DNA polymerase delta), USP10 (0.581, tumor suppressor DUB), ISG15 (0.580, interferon-stimulated ubiquitin-like modifier), USP54 (0.575, related DUB), UBC/UBB (0.568/0.549, ubiquitin), and POLI/POLH (0.556/0.549, translesion DNA polymerases). This interaction profile positions USP43 at the intersection of protein stability control, DNA damage response, and interferon signaling.\n\nHPA shows expression detected in many tissues with nucleoplasmic and nuclear speckle localization, consistent with roles in transcriptional regulation and mRNA processing. Biological processes include Ubl conjugation pathway. ClinVar contains 11 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains on chromosome 17p13.3-12 and a 3' UTR variant (c.*402A>T), though specific monogenic diseases remain uncharacterized. Conservation across mouse (1132 aa) indicates strong functional constraint.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: USP43 functions as a context-dependent substrate selector that integrates oncogenic signaling with immune regulation through competitive deubiquitination. In cancer cells, YY1-induced USP43 expression stabilizes multiple oncoproteins: c-Myc (driving glycolysis/Warburg effect in bladder cancer), FASN (suppressing ferroptosis in ovarian cancer via SLC7A11 activation), and TAZ (activating Hippo pathway in cervical carcinoma). The nuclear speckle localization suggests USP43 may process transcriptional coactivators co-transcriptionally. Paradoxically, USP43 also suppresses antiviral immunity by promoting TBK1 ubiquitination and degradation via the RNF2 axis, creating an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment. The POLD1/POLI/POLH interactions suggest USP43 may regulate DNA damage tolerance by stabilizing translesion polymerases. Pathogenic CNVs causing USP43 overexpression may drive cancer progression through simultaneous enhancement of metabolic reprogramming, ferroptosis resistance, and immune evasion—making USP43 a promising therapeutic target for combination cancer therapy.
