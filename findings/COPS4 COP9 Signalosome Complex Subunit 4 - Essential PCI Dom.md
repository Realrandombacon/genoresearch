## Description
COPS4 (COP9 Signalosome Complex Subunit 4, UniProt Q9BT78) is a 406 amino acid nuclear protein functioning as an essential structural scaffold subunit of the COP9 signalosome (CSN), a conserved multiprotein complex that regulates cullin-RING ubiquitin ligases (CRLs) through deneddylation. With only 13 literature references (genuine dark gene status), COPS4 has emerging roles as a pan-cancer prognostic biomarker regulating the tumor immune microenvironment. The protein contains multiple conserved domains: PCI domain (IPR000717, PF01399, aa 197-377) characteristic of proteasome lid and COP9 signalosome subunits, winged helix DNA-binding domain superfamily (IPR000717, IPR036388, aa 289-369), CSN4 helix-turn-helix domain (IPR041406, PF18420, aa 365-406), and PSMD12/CSN4 N-terminal domain (IPR054559, PF22241, aa 5-219). AlphaFold predicts an exceptionally well-folded structure (pLDDT 94.7, very high confidence) across all 406 residues, indicating stable tertiary architecture critical for complex assembly. STRING interactions reveal perfect integration into the COP9 signalosome complex with maximum confidence scores (0.999) for all core subunits: COPS3, COPS2, COPS6, COPS8, COPS5, GPS1/COPS1, COPS7A, COPS7B, plus interactions with NEDD8 (0.888 - the ubiquitin-like modifier removed by CSN) and RBX1 (0.857 - RING-box protein component of CRLs). HPA confirms nuclear speckle localization with ubiquitous expression across all tissues, classified as an essential protein with low tissue/cancer specificity. ClinVar documents 38 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number losses (4q21.21-22.3, 4q21.21-23, 4q13.3-22.1) and gains (4p12-q35.2, 4q12-31.21), plus single nucleotide variants (c.626A>G p.Asn209Ser, c.410+1G>A splice site), though specific COPS4-associated diseases require further characterization. Key literature findings: (1) 2025 pan-cancer analysis identified COPS4 as novel prognostic biomarker involved in immune microenvironment regulation across cholangiocarcinoma (CHOL), head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSC), esophageal carcinoma (ESCA), and liver hepatocellular carcinoma (LIHC) (3 citations), (2) 2007 chromosomal assignment study in porcine model (1 citation). Functional hypothesis: COPS4 functions as a critical structural scaffold within the COP9 signalosome, with its PCI domain mediating heterodimerization with COPS3/COPS8 to form the CSN 'lid' subcomplex. The winged helix and helix-turn-helix domains likely facilitate protein-protein interactions rather than DNA binding, positioning COPS4 at the interface between the CSN catalytic core (COPS5/JAB1 metalloprotease) and substrate CRLs. Through CSN-mediated deneddylation (removal of NEDD8 from cullins), COPS4-containing complexes regulate CRL activity cycles, controlling ubiquitination and degradation of key substrates including cell cycle regulators (p27, IκB), transcription factors (HIF-1α, p53), and signaling proteins. The 2025 cancer immune microenvironment findings suggest COPS4 dysregulation alters CRL substrate stability, affecting antigen presentation, immune checkpoint expression, and tumor-infiltrating lymphocyte recruitment. Disease mechanism: COPS4 overexpression in aggressive cancers (CHOL, HNSC, ESCA, LIHC) may cause aberrant CRL regulation, stabilizing oncoproteins or destabilizing tumor suppressors through impaired deneddylation cycles. The 38 ClinVar variants suggest potential developmental disorders, consistent with the essential protein classification. The NEDD8 (0.888) and RBX1 (0.857) interactions confirm direct involvement in cullin neddylation/deneddylation cycles. Cancer therapeutic implication: COPS4 represents a potential immunotherapy target, as modulating CSN activity could reshape the tumor immune microenvironment by altering chemokine/cytokine stability and MHC expression.
