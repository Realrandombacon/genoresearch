## Description
CCDC121 (Gene ID: 79635, UniProt Q6ZUS5) encodes a 278 amino acid coiled-coil domain-containing protein localized to actin filaments and cytosol. InterPro analysis reveals a domain of unknown function DUF4515 (IPR032777/PF14988, residues 35-240) and classification as basal body-orientation factor 1 (PTHR14845, residues 1-278). The DUF4515 domain spans 205 residues (~74% of protein length), suggesting this domain constitutes the core functional unit. Human Protein Atlas shows testis-enriched expression (23.7 nTPM in testis, tissue-enhanced pattern, detected in all tissues), with subcellular localization to actin filaments and cytosol, indicating cytoskeletal regulatory function. STRING interaction network identifies 10 high-confidence protein partners: TBC1D17 (score 0.817, Rab GTPase-activating protein involved in vesicle trafficking), KRTCAP3 (0.654, keratin-associated protein), TBC1D15 (0.618, mitochondrial fission regulator), SLC4A1AP (0.515, anion exchanger-associated protein), NRBP1 (0.448, nuclear receptor-binding protein), C2orf16 (0.447, uncharacterized protein), IFT172 (0.438, intraflagellar transport protein critical for ciliogenesis), GPN1 (0.436, GTPase involved in nuclear import), ZNF512 (0.431, zinc finger transcription factor), and FNDC4 (0.426, fibronectin type III domain protein). The interaction with IFT172 is particularly significant - IFT172 is essential for intraflagellar transport and primary cilium assembly, linking CCDC121 to ciliogenesis and basal body function consistent with Panther family annotation. ClinVar contains 15 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all copy number variations (CNVs) affecting chromosome 2p region (2p23.3-25.3 and broader 2p-q regions). Variants include large duplications (2p25.3-23.1: 12771-30565600, 2p24.1-22.2: 20938401-37327210, 2p25.3-22.3: 12771-35541353, multiple others spanning 15-40 Mb) and deletions (2p25.1-q13: 11504318-111365996). These CNVs cause 2p deletion/duplication syndromes featuring developmental delay, intellectual disability, dysmorphic features, and neurological abnormalities. Functional hypothesis: CCDC121 functions as basal body-orientation factor that regulates centrosome/cilium positioning and microtubule-actin cytoskeleton coordination during cell division and ciliogenesis. The DUF4515 domain likely mediates protein-protein interactions with IFT complex components (IFT172 interaction supports this), TBC domain Rab-GAPs (TBC1D17/TBC1D15 interactions suggest vesicle trafficking regulation), and actin-binding partners. Testis-enriched expression (23.7 nTPM) suggests specialized role in spermatogenesis where basal body positioning is critical for flagellum assembly and sperm motility. Mechanistic proposal: CCDC121 recruits IFT172 and associated intraflagellar transport machinery to basal bodies via DUF4515 domain-mediated protein interactions, coordinating microtubule nucleation from centrosomes with actin filament organization. During ciliogenesis, CCDC121 anchors basal bodies to actin cytoskeleton, ensuring proper orientation for axoneme extension. In mitosis, CCDC121 may regulate spindle orientation through actin filament interactions, affecting cell division plane and tissue architecture. CNV pathogenicity mechanism: haploinsufficiency (deletions) causes reduced CCDC121 dosage impairing basal body positioning and ciliogenesis, contributing to 2p deletion syndrome neurodevelopmental phenotypes; triplosensitivity (duplications) causes excessive CCDC121 disrupting cytoskeletal coordination and vesicle trafficking. Conservation analysis pending (AlphaFold server error), but DUF4515 domain conservation across vertebrates suggests functional constraint. This represents a "dark gene" with defined domain architecture, interaction network, tissue-specific expression, and clear clinical significance through chromosomal rearrangements.
