## Description
PPFIBP2 (Liprin-beta-2, UniProt Q8ND30) is an 876-amino acid intracellular scaffold protein that organizes receptor tyrosine phosphatase (RTP) complexes at specialized membrane domains. The protein contains three tandem sterile alpha motif (SAM) domains (IPR001660, positions 555-787) that mediate homo- and hetero-oligomerization, plus an N-terminal coiled-coil region (IPR058914, positions 108-213) for partner recruitment. PPFIBP2 shows broad tissue expression (HPA: detected in all tissues, mitochondrial localization) but functions primarily at the plasma membrane where it clusters type IIA receptor protein tyrosine phosphatases (PTPRS, PTPRD, PTPRF) with liprin-alpha family members (PPFIA1-4). STRING interactions confirm high-confidence partnerships: PPFIA2 (0.846), PTPRS (0.834), PPFIA3 (0.815), PTPRD (0.813), and PPFIA4 (0.805). Mechanistically, PPFIBP2 serves as a molecular bridge connecting RTPs to the actin cytoskeleton via CRK (0.594) and TCFL5 (0.574), regulating cell adhesion, migration, and synaptic organization. Clinically, PPFIBP2 is oncogenic when rearranged: the PPFIBP2-RET fusion (identified in Fukushima thyroid cancers, 45 citations) drives constitutive RET kinase activation. Germline loss-of-function variants (20 pathogenic ClinVar entries) predispose to lethal prostate cancer. The AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 70.1) predicts a confident extended conformation with flexible linkers between SAM domains, enabling dynamic complex assembly. With only 25 literature references despite clear disease relevance, PPFIBP2 represents an underexplored scaffold with therapeutic potential in cancer and neurodevelopment.
