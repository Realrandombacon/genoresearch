## Description
FMO4 encodes a 558 amino acid dimethylaniline monooxygenase [N-oxide-forming] 4 (UniProt P31512), a member of the flavin-containing monooxygenase (FMO) family. With 85 publications, FMO4 is recognized as a metabolic enzyme but has recently emerged as a critical oncogenic driver in lung cancer and a modulator of ferroptosis.\n\nSTRUCTURAL FEATURES: AlphaFold predicts an exceptionally confident structure (pLDDT 91.5) across all 558 residues, indicating a highly stable, well-folded enzyme. InterPro analysis confirms the canonical FMO architecture: a large FAD/NAD(P)-binding domain superfamily (IPR036188, G3DSA:3.50.50.60, residues 2-484) forming the catalytic core, and the flavin monooxygenase family domain (IPR000960, PF00743, residues 1-531). Specific FMO4 signatures (IPR002256, PR01124) distinguish it from other family members (FMO1-6). GO annotations confirm N,N-dimethylaniline monooxygenase activity (GO:0004499), FAD binding (GO:0050660), and NADP binding (GO:0050661).\n\nINTERACTION NETWORK: STRING-DB identifies 10 interaction partners linking FMO4 to drug metabolism and mitochondrial function. Phase I/II metabolism: CYP2D6 (0.928), CYP3A4 (0.925), CYP2C9 (0.590), CYP2S1 (0.411), CYP2C8 (0.409), ADH1B (0.403)—confirming its role in xenobiotic clearance alongside cytochrome P450s. Family homolog: FMO1 (0.440). Mitochondrial association: ATP5PB (0.450), ATP5F1A (0.432)—suggesting potential coupling of metabolic oxidation to energy production or ROS regulation. Lipid metabolism: LIPJ (0.423).\n\nEXPRESSION & LOCALIZATION: HPA confirms group-enriched expression with highest levels in liver (57.3 nTPM) and kidney (45.3 nTPM), consistent with its role in systemic detoxification. Detected in many tissues with cell type-enhanced specificity. Subcellular localization includes vesicles and cytosol, typical for FMOs which associate with the ER membrane.\n\nCLINICAL SIGNIFICANCE: ClinVar reports 28 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including nonsense mutations (p.Leu188Ter truncating the FAD-binding domain), large copy number gains (1q21.1-25.3, 1q21.1-44), and losses (1q23.3-25.1, 1q24.2-31.1). While specific monogenic diseases are not yet annotated, recent literature identifies FMO4 as a driver of lung adenocarcinoma.\n\nFUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: Recent literature (85 papers) reveals emerging oncogenic roles: (1) Lung cancer driver—FMO4 stabilizes the MAT2A/MAT2B complex, increasing S-adenosylmethionine (SAM) levels and suppressing ferroptosis, thereby promoting tumor growth (2025). (2) HCC immunometabolism—FMO4 shapes immuno-metabolic reconfiguration in hepatocellular carcinoma, influencing checkpoint blockade response. (3) Septic CRC prognosis—FMO4 in CD4+ naive cells predicts poor outcomes in septic colorectal cancer patients. Mechanistically, FMO4 utilizes FAD and NADPH to oxidize soft nucleophiles (nitrogen, sulfur, phosphorus) in xenobiotics and endogenous metabolites. In cancer, non-canonical substrate oxidation (potentially methionine metabolites or lipid peroxides) stabilizes MAT complexes, elevating SAM to inhibit ferroptotic lipid peroxidation. Loss-of-function would impair detoxification; gain-of-function drives metabolic reprogramming favoring tumor survival.
