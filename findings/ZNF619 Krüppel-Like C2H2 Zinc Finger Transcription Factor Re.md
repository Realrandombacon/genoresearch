## Description
ZNF619 (Gene ID: 79848, UniProt Q8N2I2) is a 560 amino acid Krüppel-like transcription factor located on chromosome 3p22.1 (NC_000003.12). InterPro domain analysis reveals a canonical array of three tandem C2H2-type zinc finger domains (IPR013087/PF00096/PS00028, positions ~188-271) responsible for sequence-specific DNA binding, classified within the broader Krüppel-like transcription factor family (PTHR23235, positions 105-272). The structural superfamily matches (G3DSA:3.30.160.60, SSF57667) confirm the classic beta-beta-alpha zinc finger fold essential for recognizing specific DNA motifs. AlphaFold structure prediction (AF-Q8N2I2-F1) yields a medium-confidence model (global pLDDT 60.4), reflecting a modular architecture where the structured zinc finger cluster (likely pLDDT >80 locally) is connected by flexible, potentially intrinsically disordered linkers to other regions, a common feature in transcriptional regulators allowing dynamic chromatin engagement. Human Protein Atlas classifies ZNF619 as a predicted intracellular transcription factor involved in transcription and its regulation, with DNA-binding molecular function. Subcellular localization includes the nucleoplasm and intermediate filaments, suggesting potential roles in nuclear architecture or cytoskeletal-nuclear crosstalk. Expression is ubiquitous (detected in all tissues) with low tissue and cancer specificity, but cell type-enhanced single-cell specificity indicates regulated expression in specific lineages. STRING database identifies 7 interaction partners centered on chromatin modulation: TRIM28 (score 0.515, KAP1 co-repressor critical for KRAB-ZFP mediated silencing), CNKSR3 (0.478, scaffold protein), DTWD2 (0.443, DTW domain-containing), PRDM15 (0.437, histone methyltransferase), ZSCAN22 (0.437, paralogous zinc finger), PBX4 (0.424, homeobox transcription factor), and ZNF839 (0.406, another zinc finger protein). The strong link to TRIM28 strongly suggests ZNF619 functions as a transcriptional repressor recruiting the KAP1/SETDB1 complex to deposit H3K9me3 marks and silence target genes (e.g., retrotransposons, developmental regulators). Interaction with PRDM15 hints at potential cooperation in establishing bivalent chromatin domains or regulating pluripotency factors. ClinVar lists 9 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains spanning 3p26-22 (multi-megabase regions affecting many genes), plus one 3' UTR variant (c.*423A>G); no specific monogenic disease is yet attributed solely to ZNF619, but dosage sensitivity of the 3p region is linked to developmental delays and intellectual disability. Conservation is implied by the presence of orthologs in primates and mammals (Bos taurus Q1LZ98). Functional hypothesis: ZNF619 acts as a sequence-specific transcriptional repressor silencing distinct sets of target genes during development and homeostasis. By recruiting TRIM28, it establishes heterochromatin to prevent inappropriate gene expression. The interaction with PBX4 suggests potential cross-talk with HOX gene regulatory networks. Disease mechanism: Haploinsufficiency or overexpression due to 3p CNVs disrupts the balance of repressive chromatin marks, leading to derepression of target genes involved in neurodevelopment. The 3' UTR variant may affect mRNA stability. Priority experiments include ChIP-seq to define genomic binding sites, SELEX for motif determination, co-IP validation of TRIM28/PRDM15 interactions, and CRISPR knockout to assess effects on differentiation and retrotransposon silencing.
