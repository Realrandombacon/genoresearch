Functional hypothesis: LOC105376360 likely represents a critical regulatory element within the 10p15.2 dosage-sensitive region, potentially functioning as a tumor suppressor enhancer coordinating KLF6 expression in epithelial tissues (cell cycle arrest, p21 induction) or as a chromatin boundary separating metabolic genes (PFKP) from mitochondrial protease genes (PITRM1). Disease mechanism involves KLF6 haploinsufficiency driving oncogenesis through loss of cell cycle control and p21 suppression, PFKP dysregulation altering glycolytic flux and Warburg metabolism in tumors, PITRM1 deficiency impairing mitochondrial protein quality control and amyloid-beta clearance (Alzheimer pathogenesis), and non-coding RNA disruption affecting ribosomal modification and antisense regulation. The 10p15.2 deletion syndrome (Ohdo-like features, developmental delay, cardiac defects) and duplication syndrome may present with cancer predisposition, metabolic reprogramming, neurodegeneration, and developmental anomalies. The absence of protein-coding features combined with ClinVar evidence (12 CNVs ranging 6-26 Mb) and KLF6-PFKP-PITRM1 gene cluster suggests this locus functions as a tissue-specific super-enhancer or topologically associating domain (TAD) boundary critical for tumor suppression-glycolysis-mitochondrial homeostasis coordination.
