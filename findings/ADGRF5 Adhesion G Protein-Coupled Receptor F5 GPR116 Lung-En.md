## Description
ADGRF5 encodes a 1346 aa type I transmembrane adhesion G protein-coupled receptor (UniProt Q8IZF2), also known as GPR116, that functions as a critical regulator of pulmonary surfactant homeostasis, glomerular filtration barrier integrity, and metabolic signaling through its unique multi-domain architecture combining extracellular adhesion modules with 7-transmembrane GPCR signaling core. This moderately studied gene (63 publications) has emerging clinical significance for breast cancer metastasis, pancreatic islet function, brown adipose thermogenesis, and cardiac arrhythmia susceptibility. The protein features complex adhesion GPCR architecture: N-terminal SEA domain (IPR000082/G3DSA:3.30.70.960, residues 166-273/169-266) mediating autoproteolytic cleavage; GPS motif (IPR000203, residues 850-1002) enabling receptor activation through tethered agonist mechanism; multiple immunoglobulin-like domains (IPR003598/IPR003599/IPR007110/G3DSA:2.60.40.10, residues 278-368/477-563/284-357/483-552) providing protein-protein interaction surfaces; class B2 secretin-like GPCR 7TM domain (IPR000832/G3DSA:1.20.1070.10/cd15254, residues 1012-1269/1010-1303/1009-1283) mediating G protein coupling; classified within immunoglobulin-repeat-containing receptor Ig-hepta/GPR116 family with GO terms for G protein-coupled receptor activity and signaling pathway. STRING interactions reveal ADGRF5 operates within moderate-confidence endothelial and lung networks: SFTPC (0.822), surfactant protein C; SFTPB (0.803), surfactant protein B; PTPRB (0.681), protein tyrosine phosphatase receptor type B; CDH5 (0.641), cadherin-5/VE-cadherin; FNDC4 (0.633), fibronectin type III domain-containing protein 4; EMCN (0.613), endomucin; MMRN2 (0.581), multimerin 2; ADGRA1 (0.572), adhesion GPCR A1; ROBO4 (0.568), roundabout homolog 4; RAMP2 (0.421), receptor activity-modifying protein 2. HPA confirms tissue-enhanced expression with lung enrichment (99.9 nTPM), detected in many tissues, group enriched cancer specificity, cell type enhanced, classified as G-protein coupled receptor/plasma protein/membrane receptor/transducer. ClinVar lists 7 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants dominated by large-scale copy number alterations across 6p region (6p21.1-q12 43.6-64.9Mb x3 gain, 6p21.1-12.3 43.6-50.9Mb loss, 6p22.1-q14.1 29.5-81.4Mb gain, 6p25.3-q27 0.16-170.9Mb gain x2, 6p21.1-12.1 45.7-54.2Mb loss, 6p21.1-12.3 45.0-48.0Mb x3 gain), demonstrating strong dosage sensitivity though specific monogenic diseases remain undefined beyond susceptibility to pulmonary and renal phenotypes. AlphaFold structure prediction yields high confidence (global pLDDT 74.5), revealing a well-folded multi-domain receptor with properly positioned SEA, Ig, GPS, and 7TM domains. Mechanistically, ADGRF5 operates through integrated modes: (1) Autoproteolytic activation—SEA domain and GPS motif enable self-cleavage generating N-terminal fragment (NTF) and C-terminal fragment (CTF) that remain non-covalently associated until mechanical force triggers tethered agonist exposure; (2) Pulmonary surfactant regulation—2024 studies demonstrate ADGRF5 essential for surfactant secretion in alveolar type II cells, with deletion causing surfactant accumulation and respiratory distress; (3) Glomerular filtration—2024 study (5 citations) shows endothelial ADGRF5 deletion disrupts glomerular filtration barrier integrity causing proteinuria; (4) Breast cancer metastasis—2024 study (15 citations) reveals ADGRF5 fuels breast cancer progression by suppressing MMP8-mediated antitumorigenic effects; (5) Pancreatic islet function—2024 study (8 citations) demonstrates dual role regulating somatostatin release and islet development; (6) Brown adipose thermogenesis—2025 study shows endothelial ADGRF5 deletion promotes fibro-inflammatory EndMT impairing adaptive thermogenesis; (7) Cardiac stress response—2024 study (2 citations) links cardiomyocyte ADGRF5 deletion to increased stress susceptibility and arrhythmia; (8) Gαs-cAMP signaling—class B2 GPCR architecture suggests coupling to Gαs stimulating adenylate cyclase. Loss-of-function causes pulmonary surfactant accumulation, glomerular barrier defects, impaired thermogenesis, cardiac arrhythmia, while overexpression drives cancer metastasis through MMP8 suppression.
