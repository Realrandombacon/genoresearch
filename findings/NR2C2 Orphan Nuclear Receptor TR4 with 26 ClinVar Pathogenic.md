## Description
NR2C2 encodes a 596 amino acid nuclear receptor subfamily 2 group C member 2 (UniProt P49116), also known as testicular receptor 4 (TR4), an orphan nuclear receptor that functions as a ligand-independent transcription factor regulating gene expression through direct DNA binding and chromatin remodeling. The protein contains canonical nuclear receptor architecture: an N-terminal activation function domain, a central DNA-binding domain with two C4-type zinc fingers (IPR001628/PF00104 at positions 114-189; cd06967 at positions 112-198) that recognize AGGTCA direct repeat response elements, and a C-terminal ligand-binding domain (IPR000536/PF00105 at positions 341-583; cd06952 at positions 361-582) that mediates dimerization and co-regulator recruitment despite lacking a known endogenous ligand. The LBD adopts a retinoid X receptor-like fold (G3DSA:1.10.565.10 at positions 348-596) characteristic of nuclear hormone receptors.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals NR2C2 forms a transcriptional regulatory network with chromatin modifiers and signaling adaptors: JAZF1 (score 0.988—JAZF zinc finger 1, prostate cancer translocation partner), NR2C2AP (0.893—NR2C2-associated protein, specific cofactor), TAB2 (0.854—TGF-beta activated kinase 1 binding protein 2, NF-κB signaling), DNMT1 (0.819—DNA methyltransferase 1, epigenetic silencing), KDM1A/LSD1 (0.771—lysine-specific demethylase 1, histone H3K4 demethylation), HDAC1 (0.728—histone deacetylase 1, transcriptional repression), TRAF6 (0.721—TNF receptor-associated factor 6, NF-κB activation), NR2C1/TR2 (0.642—paralog, heterodimerization partner), NCOR1 (0.570—nuclear receptor corepressor 1), and TRIM28/KAP1 (0.561—transcriptional intermediary factor 1β, heterochromatin formation). This positions NR2C2 as a hub connecting transcriptional regulation, epigenetic modification, and inflammatory signaling.\n\nHPA expression data classifies NR2C2 as detected in all tissues with low tissue specificity and cell type-enhanced expression patterns. Subcellular localization is nucleoplasmic, consistent with transcription factor function. Biological processes include differentiation, spermatogenesis, transcription, and transcription regulation, with molecular functions as activator, DNA-binding, receptor, and repressor.\n\nClinVar documents 26 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number gains (3p26.3-14.3 duplications spanning 53 Mb; 3p26.3-22.3 gains), duplications (single allele), deletions (single allele), intronic/splice region variants (NM_001291694.2:c.376+237G>A; c.274-3C>G; c.72+882C>T), and complex rearrangements. This suggests NR2C2 dysfunction may cause undiagnosed male infertility (given spermatogenesis annotation and 2025 Leydig cell senescence data), developmental disorders, metabolic syndromes (orphan receptors often regulate metabolism), or cancer predisposition (epigenetic regulator interactions).\n\nAlphaFold structure prediction (AF-P49116-F1) yields a global pLDDT score of 65.3 (medium confidence) across all 596 residues, indicating a partially structured protein with well-folded DNA-binding and ligand-binding domains connected by flexible linker regions—a common architecture for transcription factors requiring conformational plasticity for co-regulator exchange.\n\nLiterature analysis (81 papers) reveals emerging functional understanding with critical disease gaps. Recent 2025 studies demonstrate NR2C2 deficiency accelerates testicular Leydig cell senescence and causes male infertility in mice, establishes novel FOXA1-NR2C2 interaction in ER+ breast cancer chromatin regulation, identifies placental miR-181a-5p targeting of NR2C2 in pregnancy-associated breast cancer, and shows macrophage NR2C2 promotes inflammation via NF-κB in LPS-induced models. Earlier work defines roles in prostate cancer progression, metabolic syndrome, and as a JAZF1-NR2C2 fusion oncogene in endometrial stromal sarcoma (t(7;3)(p15;q13)). However, no Mendelian disease phenotype has been defined despite 26 pathogenic variants. Key unknowns include: specific human disease phenotypes from germline loss-of-function mutations, contribution to male infertility syndromes, role in metabolic disorders (orphan receptor ligand identity remains unknown), therapeutic potential for cancers with NR2C2-dependent epigenetic dysregulation, and whether JAZF1-NR2C2 fusion occurs in germline.
