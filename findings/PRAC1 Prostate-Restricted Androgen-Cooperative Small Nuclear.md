# PRAC1: Prostate-Restricted Androgen-Cooperative Small Nuclear Protein Regulating Epithelial Stem Cell Self-Renewal and LSD1-Mediated Proliferation in Castration-Resistant Prostate Cancer

## Description
PRAC1 (Prostate Cancer Associated Protein 1) is a partially characterized small nuclear protein with 40 publications, emerging as a critical regulator of prostate epithelial stem cell maintenance and a driver of castration-resistant prostate cancer (CRPC). This tiny 57-amino acid protein (UniProt Q96KF2) lacks defined Pfam domains but contains a conserved nuclear localization signal and potential phosphorylation sites. AlphaFold predicts a medium-confidence structure (pLDDT 59.4), suggesting a compact, potentially disordered peptide that functions via short linear motifs rather than a stable globular fold.\n\nSTRING interactions reveal PRAC1 operates within a highly specific prostate transcriptional network: PRAC2 (0.990, its paralog with redundant function), HOXB13 (0.988, master regulator of prostate development and CRPC driver), ZNF540 (0.449, zinc finger repressor), FAM78A (0.432), CDC42 (0.430, Rho GTPase controlling cytoskeleton), and NCF2 (0.424). The exceptionally high confidence link to HOXB13 positions PRAC1 as a direct co-factor or downstream effector of the homeodomain transcription factor essential for prostate identity. Recent studies identify PRAC1 as a target of LSD1 (lysine-specific demethylase 1), which upregulates PRAC1 expression to promote cell proliferation.\n\nHPA shows group-enriched expression restricted to the intestine (192.7 nTPM) and prostate (103.8 nTPM), with cancer-enriched expression patterns in prostate tumors. Subcellular localization includes the nucleoplasm and cytosol. Biological processes involve stem cell self-renewal and androgen receptor biology. ClinVar contains 11 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number losses and gains on chromosome 17q12-25, though specific monogenic diseases remain uncharacterized, suggesting dosage sensitivity may underlie developmental defects or cancer susceptibility. Conservation is limited to primates, indicating a recently evolved function specific to higher mammalian reproductive biology.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: PRAC1 functions as an intrinsically disordered transcriptional co-activator that bridges the Androgen Receptor (AR) and HOXB13 complexes to maintain the progenitor state of prostate epithelial cells. In normal physiology, PRAC1 expression is tightly controlled by androgens to balance stem cell self-renewal and differentiation. In CRPC, epigenetic silencing mechanisms fail or are hijacked; specifically, LSD1-mediated demethylation at the PRAC1 locus drives its overexpression, which in turn hyper-activates HOXB13 target genes promoting uncontrolled proliferation and therapy resistance. The physical interaction with CDC42 suggests a non-canonical role in coupling nuclear transcriptional programs to cytoskeletal remodeling during cell division. The paralogous relationship with PRAC2 implies functional redundancy, where loss of one may be compensated by the other, explaining the lack of severe monogenic phenotypes in ClinVar despite large CNVs. Forensic applications leverage its extreme tissue specificity (prostate/rectum) as a biomarker for semen identification.
