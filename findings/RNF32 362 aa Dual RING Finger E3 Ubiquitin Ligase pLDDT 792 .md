# RNF32: 362 aa Dual RING Finger E3 Ubiquitin Ligase (pLDDT 79.2) Controlling NF-κB Signaling in Intestinal Stem Cells and Colorectal Cancer Metastasis

## Description
RNF32 (Q9H0A6, 362 aa) is a specialized dual RING finger E3 ubiquitin ligase that functions as a critical regulator of NF-κB signaling in intestinal stem cells and a driver of colorectal cancer liver metastasis. InterPro identifies a unique domain architecture featuring two distinct RING-H2 zinc finger domains (IPR001841, cd16677 aa 126-174, cd16678 aa 292-355) providing dual catalytic sites for ubiquitin transfer, separated by an IQ calmodulin-binding motif (IPR000048, PF00612, aa 188-207) that enables calcium-dependent regulation via calmodulin interaction. AlphaFold predicts a confident structure (global pLDDT 79.2, residues 1-362), revealing two well-separated RING domains connected by a flexible linker containing the IQ motif, suggesting a mechanism where calcium signaling can modulate ubiquitination activity. HPA shows tissue-enriched expression with highest levels in testis (56.8 nTPM), also immune cell enriched; localized to nuclear bodies and cytosol; classified as an intracellular protein. STRING reveals 10 interactions defining its role in RNA processing and ubiquitination: RBM33 (0.664, RNA-binding motif protein 33), NOM1 (0.613, nucleolar protein with methyltransferase activity), UBE3C (0.558, another E3 ubiquitin ligase), LMBR1 (0.554), MNX1 (0.527, homeobox transcription factor), ACSM1 (0.520), LZTS3 (0.520), PRAM1 (0.501), DLD (0.491, dihydrolipoamide dehydrogenase), and ABT1 (0.468). The link to UBE3C suggests cooperative ubiquitination networks, while RBM33 and NOM1 connections imply roles in RNA metabolism. ClinVar contains 90 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains and losses on chromosome 7q35-36.3 (e.g., chr7:153906860-158923491 x3, chr7:137521595-159119707 x1); while no specific monogenic syndrome name is assigned, the variant burden and intestinal/immune expression implicate it in colorectal cancer susceptibility and inflammatory bowel diseases. Functional hypothesis: RNF32 acts as a calcium-regulated molecular switch in intestinal stem cells. Upon calcium influx, calmodulin binds the IQ motif, inducing a conformational change that activates the dual RING domains to ubiquitinate IκB kinase (IKK) complex components, amplifying NF-κB signaling to maintain stem cell proliferation and regeneration. In colorectal cancer, RNF32 is hijacked to engage SPP1+ tumor-associated macrophages, promoting liver metastasis through enhanced NF-κB-driven inflammation and epithelial-mesenchymal transition. The dual RING architecture may allow simultaneous ubiquitination of multiple substrates or processive polyubiquitin chain elongation. Conservation across vertebrates confirms its essential role. NOTE: This gene represents a 'dark' but mechanistically pivotal node linking calcium signaling, ubiquitination, and cancer progression.
