## Description
PRPS2 (Gene ID 5634, UniProt P11908) encodes a 318-amino acid phosphoribosyl pyrophosphate synthetase 2 on chromosome Xp22.2, representing a well-characterized metabolic enzyme that catalyzes the synthesis of 5-phosphoribosyl 1-pyrophosphate (PRPP) from ATP and D-ribose 5-phosphate — a central committed step in de novo purine and pyrimidine nucleotide biosynthesis. Structurally, InterPro identifies multiple conserved domains defining its enzymatic function: (1) Phosphoribosyltransferase domain (IPR000836, residues 147-274; cd06223 PRT-type I domain, residues 147-274) forming the catalytic core for substrate binding and catalysis; (2) Phosphoribosyl pyrophosphate synthetase conserved site (IPR000842, residues 128-143) containing critical catalytic residues with GO annotations for magnesium ion binding (GO:0000287), ribose phosphate diphosphokinase activity (GO:0004749), and ribonucleoside monophosphate biosynthetic process (GO:0009156); (3) Ribose-phosphate pyrophosphokinase family (IPR005946, residues 1-316; IPR037515 bacterial-type, residues 4-314; MF_00583_B HAMAP family, residues 4-314) with GO terms for magnesium ion binding and nucleotide biosynthetic process (GO:0009165); (4) N-terminal domain (IPR029099, residues 4-120) involved in allosteric regulation and subunit interactions; (5) Phosphoribosyltransferase-like superfamily (IPR029057, residues 6-306; G3DSA:3.40.50.2020, residues 6-146/147-290/291-305); (6) NF002320 ribose-phosphate diphosphokinase family. AlphaFold predicts very high-confidence structure (global pLDDT 95.3, modeled residues 1-318), indicating exceptionally well-folded enzyme with stable catalytic architecture typical of type I PRTases — this confidence level supports reliable structural modeling of active site geometry, ATP-binding pocket, and allosteric regulatory sites. Human Protein Atlas shows tissue-enhanced RNA expression detected in all tissues with highest levels in parathyroid gland (171.4 nTPM) and ovary (141.8 nTPM), nuclear speckles/vesicles/basal body subcellular localization, classification as enzyme/metabolic protein/predicted intracellular protein, involvement in nucleotide biosynthesis biological process, and kinase/transferase molecular functions. ClinVar documents 167 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants consisting primarily of large-scale copy number alterations spanning X chromosome regions (deletions: chrX:168546-47834686 x1, chrX:2631638-25008584 x1, chrX:168547-29117749 x1; duplications/gains: chrX:9415628-17605610 x3, chrX:251888-42476276 x2, chrX:6446580-24953919 x2, chrX:2757837-156030895 x2) plus gene-specific variants (splice site NM_002765.5:c.531-6del) — though specific diseases not yet assigned to PRPS2 alone, the paralog PRPS1 causes well-characterized X-linked disorders (Arts syndrome, Charcot-Marie-Tooth neuropathy type 5, PRPS superactivity) suggesting potential overlapping phenotypes. The mechanistic hypothesis: PRPS2 functions as an essential regulator of nucleotide homeostasis through: (1) In normal physiology — forms homohexameric enzyme complex (characteristic of type I PRPS enzymes) with each subunit containing N-terminal regulatory domain and C-terminal catalytic domain; catalyzes Mg2+-dependent transfer of pyrophosphate from ATP to ribose-5-phosphate generating PRPP, the universal precursor for purine/pyrimidine synthesis, NAD biosynthesis, and histidine biosynthesis; tissue-enhanced expression in parathyroid gland (171.4 nTPM) suggests roles in PTH synthesis/secretion requiring rapid nucleotide turnover; ovarian expression (141.8 nTPM) indicates importance in oocyte maturation and follicular development; nuclear speckle localization suggests potential roles in transcription-coupled nucleotide supply for RNA synthesis; basal body localization hints at ciliary function requiring GTP for dynein motor activity; interaction with pentose phosphate pathway enzymes (TKT, RPIA, RBKS) confirms integration with glucose metabolism providing ribose-5-phosphate substrate; (2) In disease — X-linked location and 167 ClinVar variants suggest potential for: (a) PRPS2 deficiency causing neurodevelopmental disorders analogous to PRPS1-related Arts syndrome (intellectual disability, ataxia, deafness, optic atrophy) through impaired purine synthesis affecting neuronal development and myelination; (b) PRPS2 superactivity causing gout and hyperuricemia via increased PRPP driving excessive purine synthesis and uric acid production; (c) CNVs spanning PRPS2 may cause contiguous gene syndromes with additional Xp22 features (ichthyosis, chondrodysplasia punctata, intellectual disability); (d) parathyroid-specific phenotypes potentially affecting calcium homeostasis given highest expression in this tissue; (e) female carrier manifestations due to X-inactivation mosaicism explaining variable expressivity; (3) High sequence/structural similarity to PRPS1 (~90% identity) suggests functional redundancy potentially buffering against loss-of-function, though subtle differences in allosteric regulation (ADP inhibition, Pi activation) may confer tissue-specific roles; (4) Well-characterized enzymatic mechanism but underexplored disease associations compared to PRPS1 represent knowledge gap requiring systematic variant functionalization. Future work should: characterize specific variant effects on enzyme kinetics via recombinant protein expression and activity assays; determine tissue-specific PRPS2 vs PRPS1 expression ratios across human tissues; assess PRPS2 contribution to Arts syndrome phenotypes in PRPS1-negative patients; investigate parathyroid-specific functions via conditional knockout models; evaluate PRPS2 roles in oocyte quality and fertility; test PRPS2 inhibitors as anticancer agents targeting nucleotide-starved tumors; determine crystal structures of disease-associated mutants; identify PRPS2-specific allosteric regulators distinct from PRPS1; explore PRPS2 contributions to cisplatin resistance through nucleotide pool modulation; assess PRPS2 expression changes during cell cycle and DNA damage response; investigate PRPS2 roles in antiviral immunity via nucleotide availability for viral replication; and screen for PRPS2 variants in undiagnosed X-linked neurodevelopmental disorder cohorts.
