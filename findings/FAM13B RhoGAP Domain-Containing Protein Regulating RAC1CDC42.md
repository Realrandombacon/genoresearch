## Description
FAM13B (Family with Sequence Similarity 13 Member B, UniProt Q9NYF5) is a 915-amino acid intracellular protein functioning as a Rho GTPase-activating protein (RhoGAP) that negatively regulates Rho-family small GTPases through catalytic acceleration of GTP hydrolysis. The protein contains a canonical RhoGAP domain (IPR000198/PF00620/SM00324, residues 23-212) belonging to the Rho GTPase activation protein superfamily (G3DSA:1.10.555.10/IPR008936/SSF48350, residues 1-220) that forms an all-helical bundle structure creating an arginine finger mechanism to stabilize the transition state during GTP hydrolysis on target GTPases. FAM13B belongs to the conserved FAM13 protein family (IPR039102/PTHR15904, residues 1-915) with a C-terminal FAM13A-like domain (IPR059029/PF26116, residues 840-909) of unknown function but potentially mediating protein-protein interactions or subcellular targeting. AlphaFold predicts a partially structured architecture (pLDDT=61.7, medium confidence) consistent with an ordered catalytic RhoGAP domain embedded within extended flexible regions enabling dynamic substrate recognition and regulatory complex assembly. HPA classifies FAM13B with GTPase activation molecular function, ubiquitous expression pattern (detected in all tissues with low tissue specificity), nucleoplasmic subcellular localization, and cell type-enhanced expression — suggesting fundamental roles in nuclear/cytoplasmic signaling across diverse cellular contexts. STRING interaction network reveals specific associations with Rho-family GTPase regulators: RAC1 (0.609, Ras-related C3 botulinum toxin substrate 1 controlling actin cytoskeleton and cell migration), CDC42 (0.603, cell division control protein 42 regulating polarity and vesicle trafficking), RASA1 (0.602, Ras p21 protein activator 1/GAP1(p120) accelerating Ras GTP hydrolysis), NME5 (0.602, nucleoside diphosphate kinase 5 involved in GTP metabolism), PKD2L2 (0.551, polycystin-2-like protein 2, ciliary calcium channel), RETREG1 (0.518, reticulophagy regulator 1/fam134b mediating ER-phagy), and SH3PXD2A (0.503, SH3 and PX domain-containing protein 2A/podosome scaffold). This interaction profile places FAM13B within Rho GTPase signaling networks where it likely functions as a negative regulator converting active GTP-bound RAC1/CDC42 to inactive GDP-bound states, thereby controlling actin cytoskeletal dynamics, cell polarity, membrane trafficking, and potentially ciliary function. ClinVar documents 19 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large chromosomal deletions and duplications spanning 5q31.1-31.2 encompassing the FAM13B locus, indicating dosage sensitivity where haploinsufficiency or triplosensitivity may contribute to developmental disorders. The combination of RhoGAP catalytic domain, RAC1/CDC42 interaction partners, ubiquitous expression, nucleoplasmic localization, and CNV-associated pathology supports a mechanistic model: FAM13B functions as a context-dependent RhoGAP that fine-tunes Rho-family GTPase signaling by accelerating GTP hydrolysis on RAC1 and CDC42, balancing active versus inactive GTPase pools to regulate cytoskeletal organization, cell migration, polarity establishment, and nuclear signaling events, with dosage disruption causing disease through dysregulated GTPase signaling cascades affecting development and tissue homeostasis.
