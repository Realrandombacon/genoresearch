## Description
MAP6D1 (MAP6 domain containing 1, also known as SL21 or MAPO6D1) is a 199 amino acid intracellular protein localized to the cytosol and Golgi membranes (UniProt Q9H9H5) with medium-confidence partially disordered architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 56.0). The protein contains the canonical Microtubule-associated protein 6 family domain (IPR007882/PTHR14759, residues 1-173) forming a ~173 aa core module (~87%) characteristic of STOP (Stable Tubule Only Polypeptide) proteins. This domain mediates calmodulin binding (GO:0005516) and microtubule binding (GO:0008017), enabling MAP6D1 to function as a calmodulin-regulated neuronal protein that binds and stabilizes microtubules against depolymerization, particularly under cold stress or calcium flux. The N-terminal region likely undergoes palmitoylation, facilitating association with Golgi membranes and linking microtubule dynamics to vesicular trafficking. Molecular functions include microtubule cytoskeleton organization (GO:0000226), essential for maintaining neuronal architecture and axonal transport.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places MAP6D1 within a focused neuronal cytoskeleton and calcium signaling network: strongest interactions with SAXO1 (0.633, a microtubule-stabilizing protein involved in ciliogenesis and neuronal development) and CALM3 (0.631, Calmodulin-3, the primary calcium sensor). High confidence links extend to other calmodulin-like proteins (CALML3, CALML4, CALML5, CALML6), confirming its role as a calcium-dependent regulator. Connections to PODXL2 (0.554, Podocalyxin-like protein 2) suggest involvement in cell adhesion or filtration barriers. COMTD1 (0.514) and PAXBP1 (0.502) links hint at roles in methylation or transcriptional regulation, while CAMK1G (0.439, Calcium/calmodulin-dependent protein kinase IG) suggests a feedback loop where MAP6D1 recruits kinases to phosphorylate downstream targets upon calcium influx.\n\nHPA expression reveals a tissue-enriched pattern with exceptional specificity to the brain (120.4 nTPM), detected in many tissues but highest in neurons. The 'cancer enhanced' status suggests upregulation in tumors, possibly reflecting re-activation of developmental programs or altered cytoskeletal dynamics in malignancy. Subcellular location in the cytosol and Golgi supports its dual role in structural support and trafficking.\n\nClinVar documents 39 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains and losses affecting the 3q26.3-3q29 region: CNV gains (3q22.1-29, 3q26.31-29 duplications) and CNV losses (3q27.1-27.2, 3q26.33-27.2 deletions). The high variant burden indicates strong dosage sensitivity. Haploinsufficiency would likely destabilize neuronal microtubules, causing axonal transport defects, neurodegeneration, or developmental delay, while duplications may cause hyper-stabilization of microtubules, impairing plasticity and contributing to 3q deletion/duplication syndrome phenotypes (intellectual disability, growth retardation, facial dysmorphism).\n\nConservation is strong across mammals: human (Q9H9H5, 199 aa), mouse (Q14BB9, 191 aa), cow (Q0P591, 192 aa), indicating evolutionary pressure to maintain the STOP-like domain.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: MAP6D1 acts as a calcium-sensitive microtubule stabilizer in neurons. Under basal conditions, it binds microtubules to prevent depolymerization. Upon calcium influx (via neuronal activity), calmodulin (CALM3) binds MAP6D1, potentially inducing a conformational change that modulates its stability or recruits CAMK1G to phosphorylate associated proteins. This dynamic regulation allows neurons to balance structural integrity with plasticity. The Golgi localization via palmitoylation suggests it also stabilizes microtubules emanating from the Golgi, crucial for polarized secretion in axons. Dysregulation via 3q CNVs disrupts this balance: loss causes microtubule collapse and transport failure (neurodevelopmental disorders), while gain causes rigid cytoskeletons resistant to remodeling, contributing to cognitive deficits in 3q syndromes.
