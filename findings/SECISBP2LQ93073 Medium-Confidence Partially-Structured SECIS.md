## Description
SECISBP2L (SECIS binding protein 2-like, also known as SBP2L, SLAN) is a 1101 amino acid nucleoplasmic RNA-binding protein (UniProt Q93073) with medium-confidence partially-structured architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 52.9), characteristic of large ribonucleoprotein complex components that contain both ordered RNA-binding domains and intrinsically disordered regions for dynamic protein-protein interactions. The protein contains a selenocysteine insertion sequence-binding protein 2 family domain (IPR040051/PTHR13284, residues 7-990) enabling SECIS RNA binding activity (GO:0035368), a ribosomal protein eL8/eL30/eS12/Gadd45 domain (IPR004038/PF01248, residues 704-801) involved in RNA recognition and ribonucleoprotein assembly, ribosomal protein eL30-like superfamily domains (IPR029064/G3DSA:3.30.1330.30/SSF55315, residues 705-813, 708-805) that form the RNA-binding core. SECISBP2L is located on chromosome 15q21.1 and is classified as a predicted intracellular protein with subcellular localization to nucleoplasm, enabling RNA binding activity and predicted to be part of ribonucleoprotein complexes.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals a network of ribonucleoprotein, stress response, and immune regulatory proteins: COPS2 (0.582, COP9 signalosome subunit 2, transcriptional regulation), HSPA5 (0.539, Heat shock 70kDa protein 5/GRP78, ER stress response), ANKRD45 (0.535, Ankyrin repeat domain-containing protein 45, unknown function), CD1C (0.517, CD1c antigen, lipid antigen presentation), EEFSEC (0.511, Elongation factor for selenocysteine incorporation, selenoprotein synthesis), ATG2B (0.510, Autophagy related 2B, autophagosome formation), FCGR3A (0.506, Fc gamma receptor IIIa, immune complex binding), FCGR3B (0.505, Fc gamma receptor IIIb, neutrophil activation), REV3L (0.499, DNA polymerase zeta catalytic subunit, translesion synthesis), and NSL1 (0.428, NDS1 non-steroidal alpha-methylglucocorticoid receptor ligand-binding region-like 1, kinetochore assembly). These interactions confirm SECISBP2L functions within RNA processing and selenoprotein synthesis pathways, with EEFSEC as a key partner linking SECISBP2L to selenocysteine incorporation machinery.\n\nHPA expression reveals SECISBP2L has low tissue specificity and is detected in all tissues surveyed, indicating ubiquitous expression consistent with housekeeping roles in selenoprotein synthesis and RNA metabolism. Subcellular localization to nucleoplasm suggests SECISBP2L functions in nuclear RNA processing or ribonucleoprotein assembly. Single cell type specificity shows cell type enriched expression, suggesting elevated levels in specific cell populations. The protein is classified as a predicted intracellular protein involved in RNA binding.\n\nClinVar documents 22 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including intronic variants (c.919+173G>T), single allele deletions, and extensive copy number losses (15q21.1-21.2, 15q15.2-21.2, 15q21.1-21.3, 15q11.2-21.2 deletions) and gains (15q11.2-21.2, 15q21.1-22.2 amplifications). The variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity. While no specific Mendelian disease is annotated, 15q CNVs are associated with developmental delay, growth abnormalities, and metabolic disorders. Given SECISBP2L's SECIS RNA binding function and EEFSEC interaction, loss-of-function may cause selenoprotein deficiency syndromes through impaired selenocysteine incorporation into selenoenzymes (glutathione peroxidases, thioredoxin reductases, deiodinases).\n\nConservation is present across vertebrates: human (Q93073, 1101 aa), mouse (Q6A098, 1086 aa), desert hamster (A0AAU9Z5E5, 1108 aa), lancelet (A0A8J9ZBD0, 1256 aa), indicating the SECISBP2L RNA-binding machinery is conserved in chordates with moderate length variation.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: SECISBP2L functions as an RNA-binding protein that recognizes SECIS (selenocysteine insertion sequence) elements in selenoprotein mRNAs, facilitating selenocysteine incorporation during translation. The medium-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 52.9) indicates a partially structured architecture with ordered RNA-binding domains (residues 704-813 L7Ae/L30e fold) embedded in disordered regions that enable dynamic ribonucleoprotein complex assembly. Through EEFSEC interaction (0.511), SECISBP2L may recruit the selenocysteine-specific elongation factor to SECIS-containing mRNAs. COPS2 interaction (0.582) links SECISBP2L to transcriptional regulation and signalosome function. HSPA5 interaction (0.539) connects SECISBP2L to ER stress response, potentially coordinating selenoprotein synthesis with cellular stress. ATG2B interaction (0.510) suggests roles in autophagy regulation through selenoprotein-dependent redox control. FCGR3A/FCGR3B interactions (0.506/0.505) indicate potential immune modulation functions. Ubiquitous expression (detected in all tissues) indicates SECISBP2L is essential for basal selenoprotein synthesis in all cell types. 15q CNVs cause SECISBP2L haploinsufficiency, potentially reducing selenoenzyme activity and causing oxidative stress, thyroid dysfunction (via deiodinase deficiency), and developmental abnormalities. SECISBP2L represents a candidate gene for selenoprotein deficiency disorders and may serve as a biomarker for impaired selenocysteine incorporation.
