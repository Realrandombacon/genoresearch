## Description
USP3 (Ubiquitin-Specific Protease 3; UniProt Q9Y6I4) is a 520 amino acid deubiquitinating enzyme with 136 publications, representing a moderately characterized DUB with emerging oncogenic roles in multiple cancer types through substrate stabilization and chromatin regulation.\n\nSTRUCTURAL ARCHITECTURE: InterPro analysis reveals canonical USP domain architecture with an N-terminal UBP-type zinc finger (IPR001607/PF02148/G3DSA:3.30.40.10, residues 1-121/1-122) that mediates protein-protein interactions and substrate recognition, plus a C-terminal peptidase C19/USP catalytic domain (IPR001394/IPR028889/PF00443/G3DSA:3.90.70.10, residues 159-508/159-511/155-517) containing the conserved catalytic triad (Cys-His-Asp) characteristic of cysteine-type deubiquitinases (GO:0004843). Two conserved USP signature motifs are present at residues 160-175 and 454-472 (IPR018200). The protein belongs to the ubiquitin carboxyl-terminal hydrolase family (IPR050185, residues 29-509) within the papain-like cysteine peptidase superfamily (IPR038765, residues 159-509). AlphaFold predicts high confidence structure (global pLDDT 78.7), indicating a well-folded two-domain architecture with the zinc finger positioned to orient ubiquitinated substrates into the catalytic cleft.\n\nINTERACTION NETWORK: STRING-DB identifies high-confidence interactions with ubiquitin and chromatin components: UBC (0.867, ubiquitin C, the cleaved product), core histones H2AC18 (0.764), H2AC20 (0.754), H2BC21 (0.734) (histone H2A/H2B variants), DDX58 (0.706, RIG-I, antiviral innate immunity receptor), ubiquitin fusion proteins UBA52 (0.705) and RPS27A (0.691), plus chromatin remodeling complex components TRRAP (0.675, transformation/transcription domain-associated protein, HAT scaffold), TADA3 (0.610, transcriptional adaptor 3, SAGA/ATAC complex), and TAF10 (0.580, TBP-associated factor 10, TFIID complex). This interaction profile establishes USP3 as a chromatin-associated DUB that deubiquitinates histone H2A/H2B to regulate transcription, while also processing free ubiquitin chains and modulating innate immune signaling via RIG-I.\n\nEXPRESSION & LOCALIZATION: HPA shows ubiquitous expression detected in all tissues with low tissue and cancer specificity, consistent with fundamental requirements for ubiquitin homeostasis and chromatin regulation across cell types. Subcellular localization includes nucleoplasm (chromatin association for histone DUB activity) and midbody ring (cytokinesis completion). Molecular functions include chromatin regulation, hydrolase, protease, and thiol protease activities. Biological processes encompass cell cycle, DNA damage response, and Ubl conjugation pathway. Classified as an enzyme/metabolic protein with cell type-enhanced single cell specificity.\n\nDISEASE LANDSCAPE: ClinVar contains 11 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number losses at 15q21.3-22.31 and deletions encompassing USP3, plus copy number gains at 15q11.2-26.3, 15q15.1-26.3, and 15q22.1-26.3, plus the intronic variant c.534-200A>T. No monogenic disease is yet definitively assigned, but 15q CNVs cause developmental anomalies. Recent 2024-2025 literature establishes prominent oncogenic roles: USP3 promotes osteosarcoma progression via EPHA2 deubiquitination and PI3K/AKT pathway activation (2024, 31 citations); drives colorectal cancer liver metastasis through USP3-AS1 lncRNA-mediated glucose-driven histone lactylation (2025, 8 citations); triggers gallbladder carcinoma development/metastasis via DNM1L (dynamin-1-like protein) deubiquitination and mitochondrial fission (2025, 2 citations); promotes clear cell renal cell carcinoma by stabilizing MYC oncoprotein and enhancing glycolysis (2025, 2 citations); and stabilizes MIC19 (CHCHD3) under hypoxic stress to promote non-small cell lung cancer progression (2025, 2 citations).\n\nCONSERVATION: Orthologs confirmed in mouse (Q91W36, 520 aa) and rat, indicating strong evolutionary constraint on the zinc finger-catalytic domain modular architecture across mammals.\n\nFUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: USP3 functions as a dual-specificity deubiquitinase regulating both chromatin dynamics and oncoprotein stability. In the nucleus, USP3 binds monoubiquitinated histone H2A (H2AK119ub) and H2B (H2BK120ub) via its zinc finger domain, positioning these substrates into the catalytic cleft for deubiquitination—this reverses Polycomb-mediated repression (H2AK119ub removal) and regulates transcription elongation (H2BK120ub removal). The TRRAP-TADA3-TAF10 interactions position USP3 within SAGA/ATAC and TFIID complexes, coupling histone DUB activity to transcriptional activation. In cytokinesis, midbody ring localization suggests USP3 clears ubiquitin marks to enable abscission. In cancer, USP3 is hijacked to stabilize oncoproteins: EPHA2 receptor tyrosine kinase (osteosarcoma), DNM1L mitochondrial fission GTPase (gallbladder carcinoma), MYC transcription factor (renal cell carcinoma), and MIC19 mitochondrial cristae organizer (lung cancer). By removing K48-linked polyubiquitin chains from these substrates, USP3 prevents proteasomal degradation, amplifying proliferative and metastatic signaling. The USP3-AS1 lncRNA axis suggests autoregulatory feedback controlling USP3 expression. Therapeutic targeting of USP3 catalytic activity could simultaneously destabilize multiple oncoproteins while restoring normal histone ubiquitination patterns.
