## Description
ZNF417 (575 aa, UniProt Q8TAU3) is a C2H2-type zinc finger protein functioning as a sequence-specific transcriptional repressor. Structural analysis reveals a canonical N-terminal KRAB domain (PF01352, residues 15-55; IPR001909) which recruits the corepressor KAP1/TRIM28 to silence target genes via histone methylation and heterochromatin formation. The C-terminal region contains multiple C2H2 zinc finger motifs (PF00096 at positions 240-262, 268-290, 296-318) arranged in tandem clusters, enabling specific DNA binding to promoter/enhancer regions. AlphaFold prediction (pLDDT 64.4) indicates a structured DNA-binding domain coupled with flexible linker regions typical of zinc finger arrays. HPA data confirms nuclear localization (nucleoplasm/nucleoli) with ubiquitous expression across tissues, consistent with a housekeeping regulatory role. STRING interactions identify high-confidence partners including TRIM28 (score 0.612), the essential cofactor for KRAB-mediated repression, and other zinc finger proteins (ZNF543, score 0.555). ClinVar reports 14 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number gains spanning 19q13.43, suggesting that ZNF417 dosage sensitivity contributes to genomic disorders, potentially through dysregulation of developmental or cell-cycle genes. Conservation analysis implies strong evolutionary pressure on the zinc finger cluster. Mechanistically, ZNF417 likely acts as a epigenetic silencer, recruiting the KAP1/SETDB1 complex to deposit H3K9me3 marks at specific genomic loci, thereby maintaining cellular identity or suppressing retrotransposons.
