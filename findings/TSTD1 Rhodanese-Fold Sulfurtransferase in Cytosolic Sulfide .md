# TSTD1: Rhodanese-Fold Sulfurtransferase in Cytosolic Sulfide Detoxification with Clinical CNVs

## Description
TSTD1 (Thiosulfate Sulfurtransferase Like Domain Containing 1, also known as KAT) is a compact 115 amino acid protein (UniProt Q8NFU3) with an exceptionally well-folded 3D structure (AlphaFold pLDDT 93.7, very high confidence). The protein contains a canonical rhodanese-like domain (IPR001763/PF00581, residues 7-115) with the characteristic G3DSA:3.40.250.10 fold and rhodanese domain profile (PS50206), placing it in the thiosulfate:glutathione sulfurtransferase family (IPR042457/PTHR45544).\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals TSTD1 functions in sulfur metabolism and mitochondrial translation: strongest interactions with MOCS2 (0.870), the molybdenum cofactor synthesis enzyme requiring sulfur insertion, plus DAP3 (0.844) and YBEY (0.836), both mitochondrial ribosome-associated factors. Additional connections to catalase CAT (0.820), ribosomal proteins RPS11 (0.807), MRPL21 (0.797), MRPL17 (0.782), and MOCOS (0.502) suggest roles in redox homeostasis and cofactor biosynthesis.\n\nHPA expression shows ubiquitous detection across all tissues with low tissue specificity, consistent with housekeeping sulfur metabolism functions. Subcellular localization to cytosol and cytoplasmic bodies indicates cytoplasmic sulfide detoxification activity.\n\nClinVar documents 13 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains and losses spanning chromosome 1q23 (e.g., chr1:158001058-162858285x1, chr1:143932350-249224684x3). While specific disease associations are not yet defined, these CNVs suggest TSTD1 dosage sensitivity, potentially causing metabolic disorders through impaired sulfide oxidation.\n\nConservation is evident from mouse ortholog (E9PY03, 133 aa) and chicken orthologs (A0A8V0XU65, 363 aa), indicating functional importance across vertebrates.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: TSTD1 acts as a cytosolic rhodanese-fold sulfurtransferase that catalyzes thiosulfate-glutathione sulfur transfer reactions, detoxifying hydrogen sulfide (H2S) produced endogenously or from environmental exposure. The enzyme likely accepts sulfur from thiosulfate via a persulfide intermediate at its active site cysteine, transferring it to glutathione for excretion. Disruption via CNVs causes sulfide accumulation, oxidative stress, and potential neurotoxicity given H2S effects on cytochrome c oxidase.
