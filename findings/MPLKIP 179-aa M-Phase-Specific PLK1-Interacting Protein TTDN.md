## Description
MPLKIP (UniProt Q8TAP9), or M-phase-specific PLK1-interacting protein (also known as TTDN1/trichothiodystrophy nonphotosensitive 1 or protein SICKLE), encodes a compact 179-amino acid intracellular protein belonging to the vertebrate-specific MPLKIP/TTDN1 family (IPR026618/IPR028265/PF15502/PTHR22446). The protein architecture features a C-terminal TTDN1/SICKLE domain (IPR028265/PF15502, aa 98-178) embedded within the broader MPLKIP family scaffold (IPR026618, aa 1-179), with no canonical enzymatic domains suggesting a scaffolding/regulatory role. AlphaFold predicts medium-confidence structure (global pLDDT: 56.3), indicating a partially folded protein with structured C-terminal region and intrinsically disordered N-terminal half—consistent with proteins that engage in dynamic protein-protein interactions and require conformational flexibility for multi-partner binding.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals MPLKIP's integration into transcription, splicing, and metabolic networks: SUGCT (0.986, succinyl-CoA:glutarate CoA-transferase), FAM3B (0.784, pancreatic beta-cell factor), GTF2H5 (0.734, general transcription factor IIH subunit 5/TTDA), FAM120C (0.719), DNAJC28 (0.717, DnaJ heat shock protein), RNF113A (0.712, RING finger E3 ligase), CWC15 (0.592, spliceosome component), SGCE (0.584, epsilon-sarcoglycan), ERCC3 (0.577, XPB helicase/nucleotide excision repair), and CTTNBP2 (0.576, cortactin-binding protein). This partner profile positions MPLKIP at the intersection of transcription-coupled repair (GTF2H5/ERCC3), spliceosome function (CWC15), and chromatin remodeling.\n\nWith only 20 papers, MPLKIP qualifies as a genuine dark gene with breakthrough disease mechanisms emerging in 2016-2023. Recent studies reveal: (1) MPLKIP mutations cause trichothiodystrophy nonphotosensitive type 4 (TTDN1)—a brittle hair syndrome with intellectual disability, ichthyosis, growth retardation, microcephaly, and hypergonadotropic hypogonadism; (2) MPLKIP maintains DBR1 (lariat debranching enzyme) protein levels, enabling proper clearance of intron lariats after splicing—loss causes lariat accumulation and ectodermal differentiation defects; (3) novel MPLKIP variants identified in Finnish patients with non-photosensitive TTD confirm genetic heterogeneity; (4) mitral regurgitation identified as phenotypic manifestation, expanding clinical spectrum; (5) homozygous G insertion causes TTDN1 with hypergonadotropic hypogonadism. HPA data confirms ubiquitous expression (detected in all tissues, low specificity) with nucleoplasmic, Golgi, and vesicular localization, classified in cell cycle/mitosis pathways and disease-related genes. ClinVar lists 52 pathogenic variants including frameshifts (p.Arg57fs, p.Gly76fs, p.Pro67fs, p.Ser133_Asn134insTer), nonsense mutations (p.Gln2Ter, p.Tyr90Ter), splice variants, and chromosome 7p14.2-13 copy number losses/gains, confirming extreme dosage sensitivity. We hypothesize MPLKIP functions as a molecular chaperone stabilizing DBR1 and coordinating spliceosome-transcription-repair coupling: the disordered N-terminal region binds DBR1 preventing proteasomal degradation, while the structured C-terminal TTDN1 domain recruits GTF2H5/ERCC3 (TFIIH complex) to sites of transcription stress—this explains the TTD phenotype since defective lariat clearance causes R-loop accumulation and transcription-blocking DNA damage specifically in highly transcribed ectodermal lineages (hair follicles, skin, neurons); the SUGCT interaction suggests metabolic coupling where acyl-CoA metabolism influences MPLKIP stability, potentially explaining variable penetrance.
