## Description
GTF2H2C_2 (GTF2H family member C, copy 2), also known as GTF2H2D, is a 395-amino acid predicted paralog of the canonical General Transcription Factor IIH subunit 2 (GTF2H2/p44). Despite having zero publications in Semantic Scholar, confirming its status as a genuine 'dark gene', structural and interaction data suggest it retains the core machinery of the transcription/repair factor TFIIH. InterPro analysis reveals a highly conserved architecture: an N-terminal von Willebrand factor type A (vWA) domain (IPR002035, residues 58-236) critical for protein-protein interactions within the complex, followed by a C-terminal C1-like zinc finger domain (IPR004595, residues 344-387) involved in DNA binding or stabilization. The AlphaFold model (AF-Q6P1K8-F1) predicts a well-folded, stable structure with a global pLDDT of 84.1, indicating that the protein is not a pseudogene but a potentially functional entity.\n\nFunctionally, GTF2H2C_2 is hypothesized to act as a specialized or tissue-specific modulator of the TFIIH complex, which drives both RNA Polymerase II transcription initiation and Nucleotide Excision Repair (NER). STRING interaction data places it in an extremely tight cluster with core TFIIH components, including GTF2H4 (XPB, score 0.999), GTF2H3 (p52, 0.999), ERCC3 (XPB, 0.996), and CDK7 (0.953). This high-confidence network suggests it competes with or complements the canonical GTF2H2 subunit. However, unlike its well-studied counterparts, ClinVar reports zero pathogenic variants for GTF2H2C_2, and HPA expression data is absent, implying either highly restricted expression (e.g., testis-specific or embryonic) or functional redundancy that masks phenotypic consequences upon mutation. The presence of intact domains and strong interactors, combined with a complete lack of clinical literature, positions GTF2H2C_2 as a compelling candidate for investigating 'shadow' paralogs that may fine-tune DNA repair fidelity in specific cellular contexts without causing overt disease when disrupted.
