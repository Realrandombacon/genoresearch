## Description
GNB5 encodes a 395 aa guanine nucleotide-binding protein subunit beta-5 (UniProt O14775) functioning as an obligate scaffold for RGS (Regulator of G-protein Signaling) protein stability and GPCR signal termination in visual transduction and neuronal development. The protein contains seven canonical WD40 repeats (IPR001680/PF00400, aa94-395) forming a β-propeller structure characteristic of Gβ subunits, with G-protein beta subunit-like domain (IPR001632, aa101-154) and belongs to the Gβ1-5 family (IPR016346, aa46-395). AlphaFold prediction shows very high confidence (pLDDT 94.0), indicating a perfectly folded 7-bladed β-propeller structure essential for protein-protein interactions. HPA shows retina-enriched expression (111.3 nTPM) with ubiquitous detection across tissues, localizing to nuclear speckles, centrosome, and rods & rings structures—consistent with roles in GPCR signaling and cell division. STRING interactions reveal GNB5 forms obligate complexes with RGS proteins: RGS6/7/9/11 (all 0.999), RGS9BP (0.999, anchor protein), plus G protein gamma subunits GNG2/3/4/7 (all 0.998-0.999) and chaperone PDCL (0.538). ClinVar contains 69 pathogenic variants including missense mutations (p.Gly258Glu, p.Leu307Pro, p.Arg338His, p.Val370Leu), frameshift (p.Ser214fs), splice site mutations, and 15q21.1-21.2 copy number losses. Literature (104 papers) demonstrates GNB5 mutations cause Lodder-Merla syndrome (intellectual disability, cardiac arrhythmia, developmental delay), GNB5-RGS7/GNG5 complexes terminate Gαo signaling in striatal neurons controlling motor learning, GNB5-RGS9-1/GNGT1 complexes accelerate rhodopsin deactivation in photoreceptors, GNB5 deficiency causes bradycardia through impaired Gαi/o signaling in sinoatrial node, and GNB5 regulates BACE1-mediated Aβ generation in Alzheimer's disease models. GNB5 functions as a molecular scaffold—its WD40 β-propeller simultaneously binds RGS proteins (via GGL domain interface) and Gγ subunits, stabilizing RGS proteins from proteasomal degradation while positioning RGS catalytic domains for GTPase acceleration on Gα subunits. This dual role explains why GNB5 loss causes both visual defects (delayed phototransduction recovery) and neurodevelopmental phenotypes (sustained Gαo signaling disrupting neuronal migration and synaptogenesis). Retinal enrichment reflects specialized demand for rapid GPCR signal termination in phototransduction cascades.
