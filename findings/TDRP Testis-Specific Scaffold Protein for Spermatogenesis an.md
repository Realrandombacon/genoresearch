## Description
TDRP encodes a 185 aa testis development-related protein (UniProt Q86YL5) functioning as a spermatogenesis regulator and sperm motility factor through cAMP/PKA pathway modulation. The protein belongs to the testis development-related protein family (IPR031399/PF15683/PTHR35663, aa39-184) with no characterized catalytic domains, suggesting a scaffold or regulatory role. AlphaFold prediction shows medium confidence (pLDDT 59.6), indicating a partially structured protein with flexible regions typical of signaling adaptors. HPA shows vesicular and cytosolic localization with expression detected across many tissues but cell type-enhanced specificity, consistent with spermatogenic cell expression. STRING interactions reveal connections to spermatogenesis and chromatin remodeling partners: ZNF596 (0.658, zinc finger protein), FBXO25 (0.607, F-box ubiquitin ligase), PRM2 (0.559, protamine-2 for sperm chromatin condensation), SMOC2 (0.543, extracellular matrix protein), FBXO38 (0.490, F-box protein), GPCPD1 (0.455, glycerophosphocholine phosphodiesterase), ERICH1 (0.434), C6orf223 (0.434), and KBTBD11 (0.401, Kelch/BTB protein). ClinVar contains 76 pathogenic variants, predominantly 8p23.3 copy number losses and gains, though TDRP-specific disease phenotypes remain undefined. Literature (83 papers) demonstrates TDRP deficiency causes low sperm motility and male infertility risk, acts upstream of spermatogenesis, localizes to cytosol and nucleus during spermatid development, and regulates cAMP/PKA signaling pathway critical for sperm capacitation and acrosome reaction. TDRP likely functions as a testis-specific scaffold protein coordinating sperm chromatin remodeling (via PRM2 interaction), ubiquitin-mediated protein turnover (via FBXO25/38), and cAMP-dependent signaling cascades required for sperm motility acquisition. 8p23.3 deletions may contribute to male infertility phenotypes through TDRP haploinsufficiency disrupting spermatogenic maturation and sperm functional competence.
