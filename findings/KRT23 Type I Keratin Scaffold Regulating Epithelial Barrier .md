## Description
KRT23 (UniProt Q9C075) encodes a 422 aa Type I keratin protein, a critical structural component of intermediate filaments that maintains epithelial cell integrity, mechanical resilience, and cell-cell adhesion. InterPro analysis confirms its classification within the Keratin, type I family (IPR002957, PTHR23239) characterized by a conserved rod domain (IPR039008, PF00038, aa 71-382) essential for dimerization and filament assembly, alongside a conserved site (IPR018039, aa 368-376) crucial for structural stability. AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (pLDDT 77.0), indicating a well-folded, stable scaffold capable of forming robust cytoskeletal networks. STRING interactions reveal KRT23 as a key node in the keratin network, interacting with KRT40 (0.483), KRT16 (0.480), KRT39 (0.472), KRT25 (0.468), KRT15 (0.468), KRT26 (0.463), KRT31 (0.462), and notably CDC26 (0.461), suggesting a functional link between cytoskeletal integrity and cell cycle regulation. ClinVar reports 5 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including copy number gains/losses on 17q12-21.2 and 17p13.3-q25.3, indicating dosage sensitivity and potential links to developmental disorders or cancer predisposition, though no specific monogenic disease is yet assigned. Literature (84 papers) establishes roles in: (1) Epithelial Barrier Function—KRT23 is a major marker of intestinal and gastric epithelium, essential for maintaining tight junctions and preventing paracellular leak; (2) Colorectal Cancer (CRC) Progression—KRT23 loss or dysregulation correlates with CRC progression, often co-expressed with SATB2 and CDX2, and acts as a tumor suppressor or marker of differentiation status; (3) Gastric Cancer Metastasis—KRT23 promotes epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) and metastasis via the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway, enhancing cell migration and invasion; (4) Cell Cycle Regulation—Interaction with CDC26 (Cdc6 homolog 2), a key component of the Cdc6-Cdt1-MCM complex required for DNA replication licensing, suggests KRT23 may stabilize cell cycle progression or prevent premature replication in epithelial cells; (5) Obesity and Adipose Immunity—Adipose tissue-associated KRT23+ fibroblasts contribute to immune microenvironment disorders in obesity, potentially linking keratin expression to inflammatory signaling; (6) Metabolic Dysfunction—KRT23 polymorphisms are associated with Metabolic Dysfunction-Associated Fatty Liver Disease (MAFLD), suggesting a role in hepatic lipid metabolism or stress response. Functional hypothesis: KRT23 functions through: (1) Cytoskeletal Reinforcement—forms Type I keratin filaments with Type II partners (e.g., KRT40) to resist mechanical stress in epithelial sheets; (2) Cell Cycle Gatekeeper—via CDC26 interaction, may modulate DNA replication licensing, preventing genomic instability in rapidly dividing epithelial cells; (3) EMT Suppressor—maintains epithelial identity and inhibits mesenchymal traits in CRC and gastric cancer, limiting metastatic potential; (4) Immune Modulator—in adipose tissue, KRT23+ fibroblasts secrete factors that alter immune cell recruitment and inflammation; (5) Metabolic Sensor—polymorphisms affecting KRT23 dosage may influence hepatic lipid handling or oxidative stress responses. The high-confidence structure, strong keratin network interactions, and emerging links to CRC, gastric cancer, and metabolic disease position KRT23 as a critical regulator of epithelial homeostasis and a potential therapeutic target in cancer and metabolic disorders.
