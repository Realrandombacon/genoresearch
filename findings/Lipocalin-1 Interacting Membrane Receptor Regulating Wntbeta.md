## Description
LMBR1L (Limb Development Membrane Protein 1-Like) is a 489 amino acid transmembrane protein functioning as a Lipocalin-1 Interacting Membrane Receptor (LIMR). InterPro analysis confirms its identity as a member of the LMBR1-like membrane protein family (IPR006876, PF04791) with two distinct transmembrane domains (residues 24-254 and 281-453) and specific lipocalin-interacting motifs (IPR008075, PR01692, PTHR12625) spanning the entire extracellular region. AlphaFold structure (AF-Q6UX01-F1) predicts a high-confidence fold (pLDDT 80.8) consistent with a stable multi-pass membrane receptor architecture. HPA data classifies LMBR1L as an essential predicted membrane protein involved in endocytosis and Wnt signaling, with ubiquitous tissue distribution and enhanced expression in specific immune cell subsets. STRING interactions reveal a highly specific functional network centered on lipocalin binding and signaling regulation: strong association with LCN1 (Lipocalin-1, score 0.960), suggesting a direct ligand-receptor relationship; connections to AHRR (Aryl Hydrocarbon Receptor Repressor, 0.674) and FKBP10 (0.570); and links to cell cycle regulators CDK4 (0.411) and ubiquitin ligase FBXL20 (0.511). ClinVar lists 13 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including splice site mutations (c.1008+1G>A) and large copy number gains on chromosome 12, though no specific monogenic disease phenotype is yet fully defined. Literature (14 papers) identifies LMBR1L as a critical regulator of lymphopoiesis via modulation of Wnt/beta-catenin signaling, where it acts to restrain excessive pathway activation; loss of function leads to hematopoietic defects resembling Pelger-Huët anomaly in animal models. Recent pan-cancer analyses highlight LMBR1L as a prognostic biomarker in colorectal cancer and diffuse large B-cell lymphoma, likely through its control of immune cell differentiation and Wnt-driven proliferation. With limited mechanistic characterization despite clear roles in development and immunity, LMBR1L represents a dark gene encoding a specialized lipocalin receptor bridging lipid transport signals to transcriptional regulation.
