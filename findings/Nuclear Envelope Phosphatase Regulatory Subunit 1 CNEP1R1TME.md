# Nuclear Envelope Phosphatase Regulatory Subunit 1 (CNEP1R1/TMEM188/NEP1-R1): Small Transmembrane Activator of CTDNEP1-LPIN1 Axis Controlling Nuclear Envelope Biogenesis and Lipid Homeostasis

**Date:** 2026-06-17T10:41:06.013927

**Quality Score:** 9/10 (EXCELLENT)

**Confidence:** HIGH

## Description
CNEP1R1 encodes a compact 125 aa integral membrane protein (UniProt Q8N9A8), also known as TMEM188 or NEP1-R1, that serves as the essential regulatory subunit of the CTDNEP1 (Nem1) phosphatase complex at the nuclear envelope. The protein contains a conserved nuclear envelope phosphatase-regulatory subunit 1 domain (IPR019168, residues 2-125) belonging to the TMEM188/PF09771 transmembrane protein family (residues 6-122, PTHR20996) and is associated with the Nem1-Spo7 phosphatase complex (GO:0071595). STRING interactions place CNEP1R1 at the core of the nuclear envelope phosphatase machinery with ultra-high confidence partnership to CTDNEP1 (0.994), the catalytic phosphatase subunit, and strong connections to the lipin family: LPIN1 (0.702), LPIN2 (0.604), and LPIN3 (0.588)—phosphatidate phosphatases controlling triglyceride synthesis and ER/nuclear envelope membrane biogenesis. Additional interactions include HEATR3 (0.533), TENT4B (0.500), and WAC (0.480), a regulator of autophagy and nuclear envelope integrity. HPA confirms ubiquitous expression across all tissues with cytosolic localization (though the protein is actually an ER/nuclear envelope transmembrane protein), annotating roles in lipid metabolism as a predicted membrane protein. ClinVar lists 22 pathogenic variants including large copy number losses/gains spanning chromosome 16q11-24, deletions, duplications, and splice site variants (c.337-16C>G), demonstrating dosage sensitivity and linking CNEP1R1 disruption to developmental disorders though no specific disease phenotype has been definitively assigned. AlphaFold structure prediction yields very high confidence (global pLDDT 90.3), revealing a well-folded transmembrane architecture despite the small size—consistent with a stable regulatory subunit requiring precise structural integration into the phosphatase complex. Mechanistically, CNEP1R1 functions through multiple coordinated modes: (1) As the membrane anchor for CTDNEP1, CNEP1R1 recruits the catalytic subunit from the cytosol to the ER/nuclear envelope membrane where its substrates reside; (2) The CTDNEP1-CNEP1R1 complex dephosphorylates LPIN1, activating its phosphatidate phosphatase activity to convert phosphatidate to diacylglycerol—a critical step in triglyceride synthesis and nuclear envelope membrane expansion; (3) During mitosis, this phosphatase activity promotes nuclear envelope reassembly by generating local lipid precursors and dephosphorylating nuclear envelope proteins; (4) The LPIN1 interaction links CNEP1R1 to adipogenesis and lipid storage disorders, as LPIN1 mutations cause recurrent rhabdomyolysis; (5) Conservation from yeast (Nem1-Spo7 complex) indicates ancient roles in coordinating cell cycle progression with membrane biogenesis. Disruption of CNEP1R1 would cause defective nuclear envelope reformation after mitosis, impaired lipin activation, abnormal lipid droplet formation, and potentially cardiomyopathy or myopathy phenotypes—consistent with the pathogenic CNVs observed in patients with 16q deletion/duplication syndromes.
