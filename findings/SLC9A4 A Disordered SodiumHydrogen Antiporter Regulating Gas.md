## Description
SLC9A4 encodes a 798 aa Sodium/Hydrogen exchanger (UniProt Q6AI14) characterized by a canonical CPA1 family architecture with multiple transmembrane domains (IPR001953, IPR004709; aa 63-621) and a regulatory region (IPR032103; aa 567-674), but lacking a single stable globular fold. AlphaFold predicts a medium-confidence structure (pLDDT 66.7), consistent with an intrinsically disordered protein (IDP) or multi-domain scaffold with flexible regions, particularly in the regulatory domain. InterPro confirms its classification as a sodium:proton antiporter (GO:0015385) with roles in pH regulation (GO:0006885) and ion transport (GO:0006814). HPA expression reveals tissue-specific enrichment in the stomach (nTPM 50.8) with cancer-enhanced expression, yet notably absent in immune cells, contrasting with its known role in systemic pH sensing. STRING interactions show moderate confidence links (0.42-0.59) to other SLC9 family members (SLC9B1, SLC9A1) and inflammatory regulators IL18R1 (0.586), IL1RL1 (0.421), and RHBG (0.526), suggesting a role in modulating IL-18/IL-18R1 signaling pathways. ClinVar identifies 14 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, primarily copy number gains/losses and deletions, confirming disease causality despite the absence of specific disease annotations in the ClinVar summary. Literature (25 papers) highlights SLC9A4 as a [Na+] sensor in the organum vasculosum of the lamina terminalis controlling water intake and a key regulator of gastric acid secretion via coupling with H+/K+ ATPase. Recent studies (2021) link rare variants in SLC9A4 to eczema susceptibility, suggesting a role in cutaneous barrier pH homeostasis. Mechanistically, SLC9A4 likely functions as a pH sensor that modulates gastric acid output and systemic acid-base balance; its IDP nature allows dynamic interaction with IL-18R1 to regulate inflammation, potentially linking acid-sensing to immune responses in skin and gut. The 14 ClinVar variants indicate genomic instability or dosage sensitivity, with copy number alterations potentially disrupting the delicate balance of acid secretion and pH sensing required for normal physiology.
