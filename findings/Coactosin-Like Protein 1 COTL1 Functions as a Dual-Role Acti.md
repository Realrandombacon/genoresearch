## Description
COTL1 (Coactosin-like protein 1) is a small 142 aa actin-binding protein that uniquely bridges cytoskeletal organization and inflammatory lipid mediator production. With 67 literature references, COTL1 represents a 'partially characterized' dark gene where its dual binding to F-actin and 5-lipoxygenase (ALOX5) is established, but its precise role in disease contexts like osteopetrosis, sepsis, and cancer progression is emerging. InterPro analysis reveals a canonical Actin-Depolymerizing Factor Homology (ADF-H) domain (IPR002108/PF00241, aa 2-130) belonging to the cofilin/tropomyosin-type actin-binding family, embedded within a severin-like superfamily fold (G3DSA:3.40.20.10, aa 1-142). Unlike cofilin which severs actin filaments, COTL1 stabilizes F-actin bundles, a functional divergence encoded within this conserved structural scaffold. AlphaFold predicts a very high-confidence structure (pLDDT 90.4), indicating a rigid, well-folded globular domain optimized for specific protein-protein interactions. HPA shows COTL1 is highly expressed in lymphoid tissues (349.9 nTPM) and enriched in blood cells, localizing to the cytosol, consistent with a role in immune cell motility and activation. STRING interactions reveal an exceptionally strong partnership with the arachidonate 5-lipoxygenase ALOX5 (0.989) and its activating protein ALOX5AP (0.796), placing COTL1 directly in the leukotriene biosynthesis pathway. It also interacts with core cytoskeletal components: ACTB (0.645), CAP1 (0.634), CFL1 (0.582), and ACTG1 (0.567), confirming its role in actin dynamics. ClinVar lists 49 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains and losses on chromosome 16q, implicating COTL1 dosage sensitivity in developmental disorders, although specific monogenic phenotypes are not yet fully defined. Recent literature (2023-2025) establishes COTL1 as: (1) Essential regulator of adipocyte differentiation and obesity; (2) Immunological and prognostic biomarker in low-grade glioma and pan-cancer analyses; (3) Critical mediator of neonatal sepsis-induced lung injury via BATF transcriptional regulation; (4) Key driver of platelet-mediated osteoclastogenesis, where loss causes osteopetrosis.
