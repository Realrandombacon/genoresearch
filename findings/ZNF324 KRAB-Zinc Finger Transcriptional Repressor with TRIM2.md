## Description
ZNF324 encodes a 553 amino acid nuclear transcriptional repressor containing an N-terminal KRAB domain (IPR001909, PF01352, residues 1-72) and three C-terminal C2H2-type zinc fingers (IPR013087, PF00096, residues 257-279/285-307/313-335). AlphaFold prediction (pLDDT 64.0) indicates a partially structured architecture with ordered zinc finger DNA-binding domains and flexible linker regions. STRING analysis reveals a transcriptional co-repression network centered on TRIM28/KAP1 (0.573), the canonical KRAB domain co-repressor that recruits SETDB1 histone methyltransferase and HP1 heterochromatin proteins. Additional partners include ZNF446 (0.559), ZNF324B (0.534, paralog), ZNF764 (0.522), and ZNF224 (0.436), suggesting heterodimerization among KRAB-ZNF family members. HPA shows ubiquitous expression across all tissues with low specificity, consistent with housekeeping transcriptional repression function. Subcellular localization is nucleoplasmic/nuclear membrane, matching DNA-binding activity. ClinVar documents 12 pathogenic variants, predominantly 19q13.43 copy number gains, implicating ZNF324 dosage in disease. The mechanistic hypothesis: ZNF324 functions as a sequence-specific DNA-binding repressor that recruits TRIM28/SETDB1 complexes via the KRAB domain, depositing H3K9me3 heterochromatin marks at target gene promoters. Zinc fingers confer DNA sequence specificity, while the KRAB domain mediates protein-protein interactions with the co-repressor machinery. Ubiquitous expression suggests roles in maintaining basal gene silencing programs across cell types. CNV pathogenicity indicates precise ZNF324 stoichiometry is critical, potentially through dysregulation of cell cycle genes (G1/S transition per Gene Ontology).
