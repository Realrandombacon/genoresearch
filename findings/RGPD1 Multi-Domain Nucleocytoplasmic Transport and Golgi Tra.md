## Description
RGPD1 (RANBP2-like and GRIP domain-containing protein 1, UniProt P0DJD0) is a massive 1748-amino acid intracellular protein located on chromosome 2p11.2. InterPro analysis reveals complex multi-domain architecture characteristic of trafficking regulators: two Ran-binding domains (IPR000156, aa 1021-1157/1318-1454) mediating interactions with RAN GTPase for nucleocytoplasmic transport, a C-terminal GRIP domain (IPR000237, aa 1685-1735) for Golgi membrane targeting, multiple TPR repeats (IPR019734/IPR013105, aa 51-84) forming protein-protein interaction scaffolds, PH-like domains (aa 1006-1157/1302-1461) for phospholipid binding, and a GCC2-type Rab-binding domain (IPR032023, aa 16). GO annotations indicate roles in intracellular transport (GO:0046907) and protein binding (GO:0005515). AlphaFold structure prediction achieves medium confidence (pLDDT 65.2), indicating a partially structured protein with ordered functional domains connected by flexible linkers — typical of large trafficking adaptors. Human Protein Atlas shows testis-enriched expression (22.6 nTPM — highest among all tissues), with placenta (9.2 nTPM) and cervix (8.0 nTPM) expression, group-enriched RNA specificity, and subcellular localization to nuclear membrane and vesicles — consistent with dual roles in nuclear transport and Golgi-vesicle trafficking. STRING interaction network reveals 10 partners comprising the nucleocytoplasmic transport and Golgi machinery: GRIP1 (0.863, highest confidence — Golgi targeting protein), RAN (0.721, Ras-related nuclear protein GTPase), RANGAP1 (0.639, RAN GTPase activating protein), RCC1 (0.636, regulator of chromosome condensation, RAN guanine nucleotide exchange factor), GCC1 (0.559, golgin coiled-coil protein 1), plus peptidyl-prolyl cis-trans isomerases PPIH/PPIA/PPIAL4A/PPIAL4C (0.559/0.550/0.540/0.536) facilitating protein folding during transport. This interactome confirms RGPD1 functions at the intersection of nuclear pore transport (via RAN/RANGAP1/RCC1) and Golgi vesicle tethering (via GRIP domain/GCC1). ClinVar contains 12 pathogenic variants including massive copy-number gains and losses across 2p25.3-q37.3, demonstrating dosage sensitivity where genomic rearrangements disrupting chromosome 2 cause disease phenotypes potentially affecting intracellular trafficking. Conservation across metazoans (fruit fly A0A0K8UKN5, 2857 aa; melon fly A0A0A1XND7, 2864 aa; Trichinella E5S9I9, 1039 aa; ant A0A836EG09, 2575 aa) indicates ancient evolutionary origin tied to fundamental eukaryotic trafficking systems. RGPD1 likely functions as a multi-functional trafficking adaptor, using its Ran-binding domains to regulate nucleocytoplasmic transport through RAN-GTP cycling while its GRIP domain anchors the protein to trans-Golgi membranes for vesicle tethering, coordinating nuclear import/export with secretory pathway flux — particularly critical in testis where high rates of protein synthesis and cellular remodeling occur during spermatogenesis.
