# FAM92B (CIBAR2): BAR Domain Protein Regulating Ciliogenesis and Basal Body Function via Membrane Curvature Sensing

**Date:** 2026-04-06T13:00:59.003975

**Quality Score:** 3.15/10  (E=5.2, D=1.1) [WEAK]

## Description
FAM92B (Family with Sequence Similarity 92 Member B, also CIBAR2, CBY1-interacting BAR domain-containing protein 2) is a 304 amino acid intracellular protein (UniProt Q6ZTR7) with a well-folded BAR domain architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 80.9, high confidence), characteristic of proteins that sense and induce membrane curvature during ciliogenesis and vesicle trafficking.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals FAM92B contains a canonical BAR (Bin/Amphiphysin/Rvs) domain (IPR035590/cd07598/PF06730, residues 6-216; G3DSA:1.20.1270.60/SSF103657, residues 4-214) conferring cilium assembly activity (GO:0060271). The protein belongs to the CBY1-interacting BAR domain-containing protein family (IPR009602/PTHR21223, residues 4-262). The BAR domain forms a curved dimeric structure that binds phospholipid membranes, facilitating membrane deformation required for ciliary vesicle formation or transition zone organization.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals FAM92B functions within the ciliary and basal body network: strongest interactions with NCF4 (0.664, neutrophil cytosolic factor 4), KLHL36 (0.628, kelch-like protein 36), DYDC2 (0.613, dynactin domain-containing protein 2), KIAA0513 (0.608, protein kiaa0513), ATG16L1 (0.551, autophagy-related protein 16-1), PSTPIP1 (0.527, proline-serine-threonine phosphatase interacting protein 1), CBY1 (0.526, chibby homolog 1), SNX33 (0.519, sorting nexin-33), DZIP1L (0.498, zinc finger protein DZIP1L), and PHOX2B (0.402). The interaction with CBY1 (chibby) confirms its role as a CBY1-interacting partner, while DZIP1L and KIAA0513 are known ciliary gate components. ATG16L1 suggests potential links to autophagy-mediated ciliary resorption.\n\nHPA expression shows tissue-enhanced specificity with highest expression in fallopian tube (80.4 nTPM) and choroid plexus (33.4 nTPM), consistent with roles in ciliated epithelia and cerebrospinal fluid production. The protein is classified as predicted intracellular with involvement in cilium biogenesis/degradation. Cancer-enhanced expression suggests potential roles in tumor cell ciliation defects.\n\nClinVar documents no pathogenic variants, indicating either redundancy with paralogs (e.g., FAM92A/CIBAR1) or that loss-of-function is not yet associated with recognized Mendelian disorders. However, given the critical nature of cilia, mutations may cause undiagnosed ciliopathies.\n\nConservation is evident from mouse ortholog (Q3V2J0, 292 aa), cow ortholog (Q1RMK1, 288 aa), and the presence of a paralog FAM92A (CIBAR1), indicating functional importance in vertebrate ciliary biology.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: FAM92B acts as a BAR domain scaffolding protein that localizes to the ciliary basal body and transition zone to regulate membrane curvature during ciliogenesis. By binding to curved membranes at the base of the growing cilium, FAM92B facilitates the fusion of ciliary vesicles or stabilizes the ciliary pocket. Interaction with CBY1 suggests a mechanism where FAM92B recruits or stabilizes CBY1 to inhibit Wnt/beta-catenin signaling, promoting ciliary assembly. High expression in fallopian tube and choroid plexus correlates with tissues requiring robust ciliary function for fluid movement. The link to ATG16L1 implies a role in coordinating ciliary disassembly via autophagy during cell cycle entry. Loss of FAM92B likely disrupts ciliary gate integrity or vesicle trafficking, leading to ciliopathy phenotypes such as hydrocephalus (choroid plexus defect) or infertility (fallopian tube cilia defect).
