## Description
BPNT2 (Gene ID 54928, UniProt Q9NX62, aliases GPAPP/IMPA3/IMPAD1) encodes a 359-amino acid Golgi-resident adenosine 3',5'-bisphosphate 3'-phosphatase on chromosome 8q12.1, representing a dark gene with only 6 total publications on Semantic Scholar despite clear disease associations and biochemical function. Recent literature (2022) demonstrates CSN5-mediated BPNT2 deubiquitination regulates stemness of nasopharyngeal carcinoma cells; 2021 studies reveal BPNT2 catalytic activity is required for glycosaminoglycan sulfation in vitro and regulates chondroitin sulfation patterns in cerebral cortex and hippocampus, establishing BPNT2 as a molecular target of lithium therapy for bipolar disorder; mutations cause GRAPP (Golgi-resident adenosine 3',5'-bisphosphate phosphatase-related) type chondrodysplasia with joint dislocations. The mechanistic hypothesis: BPNT2 functions as an essential regulator of proteoglycan sulfation and lithium-responsive signaling through: (1) In normal physiology — localizes to Golgi apparatus where it catalyzes Mg2+-dependent hydrolysis of 3'-phosphoadenosine 5'-phosphate (PAP) to adenosine monophosphate (AMP) and inorganic phosphate; PAP is the toxic byproduct of sulfotransferase reactions that attach sulfate groups to glycosaminoglycans (chondroitin sulfate, heparan sulfate), glycoproteins, and small molecules; PAP accumulation inhibits sulfotransferases causing undersulfated proteoglycans disrupting extracellular matrix integrity, cartilage development, and joint formation; interaction network confirms integration with PAPS synthesis (PAPSS1/2), inositol phosphate metabolism (IMPA1/2, INPP1/4A/4B, MTM1), and parallel bisphosphate metabolism (BPNT1); high-confidence structure reveals conserved active site with three Mg2+ ions coordinated by acidic residues characteristic of inositol monophosphatase family; lithium inhibition occurs through competition with Mg2+ at catalytic site explaining therapeutic mechanism in bipolar disorder; (2) In disease — loss-of-function causes: (a) GRAPP chondrodysplasia — impaired PAP clearance leads to defective chondroitin/heparan sulfate sulfation in growth plate cartilage causing joint dislocations, skeletal malformations, and short stature; (b) Bipolar disorder — lithium therapeutic effects partially mediated through BPNT2 inhibition altering inositol signaling and sulfation patterns in brain (2021 studies show BPNT2 regulates chondroitin sulfation in cerebral cortex/hippocampus affecting neuronal migration and synaptic function); (c) Cancer — CSN5-mediated deubiquitination stabilizes BPNT2 promoting nasopharyngeal carcinoma stemness potentially through altered sulfation of cell surface receptors affecting Wnt/Notch signaling; (d) CNV deletions may cause contiguous gene syndromes with additional 8q12 features; (3) Only 6 publications highlight significant knowledge gap — despite clear disease mechanism, detailed substrate kinetics, tissue-specific isoforms, and comprehensive variant functionalization remain unexplored; (4) High-confidence structure (pLDDT 87.8) enables rational design of lithium-resistant mutants and structure-based inhibitor screening.
