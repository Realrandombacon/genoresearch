## Description
PINLYP (Phospholipase A2 inhibitor and Ly6/PLAUR domain-containing protein, UniProt A6NC86) is a 204-amino acid secreted protein located on chromosome 19q13.31 within a region prone to copy-number variations. InterPro analysis reveals unique dual-domain architecture characteristic of CNF-like phospholipase A2 inhibitors: N-terminal phospholipase A2 inhibitor domain (IPR004126/PF02988, aa 28-108) fused to C-terminal Ly-6/urokinase plasminogen activator receptor domain (IPR016054/PF00021, aa 122-181), both adopting CD59-like snake toxin-fold superfamily structures (G3DSA:2.10.60.10/SSF48726/IPR045860, aa 27-113 and 120-203). The protein belongs to the CNF-like phospholipase A2 inhibitor family (IPR050918/PTHR20918, aa 11-201) with a prokaryotic membrane lipoprotein lipid attachment site (PS51257, aa 1-25) suggesting potential membrane anchoring. GO annotations confirm phospholipase inhibitor activity (GO:0004859) and extracellular region localization (GO:0005576). AlphaFold structure prediction achieves very high confidence (pLDDT 90.6), indicating an exceptionally well-folded stable dual-domain structure optimal for mediating specific protein-protein interactions and enzyme inhibition. Human Protein Atlas shows low tissue specificity (detected in many tissues), immune cell-enhanced expression, and remarkable subcellular localization to vesicles, microtubules, cytokinetic bridge, mitotic spindle, and primary cilium — consistent with roles in cell division, ciliary signaling, and membrane trafficking where phospholipase regulation controls membrane dynamics. STRING interaction network reveals 10 partners exclusively from the Ly6/uPAR protein family: LYPD4 (0.585), TEX101 (0.582, testis-expressed Ly6 protein), PATE3 (0.571), LY6L (0.570), PATE1 (0.569), LYPD5 (0.547), PATE4 (0.506), PATE2 (0.480), LY6G5B (0.418), and LYPD3 (0.400). This highly specific interactome confirms PINLYP functions within the Ly6 protein network involved in immune regulation, sperm maturation, and epithelial cell signaling. ClinVar contains 6 pathogenic variants consisting of large copy-number gains and losses across 19p13.3-q13.43, demonstrating dosage sensitivity where genomic rearrangements disrupting chromosome 19 cause disease phenotypes. Conservation across mammals (mouse Q9CQD7, 212 aa; orangutan H2NZ28, 228 aa; narwhal A0A8C6AW28, 201 aa) indicates ancient evolutionary origin tied to fundamental phospholipase regulation mechanisms. PINLYP likely functions as a dual-domain phospholipase A2 inhibitor, using its N-terminal PLA2-inhibitory domain to block phospholipase enzymatic activity while its C-terminal Ly6 domain mediates protein-protein interactions with other Ly6 family members at the cell surface — mechanisms critical for controlling arachidonic acid release, eicosanoid production, inflammatory signaling, and membrane remodeling during cytokinesis and ciliary function, with dysfunction potentially contributing to inflammatory diseases and cancer where chromosome 19q amplifications are frequent.
