## Description
DOP1A (DOP1 leucine zipper-like protein A, also known as DOPEY1, KIAA1117) is a 2465 amino acid intracellular protein (UniProt Q5JWR5) with medium-confidence partially-structured architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 66.9), characteristic of large scaffold proteins with ordered domains interspersed with flexible linkers. The protein contains multiple TPR (tetratricopeptide repeat) domains that mediate protein-protein interactions: DOP1 N-terminal domain (IPR007249/PF04118, residues 11-295), DOP1 middle TPR domain (IPR056458/PF24597, residues 319-474), DOP1 TPR domain (IPR056459/PF24601, residues 1363-1737), DOP1 C-terminal domain (IPR056457/PF24598, residues 1904-2404), and DOP1B-like TPR repeats (PF26706, residues 788-991). DOP1A belongs to the DOPEY-related family (IPR040314/PTHR14042, residues 1-2464) involved in Golgi to endosome transport (GO:0006895). DOP1A is located on chromosome 6q14.1 and enables protein transport functions, functioning in Golgi to endosome transport and endoplasmic reticulum organization.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals a high-confidence network of Golgi-endosome trafficking regulators: MON2 (0.981, MON2 homolog, Golgi-endosome trafficking), NEO1 (0.595, Neogenin-1, membrane trafficking), ATP9B (0.578, Phospholipid-transporting ATPase IB, lipid flipping), ATP9A (0.564, Phospholipid-transporting ATPase IA), WASHC4 (0.455, WASH complex subunit 4, endosomal actin polymerization), SNX3 (0.452, Sorting nexin-3, endosomal sorting), SLC66A2 (0.447, Solute carrier family 66 member 2), EFHD2 (0.429, EF-hand domain-containing protein D2, calcium signaling), KIAA0355 (0.416, Uncharacterized protein), and CZIB (0.409, Centrosomal protein of 131 kDa B). These interactions confirm DOP1A functions within the Golgi-endosome trafficking pathway, potentially coordinating vesicle formation and cargo sorting.\n\nHPA expression reveals DOP1A has low tissue specificity and is detected in all tissues, consistent with a housekeeping role in membrane trafficking across cell types. Subcellular localization includes nucleoplasm and vesicles, consistent with Golgi-endosome localization. The protein is classified as a predicted intracellular and membrane protein involved in protein transport and transport biological processes. Low cell type specificity suggests ubiquitous expression in all cell types.\n\nClinVar documents 29 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including copy number loss (6q14.1-14.3 deletion), single nucleotide variants in DOP1A (c.1485+99A>T intronic, c.1455+60G>T intronic), and variants in the neighboring PGM3 gene (c.1366-2A>T splice site, c.*43C>G 3' UTR, c.1391C>T p.Thr464Ile, c.1415_1442dup p.Asn482fs, c.1500AGA[1] p.Glu501del, c.1432C>T p.Gln478Ter nonsense). The variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity. PGM3 mutations cause immunodeficiency, developmental delay, and atopy. Given DOP1A's proximity to PGM3 and role in vesicular trafficking, loss-of-function may contribute to PGM3-related immunodeficiency through impaired glycosylation enzyme trafficking.\n\nConservation is strong across vertebrates: human (Q5JWR5, 2465 aa), mouse (Q8BL99, 2399 aa), indicating an ancient and essential function in membrane trafficking conserved in mammals.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: DOP1A functions as a large TPR-containing scaffold protein that coordinates Golgi-to-endosome vesicle trafficking through multiple protein-protein interaction domains. The TPR repeats (residues 319-474, 788-991, 1363-1737) provide binding surfaces for trafficking regulators including MON2 (Golgi-endosome tethering), NEO1 (membrane trafficking receptor), and ATP9A/ATP9B (phospholipid flippases that maintain membrane asymmetry). WASHC4 and SNX3 interactions link DOP1A to endosomal actin polymerization and cargo sorting machinery. The partially-structured architecture (pLDDT 66.9) with flexible linkers between ordered TPR domains may enable DOP1A to adopt multiple conformations for coordinating different trafficking steps. Nucleoplasmic and vesicular localization suggests DOP1A functions at Golgi-endosome contact sites and potentially in nuclear-cytoplasmic transport. 6q CNVs and PGM3-linked variants cause DOP1A/PGM3 dysfunction, potentially disrupting glycosylation enzyme trafficking and causing immunodeficiency phenotypes through impaired antibody glycosylation and lymphocyte development. DOP1A represents a candidate gene for congenital disorders of glycosylation and primary immunodeficiency.
