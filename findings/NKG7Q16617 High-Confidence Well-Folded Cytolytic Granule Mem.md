## Description
NKG7 (Natural killer cell granule protein 7, also known as GMP-17 or p15-TIA-1) is a 165 amino acid integral membrane protein localized to cytolytic granule membranes and plasma membrane vesicles (UniProt Q16617) with high-confidence well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 87.1). The protein defines the PMP-22/EMP/MP20/Claudin superfamily (IPR004031/PTHR10671, PF00822/G3DSA:1.20.140.150, residues 11-149), featuring a characteristic 4-transmembrane helix bundle structure typical of tight junction and vesicle trafficking proteins. The conserved PMP-22/EMP signature motif (IPR004032/PS01221, residues 18-46) within the first extracellular loop is critical for protein stability and oligomerization. Molecular functions include cytolysis, inflammatory response, and defense response to protozoa, placing NKG7 within the innate immune cytotoxicity pathway.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals an exceptionally tight network of cytolytic effector molecules: strongest interactions with GNLY (0.951, Granulysin, antimicrobial peptide), GZMA (0.937, Granzyme A), GZMK (0.933, Granzyme K), and PRF1 (0.933, Perforin-1, pore-forming protein). High confidence links include KLRD1 (0.907, CD94, NK cell receptor), CD8A (0.904, T-cell co-receptor), CST7 (0.900, Cystatin F), and CTSW (0.899, Cathepsin W). This profile confirms NKG7 as a core component of the cytolytic granule machinery, likely functioning alongside perforin and granzymes to facilitate target cell lysis. The interaction with KLRD1 suggests regulation by activating NK receptors.\n\nHPA expression reveals a group-enriched pattern with massive overexpression in bone marrow (568.3 nTPM) and lymphoid tissue (161.0 nTPM). Expression is restricted to immune cells, specifically NK cells and cytotoxic T lymphocytes (CTLs). Subcellular localization to vesicles confirms its role in granule storage and release. Low cancer specificity indicates physiological immune surveillance rather than oncogenic drive.\n\nClinVar documents 15 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, exclusively large copy number gains and deletions affecting chromosome 19q (19q13.33-13.43 duplications, 19p13.3-q13.43 gains). The variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity. While no specific Mendelian disease is annotated, NKG7 haploinsufficiency or overexpression likely causes immunodeficiency or autoimmunity. 19q CNVs are associated with developmental delay and immune dysregulation; NKG7 disruption may impair NK cell function, leading to increased susceptibility to viral infections or protozoal diseases (e.g., Leishmania, Toxoplasma).\n\nConservation is strong across mammals: human (Q16617, 165 aa), mouse (Q99PA5, 165 aa), cow (Q2KJ11, 165 aa), rat (A6JAH6, 165 aa), indicating evolutionary pressure to maintain the 4-TM topology.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: NKG7 functions as a structural regulator of cytolytic granule biogenesis and fusion. As a member of the PMP-22/Claudin family, it likely forms homo- or hetero-oligomers that stabilize the granule membrane or facilitate pore formation during degranulation. Upon NK cell activation via KLRD1/CD94, NKG7 translocates to the immunological synapse, where it assists perforin (PRF1) in inserting pores into the target membrane, allowing granzymes (GZMA, GZMK) to enter and induce apoptosis. The conserved extracellular loop may mediate granule-granule fusion or granule-plasma membrane docking. Loss of NKG7 impairs granule integrity or release efficiency, reducing cytotoxic capacity. Overexpression may cause excessive tissue damage or autoimmunity. NKG7 represents a potential biomarker for NK cell activity and a target for enhancing immunotherapy efficacy.
