## Description
MED10 (Mediator of RNA polymerase II transcription subunit 10, also known as NUT2, TRG20) is a 135 amino acid nuclear protein (UniProt Q9BTT4) with very-high-confidence well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 92.8), characteristic of Mediator complex subunits that form stable structural cores for transcriptional coactivation. The protein contains a Mediator complex subunit Med10 family domain (IPR019145, residues 9-125) enabling transcription coregulator activity (GO:0003712) and regulation of transcription by RNA polymerase II (GO:0006357), belongs to the transcription factor subunit Med10 of Mediator complex family (PF09748, residues 9-125), and is classified as a Mediator complex subunit 10 family member (PTHR13345, residues 3-129) involved in mediator complex assembly (GO:0016592). MED10 is located on chromosome 5p15.31 and functions as an essential component of the Mediator complex, a multi-subunit coactivator that bridges DNA-binding transcription factors with RNA polymerase II for transcription initiation.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals an extremely high-confidence network of Mediator complex subunits forming the structural core: MED4 (0.999, Mediator complex subunit 4), MED9 (0.999, Mediator complex subunit 9), MED15 (0.999, Mediator complex subunit 15), MED21 (0.999, Mediator complex subunit 21), MED7 (0.999, Mediator complex subunit 7), MED26 (0.999, Mediator complex subunit 26), MED20 (0.999, Mediator complex subunit 20), MED28 (0.998, Mediator complex subunit 28), MED31 (0.997, Mediator complex subunit 31), and MED17 (0.997, Mediator complex subunit 17). These interactions confirm MED10 functions within the Mediator complex head module, with MED4, MED9, MED15, MED21, and MED7 forming the core structural scaffold (all 0.999 confidence) that mediates polymerase II recruitment and transcription initiation.\n\nHPA expression data is not available for MED10 in the Human Protein Atlas database (ENSG00000291745), suggesting the gene may be annotated under a different identifier or expression data is limited. However, based on its essential role in the Mediator complex and conservation across eukaryotes, MED10 is expected to have ubiquitous expression in all tissues requiring active transcription.\n\nClinVar documents 109 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including single allele deletions, intronic variants (c.122+164T>G), synonymous variants (c.354G>A p.Pro118=), and extensive copy number losses (5p15.33-14.1, 5p15.33-15.2, 5p15.33-15.31, 5p15.33-14.3 deletions) and gains (5p15.33-13.3 amplifications). The variant burden indicates strong dosage sensitivity. While no specific Mendelian disease is annotated, 5p CNVs are associated with Cri-du-chat syndrome (5p deletion syndrome), developmental delay, intellectual disability, and growth abnormalities. Given MED10's essential role in the Mediator complex and RNA polymerase II transcription, loss-of-function may cause severe developmental disorders through global transcriptional dysregulation.\n\nConservation is excellent across eukaryotes: human (Q9BTT4, 135 aa), mouse (Q9CXU0, 135 aa), zebrafish (Q0VIA1, 134 aa), Saccharomyces cerevisiae NUT2 (Q06213, 157 aa), Schizosaccharomyces pombe med10 (P87310, 144 aa), indicating the Med10 subunit architecture is highly conserved from yeast to humans with nearly identical protein length, suggesting strong purifying selection on the Mediator complex core structure.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: MED10 functions as an essential structural component of the Mediator complex head module that bridges sequence-specific transcription factors with RNA polymerase II for transcription initiation. The very-high-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 92.8) indicates a stable folded architecture that forms part of the Mediator complex structural core. Through MED4, MED9, MED15, MED21, and MED7 interactions (all 0.999), MED10 forms the head module scaffold that directly contacts RNA polymerase II and stabilizes the pre-initiation complex. MED26, MED20, MED28, MED31, and MED17 interactions (0.999/0.999/0.998/0.997/0.997) link MED10 to additional Mediator subunits that coordinate transcription factor recruitment and polymerase II C-terminal domain (CTD) phosphorylation. MED10's position in the head module enables it to transmit activation signals from enhancer-bound transcription factors to the basal transcription machinery. 5p CNVs cause MED10 haploinsufficiency or overexpression, potentially disrupting Mediator complex stoichiometry and causing global transcriptional dysregulation, developmental abnormalities, and intellectual disability. MED10 represents an essential gene for RNA polymerase II transcription and may serve as a therapeutic target for transcription-related disorders through Mediator complex modulation.
