## Description
TBC1D5 (TBC1 Domain Family Member 5) is a 795 amino acid Rab GTPase-activating protein (Rab-GAP) that serves as a critical regulatory component of the retromer complex, controlling endosome-to-Golgi retrieval trafficking through inactivation of Rab7 GTPase. InterPro analysis identifies canonical Rab-GAP-TBC domains (IPR000195, PF00566, PS50086, SM00164, residues 78-384) with GTPase activation capability, classified within the Rab-GAP-TBC superfamily (IPR035969, residues 63-265 and 303-414) and the broader Rab GTPase-activating family (PTHR22957, residues 49-393), with structural homology to Ypt/Rab-GAP domains (G3DSA:1.10.472.80, SSF47923). AlphaFold structure (AF-Q92609-F1) predicts medium confidence (pLDDT 64.6) across the 795 residue sequence, indicating a partially structured protein with ordered catalytic TBC domains connected to flexible regulatory regions, consistent with GAPs that undergo conformational changes during substrate engagement. HPA data classifies TBC1D5 as a predicted intracellular and membrane protein with low tissue specificity (detected in all tissues), localized to Golgi apparatus and vesicles, functioning in autophagy, protein transport, and GTPase activation. STRING interactions reveal exceptionally tight coupling to the retromer cargo-selective complex: VPS29 (0.999), VPS35 (0.997), VPS26A (0.991), VPS26B (0.914), confirming direct physical association; strong connections to sorting nexins SNX1/2/5/6 (0.950-0.970) that mediate membrane curvature and cargo recognition; and links to autophagy machinery GABARAPL1/2 (0.927), positioning TBC1D5 at the intersection of endosomal trafficking and autophagic degradation. ClinVar lists 27 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number gains on 3p26.3-14.3, duplications, deletions, and splice variants (c.1246-3del, c.995+123C>T), though no specific monogenic syndrome is yet defined. Literature (68 papers) establishes TBC1D5 as a multifunctional regulator: the SARS-CoV-2 virulence factor ORF3a directly binds TBC1D5 to block lysosome function by disrupting the Rab7 GTPase cycle, facilitating viral egress; TBC1D5 inhibition enhances Rab7 activity and improves mitophagy in Alzheimer's disease models, suggesting therapeutic potential for neurodegeneration; TBC1D5 reverses HIF-2α-driven tumor progression and lipid metabolism dysregulation in clear cell renal cell carcinoma through autophagy modulation; and the retromer-TBC1D5 complex is captured by autophagy to inhibit receptor recycling, revealing crosstalk between trafficking pathways. As a dark gene with established molecular function but incompletely characterized disease mechanisms, TBC1D5 represents a critical node connecting endosomal sorting, autophagic clearance, viral pathogenesis, and metabolic regulation through Rab7 GTPase control.
