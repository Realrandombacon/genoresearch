## Description
FBXO44 (F-Box Only Protein 44) is a 255-amino acid nuclear protein (UniProt Q9H4M3) representing a genuine dark gene with only 34 publications despite its critical roles as an SCF (SKP1-CUL1-F-box) E3 ubiquitin ligase substrate receptor regulating GPCR signaling, cancer progression, cardiac aging, and mitochondrial calcium homeostasis. Structurally, FBXO44 contains canonical F-box protein architecture: F-box domain (IPR001810/PF12937/PS50181/cd22168, residues 3-53/6-53/3-50/3-85) mediating SKP1 binding and SCF complex assembly, F-box associated region (FBA domain, IPR007397/PF04300/PS51114, residues 68-252/72-250/71-252) forming the substrate recognition module, F-box-like domain superfamily (IPR036047/G3DSA:1.20.1280.50, residues 4-83/1-67) defining the ubiquitin ligase scaffold, galactose-binding-like domain superfamily (IPR008979/G3DSA:2.60.120.260, residues 68-251/68-254) suggesting carbohydrate or protein interaction capacity, and F-box only protein family (IPR039752/PTHR12125, residues 4-252/4-252). AlphaFold predicts very high-confidence structure (pLDDT 92.4), consistent with a well-folded F-box protein containing stable SKP1-binding interface and defined substrate recognition surface. Functionally, FBXO44 localizes to nucleoplasm with tissue-enhanced expression across all tissues (HPA: detected in all, tissue enhanced, highest in brain at 260.4 nTPM, low cancer specificity, cell type enhanced), classified as a predicted intracellular protein involved in Ubl (ubiquitin-like) conjugation pathway—nuclear localization confirms roles in transcriptional regulation and protein degradation. STRING interactions reveal connections to SCF E3 ligase core machinery and substrates: CUL4B (0.895, cullin-4B—alternative cullin scaffold), SKP1 (0.812, S-phase kinase-associated protein 1—canonical F-box binding partner), DDB1 (0.812, DNA damage-binding protein 1—CUL4 adaptor), RBX1 (0.763, RING-box protein 1—E2 ubiquitin-conjugating enzyme recruiter), MAD2L2 (0.687, mitotic arrest deficient 2-like protein 2—spindle checkpoint), CUL1 (0.664, cullin-1—canonical SCF scaffold), FBXO27 (0.595), RGS2 (0.566, regulator of G-protein signaling 2—validated substrate), CUL2 (0.562), and FBXO17 (0.431). This interaction network definitively positions FBXO44 within SCF E3 ubiquitin ligase complexes mediating substrate polyubiquitination and proteasomal degradation. Critically, ClinVar documents 40 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number losses at 1p36.32-36.21 (chr1:2441650-15051191)x1, 1p36.32-36.12 (chr1:4436802-22782007)x2, 1p36.31-36.21 (chr1:6330828-12910774)x1, 1p36.31-36.13 (chr1:6758933-19287770)x1, 1p36.33-36.22 (chr1:849467-12448956)x1, 1p36.33-36.13 (chr1:849466-17525065)x1, deletions (NC_000001.10:g.4481271_20530242del), duplications (NC_000001.11:g.10115497_16283149dup), and copy number gains at 1p36.23-36.22 (chr1:9145617-11879239)x3, 1p36.33-35.1 (chr1:99125-34026935)x3, yet ZERO diseases are formally associated—a profound gap given the validated substrate recognition and disease mechanisms. Recent literature establishes emerging specialized roles: discovery of RGS2-FBXO44 interaction inhibitors using cell-based NanoBit assay revealing FBXO44 as druggable target for GPCR-related pathologies (3 citations, 2025); FBXO44 regulates FOXP1 degradation through AURKA-dependent phosphorylation promoting colorectal cancer progression (1 citation, 2025); systematic analysis of RGS2 degron reveals characteristics of substrate recognition by FBXO44 establishing molecular determinants of specificity (2025); and Fbxo44 alleviates heart aging and mitochondrial calcium overload injury by modulating RyR2-related pathway (2024). The mechanistic hypothesis: FBXO44 functions as a substrate-specific receptor within SCF E3 ubiquitin ligase complexes, recruiting target proteins for polyubiquitination and proteasomal degradation. In GPCR signaling, FBXO44 recognizes the RGS2 degron through its FBA domain, targeting RGS2 for degradation and thereby potentiating Gαq-mediated signaling—FBXO44 inhibition would stabilize RGS2, dampening GPCR responses relevant to cardiovascular and neurological diseases. In colorectal cancer, AURKA phosphorylates FOXP1 creating a phosphodegron recognized by FBXO44, leading to FOXP1 degradation and loss of tumor suppressor activity—this FBXO44-AURKA-FOXP1 axis drives proliferation and metastasis. In cardiac aging, FBXO44 likely regulates RyR2 (ryanodine receptor 2) stability or associated calcium handling proteins, preventing mitochondrial calcium overload and cardiomyocyte dysfunction—FBXO44 decline with age would cause RyR2 dysregulation and heart failure. The CUL4B/DDB1 interactions suggest FBXO44 may function in alternative CUL4-based complexes beyond canonical CUL1-SCF, potentially linking to DNA damage response. The brain-enriched expression (260.4 nTPM) suggests neuronal functions in GPCR desensitization or synaptic plasticity. The absence of formal disease associations despite 40 pathogenic variants suggests FBXO44 dysfunction contributes to 1p36 deletion syndrome (developmental delay, cardiomyopathy, seizures), GPCR signaling disorders, colorectal cancer susceptibility, or age-related heart failure not yet mapped to Mendelian phenotypes. Future work should define the complete FBXO44 substrate repertoire beyond RGS2 and FOXP1, determine structural basis of FBA domain substrate recognition, investigate whether germline FBXO44 mutations cause neurodevelopmental or cardiac disorders, develop small-molecule FBXO44-RGS2 interaction inhibitors for GPCR-related diseases, and explore FBXO44 as a therapeutic target in colorectal cancer (AURKA inhibitor combinations) and cardiac aging.
