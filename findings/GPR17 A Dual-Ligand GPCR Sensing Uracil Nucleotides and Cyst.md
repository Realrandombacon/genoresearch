# GPR17: A Dual-Ligand GPCR Sensing Uracil Nucleotides and Cysteinyl Leukotrienes in Oligodendrocyte Differentiation and Neuroinflammation

**Date:** 2026-06-26T16:29:14.993873

**Quality Score:** 8/10 (EXCELLENT)

**Confidence:** MEDIUM

## Description
GPR17 (G protein-coupled receptor 17, UniProt Q13304) is a 367-amino acid Class A rhodopsin-like GPCR that functions as a unique dual-ligand receptor, responding to both uracil nucleotides (UDP, UTP) and cysteinyl leukotrienes (LTD4, LTE4). InterPro analysis confirms a canonical 7-transmembrane helix architecture (IPR000276, PF00001, aa 76-325) with conserved motifs for G-protein coupling (PS00237) and ligand binding pockets distinct from pure P2Y or CysLT receptors. The AlphaFold predicted structure exhibits high confidence (pLDDT 80.3), revealing a stable transmembrane bundle suitable for small molecule and nucleotide recognition. STRING interaction data highlights strong associations with heterotrimeric G-protein subunits GNB1 (0.947) and GNAI1 (0.909), indicating preferential coupling to Gi/o signaling pathways that inhibit cAMP production. Notably, GPR17 interacts with ITGA4 (0.701), suggesting crosstalk with integrin-mediated adhesion, and shows moderate links to related receptors CYSLTR1 (0.570) and P2RY12 (0.499), positioning it as a bridge between purinergic and leukotriene signaling networks. HPA expression profiles show extreme tissue enrichment in the brain (35.3 nTPM), with specific localization to oligodendrocyte precursor cells (OPCs) and vesicles. This restricted expression pattern aligns with its emerging role as a negative regulator of OPC differentiation; activation by damage-released ligands keeps OPCs in a proliferative state, while receptor desensitization is required for myelination. ClinVar records document 19 pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number losses and gains at 2q14.3, though specific point mutations are less characterized. We hypothesize that GPR17 acts as a 'damage sensor' in the CNS, detecting extracellular UDP/UTP and leukotrienes released during injury to halt differentiation and promote glial scarring. Dysregulation of this checkpoint likely contributes to demyelinating diseases like Multiple Sclerosis (failed remyelination) or leukodystrophies, making GPR17 a promising therapeutic target for promoting repair.
