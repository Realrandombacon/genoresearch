## Description
SPINK4 (Serine peptidase inhibitor Kazal type 4, also known as PEC-60 or HEL136) is an 86 amino acid secreted protein localized to the Golgi apparatus, vesicles, and nucleoplasm (UniProt O60575) with high-confidence well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 84.8). The protein features a canonical C-terminal Kazal-type serine protease inhibitor domain (IPR002350/IPR036058/PF00050/SM00280, residues 31-86) forming a compact ~56 aa module (~65%) that defines its inhibitory function. This domain belongs to the broader Kazal-like superfamily (G3DSA:3.30.60.30/SSF100895) and contains the conserved reactive site signature (PS00282, residues 46-68) critical for binding target proteases. The SPINK4-like family (IPR039932/PTHR21179, residues 29-86) confirms its specific evolutionary lineage. Molecular functions include serine-type endopeptidase inhibitor activity (GO:0004867) and protein binding (GO:0005515), placing SPINK4 within the mucosal defense and digestive regulation machinery.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places SPINK4 within a specialized intestinal goblet cell network: strongest interaction with MUC2 (0.659, Mucin-2, the primary structural component of the intestinal mucus barrier), indicating a co-secretory role in mucus layer formation. High confidence links include SPINK2 (0.652, Serine peptidase inhibitor Kazal type 2, a testis-specific paralog suggesting functional divergence), TFF3 (0.597, Trefoil factor 3, a key mucus-stabilizing peptide), and CLCA1 (0.569, Calcium-activated chloride channel regulator 1, involved in mucus secretion). Connections to ZG16 (0.559, Zymogen granule protein 16, a bacteria-agglutinating lectin), ITLN1 (0.554, Intelectin-1, a microbial pattern recognition receptor), and AGR2 (0.548, Anterior gradient protein 2, essential for MUC2 folding) reinforce its role in the secretory pathway of goblet cells. GUCA2A (0.521, Guanylate cyclase activator 2A), DEFA6 (0.483, Defensin alpha 6), and REG4 (0.442, Regenerating islet-derived protein 4) complete the antimicrobial and repair network.\n\nHPA expression reveals a tissue-enriched pattern with highest levels in the intestine (914.5 nTPM), specifically in goblet cells. The 'group enriched' cancer specificity suggests dysregulation in colorectal cancers. Subcellular localization in the Golgi and vesicles confirms its trajectory through the secretory pathway prior to release into the lumen.\n\nClinVar documents 69 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains affecting the 9p24-13 region: CNV gains (9p24.3-q21.13, 9p24.3-13.1 duplications) and internal duplications. The high variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity. Haploinsufficiency may impair the inhibition of luminal serine proteases (e.g., trypsin, chymotrypsin, or bacterial proteases), leading to excessive degradation of the MUC2 mucus layer, barrier dysfunction, and susceptibility to inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) or infectious colitis. Duplications may cause over-inhibition of proteases, altering digestion or mucus rheology.\n\nConservation is strong across mammals: human (O60575, 86 aa), mouse (O35679, 86 aa), pig (P37109, 86 aa), indicating evolutionary pressure to maintain the Kazal fold and reactive site.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: SPINK4 functions as a guardian of the intestinal mucus barrier by inhibiting specific serine proteases that could otherwise degrade MUC2 or activate harmful inflammatory cascades. Secreted by goblet cells alongside MUC2 and TFF3, SPINK4 likely targets host proteases (e.g., trypsin leaking from pancreatic ducts) or bacterial proteases (e.g., from Enterobacteriaceae) to preserve mucus integrity. The compact Kazal domain uses its reactive site loop to bind the protease active site in a substrate-mimic fashion, forming a stable complex. Interaction with AGR2 suggests proper folding in the ER/Golgi is critical. Loss of SPINK4 leads to unchecked proteolysis, thinning of the mucus layer, bacterial encroachment, and chronic inflammation (colitis). In 9p duplication syndromes, excess SPINK4 may disrupt normal protease-dependent signaling or digestion.
