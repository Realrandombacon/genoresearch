## Description
LOC129389650 is a 200bp cis-regulatory element (CRE) located on chromosome 6q23.2 (GRCh38: 134957216-134957416), functionally validated as a transcriptional silencer through STARR-seq massively parallel reporter assays (MPRAs) in K562 erythroleukemia cells. This locus lacks canonical promoter, enhancer, or CTCF-bound insulator chromatin signatures but demonstrates repressive activity against the super core promoter SCP1. As a non-coding regulatory sequence, it has no UniProt accession, protein domains, STRING interaction partners, or Human Protein Atlas expression data (query not applicable for non-coding locus).\n\nClinVar analysis reveals 3 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variants disrupting this region, representing LOW CNV burden (compared to LOC129389757: 16 variants, ZNF716: 9 variants, LOC129390592: 9 variants, LOC129389193: 19 variants, LOC101928389: 82 variants). The variant spectrum shows MODERATE DUPLICATION BIAS: 2 copy number gains (duplications spanning 6q23.2-27 ~32 Mb and 6q13-24.1 ~68 Mb) versus 1 deletion at the locus (single allele deletion). This ~2:1 ratio (67% duplications, 33% deletions) indicates moderate gain-of-function dosage sensitivity where increased silencing activity is somewhat more pathogenic than reduced silencing, contrasting with balanced elements like LOC129389757 (~50:50) and extreme duplication-biased elements like LOC129389193 (100% duplications).\n\nThe genomic context places LOC129389650 within 6q23.2, a region associated with 6q deletion syndromes (characterized by intellectual disability, growth retardation, cardiac defects, and craniofacial dysmorphism). Nearby 6q23 genes include MYB (proto-oncogene, hematopoietic transcription factor, leukemia), AHI1 (Abelson helper integration site-1, Joubert syndrome, cerebellar development), and QKI (KH domain containing RNA binding, myelination, oligodendrocyte differentiation). The 6q23 region is critical for neurodevelopment, hematopoiesis, and RNA processing.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: LOC129389650 functions as a distal silencer within the 6q23.2 topologically associating domain (TAD), potentially regulating MYB, AHI1, or QKI in hematopoietic and neuronal contexts. The moderate ~2:1 duplication bias suggests that GAIN-of-silencing (triplication, 150% activity) is moderately more pathogenic than LOSS-of-function (haploinsufficiency, 50% activity). This indicates the target gene(s) tolerate reduced repression better than excessive silencing. The mechanism likely involves recruitment of co-repressor complexes that maintain basal expression of MYB (critical for hematopoietic progenitor maintenance) or AHI1 (cerebellar development). In K562 cells (erythroleukemia), this silencer may regulate MYB or globin locus regulators. The 6q23.2 location near MYB (oncogenic transcription factor) and AHI1 (Joubert syndrome gene) raises the possibility that LOC129389650 prevents ectopic silencing of essential hematopoietic and neurodevelopmental genes. MYB overexpression causes T-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia. AHI1 mutations cause Joubert syndrome (cerebellar vermis hypoplasia, ataxia, intellectual disability). The LOW CNV burden (3 variants) indicates this element has modest mutational target size, with moderate gain-of-function dosage sensitivity suggesting 6q23 duplication phenotypes may involve silencer-mediated gene dysregulation.
