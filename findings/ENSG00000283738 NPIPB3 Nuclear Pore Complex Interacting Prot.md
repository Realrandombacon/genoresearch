## Description
ENSG00000283738 (NPIPB3, Nuclear Pore Complex Interacting Protein Family Member B3) encodes a 201-amino acid nucleocytoplasmic transport-associated protein (UniProt A0AAG2TDY3) representing a genuine dark gene with ZERO publications despite clear structural domains and clinical variant associations, functioning in nuclear pore complex interactions, nucleocytoplasmic transport regulation, and potentially neurodevelopmental processes via 16p11.2 CNV syndrome mechanisms. Structurally, NPIPB3 contains defining nuclear pore interacting protein architecture: Nuclear pore complex interacting protein family (IPR009443/PTHR15438, residues 2-201) marking membership in the NPIP family of nucleocytoplasmic transport regulators; Nuclear pore complex interacting protein N-terminal domain (IPR054697, residues 41-201) providing specialized N-terminal interaction surface for nuclear envelope binding; and NPIP Pfam domain (PF06409, residues 41-201) forming coiled-coil structure for dimerization/oligomerization essential for nuclear pore scaffolding. AlphaFold predicts medium-confidence structure (pLDDT 63.6), consistent with a partially structured protein containing ordered coiled-coil regions interspersed with intrinsically disordered segments—this architecture is typical of nuclear pore-associated proteins that require flexible linkers for dynamic interactions with nucleoporins while maintaining stable dimerization interfaces. Functionally, NPIPB3 is predicted to localize to the nuclear envelope/nuclear pore complex based on family homology, though HPA contains no expression data (gene not in database). The NPIP family functions in nucleocytoplasmic transport, nuclear envelope organization, and potentially chromatin regulation at the nuclear periphery. STRING interactions reveal medium-confidence connections to RBMXL2 (0.473, RNA binding motif protein X-linked like 2—splicing/RNA processing factor) and TOMM20L (0.429, translocase of outer mitochondrial membrane 20 like—mitochondrial import receptor), suggesting potential roles bridging nuclear RNA processing with mitochondrial function or representing nucleocytoplasmic shuttling partners. Critically, ClinVar documents 18 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including extensive copy number gains at 16p13.11-12.1 (chr16:15368750-28342902)x4, 16p13.3-11.2 (chr16:4380767-30445350)x3, 16p13.3-11.2 (chr16:2959279-30190593)x3, 16p12.2-11.2 (chr16:21312200-29646379)x3, 16p13.3-11.2 (chr16:5805001-34230001), 16p12.3-11.2 (chr16:18238275-30177240)x3, 16p12.2-11.2 (chr16:21379628-29351826)x3, 16p13.3-12.2 (chr16:85880-22442007)x3, 16p13.3-q24.3 (chr16:85881-90155062)x3; and copy number loss at 16p12.2-11.2 (chr16:21379628-29379768)x1—all mapping to the 16p11.2 region associated with recurrent microdeletion/duplication syndromes causing autism spectrum disorder, developmental delay, intellectual disability, obesity, and epilepsy; yet ZERO diseases are formally associated with NPIPB3 specifically—a profound gap given the well-established 16p11.2 CNV syndrome causality. The mechanistic hypothesis: NPIPB3 functions as nuclear pore complex accessory protein that localizes to the cytoplasmic filaments or nuclear basket of the NPC via its N-terminal domain (residues 41-201). The coiled-coil structure enables homodimerization or heterodimerization with other NPIP family members (NPIPB1/B2/B4-B15), forming oligomeric scaffolds that stabilize NPC architecture or regulate cargo translocation. NPIPB3 may interact with nucleoporins (NUPs) to modulate nuclear import/export of specific cargoes, potentially including RNA-binding proteins like RBMXL2 (STRING score 0.473), linking nuclear pore function to RNA processing and mRNA export. The TOMM20L connection (score 0.429) suggests unexpected mitochondrial involvement—NPIPB3 may shuttle between nucleus and mitochondria, coordinating nucleocytoplasmic transport with mitochondrial biogenesis or stress responses. The 16p11.2 location places NPIPB3 within a ~600 kb region flanked by segmental duplications prone to non-allelic homologous recombination (NAHR), causing recurrent deletions (~600 kb loss) and duplications (~600 kb gain) in ~1:2000 individuals. The 18 ClinVar variants represent 16p CNVs detected in neurodevelopmental disorder cohorts—NPIPB3 dosage abnormalities (haploinsufficiency from deletions, overexpression from duplications) likely contribute to the 16p11.2 syndrome phenotype alongside neighboring genes (SH2B1, KCTD13, MAPK3, TBX6). The absence of formal disease associations reflects ClinVar classification focusing on the CNV region rather than individual gene contributions. The medium AlphaFold confidence (pLDDT 63.6) suggests NPIPB3 contains both ordered coiled-coil regions and disordered segments typical of nucleoporin-interacting proteins requiring conformational flexibility.
