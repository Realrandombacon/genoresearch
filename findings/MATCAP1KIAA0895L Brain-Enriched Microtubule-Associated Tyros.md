## Description
MATCAP1 (microtubule-associated tyrosine carboxypeptidase 1), also known as KIAA0895L or TMCP1, is a 471-amino acid enzyme encoded on chromosome 16q22.1 that catalyzes tubulin tyrosine carboxypeptidase activity—a critical post-translational modification regulating microtubule function. InterPro analysis reveals the defining microtubule-associated tyrosine carboxypeptidase family domain (IPR012548, positions 1-470; PF08014, positions 235-456) and a DUF1704 domain (SM01154, positions 152-461) characteristic of this enzyme family. AlphaFold structure prediction shows high confidence (global pLDDT 76.6), indicating a well-folded, stable enzyme with defined catalytic architecture. HPA expression demonstrates brain-enriched specificity (79.4 nTPM in brain) with tissue-enhanced expression detected across all tissues, and subcellular localization to nucleoplasm and vesicles. STRING interactions reveal strong associations with tubulin proteins: TUBB3 (score 0.901) and TUBA1B (score 0.900)—both high-confidence interactions confirming its role in microtubule biology. Additional partners include EXOC3L1 (0.591), ESPNL (0.560), B3GNT9 (0.477), PITHD1 (0.474), LRRC29 (0.447), GABPB1 (0.439), TMEM208 (0.435), and FBXL8 (0.418). ClinVar contains zero variants—no known pathogenic mutations or disease associations despite its essential enzymatic function. Literature analysis shows only 4 total papers (DARK status), with key 2023 studies identifying MATCAP1 as part of a carboxypeptidase family catalyzing α- and β-tubulin tail processing and deglutamylation, and characterizing it as the MATCAP detyrosinase. MATCAP1 functions by removing C-terminal tyrosine residues from α-tubulin, generating detyrosinated tubulin (Δ2-tubulin)—a modification that stabilizes microtubules, regulates motor protein trafficking, and influences cell division, neuronal development, and intracellular transport. The brain-enriched expression pattern suggests particular importance in neuronal microtubule dynamics, axonal transport, and brain development (as noted in Alliance of Genome Resources). Despite its fundamental role in microtubule regulation, MATCAP1 remains understudied compared to other tubulin-modifying enzymes like CCP1-6, representing a genuine dark gene in cytoskeletal biology with potential implications for neurodevelopmental disorders and cancer (where microtubule dynamics are frequently dysregulated).
