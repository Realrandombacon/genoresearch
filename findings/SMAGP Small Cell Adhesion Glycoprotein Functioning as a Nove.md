## Description
SMAGP (Q0VAQ4) encodes a 97 amino acid small transmembrane glycoprotein that functions as a critical cell surface regulator of cell adhesion, immune evasion, and metabolic reprogramming in cancer contexts. Originally characterized as a cell adhesion molecule, recent discoveries identify SMAGP as a novel 'Don't Eat Me' signal overexpressed in gastrointestinal (GI) cancers and bladder cancer, enabling tumor cells to evade macrophage-mediated phagocytosis similar to CD47 but via a distinct mechanism. InterPro analysis reveals a compact architecture specific to this protein family: a Small Cell Adhesion Glycoprotein family domain (IPR043243/PF28228/PTHR47394, positions 1-97/29-63/1-97) defining the entire protein as a unique functional unit; a transmembrane helix domain (IPR060107, positions 29-63) anchoring the protein in the plasma membrane with an extracellular N-terminus and cytoplasmic C-terminus topology; and classification as a predicted membrane protein consistent with its role as a cell surface receptor. AlphaFold predicts a confident structure (pLDDT 71.1) for this small protein, suggesting a stable fold where the transmembrane helix (positions 29-63) is well-defined while the short extracellular and intracellular domains may adopt specific conformations for ligand binding or signal transduction.
