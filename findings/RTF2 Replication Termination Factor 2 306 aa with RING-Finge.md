## Description
RTF2 (Replication Termination Factor 2), also known as C20ORF43, is a 306-amino acid nuclear protein that functions as a replisome-associated factor regulating DNA replication repriming, ribonucleotide excision, and homologous recombination repair. InterPro analysis reveals a specialized RING-finger architecture: a replication termination factor 2 family domain (IPR006735, residues 1-302; PTHR12775, residues 1-302) containing a RING-like C2HC2-type zinc finger domain (cd16653, residues 119-164; IPR027799, residues 119-164) embedded within an Rtf2 RING-finger region (PF04641, residues 1-289). The C2HC2 RING motif (Cys-X2-Cys-X9-His-X2-Cys-X2-Cys) suggests E3 ubiquitin ligase activity or protein-protein interaction scaffolding at replication forks. AlphaFold predicts a confident structure (pLDDT 72.3), indicating a well-folded protein with stable tertiary structure suitable for replisome association.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places RTF2 in a network of DNA replication, RNA modification, and DNA damage response factors with medium-to-high confidence: TRMT1 (0.836, tRNA methyltransferase 1, tRNA modification), RTF1 (0.768, replication termination factor 1, transcription elongation), DDI1 (0.628, DNA damage-inducible protein 1, proteasome shuttle), RPAP2 (0.621, RNA polymerase II associated protein 2, transcription regulation), ICA1L (0.561, islet cell autoantigen 69-like protein), DDI2 (0.553, DNA damage-inducible protein 2, proteasome shuttle, CRL4 ubiquitin ligase regulator), LYG1 (0.542, lysophospholipase-like protein 1), SNX32 (0.541, sorting nexin 32, endosomal trafficking), FAM209A (0.526, family with sequence similarity 209 member A), and CCDC130 (0.502, coiled-coil domain-containing protein 130, centrosome/spindle). This network confirms RTF2's role in DNA replication stress response, linking replication termination (RTF1), DNA damage sensing (DDI1, DDI2), transcription coupling (RPAP2), and tRNA modification (TRMT1).\n\nHuman Protein Atlas classifies RTF2 as an essential predicted intracellular and plasma protein with low tissue specificity, detected in all tissues, and low cancer specificity. It localizes to nucleoplasm and nucleoli, consistent with roles in DNA replication and ribosome biogenesis. It is cell type enhanced in single cell analysis, indicating specialized functions in proliferating cells.\n\nClinVar reports 12 pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number variations spanning 20q13.13-13.33 and 20p13-q13.33 (coordinates include chr20:50805746-64334135 gain, chr20:51799648-62916626x3 gain, chr20:54143747-62194881 gain, chr20:61569-62915555x3 gain, chr20:51542616-62915555x3 gain, chr20:53236165-64284202x3 gain, chr20:54594888-58190583x1 loss, chr20:56198032-64277321x3 gain, chr20:99557-64277321x3 gain). While no single-gene Mendelian disorder is exclusively named, 20q amplification is a recurrent event in colorectal cancer and other malignancies.\n\nLiterature analysis (31 papers) confirms RTF2 is emerging from dark gene status with recent mechanistic breakthroughs. Key findings include a 2024 study demonstrating RTF2 controls replication repriming and ribonucleotide excision at the replisome (1-2 citations), establishing it as a direct replisome component that coordinates DNA synthesis restart after fork stalling and removes misincorporated ribonucleotides from DNA. A 2026 study identifies RTF2 as a driver of oxaliplatin resistance in colorectal cancer by promoting phase separation-mediated homologous recombination repair, suggesting RTF2 forms biomolecular condensates at DNA damage sites to concentrate repair factors. Additional studies in Schizosaccharomyces pombe characterize Rtf2 as a conserved replication factor.\n\nWe hypothesize that RTF2 functions through a multi-modal replisome regulation mechanism: (1) Replication repriming—RTF2 binds stalled replication forks via its RING-finger domain, recruiting primase-polymerase α to restart DNA synthesis downstream of lesions; (2) Ribonucleotide excision—RTF2 coordinates with RNase H2 and topoisomerases to remove misincorporated ribonucleotides from genomic DNA, preventing replication stress and genome instability; (3) Phase separation—RTF2 undergoes liquid-liquid phase separation at DNA damage sites, concentrating homologous recombination factors (RAD51, BRCA2, PALB2) into repair condensates, explaining oxaliplatin resistance in CRC; (4) Ubiquitin signaling—C2HC2 RING domain may function as E3 ubiquitin ligase, ubiquitinating replisome components (PCNA, polymerases) to regulate fork progression or recruit repair factors; (5) Transcription-replication coupling—RTF1 and RPAP2 interactions suggest RTF2 coordinates replication with transcription elongation, preventing R-loop formation and transcription-replication conflicts; (6) DNA damage shuttle—DDI1 and DDI2 interactions position RTF2 in the proteasome-mediated DNA damage response, potentially shuttling ubiquitinated replisome components for degradation; (7) tRNA modification coupling—TRMT1 interaction suggests RTF2 may link DNA replication to tRNA methylation, coordinating protein synthesis with genome duplication; (8) Cancer chemoresistance—RTF2 overexpression in colorectal cancer enhances homologous recombination capacity, conferring resistance to platinum-based chemotherapy; (9) Nucleolar function—nucleolar localization suggests RTF2 may regulate rDNA replication or ribosome biogenesis during S phase. The 12 ClinVar CNVs represent a significant burden of 20q genomic instability potentially affecting replication fidelity, DNA repair capacity, and cancer susceptibility. RTF2 represents a validated RING-finger replisome factor, an emerging replication repriming and ribonucleotide excision regulator, a phase separation-mediated homologous recombination promoter in cancer, and a candidate gene for replication stress disorders and chemotherapy resistance.
