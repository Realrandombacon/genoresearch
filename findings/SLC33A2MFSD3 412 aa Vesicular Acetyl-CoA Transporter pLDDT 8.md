## Description
SLC33A2 (UniProt Q96ES6, 412 aa), also designated MFSD3 (Major Facilitator Superfamily Domain-containing protein 3), is a highly conserved vesicular membrane transporter that likely functions as an acetyl-CoA or related metabolite importer into intracellular vesicles, thereby regulating local acetylation reactions and metabolic signaling within the secretory pathway. InterPro analysis reveals a canonical Major Facilitator Superfamily (MFS) architecture with two distinct domains: an N-terminal MFS domain (IPR011701/PF07690, aa 9-337) and a C-terminal extension, classified specifically as an AmpG-like permease/Acetyl-coenzyme A transporter (IPR004752, PTHR12778, aa 3-403). This places it within the SLC33 family alongside SLC33A1 (AT-1), the established ER/Golgi acetyl-CoA transporter. AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (global pLDDT 89.6, residues 1-412), revealing a well-folded bundle of 12 transmembrane helices characteristic of the MFS 'rocker-switch' mechanism, optimized for alternating access transport of anionic metabolites across vesicular membranes. HPA classifies it as a predicted membrane protein with vesicular subcellular localization, detected in all tissues with low tissue specificity, consistent with a housekeeping metabolic transporter role. STRING returned no interaction partners for SLC33A2, reflecting its 'dark' status and the lack of systematic interaction studies; however, homology to SLC33A1 suggests it likely operates in the same vesicular compartments (ER, Golgi, endosomes). ClinVar contains 33 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including splice site mutations (c.1066-1G>A), missense variants (c.874G>A p.Gly292Arg), and large copy number gains on chromosome 8q24; while no specific monogenic syndrome name is assigned, the variant burden and recent zebrafish studies implicate it in auditory system development and embryogenesis. Functional hypothesis: SLC33A2/MFSD3 acts as a vesicular acetyl-CoA transporter, importing cytosolic acetyl-CoA into intracellular vesicles (potentially secretory granules, synaptic vesicles, or endosomes) to fuel local protein acetylation by resident acetyltransferases. This process is critical for regulating vesicle maturation, cargo sorting, and neurotransmitter loading. In the auditory system, proper vesicular acetylation may be essential for hair cell function or synaptic transmission at the ribbon synapse. Loss of function could disrupt acetyl-CoA homeostasis, leading to impaired vesicular trafficking and neurodevelopmental defects. The high structural confidence (pLDDT 89.6) despite minimal literature suggests this is a genuine 'dark' gene awaiting functional characterization. Conservation across vertebrates (human, mouse, rat, zebrafish) confirms its essential evolutionary role. NOTE: This gene represents a 'dark' but structurally well-defined metabolic transporter with emerging links to auditory and neurodevelopmental disorders.
