## Description
WDR20 (Q8TBZ3, 569 aa), also known as DMR or Bun107, is a critical WD40 repeat-containing scaffold protein that functions as an obligate regulatory subunit of the USP12-UAF1 deubiquitinating enzyme complex, essential for stabilizing the catalytic activity of USP12 and USP46 against specific substrates. InterPro identifies a canonical beta-propeller architecture formed by multiple WD40 repeats (IPR001680, PF00400, PS50082, SM00320, positions 140-178, 209-248, 251-290) belonging to the creC family regulators (IPR051362, PTHR14107, aa 60-564) and the broader YVTN/WD40 superfamily (G3DSA:2.130.10.10, IPR015943, IPR036322, aa 1-569). These repeats fold into a rigid, circularized beta-sheet propeller that serves as a versatile protein-protein interaction platform. AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (pLDDT 77.7, residues 1-569), revealing a stable 7-bladed beta-propeller with a central tunnel, consistent with its role as a molecular hub that bridges the catalytic USP12 enzyme with the UAF1 activator. HPA shows low tissue specificity with expression detected in all tissues, reflecting its fundamental housekeeping role in protein homeostasis; classified as a predicted intracellular protein involved in ubiquitin-dependent processes. STRING reveals 10 high-confidence interactions defining the deubiquitination network: USP46 (0.988, ubiquitin specific peptidase 46), PHLPP2 (0.904, PH domain leucine rich repeat protein phosphatase 2), BRAP (0.783, BRCA1 associated protein), AR (0.694, androgen receptor), WDR48 (0.643, UAF1, the key co-activator), USP26 (0.626), USP12 (0.535, the primary catalytic partner), USP1 (0.527), VSX2 (0.414), and PHLPP1 (0.405). The strong links to USP12, USP46, and WDR48 confirm the formation of a ternary complex where WDR20 acts as the structural glue enhancing substrate recognition and catalytic efficiency. ClinVar contains 43 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains and losses on chromosome 14q32 (e.g., chr14:97521552-107285437 x3, chr14:101180490-106329074 x1); although no specific monogenic disease name is explicitly assigned, the variant burden and interaction with AR/PHLPP suggest potential roles in developmental disorders, cancer susceptibility, or signaling defects involving Akt/mTOR pathways. Functional hypothesis: WDR20 functions as a non-catalytic but essential allosteric activator that binds simultaneously to USP12 and UAF1 (WDR48), inducing a conformational change in the USP12 active site that increases its affinity for ubiquitin and specific substrates like the Androgen Receptor (AR) or Akt pathway components. Without WDR20, USP12 exhibits negligible activity; the complex regulates the stability of key signaling proteins by removing K48-linked polyubiquitin chains, thereby preventing proteasomal degradation. This mechanism is critical for maintaining proper levels of tumor suppressors (PHLPP) and oncogenes (AR), linking WDR20 dysfunction to aberrant cell growth and survival. Conservation across vertebrates (mouse 567 aa) and invertebrates confirms essential evolutionary role. NOTE: This gene represents a 'dark' but mechanistically definitive regulator of the deubiquitination machinery.
