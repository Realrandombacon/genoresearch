# PRRC1: ITPase-Like NTP Phosphatase Regulating COPII Vesicle Trafficking from ER to Golgi

## Description
PRRC1 (Proline Rich Coiled-Coil 1) is a 445 amino acid protein (UniProt Q96M27) with a partially structured conformation (AlphaFold pLDDT 67.8, medium confidence), consistent with proteins containing both ordered catalytic domains and flexible regions for protein-protein interactions. The protein contains a non-canonical purine NTP phosphatase/PRRC1 domain (IPR026533/PF01931/DUF84, residues 260-376) within an ITPase-like structural superfamily (IPR029001/SSF52972, residues 247-437; G3DSA:3.90.950.10, residues 247-387), placing it in the PRRC1 family (IPR026534/PTHR23276, residues 2-443).\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals PRRC1 functions in COPII vesicle-mediated ER-to-Golgi trafficking: strongest interactions with SEC23A (0.796) and SEC24C (0.728), core COPII coat complex components, plus SEC24A (0.684), SAR1A (0.614, the small GTPase initiating COPII assembly), SEC24D (0.592), SEC23IP (0.572, Sec23-interacting protein), SEC23B (0.526), SEC24B (0.512), MINAR2 (0.507), and ARCN1 (0.475, COPI coatomer subunit). This interaction network positions PRRC1 as a COPII vesicle regulator.\n\nHPA expression shows ubiquitous detection across all tissues with low tissue specificity and vesicle subcellular localization, consistent with housekeeping secretory pathway functions. The protein is classified as predicted intracellular and membrane-associated.\n\nClinVar documents 18 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large chromosome 5q copy number variations (gains and losses spanning 5q14.3-q35.3 regions). While specific PRRC1 point mutations are not yet catalogued, these CNVs suggest dosage sensitivity potentially causing developmental disorders through impaired protein trafficking.\n\nConservation is evident from mouse ortholog (Q3UPH1, 443 aa), rat ortholog (Q3T1I4, 443 aa), and zebrafish ortholog (Q5XJA3, 435 aa), indicating functional importance across vertebrates.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: PRRC1 acts as a non-canonical NTP phosphatase that hydrolyzes inosine triphosphate (ITP) or related purine NTPs to regulate COPII vesicle formation and cargo selection. The ITPase-like domain may prevent incorporation of non-canonical nucleotides into nascent proteins or modulate SAR1A GTPase cycling during COPII coat assembly. By interacting with SEC23/SEC24 heterodimers, PRRC1 could coordinate nucleotide pool quality control with secretory trafficking, ensuring only properly folded cargo exits the ER. CNV-mediated dosage imbalance disrupts ER-to-Golgi transport, causing developmental phenotypes through impaired secretion of growth factors or membrane proteins.
