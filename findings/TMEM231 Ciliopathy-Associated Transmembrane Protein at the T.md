# TMEM231: Ciliopathy-Associated Transmembrane Protein at the Transition Zone

**Date:** 2026-03-23T01:49:22.433557

**Quality Score:** 4.25/10  (E=6.9, D=1.6) [MODERATE]

## Description
TMEM231 encodes a 316-amino acid transmembrane protein (UniProt Q9H6L2) localized to the ciliary transition zone, a critical diffusion barrier between the cilium and cytoplasm. InterPro analysis reveals membership in the CHST5 carbohydrate sulfotransferase family (IPR019306, PF10149, PTHR14605), though TMEM231 lacks catalytic residues suggesting neofunctionalization. AlphaFold predicts a well-folded structure (pLDDT 88.4) with multiple transmembrane helices. STRING interactions show exclusive association with ciliopathy proteins: B9D1 (0.998), TCTN1 (0.995), CC2D2A (0.994), TMEM67 (0.993), TCTN2 (0.991), TMEM216 (0.987), MKS1 (0.982), CEP290 (0.978), TMEM17 (0.967), B9D2 (0.951) - forming the MKS/Joubert syndrome module. HPA shows enriched expression in fallopian tube (69.1 nTPM) and choroid plexus (38.2 nTPM), tissues with high ciliary density. ClinVar contains 136 pathogenic variants including frameshifts (p.Ala88fs, p.Glu57fs, p.Gln169fs) and nonsense mutations (p.Ser103Ter, p.Tyr171Ter, p.Trp83Ter) causing Joubert syndrome and Meckel syndrome. Conservation spans vertebrates (mouse 315aa, rat 315aa, bovine 316aa, chicken 320aa). Mechanistically, TMEM231 likely functions as a structural scaffold within the transition zone diffusion barrier, recruiting ciliary cargo and preventing inappropriate protein entry. Loss-of-function disrupts ciliary gating, causing developmental defects in neural tube (Joubert) and renal/retinal tissues (Meckel). NOTE: This gene is NOT truly "dark" - it is well-characterized in ciliopathy literature with clear disease mechanisms.
