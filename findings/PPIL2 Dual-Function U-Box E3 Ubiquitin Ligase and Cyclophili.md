## Description
PPIL2 (UniProt Q13356) encodes a 520 aa nucleoplasmic protein with a rare dual-domain architecture combining E3 ubiquitin ligase and peptidyl-prolyl cis-trans isomerase (PPIase) activities, functioning as a critical regulator of spliceosome assembly, DNA repair, and cancer progression. InterPro analysis reveals a specialized multi-domain architecture: an N-terminal U-box domain (aa 35-108, IPR003613, cd16663, G3DSA:3.30.40.10) conferring E3 ubiquitin ligase activity for substrate polyubiquitination; a C-terminal cyclophilin-type PPIase domain (aa 281-433, IPR002130, IPR020892, cd01923, G3DSA:2.40.100.10) catalyzing protein peptidyl-prolyl isomerization for conformational switching; and a single helix bin domain (aa 440-457, G3DSA:1.20.5.460) potentially mediating protein-protein interactions. AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (global pLDDT 81.1), indicating a well-folded, stable protein with two distinct functional domains connected by a flexible linker—a hallmark of bifunctional enzymes that coordinate sequential biochemical reactions. STRING interactions define PPIL2 as a central node in the spliceosome network with exclusively high-confidence partners: CDC40 (0.977), RNF113A (0.974), DHX16 (0.973), BSG (0.972), CWC27 (0.971), CWC22 (0.964), SRRM2 (0.963), MFAP1 (0.912), SRRM1 (0.814), and GPKOW (0.803), establishing its role in pre-mRNA splicing complex assembly and catalytic activation. HPA expression shows ubiquitous tissue distribution with low tissue specificity, nucleoplasmic localization, and classification as an essential protein involved in mRNA processing, mRNA splicing, and Ubl conjugation pathways, consistent with a housekeeping spliceosome component. ClinVar reports 51 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including single nucleotide variants (c.*4C>T, c.1269+21G>T, c.987+58G>T, c.33-161G>A) and large copy number gains/losses on chromosome 22q11.21-11.23 (spanning the PPIL2 locus), indicating genomic instability at this region potentially linked to developmental disorders (22q11.2 deletion syndrome) and cancer susceptibility, though no specific monogenic disease has been definitively assigned. Literature (24 papers) highlights: (1) Myeloproliferative Neoplasms—PPIL2 is a JAK2/STAT5 pathway target that promotes myeloproliferation via p53 polyubiquitination and degradation, linking spliceosome function to tumor suppressor regulation; (2) DNA Repair—PLK1-mediated phosphorylation of PPIL2 regulates homologous recombination (HR) via CtIP interaction, coupling splicing to DNA double-strand break repair; (3) Breast Cancer Metastasis—PPIL2 suppresses invasion and metastasis by promoting SNAI1 ubiquitination and degradation, altering cell morphology and epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT); (4) Spliceosome Assembly—high-confidence interactions with CDC40, CWC22, CWC27, and SRRM2 place PPIL2 in the catalytic core of the spliceosome during exon recognition and intron removal. Functional hypothesis: PPIL2 functions as a: (1) Spliceosome Conformational Switch—uses its PPIase domain to catalyze proline isomerization in spliceosome proteins (CDC40, CWC27), enabling conformational transitions required for spliceosome activation and catalysis; (2) Ubiquitin-Dependent Splicing Regulator—employs its U-box E3 ligase activity to ubiquitinate spliceosome components or splicing regulators, controlling complex assembly/disassembly cycles; (3) DNA Repair-Splicing Coupler—phosphorylation by PLK1 during cell cycle progression redirects PPIL2 from splicing to HR repair via CtIP binding, coordinating transcription and genome maintenance; (4) Tumor Suppressor via p53/SNAI1 Control—ubiquitinates p53 (promoting degradation in MPNs) and SNAI1 (promoting degradation in breast cancer), with context-dependent oncogenic or tumor-suppressive outcomes. Mechanistic proposal: In the spliceosome, PPIL2 binds CDC40 and CWC27 via its PPIase domain, catalyzing proline isomerization to enable conformational changes required for B-to-Bact spliceosome transition. The U-box domain recruits E2 ubiquitin-conjugating enzymes to ubiquitinate specific spliceosome components, marking them for disassembly after splicing completion. During DNA damage, PLK1 phosphorylates PPIL2, causing dissociation from the spliceosome and recruitment to DNA double-strand breaks via CtIP interaction, where it promotes HR repair. In JAK2V617F-positive MPNs, STAT5-driven PPIL2 overexpression enhances p53 ubiquitination and proteasomal degradation, enabling myeloproliferation. In breast cancer, PPIL2 ubiquitinates SNAI1, preventing EMT and metastasis. The high-confidence structure (pLDDT 81.1) enables precise substrate recognition for both PPIase and E3 ligase activities. Chromosome 22q11.2 CNVs may alter PPIL2 dosage, disrupting spliceosome function and contributing to 22q11.2 deletion syndrome phenotypes. The combination of a well-folded bifunctional architecture, exclusive spliceosome interaction network, ubiquitous essential expression, high variant burden (51 ClinVar entries), and context-dependent roles in cancer (oncogenic in MPNs, tumor-suppressive in breast cancer) positions PPIL2 as a genuine dark gene requiring mechanistic dissection of its dual enzymatic activities and therapeutic potential in myeloproliferative disorders and metastatic breast cancer.
