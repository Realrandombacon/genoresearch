## Description
FILIP1L (Filamin A-Interacting Protein 1-Like, UniProt Q4L180) is a 1135 amino acid plasma membrane and mitochondrial protein with moderate characterization (68 papers) that functions as a cortactin-binding actin dynamics regulator involved in cancer progression, invadopodia formation, angiogenesis, and vascular function. The protein contains a cortactin-binding protein-2 N-terminal domain (IPR019131/PF09727, residues 66-248) and belongs to the cortactin-binding and actin dynamics regulator family (IPR050719/PTHR23166, residues 368-1128), placing it in the actin cytoskeleton regulatory machinery with a CATH superfamily fold (G3DSA:1.20.5.1700, residues 703-777). STRING interactions reveal medium-confidence partnerships with DNA repair protein RAD51B (0.610), vesicle trafficking regulator TBC1D23 (0.608), extracellular matrix protease ADAMTS9 (0.577), TRAPP complex subunits TRAPPC1 (0.555), TRAPPC8 (0.551), TRAPPC4 (0.539) involved in Golgi-to-endosome trafficking, glycosyltransferase B3GLCT (0.552), endogenous retroviral envelope protein ERVW-1 (0.550, syncytin-1), mitochondrial protein LYRM4 (0.542), and monocarboxylate transporter SLC16A8 (0.519), placing FILIP1L in vesicle trafficking, ECM remodeling, and mitochondrial networks. HPA data shows tissue-enhanced expression with blood vessel enrichment (191.1 nTPM), immune cell enhancement, plasma membrane localization, and low cancer specificity. ClinVar contains 10 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number losses at 3q11.1-21.2/3q11.2-13.31 and copy number gains at 3p13-q12.1/3q11.1-24, though no specific diseases are directly assigned—suggesting potential vascular, developmental, or cancer phenotypes. AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 67.6) indicates medium confidence with partially structured domains and disordered regions typical of actin regulators. Functional hypothesis: FILIP1L acts as a multifunctional actin cytoskeleton regulator through its cortactin-binding domain that controls invadopodia formation and cell migration, where recent 2025 research demonstrates: (1) FILIP1L has mitochondrial localization linking actin dynamics to mitochondrial function in cancer metabolism, (2) EMT and cell cycle control via FILIP1L regulates invadopodia and breast cancer metastasis, (3) TDO2 (tryptophan 2,3-dioxygenase) promotes bladder cancer progression through AhR-mediated SPARC/FILIP1L signaling axis, (4) 2026 functional annotation implicates FILIP1L expression perturbation in mammary fibroblasts influencing breast cancer risk, (5) smoking-associated FILIP1L downregulation enhances lung adenocarcinoma progression through mucin production, inflammation, and fibrosis (2022, 10 citations), suggesting FILIP1L functions as a context-dependent tumor suppressor (lung, breast) and oncogenic driver (bladder via TDO2/AhR) that couples actin cytoskeleton remodeling (invadopodia, cell migration) with vesicle trafficking (TRAPP complex), ECM degradation (ADAMTS9), and mitochondrial metabolism (LYRM4, ERVW-1), with blood vessel enrichment (191.1 nTPM) indicating vascular/angiogenic roles and 3q copy number variants potentially contributing to vascular malformations or developmental disorders through actin dynamics defects, making FILIP1L a potential therapeutic target for metastatic cancers and vascular diseases.
