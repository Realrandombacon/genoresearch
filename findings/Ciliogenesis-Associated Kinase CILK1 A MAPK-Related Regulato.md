## Description
CILK1 (Ciliogenesis Associated Kinase 1), also known as ICK (Intestinal Cell Kinase), is a 632-amino acid serine/threonine protein kinase belonging to the male germ cell-associated kinase (MAK) family within the broader CMGC group of kinases. InterPro analysis confirms a canonical N-terminal protein kinase domain (IPR000719/PF00069, positions 4-284) with a conserved ATP-binding site (IPR017441, 10-34) and active site signature (IPR008271, 121-133), classified under the MAP kinase-like family (IPR050117, 3-329), suggesting evolutionary divergence from classical MAPKs to regulate ciliary functions. STRING interactions reveal a dual functional network: strong connections to DNA replication licensing machinery including CCNL2 (0.865), CDC6 (0.800), MCM3/4/5/2/7 (0.787-0.800), ORC3/2 (0.782-0.792), and CDT1 (0.544), alongside ciliary regulators, indicating a potential role in coordinating cell cycle progression with ciliogenesis or a bifunctional mechanism where CILK1 phosphorylates replication factors to control S-phase entry in ciliated cells. HPA data shows ubiquitous expression across all tissues with low specificity, enhanced detection in specific cell types, and precise subcellular localization to the Golgi apparatus, vesicles, and critically the primary cilium tip, consistent with its role as a ciliary kinase; it is classified as a disease-related gene and potential drug target involved in cilium biogenesis. ClinVar contains 26 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including copy number losses (6p12.3-12.1), frameshifts (p.Tyr555fs), and missense mutations (p.Leu146Val, p.Glu80Lys, p.Pro509Ser), which cause cranioectodermal dysplasia (CED/Sensenbrenner syndrome), a severe ciliopathy characterized by skeletal abnormalities, renal failure, liver fibrosis, and ectodermal defects; recent studies (2025) also link CILK1 variants to Joubert syndrome via failure to activate KATNIP and to epilepsy. AlphaFold structure (AF-Q9UPZ9-F1) predicts medium confidence folding (pLDDT 60.8), reflecting a structured kinase domain coupled with flexible regulatory regions typical of signaling kinases. With only 33 papers, CILK1 represents a 'gray' gene transitioning from dark: while its identity as a ciliary kinase is established, the mechanistic link between its kinase activity, DNA replication licensing partners, and specific ciliopathy phenotypes remains incompletely resolved. The mechanistic hypothesis posits that CILK1 functions as a spatial regulator at the ciliary tip that phosphorylates specific substrates to control intraflagellar transport (IFT) and ciliary length, while simultaneously monitoring cell cycle status via interactions with MCM/ORC complexes to prevent cilia disassembly during inappropriate cell cycle phases, with loss-of-function leading to defective Hedgehog signaling (causing skeletal/renal defects) and aberrant cell cycle re-entry in post-mitotic tissues.
