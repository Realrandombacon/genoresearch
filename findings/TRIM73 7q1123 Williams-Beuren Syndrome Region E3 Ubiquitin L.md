## Description
TRIM73 (Tripartite Motif Containing 73, also TRIM50B) is a 250 amino acid cytosolic protein (UniProt Q86UV7) with a well-folded TRIM domain architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 88.6, high confidence), characteristic of RING-type E3 ubiquitin ligases involved in innate immune responses and protein homeostasis.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals TRIM73 contains the canonical TRIM tripartite motif: an N-terminal RING-type zinc finger (IPR001841/IPR027370/PF13445, residues 16-56; G3DSA:3.30.40.10, residues 3-80) conferring E3 ubiquitin ligase activity, followed by a B-box type 2 zinc finger (IPR000315/PF00643, residues 84-125; G3DSA:3.30.160.60, residues 81-151) for protein-protein interactions and substrate recognition. The protein belongs to the tripartite motif-containing family (IPR050143, residues 12-230) and is specifically grouped with TRIM50/TRIM74 subfamily (cd19787, residues 87-125). The RING domain contains the conserved C3HC4 zinc-coordinating signature (PS00518/PS50089, residues 31-40) essential for ubiquitin transfer.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals TRIM73 functions within the TRIM E3 ligase network: strongest interactions with ALKAL1 (0.540, ALK and LTK ligand 1), TRIM52 (0.480, tripartite motif-containing protein 52), TRIM56 (0.467, tripartite motif-containing protein 56), BBOX1 (0.447, gamma-butyrobetaine hydroxylase), RASA4B (0.445, Ras GTPase activating protein 4B), TMEM270 (0.418), TRIM40 (0.418, tripartite motif-containing protein 40), FAM86C1 (0.418), RNF113B (0.416, RING finger protein 113B), and SPANXN5 (0.407). The TRIM52, TRIM56, and TRIM40 interactions confirm clustering within the TRIM family, while RNF113B represents another RING-type E3 ligase partner.\n\nHPA expression shows ubiquitous detection across all tissues with low tissue specificity and cytosolic subcellular localization, consistent with housekeeping ubiquitination functions. The protein is classified as predicted intracellular with cell type enhanced single cell specificity and low cancer/immune cell specificity.\n\nClinVar documents 25 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all large chromosome 7q11.23 copy number variations (deletions and duplications spanning 7q11.22-q11.23 and 7q11.23-q21.3 regions). These CNVs overlap the Williams-Beuren syndrome critical region (WBSCR), suggesting TRIM73 dosage sensitivity contributes to Williams-Beuren syndrome (deletion) or 7q11.23 duplication syndrome phenotypes through impaired ubiquitin-mediated protein regulation.\n\nConservation is evident from the TRIM family expansion in primates, with TRIM73 located in a cluster with TRIM50, TRIM51, and TRIM74 on chromosome 7q11.23, indicating recent evolutionary expansion for immune and developmental functions.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: TRIM73 acts as a RING-type E3 ubiquitin ligase that ubiquitinates specific substrates involved in innate immune signaling and protein quality control. The RING domain recruits E2 ubiquitin-conjugating enzymes to transfer ubiquitin to target proteins, while the B-box domain mediates substrate recognition or oligomerization. Interaction with TRIM52, TRIM56, and TRIM40 suggests functional redundancy or cooperative assembly into larger ubiquitination complexes. TRIM56 is known to ubiquitinate TBK1 and IRF3 in antiviral responses, suggesting TRIM73 may participate in similar innate immune pathways. Cytosolic localization and ubiquitous expression indicate roles in constitutive protein homeostasis. CNV-mediated dosage imbalance in the 7q11.23 region disrupts ubiquitin ligase stoichiometry, contributing to Williams-Beuren syndrome phenotypes (deletion: developmental delay, cardiovascular defects, hypersocial personality) or duplication syndrome (speech delay, autism spectrum features) through impaired ubiquitin-mediated regulation of neurodevelopmental proteins.
