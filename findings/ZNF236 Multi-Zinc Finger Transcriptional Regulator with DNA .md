# ZNF236: Multi-Zinc Finger Transcriptional Regulator with DNA Repair and Chromatin Remodeling Functions

**Date:** 2026-03-20T13:12:57.260592

## Description
ZNF236 encodes a massive 1845 amino acid C2H2 zinc finger transcription factor localized to chromosome 18q23. InterPro analysis reveals extensive C2H2-type zinc finger domains (IPR013087, IPR036236, PF00096, PF12874, PF13465, PF13912) distributed throughout the protein sequence, with at least 15-20 individual zinc finger motifs spanning residues 35-1205. The G3DSA:3.30.160.60 Classic Zinc Finger homologous superfamily and PTHR24396 family assignment confirm ZNF236 as a canonical transcriptional regulator with zinc finger domains.\n\nSTRING interaction network reveals high-confidence partnerships with ZNF407 (0.819), another multi-zinc finger protein, suggesting functional redundancy or co-regulation. Medium-confidence interactions include ERCC6 (0.661) and ERCC6L2 (0.498), nucleotide excision repair factors, linking ZNF236 to DNA damage response pathways. Additional partners BAALC (0.565, leukemia-associated), CAPN15 (0.522, calpain protease), CCAR2 (0.487, cell cycle regulator), and MLLT3 (0.474, mixed-lineage leukemia translocation partner) suggest roles in hematopoiesis, cell cycle control, and chromatin remodeling.\n\nHPA data shows ZNF236 is ubiquitously expressed (detected in all tissues) with low tissue specificity, localized to nucleoplasm, cell junctions, and cytosol. The protein class annotation includes "Essential proteins" and "Transcription factors," consistent with its predicted DNA-binding transcription factor activity for RNA polymerase II.\n\nClinVar contains 165 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains and losses affecting 18q11.1-23 regions (ranging from 20-80 Mb). The dosage sensitivity and large genomic rearrangements suggest ZNF236 haploinsufficiency contributes to 18q deletion syndrome phenotypes, including developmental delay and neurological abnormalities.\n\nThe combination of extensive zinc finger arrays, DNA repair protein interactions, and ubiquitous expression suggests ZNF236 functions as a genome-wide chromatin organizer that coordinates transcriptional responses to genotoxic stress, potentially recruiting repair machinery to damaged loci through its multivalent DNA-binding capacity.
