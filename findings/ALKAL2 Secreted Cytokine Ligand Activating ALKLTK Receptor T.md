## Description
ALKAL2 (ALK and LTK Ligand 2, also known as FAM150B) is a 152 amino acid secreted cytokine that serves as a potent activating ligand for the ALK (Anaplastic Lymphoma Kinase) and LTK (Leukocyte Tyrosine Kinase) receptor tyrosine kinases. InterPro analysis identifies ALKAL2 as a member of the ALK and LTK ligand family (IPR029364, PF15129, residues 33-149; PTHR28676, residues 1-149) with receptor tyrosine kinase activator activity (GO:0030298) and receptor binding capability (GO:0030971). AlphaFold structure (AF-Q6UX46-F1) predicts high confidence (pLDDT 70.1) across the entire 152 residue sequence, indicating a well-folded secreted protein with stable tertiary structure suitable for receptor engagement. HPA data classifies ALKAL2 as tissue-enhanced with exceptionally high expression in the adrenal gland (212.1 nTPM), and as a predicted secreted protein functioning as a cytokine, consistent with its role as an endocrine/paracrine signaling molecule. STRING interactions reveal extremely tight functional coupling to its primary receptors ALK (score 0.968) and LTK (0.560), confirming its identity as their cognate ligand; additional connections to SH3YL1 (0.605), FAM110C (0.561), and SKOR1 (0.523) suggest downstream signaling network involvement. ClinVar lists 42 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number gains and losses on chromosome 2p25.3, deletions (NC_000002.12:g.100000_1793802del), and splice variants (c.454-9T>G), implicating dosage sensitivity in disease though no specific monogenic syndrome is yet defined. Literature (32 papers) establishes ALKAL2 as a critical oncogenic driver in MYCN-amplified neuroblastoma where it potentiates tumor growth even without ALK mutation, a mediator of persistent visceral hypersensitivity following colitis through nociceptive neuron activation, and a biomarker associated with coronary atherosclerosis in type 2 diabetes. As a dark gene with emerging therapeutic relevance, ALKAL2 represents a master regulator of ALK/LTK signaling bridging neurodevelopment, cancer biology, pain physiology, and metabolic disease through paracrine cytokine signaling.
