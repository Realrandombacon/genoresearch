## Description
LOC105372028 is an uncharacterized genomic locus located on chromosome 18q11.2 (GRCh38: 24725780-24766644), spanning approximately 41kb. Despite its gene-like identifier, it lacks a canonical protein-coding annotation, has no assigned UniProt accession, and could not be resolved to an Ensembl ID for Human Protein Atlas expression analysis. NCBI Gene classifies it simply as "uncharacterized," suggesting it may represent a non-coding RNA, a pseudogene, or a misannotated regulatory element rather than a functional protein-coding gene. The absence of protein domains (InterPro) and interaction partners (STRING) further supports a non-coding classification.\n\nClinVar analysis reveals a high burden of 17 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variants disrupting this region. The variant spectrum is dominated by massive whole-arm or multi-arm duplications spanning nearly the entire chromosome 18 (e.g., 18p11.32-q23, ~80Mb; 18q11.1-23, ~59Mb). A single deletion event is also recorded. The overwhelming bias towards large-scale duplications (16:1 ratio) suggests that this locus resides within a genomic region highly susceptible to replication errors or non-allelic homologous recombination (NAHR), likely due to flanking segmental duplications or low-copy repeats common on chromosome 18. These CNVs are associated with 18q deletion/duplication syndrome (De Grouchy syndrome), characterized by hypomyelination, intellectual disability, short stature, and ear anomalies. While the large size of these CNVs makes it difficult to attribute the phenotype solely to LOC105372028, its consistent inclusion in the critical interval suggests it may harbor dosage-sensitive regulatory elements.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: LOC105372028 functions as a non-coding regulatory hub, potentially containing enhancers or silencers critical for the expression of neighboring genes involved in neural development and myelination. Given its location on 18q, a region rich in genes implicated in nervous system function (e.g., TSHZ1, MBP), this locus may act as a long-range regulator via chromatin looping. The extreme bias towards duplications in ClinVar implies that triplication of this regulatory landscape leads to pathological overexpression of target genes, disrupting the delicate balance required for proper myelination and neurodevelopment. Alternatively, LOC105372028 may encode a novel long non-coding RNA (lncRNA) that acts as a molecular sponge or scaffold for chromatin modifiers. The lack of protein-coding potential shifts the mechanistic focus to transcriptional regulation; loss or gain of this locus likely contributes to the hypomyelination and cognitive deficits seen in 18q syndromes by dysregulating key developmental drivers in cis.
