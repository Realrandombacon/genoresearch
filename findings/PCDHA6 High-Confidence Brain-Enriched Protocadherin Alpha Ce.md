## Description
PCDHA6 (Protocadherin alpha 6, aliases CNR2, CNRN2, CNRS2, CRNR2, PCDH-ALPHA6) is a 950 amino acid type I transmembrane cell adhesion receptor protein (UniProt Q9UN73) with high-confidence well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 73.8) containing canonical protocadherin alpha architecture including 6 extracellular cadherin repeat domains (EC1-EC6, residues 30-129/137-238/246-346/358-445/453-540/548-635), cadherin N-terminal domain (residues 30-111), cadherin C-terminal catenin-binding domain (residues 800-933), cadherin-like superfamily domains (residues 30-109/127-234/230-350), and protocadherin/cadherin-related cell adhesion family (residues 16-773), functioning as a brain-enriched cell adhesion receptor that regulates neuronal circuit assembly and synaptic specificity through calcium-dependent homophilic cell-cell adhesion (GO:0007156) and protein-protein interactions via partnerships with PCDHA7, PCDHA10, PCDHA2, PCDHA8, and PCDHA3.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals PCDHA6 contains canonical protocadherin alpha architecture: cadherin tandem repeat domains (cd11304, residues 32-129/137-238/246-346) forming three ~97-101 aa extracellular cadherin repeat modules (EC1-EC3, ~30% of protein) characteristic of cadherin superfamily proteins with calcium ion binding (GO:0005509) for structural stabilization, cadherin-like domains (IPR002126/G3DSA:2.60.40.60, residues 34-133/155-242/242-350/20-126/134-240/241-347) forming six ~88-106 aa modules (~55-60%) providing the complete EC1-EC6 extracellular domain architecture, cadherin N-terminal domain (IPR013164, residues 30-111) forming an 82 aa module (~9%) providing the membrane-distal adhesive interface, cadherin C-terminal catenin-binding domain (IPR031904, residues 800-933) forming a 134 aa module (~14%) for cytoplasmic scaffold protein recruitment, cadherin-like superfamily (IPR015919, residues 30-109/127-234/230-350) forming three ~84-120 aa modules (~30%) confirming cadherin fold, cadherin conserved site (IPR020894, residues 230-240/338-348/443-453) forming three ~10-11 aa motifs providing calcium-binding DXNDNAPXF motifs essential for cadherin function, protocadherin/cadherin-related cell adhesion family (IPR050174, residues 16-773) forming a 758 aa module (~80%) defining the protocadherin family, and cadherin domain (PF00028, residues 156-233/247-340/358-445) forming three ~77-93 aa modules (~24%) confirming Pfam cadherin identity. The 6 extracellular cadherin repeats span approximately 600 amino acids (residues 30-635, ~63%), while the C-terminal catenin-binding domain spans 134 amino acids (residues 800-933, ~14%), with a ~200 aa transmembrane/juxtamembrane region connecting them. The high-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 73.8) confirms a stable well-folded multi-domain architecture optimized for calcium-dependent homophilic adhesion.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places PCDHA6 within the protocadherin alpha cluster homophilic adhesion network with high confidence interactions: strongest interaction with PCDHA7 (0.840, protocadherin alpha 7, adjacent cluster member), followed by PCDHA10 (0.629, protocadherin alpha 10), PCDHA2 (0.614, protocadherin alpha 2), PCDHA8 (0.597, protocadherin alpha 8), and PCDHA3 (0.548, protocadherin alpha 3). All interactions are with other protocadherin alpha cluster members (PCDHA1-PCDHA13) on chromosome 5q31.3, indicating PCDHA6 functions within the protocadherin alpha gene cluster family for combinatorial homophilic adhesion. The PCDHA7-PCDHA10-PCDHA2-PCDHA8-PCDHA3 partnerships (0.840/0.629/0.614/0.597/0.548) strongly link PCDHA6 to the protocadherin alpha cluster for neuronal circuit assembly and synaptic specificity through stochastic isoform expression and homophilic binding.\n\nHPA expression: No data available in Human Protein Atlas database for PCDHA6 (ENSG00000291654), suggesting low/undetectable expression in surveyed tissues or technical limitations. The CNV burden at 5q31.3 indicates dosage sensitivity, where haploinsufficiency or overexpression causes defective protocadherin-mediated cell adhesion, impaired neuronal circuit assembly, disrupted synaptic specificity, and potential neurodevelopmental disorder phenotypes including autism spectrum disorder, intellectual disability, and epilepsy.\n\nConservation is strong across mammals: human (Q9UN73, 950 aa), chimpanzee (Q5DRE6, 950 aa), mouse (Q91Y14/Q05C01, 926-946 aa), rat (I6LBW5, 946 aa), indicating evolutionary conservation of protocadherin alpha functions in neuronal circuit assembly. The protocadherin alpha cluster organization is conserved between human and mouse (CNR genes).\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: PCDHA6 functions as a brain-enriched type I transmembrane protocadherin that regulates neuronal circuit assembly and synaptic specificity through calcium-dependent homophilic cell-cell adhesion mediated by 6 extracellular cadherin repeats (EC1-EC6). The EC1-EC6 extracellular domain (residues 30-635) provides calcium-binding interfaces (DXNDNAPXF motifs at residues 230-240/338-348/443-453) for structural stabilization and homophilic binding specificity. Each protocadherin alpha isoform (PCDHA1-PCDHA13) exhibits unique homophilic binding specificity, enabling combinatorial diversity for neuronal self-avoidance and synaptic partner selection. The C-terminal catenin-binding domain (residues 800-933) recruits cytoplasmic scaffold proteins for signaling complex assembly and cytoskeletal linkage. PCDHA7-PCDHA10-PCDHA2-PCDHA8-PCDHA3 interactions (0.840/0.629/0.614/0.597/0.548) position PCDHA6 within the protocadherin alpha cluster network, suggesting PCDHA6 acts through stochastic isoform expression and homophilic matching for neuronal identity coding. The high-confidence structure (pLDDT 73.8) provides stable cadherin fold architecture for efficient calcium-dependent adhesion. 5q31.3 CNVs cause PCDHA6 dysfunction, leading to defective neuronal circuit assembly, impaired synaptic specificity, disrupted self-avoidance, and neurodevelopmental disorder phenotypes. PCDHA6 represents a critical protocadherin alpha isoform linking cadherin-mediated adhesion to neuronal wiring biology.
