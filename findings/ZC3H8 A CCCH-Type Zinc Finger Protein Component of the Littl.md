# ZC3H8: A CCCH-Type Zinc Finger Protein Component of the Little Elongation Complex Regulating snRNA Transcription and Thymocyte Apoptosis

**Date:** 2026-06-26T17:13:56.945983

**Quality Score:** 8/10 (EXCELLENT)

**Confidence:** MEDIUM

## Description
ZC3H8 (Zinc Finger CCCH-Type Containing 8, UniProt Q8N5P1), also known as Fliz1, is a 291-amino acid nuclear RNA-binding protein that functions as a component of the Little Elongation Complex (LEC) regulating small nuclear RNA (snRNA) transcription by RNA polymerase II and III. InterPro analysis reveals a characteristic CCCH-type zinc finger architecture with three tandem C-x8-C-x5-C-x3-H motifs (IPR000571, IPR036855, PS50103, PF00642, PF18345, PF22623, aa 191-286) that coordinate zinc ions for structural stability and mediate sequence-specific RNA recognition; the protein belongs to the suppressor of sable-like family (IPR045124, aa 19-289) conserved from Drosophila to humans. The AlphaFold predicted structure shows medium confidence (pLDDT 67.1), consistent with a partially structured protein containing ordered zinc finger domains connected by flexible linkers—a common feature of transcriptional regulators that must engage multiple partners. STRING interaction data places ZC3H8 at the core of the LEC transcription machinery with very high confidence associations to: ELL (0.919) and ELL2/ELL3 (0.910-0.913), Eleven-nineteen Lysine-rich Leukemia proteins that enhance Pol II elongation; AFF4 (0.919), an LEC scaffold protein; NELFB/NELFE (0.913-0.918), Negative Elongation Factor subunits that pause Pol II; SUPT5H (0.912), Spt5 elongation factor; CDC73 (0.913), Parafibromin component of the PAF1 complex; and ICE1/ICE2 (0.901-0.906), Little Elongation Complex subunits 1 and 2. HPA expression profiles confirm ubiquitous detection across tissues with nucleoplasmic and nuclear body localization (including Cajal bodies and histone locus bodies where snRNA transcription and processing occur); ZC3H8 is classified as an essential protein with repressor activity, RNA-binding function, and roles in apoptosis and transcription regulation. ClinVar records document 36 pathogenic variants predominantly consisting of copy number losses and gains at 2q13 (spanning 111-113 Mb), implicating ZC3H8 dosage sensitivity in developmental disorders though specific phenotypes remain undefined. We hypothesize that ZC3H8 functions as an RNA recognition module within the LEC: the triple CCCH zinc fingers bind specific sequence elements in snRNA gene promoters or nascent snRNA transcripts; this recruits or stabilizes the LEC (ELL-AFF4-NELF-ICE1/2) at snRNA loci; the complex coordinates transcription initiation-to-elongation transition by modulating NELF-mediated pausing and ELL-dependent elongation enhancement; localization to Cajal bodies positions ZC3H8 near snRNA processing machinery (coilin, SMN complex) for coupled transcription-processing; the apoptotic regulatory role suggests ZC3H8 may control expression of pro-survival snRNAs or directly interact with apoptosis regulators in thymocytes; loss of ZC3H8 would disrupt snRNA biogenesis, impair spliceosome assembly, and cause widespread splicing defects contributing to developmental pathology.
