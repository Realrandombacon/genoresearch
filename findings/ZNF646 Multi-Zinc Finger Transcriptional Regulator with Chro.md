## Description
ZNF646 encodes an 1832 amino acid nuclear transcription factor containing extensive C2H2-type zinc finger arrays (IPR013087, PF00096, PF13912) distributed across multiple positions (8-36, 48-70, 75-102, 239-261, 848-871, 1731-1752 aa). AlphaFold prediction (pLDDT 49.8) indicates a predominantly disordered architecture, characteristic of scaffold transcription factors that engage in dynamic protein-protein interactions. STRING analysis reveals a chromatin modification interaction network: BRPF1 (bromodomain/PHD finger chromatin reader, 0.632), KAT8 (histone acetyltransferase, 0.570), RNF40 (E3 ubiquitin ligase for H2B ubiquitination, 0.518), and PRKG1 (cGMP-dependent kinase, 0.628). Additional zinc finger partners include ZNF843 (0.540), ZNF592 (0.531), and ZNF316 (0.492), suggesting homo/hetero-oligomerization. HPA shows testis-enriched expression (24.3 nTPM) with nuclear/nucleoplasmic localization, consistent with germline-specific transcriptional regulation. ClinVar documents 14 pathogenic variants, predominantly 16p11.2 copy number losses/gains, implicating ZNF646 dosage in neurodevelopmental disorders (16p11.2 syndrome). The mechanistic hypothesis: ZNF646 functions as a sequence-specific DNA-binding scaffold that recruits chromatin remodeling complexes (BRPF1-KAT8 histone acetylation, RNF40 H2B ubiquitination) to target gene promoters. Disordered regions enable conformational flexibility for multi-protein complex assembly. Testis enrichment suggests roles in spermatogenesis-specific gene regulation. CNV pathogenicity indicates precise ZNF646 stoichiometry is critical for neurodevelopment, potentially through regulation of neuronal differentiation genes.
