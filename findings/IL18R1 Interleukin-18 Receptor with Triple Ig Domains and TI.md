# IL18R1: Interleukin-18 Receptor with Triple Ig Domains and TIR Signaling Module Driving Th1 Immune Responses, NK Cell Activation, and Inflammatory Diseases via IL18RAP Heterodimerization and MyD88 Recruitment

## Description
IL18R1 (Interleukin-18 receptor 1, also known as IL-1Rrp or CD218a, Q13478) encodes a 541 aa type I transmembrane receptor functioning as the ligand-binding chain of the IL-18 receptor complex that mediates pro-inflammatory cytokine signaling essential for Th1 immune responses, NK cell cytotoxicity, and IFN-γ production. Structural architecture reveals a modular ectodomain-intracellular organization characteristic of the IL-1 receptor family: three tandem immunoglobulin-like domains (IPR003599/IPR007110/G3DSA:2.60.40.10, residues 25-112/125-207/222-316/18-114/115-209/210-318) forming a compact ligand-binding platform that recognizes IL-18 with high specificity, followed by a transmembrane segment, and a cytoplasmic Toll/Interleukin-1 Receptor (TIR) domain (IPR000157/IPR035897/G3DSA:3.40.50.10140, residues 373-528/365-522/373-522) serving as the signaling module for downstream adapter recruitment; AlphaFold prediction (pLDDT 79.2, high confidence) confirms a well-folded structure with defined Ig domains and structured TIR domain consistent with stable receptor capable of conformational changes upon ligand engagement. STRING interactions define the complete IL-18 signaling network: IL18BP (0.921, highest confidence) as the soluble decoy receptor that sequesters IL-18 to prevent IL18R1 activation, SIGIRR (0.919) as a negative regulator dampening IL-18 signaling through inhibitory heterodimerization, IL12RB1 (0.831) linking IL-18 to IL-12 pathways for synergistic Th1 polarization, IL18 (0.653) as the primary agonist ligand, IL18RAP (0.404) as the essential co-receptor chain that heterodimerizes with IL18R1 upon IL-18 binding to form the complete signaling complex, IL33/IL37 (0.655/0.574) as related IL-1 family cytokines, IL1B/IL1A (0.532/0.431) indicating crosstalk with IL-1 pathways, and IL1RAP (0.505) as paralogous co-receptor. HPA localizes IL18R1 to mitochondria (unexpected non-canonical localization potentially indicating metabolic regulation of immune signaling) with tissue-enhanced expression highest in lung (11.3 nTPM), detected in many tissues, immune cell enhanced blood cell specificity, and group enriched single cell type specificity reflecting restricted expression to immune lineages. ClinVar documents 14 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants predominantly consisting of large copy number alterations on chromosome 2: losses at 2p25.1-q13 (chr2:11504318-111365996x1) and 2q11.2-12.2 (chr2:101234070-105679157x1) deleting the locus, and gains at 2p25.3-q37.3 (chr2:1-243199373, chr2:12771-242783384x3), 2q11.1-13 (chr2:96353030-114045463x3, chr2:94678532-110602409x3), and 2q11.2-13 (chr2:101710825-110791418x3) duplicating the region; these CNVs are associated with immunodeficiency, autoinflammatory disorders, and susceptibility to infections due to dysregulated IL-18 signaling. Literature establishes IL18R1 as critical for host defense against intracellular pathogens where IL-18 binding induces IL18R1-IL18RAP heterodimerization bringing TIR domains into proximity for MyD88 adapter recruitment, triggering IRAK kinase activation, TRAF6 ubiquitination, and NF-κB/MAPK signaling cascades that drive IFN-γ production from NK and T cells; mitochondrial localization suggests potential metabolic checkpoint function where IL18R1 may regulate oxidative phosphorylation during immune activation; high conservation from mouse (Q61098, 537 aa) confirms evolutionary preservation of this inflammatory pathway; SIGIRR interaction provides negative feedback preventing excessive inflammation; IL18BP competition offers therapeutic targeting opportunity in autoinflammatory diseases.
