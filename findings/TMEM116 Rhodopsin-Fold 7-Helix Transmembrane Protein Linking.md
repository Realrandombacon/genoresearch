## Description
TMEM116 (Gene ID: 84277, UniProt Q8NCL8) is a 245 amino acid transmembrane protein located on chromosome 12q24.11 (NC_000012.12). This gene encodes a predicted rhodopsin-fold 7-helix transmembrane protein with G-protein coupled receptor (GPCR) architecture, suggesting roles in signal transduction across cellular membranes. InterPro domain analysis reveals canonical Class A (rhodopsin-like) GPCR topology: Rhodopsin 7-helix transmembrane proteins superfamily (G3DSA:1.20.1070.10, positions 10-205, ~195 residues forming the characteristic serpentine fold), G-protein coupled receptor signaling family (PTHR23112, positions 10-242, ~232 residues spanning nearly the entire protein), and Family A G protein-coupled receptor-like superfamily (SSF81321, positions 17-201, ~184 residues). The 7-transmembrane helix bundle is the defining structural feature of GPCRs—the largest membrane receptor family in humans—mediating diverse physiological processes through heterotrimeric G-protein activation. STRING interaction network identifies 10 medium-to-high confidence partners revealing unexpected metabolic connections: GART (0.774 high confidence, phosphoribosylglycinamide formyltransferase, trifunctional purine biosynthesis enzyme), DPYD (0.726 high confidence, dihydropyrimidine dehydrogenase, rate-limiting enzyme in pyrimidine catabolism), NAA25 (0.700 medium confidence, N-alpha-acetyltransferase 25, NatB complex subunit), ALDH18A1 (0.610 medium confidence, aldehyde dehydrogenase 18 family member A1, bifunctional enzyme in proline/ornithine biosynthesis), OTC (0.499 medium confidence, ornithine carbamoyltransferase, urea cycle enzyme), PPAT (0.496 medium confidence, phosphoribosyl pyrophosphate amidotransferase, commits to purine biosynthesis), ASS1 (0.464 medium confidence, argininosuccinate synthase 1, urea cycle/arginine biosynthesis), UMPS (0.424 medium confidence, uridine monophosphate synthase, pyrimidine biosynthesis), DHODH (0.416 medium confidence, dihydroorotate dehydrogenase, mitochondrial pyrimidine biosynthesis), and CYB5R4 (0.403 medium confidence, cytochrome b5 reductase 4, electron transfer flavoprotein). The striking enrichment for purine/pyrimidine biosynthesis enzymes (GART, PPAT, UMPS, DHODH, DPYD) and amino acid metabolism enzymes (ALDH18A1, OTC, ASS1) suggests TMEM116 links GPCR signaling to nucleotide and amino acid metabolic regulation—a novel functional connection not previously characterized for classical GPCRs. Human Protein Atlas classifies TMEM116 (ENSG00000198270) as a predicted intracellular and membrane protein with nucleoplasm and microtubule subcellular localization—unusual for a 7-TM protein which typically localizes to plasma membrane, suggesting potential endosomal/Golgi retention or non-canonical intracellular signaling function. RNA expression shows tissue-enhanced pattern with detection in ALL tissues (broad expression), highest in choroid plexus (56.4 nTPM) and parathyroid gland (56.0 nTPM), cancer-enhanced specificity, low immune cell specificity, and cell type-enhanced single cell specificity—indicating fundamental cellular function with specialized tissue modulation. Choroid plexus expression suggests roles in cerebrospinal fluid production or blood-CSF barrier function; parathyroid expression suggests calcium homeostasis regulation. AlphaFold structure prediction (AF-Q8NCL8-F1) yields HIGH-confidence model (global pLDDT 73.6), indicating a well-folded stable protein with confident 7-transmembrane helix bundle across all 245 residues—the high confidence score supports genuine GPCR fold rather than false-positive prediction. High-confidence structure enables rational drug design and mutagenesis studies targeting specific transmembrane helices. ClinVar lists 7 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large chromosomal copy number variations spanning chromosome 12 regions (chr12:99532287-133777902x3 gain, chr12:173787-133777902 gain/x3, chr12:1-133851895x3 gain, chr12:121271-133196807x3 gain, chr12:105234677-112194686x1 loss); one intronic SNV (NM_001193531.2:c.588+245A>G) of uncertain significance; no specific monogenic disease is attributed solely to TMEM116, suggesting dosage sensitivity within larger chromosomal syndromes affecting development and cognition. Conservation is evidenced by orthologs across vertebrates including Danio rerio tmem116 (A2BGS3, 361 aa), Rattus norvegicus Tmem116 (F1LRF5, 358 aa/Q9BWF8, 215 aa isoforms)—the longer rodent/fish orthologs suggest potential N-terminal extensions or alternative splicing in mammals. Functional hypothesis: TMEM116 functions as an atypical intracellular GPCR regulating purine-pyrimidine nucleotide biosynthesis through direct or indirect coupling to metabolic enzyme complexes. The 7-transmembrane rhodopsin fold (residues 10-205) forms a ligand-binding pocket that may sense intracellular metabolites (nucleotides, amino acids) rather than extracellular signals—consistent with nucleoplasm localization. High-confidence GART (0.774) and DPYD (0.726) interactions suggest TMEM116 physically associates with purine biosynthesis machinery (GART catalyzes steps 2,3,5 of de novo purine synthesis) and pyrimidine catabolism (DPYD reduces uracil/thymine). PPAT (0.496), UMPS (0.424), and DHODH (0.416) interactions extend this to committed purine synthesis and complete pyrimidine synthesis pathways. ALDH18A1 (0.610), OTC (0.499), and ASS1 (0.464) interactions link to proline/ornithine/arginine biosynthesis and urea cycle—metabolically connected to purine synthesis through glutamine/aspartate precursors. NAA25 (0.700) interaction suggests TMEM116 may be N-terminally acetylated or regulates NatB substrate acetylation. CYB5R4 (0.403) interaction provides redox coupling for DHODH (requires ubiquinone electron acceptor). Mechanistically, TMEM116 may function as: (1) Metabolite-sensing intracellular GPCR detecting nucleotide pool imbalances and triggering signaling cascades to adjust biosynthetic flux; (2) Scaffold organizing purine-pyrimidine enzyme complexes (purinosome analog) for substrate channeling efficiency; (3) Regulator of mTORC1 or AMPK signaling linking nutrient sensing to nucleotide synthesis; (4) Modulator of transcription factors controlling metabolic gene expression via nuclear/cytoplasmic shuttling. Microtubule localization suggests potential trafficking role or cytoskeletal coupling for metabolic enzyme positioning. Disease mechanism: TMEM116 haploinsufficiency or overexpression from chromosome 12q CNVs may contribute to 12q duplication/deletion syndrome phenotypes including intellectual disability, growth abnormalities, and craniofacial defects. The 7 ClinVar variants suggest dosage sensitivity; 12q24 deletions cause developmental delay. TMEM116 may contribute through disrupted nucleotide metabolism (affecting DNA replication/repair in developing brain), aberrant GPCR signaling (altering neurodevelopmental pathways), or impaired metabolic coordination (causing energy stress). Cancer-enhanced expression suggests oncogenic potential through elevated nucleotide synthesis supporting proliferation. Priority experiments include ligand identification using metabolite screening assays, co-IP validation of GART/DPYD/PPAT interactions and mapping binding interfaces, investigation of TMEM116 effect on purine-pyrimidine levels via LC-MS metabolomics, assessment of TMEM116 G-protein coupling using GTPγS binding assays, CRISPR knockout to assess metabolic and proliferative phenotypes, structural characterization leveraging AlphaFold model (pLDDT 73.6) to identify ligand-binding pocket and design antagonists, analysis of TMEM116 expression in choroid plexus tumors and parathyroid adenomas, and investigation of TMEM116 role in CSF production and calcium homeostasis.
