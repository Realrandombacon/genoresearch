## Description
MFSD2A encodes major facilitator superfamily domain containing 2A (MFSD2A/SLC59A1), a 543 aa sodium-dependent lysophosphatidylcholine symporter (UniProt Q8NA29) belonging to the Major Facilitator Superfamily (MFS) of transporters. InterPro reveals characteristic MFS transporter architecture: MFS transporter superfamily domain (IPR036259, residues 46-515), lactose permease-like family (IPR039672, residues 35-528), MFS/sugar transport protein domain (PF13347, residues 48-509), and two MFS general substrate transporter-like domains (G3DSA:1.20.1250.20, residues 46-286 and 302-515) forming the canonical 12-transmembrane helix bundle. AlphaFold predicts high confidence structure (pLDDT 80.6) consistent with well-folded membrane transporter. STRING reveals functional clustering with endogenous retroviral envelope proteins and tight junction components: ERVFRD-1/syncytin-2 (0.998, highest confidence partner), ERVW-1/syncytin-1 (0.949), ZNF335 (0.682, microcephaly-associated transcription factor), TK1 (0.675, thymidine kinase), SYN2 (0.640, synaptogyrin-2), SLC1A5 (0.629, glutamine transporter), SASS6 (0.619, centriole assembly), and CLDN5 (0.614, claudin-5 blood-brain barrier tight junction protein). HPA shows tissue-enhanced expression with liver predominance (153.9 nTPM), epididymis (92.1 nTPM), skin (84.5 nTPM), and seminal vesicle (75.3 nTPM), plasma membrane and cytoplasmic localization, classified as disease-related gene/transporter/potential drug target involved in lipid transport and symport. ClinVar lists 25 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including in-frame deletion Trp146_Tyr147del, missense variants Asp93Gly/Met472Leu/Arg465His, nonsense Cys152Ter, frameshift mutations Cys251fs/Gln462fs, and splice site mutations causing autosomal recessive primary microcephaly type 15 (MCPH15) and neurodevelopmental disorder with microcephaly seizures and short stature (NEDMISBA). Functional mechanism: MFSD2A represents a paradigm-shifting discovery as the first identified sodium-dependent lysophosphatidylcholine (LPC) transporter that mediates blood-brain barrier (BBB) integrity through a unique mechanism distinct from classical nutrient transporters. Unlike other MFS members transporting small molecules, MFSD2A transports bulky LPC molecules (particularly LPC-DHA carrying docosahexaenoic acid) across the BBB endothelium via an alternating access mechanism coupling sodium gradient to LPC uptake. This transport is essential for: (1) brain DHA accretion during development supporting neuronal membrane synthesis and function, (2) suppression of transcytosis in BBB endothelial cells maintaining barrier tightness by inhibiting caveolae formation, and (3) regulation of angiogenesis and vascular development. The syncytin interaction network suggests potential evolutionary co-option of retroviral envelope proteins for placental/BBB barrier formation. Recent research reveals expanded roles beyond microcephaly: cognitive impairment spectrum, retinal degeneration, growth restriction, and potential involvement in Alzheimer disease via DHA homeostasis. Despite mechanistic understanding of LPC transport and BBB function, complete substrate specificity profile, regulatory mechanisms controlling expression at BBB versus peripheral tissues, isoform-specific functions, therapeutic potential for enhancing brain DHA delivery in neurodegeneration, and precise genotype-phenotype correlations for the 25+ pathogenic variants remain incompletely characterized - representing genuine dark aspects of this critical lipid transporter.
