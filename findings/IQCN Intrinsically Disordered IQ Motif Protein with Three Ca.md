## Description

InterPro analysis reveals defining IQ motif architecture: three IQ calmodulin-binding motifs (IPR000048/PF00612/PS50096/SM00015, aa 102-124/103-132, 925-947/929-954, 948-970/950-978) characteristic of proteins that bind EF-hand calcium sensors like calmodulin in a calcium-dependent or calcium-independent manner, plus P-loop containing nucleoside triphosphate hydrolase superfamily domains (IPR027417/G3DSA:1.20.5.190/SSF52540, aa 923-993/103-145/928-969/1099-1157/1113-1155) suggesting potential ATP/GTP binding capability, classified within Cell Division and Developmental Signaling Domain-Containing Protein family (IPR052318/PTHR22590, aa 29-872). GO annotations confirm protein binding molecular function (GO:0005515) and developmental protein role. AlphaFold structure prediction achieves low confidence (pLDDT 45.7), indicating a predominantly intrinsically disordered protein (IDP) with flexible conformation — this architecture is ideal for calmodulin-binding proteins where disorder enables induced-fit binding and conformational switching upon calcium/calmodulin engagement. Human Protein Atlas shows tissue-enriched expression (ENSG00000130518) with top expression in testis (30.1 nTPM), low cancer specificity, immune cell enhanced blood cell specificity, cell type enhanced specificity, with subcellular localization to acrosome and equatorial segment — these are sperm-specific structures critical for egg penetration and fertilization, definitively placing IQCN in male reproductive biology. The calmodulin triad (CALM3/CALML3/CALM2) definitively establishes IQCN as a calcium-regulated effector protein. ClinVar contains 15 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including frameshift mutations (c.1061_1062del p.Tyr354fs, c.2453_2454del p.Gln818fs), nonsense mutation (c.910C>T p.Gln304Ter), missense variants (I1014V, R1125H, S1003F, T1305S), and copy number gains on chromosome 19p13.12-12/19p13.3-q13.43 — these loss-of-function variants suggest IQCN haploinsufficiency causes disease, though no specific disease is formally assigned, potentially indicating male infertility, spermatogenic failure, or subtle fertilization defects not yet catalogued. Conservation across vertebrates (Mus macaque Q8WNU4 1166 aa, Mus musculus A0A1D5RMD1 1437 aa) indicates ancient evolutionary origin tied to fundamental calcium signaling processes. IQCN likely functions as a calcium/calmodulin-dependent regulatory protein that localizes to the sperm acrosome and equatorial segment, where it modulates calcium influx during capacitation and acrosome reaction — the three IQ motifs enabling graded responses to calcium concentration changes, coordinating downstream effectors for sperm-egg recognition, membrane fusion, and fertilization success, with pathogenic variants causing male factor infertility through disrupted calcium signaling and acrosomal dysfunction.
