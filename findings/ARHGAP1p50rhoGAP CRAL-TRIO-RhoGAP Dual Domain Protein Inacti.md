## Description
ARHGAP1 (439 aa, UniProt Q07960), also known as p50rhoGAP, CDC42GAP, RHOGAP, or RHOGAP1, is a cytosolic and vesicle-localized GTPase activating protein that functions as a critical negative regulator of Rho family small GTPases (RHOA, CDC42, RAC1, RHOC) through its C-terminal RhoGAP catalytic domain, thereby controlling actin cytoskeleton dynamics, cell migration, cancer metastasis, and osteoclast-mediated bone remodeling. The protein architecture features two major functional domains: an N-terminal CRAL-TRIO lipid binding domain (IPR001251/IPR036865/PF13716/G3DSA:3.40.525.10/cd00170, aa63-218/64-216/82-215/66-216/77-204) that may bind small hydrophobic molecules or mediate protein-protein interactions for membrane recruitment; and a C-terminal Rho GTPase-activating protein domain (IPR000198/IPR049592/IPR008936/PF00620/PS50238/G3DSA:1.10.555.10/cd04404, aa244-431/239-431/235-431/260-404/244-431/235-431/239-431) containing the catalytic arginine finger that accelerates GTP hydrolysis on Rho family GTPases converting them from active GTP-bound to inactive GDP-bound state. HPA confirms low tissue specificity with ubiquitous expression detected in all tissues, low cancer specificity, low immune cell specificity, cell type-enhanced single cell specificity, vesicular/primary cilium/cytosolic subcellular localization, and classification as a plasma protein/predicted intracellular-membrane protein with GTPase activation molecular function. Mechanistically, ARHGAP1 forms ultra-high-confidence interactions within the Rho GTPase regulatory network: RHOA (STRING 0.999) and CDC42 (0.999) are the primary substrates whose GTPase activity ARHGAP1 inactivates; BNIP2 (0.931) is Bcl-2/adenovirus E1B 19 kDa-interacting protein 2 that forms a complex with ARHGAP1 regulating cell survival; TRIP10 (0.876) is thyroid hormone receptor interactor 10 linking to endocytic trafficking; NDEL1 (0.868) is nudE-like protein 1 involved in microtubule organization and neuronal migration; AMOT (0.864) is angiomotin controlling cell polarity and tight junction formation; RAC1 (0.855) and RHOC (0.704) are additional Rho family substrates; WASL (0.532) is Wiskott-Aldrich syndrome protein-like linking to actin polymerization; RASA1 (0.405) is RAS p21 protein activator 1 representing parallel GAP regulation of RAS pathway. AlphaFold prediction (pLDDT 82.0) reveals high-confidence well-folded structure with N-terminal CRAL-TRIO lipid-binding domain forming a hydrophobic pocket connected to C-terminal RhoGAP domain with characteristic alpha-helical bundle containing the catalytic arginine finger residue consistent with crystallographic structures of RhoGAP proteins showing GAP-Rho GTPase complex interfaces. Clinical significance includes 14 pathogenic/likely pathogenic ClinVar variants: intronic variants c.1027+74C>T and c.1132-12T>G affecting splicing, large deletion (NC_000011.9:g.(?_45827353)_(47804770_?)del), copy number gains spanning 11p11.2-q12.2/11p13-q25/11p15.5-q25 (chr11:51581311-54891247, 32799481-134938470, 230616-134938470 amplifications), copy number loss at 11p12-11.2 (chr11:40117145-46920718 deletion), and single allele deletions. Conservation across mammals (mouse Q5FWK3 439 aa) indicates essential RhoGAP function maintained over ~90 million years. Literature (60 papers, WELL-STUDIED status) establishes ARHGAP1 in cancer metastasis and bone biology: 2026 studies demonstrate tumor-educated extracellular vesicle-derived LINC01116 drives non-small cell lung cancer progression and immunosuppression by sponging miR-3614-5p to upregulate ARHGAP1; 2023 research shows ARHGAP1 role in Rho GTPase inactivation during breast cancer MCF-7 cell metastasis after doxorubicin treatment; 2021 work identifies osteoclast-derived small extracellular vesicles inducing osteogenic differentiation via inhibiting ARHGAP1 in bone remodeling; 2021 studies show circRNA RPPH1 facilitates breast cancer development by regulating miR-542-3p/ARHGAP1 pathway. The functional hypothesis posits that ARHGAP1 acts as a 'Rho GTPase brake controlling cytoskeletal dynamics and cell motility': in resting cells, ARHGAP1 resides in cytosol and vesicles with CRAL-TRIO domain potentially binding lipid second messengers; upon growth factor or integrin stimulation, Rho GTPases become activated promoting actin stress fiber formation and focal adhesion assembly; ARHGAP1 is recruited to membranes where its RhoGAP domain inserts catalytic arginine into Rho-GTP active site accelerating intrinsic GTP hydrolysis by 1000-fold; this inactivation reverses cytoskeletal changes limiting cell migration and invasion; in cancer, oncogenic lncRNAs (LINC01116) and circRNAs (RPPH1) sponge tumor-suppressive miRNAs (miR-3614-5p, miR-542-3p) elevating ARHGAP1 which paradoxically may promote metastasis through altered Rho signaling balance; in bone, osteoclast sEVs inhibit ARHGAP1 in osteoblasts promoting osteogenic differentiation through sustained Rho activity; in primary cilium, ARHGAP1 may regulate ciliary signaling through CDC42 control affecting developmental pathways; therapeutic strategies include ARHGAP1 inhibitors for osteoporosis treatment, ARHGAP1 modulation for cancer metastasis prevention, and ARHGAP1-targeted interventions for skeletal disorders.
