## Description
LOC105369209 is an uncharacterized genomic locus on chromosome 20q13.33 (NC_000020.11:62228242-62236300) spanning approximately 8.1 kb. Comprehensive database searches reveal no evidence of protein-coding function: no UniProt accession was identified, precluding InterPro domain analysis and AlphaFold structure prediction. STRING database returned no protein interaction partners, confirming absence of canonical protein-coding function. Human Protein Atlas could not resolve this locus to an Ensembl ID, indicating no detectable protein expression in surveyed tissues. However, ClinVar analysis reveals 8 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants in this region, all copy number gains (trisomy duplications) encompassing the 20q13.33 region: chr20:50805746-64334135 (~13.5 Mb), chr20:53236165-64284202 (~11.0 Mb), chr20:57229415-64273089 (~7.0 Mb), chr20:61326549-64277326 (~3.0 Mb focal 20q13.33 amp), chr20:56198032-64277321 (~8.1 Mb), chr20:99557-64277321 (~64.2 Mb whole-arm trisomy), chr20:59041966-64277321 (~5.2 Mb), chr20:44787704-64277321 (~19.5 Mb). These structural variants are associated with 20q13.33 duplication syndrome featuring developmental delay, intellectual disability, craniofacial dysmorphism, skeletal anomalies, and cardiac defects. The 20q13.33 region contains several dosage-sensitive oncogenes and developmental genes including AURKA (aurora kinase A, critical for mitotic spindle assembly), ZNF217 (zinc finger protein 217, transcriptional repressor amplified in breast cancer), and SRC (proto-oncogene tyrosine-protein kinase Src, signal transduction). LOC105369209 likely functions as part of the 20q13.33 cis-regulatory landscape or encodes a non-coding RNA whose copy number gain contributes to 20q13.33 amplification syndrome pathogenesis through disruption of chromatin topology, topologically associating domain (TAD) boundaries, or long-range dysregulation of dosage-sensitive oncogenes and developmental regulators. The absence of protein-coding features combined with pathogenic CNV burden (8 variants) indicates this locus resides within a critical dosage-sensitive genomic region where trisomy disrupts cell cycle regulation, transcriptional control, and developmental processes via gene dosage effects and 3D genome reorganization.
