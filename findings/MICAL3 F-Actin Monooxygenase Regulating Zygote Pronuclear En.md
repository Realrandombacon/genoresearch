## Description
MICAL3 (UniProt Q7RTP6) encodes a 2002 aa F-actin monooxygenase functioning as a master regulator of cell cycle progression, zygote pronuclear envelope breakdown, and breast cancer stem cell (CSC) expansion. InterPro analysis reveals a complex domain architecture: a canonical FAD/NAD(P)-binding domain (aa 1-494, IPR036188, G3DSA:3.50.50.60) essential for flavin-dependent monooxygenase activity; a Calponin Homology (CH) domain (aa 515-625, IPR001715, cd21251) mediating actin-binding and protein-protein interactions; a LIM domain (aa 764-818, IPR001781, cd09439) facilitating oligomerization and signaling complex assembly; and a bMERB domain (aa 1841-1990, IPR022735) potentially involved in membrane association. AlphaFold predicts a medium-confidence structure (pLDDT 58.2), consistent with a partially structured protein containing ordered catalytic and binding domains flanked by intrinsically disordered regions suitable for dynamic actin remodeling and signaling complex assembly. STRING interactions define MICAL3 as a central node in the cytoskeletal and signaling network with high-confidence partners: NEDD9 (0.837), an E3 ubiquitin ligase adaptor linking MICAL3 to the ubiquitin-proteasome system; ERC1 (0.780), a Rab GTPase-activating protein regulating endocytosis and vesicle trafficking; and multiple Rab GTPases including RAB6A (0.679), RAB13 (0.644), RAB8A (0.595), RAB8B (0.590), and RAB10 (0.538), suggesting roles in microtubule-actin crosstalk and vesicular transport. HPA expression shows low tissue specificity but immune cell-enriched expression in blood cells, consistent with roles in hematopoiesis and immune cell activation. ClinVar reports 42 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains/losses on chromosome 22q11.1-11.21, indicating dosage sensitivity and disease relevance despite no single monogenic disease yet assigned. Literature (37 papers) reveals emerging functional characterization: (1) Zygote development—MICAL3 controls pronuclear envelope breakdown by regulating F-actin dynamics via interaction with CHK1, a process defective in human zygote arrest; (2) Breast cancer—Semaphorin signaling via MICAL3 induces symmetric cell division to expand breast cancer stem-like cells, promoting tumor initiation and metastasis; (3) Structural insights—crystal structures reveal the mechanism of flavin-dependent monooxygenase activity and actin-binding; (4) Cell cycle—MICAL3 regulates G2/M transition and cytokinesis. Functional hypothesis: MICAL3 functions through: (1) F-Actin Remodeling—monooxygenase activity oxidizes cysteine residues in actin or associated proteins to alter actin polymerization dynamics, facilitating pronuclear envelope breakdown and CSC symmetric division; (2) Signaling Integration—interacts with NEDD9 to ubiquitinate Rab GTPases (RAB6A, RAB10), promoting vesicle trafficking required for cytokinesis and CSC self-renewal; (3) Semaphorin Pathway—acts downstream of semaphorin signaling to modulate cytoskeletal rearrangements essential for CSC expansion. Mechanistic proposal: In breast cancer, semaphorin ligands activate MICAL3, which oxidizes actin filaments to promote symmetric CSC division; loss of MICAL3 function shifts division to asymmetric, depleting the CSC pool. In zygotes, MICAL3-CHK1 interaction regulates F-actin to dissolve the pronuclear envelope, a step defective in CHK1 mutations. Dosage sensitivity (ClinVar CNVs) suggests haploinsufficiency or overexpression drives developmental defects or cancer progression. Cross-domain reasoning links the FAD-monooxygenase activity to actin regulation, the CH domain to Rab GTPase activation via NEDD9, and the LIM domain to complex stability, positioning MICAL3 as a master regulator of cytoskeletal dynamics and cell fate decisions.
