# ZNF761: Prostate-Enriched KRAB Zinc Finger Transcriptional Repressor with TRIM28 Co-Repressor Interaction

## Description
ZNF761 (Gene ID: 388561, UniProt Q86XN6) is a 746 amino acid Krüppel-associated box (KRAB) domain-containing zinc finger protein located on chromosome 19q13.42 (NC_000019.10, positions 53431983-53458260, ~26kb genomic region). This gene encodes a canonical KRAB-zinc finger transcription factor architecture with clear molecular function predictions but incompletely characterized biological roles and target genes. InterPro domain analysis reveals N-terminal KRAB box domain (IPR001909/PF01352, positions 8-81/8-48; cd07765 KRAB-A box, positions 8-47) functioning as transcriptional repression module, followed by multiple C2H2-type zinc finger DNA-binding domains (IPR013087/PF00096, positions 215-242/242-269/270-297/270-292/326-348/354-376) enabling sequence-specific DNA recognition. Additional domains include PF13465 zinc-finger double domain (positions 480-505) and PF13912 C2H2-type zinc finger. GO annotations confirm regulation of DNA-templated transcription (GO:0006355), RNA polymerase II cis-regulatory region sequence-specific DNA binding, DNA-binding transcription factor activity, and nuclear localization. STRING interaction network identifies 6 medium-confidence protein partners: ZSCAN20 (0.572, zinc finger and SCAN domain-containing protein 20, another KRAB-ZFP suggesting heterodimerization or co-regulation), TRIM28 (0.522, also known as KAP1/TIF1β—the universal KRAB domain co-repressor that recruits SETDB1 histone methyltransferase and HP1 for heterochromatin formation), ZNF79 (0.469, another KRAB-ZFP), ZNF697 (0.448, KRAB-ZFP), RNF113A (0.410, ring finger protein 113A, E3 ubiquitin ligase), and an uncharacterized isoform ENSP00000470615 (0.540). The TRIM28 interaction is particularly significant as it places ZNF761 within the canonical KRAB-ZFP silencing machinery. Human Protein Atlas classifies ZNF761 (ENSG00000160336) as a predicted intracellular protein with remarkable prostate-enriched expression (69.3 nTPM top tissue, tissue enriched specificity), detected in all tissues but highly elevated in prostate, cancer-enhanced expression pattern, and low immune cell specificity. Biological process annotations include transcription and transcription regulation; molecular function includes DNA-binding. AlphaFold structure prediction (AF-Q86XN6-F1) yields medium-confidence model (global pLDDT 67.4), consistent with KRAB-ZFP architecture where zinc finger domains form ordered structures while linker regions and regulatory domains remain flexible/intrinsically disordered—this is typical for transcription factors that require conformational flexibility for partner recruitment. ClinVar lists 17 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains spanning chromosome 19q13 regions (e.g., chr19:49625130-57647352x3, chr19:48463931-57095254x3, chr19:50489390-59095359x3); two splice region variants (c.15+180A>G, c.15+4A>G) are also listed. No specific monogenic disease is attributed solely to ZNF761 point mutations, suggesting dosage sensitivity rather than loss-of-function pathology. Conservation evidenced by primate orthologs: Pan troglodytes (A0A2J8Q7J4, 746 aa identical length), Pongo abelii (A0A2J8UA16, 774 aa isoform 3), Nomascus leucogenys (G1R2R1, 747 aa), and Mandrillus leucophaeus (A0A2K5Y6M1, 496 aa truncated)—identical lengths in great apes indicating strong evolutionary constraint. Functional hypothesis: ZNF761 functions as a sequence-specific transcriptional repressor that recognizes target DNA motifs via its array of C2H2 zinc fingers and recruits TRIM28/KAP1 co-repressor complex through its KRAB domain, leading to H3K9me3 deposition, HP1 recruitment, and heterochromatin formation at target loci. Prostate-enriched expression (69.3 nTPM, highest among all tissues) suggests potential role in prostate-specific gene regulation, possibly controlling androgen-responsive genes or prostate differentiation programs. Cancer-enhanced expression pattern indicates possible oncogenic function through silencing of tumor suppressor genes in prostate cancer or other malignancies. Disease mechanism: ZNF761 haploinsufficiency or overexpression may contribute to chromosome 19q13 deletion/duplication syndromes, though specific phenotype remains undefined. The 17 ClinVar CNVs suggest dosage sensitivity; triplication may cause aberrant silencing of target genes while deletion may cause derepression. TRIM28 interaction confirms ZNF761 operates within epigenetic silencing pathway; disruption may alter chromatin states at developmental or tissue-specific genes. Prostate enrichment predicts potential contribution to prostate cancer progression through dysregulated transcriptional programs. Priority experiments include ChIP-seq to identify DNA binding motifs and genomic targets, co-IP validation of TRIM28 interaction, CRISPR knockout in prostate cell lines (LNCaP, PC-3) to assess transcriptional consequences and proliferation effects, investigation of ZNF761 role in androgen receptor signaling, assessment of ZNF761 expression levels in prostate cancers versus normal tissue, and determination of whether ZNF761 regulates prostate-specific antigen (PSA/KLK3) or other clinically relevant genes.
