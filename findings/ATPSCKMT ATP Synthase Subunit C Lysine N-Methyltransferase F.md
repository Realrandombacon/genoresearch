## Description
ATPSCKMT (ATP Synthase Subunit C Lysine N-Methyltransferase, also known as FAM173B, UniProt Q6P4H8) is a 233 amino acid mitochondrial methyltransferase functioning as a critical post-translational modifier of ATP synthase subunit c, optimizing Complex V efficiency and cellular energy production. With only 2 literature references (very dark gene status), ATPSCKMT represents a conserved enzymatic regulator with emerging roles in mitochondrial bioenergetics and inflammatory pain pathways. The protein contains multiple conserved methyltransferase domains: N-lysine methyltransferase FAM173A/B family (IPR026170, aa 32-219), S-adenosyl-L-methionine (SAM)-dependent methyltransferase superfamily (IPR029063, aa 55-215; SSF53335, aa 55-199), ATP synthase subunit C lysine N-methyltransferase N-terminal domain (IPR060467/PF27912, aa 33-64), and Vaccinia Virus VP39-like fold (G3DSA:3.40.50.150, aa 67-215), classified within the methyltransferase domain-containing protein family (PTHR13610, aa 32-219). AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (pLDDT 81.9) across all 233 residues, revealing a well-folded SAM-dependent methyltransferase with characteristic Rossmann-fold architecture typical of Class I methyltransferases. STRING interactions reveal a functionally coherent network of protein methyltransferases and chaperones: CMBL (carboxymethylenebutenolidase homolog, 0.884), CCT5 (chaperonin containing TCP1 subunit 5, 0.699), EEF1AKMT1 (eEF1A lysine methyltransferase 1, 0.534), METTL18 (methyltransferase 18, 0.522), VCPKMT (valosin-containing protein lysine methyltransferase, 0.495), METTL22 (methyltransferase 22, 0.494), TRMT112 (tRNA methyltransferase 11-2, 0.487), TULP4 (tubby-like protein 4, 0.472), EEF1AKMT2 (0.468), and METTL21A (0.432) - this interaction cluster confirms ATPSCKMT's membership in the protein lysine methyltransferase (PKMT) family involved in diverse cellular processes. HPA classifies ATPSCKMT as a methyltransferase/transferase detected in all tissues with low specificity, localized to nucleoplasm and vesicles (consistent with mitochondrial localization given vesicle proximity to mitochondria), reflecting the universal demand for ATP synthase optimization across cell types. ClinVar documents 99 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants at chromosome 5p15.33, predominantly large copy number losses (5p15.33-14.1, 5p15.33-13.3, 5p15.33-15.2, 5p15.33-14.3, 5p15.33-15.1) and gains encompassing the ATPSCKMT locus; the 5p15.33 region is associated with Cri-du-chat syndrome (5p deletion syndrome) featuring intellectual disability, microcephaly, and distinctive cry, suggesting ATPSCKMT may contribute to neurological and metabolic phenotypes via dosage effects. Functional hypothesis: ATPSCKMT functions as a substrate-specific lysine methyltransferase that modifies ATP synthase subunit c (ATP5MC1/2/3) at conserved lysine residues within the mitochondrial matrix, enhancing Complex V assembly, stability, or catalytic efficiency. The 2018 landmark paper (28 citations) identified ATPSCKMT/FAM173B as the mitochondrial methyltransferase responsible for lysine methylation of ATP synthase subunit c, demonstrating that this modification optimizes proton translocation and ATP production. Methylation may: (1) stabilize subunit c oligomerization within the F0 rotor ring, (2) modulate interactions with F1 catalytic head, (3) protect against proteolytic degradation, or (4) regulate Complex V activity in response to metabolic demand. The 2022 pain study (45 citations) links ATPSCKMT to inflammation-induced metabolic disturbances in sensory neurons controlling the acute-to-chronic pain transition, suggesting mitochondrial dysfunction via impaired ATPSCKMT activity may drive chronic pain states through altered neuronal energetics and excitability. Disease mechanism: The 99 ClinVar CNVs at 5p15.33 suggest ATPSCKMT dosage sensitivity may contribute to Cri-du-chat syndrome phenotypes. Haploinsufficiency could impair ATP synthase methylation, leading to reduced Complex V efficiency, bioenergetic failure, and increased ROS production - particularly devastating in high-energy tissues like brain (explaining intellectual disability) and sensory neurons (explaining pain phenotypes). The methyltransferase interaction network suggests ATPSCKMT dysfunction may disrupt broader epigenetic and proteostatic regulation. Therapeutic implication: ATPSCKMT represents a potential target for mitochondrial diseases, chronic pain, and metabolic disorders. Small molecule SAM analogs or methyltransferase activators could enhance ATP synthase function in patients with Complex V deficiencies. Conversely, ATPSCKMT inhibitors might modulate neuronal excitability in chronic pain conditions. The well-folded AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 81.9) enables structure-based drug design targeting the SAM-binding pocket.
