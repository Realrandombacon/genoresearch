## Description
TJP2 encodes a 1190 aa tight junction protein 2 (UniProt Q9UDY2), also known as ZO-2 or zonula occludens-2, functioning as a membrane-associated guanylate kinase (MAGUK) scaffold protein that organizes tight junction complexes in epithelial and endothelial cells, maintaining paracellular barrier integrity and cell polarity. The protein contains three N-terminal PDZ domains (IPR001478/cd06727/cd06728/cd06729, aa32-118, aa306-384, aa509-590) for transmembrane protein binding, one SH3 domain (IPR001452/cd12027/G3DSA:2.30.30.40, aa604-669) for signaling protein recruitment, and a C-terminal guanylate kinase-like domain (G3DSA:3.40.50.300, aa732-879) for protein-protein interactions, belonging to the tight junction protein ZO family (IPR005417). AlphaFold prediction shows medium confidence (pLDDT 63.0), consistent with multi-domain scaffold proteins containing flexible linker regions between structured domains. HPA shows ubiquitous expression across all tissues with low tissue specificity, localizing to nucleoplasm, plasma membrane, and cell junctions, with disease involvement in intrahepatic cholestasis. STRING interactions reveal TJP2 forms tight junction complexes: YAP1 (0.990, Hippo pathway transcriptional coactivator), CLDN5 (0.989, claudin-5), OCLN (0.973, occludin), F11R (0.956, JAM-A), TJP1/ZO-1 (0.849, paralog), MARVELD2 (0.774, tricellulin), CGN (0.575, cingulin), AFDN (0.566, afadin), CLDN7/CLDN1 (0.425-0.426, claudins). ClinVar contains 251 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including nonsense mutations (p.Glu157Ter, p.Glu616Ter), frameshifts (p.Ser232fs), splice site mutations (c.2880+1G>A, c.3322-1G>T), missense variants (p.Gly218Ser, p.Asp1116Asn, p.Ile114Val), and deletions. Literature (181 papers) demonstrates TJP2 mutations cause progressive familial intrahepatic cholestasis type 4 (PFIC4) featuring severe neonatal cholestasis, pruritus, elevated bile acids, and liver failure; TJP2 variants cause autosomal dominant hearing loss DFNA51 with cochlear barrier dysfunction; TJP2 deletion in retinal pigment epithelium causes progressive retinal degeneration and blood-retinal barrier breakdown; TJP2 binds YAP1 regulating Hippo pathway transcriptional activity and cell proliferation; TJP2 deficiency treated with ileal bile acid transporter inhibitor odevixibat shows clinical benefit; TJP2 genotype correlates with PFIC4 clinical course and transplant-free survival; and ZNF582 enhances TJP2-ERK2 binding inhibiting renal cell carcinoma progression. TJP2 functions as a multi-functional scaffold—its PDZ domains bind claudins, occludin, and JAMs anchoring them to actin cytoskeleton; SH3 domain recruits signaling kinases; GK domain mediates protein complex assembly. TJP2 shuttles between membrane junctions and nucleus, where it regulates YAP1/TEAD transcriptional activity linking barrier function to cell proliferation control. PFIC4 pathogenesis reflects bile canalicular barrier failure: TJP2 loss disrupts tight junction sealing, causing bile acid leakage into bloodstream, hepatocyte toxicity, and cholestatic liver disease. DFNA51 hearing loss reflects stria vascularis barrier dysfunction impairing endolymph homeostasis. Retinal degeneration demonstrates TJP2's essential role in blood-retinal barrier maintenance. Cancer associations suggest TJP2 loss promotes epithelial-mesenchymal transition and metastasis through barrier disruption and YAP1 dysregulation.
