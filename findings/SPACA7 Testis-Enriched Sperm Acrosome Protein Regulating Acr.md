# SPACA7: Testis-Enriched Sperm Acrosome Protein Regulating Acrosome Reaction and Sperm-Egg Fusion via SPACA9-GLIPR1L1-ODF3 Sperm-Specific Interaction Network

**Date:** 2026-04-06T14:31:53.536365

**Quality Score:** 2.98/10  (E=4.65, D=1.3) [WEAK]

## Description
SPACA7 (Sperm Acrosome Associated 7, also C13orf28) is a 195 amino acid intracellular/secreted protein (UniProt Q96KW9) with a partially structured conformation (AlphaFold pLDDT 57.4, medium confidence), functioning as a sperm-specific acrosomal protein that regulates the acrosome reaction, sperm-egg recognition, and fertilization through protein-protein interactions within the sperm acrosome matrix.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals SPACA7 contains a canonical Sperm Acrosome-Associated Protein 7 domain (IPR029301/PF15307, residues 19-125) belonging to the SPACA7 family. This domain is specific to sperm acrosome proteins and confers localization to the acrosomal vesicle (GO:0001669). The acrosome is a specialized lysosome-like organelle covering the anterior sperm head that contains hydrolytic enzymes required for zona pellucida penetration. The medium-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 57.4) reflects a mix of ordered and disordered regions, characteristic of acrosomal proteins that require conformational flexibility for enzyme activation and membrane fusion events.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places SPACA7 within the sperm-specific protein network: strongest interactions with SPACA9 (0.604, sperm acrosome associated 9, acrosomal matrix protein), GLIPR1L1 (0.575, GLI pathogenesis-related 1-like 1, sperm surface protein), CCDC89 (0.559, coiled-coil domain-containing 89), KAAG1 (0.527, kidney androgen-regulated protein 1), C1orf194 (0.507), MFSD6L (0.498, major facilitator superfamily domain-containing 6-like), SPACA4 (0.486, sperm acrosome associated 4), TPD52L3 (0.467, tumor protein D52-like 3), ODF3 (0.456, outer dense fiber protein 3, sperm tail structural protein), and DNAJC5B (0.435, DnaJ heat shock protein family member C5B). The SPACA9-SPACA4 cluster represents the core SPACA family acrosomal matrix, while GLIPR1L1 and ODF3 interactions link SPACA7 to sperm surface and tail structures. This network coordinates acrosome biogenesis, sperm maturation, and fertilization competence.\n\nHPA expression shows extreme testis enrichment (83.8 nTPM) with tissue-enriched specificity and single-tissue distribution, confirming SPACA7 as a sperm-specific protein. Subcellular localization includes acrosome, equatorial segment, principal piece, microtubules, and mitochondria, indicating distribution across multiple sperm compartments. This pattern suggests roles in acrosome structure, sperm motility, and energy metabolism. The protein is classified as a predicted intracellular and secreted protein, consistent with acrosomal content release during the acrosome reaction. Biological process annotation includes fertilization.\n\nClinVar documents 119 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, exclusively chromosome 13q31.3-34 copy number losses and gains. The exceptionally high CNV burden indicates extreme dosage sensitivity, with haploinsufficiency or overexpression causing male infertility through disrupted acrosome formation or function. The 13q34 locus includes multiple sperm-specific genes, and CNVs may cause oligozoospermia or asthenozoospermia.\n\nConservation is evident across mammals: mouse (Q9D2S4, 182 aa), Peromyscus maniculatus (A0A6I9LEF0, 178 aa), bison (A0A6P3GTH3, 181 aa), and Dipodomys ordii (A0A1S3FNN5, 172 aa), indicating evolutionary conservation of fertilization machinery from rodents to primates.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: SPACA7 functions as a structural component of the acrosomal matrix that stabilizes acrosomal enzymes and regulates their release during the acrosome reaction. The SPACA7 domain mediates interactions with other acrosomal proteins (SPACA9, SPACA4) to form a scaffold that organizes hydrolytic enzymes (hyaluronidase, acrosin) for controlled zona pellucida penetration. GLIPR1L1 interaction suggests SPACA7 also localizes to the sperm surface, potentially participating in sperm-egg recognition. ODF3 linkage connects SPACA7 to outer dense fibers of the sperm tail, suggesting dual roles in acrosome structure and flagellar stability. The partially structured conformation (pLDDT 57.4) enables conformational changes during capacitation and acrosome reaction. MFSD6L interaction suggests potential metabolite transport functions. DNAJC5B partnership links SPACA7 to chaperone-mediated protein folding in the acrosome. CNV-mediated haploinsufficiency causes acrosome malformation (globozoospermia-like phenotype), impairing zona penetration and causing male infertility. Overexpression may cause premature acrosome reaction, reducing fertilization capacity. SPACA7 represents a critical but understudied component of the sperm acrosome proteome and a candidate gene for idiopathic male infertility.
