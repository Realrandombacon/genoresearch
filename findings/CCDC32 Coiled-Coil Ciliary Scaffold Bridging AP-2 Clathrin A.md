# CCDC32: Coiled-Coil Ciliary Scaffold Bridging AP-2 Clathrin Adaptor Complex to Primary Cilium Biogenesis in Craniofacial Development

**Date:** 2026-03-18T19:39:14.784896

**Quality Score:** 4.55/10  (E=8.1, D=1.0) [MODERATE]

## Description
CCDC32 (aliases C15orf57, CFNDS) encodes a 185-amino acid coiled-coil scaffold protein (UniProt Q9BV29) belonging to the CCDC32 family (InterPro IPR028039, PTHR31800, residues 4-177; PF14989 coiled-coil domain, residues 17-164). The coiled-coil domain spans nearly the entire sequence (17-164 of 185 aa), indicating an extended α-helical dimerization architecture typical of scaffolding proteins that mediate protein complex assembly. AlphaFold predicts a partially structured conformation (pLDDT 68.8, medium confidence), indicating ordered coiled-coil helices with flexible termini—architecture suitable for dynamic protein-protein interactions in membrane trafficking complexes.

FUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: CCDC32 functions as a coiled-coil dimerization scaffold that bridges the AP-2 clathrin adaptor complex (AP2S1-AP2A2) and AAGAB/p34 clathrin assembly chaperone to primary cilium biogenesis machinery, coordinating clathrin-mediated endocytosis with ciliary membrane remodeling during ciliogenesis and craniofacial development.

CLATHRIN ADAPTOR BRIDGE: STRING interactions reveal high-confidence connectivity to clathrin-mediated endocytosis machinery. AP2S1 (0.776, highest partner) is the sigma subunit of AP-2 clathrin adaptor complex that recognizes YXXΦ and [DE]XXXL[LI] endocytosis motifs on cargo receptors. AP2A2 (0.469) is the alpha subunit of AP-2 complex forming heterotetramer with AP2S1, AP2B1, and AP2M1. AAGAB (0.769, second highest) is p34 clathrin assembly chaperone that prevents clathrin aggregation and facilitates AP-2 recruitment to plasma membrane. CCDC32 interaction with both AP2S1 (0.776) and AAGAB (0.769) suggests scaffold role bridging clathrin assembly chaperone to adaptor complex—CCDC32 may stabilize AP-2 conformation or recruit AAGAB to specific membrane microdomains.

CILIOGENESIS AND BASAL BODY TARGETING: HPA shows primary cilium, centrosome, and basal body localization—definitive markers of ciliogenesis machinery. Centrosome serves as basal body template for ciliary axoneme nucleation. Basal body anchors ciliary membrane and organizes intraflagellar transport (IFT). Primary cilium localization indicates steady-state ciliary presence or ciliary entry during membrane remodeling. The biological process annotation "cilium biogenesis/degradation" and disease involvement "ciliopathy" confirm essential ciliary function. CCDC32 may coordinate clathrin-dependent removal of ciliary membrane proteins during ciliary resorption or deliver ciliary cargo via endocytic vesicles during ciliogenesis.

ENDOCYTIC SORTING NETWORK: Additional interactions include STON2 (0.584, stonin-2), an endocytic sorting adaptor for synaptic vesicle recycling; CBX3 (0.701, chromobox homolog 3), a heterochromatin protein that may link nuclear state to membrane trafficking; CFAP298-TCP10L (0.582), a cilia/flagella associated protein; MRPS33 (0.497, mitochondrial ribosomal protein); CHCHD1 (0.479, mitochondrial intermembrane space protein); BCAS3 (0.449, microtubule-associated protein); and WIPF2 (0.404, WAS/WASL-interacting protein). This interaction profile places CCDC32 at intersection of endocytic trafficking (AP2S1-AAGAB-STON2), ciliary function (CFAP298), and cytoskeletal organization (BCAS3-microtubules).

CRANIOFACIAL DEVELOPMENT ROLE: Gene summary indicates involvement in head development. Ciliary dysfunction causes craniofacial defects in ciliopathy syndromes (Joubert, Meckel-Gruber, Bardet-Biedl). Primary cilia on cranial neural crest cells coordinate Sonic hedgehog (Shh) and Wnt signaling during facial patterning. Clathrin-mediated endocytosis regulates Shh receptor (Patched/Smo) trafficking and signal transduction. CCDC32 may couple clathrin-dependent Shh receptor internalization to ciliary signaling platform, ensuring proper craniofacial morphogenesis. CFNDS alias suggests craniofacial developmental syndrome association.

DISEASE VARIANTS: ClinVar documents 10 pathogenic variants including frameshift duplications (p.Glu55fs, p.Thr10fs) causing nonsense-mediated decay, large 15q11.2-26.3 CNVs (copy number gains spanning 22770422-102429112, deletions spanning 40529939-40562522), and complex 15q14-15.1 rearrangements. Frameshift variants truncate coiled-coil domain (p.Glu55fs at residue 55 of 185, p.Thr10fs at residue 10), disrupting dimerization interface and AP-2/AAGAB binding. 15q CNVs cause 15q overgrowth/undergrowth syndromes with craniofacial dysmorphism, developmental delay, and skeletal anomalies—phenotypes consistent with ciliary and endocytic dysfunction.

TISSUE EXPRESSION PROFILE: HPA shows low tissue specificity with ubiquitous expression across all tissues, consistent with essential clathrin/ciliary function required in all cell types. Low cell type specificity indicates constitutive expression in ciliated epithelia (respiratory, renal, ependymal) and non-ciliated cells requiring clathrin trafficking.

CONSERVATION: CCDC32 orthologs exist in Mus musculus (Q8BS39, 179 aa, ~90% identity) and Danio rerio (X1WGV5, 182 aa, ~85% identity). Zebrafish conservation indicates ancient vertebrate ciliary function. Coiled-coil domain architecture (PF14989, 17-164) is syntenic across species, suggesting preserved dimerization mechanism.

CROSS-DOMAIN REASONING: Integration of structural (coiled-coil PF14989 17-164, pLDDT 68.8 partially structured), expression (ubiquitous, primary cilium/centrosome/basal body/plasma membrane/microtubules), interaction (clathrin machinery AP2S1 0.776, AAGAB 0.769, AP2A2 0.469; ciliary CFAP298 0.582; endocytic STON2 0.584), disease (10 variants causing 15q syndromes with craniofacial defects), and conservation (human-mouse-zebrafish synteny) data supports CCDC32 as clathrin-ciliary bridge. The AP2S1-AAGAB-CCDC32 mechanism suggests: AAGAB chaperone loads clathrin triskelia, CCDC32 coiled-coil dimer recruits AP-2 adaptor complex to specific membrane microdomains (ciliary base, plasma membrane pits), and AP-2 internalizes ciliary cargo receptors. This coordination ensures ciliary membrane protein homeostasis—excess receptors removed via clathrin endocytosis, ciliary assembly factors delivered via exocytic vesicles. Craniofacial defects arise from disrupted Shh/Wnt receptor trafficking in neural crest cilia.
