# ILVBL/HACL2: Thiamine Pyrophosphate-Dependent 2-Hydroxyacyl-CoA Lyase Catalyzing Peroxisomal Alpha-Oxidation of Branched-Chain 2-Hydroxy Fatty Acids with Links to Aspirin-Exacerbated Respiratory Disease

**Quality Score:** 9/10 (EXCELLENT)

**Confidence:** MEDIUM

## Description
ILVBL (A1L0T0), also known as HACL2 (2-hydroxyacyl-CoA lyase 2), encodes a 632 amino acid thiamine pyrophosphate (TPP)-dependent lyase that catalyzes the carbon-carbon bond cleavage of 2-hydroxyacyl-CoA substrates during peroxisomal alpha-oxidation of branched-chain fatty acids. InterPro analysis reveals a multi-domain TPP-dependent enzyme architecture: an N-terminal TPP-binding domain (IPR012001, positions 53-167) for cofactor recognition; a central TPP enzyme domain (IPR012000, positions 273-403) forming the active site; a C-terminal TPP-binding domain (IPR011766, positions 467-618) completing the catalytic machinery; and a conserved TPP-binding site (IPR000399, positions 504-523) with magnesium ion binding capability (GO:0000287); the protein belongs to the TPP-binding enzyme superfamily (IPR029061) and DHS-like NAD/FAD-binding domain superfamily (IPR029035, positions 203-431). AlphaFold predicts an exceptionally well-folded structure (pLDDT 94.4, very high confidence) consistent with a stable enzymatic core requiring precise active site geometry for TPP-dependent catalysis. STRING interactions demonstrate exclusive partnerships with metabolic enzymes: AGXT (0.879, alanine-glyoxylate aminotransferase in peroxisomal glyoxylate metabolism), CS (0.870, citrate synthase in TCA cycle), MRTFA (0.863, myocardin-related transcription factor), HDDC3 (0.846, HD domain-containing protein), mitochondrial cytochrome c oxidase subunits MT-CO1/2/3 (0.817-0.829), BCAT2 (0.790, branched-chain amino acid aminotransferase), and HAO2 (0.761, hydroxyacid oxidase 2 in peroxisomal fatty acid oxidation), placing ILVBL at the intersection of peroxisomal and mitochondrial metabolism. HPA shows ubiquitous expression across all tissues with low tissue specificity, localization to Golgi apparatus and vesicles consistent with peroxisomal targeting, classified under fatty acid metabolism and lipid metabolism biological processes with lyase molecular function. ClinVar contains 4 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number losses spanning chromosome 19p13.12-13.11 regions (e.g., chr19:13533925-15371089 deletions), though specific monogenic disease associations remain undefined. Literature (only 10 papers) demonstrates ILVBL polymorphisms associate with aspirin-exacerbated respiratory disease (AERD) and percent decline of forced expired volume in asthma patients; the gene shares similarity with bacterial acetolactate synthases (AHAS) catalyzing the first step in branched-chain amino acid biosynthesis. The mechanistic hypothesis: ILVBL functions as a peroxisomal 2-hydroxyacyl-CoA lyase enabling alpha-oxidation of 3-methyl-branched fatty acids (e.g., phytanic acid metabolites) that cannot undergo beta-oxidation due to methyl branch interference; the TPP cofactor forms a reactive carbanion at C2 of the thiazolium ring, attacking the carbonyl carbon of 2-hydroxyacyl-CoA substrates to cleave the C1-C2 bond, releasing formyl-CoA and a chain-shortened aldehyde; interaction with AGXT and HAO2 suggests coordinated peroxisomal metabolism of glyoxylate and 2-hydroxy fatty acids; BCAT2 interaction links ILVBL to branched-chain amino acid catabolism, potentially providing metabolic cross-talk between fatty acid alpha-oxidation and amino acid degradation; mitochondrial interactions (MT-CO1/2/3, CS) suggest coupling between peroxisomal alpha-oxidation products and mitochondrial energy metabolism; AERD association implies ILVBL variants alter eicosanoid metabolism or inflammatory mediator production through disrupted fatty acid handling; copy number losses on 19p13.12 may cause haploinsufficiency leading to accumulation of toxic 2-hydroxyacyl-CoA intermediates, potentially contributing to neurological or metabolic phenotypes.
