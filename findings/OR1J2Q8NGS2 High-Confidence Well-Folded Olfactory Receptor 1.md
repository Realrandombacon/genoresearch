## Description
OR1J2 (Olfactory receptor family 1 subfamily J member 2, also known as HG152, HSA5, OR1J3, OR1J5, OR9-19, OST044) is a 313 amino acid G-protein coupled receptor (UniProt Q8NGS2) with high-confidence well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 88.2), characteristic of stable 7-transmembrane domain olfactory receptors that function in odorant detection and neuronal signal transduction. The protein belongs to the olfactory receptor family (IPR000725/PF13853, residues 33-306) involved in olfaction and sensory transduction, contains GPCR rhodopsin-like domains (IPR000276/IPR017452, residues 26-50/59-80/104-126/41-290) with the conserved 7-transmembrane helix structure, has a G-protein coupled receptors family 1 signature (PS00237, residues 110-126) required for G-protein coupling, belongs to olfactory receptor families 1 and 7 (cd15918, residues 26-294), and has a rhodopsin 7-helix transmembrane protein fold (G3DSA:1.20.1070.10, residues 20-310). OR1J2 is located on chromosome 9q33.2 and enables olfactory receptor activity (GO:0004984) and G protein-coupled receptor signaling pathway (GO:0007186), functioning in olfaction and sensory transduction as part of the olfactory signal transduction cascade.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals a high-confidence network of olfactory signaling components: OR1J4 (0.787, Olfactory receptor 1J4, closely related olfactory receptor), GNAL (0.686, Guanine nucleotide-binding protein G(olf) subunit alpha, olfactory-specific G-protein), GNB1 (0.682, Guanine nucleotide-binding protein G(I)/G(S)/G(T) subunit beta-1, G-protein beta subunit), ARRB2 (0.679, Beta-arrestin-2, receptor desensitization), ARRB1 (0.679, Beta-arrestin-1), GNG7 (0.678, Guanine nucleotide-binding protein G(I)/G(S)/G(O) subunit gamma-7), PRKACG (0.653, cAMP-dependent protein kinase catalytic subunit gamma, cAMP signaling), GRK2 (0.569, G-protein coupled receptor kinase 2, receptor phosphorylation), GNG13 (0.566, Guanine nucleotide-binding protein subunit gamma-13), and GRK3 (0.547, G-protein coupled receptor kinase 3). These interactions confirm OR1J2 functions within the canonical olfactory signal transduction pathway, coupling to G(olf) proteins to activate adenylate cyclase and generate cAMP signals.\n\nHPA expression reveals OR1J2 is not detected in the HPA tissue panel, which is expected for olfactory receptors as they are specifically expressed in olfactory sensory neurons of the olfactory epithelium, not in the tissues surveyed by HPA. The protein is classified as a G-protein coupled receptor and predicted membrane protein involved in olfaction and sensory transduction biological processes. This tissue-restricted expression pattern is characteristic of the olfactory receptor gene family, with individual receptors expressed in specific subsets of olfactory neurons.\n\nClinVar documents 28 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including copy number gains (9q33.2-34.3, 9p24.3-q34.3, 9q32-34.11, 9q21.11-34.3 amplifications) and copy number losses (9q33.2-33.3, 9q32-33.3, 9q31.1-33.3, 9q33.1-33.3 deletions). The variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity. While no specific Mendelian disease is annotated, 9q CNVs are associated with developmental delay, intellectual disability, and sensory deficits. Given OR1J2's role in odorant detection, loss-of-function may contribute to anosmia or hyposmia phenotypes through impaired olfactory signal transduction. Olfactory dysfunction is associated with neurodegenerative diseases including Parkinson's and Alzheimer's disease.\n\nConservation is strong across vertebrates: human (Q8NGS2, 313 aa), greater horseshoe bat (A0A671FXB4, 313 aa), indicating an ancient and essential function in chemosensation conserved in mammals.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: OR1J2 functions as a 7-transmembrane G-protein coupled receptor in olfactory sensory neurons that detects specific odorant molecules and initiates neuronal signaling. The well-folded structure (pLDDT 88.2) forms a stable binding pocket within the transmembrane helices for odorant recognition. Upon odorant binding, OR1J2 activates the heterotrimeric G-protein G(olf) (GNAL-GNB1-GNG7/13), which stimulates adenylate cyclase III to produce cAMP. Elevated cAMP activates PRKACG (cAMP-dependent protein kinase) and opens cyclic nucleotide-gated ion channels, depolarizing the olfactory neuron. ARRB1/2 mediate receptor desensitization and internalization, while GRK2/3 phosphorylate the activated receptor to terminate signaling. OR1J4 interaction suggests potential receptor heterodimerization or co-expression in the same olfactory neuron subset. 9q CNVs cause OR1J2 dosage imbalance, potentially disrupting olfactory neuron development or function, contributing to anosmia phenotypes. Reduced olfactory function may serve as an early biomarker for neurodegenerative diseases. OR1J2 represents a candidate gene for congenital anosmia and may contribute to olfactory deficits in Parkinson's and Alzheimer's disease.
