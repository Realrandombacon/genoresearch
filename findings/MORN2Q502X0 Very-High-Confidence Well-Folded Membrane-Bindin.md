## Description
MORN2 (MORN repeat containing 2, BLOCK27, MOPT) is a compact 79 amino acid intracellular protein localized to the nucleoplasm and acrosomal vesicle (UniProt Q502X0) with very-high-confidence well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 91.6) containing canonical membrane-targeting architecture including two tandem MORN repeats (IPR003409/PF02493, residues 13-36 and 36-57) forming a ~45 aa core module (~57%) defining MORN2 classification with potential plasma membrane-binding activity, MORN repeat-containing protein family (IPR052849/PTHR46917, residues 1-75) encompassing nearly the full length, structural homology to the N-terminal domain of Histone H3 K4-specific methyltransferase SET7/9 (G3DSA:2.20.110.10/SSF82185, residues 1-73/1-64), and molecular functions including cell differentiation, spermatogenesis, acrosome biogenesis, ubiquitous expression with tissue enhancement (choroid plexus 181.8 nTPM, testis 170.3 nTPM), low cancer/immune specificity, predicted intracellular proteins classification, functioning as a small very-high-confidence well-folded membrane-binding scaffold that anchors protein complexes to the plasma membrane or acrosomal membrane through MORN-mediated lipid interaction, oligomerization, and MORN2 network assembly via partnerships with C11orf97, C8orf74, TMEM239, MORN4, ARHGEF33, SPA17, OSCP1, and ANKRD35.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals MORN2 is a member of the MORN (Membrane Occupation and Recognition Nexus) repeat family, motifs known to bind phospholipids and target proteins to membranes. The presence of two consecutive MORN repeats (residues 13-57) suggests a high-affinity membrane anchor, critical for its role in the acrosome, a specialized secretory vesicle in sperm heads. The surprising structural homology to the N-terminal domain of SET7/9 (a histone methyltransferase) despite lacking catalytic residues suggests MORN2 may have evolved from an ancestral enzyme to become a dedicated structural adaptor, retaining the fold but repurposing the surface for protein-protein interactions rather than catalysis. The very-high-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 91.6) confirms a stable, compact globular fold, unusual for such a small protein, indicating strong evolutionary constraint.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places MORN2 within a network of poorly characterized but likely co-expressed factors: strongest interactions with C11orf97 (0.693) and C8orf74 (0.603), suggesting a functional module of small membrane-associated proteins. Links to TMEM239 (0.506, transmembrane protein) and MORN4 (0.453, another MORN repeat protein) reinforce the membrane localization hypothesis. The connection to SPA17 (0.426, Sperm Associated Antigen 17) is particularly insightful, directly linking MORN2 to spermatogenesis and acrosome function. Additional partners like ARHGEF33 (RhoGEF), OSCP1, and ANKRD35 suggest roles in cytoskeletal organization and signal transduction at the membrane interface.\n\nHPA expression shows a tissue-enhanced pattern with exceptional levels in the choroid plexus (181.8 nTPM) and testis (170.3 nTPM). The choroid plexus expression suggests a role in blood-CSF barrier integrity or ciliary function (as MORN proteins are often ciliary), while testis expression aligns with its predicted role in spermatogenesis and acrosome formation. Subcellular location is confirmed as nucleoplasm (potentially a transient pool or artifact) and acrosomal vesicle, the latter being the functional site.\n\nClinVar documents 21 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains and losses affecting the 2p25.3-p21 region: CNV gains (2p25.3-q37.3 whole arm, 2p23.3-21 duplications) and CNV losses (2p25.1-q13, 2p23.2-21, 2p23.3-21 deletions). Two specific SNVs (c.354-172G>T, c.71A>G p.Tyr24Cys) are also listed. The moderate variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity of the 2p locus. Haploinsufficiency likely disrupts acrosome formation leading to male infertility or defects in choroid plexus function, while duplications may cause toxic aggregation or mislocalization of membrane complexes, contributing to neurodevelopmental phenotypes associated with 2p deletion/duplication syndromes.\n\nConservation is observed in mammals: human (Q502X0, 79 aa), mouse (Q6UL01, 79 aa), cow (Q32KU3, 79 aa), indicating strict preservation of the dual-MORN architecture.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: MORN2 functions as a rigid membrane anchor that recruits specific effector proteins (like SPA17 or ARHGEF33) to the acrosomal membrane during spermatogenesis or to the apical membrane of choroid plexus epithelial cells. The dual MORN repeats provide high-affinity binding to phosphoinositides, locking the protein in place. The SET7/9-like fold provides a stable platform for protein-protein interactions, potentially assembling a signaling complex required for acrosome reaction or ciliary beating. Dysregulation via 2p CNVs disrupts this precise stoichiometry: loss leads to failed acrosome biogenesis (infertility) or barrier defects, while gain may saturate membrane binding sites or sequester partners, driving the pathogenesis of 2p syndromes.
