# RNF112: Brain-Enriched RING Finger E3 Ligase with Unusual GBP Domain Architecture and 17p11.2 Dosage Sensitivity

**Date:** 2026-03-28T03:19:29.457950

**Quality Score:** 4.03/10  (E=5.85, D=2.2) [MODERATE]

## Description
RNF112 (Gene ID: 23365, UniProt Q9ULX5) is a 631 amino acid RING finger protein located on chromosome 17p11.2 (NC_000017.11). InterPro domain analysis reveals an unusual multidomain architecture combining E3 ubiquitin ligase and GTPase functions: N-terminal C3HC4-type RING finger domain (IPR001841/IPR018957/PF00097, positions 47-120; cd16538 RNF112-specific RING, positions 53-104) conferring E3 ubiquitin-protein ligase activity through zinc-coordinating cysteine/histidine residues that recruit E2 ubiquitin-conjugating enzymes for substrate ubiquitination. Remarkably, RNF112 contains a central guanylate-binding protein (GBP) domain (IPR015894, positions 163-300; IPR030386 GB1/RHD3-type G domain, positions 166-397; cd01851 GBP family N-terminal, positions 163-394) with P-loop containing nucleoside triphosphate hydrolase superfamily signature (IPR027417, positions 133-416), indicating GTP binding (GO:0005525) and GTPase activity (GO:0003924) - an unprecedented combination in RING finger proteins suggesting dual regulatory mechanisms. AlphaFold structure prediction (AF-Q9ULX5-F1) shows medium global confidence (pLDDT 68.7), indicating a partially structured protein with ordered catalytic domains interspersed with flexible linker regions typical of regulatory proteins requiring conformational dynamics. Human Protein Atlas classifies RNF112 as an enzyme and predicted intracellular/membrane protein with transferase molecular function, localized to nuclear speckles - subnuclear domains involved in pre-mRNA splicing and RNA processing. RNA expression is tissue-enriched with exceptional brain specificity (105.5 nTPM, highest among all tissues), cancer-enriched pattern indicating upregulation in malignant cells, not detected in immune cells, and cell type-enhanced specificity. Biological processes include neurogenesis and Ubl (ubiquitin-like) conjugation pathway. STRING database identifies 10 protein-protein interaction partners: STX2 (score 0.672, syntaxin-2 - SNARE protein involved in vesicle trafficking), STX1A (0.628, syntaxin-1A - neuronal exocytosis), ST8SIA2 (0.589, alpha-N-acetylneuraminide alpha-2,8-sialyltransferase 2 - glycosylation enzyme), LLGL1 (0.579, lethal giant larvae homolog 1 - cell polarity regulator), PHGDH (0.572, phosphoglycerate dehydrogenase - serine biosynthesis), CREM (0.459, cAMP responsive element modulator - transcription factor), ARF1 (0.455, ADP-ribosylation factor 1 - small GTPase regulating vesicle trafficking), RHBDD3 (0.448, rhomboid domain containing 3 - intramembrane protease), EPN2 (0.447, epsin-2 - endocytosis adaptor), PPP1R12C (0.407, protein phosphatase 1 regulatory subunit 12C - cytoskeletal regulation). The syntaxin cluster (STX2, STX1A) and ARF1 interaction strongly suggest RNF112 regulates membrane trafficking pathways, potentially linking ubiquitination to vesicle fusion events. ClinVar contains 100 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants affecting RNF112: predominantly copy number losses spanning 17p11.2 regions (ranging from ~2-5Mb deletions at chr17:16651293-20450566 to larger 17p13.3-11.2 gains), plus complex variants, indicating dosage sensitivity. No specific diseases are associated with RNF112 alone, but 17p11.2 deletions cause Potocki-Lupski syndrome (duplication) and Smith-Magenis syndrome (deletion), suggesting RNF112 contributes to neurodevelopmental phenotypes. Functional hypothesis: RNF112 functions as a brain-enriched E3 ubiquitin ligase that uniquely couples GTPase-dependent conformational switching to substrate recognition and ubiquitination. The GBP domain may act as a molecular switch - GTP binding induces conformational changes that expose the RING domain for E2 recruitment or reveal substrate-binding interfaces. Nuclear speckle localization suggests roles in regulating RNA processing factors through ubiquitination, potentially coupling transcription/splicing to protein degradation. Syntaxin and ARF1 interactions indicate additional plasma membrane functions regulating neurotransmitter release or receptor trafficking in neurons. Disease mechanism: RNF112 haploinsufficiency in 17p11.2 deletion syndromes disrupts neuronal protein homeostasis, impairing synaptic vesicle cycling, neurotransmitter receptor turnover, and RNA processing factor stability, contributing to intellectual disability, autism spectrum features, and sleep disturbances characteristic of Smith-Magenis syndrome. Cancer-enriched expression suggests oncogenic potential through dysregulated ubiquitination of tumor suppressors or cell cycle regulators. Priority experiments include identification of RNF112 ubiquitination substrates via ubiquitin remnant profiling, determination of GTP/GDP effects on RING domain E3 ligase activity, investigation of RNF112-syntaxin complex formation in neuronal cells, CRISPR knockout to assess nuclear speckle morphology and splicing factor stability, characterization of RNF112 expression across brain regions and neuronal subtypes, and analysis of RNF112 mutations in neurodevelopmental disorder cohorts.
