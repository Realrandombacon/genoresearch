## Description
HDAC2 (Histone deacetylase 2) is a well-studied gene with a significant clinical data gap. Biochemically, HDAC2 is a Class I Histone Deacetylase (UniProt Q92769, 488 aa) with a well-folded structure (AlphaFold pLDDT 85.6). It functions as a chromatin regulator and repressor, ubiquitously expressed in the nucleus. STRING analysis identifies it as a central hub interacting with RBBP4 (score 0.999), DNMT1 (DNA methyltransferase), MTA1/2, SMARCA4 (SWI/SNF), RCOR1 (N-CoR), and EZH2 (PRC2), linking it to transcriptional repression, DNA methylation, and chromatin remodeling. ClinVar lists 22 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants (mostly large copy number losses/gains on chr6), yet 0 specific diseases are cataloged. Despite being a core tumor suppressor with 3,656 papers linking it to cancer, angiogenesis, and metabolism, the specific genetic syndromes caused by its CNVs remain uncataloged. Mechanistically, HDAC2 likely causes broad epigenetic dysregulation by simultaneously deacetylating histones and interacting with DNA methyltransferases and repressive complexes, preventing the emergence of discrete, catalogable phenotypes.
