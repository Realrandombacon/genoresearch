# HIC1: A BTB-ZNF Transcriptional Repressor Silencing Wnt Target Genes via SUMOylation-Dependent Chromatin Remodeling Complexes

**Date:** 2026-06-26T17:08:54.341298

**Quality Score:** 8/10 (EXCELLENT)

**Confidence:** MEDIUM

## Description
HIC1 (Hypermethylated in Cancer 1, UniProt Q14526), also known as ZBTB29, is a 733-amino acid epigenetic transcriptional repressor that functions as a critical tumor suppressor by silencing Wnt/β-catenin target genes and cell cycle regulators. InterPro analysis reveals a canonical ZBTB family architecture: an N-terminal BTB/POZ domain (IPR000210, PF00651, PS50097, aa 37-153) that mediates homodimerization and recruitment of corepressor complexes; followed by five C-terminal C2H2 zinc finger domains (IPR013087, PF00096, PS00028, PS50157, aa 435-585) that recognize specific GC-rich DNA sequences in target gene promoters. The AlphaFold predicted structure shows low global confidence (pLDDT 50.0), indicating extensive intrinsically disordered regions connecting the structured BTB and zinc finger domains—a feature common in transcriptional regulators that must engage multiple protein partners and chromatin surfaces. STRING interaction data places HIC1 within epigenetic silencing and Wnt signaling networks with high confidence associations to: UBE2I (0.767), the SUMO-conjugating enzyme that modifies HIC1; DNMT1 (0.761), DNA methyltransferase recruited for promoter methylation; RASSF1 (0.720), another tumor suppressor; PIAS2 (0.716), a SUMO E3 ligase enhancing HIC1 repression; CTNNB1 (0.716), β-catenin whose transcriptional activity HIC1 antagonizes; CTBP1/CTBP2 (0.420-0.650), C-terminal binding protein corepressors; and SIRT1 (0.517), NAD-dependent deacetylase cooperating in silencing. HPA expression profiles confirm ubiquitous detection with immune cell enhancement and nuclear localization, consistent with genome-wide transcriptional regulation; HIC1 is classified as a tumor suppressor involved in Wnt signaling. ClinVar records document 67 pathogenic variants predominantly consisting of large copy number losses at 17p13.3 (the Miller-Dieker syndrome critical region), with deletions spanning 150kb-5.7Mb that remove HIC1 along with neighboring genes like TP53. We hypothesize that HIC1 functions as a sequence-specific epigenetic silencer: the BTB domain recruits CtBP corepressors, HDACs, and the SUMO machinery (UBE2I/PIAS2); SUMOylation of HIC1 enhances recruitment of DNMT1 for DNA methylation and creates binding surfaces for additional repressors; the zinc fingers anchor HIC1 to GC-rich promoter elements of Wnt target genes (e.g., AXIN2, CCND1); this multi-layered repression prevents aberrant β-catenin-driven proliferation; loss of HIC1 through deletion or promoter hypermethylation derepresses Wnt signaling, driving tumorigenesis in breast, colon, and medulloblastoma; the intrinsically disordered linkers enable conformational flexibility for engaging diverse chromatin contexts.
