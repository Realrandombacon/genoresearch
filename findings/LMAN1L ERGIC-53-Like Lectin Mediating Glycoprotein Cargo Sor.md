## Description
LMAN1L (Lectin Mannose Binding 1 Like, UniProt Q9HAT1) is a 526-amino acid type I transmembrane lectin functioning as an intracellular cargo receptor in the early secretory pathway that mediates the selective export of specific glycoproteins from the endoplasmic reticulum (ER) to the Golgi apparatus via COPII-coated vesicles. InterPro analysis confirms characteristic legume-like lectin architecture: N-terminal Legume-like lectin domain (IPR005052/PF03388/PS51328, aa 31-253/32-253/31-252) providing carbohydrate recognition activity for high-mannose N-glycans; Concanavalin A-like lectin/glucanase domain superfamily (IPR013320/SSF49899/G3DSA:2.60.120.200, aa 30-254/30-254/29-260) defining the structural fold for sugar binding; Intracellular Lectin and Glycoprotein Transporter family (IPR051136/PTHR12223, aa 10-486) placing LMAN1L in the specialized ERGIC-53 subfamily of cargo receptors. Functional hypothesis: LMAN1L functions as a cargo receptor where its legume-like lectin domain (aa 31-253) binds high-mannose N-glycans on specific client glycoproteins in the ER lumen; it forms a heterotetrameric complex with MCFD2 (STRING 0.823) to expand cargo specificity beyond LMAN1's repertoire; the complex recruits COPII coat components (SEC23A, SEC13) to package cargo into transport vesicles; in the Golgi, pH-dependent conformational changes trigger cargo release; LMAN1L may specialize in transporting glycoproteins critical for prostate function, cardiac integrity, or tumor progression.
