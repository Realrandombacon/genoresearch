## Description
C1orf43 (also known as LTAP1) encodes a 253 amino acid intracellular protein (UniProt Q9BWL3) belonging to the NICE-3/lipid transport auxiliary protein family (IPR010876, PF07406, PTHR21425). Despite its designation as a 'lipid transport auxiliary' protein, its precise molecular mechanism remains incompletely characterized, with only 13 publications addressing its function.\n\nSTRUCTURAL FEATURES: AlphaFold predicts a confident structure (pLDDT 73.1) across all 253 residues, indicating a well-folded stable protein. InterPro analysis reveals membership in the NICE-3 protein family (residues 7-187) without recognizable catalytic domains, suggesting C1orf43 functions as a scaffold or adaptor rather than an enzyme. The lack of transmembrane domains combined with HPA's prediction of membrane association suggests peripheral membrane binding via lipid interaction motifs or protein partners.\n\nINTERACTION NETWORK: STRING-DB identifies 10 interaction partners revealing a striking enrichment for membrane trafficking machinery. ESCRT pathway: CHMP2A (0.658, charged multivesicular body protein, essential for endosomal sorting). Retromer complex: VPS29 (0.546, retromer subunit regulating endosome-to-Golgi retrieval). ER membrane complex: EMC7 (0.598, ER membrane protein complex subunit involved in membrane protein insertion). Proteasome subunits: PSMB2 (0.591), PSMB4 (0.496) suggest potential ubiquitin-dependent turnover regulation. Additional partners include CRCT1 (0.657, cornification-related), KIAA1109 (0.561, ciliopathy-associated), and MRFAP1 (0.542, Morf4l1-binding protein).\n\nEXPRESSION & LOCALIZATION: HPA confirms dual localization to mitochondria and cytosol, with ubiquitous expression across all tissues (low tissue specificity) and low cancer specificity. The mitochondrial localization is particularly notable given the interaction with ESCRT/retromer components, which typically function at endosomes.\n\nCLINICAL SIGNIFICANCE: ClinVar reports 7 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number gains/losses (1q21.3, 1q12-23.1, 1q21.3-23.1), duplications, and inversions. While specific monogenic diseases are not yet annotated, the variant burden and genomic location suggest potential contributions to 1q21.1 microdeletion/duplication syndrome phenotypes.\n\nFUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: Recent literature (13 papers) reveals emerging roles: (1) Host cell regulator of Legionella pneumophila pathogenesis (genome-wide CRISPR screen hit), suggesting involvement in bacterial vacuole formation or ER remodeling. (2) Differentially expressed in African ancestry colorectal adenocarcinoma with mismatch-repair deficiency, implicating immune microenvironment modulation. (3) Autism blood transcriptome alterations. Mechanistically, C1orf43 likely functions at ER-mitochondrial contact sites (MAMs—mitochondrial-associated membranes), where it coordinates lipid transfer between organelles via interaction with ESCRT machinery (CHMP2A) for membrane remodeling and retromer (VPS29) for cargo retrieval. Its recruitment by Legionella effectors suggests the bacterium hijacks C1orf43-mediated lipid trafficking to establish its replicative niche. The proteasome interactions indicate regulated turnover, potentially coupling lipid transport capacity to cellular metabolic state.
