## Description
PKIG (cAMP-dependent protein kinase inhibitor gamma, UniProt Q9Y2B9) is a 76-amino acid intracellular protein functioning as a specific inhibitor of cAMP-dependent protein kinase A (PKA). InterPro analysis confirms membership in the PKI inhibitor family (IPR004171, PF02827, PIRSF001667, PTHR15416; positions 1-76) with GO annotations for cAMP-dependent protein kinase inhibitor activity (GO:0004862) and negative regulation of protein kinase activity (GO:0006469). STRING interactions reveal high-confidence partnerships with PKA catalytic subunits PRKACA (0.653), PRKACB (0.764), PRKACG (0.551) and related inhibitors PKIA (0.982), PKIB (0.637), plus ADA (0.719) and FAM171B (0.428), confirming role in cAMP signaling regulation. HPA shows ubiquitous expression across all tissues with low tissue specificity, subcellular localization to nucleoplasm, vesicles, plasma membrane, and cytosol, with blood cell group enrichment. AlphaFold predicts medium-confidence structure (pLDDT 62.9) consistent with partially disordered regulatory protein. ClinVar documents 11 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number losses/gains on chromosome 20q11-13, though specific disease associations remain undefined. Literature (15 papers) reveals PKIG reciprocally regulates osteoblast and adipocyte differentiation by downregulating leukemia inhibitory factor (LIF, 12 citations, 2013), affects gene expression in glioma cells under glucose deprivation via ERN1-dependent manner (3 citations, 2020), and appears in microRNA regulatory networks in essential thrombocytosis (9 citations, 2018). Functional hypothesis: PKIG acts as a metabolic and lineage specification switch by modulating PKA signaling thresholds, balancing osteogenic versus adipogenic differentiation through LIF suppression, and contributing to hematopoietic regulation in thrombocytosis. Mechanistic proposal: PKIG binds PKA catalytic subunits via pseudosubstrate inhibition, preventing phosphorylation of CREB and other substrates, thereby altering transcriptional programs governing cell fate decisions in bone marrow stromal cells and potentially contributing to copy number variant-associated developmental disorders.
