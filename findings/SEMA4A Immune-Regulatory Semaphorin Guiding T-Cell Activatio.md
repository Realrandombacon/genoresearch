# SEMA4A: Immune-Regulatory Semaphorin Guiding T-Cell Activation and Retinal Homeostasis via Plexin-B Receptors with Mutations in Cone-Rod Dystrophy

**Quality Score:** 10/10 (EXCELLENT)

## Description
SEMA4A (Semaphorin-4A, UniProt Q9H3S1) is a 761-amino acid type I transmembrane protein belonging to the class 4 semaphorin family, functioning as a critical bidirectional signaling molecule in both the immune system and the central nervous system. Despite 256 literature papers, SEMA4A remains under-characterized regarding its specific mechanistic roles in human disease compared to other semaphorins. The protein architecture features a large extracellular region dominated by a conserved Sema domain (IPR001627, PF01403, cd11256, aa 36-495) responsible for receptor binding and dimerization, followed by a PSI domain (IPR016201, aa 496-548) and three immunoglobulin-like C2-type domains (IPR060660, aa 563-652) stabilizing the structure, a single transmembrane helix, and a short cytoplasmic tail mediating reverse signaling. The AlphaFold predicted structure shows high confidence (pLDDT 84.3), indicating a rigid, well-folded extracellular scaffold capable of precise receptor engagement. STRING interaction network places SEMA4A within the axon guidance and immune synapse machinery with high-confidence associations to: PLXNB3 (0.972), PLXNB1 (0.931), and PLXNB2 (0.912), plexin-B family receptors serving as primary high-affinity binding partners; AIPL1 (0.774), aryl-hydrocarbon interacting protein-like 1 linked to retinal dystrophy; PLXND1 (0.739), plexin-D1; ABCA4 (0.732), ATP-binding cassette transporter mutated in Stargardt disease; NRP1 (0.670), neuropilin-1 co-receptor; CERKL (0.654), ceramide kinase-like protein associated with retinitis pigmentosa; and PRPF3 (0.644), pre-mRNA processing factor 3. HPA expression profiles demonstrate tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in epididymis (48.4 nTPM), group-enriched expression in blood cells (particularly T-cells and dendritic cells), classification under adaptive immunity, angiogenesis, neurogenesis, and differentiation processes, and direct involvement in cone-rod dystrophy (CORD10) and retinitis pigmentosa (RP35). ClinVar records document 41 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number gains on chromosome 1q (spanning 1q21.1-25.3, 143-185 Mb) and losses, plus specific missense variants such as p.Glu459Asp (c.1377G>C) within the Sema domain potentially disrupting receptor binding affinity; these mutations cause autosomal dominant cone-rod dystrophy characterized by progressive vision loss, color vision defects, and macular degeneration. Literature analysis reveals dual functionality: (1) In immunity, SEMA4A is upregulated on activated dendritic cells and binds CD72 and TIM-2 on T-cells to lower activation thresholds, promote Th1 differentiation, and sustain germinal center reactions; (2) In the retina, SEMA4A maintains photoreceptor integrity through unknown plexin-mediated signaling, where loss-of-function leads to photoreceptor apoptosis. We hypothesize that SEMA4A functions through a context-dependent mechanism: (1) In immune synapses, membrane-bound SEMA4A on antigen-presenting cells engages Plexin-B1/B2/B3 or CD72 on naive T-cells, triggering forward signaling that activates RhoA/ROCK pathways to reorganize the actin cytoskeleton for stable synapse formation while delivering co-stimulatory signals enhancing IL-2 production; (2) Reverse signaling through the SEMA4A cytoplasmic tail in dendritic cells modulates cytokine secretion profiles favoring Th1 responses; (3) In the retina, SEMA4A expressed by Müller glia or photoreceptors interacts with Plexin receptors on neighboring cells to maintain structural adhesion and suppress apoptotic pathways; (4) Mutations in the Sema domain (e.g., E459D) destabilize the dimer interface or block receptor docking, leading to haploinsufficiency or dominant-negative effects causing photoreceptor degeneration; (5) In cancer, SEMA4A may exhibit tumor-suppressive or promotive roles depending on the microenvironment, potentially regulating angiogenesis via Plexin-D1. The high-confidence structure suggests drugability for blocking immune activation in autoimmunity or enhancing survival in retinal degeneration.
