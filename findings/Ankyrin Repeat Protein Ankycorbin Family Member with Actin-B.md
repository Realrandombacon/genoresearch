## Description
ANKRD24 (UniProt Q8TF21) is a 1146 amino acid ankyrin repeat domain-containing protein representing an understudied component of the human dark genome with emerging roles in cytoskeletal organization and neurodevelopment. The protein architecture is dominated by multiple ankyrin repeat motifs: IPR002110 ankyrin repeats at positions 81-113, 114-212, 213-245 mediating protein-protein interactions (GO:0005515); IPR036770 ankyrin repeat-containing domain superfamily spanning residues 34-267 forming a structured solenoid fold; and membership in the IPR042420 Ankycorbin/UACA family (residues 42-316) with annotated actin binding capability (GO:0003779). The G3DSA:1.25.40.20 ankyrin repeat-containing domain appears at two positions (34-187, 188-267), while PF00023/PF12796 Pfam families and PS50088/PS50297 profiles confirm the repetitive ankyrin architecture. SMART SM00248 identifies three discrete ankyrin repeats at 81-110, 114-143, 147-178. PTHR24129 classifies this as ANKYCORBIN family. AlphaFold predicts a medium-confidence structure (global pLDDT 69.4) spanning the full 1-1146 residue length, consistent with a protein containing ordered ankyrin repeat domains connected by flexible linker regions—structural characteristics typical of scaffold proteins requiring conformational adaptability for cytoskeletal complex assembly. HPA classifies ANKRD24 as a predicted intracellular protein with tissue-enhanced RNA expression detected in many tissues, showing highest expression in brain (32.9 nTPM) followed by testis (13.7 nTPM), suggesting specialized function in neuronal cells and spermatogenesis. Subcellular localization to microtubules and cytosol aligns perfectly with the ankyrin repeat domain's predicted role in cytoskeletal organization and microtubule-associated complex assembly. STRING interaction network reveals medium-confidence associations with functionally relevant partners: C1QTNF4 (0.559, complement C1q tumor necrosis factor-related protein 4, adipokine with metabolic functions), GRXCR1 (0.452, glutaredoxin domain-containing cysteine-rich protein 1, critical for stereocilia development and hearing), F8A3 (0.420, coagulation factor VIII associated 3, X-linked gene), CCDC184 (0.408, coiled-coil domain-containing 184), RASL11A (0.401, RAS-like family 11 member A, small GTPase), CENPL (0.400, centromere protein L, kinetochore component). This interaction profile suggests roles in cytoskeletal dynamics (GRXCR1 links to actin/stereocilia), cell division (CENPL kinetochore), and small GTPase signaling (RASL11A). ClinVar contains 21 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including both large copy number alterations affecting chromosome 19p13.3 (chr19:3134687-5513059, chr19:260912-7246777, chr19:3501624-5357124, chr19:3976203-4345430, chr19:260912-4384674, chr19:260911-4788357, chr19:3076808-4796782, chr19:3120160-9732820, chr19:260912-58956888) and a protein-truncating frameshift mutation (c.1934_1937del p.Thr645fs). The 19p13.3 region is dosage-sensitive and deletions/duplications cause neurodevelopmental disorders including intellectual disability, autism spectrum disorder, and seizures. While no specific disease is currently attributed solely to ANKRD24 mutations, its location within this critical region combined with brain-enriched expression and cytoskeletal function suggests haploinsufficiency may contribute to 19p13.3 syndrome phenotypes. Evolutionary conservation across mammals (mouse Q80VM7, 985 aa; rat D3Z908, 988 aa) and birds (golden eagle A0A663FFF6, 960 aa) indicates strong functional constraint. The mechanistic model positions ANKRD24 as a cytoskeletal scaffold protein using its ankyrin repeat domains (residues 34-267) for protein-protein interactions and the Ankycorbin family module (residues 42-316) for direct actin binding, potentially linking microtubules to actin filaments in neuronal growth cones, dendritic spines, or axonal transport complexes. Brain-enriched expression (32.9 nTPM) suggests involvement in neuronal morphogenesis, synaptic plasticity, or axon guidance. The frameshift mutation at Thr645 would truncate the protein, likely causing loss-of-function through nonsense-mediated decay or production of a dominant-negative fragment. Dosage alteration through CNVs would disrupt stoichiometric balance of cytoskeletal complexes, impairing neuronal migration, axon pathfinding, or synapse formation—mechanisms underlying neurodevelopmental phenotypes observed in 19p13.3 CNV syndromes.
