# LOC112061823: 11q24 Enhancer with High Dosage Sensitivity in Jacobsen Syndrome Region

**Date:** 2026-03-19T02:57:24.746089

**Quality Score:** 3.95/10  (E=4.7, D=3.2) [MODERATE]

## Description
LOC112061823 is a Sharpr-MPRA regulatory region 13105 located on chromosome 11q24.1 (genomic coordinates: 130037073-130037367 on NC_000011.10). This 295 bp genomic sequence was predicted as a transcriptional regulatory region based on ENCODE chromatin state analysis and experimentally validated as a functional enhancer using Sharpr-MPRA in HepG2 liver carcinoma cells, indicating active transcriptional regulation in hepatic tissue. As a non-coding regulatory element, it lacks protein-coding potential (no UniProt entry, no InterPro domains, no AlphaFold structure, no STRING interactions, no HPA protein expression). ClinVar analysis identified 36 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly copy number losses (deletions) with some duplications, indicating strong dosage sensitivity where haploinsufficiency is the primary pathogenic mechanism. The variant count (36 total) is substantially higher than LOC113939913 (4 variants on 10q) and LOC113939919 (11 variants on 11q), suggesting this enhancer regulates critical genes in the 11q24-25 region. The 11q24-25 chromosomal region is associated with Jacobsen syndrome (11q terminal deletion syndrome), which presents with thrombocytopenia, cardiac defects, craniofacial abnormalities, and developmental delay. Critical genes in this region include FLI1 (friend leukemia integration 1 transcription factor - thrombocytopenia), ETS1 (ETS proto-oncogene 1 - immune development), and KMT2A/MLL (lysine methyltransferase 2A - leukemia). As an enhancer active in liver cells, this element likely regulates expression of one or more of these critical genes in cis. The predominance of deletion pathogenicity suggests loss of enhancer activity causes haploinsufficiency of target genes, contributing to Jacobsen syndrome phenotypes. This enhancer may specifically regulate FLI1 expression in megakaryocyte/liver lineages, explaining the thrombocytopenia characteristic of 11q deletion syndrome.
