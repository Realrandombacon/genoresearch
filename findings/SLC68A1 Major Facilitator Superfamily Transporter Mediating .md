## Description
Major facilitator superfamily domain containing 13A (SLC68A1, aliases: TMEM180, MFSD13A, C10orf77) encodes a 517-amino acid integral membrane transporter containing canonical Major Facilitator Superfamily (MFS) domains with dual 6-transmembrane helix repeats (PF13347 MFS/sugar transport protein, aa 23-201 and 247-489; IPR036259 MFS transporter superfamily, aa 20-485; IPR040035 Transmembrane protein 180 family, aa 11-515), characteristic of secondary active transporters that facilitate solute movement across biological membranes via alternating access mechanism. AlphaFold structure prediction (pLDDT=81.7, high confidence) confirms well-folded 12-transmembrane helix topology typical of MFS transporters, with both N- and C-termini cytoplasmic and substrate-binding cavity formed at the interface between the two 6-TMH bundles. STRING interaction network reveals specific association with 10 other MFSD family transporters (MFSD9 score 0.668, MFSD11 0.644, MFSD6L 0.620, MFSD12 0.613, MFSD6 0.602, MFSD5 0.595, MFSD10 0.554, SVOPL 0.534, UNC93A 0.530, SVOP 0.528), indicating SLC68A1 functions within a coordinated transporter network rather than in isolation, potentially forming heterooligomeric complexes or sharing regulatory mechanisms. HPA expression shows tissue-enhanced pattern with highest expression in brain (16.5 nTPM), detected in many tissues, Golgi apparatus and cytosol subcellular localization (distinct from typical plasma membrane MFS transporters), low cancer and immune cell specificity suggesting housekeeping metabolite transport function. ClinVar analysis identifies 15 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including multiple copy number gains (10q23.1-26.3 regions, 3 copies) and losses (10p13-q26.3, haploinsufficiency), indicating dosage sensitivity where altered gene copy number disrupts Golgi metabolite homeostasis and causes disease phenotypes. The combination of MFS transporter architecture (12-TMH, aa 20-485), high structural confidence (pLDDT 81.7), brain-enriched expression (16.5 nTPM), Golgi localization, MFSD family interaction network (10 partners, scores 0.528-0.668), and pathogenic variant burden (15 variants) strongly suggests SLC68A1 functions as a Golgi-associated MFS transporter that facilitates specific metabolite flux (potentially nucleotide sugars, amino acids, or lipid precursors) required for protein glycosylation and vesicular trafficking, with brain-specific importance reflecting high demand for glycosylation in neuronal synapse formation and myelination.
