# RHOBTB3: Atypical Rho GTPase with BTB Domains Functioning as CUL3 E3 Ligase Adaptor Regulating Endosome Biogenesis, Autophagy, and HIF-1 Signaling in Cancer and UV-Induced Skin Damage

## Description
RHOBTB3 (O94955) encodes a 611 amino acid atypical Rho GTPase belonging to the Rho-related BTB domain-containing protein family that functions as a substrate adaptor for CUL3-based E3 ubiquitin ligase complexes regulating vesicular trafficking, autophagy, and hypoxia signaling. InterPro analysis reveals a multimodular architecture: an N-terminal small GTPase domain (IPR001806/G3DSA:3.40.50.300, positions 88-188/42-215) containing P-loop nucleotide triphosphate hydrolase motifs for GTP binding and hydrolysis (GO:0003924/GO:0005525); two BTB/POZ domains (IPR000210/G3DSA:3.30.710.10, positions 254-386/415-518/236-393/410-519) mediating protein-protein interactions and CUL3 ligase recruitment classified within SKP1/BTB/POZ superfamily (IPR011333, positions 233-383/398-565); and a C-terminal BACK domain (BTB and C-terminal Kelch, cd18532, positions 529-611) completing the characteristic RHOBTB architecture. AlphaFold predicts a well-folded structure (pLDDT 78.9, high confidence) consistent with stable multi-domain organization required for coordinated GTPase activity and ubiquitin ligase adaptor function. STRING interactions demonstrate partnerships with endosomal trafficking and ubiquitination machinery: CUL3 (0.931), the cullin scaffold protein forming E3 ligase complexes with BTB adaptors; HGS (0.867), hepatocyte growth factor-regulated tyrosine kinase substrate in ESCRT pathway; RAB9A (0.840) and RAB9B (0.645), Rab GTPases regulating late endosome to Golgi trafficking; GCC2 (0.758), golgin coiled-coil protein at Golgi-endosome contacts; VTI1A (0.756), vesicle transport through interaction with t-SNAREs; LRRC41 (0.689), leucine-rich repeat protein; VHL (0.664) and LIMD1 (0.664), von Hippel-Lindau tumor suppressor pathway components; and PLIN3 (0.548), perilipin lipid droplet protein. HPA shows ubiquitous expression across all tissues with low specificity, vesicle localization consistent with endosomal trafficking function, classified under transport biological process and hydrolase molecular function with immune cell-enhanced expression. ClinVar contains 24 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number losses spanning chromosome 5q14.3-23.3 regions (e.g., chr5:90079852-103658165 deletions, chr5:93828571-123711334 losses, chr5:89949118-129317455 gains) and missense variants (c.238G>T p.Asp80Tyr, c.683-4A>G splice variant), though specific monogenic disease associations remain undefined. Literature (73 papers) demonstrates RHOBTB3 forms a complex with SHIP164 and Vps26B to drive biogenesis of Rab14-positive endosome buds at Golgi-endosome contacts for cargo recycling; RHOBTB3 functions as novel autophagy regulator by suppressing AMBRA1 stability affecting cellular homeostasis; diosmetin attenuates UV-induced skin damage by diminishing RHOBTB3/PHD2 complex formation reducing HIF-1α ubiquitination in keratinocytes; miR-142-3p promotes renal cell carcinoma progression by targeting RHOBTB3 to regulate HIF-1 signaling and GGT/GSH pathways. The mechanistic hypothesis: RHOBTB3 functions dually as (1) an atypical GTPase cycling between GTP/GDP states regulating membrane dynamics at Golgi-endosome contact sites, and (2) a CUL3 E3 ligase adaptor recruiting specific substrates (AMBRA1, HIF-1α regulators) for ubiquitin-mediated degradation; the two BTB domains enable simultaneous CUL3 binding and substrate recognition while the GTPase domain provides membrane association and conformational switching; RAB9A/RAB9B interactions place RHOBTB3 at late endosome-to-Golgi retrograde trafficking hubs where it coordinates cargo sorting into Rab14-positive recycling endosomes via SHIP164-Vps26B complex assembly; VHL/LIMD1 interactions suggest cross-talk with hypoxia signaling pathways explaining RHOBTB3's role in HIF-1α regulation through PHD2 complex modulation; copy number losses on 5q may disrupt endosomal trafficking and autophagy contributing to neurodevelopmental phenotypes, while gains may enhance growth factor receptor recycling promoting oncogenic signaling; high-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 78.9) indicates stable domain interfaces enabling coordinated GTPase-ligase adaptor switching during vesicle budding cycles.
