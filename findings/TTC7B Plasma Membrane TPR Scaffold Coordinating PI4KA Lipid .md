# TTC7B: Plasma Membrane TPR Scaffold Coordinating PI4KA Lipid Kinase Recruitment and AKT Signaling in Brain Development and Cancer Progression via EFR3-FAM126 Complex Assembly

## Description
TTC7B (Tetratricopeptide Repeat Protein 7B) is a partially characterized membrane-associated scaffold protein with 30 publications, emerging as a critical regulator of phosphatidylinositol 4-phosphate (PI4P) homeostasis and AKT signaling pathways. This 843-amino acid protein (UniProt Q86TV6) features an extensive tetratricopeptide repeat (TPR) architecture forming a stable helical scaffold. InterPro analysis reveals multiple TPR domains (IPR019734/PF13181/PF13424, positions 397-430/515-548/549-582/730-763/798-831), a TTC7-specific N-terminal domain (IPR045819/PF19440, positions 1-386), and membership in the endocytosis and PI4K-regulating protein family (IPR051722/PTHR23083, positions 7-843). AlphaFold predicts high-confidence structure (pLDDT 85.0), indicating a well-folded elongated TPR superhelix typical of protein-protein interaction scaffolds.\n\nSTRING interactions reveal TTC7B operates within a tightly coordinated plasma membrane lipid signaling complex: FAM126A/hyccin (0.995, paralog with overlapping function), EFR3B (0.931, PI4KA recruitment factor), TMEM150A (0.665, transmembrane protein), FAM126B (0.662, second paralog), C2orf50 (0.593), PI4KB (0.531, phosphatidylinositol 4-kinase beta), TMED6 (0.492, transmembrane emp24 domain-containing protein), TEX2 (0.485), EFR3A (0.477, paralog of EFR3B), and PI4KA (0.420, phosphatidylinositol 4-kinase alpha). The exceptionally high confidence link to FAM126A and EFR3B positions TTC7B as a core component of the EFR3-TTC7-FAM126 complex that recruits PI4KA to the plasma membrane for PI4P synthesis.\n\nHPA shows tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in brain (105.9 nTPM), consistent with roles in neuronal development and myelination. Subcellular localization is plasma membrane, matching its function in lipid kinase recruitment. Biological processes involve endocytosis and phosphoinositide metabolism. ClinVar contains 19 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number losses and gains spanning chromosome 14q31.1-32.33, though specific monogenic diseases remain uncharacterized—suggesting dosage sensitivity may underlie neurodevelopmental disorders. Conservation across mouse (Ttc7b, 843 aa) indicates strong functional constraint.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: TTC7B functions as an essential scaffold that bridges the EFR3 membrane anchor proteins with PI4KA lipid kinase, enabling localized production of PI4P at the plasma membrane. This PI4P pool serves multiple critical functions: (1) recruiting downstream effectors for clathrin-mediated endocytosis, (2) serving as precursor for PIP2 and PIP3 second messengers that activate AKT signaling, and (3) maintaining plasma membrane identity through lipid compartmentalization. Recent 2025 studies reveal TTC7B triggers the PI4KA-AKT1-RXRA-FTO axis in colon cancer, where it inhibits proliferation by increasing RNA m6A methylation—paradoxically, in head and neck cancer, TTC7B activates AKT-JKAMP signaling to promote tumor progression, indicating context-dependent oncogenic versus tumor suppressive roles. The nanobody development targeting TTC7B-EFR3 interaction (2025) validates this complex as druggable for cancers with PI4KA dysregulation. FAM126A mutations cause hypomyelinating leukodystrophy, suggesting TTC7B deficiency may cause similar neurodevelopmental phenotypes through impaired PI4P-dependent myelination. Large 14q CNVs likely cause developmental delay, intellectual disability, or neuropsychiatric disorders via haploinsufficiency or triplosensitivity affecting TTC7B dosage.
