# PAFAH1B2: Cytosolic SGNH Hydrolase Catalytic Subunit Degrading Platelet-Activating Factor in Lipid Signaling with Roles in Cancer Metastasis, Vascular Remodeling, and Hypoxia Response

## Description
PAFAH1B2 (P68402) encodes a 229 amino acid catalytic subunit of platelet-activating factor (PAF) acetylhydrolase IB, a cytosolic enzyme complex that hydrolyzes the sn-2 acetyl group of PAF and oxidized phospholipids, terminating pro-inflammatory lipid signaling and maintaining membrane lipid homeostasis. InterPro analysis reveals a canonical SGNH hydrolase architecture: an SGNH hydrolase-type esterase domain (IPR013830/PF13472, positions 44-203) embedded within the SGNH hydrolase superfamily (IPR036514/G3DSA:3.40.50.1110, positions 1-229) and PAF acetylhydrolase family (PTHR11852, positions 23-225), with the catalytic serine nucleophile positioned within the GDSL-like lipase/acylhydrolase fold essential for ester bond hydrolysis of PAF's sn-2 acetyl group. AlphaFold predicts an exceptionally well-folded structure (pLDDT 93.9, very high confidence) consistent with stable enzymatic architecture required for precise substrate positioning and catalysis. STRING interactions demonstrate exclusive partnerships with lipid metabolism machinery: PAFAH1B3 (0.998), the related catalytic subunit forming heterotrimeric complexes; PAFAH2 (0.923), the ER-associated PAF acetylhydrolase; PLA2G7 (0.906), the plasma PAF acetylhydrolase; LPCAT1/2/4 (0.900-0.901), lysophosphatidylcholine acyltransferases in Lands cycle; PAFAH1B1/LIS1 (0.901), the regulatory subunit linking to neuronal migration; CHPT1 (0.900), cholinephosphotransferase; CEPT1 (0.900), choline/ethanolamine phosphotransferase; and NDEL1 (0.816), a dynein regulator linking PAF signaling to cytoskeletal dynamics. HPA shows ubiquitous expression across all tissues with low specificity, nucleoli/plasma membrane/cytosol localization consistent with dual roles in lipid metabolism and potential nuclear functions, classified under lipid degradation/lipid metabolism/hydrolase activity biological processes. ClinVar contains 27 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number gains spanning chromosome 11q23.3-25 regions (e.g., chr11:115887338-126148523 duplications, chr11:116683755-134937416 gains) and deletions (chr11:104288964-134937416 losses), though specific monogenic disease associations remain undefined. Literature (54 papers) demonstrates PAFAH1B2 is a HIF1α target gene promoting metastasis in pancreatic cancer; miR-212-5p targets PAFAH1B2 to suppress vascular proliferation and contraction via RhoA downregulation in hypertension; linc00662/miR-145-5p/PAFAH1B2 axis regulates lung cancer cell proliferation; cathepsin G-induced malignant progression involves PAFAH1B2-mediated PAF signaling suppression; LOX-PAFAH1B2 interaction characterized via molecular docking. The mechanistic hypothesis: PAFAH1B2 functions as the catalytic engine of cytosolic PAF acetylhydrolase IB complexes, hydrolyzing PAF and oxidized phospholipids generated during oxidative stress to terminate pro-inflammatory signaling and prevent excessive leukocyte activation; under hypoxic conditions, HIF1α induces PAFAH1B2 expression to modulate lipid mediator profiles favoring tumor cell survival and metastatic dissemination; in vascular smooth muscle cells, PAFAH1B2 regulates RhoA-dependent contraction pathways through PAF degradation, with miR-212-5p providing post-transcriptional control preventing excessive vasoconstriction in hypertension; the heterotrimeric complex with PAFAH1B1/LIS1 and PAFAH1B3 suggests coupling between lipid signaling and microtubule dynein motor function during neuronal migration and cell division; copy number gains on 11q may drive oncogenic programs through elevated PAF degradation altering tumor microenvironment inflammation, while losses may predispose to inflammatory disorders through PAF accumulation; exceptionally high-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 93.9) indicates rigid catalytic pocket geometry enabling precise substrate orientation for efficient acetyl group hydrolysis.
