## Description
NPW encodes a 165 aa neuropeptide precursor (UniProt Q8N729) processed into bioactive peptides (NPW-23, NPW-30) localized to secretory vesicles, functioning as a critical regulator of energy homeostasis, stress response, and cancer progression. InterPro analysis confirms its canonical Neuropeptide B/W architecture: Neuropeptide B/W precursor family domain (IPR013297, aa 8-138) with G protein-coupled receptor binding activity (GO:0001664), and Neuropeptide W precursor motifs (IPR013299, aa 36-46/62-73/98-112) essential for receptor activation. AlphaFold predicts medium-confidence structure (pLDDT 58.3), characteristic of neuropeptide precursors with intrinsically disordered regions that become structured upon receptor binding. STRING interactions reveal a specialized neuropeptide network: NPY (0.675, neuropeptide Y, primary partner), CORT (0.670, cortistatin), POMC (0.668, proopiomelanocortin), NPB (0.598, neuropeptide B paralog), NPBWR1 (0.510, GPR7 receptor), NPBWR2 (0.571, GPR8 receptor), and GAL (0.499, galanin). HPA expression shows liver enrichment (25.1 nTPM) with cancer enhancement, consistent with roles in metabolic regulation and tumor growth. ClinVar reports 33 pathogenic variants, predominantly large 16p13.3 copy number gains (x4) and losses (x4), suggesting dosage sensitivity; specific deletions may disrupt peptide processing. Literature analysis reveals a critical naming collision: 961 papers indexed under 'NPW' are predominantly about 'Negative Pressure Wave' pipeline leak detection technology, NOT the neuropeptide - genuine biological characterization is limited to ~50 papers. Functional hypothesis: NPW functions through: (1) GPCR Signaling - activating NPBWR1/NPBWR2 receptors to modulate cAMP/Ca2+ signaling in hypothalamus and peripheral tissues; (2) Energy Homeostasis - regulating food intake, body weight, and glucose metabolism via central and hepatic pathways; (3) Stress Response - interacting with CORT/POMC to modulate HPA axis activity; (4) Cancer Progression - overexpression driving tumor growth via autocrine/paracrine neuropeptide signaling. Disease implications include Obesity, Metabolic Syndrome, Anxiety Disorders, and various Cancers. Despite clinical variant burden, the precise physiological substrates and full signaling cascade remain partially unexplored due to literature naming confusion.
