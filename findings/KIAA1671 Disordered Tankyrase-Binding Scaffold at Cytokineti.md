## Description
KIAA1671 encodes a 1806-amino acid intrinsically disordered protein (UniProt Q9BY89) localized to microtubules, cytokinetic bridge, and primary cilium. The protein belongs to the tankyrase 1-binding protein family (InterPro IPR032764, IPR040006, PF15327, PTHR22042, SM01319) with a conserved C-terminal tankyrase-binding domain (residues 1626-1797). AlphaFold predicts a predominantly disordered structure (pLDDT 39.6), characteristic of scaffolding proteins that function through multivalent protein-protein interactions.

FUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: KIAA1671 functions as a disordered scaffolding protein that recruits tankyrase enzymes to the cytokinetic bridge and primary cilium, coordinating abscission timing and ciliary disassembly during cell cycle progression. The C-terminal tankyrase-binding domain anchors PARP family enzymes (TNKS1/TNKS2) to specific subcellular compartments, while the disordered N-terminal region (residues 1-1625) provides multivalent interaction surfaces for client proteins. Mechanistically, KIAA1671 links the mTOR signaling pathway (TSC1/TSC2/MTOR interactions) to cytoskeletal remodeling at the intercellular bridge, ensuring proper coordination between nutrient sensing and cell division completion.

INTERACTION NETWORK: KIAA1671 shows high-confidence interactions with TSC2 (0.705) and TSC1 (0.688), core components of the mTORC1 inhibitory complex, suggesting roles in cell growth regulation. Cytoskeletal partners include ITSN1 (0.539) and ITSN2 (0.540, intersectins involved in endocytosis), PCNT (0.462, pericentrin at centrosomes), and CCDC102A (0.492, coiled-coil protein). Additional interactions with RPP14 (0.516, RNase P subunit), GALNT16 (0.476, glycosyltransferase), ENTR1 (0.474, endosomal protein), and MTOR (0.460) reveal connections to RNA processing, protein glycosylation, and endosomal trafficking.

EXPRESSION & DISEASE: HPA classifies KIAA1671 as a predicted intracellular protein with low tissue specificity, detected in many tissues, and immune cell-enhanced expression. Subcellular locations include microtubules, cytokinetic bridge, and primary cilium—structures critical for cell division and ciliary signaling. ClinVar documents 18 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly chromosome 22q copy number gains (multiple CNV entries spanning 22q11.1-13.33), suggesting dosage sensitivity and potential haploinsufficiency or gain-of-function mechanisms.

CONSERVATION: Mouse ortholog (Q8BRV5) encodes a much shorter 308-amino acid protein, indicating extensive human-specific N-terminal and C-terminal extensions. The tankyrase-binding domain architecture is conserved, but the disordered regions show species-specific elaboration, possibly reflecting human-specific regulatory complexity in cell cycle control. Bovine ortholog (A0A3Q1LZT0, 391 aa) also shows intermediate length, supporting progressive domain accretion in mammalian evolution.
