## Description
THAP8 (THAP domain-containing protein 8, UniProt Q8NA92) is a 274-amino acid nuclear transcription factor located on chromosome 19q13.11 within a region prone to extensive copy-number variations. InterPro analysis reveals canonical THAP-type zinc finger architecture: N-terminal THAP domain (IPR006612/PF05485/PS50950/SM00692/SM00980, aa 1-91/5-85/26-90/3-91) belonging to the THAP domain-containing protein family (IPR052224/PTHR46927, aa 1-151) that forms a C2CH zinc-coordinating motif characteristic of this atypical zinc finger subfamily. The THAP domain belongs to the glucocorticoid receptor-like DNA-binding domain superfamily (SSF57716, aa 1-86), confirming DNA-binding capability. GO molecular function annotations confirm DNA-binding activity, and protein class indicates transcription factor functionality with nucleoplasm subcellular localization. AlphaFold structure prediction achieves high confidence (pLDDT 71.9), indicating a well-folded protein with stable THAP zinc finger fold connected to structured C-terminal regions — architecture consistent with chromatin-associated transcriptional regulators. Human Protein Atlas shows low tissue specificity with expression detected in many tissues, low cancer and immune cell specificity, cell type-enhanced patterns, and nucleoplasm localization — suggesting ubiquitous roles in transcriptional regulation across cell types. This highly specific THAP family interactome suggests functional redundancy or cooperative assembly within transcriptional regulatory complexes, potentially recruiting chromatin modifiers like PRMT7 and HCFC1 for epigenetic regulation. ClinVar contains 12 pathogenic variants consisting predominantly of large copy-number gains and losses across 19q11-13.33, demonstrating dosage sensitivity where genomic rearrangements disrupting chromosome 19 cause disease phenotypes. Conservation across vertebrates (chicken A0A8V0XMD0, 308 aa; Anolis carolinensis H9G6G7, 509 aa) indicates ancient evolutionary origin tied to fundamental transcriptional mechanisms. THAP8 likely functions as a sequence-specific DNA-binding transcription factor, using its THAP zinc finger domain to recognize target gene promoters and recruit chromatin-modifying complexes (via HCFC1/PRMT7 interactions) to regulate gene expression programs involved in cell proliferation, differentiation, or stress responses — dysfunction may contribute to developmental disorders and cancers where chromosome 19q rearrangements are frequent.
