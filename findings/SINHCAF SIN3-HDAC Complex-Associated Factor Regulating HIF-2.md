## Description
SINHCAF (also known as FAM60A) encodes a 221 aa SIN3-HDAC complex-associated factor (UniProt Q9NP50) located at 12p13.33, functioning as a critical regulatory subunit of the SIN3A-histone deacetylase (HDAC) corepressor complex that mediates transcriptional repression of specific targets including HIF-2α and genes required for primordial germ cell (PGC) specification. InterPro analysis reveals a unique, conserved architecture: full-length SINHCAF/FAM60A family membership (IPR026065/PF15396/PTHR13422, aa 1-219/2-197) with no homology to other known protein domains, suggesting it acts as a specialized adaptor or scaffold protein rather than an enzyme. AlphaFold predicts a confident structure (pLDDT 72.7), indicating a well-folded globular protein despite lacking recognizable domain motifs, likely forming a stable interface for binding SIN3A and HDACs. STRING interactions reveal an exclusive, high-confidence network within the SIN3A-HDAC repressive machinery: SIN3A (0.990, core scaffold), HDAC2 (0.976), SAP30 (0.974), HDAC1 (0.967), SUDS3 (0.963), BRMS1 (0.958), ING1 (0.952), SAP30L (0.910), ING2 (0.725), BRMS1L (0.667), confirming its role as an integral complex component. HPA expression data is unavailable, but literature indicates ubiquitous expression with functional importance in germ cells and macrophages. ClinVar reports 21 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, exclusively large 12p copy number gains (x3, x4) spanning 12p13.33-11.1, suggesting extreme dosage sensitivity where overexpression disrupts developmental programs (e.g., Pallister-Killian syndrome features). Literature (8 papers) details emerging mechanistic roles: (1) HIF-2α Repression - SINHCAF/SIN3A specifically recruiting HDACs to the EPAS1 promoter to repress HIF-2α under normoxia, with loss leading to pseudohypoxic signaling and poor cancer survival; (2) PGC Specification - SINHCAF-dependent histone deacetylation essential for silencing somatic programs and activating germline factors during early embryogenesis; (3) Macrophage/TB Response - BATF2/SINHCAF axis regulating macrophage quantity/function in Mycobacterium tuberculosis infection via TTC23 and Wnt/β-catenin pathway modulation. Functional hypothesis: SINHCAF likely functions through: (1) Complex Stabilization - acting as a structural 'glue' or allosteric regulator within the SIN3A-HDAC complex, enhancing recruitment to specific genomic loci (HIF-2α targets, germline genes); (2) Target Specificity - conferring substrate selectivity to the general HDAC machinery, distinguishing HIF-2α repression from other SIN3A targets; (3) Developmental Switch - facilitating the epigenetic transition from somatic to germline identity by enforcing robust deacetylation of pluripotency/somatic genes; (4) Immune Modulation - integrating BATF2 signaling with chromatin remodeling to tune macrophage responses to intracellular pathogens via Wnt pathway crosstalk; (5) Dosage Sensitivity - 12p amplification causing SINHCAF overexpression, potentially hyper-repressing HIF-2α or germline factors, contributing to developmental defects in 12p duplication syndromes. The lack of homology to other domains suggests SINHCAF represents a vertebrate-specific innovation in epigenetic regulation. Disease implications include: cancer prognosis (HIF-2α driven tumors), infertility (PGC failure), tuberculosis susceptibility, and 12p duplication syndromes. Despite only 8 papers, SINHCAF's precise binding interface within SIN3A-HDAC, full range of target genes, and therapeutic potential in hypoxia-driven cancers remain underexplored.
