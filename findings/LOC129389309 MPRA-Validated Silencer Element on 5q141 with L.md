## Description
LOC129389309 is a 200bp cis-regulatory element (CRE) located on chromosome 5q14.1 (GRCh38: 82514735-82514935), functionally validated as a transcriptional silencer through STARR-seq massively parallel reporter assays (MPRAs) in K562 erythroleukemia cells. This element lacks canonical promoter, enhancer, or CTCF-bound insulator chromatin signatures but demonstrates robust repressive activity against the super core promoter SCP1. As a non-coding regulatory locus, it has no UniProt accession, protein domains, or STRING interaction partners. Human Protein Atlas could not resolve this locus to an Ensembl ID, consistent with its non-coding regulatory classification.\n\nClinVar analysis reveals 1 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variant disrupting this region—a single large duplication spanning 5q13.3-22.1 (~36.6Mb). This duplication-only burden (0 deletions:1 duplication) suggests triplosensitivity where silencer gain causes hyper-repression of target genes. The 5q13-22 region is gene-dense, containing critical dosage-sensitive genes such as SMN1/SMN2 (Survival Motor Neuron 1/2, deletions cause spinal muscular atrophy), NRG1 (Neuregulin-1, schizophrenia susceptibility), and multiple developmental transcription factors including IRX1 (Iroquois Homeobox 1).\n\nHYPOTHESIS: LOC129389309 functions as a critical distal silencer within the 5q14 topologically associating domain (TAD), regulating expression of dosage-sensitive developmental and neuronal genes. Candidate targets include IRX1 (cardiac and neural development) or NRG1 (neural crest migration, schizophrenia risk). The silencer likely recruits co-repressor complexes (PRC2, NCoR/SMRT, HDACs) to deposit H3K27me3 marks at target promoters. The low CNV burden with duplication-only pattern (0:1 ratio) suggests triplosensitivity intolerance where silencer gain causes excessive repression of neuronal/developmental genes. The K562 validation suggests potential roles in hematopoietic lineage specification. Duplication may cause NRG1 or IRX1 hyper-repression leading to neurodevelopmental phenotypes or cardiac defects, contributing to 5q duplication syndrome.
