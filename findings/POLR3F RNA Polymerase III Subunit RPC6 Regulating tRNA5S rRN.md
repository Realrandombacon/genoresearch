## Description
POLR3F (RNA polymerase III subunit F/RPC6, 316 aa, UniProt Q9H1D9) is a partially characterized RNA polymerase III subunit with emerging roles in antiviral immunity and POLR3-related disorders, representing an understudied transcriptional machinery component. Structural analysis reveals: (1) RNA polymerase Rpc34 family domain (IPR007832/PF05158, aa 13-315) defining RPC6 subunit identity; (2) Two winged helix DNA-binding domains (IPR036390/G3DSA:1.10.10.10, aa 13-77 and aa 89-154) enabling DNA recognition and transcription factor interactions; (3) RNA polymerase Rpc34-like fold (IPR016049/PTHR12780, aa 14-316) for Pol III complex assembly; (4) Winged helix superfamily architecture (SSF46785, aa 12-78 and aa 79-155) characteristic of transcriptional regulators. AlphaFold structure shows high confidence (pLDDT 84.7), indicating stable folded structure with dual DNA-binding modules positioned for promoter recognition. ClinVar contains 20 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including splice site mutations (c.430-10T>G, c.429+1G>A, c.317-11C>G), large deletions (20p13-11.21), copy number gains, and missense variants (p.Met232Lys, p.Ile45Val), indicating clinical significance in POLR3-related disorders though specific disease associations require further characterization. Literature (13 papers) reveals emerging roles: (1) POLR3-related disorders - comprehensive genotype-phenotype analysis links POLR3F mutations to 4H leukodystrophy (hypomyelination, hypodontia, hypogonadotropic hypogonadism) and Wiedemann-Steiner-like syndrome; (2) antiviral defense - RNA Polymerase III suppresses varicella zoster virus (VZV) in human neurons via cytosolic DNA sensing triggering interferon response; (3) VZV CNS vasculitis - POLR3 gene mutations in identical twins predispose to severe VZV infection in CNS/lungs; (4) osteosarcoma - POLR3F identified as serum marker protein candidate. Functional hypothesis: POLR3F functions as (a) essential Pol III subunit facilitating transcription initiation of tRNA, 5S rRNA, U6 snRNA, and other small non-coding RNAs through winged helix domain-mediated DNA promoter recognition, and (b) cytosolic DNA sensor detecting viral DNA (VZV, other herpesviruses) to trigger RIG-I-like receptor signaling and type I interferon production for antiviral defense. Mechanistic proposal: POLR3F integrates into Pol III holoenzyme via interactions with POLR3A/POLR3B catalytic core and POLR3C/POLR3D assembly factors, positioning dual winged helix domains to recognize type 2/3 gene promoters (tRNA, 5S rRNA genes) and recruit transcription factors (TFIIIB, TFIIIC) for pre-initiation complex assembly—during viral infection, cytosolic POLR3F (or Pol III complex) binds AT-rich viral DNA sequences, triggering conformational change that activates MAVS/STING signaling cascades inducing IFN-β and antiviral ISGs—pathogenic variants disrupt either transcriptional function (causing POLR3-related leukodystrophy via impaired tRNA synthesis affecting myelination) or antiviral sensing (predisposing to severe VZV CNS infection) depending on mutation location in winged helix domains versus protein interaction interfaces—this dual functionality positions POLR3F at intersection of basal transcription machinery and innate immune surveillance, with therapeutic implications for leukodystrophy treatment (gene therapy, tRNA supplementation) and antiviral enhancement (POLR3 agonists for herpesvirus infections).
