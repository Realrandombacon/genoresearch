## Description
SLC25A25 (Q6KCM7, 469 aa), also known as SCaMC-2 (Small Calcium-Binding Mitochondrial Carrier 2) or MCSC, is a member of the solute carrier family 25 localized to the mitochondrial inner membrane. InterPro identifies a unique bipartite architecture: an N-terminal regulatory domain containing two EF-hand calcium-binding motifs (IPR002048, G3DSA:1.10.238.10, aa 26-168; PF13499, aa 80-138) and a C-terminal transporter domain belonging to the mitochondrial carrier family (IPR002067, IPR023395, PF00153, G3DSA:1.50.40.10, aa 180-465). The transporter domain exhibits the canonical triple-repeat structure of mitochondrial carriers with three homologous domains (aa 184-272, 278-366, 375-464). AlphaFold predicts a confident structure (pLDDT 73.9, residues 1-469) showing the EF-hand pair connected by a linker to the 6-transmembrane helix carrier bundle. HPA shows liver-enriched expression (108.5 nTPM) with ubiquitous tissue distribution; localized to mitochondria and vesicles; classified as transporter involved in antiport. STRING reveals 10 extremely high-confidence interactions exclusively with Complex I (NADH:ubiquinone oxidoreductase) subunits: MT-ND1 (0.879), MT-ND3 (0.879), MT-ND4 (0.876), MT-ND2 (0.862), MT-ND5 (0.861), NDUFA8 (0.853), NDUFB7 (0.849), NDUFB10 (0.848), NDUFV1 (0.842), and NDUFA13 (0.835). ClinVar contains 40 pathogenic variants including copy number losses, gains, and deletions spanning 9q regions, though no specific monogenic diseases are yet assigned. Functional hypothesis: SLC25A25 functions as a calcium-regulated mitochondrial adenine nucleotide transporter (likely an ATP-Mg/Pi carrier or ADP/ATP exchanger) that couples cytosolic calcium signals to mitochondrial energy metabolism. The N-terminal EF-hands (Ca2+ Kd ~1-10 µM) act as a switch: upon Ca2+ binding, they undergo conformational change to activate the C-terminal carrier, stimulating ATP/ADP exchange to match oxidative phosphorylation demand. The exclusive, ultra-strong interaction network with Complex I subunits (scores >0.83) suggests SCaMC-2 physically associates with or functionally regulates Complex I assembly/activity, potentially providing local ATP for assembly or modulating proton motive force. Liver enrichment (108.5 nTPM) aligns with high metabolic demand for gluconeogenesis and urea cycle. Pathogenic CNVs may disrupt mitochondrial calcium homeostasis and ATP supply, leading to bioenergetic failure and potential neurodegeneration or myopathy. Conservation across vertebrates (mouse, rat 469 aa; zebrafish 469 aa) confirms essential role. NOTE: This gene is well-characterized as a calcium-binding mitochondrial carrier with established links to respiratory chain function.
