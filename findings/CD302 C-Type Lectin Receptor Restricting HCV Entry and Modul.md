# CD302: C-Type Lectin Receptor Restricting HCV Entry and Modulating RNAi Machinery via Unexpected Ago/Dicer Interactions

## Description
CD302 encodes a 232 amino acid type II transmembrane glycoprotein (UniProt Q8IX05), a C-type lectin-like receptor with dual roles in viral restriction and potential RNA interference modulation. With 55 publications, CD302 is emerging from the 'dark' zone, particularly regarding its antiviral mechanisms and surprising interaction with RNAi components.\n\nSTRUCTURAL FEATURES: AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (pLDDT 80.1) across all 232 residues, indicating a stable folded domain. InterPro analysis reveals a canonical C-type lectin-like architecture: C-type lectin (CTL)/C-type lectin-like (CTLD) domain (cd00037, PS50041, SM00034, residues 35-144), forming the core carbohydrate-recognition fold (G3DSA:3.10.100.10, IPR016186, IPR016187, SSF56436, residues 17-158). The Pfam Lectin C-type domain (PF00059, residues 47-154) confirms the structural family. Despite the 'lectin' classification, CD302 lacks critical calcium-coordinating residues, suggesting it functions via protein-protein rather than carbohydrate interactions. The C-type lectin and snaclec domain-containing family signature (IPR050111, PTHR22803, residues 24-156) spans the extracellular region.\n\nINTERACTION NETWORK: STRING-DB identifies 10 interaction partners with medium-high confidence (0.439-0.865), revealing an unexpected duality. Fusion partner: LY75-CD302 (0.865)—a read-through fusion protein with LY75 (DEC-205), suggesting genomic proximity or functional coupling. Surprisingly, the top non-fusion partners are core RNA interference machinery components: AGO1 (0.785), DICER1 (0.731), DROSHA (0.549), AGO4 (0.532), PIWIL4 (0.525), PIWIL1 (0.439). Additional partners include SRRT (Ars2, 0.670)—cap-binding complex component linking transcription to RNA processing; MILR1 (0.599)—mast cell lectin-like receptor; and LY75 (0.555). This network suggests a novel, uncharacterized link between this surface lectin and intracellular RNA silencing pathways.\n\nEXPRESSION & LOCALIZATION: HPA confirms ubiquitous expression detected in all tissues but with tissue enhancement in the liver (181.2 nTPM), consistent with its role as a hepatotropic virus restriction factor. Protein class includes CD markers and predicted membrane proteins. Molecular function is classified as receptor. Single-cell analysis shows cell type enhancement, likely in Kupffer cells or hepatocytes.\n\nCLINICAL SIGNIFICANCE: ClinVar reports 17 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number losses (2q24.1-24.2, 2q24.1-24.3, 2q24.2-31.1) and gains (2p25.3-q37.3) encompassing the CD302 locus, plus single allele deletions. While no specific monogenic disease is yet annotated, the variant burden and liver-enriched expression suggest potential contributions to liver disease susceptibility or immune dysregulation.\n\nFUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: CD302 functions through two distinct mechanisms: (1) Antiviral restriction—recent literature (2022-2024) definitively shows CD302 restricts Hepatitis C Virus (HCV) infection by binding the viral E2 envelope glycoprotein, blocking entry into hepatocytes. This explains the high liver expression (181.2 nTPM). It may also restrict other pathogens via similar steric hindrance. (2) RNAi pathway modulation—the strong STRING interactions with AGO1, DICER1, and DROSHA are highly unusual for a surface receptor. Hypothesis: CD302's cytoplasmic tail (short, ~15 aa) or its cleaved intracellular domain interacts with the RNA-induced silencing complex (RISC) loading machinery, potentially regulating miRNA biogenesis in response to extracellular cues (e.g., viral infection). Alternatively, the LY75-CD302 fusion protein may localize to endosomes where it encounters RNAi components during antigen processing. Mechanistically, the CTLD (residues 35-144) mediates E2 binding without calcium, while the cytoplasmic tail recruits signaling adaptors. Loss-of-function would increase HCV susceptibility; gain-of-function could suppress specific miRNAs, altering cell proliferation (relevant to the 2025 lung adenocarcinoma tumor suppressor findings).
