# BLTP2: Bridge-Like Lipid Transfer Protein Orchestrating ER-PM Contact Sites, Phosphatidylethanolamine Homeostasis, Plasma Membrane Fluidity, and Exosome Biogenesis via SCAMP3-Dependent Tethering in Breast Cancer Aggressiveness and Tumorigenesis

## Description
BLTP2 (Bridge-like Lipid Transfer Protein Family Member 2, also known as KIAA0100 or Hobbit homolog, Q14667) encodes a massive 2235 aa lipid transport protein functioning as a molecular bridge spanning endoplasmic reticulum-plasma membrane (ER-PM) contact sites to facilitate non-vesicular lipid exchange and maintain membrane homeostasis. Structural architecture reveals a conserved GFWDK motif-containing RBG unit (IPR019441/SM01214, residues 1028-1160) likely forming the core lipid transfer domain, embedded within a large Hobbit/FMP27/BTLP2 family scaffold (IPR045167/PF10344/PTHR15678, residues 4-2224/7-2207) that creates an extended rod-like structure capable of spanning ~20-30 nm inter-organelle distances; AlphaFold prediction (pLDDT 70.8) confirms confident folding consistent with a stable structural tether, though flexible linkers may enable dynamic conformational changes required for lipid extraction/delivery. While STRING interactions were not retrieved (likely due to recent characterization), literature defines critical interaction networks: SCAMP3 (Secretory Carrier Membrane Protein 3) mediates tethering to PM-connected tubular recycling endosomes, Hoi1 (yeast ortholog regulator) targets BLTP2 to contact sites, and Vps13-like machinery coordinates phosphatidylethanolamine (PE) transfer. HPA localizes BLTP2 to nuclear speckles and cytosol as an intracellular protein with ubiquitous low-specificity expression detected in all tissues, reflecting fundamental housekeeping role in membrane biology. ClinVar documents 11 pathogenic variants including missense mutations p.His137Gln/p.Arg410Gly within the N-terminal region potentially disrupting membrane association or protein stability, silent variants, and extensive 17p11.2-q21.2/17q11.1-11.2 copy number gains (chr17:21690653-38772647, chr17:25274363-31685464 triplications) altering gene dosage. Literature (16 papers, highly recent 2025) demonstrates emerging oncogenic mechanisms: BLTP2 regulates phosphatidylethanolamine levels to maintain plasma membrane fluidity driving breast cancer aggressiveness (15x citations, 2025), multiple interactions recruit BLTP2 to ER-PM contacts controlling plasma membrane dynamics (3x citations, 2025), yeast ortholog studies confirm Hoi1-mediated targeting for lipid homeostasis (2x citations, 2025), BLTP2 orchestrates lysobisphosphatidic acid (LBPA) synthesis and exosome biogenesis via SCAMP3-dependent ER contacts promoting tumorigenesis (1x citation, 2025). Mechanistic hypothesis: BLTP2 functions as a physical tether and lipid conduit at ER-PM junctions; the extended Hobbit domain spans the cytosolic gap between organelles while the central RBG/GFWDK motif extracts phosphatidylethanolamine from the ER leaflet and transfers it to the plasma membrane to regulate fluidity and curvature; SCAMP3 interaction anchors the C-terminus to recycling endosomes/PM enabling coordinated lipid flux during membrane repair and expansion; in cancer, BLTP2 overexpression increases PE content enhancing membrane fluidity required for rapid cell migration, invasion, and exosome release; LBPA synthesis regulation links BLTP2 to late endosomal sorting and extracellular vesicle production facilitating tumor-stroma communication; 17q CNVs amplify BLTP2 dosage hyperactivating lipid transfer pathways supporting metastatic phenotype; conservation from yeast (FMP27/Hobbit) to plants (SABRE) underscores ancient role in eukaryotic membrane contact site architecture.
