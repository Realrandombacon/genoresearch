## Description
SPSB4 (SplA/ryanodine receptor domain and SOCS box containing 4, also known as SSB-4 or SSB4) is a 273 amino acid intracellular protein localized to the nucleoplasm and Golgi apparatus (UniProt Q96A44) with high-confidence well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 89.1). The protein features a canonical bipartite E3 ubiquitin ligase adaptor architecture comprising an N-terminal B30.2/SPRY domain (IPR001870/IPR003877/PF00622, residues 34-233/95-230) forming a ~174-199 aa module (~65-73%) that mediates substrate recognition via protein-protein interactions (GO:0005515), and a C-terminal SOCS box domain (IPR001496/PF07525, residues 233-273/236-272) forming a ~37-40 aa module (~14%) that recruits the elongin BC-cullin 5 ubiquitin ligase complex. The SPRY domain adopts a concanavalin A-like lectin/glucanase fold (IPR013320/G3DSA:2.60.120.920), while the SOCS box belongs to the SOCS box-like superfamily (IPR036036/G3DSA:1.10.750.20). Molecular functions include ubiquitin-like ligase-substrate adaptor activity, protein polyubiquitination, and ubiquitin-dependent protein catabolism, placing SPSB4 within the F-box/SPRY and SOCS box protein family (IPR050672).\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places SPSB4 at the core of an E3 ubiquitin ligase machinery: strongest interaction with ELOC (0.970, Elongin-C, a component of the VHL E3 ligase complex), confirming its role in recruiting the elongin BC complex. High confidence links include CUL5 (0.865, Cullin-5, the scaffold protein of the ECS E3 ligase), CISH (0.806, Cytokine-inducible SH2-containing protein, another SOCS family member), and RNF7 (0.766, Ring finger protein 7, an E2 ubiquitin-conjugating enzyme). The interaction with PAWR (0.741, PRKC apoptosis WT1 regulator, also known as Par-4) is particularly significant as PAWR is a pro-apoptotic tumor suppressor; SPSB4 may target PAWR for degradation, regulating apoptosis thresholds. ELOB (0.634, Elongin-B) completes the elongin BC heterodimer. Connections to NOS2 (0.584, Nitric oxide synthase 2) suggest SPSB4 may regulate inflammatory responses by controlling NOS2 stability. UBE2F (0.538) and UBA3 (0.510) are ubiquitin-conjugating enzymes, while MYCBP2 (0.530) is another E3 ligase involved in axon regeneration.\n\nHPA expression reveals a tissue-enhanced pattern with highest levels in pancreas (11.9 nTPM) and testis (6.9 nTPM), detected in some tissues. The 'cancer enhanced' status suggests upregulation in tumors, consistent with its role in degrading tumor suppressors like PAWR. Subcellular location in the nucleoplasm and Golgi apparatus indicates it may regulate both nuclear signaling (transcription factor turnover) and secretory pathway quality control.\n\nClinVar documents 22 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains and losses affecting the 3q22.1-3q29 region: CNV gains (3q22.1-29, 3q22.3-26.1, 3q13.31-26.31 duplications) and CNV losses (3q22.1-25.1, 3q23, 3q22.3-26.1 deletions). The moderate-to-high variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity. Haploinsufficiency would likely impair substrate degradation, causing accumulation of pro-apoptotic factors (PAWR) or inflammatory mediators (NOS2), potentially leading to excessive cell death or inflammation. Duplications may cause hyper-degradation of tumor suppressors, contributing to oncogenesis or 3q deletion/duplication syndrome phenotypes (developmental delay, growth abnormalities, cancer predisposition).\n\nConservation is strong across mammals: human (Q96A44, 273 aa), mouse (Q8R5B6, 273 aa), indicating evolutionary pressure to maintain the SPRY-SOCS architecture.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: SPSB4 functions as a substrate-specific adaptor for the ECS (Elongin-Cullin-SOCS) E3 ubiquitin ligase complex. The SPRY domain recognizes specific substrates (like PAWR or NOS2) through protein-protein interactions, while the SOCS box recruits the ELOC-ELOB heterodimer and CUL5 scaffold to assemble the active E3 ligase. This complex then recruits E2 ubiquitin-conjugating enzymes (RNF7, UBE2F) to polyubiquitinate the substrate, targeting it for proteasomal degradation. In the nucleoplasm, SPSB4 may regulate transcription factors or apoptotic regulators; in the Golgi, it may control quality control of secretory proteins. The cancer-enhanced expression suggests SPSB4 may be oncogenic by degrading tumor suppressors. Dysregulation via 3q CNVs disrupts proteostasis: loss causes substrate accumulation (apoptosis, inflammation), while gain causes excessive degradation (cancer progression, developmental defects).
