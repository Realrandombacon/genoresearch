## Description
TMEM160 (Gene ID: 84277, UniProt Q9NX00) is a 188 amino acid transmembrane protein located on chromosome 19q13.32 (NC_000019.10). This gene encodes a predicted membrane protein with transporter molecular function, though specific transport substrate remains uncharacterized. InterPro domain analysis reveals TMEM160-specific architecture: Transmembrane protein 160 family (IPR026801, positions 1-187, ~187 residues spanning nearly entire protein), Transmembrane protein 160 Pfam domain (PF27853, positions 1-172, ~172 residues), and TRANSMEMBRANE PROTEIN 160 Panther family (PTHR16236, positions 1-187, ~187 residues)—indicating this protein defines its own structural family without homology to characterized transporter superfamilies. RNA expression shows tissue-enhanced pattern with detection in many tissues, low cancer specificity, low immune cell specificity, and cell type enhanced single cell specificity. The 99.9 nTPM brain expression strongly suggests roles in neurotransmission, neuronal metabolism, synaptic function, or neural development. Conservation evidenced by orthologs: Mus musculus Tmem160 (Q9D938, 188 aa, 100% length), Bos taurus TMEM160 (Q24JY6, 188 aa, 100% length)—indicating strong functional constraint across mammals. Functional hypothesis: TMEM160 functions as a brain-enriched transmembrane transporter or channel coordinating mitochondrial-cytosolic metabolic coupling through interactions with MTIF3 (mitochondrial translation) and SEC16B (ER-Golgi trafficking). The exceptional brain expression (99.9 nTPM) suggests specialized neural metabolic demands—neurons require intense ATP production and precise calcium homeostasis. MTIF3 interaction (0.650) places TMEM160 near mitochondrial protein synthesis machinery, potentially regulating mitochondrial membrane potential or metabolite exchange required for oxidative phosphorylation. SEC16B interaction (0.599) connects to ER exit sites and COPII vesicle formation, suggesting TMEM160 may traffic between ER and Golgi or localize to ER-mitochondria contact sites (MAMs—mitochondria-associated membranes). DNAJC27 interaction (0.621) indicates ER chaperone association, supporting ER localization or quality control. NUDT3 (0.625) and GNPDA2 (0.604) metabolic enzyme interactions suggest TMEM160 may transport metabolic intermediates (nucleotide diphosphates, glucosamine-6-phosphate) between compartments. ZNF608 interaction (0.691, highest score) is surprising for a membrane protein—may indicate TMEM160 regulates nuclear signaling through cleavage/release of intracellular domain (regulated intramembrane proteolysis paradigm like Notch/SREBP) or modulates transcription factor activity indirectly through metabolic signaling. POC5 interaction (0.601) suggests potential centrosomal/ciliary localization—cilia are critical for neural development (Sonic hedgehog signaling). TNNI3K interaction (0.591) links to kinase signaling pathways relevant to cardiac and neural function. QPCTL interaction (0.545) suggests protein modification roles (pyroglutamate formation). Mechanistically, TMEM160 may function as: (1) Mitochondrial metabolite transporter exchanging ATP/ADP, calcium, or metabolic intermediates at MAMs; (2) ER-Golgi trafficking regulator controlling secretory pathway flux in neurons; (3) Calcium channel/regulator at ER-plasma membrane junctions critical for neuronal excitability; (4) Metabolic sensor coupling hexosamine pathway (GNPDA2) to mitochondrial function (MTIF3); (5) Signaling scaffold organizing multi-protein complexes at membrane contact sites; (6) Substrate for regulated intramembrane proteolysis releasing intracellular domain for nuclear signaling (explaining ZNF608 interaction). Disease mechanism: TMEM160 haploinsufficiency or overexpression from chromosome 19q13 CNVs may contribute to 19q13 deletion/duplication syndrome phenotypes including intellectual disability, developmental delay, seizures, autism spectrum features, and movement disorders. The 11 ClinVar variants suggest dosage sensitivity; 19q13 rearrangements cause neurodevelopmental phenotypes. TMEM160 may contribute through disrupted mitochondrial metabolism (causing neuronal energy deficit), impaired ER-Golgi trafficking (affecting synaptic protein delivery), aberrant calcium homeostasis (causing neuronal hyperexcitability/seizures), or defective ciliary function (disrupting neural patterning). Priority experiments include subcellular localization by immunofluorescence (mitochondria, ER, Golgi, plasma membrane markers), colocalization with MTIF3 (mitochondria), SEC16B (ER exit sites), DNAJC27 (ER); mitochondrial function assays (oxygen consumption rate, ATP production, membrane potential) in TMEM160 knockout neurons; ER-Golgi trafficking assays (cargo secretion, Golgi morphology); calcium imaging in TMEM160-deficient neurons; metabolomics to identify transported substrates; co-IP validation of MTIF3, SEC16B, ZNF608, DNAJC27 interactions; structural analysis leveraging AlphaFold model (pLDDT 75.9) to predict transmembrane topology and substrate-binding pockets; investigation of TMEM160 role in iPSC-derived neurons and brain organoids; analysis of TMEM160 expression in 19q syndrome patient cells and neurodevelopmental disorder brains.
