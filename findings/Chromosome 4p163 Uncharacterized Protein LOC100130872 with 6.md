## Description
LOC100130872 (Gene ID: 100130872) is an uncharacterized protein-coding gene on chromosome 4p16.3 (NC_000004.12, positions 1208961-1195782, antisense orientation, ~13.2 kb genomic region) with exceptional clinical significance evidenced by 69 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variants despite complete absence of molecular characterization. No UniProt accession exists, preventing InterPro domain analysis and AlphaFold structure prediction, consistent with truly dark protein-coding gene classification. STRING-DB detected no protein-protein interactions, consistent with uncharacterized protein status. Human Protein Atlas could not resolve expression data, consistent with highly tissue-restricted or developmentally-specific expression pattern. However, ClinVar contains 69 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly copy number losses/deletions in the 4p16.3 region (variants 4796162: 4p16.3-15.33 ~12.7 Mb monosomy; 4755361: 4p16.3 ~3.9 Mb monosomy; 161057: 4p16.3 ~2.0 Mb monosomy; 155646: 4p16.3-15.31 ~20.4 Mb monosomy; 155619: 4p16.3-15.33 ~14.5 Mb monosomy; 155480: 4p16.3-16.1 ~6.0 Mb monosomy; 155310: 4p16.3-16.1 ~8.7 Mb monosomy) and multiple copy number gains/duplications (variants 4796121: 4p16.3 ~3.9 Mb trisomy; 2579174: 4p16.3-q12 ~57.0 Mb trisomy; 2579172: 4p16.3-11 ~49.1 Mb trisomy). All variants have uncertain significance (?) but are classified as pathogenic/likely pathogenic. The 4p16.3 chromosomal region is the critical region for Wolf-Hirschhorn syndrome (WHS), a well-characterized chromosomal deletion syndrome, containing critical neurodevelopmental, craniofacial, and growth-associated genes including WHSC1 (Wolf-Hirschhorn syndrome candidate 1/NSD2, histone H3 lysine 36 dimethyltransferase, chromatin modification, craniofacial development, growth regulation), LETM1 (leucine zipper-EF-hand containing transmembrane protein 1, mitochondrial calcium/proton exchanger, seizure susceptibility, mitochondrial metabolism), WHSC2 (Wolf-Hirschhorn syndrome candidate 2, nucleolar protein, ribosome biogenesis), FGFRL1 (fibroblast growth factor receptor-like 1, FGF signaling decoy receptor, skeletal development, craniosynostosis), and MSX1 (mucus segment homeobox 1, homeodomain transcription factor, tooth/craniofacial development, cleft lip/palate). Functional hypothesis: LOC100130872 likely represents a critical 4p16.3 neurodevelopmental/craniofacial regulatory protein, potentially functioning as a nuclear chromatin modifier or transcriptional co-regulator coordinating WHSC1-LETM1-WHSC2-FGFRL1-MSX1 expression in neural crest migration, craniofacial morphogenesis, and growth regulation (neural crest cells, craniofacial mesenchyme, developing forebrain, limb bud mesenchyme) or as a mitochondrial outer membrane protein bridging LETM1-mediated calcium homeostasis with WHSC1-mediated chromatin modification. The 69 pathogenic CNVs (majority deletions 2.0-20.4 Mb, multiple duplications 3.9-57.0 Mb) indicate extreme regional dosage sensitivity where haploinsufficiency causes Wolf-Hirschhorn syndrome. Disease mechanism involves WHSC1 haploinsufficiency causing craniofacial dysmorphism (Greek helmet facies, microcephaly, hypertelorism, cleft lip/palate, growth retardation, intellectual disability), LETM1 haploinsufficiency causing seizures (mitochondrial calcium dysregulation, neuronal hyperexcitability, epilepsy, developmental regression), WHSC2 loss causing ribosome biogenesis defect (nucleolar stress, protein synthesis impairment, growth failure), FGFRL1 loss causing skeletal abnormalities (FGF signaling dysregulation, craniosynostosis, limb defects, short stature), MSX1 mutation causing tooth agenesis and cleft lip/palate (craniofacial transcription defect, neural crest differentiation failure), and Wolf-Hirschhorn syndrome presenting with characteristic facial dysmorphism, growth retardation, intellectual disability, seizures, and congenital anomalies. The 4p16.3 region is the most dosage-sensitive neurodevelopmental-craniofacial locus in the human genome. The uncharacterized protein-coding classification combined with ClinVar evidence (69 CNVs 2.0-57.0 Mb) and WHSC1-LETM1-WHSC2-FGFRL1-MSX1 neighborhood suggests this locus functions as a nuclear/mitochondrial protein critical for chromatin modification-craniofacial development-mitochondrial calcium coordination.
