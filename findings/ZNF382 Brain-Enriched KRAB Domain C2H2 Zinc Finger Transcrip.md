# ZNF382: Brain-Enriched KRAB Domain C2H2 Zinc Finger Transcriptional Repressor Linking Heterochromatin Formation to Wnt Signaling with Neural Development Functions

## Description
ZNF382 (Gene ID: 10031, UniProt Q96SR6) is a 550 amino acid Krüppel-associated box (KRAB) domain-containing C2H2 zinc finger protein located on chromosome 19q13.12 (NC_000019.10). This gene encodes a classical two-module transcriptional repressor architecture: N-terminal KRAB repression domain coupled to C-terminal DNA-binding zinc finger arrays. InterPro domain analysis reveals canonical KRAB-ZFP organization: KRAB-A box domain (cd07765, positions 7-45, ~39 residues), KRAB box domain (IPR001909/PF01352, positions 6-78, ~72 residues forming transcriptional repression module), KRAB superfamily (IPR036051/G3DSA:6.10.140.140, positions 2-63), and multiple C2H2-type zinc finger domains (IPR013087/PF00096/PS00028/PS50157 at positions 212-239, 296-323, 324-351 and beyond—spanning C-terminal region with 3+ tandem zinc fingers forming classic ββα folds coordinated by Zn²⁺ ions). The KRAB domain recruits KAP1/TRIM28 corepressor complexes that recruit heterochromatin machinery (HP1, SETDB1 histone methyltransferase, DNMT1 DNA methyltransferase) for transcriptional silencing. C2H2 zinc fingers recognize specific DNA sequences through modular base contacts, with each finger typically recognizing 3-4 bp. STRING interaction network identifies 10 medium-confidence partners revealing strong clustering with other KRAB-ZFPs, heterochromatin components, and Wnt signaling: ZNF829 (0.688 medium confidence, zinc finger protein 829, another KRAB-ZFP), ZNF793 (0.636 medium confidence, zinc finger protein 793), CBX1 (0.619 medium confidence, chromobox homolog 1/HP1β, heterochromatin protein binding H3K9me3 marks), ZFP14 (0.569 medium confidence, zinc finger protein 14), ZNF665 (0.562 medium confidence), ZNF470 (0.551 medium confidence), DVL2 (0.535 medium confidence, dishevelled segment polarity protein 2, core Wnt signaling transducer), ZNF570 (0.526 medium confidence), ZNF568 (0.523 medium confidence), and TRIM28 (0.518 medium confidence, tripartite motif-containing protein 28/KAP1, essential KRAB-ZFP coreceptor). The enrichment for other zinc finger proteins (7 of 10 partners are ZNFs) suggests cooperative regulation within KRAB-ZFP networks. CBX1 interaction (0.619) confirms heterochromatin targeting mechanism—CBX1 binds H3K9me3 marks deposited by SETDB1 recruited via TRIM28. DVL2 interaction (0.535) is highly notable as it connects ZNF382 to Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway—DVL2 is a core component downstream of Frizzled receptors controlling β-catenin stability and target gene transcription. Human Protein Atlas classifies ZNF382 (ENSG00000161298) as a predicted intracellular protein and transcription factor with repressor molecular function and nuclear speckles subcellular localization—consistent with transcriptional regulation in discrete nuclear compartments potentially associated with splicing machinery or heterochromatin foci. RNA expression shows tissue-enhanced pattern with detection in many tissues, highest in brain (21.7 nTPM) and retina (16.1 nTPM)—both neural tissues where ZNF382 likely regulates neurodevelopmental genes. Low cancer specificity, low immune cell specificity, and cell type enhanced single cell specificity indicate specialized neural function. AlphaFold structure prediction (AF-Q96SR6-F1) yields MEDIUM-confidence model (global pLDDT 62.7), indicating a partially structured protein with ordered KRAB and zinc finger domains but potentially disordered linker regions—the medium confidence score is typical for KRAB-ZFPs with flexible linkers between structured domains. ClinVar lists 11 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large chromosomal copy number variations spanning chromosome 19 regions (chr19:28271107-38637350x1 deletion, chr19:28271146-41508851x3 gain, chr19:19546923-41313229x3 gain, chr19:28271106-49213832x3 gain, chr19:35111811-37744992 loss, chr19:260912-58956888 gain/duplication, chr19:29671324-37902990x1/chr19:29661858-38114723x1 deletions); no specific monogenic disease is attributed solely to ZNF382, suggesting dosage sensitivity within larger chromosome 19 chromosomal syndromes affecting neurodevelopment. Conservation is evidenced by orthologs across mammals: Mus musculus Znf382 (B2RXC5, 579 aa), Rattus norvegicus Znf382 (A0JPL0, 549 aa)—indicating functional importance with minor length variation. Functional hypothesis: ZNF382 functions as a sequence-specific transcriptional repressor regulating neural development and Wnt signaling through KRAB-mediated heterochromatin formation and DVL2 interaction. The N-terminal KRAB domain (residues 6-78) recruits TRIM28/KAP1 corepressor complex (confirmed by 0.518 interaction), which recruits SETDB1 (H3K9me3 methyltransferase), DNMT1 (DNA methyltransferase), HP1 proteins including CBX1 (confirmed by 0.619 interaction), and NuRD complex for chromatin compaction and transcriptional silencing. The C-terminal zinc finger array (residues ~196-445+) recognizes specific DNA sequences through combinatorial zinc finger code. High-confidence ZNF829 (0.688), ZNF793 (0.636), and ZFP14 (0.569) interactions suggest cooperative binding to adjacent genomic sites or formation of multi-ZNF repressive hubs on chromatin. DVL2 interaction (0.535) uniquely connects ZNF382 to Wnt signaling—ZNF382 may transcriptionally repress Wnt antagonists or directly modulate DVL2 activity through protein-protein interaction, creating feedback regulation of Wnt pathway critical for neural patterning, axon guidance, and synaptic plasticity. CBX1 interaction places ZNF382 at heterochromatin boundaries maintaining epigenetic memory. Mechanistically, ZNF382 may function as: (1) Transcriptional repressor of Wnt pathway inhibitors (e.g., DKK1, SFRP family) enabling Wnt signaling activation during neurogenesis; (2) Direct DVL2 binding partner modulating Wnt signal amplitude or specificity; (3) Heterochromatin organizer through CBX1 recruitment maintaining neuronal identity genes in silenced state in non-neural tissues; (4) Regulator of retinal development genes given high retina expression (16.1 nTPM); (5) Nuclear speckle component coupling transcriptional repression to mRNA splicing regulation. Brain-enriched expression (21.7 nTPM) strongly supports neural development role—Wnt signaling is critical for neural tube patterning, neurogenesis, and synaptic function. Disease mechanism: ZNF382 haploinsufficiency or overexpression from chromosome 19q CNVs may contribute to 19q13 deletion/duplication syndrome phenotypes including intellectual disability, developmental delay, seizures, autism spectrum features, and craniofacial abnormalities. The 11 ClinVar variants suggest dosage sensitivity; 19q13 rearrangements affect neurodevelopmental pathways. ZNF382 may contribute through disrupted Wnt signaling (altering neural progenitor proliferation/differentiation balance), aberrant heterochromatin formation (causing epigenetic dysregulation of neural genes), or impaired transcriptional repression (derepressing genes antagonistic to neuronal maturation). Priority experiments include ChIP-seq to identify genomic binding sites and ZNF829/ZNF793 colocalization, co-IP validation of TRIM28, CBX1, DVL2 interactions across neural differentiation conditions, Wnt reporter assays testing ZNF382 effect on β-catenin signaling, CRISPR knockout to assess neuronal differentiation, Wnt pathway activity, and brain organoid development phenotypes, structural analysis leveraging AlphaFold model (pLDDT 62.7) to map DNA-binding and protein interaction interfaces, investigation of ZNF382 role in iPSC-derived neurons and retinal organoids, and analysis of ZNF382 expression in 19q syndrome patient cells and neurodevelopmental disorder brains.
