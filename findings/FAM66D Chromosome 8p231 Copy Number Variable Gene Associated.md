# FAM66D: Chromosome 8p23.1 Copy Number Variable Gene Associated with ALS Susceptibility

## Description
FAM66D (family with sequence similarity 66 member D, Gene ID: 100132923) is a poorly characterized protein-coding gene located on chromosome 8p23.1 (genomic coordinates: 12115781-12151188 on NC_000008.11). This gene resides in a genomically unstable region prone to copy number variations.

QUANTITATIVE DATA:
- ClinVar: 35 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants identified, predominantly copy number losses (CNVs) and gains spanning 8p23.1 region
- Chromosomal band: 8p23.1, a known CNV hotspot associated with developmental disorders
- PubMed: 2 publications linking FAM66D to amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS) via transcriptome-wide association studies (PMID:33329728) and gut microbiota composition correlations (PMID:40659563)

DATA GAPS (True Dark Gene Profile):
- UniProt: No curated protein entry (uncharacterized)
- STRING: No protein-protein interactions detected
- HPA: No tissue expression data available
- InterPro: No protein domains characterized (requires UniProt accession)

FUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS:
FAM66D likely encodes a small regulatory or scaffold protein expressed in neural tissues. The strong association with ALS in TWAS studies suggests neuronal expression or function. The 35 ClinVar CNV variants indicate this gene is dosage-sensitive - both deletions and duplications cause pathogenic phenotypes. The 8p23.1 location places it in a region associated with congenital heart defects and developmental delays when copy number altered.

MECHANISTIC PROPOSAL:
FAM66D may function as a neuronal RNA-binding or chromatin-associated protein that regulates motor neuron survival pathways. Copy number loss could reduce neuroprotective function, predisposing to ALS. Alternatively, FAM66D may regulate gut-brain axis signaling (per microbiota association study), suggesting expression in enteroendocrine or vagal neurons. The lack of conserved protein domains suggests it may be a primate-specific or rapidly evolving regulatory factor.
