## Description
NAPSA encodes napsin A (NAPA/KAP), a 420 aa aspartic peptidase family A1 member (UniProt O96009) belonging to the pepsin-like protease superfamily that functions in surfactant protein B/C maturation within type II pneumocytes. InterPro reveals canonical aspartic protease architecture: peptidase family A1 domain (IPR001461/PF00026/PTHR47966, residues 68-399) containing the catalytic machinery, dual aspartic acid active sites (IPR001969/PS00141, residues 93-104 and 280-291) forming the catalytic dyad essential for peptide bond hydrolysis, acid protease superfamily (G3DSA:2.40.70.10/SSF50630/IPR021109, residues 9-401) adopting the characteristic bilobal fold with interdomain cleft, and eukaryotic aspartyl protease profile (PS51767, residues 78-399). AlphaFold predicts high confidence structure (pLDDT 82.4) consistent with well-folded bilobal aspartic protease typical of pepsin family enzymes with structured catalytic cleft between N- and C-terminal domains. STRING reveals functional clustering with lung epithelial markers and transcription factors: KRT7 (0.881, cytokeratin-7 lung adenocarcinoma marker), NKX2-1/TTF1 (0.879, thyroid transcription factor-1 master regulator of lung development), C17orf97 (0.814, uncharacterized lung protein), SFTPB (0.797) and SFTPC (0.736, surfactant proteins B/C requiring proteolytic maturation), PAX8 (0.720, paired box-8 thyroid/kidney transcription factor), CDX2 (0.720, caudal-type homeobox intestinal marker), HNF1B (0.710, hepatocyte nuclear factor-1β renal development), KRT20 (0.464) and KRT5 (0.423, cytokeratins marking epithelial differentiation). HPA shows tissue-enriched expression with extreme lung specificity (983.7 nTPM, highest in dataset), cancer-enriched RNA pattern, classified as enzyme/plasma protein/predicted secreted protein with aspartyl protease/hydrolase/protease molecular function. ClinVar lists 16 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants consisting entirely of copy number gains spanning 19q13.31-13.43/19p13.3-q13.43 and splice site mutations (c.84-8C>A, c.1035+5G>A) though specific monogenic disease phenotypes remain undefined suggesting dosage sensitivity may contribute to pulmonary dysfunction syndromes. Functional mechanism: NAPSA operates through classic aspartic protease catalysis where the twin aspartate residues (Asp93/Asp280) activate water molecule for nucleophilic attack on peptide bonds within pro-surfactant protein substrates. The enzyme is synthesized as inactive zymogen requiring autocatalytic or trans-catalytic removal of N-terminal propeptide for activation, then trafficked to lamellar bodies where it processes pro-SP-B (38 kDa→8 kDa mature) and pro-SP-C (21 kDa→3.7 kDa mature) through specific cleavage events essential for surfactant biophysical function reducing alveolar surface tension. Recent literature reveals expanded disease relevance beyond surfactant maturation: (1) lung adenocarcinoma diagnostic biomarker with superior specificity versus TTF1 in poorly differentiated tumors (2024-2025), (2) thyroid carcinoma marker via PAX8 co-expression indicating shared developmental program, (3) clear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC) single-cell RNA sequencing revealing tumor-specific expression patterns (2025), (4) potential therapeutic target given secreted protease accessibility. Despite mechanistic understanding of surfactant processing, complete substrate repertoire beyond SP-B/SP-C, regulatory mechanisms controlling zymogen activation timing, precise contribution of 19q CNVs to pulmonary phenotypes, genotype-phenotype correlations for splice variants affecting catalytic domain folding, cell-type specific expression patterns across lung epithelial subtypes, and therapeutic potential for NAPSA inhibition in cancer remain incompletely characterized - representing genuine dark aspects of this clinically important lung-specific protease with emerging oncological utility.
