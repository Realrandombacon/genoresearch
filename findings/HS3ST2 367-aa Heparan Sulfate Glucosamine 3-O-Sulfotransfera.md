## Description
HS3ST2 (UniProt Q9Y278), or heparan sulfate glucosamine 3-O-sulfotransferase 2, encodes a 367-amino acid Golgi-resident enzyme belonging to the heparan sulfate sulfotransferase family (IPR000863/IPR037359/PF00685/PTHR10605/G3DSA:3.40.50.300, aa 14-367). The protein architecture features a canonical sulfotransferase fold with a P-loop containing nucleoside triphosphate hydrolase superfamily domain (IPR027417/SSF52540, aa 106-367) that binds the universal sulfate donor 3'-phosphoadenosine-5'-phosphosulfate (PAPS), and a specific catalytic core (IPR000863/PF00685, aa 115-350) that transfers sulfate to the 3-OH position of glucosamine residues in heparan sulfate chains. AlphaFold predicts high-confidence structure (global pLDDT: 82.8), indicating a well-folded enzymatic core consistent with precise substrate recognition required for generating rare 3-O-sulfated motifs that act as high-affinity ligands for viral glycoproteins and pathological proteins.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals HS3ST2's tight integration into heparan sulfate biosynthesis and proteoglycan networks: SULT1C3 (0.762), SULT1C4 (0.754), SULT1B1 (0.731), HS2ST1 (0.685, 2-O-sulfotransferase), SDC2 (0.604, syndecan-2), GPC1 (0.562, glypican-1), SDC1 (0.545, syndecan-1), HSPG2 (0.538, perlecan), and HS6ST1 (0.403, 6-O-sulfotransferase). This partner profile positions HS3ST2 as a key modifier of cell surface proteoglycans (syndecans/glypicans/perlecan), working in concert with 2-O and 6-O sulfotransferases to create the 'heparan sulfate code' that dictates ligand binding specificity.\n\nWith 109 papers but emerging mechanistic roles in neurodegeneration, HS3ST2 qualifies as a dark gene with breakthrough implications for Alzheimer's disease and viral entry. Recent studies reveal: (1) HS3ST2 expression induces cell-autonomous aggregation of tau protein, linking 3-O-sulfated heparan sulfate to neurofibrillary tangle formation in tauopathies; (2) a novel TGFβ1–HS3ST2–tau axis regulates synaptic integrity, suggesting HS3ST2 as a therapeutic target for preventing tau propagation; (3) neuronal extracellular vesicles deliver miR-99a to suppress HS3ST2 locally, regulating axon development in cortical neurons; (4) METTL14-mediated m6A methylation upregulates HS3ST2 to drive M1 macrophage polarization in COPD; (5) miR-100 shuttled by MSC-derived EVs inhibits HS3ST2 to suppress endometriosis. HPA data confirms tissue-enhanced expression with brain (19.8 nTPM) as top tissue, reflecting critical roles in neural development; cancer-enhanced expression suggests involvement in tumor microenvironment remodeling. ClinVar lists 29 pathogenic variants including 16p12.2-11.2 copy number losses/gains, confirming dosage sensitivity potentially linked to 16p11.2 deletion/duplication syndrome phenotypes (autism, obesity, developmental delay). We hypothesize HS3ST2 functions as a 'molecular switch' in the heparan sulfate code: by installing rare 3-O-sulfate groups on specific glucosamine residues within syndecan/glycan chains, it creates high-affinity binding sites for HSV-1 glycoprotein D (viral entry), pathological tau seeds (aggregation nucleation), and morphogens (axon guidance); the 16p CNVs disrupt the stoichiometry of sulfotransferases, altering the global sulfation pattern and leading to neurodevelopmental defects; in Alzheimer's, aberrant HS3ST2 upregulation generates excessive 3-O-sulfated motifs that accelerate tau fibrillization and spread between neurons, making it a prime target for disease-modifying therapies.
