# ZNF335: C2H2 Zinc Finger Transcriptional Coactivator Essential for Neural Development

**Date:** 2026-03-23T00:59:04.173006

**Quality Score:** 2.5/10  (E=4.0, D=1.0) [WEAK]

## Description
ZNF335 encodes a 1342 amino acid zinc finger protein (UniProt Q9H4Z2) that functions as a transcriptional coactivator for ligand-bound nuclear hormone receptors. The protein contains multiple C2H2-type zinc finger domains (IPR013087, PF00096, PF13909, PF13912) positioned at residues 245-268, 465-492, 495-522, 562-585, 590-612, and 1076-1099, indicating DNA-binding capability. ZNF335 does not directly bind nuclear receptors but interacts with the NRC coactivator complex to alter local chromatin structure and enhance transcriptional activation. HPA data shows ubiquitous expression across all tissues (low tissue specificity) with nucleoplasmic localization, consistent with its role as a transcription factor. The gene is classified as essential and disease-related, with 71 pathogenic/likely pathogenic ClinVar variants including frameshift mutations (p.Gln1192fs, p.Val107fs), nonsense variants (p.Glu1100Ter, p.Arg660Ter), and splice site mutations. These variants cause autosomal recessive primary microcephaly (MCPH10) and intellectual disability, demonstrating ZNF335 is critical for neural progenitor cell proliferation and brain development. The multiple zinc finger arrays suggest ZNF335 recognizes specific DNA sequences at neurodevelopmental gene promoters, recruiting chromatin remodeling complexes through NRC interaction to maintain transcriptional programs required for cortical neurogenesis.
