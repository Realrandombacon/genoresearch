## Description
HIBADH encodes 3-hydroxyisobutyrate dehydrogenase (HIBADH), a 336 aa mitochondrial oxidoreductase (UniProt P31937) catalyzing the NAD+-dependent oxidation of 3-hydroxyisobutyrate to methylmalonate semialdehyde in the third step of L-valine catabolism. InterPro reveals characteristic dehydrogenase architecture: 3-hydroxyisobutyrate dehydrogenase family domain (IPR011548, residues 44-331), conserved catalytic site (IPR002204, residues 44-57), NAD(P)-binding Rossmann-like domain (G3DSA:3.40.50.720, residues 35-201), and 6-phosphogluconate dehydrogenase-like C-terminal domain superfamily (IPR008927/G3DSA:1.10.1040.10, residues 202-335) mediating cofactor binding and substrate recognition. AlphaFold predicts very high confidence structure (pLDDT 90.6) consistent with stable folded conformation typical of conserved metabolic enzymes. STRING reveals tight functional clustering within valine degradation pathway: HIBCH (0.998, 3-hydroxyisobutyryl-CoA hydrolase, upstream partner), ALDH6A1 (0.996, methylmalonate semialdehyde dehydrogenase, downstream partner), plus related aldehyde dehydrogenases ALDH1B1 (0.941), ALDH9A1 (0.939), ABAT (0.938, GABA transaminase), EHHADH (0.936), ALDH3A2 (0.929), ALDH7A1 (0.927), ALDH2 (0.599), and AGXT2 (0.440). HPA shows ubiquitous expression across all tissues with mitochondrial localization, classified as enzyme/metabolic protein/plasma protein involved in branched-chain amino acid catabolism. ClinVar lists 29 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number gains at 7p15.2-14.3, deletions at 7p21.1-14.3, and splice site mutations though specific monogenic disease phenotypes remain incompletely defined. Functional mechanism: HIBADH occupies a critical metabolic node converting 3-hydroxyisobutyrate (3-HIB), a circulating metabolite derived from valine catabolism and also produced from methylmalonyl-CoA via propionyl-CoA pathway, to methylmalonate semialdehyde which is subsequently converted by ALDH6A1 to propionyl-CoA entering the TCA cycle. This reaction represents a rate-limiting step controlling flux through valine degradation and 3-HIB signaling. Elevated 3-HIB serves as a biomarker for insulin resistance and type 2 diabetes. Recent literature reveals expanded disease relevance: calcium oxalate nephrolithiasis protection via oxidative stress/apoptosis modulation in kidney tubular cells (2025/5 citations), tumor cell-macrophage metabolic interactions affecting immunotherapy response in advanced NSCLC through BCAT1-HIBADH axis (2025), lncRNA-mediated regulation of autophagy-lysosome pathway in amyotrophic lateral sclerosis via cathepsin D targeting (2023/6 citations), and identification of HIBADH deficiency as a novel inborn error of valine metabolism with developmental delay and metabolic acidosis (2021/12 citations). Despite mechanistic understanding of catalytic function, regulatory mechanisms controlling tissue-specific expression, contribution to diabetic metabolite signaling, precise pathophysiology of HIBADH deficiency syndrome, and therapeutic potential for modulating valine catabolism in cancer immunotherapy remain incompletely characterized - representing genuine dark aspects of this central metabolic enzyme.
