## Description
MIEF2 (UniProt Q96C03), also known as MID49 (Mitochondrial Dynamics Protein 49), encodes a 454 aa integral mitochondrial outer membrane protein that functions as a critical regulator of mitochondrial fission-fusion dynamics, acting as a receptor and scaffold for the fission GTPase DNM1L/Drp1. InterPro analysis reveals a specialized domain architecture: a MAB21L/Cyclic GMP-AMP synthase-like receptor domain (IPR024810, SM01265, aa 181-448) forming the core structural scaffold; a Mab-21-like HhH/H2TH-like domain (IPR046906, PF20266, aa 388-439) potentially involved in nucleic acid or protein binding; a MID51-like C-terminal domain (IPR049097, PF21297, aa 146-338) shared with its paralog MID51/MIEF1, suggesting conserved mechanistic features; and classification within the mitochondrial dynamics protein MID49/MID51 family (IPR045909, PTHR16451) with GO annotations for mitochondrion organization and mitochondrial outer membrane localization. AlphaFold predicts a confident structure (pLDDT 76.0), indicating a well-folded protein capable of stable membrane integration and protein-protein interactions. STRING interactions define MIEF2 as a central hub in the mitochondrial dynamics network: DNM1L/Drp1 (0.999), the master fission GTPase, representing its primary functional partner; FIS1 (0.915) and MFF (0.755), other outer membrane fission receptors, suggesting cooperative or competitive recruitment of Drp1; MFN1 (0.829) and MFN2 (0.815), mitofusin fusion proteins, positioning MIEF2 at the fission-fusion decision point; OPA1 (0.631), the inner membrane fusion GTPase, implying cross-membrane coordination; GFM2 (0.890) and GFM1 (0.519), mitochondrial translation elongation factors, linking dynamics to protein synthesis; MRRF (0.710), a mitochondrial ribosome recycling factor; and DENR (0.556), a translation reinitiation factor. ClinVar reports 56 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all large 17p11.2 CNVs (Potocki-Lupski/Smith-Magenis syndrome regions), though no monogenic disease is yet assigned solely to MIEF2 point mutations, suggesting dosage sensitivity. Literature (31 papers) establishes roles in: (1) Mitochondrial fission regulation—MIEF2 recruits DNM1L to fission sites, though paradoxically can also inhibit excessive fission; (2) Cancer metabolism—MIEF2 reprograms lipid metabolism via ROS/AKT/mTOR signaling in ovarian cancer, drives glycolytic reprogramming in hepatocellular carcinoma via lncRNA PRR34-AS1 regulation; (3) Alcoholic liver disease—MARCH5-mediated ubiquitination and degradation of MIEF2 protects against hepatic steatosis and mitochondrial fragmentation; (4) Chemotherapy sensitivity—MIEF2 expression correlates with cisplatin sensitivity in kidney renal papillary cell carcinoma; (5) Pregnancy-related thrombocytopenia—emerging links to megakaryocyte function. Functional hypothesis: MIEF2 functions through: (1) Drp1 Receptor/Scaffold—acts as a membrane-anchored platform recruiting cytosolic DNM1L to fission sites via direct binding, competing/cooperating with FIS1 and MFF to regulate fission frequency; (2) Fission-Fusion Rheostat—by balancing Drp1 recruitment against mitofusin activity, MIEF2 sets the threshold for mitochondrial network fragmentation versus elongation, adapting to metabolic demands; (3) Metabolic Sensor—ROS/AKT/mTOR pathway integration suggests MIEF2 responds to nutrient and stress signals to remodel mitochondrial architecture accordingly; (4) Quality Control Gatekeeper—coordination with GFM factors links fission to mitochondrial translation, potentially isolating damaged segments for mitophagy; (5) Disease Mechanism—17p11.2 CNVs disrupting MIEF2 dosage may contribute to Potocki-Lupski/Smith-Magenis syndrome phenotypes via mitochondrial dysfunction, while cancer-associated overexpression drives metabolic reprogramming and chemoresistance through enhanced fission-mediated metabolic flexibility. The high-confidence structure, near-perfect DNM1L interaction, and emerging cancer/metabolic disease links position MIEF2 as a therapeutically targetable node in mitochondrial dynamics.
