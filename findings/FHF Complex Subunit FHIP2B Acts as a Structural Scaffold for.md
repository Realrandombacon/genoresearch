## Description
FHIP2B (FHF Complex Subunit HOOK-Interacting Protein 2B) is a 743 aa intracellular protein functioning as an essential structural component of the FHF (FTS-Hook-FHIP) complex, which regulates endosomal cargo sorting, lysosomal positioning, and primary cilia formation. Despite having zero direct literature publications on Semantic Scholar, its function can be robustly inferred from domain architecture and homology to characterized paralogs. InterPro analysis reveals a unique domain organization: a large N-terminal region containing Retinoic Acid Induced 16 (RAI16)-like repeats (PF10257, aa 79-476) that likely mediate protein-protein interactions within the FHF complex, followed by a C-terminal domain of unknown function (DUF5917/PF19314, aa 600-692) and a highly conserved KELAA motif (IPR045668/PF19311, aa 699-727). The KELAA motif is a critical signature for binding to the Hook family proteins (HOOK1/2/3), which serve as adaptors linking dynein-dynactin motors to endosomal membranes. AlphaFold predicts a confident global structure (pLDDT 79.5), indicating a stable, elongated scaffold protein capable of bridging multiple partners. HPA shows ubiquitous expression (detected in all tissues) with low specificity, localizing to the nucleoplasm and cytosol, consistent with a housekeeping role in vesicular transport. ClinVar lists 85 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number losses and gains on chromosome 8p (8p23.3-21.2), implicating FHIP2B haploinsufficiency in 8p deletion syndrome phenotypes including intellectual disability, cardiac defects, and microcephaly, although no specific monogenic disorder is yet attributed solely to point mutations. The absence of STRING interactions reflects its novelty and lack of high-throughput data, but mechanistic inference from the FHF complex logic suggests tight coupling with HOOK3, FTS (Folikulin), and dynein motor components.
