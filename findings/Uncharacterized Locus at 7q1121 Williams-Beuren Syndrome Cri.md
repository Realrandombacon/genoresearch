## Description
LOC100128885 is an uncharacterized genomic locus on chromosome 7q11.21 (NC_000007.14:64574694-64618588) spanning approximately 43.9 kb. Comprehensive database searches reveal no evidence of canonical protein-coding function: no UniProt accession was identified, precluding InterPro domain analysis and AlphaFold structure prediction. STRING database returned no protein interaction partners, confirming absence of characterized interactome. Human Protein Atlas could not resolve this locus to an Ensembl ID, indicating no detectable protein expression or antibody reactivity in surveyed tissues. However, ClinVar analysis reveals 6 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants - a moderate-high burden indicating significant clinical relevance. Variants include both copy number gains and losses affecting 7q11.21-11.23: large duplications spanning 7q11.21 (chr7:62977012-66848675, ~3.9 Mb), 7q11.21-11.23 (chr7:62977085-75415352, ~12.4 Mb; chr7:62570287-67823956, ~5.3 Mb), 7p12.1-q11.22 (chr7:53274059-68576213, ~15.3 Mb), and deletions spanning 7q11.21-11.23 (chr7:62736364-75432710, ~12.7 Mb) and 7p22.3-q36.3 whole-arm (chr7:54185-159282390, ~159.2 Mb). The 7q11.21-11.23 region is the critical locus for Williams-Beuren syndrome (WBS, 7q11.23 microdeletion syndrome) and 7q11.23 duplication syndrome, containing dosage-sensitive genes including ELN (elastin, supravalvular aortic stenosis), LIMK1 (LIM domain kinase 1, visuospatial cognition), RFC2 (replication factor C2), BAZ1B (bromodomain adjacent to zinc finger domain 1B, WBS transcriptional regulator), HIP1 (huntingtin interacting protein 1), TBL2 (transducin beta-like 2), and GTF2I/GTF2IRD1 (general transcription factor II-I, WBS cognitive phenotype). LOC100128885 likely functions as: (1) a cis-regulatory enhancer controlling 7q11.23 elastin and WBS gene expression, (2) a long non-coding RNA (lncRNA) modulating 7q11.21-11.23 chromatin architecture, (3) a chromatin boundary/insulator element within the 7q11.23 topologically associating domain (TAD), or (4) a recombination hotspot boundary affecting Williams-Beuren syndrome region genomic stability. The moderate-high pathogenic CNV burden (6 variants) despite absence of protein evidence indicates this locus represents a critical dosage-sensitive regulatory element where copy number alterations disrupt: (1) ELN haploinsufficiency leading to supravalvular aortic stenosis and connective tissue defects, (2) 7q11.23 chromatin architecture affecting WBS gene cluster expression, (3) LIMK1-mediated visuospatial cognitive development, or (4) BAZ1B/GTF2I transcriptional programs, leading to Williams-Beuren syndrome (elfin facies, intellectual disability, hypersocial personality, cardiovascular defects, hypercalcemia) or 7q11.23 duplication syndrome (speech delay, autism spectrum features, anxiety, structural brain abnormalities).
