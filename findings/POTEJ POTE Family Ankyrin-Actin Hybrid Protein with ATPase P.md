## Description
POTEJ (POTE ankyrin domain family member J, UniProt P0CG39) is a 1038-amino acid intracellular protein located on chromosome 2q14.2 within the POTE gene cluster. InterPro analysis reveals complex multi-domain architecture characteristic of POTE family proteins: N-terminal ankyrin repeat domain superfamily (IPR036770/G3DSA:1.25.40.20, aa 90-376) with multiple ankyrin repeats (IPR002110, aa 106-199/201-299/300-329) mediating protein-protein interactions, central CCDC144C-like coiled-coil domain (IPR039497, aa 556-665) enabling dimerization or scaffolding functions, and C-terminal actin family domain (IPR004000/PF00022, aa 666-1038) with nucleotide-binding domain (IPR043129/G3DSA:3.30.420.40, aa 666-815/809-1038) and conserved actin sites (IPR004001/IPR020902). This unique ankyrin-actin hybrid architecture positions POTEJ as a potential cytoskeletal regulator linking protein interaction networks to ATP-dependent conformational changes. AlphaFold structure prediction achieves high confidence (pLDDT 72.1), indicating a well-folded stable protein with defined domain boundaries suitable for structured protein-protein interactions. Human Protein Atlas shows no detection in standard tissue panels but cell type enriched specificity, consistent with cancer-testis antigen expression patterns restricted to germ cells and malignancies. STRING interaction network reveals 5 partners: CAP2 (0.617, cyclase-associated protein regulating actin dynamics), BCL7A (0.603, B-cell lymphoma protein involved in chromatin remodeling), TRIM49C (0.474, tripartite motif ubiquitin ligase), NMT1 (0.462, N-myristoyltransferase for membrane targeting), and AGAP4 (0.402, ArfGAP with GTPase domain). This interactome suggests POTEJ functions at the intersection of cytoskeletal dynamics (CAP2), transcriptional regulation (BCL7A), and membrane trafficking (AGAP4, NMT1). ClinVar contains 13 pathogenic variants including massive copy-number gains and losses across 2p25.3-q37.3 and 2q12.2-22.3, demonstrating dosage sensitivity where genomic rearrangements disrupting chromosome 2 cause disease phenotypes. Conservation across primates (chimpanzee A0A2J8ISC3, 958 aa; orangutan A0A2J8XSK3, 241 aa) indicates evolutionary origin tied to reproductive function. POTEJ likely functions as a cytoskeletal adaptor protein, using its ankyrin repeats to recruit binding partners while its actin-like ATPase domain provides energy-dependent conformational switching, potentially coordinating actin polymerization with cellular processes during spermatogenesis or cancer cell proliferation.
