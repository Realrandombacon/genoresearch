## Description
GPR68 (Q15743, 365 aa), also known as OGR1 (Ovarian Cancer G Protein-Coupled Receptor 1), is a class A rhodopsin-like G protein-coupled receptor functioning as a proton sensor and sphingosylphosphorylcholine (SPC) receptor. InterPro identifies a canonical 7-transmembrane architecture: rhodopsin-like GPCR domain (IPR000276, PF00001, PS00237, PS50262, aa 23-294), OGR1-specific SPC receptor signatures (IPR005389, PR01564, aa 1-29, 91-103), and the broader 7TM receptor superfamily (IPR017452, G3DSA:1.20.1070.10, cd15367, aa 22-301). AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (pLDDT 80.4, residues 1-365), indicating a stable, well-folded receptor with the characteristic inverted 7-helix bundle and extracellular ligand-binding pocket optimized for proton coordination. HPA shows tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in pituitary gland (11.7 nTPM), detected in many tissues; classified as a disease-related gene, potential drug target, and GPCR involved in signal transduction; associated with amelogenesis imperfecta and tumor suppression. STRING reveals 10 interactions linking it to pH sensing and enamel formation: MAP3K4 (0.761, mitogen-activated protein kinase kinase kinase 4), ODAPH (0.685, odontogenesis associated phosphoprotein, critical for enamel), SLC24A4 (0.586, sodium/calcium exchanger in ameloblasts), FAM83H (0.585, enamel mineralization protein), LPAR1 (0.567, lysophosphatidic acid receptor), GRK2 (0.531, GPCR kinase for desensitization), GPR162 (0.506), GPR39 (0.495, zinc-sensing GPCR), GNAQ (0.464, Gq alpha subunit), and GPR132 (0.402). The strong links to ODAPH, SLC24A4, and FAM83H confirm its role in ameloblast function and enamel biomineralization. ClinVar contains 25 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including frameshifts (c.975dup/p.Glu326fs, c.78_83delinsC/p.Val27fs) and large copy number losses/gains (chr14:79886061-96870809 deletion, chr14:88580184-107285437 duplication) affecting the 14q32 region, causing autosomal recessive amelogenesis imperfecta type IG (AI3G) characterized by hypomaturation enamel defects. Functional hypothesis: GPR68/OGR1 functions as a proton-activated Gs-coupled receptor (pH 7.8 inactive → pH 6.8 active) that detects extracellular acidification in mineralizing tissues. In ameloblasts, it senses the acidic microenvironment generated during enamel matrix secretion and mineralization, triggering cAMP/PKA signaling via GNAQ to regulate ion transporters (SLC24A4) and enamel proteins (ODAPH, FAM83H). The receptor likely operates in a feedback loop where proton release during hydroxyapatite crystal growth activates OGR1, which then modulates pH homeostasis and matrix processing. Beyond enamel, OGR1 acts as a tumor suppressor in ovarian and other cancers by sensing tumor acidosis and inducing growth arrest or apoptosis. Conservation across vertebrates (mouse 365 aa, bovine 361 aa) confirms essential role in mineralization and pH sensing. NOTE: While mechanistically characterized, tissue-specific signaling partners and therapeutic targeting remain active research areas.
