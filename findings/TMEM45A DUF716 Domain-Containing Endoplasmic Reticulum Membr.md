## Description
TMEM45A (Gene ID: 55076, UniProt Q9NWC5, aliases DERP7, DNAPTP4) is a 275 amino acid transmembrane protein located on chromosome 3q12.2 (NC_000003.12, positions 100492618-100577443, ~85kb genomic region). This gene encodes a member of the transmembrane protein 45 family with a conserved domain of unknown function DUF716. InterPro domain analysis reveals DUF716 domain (IPR006904/PF04819, positions 121-241) representing a conserved protein family of unknown function, TMEM45 family signature (IPR042127, positions 1-275 spanning full length), and panther family PTHR16007 (EPIDIDYMAL MEMBRANE PROTEIN E9-RELATED, positions 1-275). The DUF716 domain occupies the central region of the protein (~120 residues), suggesting it constitutes the core functional module. GO annotations are limited but protein class is predicted membrane protein. STRING interaction network identifies 10 medium-to-high confidence protein partners: GHITM (0.787, GHSR-interacting transmembrane protein, mitochondrial outer membrane), MINDY3 (0.772, motif-interacting with ubiquitin-containing novel DUB family member 3, deubiquitinating enzyme), COPS7A (0.714, COP9 signalosome subunit 7A, regulatory complex involved in protein degradation), BLOC1S6 (0.631, biogenesis of lysosomal organelles complex 1 subunit 6), TMEM158 (0.588, another transmembrane protein), TMEM9 (0.579, transmembrane protein 9), TBC1D8B (0.548, TBC1 domain family member 8B, Rab GTPase activating protein), TMEM212 (0.543, transmembrane protein 212), MIPOL1 (0.478, mirror image polydactyly 1), and TMEM14A (0.408, transmembrane protein 14A). The interaction profile suggests roles in membrane trafficking, protein degradation pathways (COPS7A, MINDY3), and lysosomal biogenesis (BLOC1S6). Human Protein Atlas classifies TMEM45A (ENSG00000181458) as a predicted membrane protein with endoplasmic reticulum and vesicle subcellular localization. RNA tissue specificity is 'tissue enriched' with highest expression in skin (449.0 nTPM—very high expression level), detected in all tissues but with strong skin enrichment. Cancer specificity is low, not detected in immune cells, with cell type-enhanced single cell specificity. This expression pattern strongly suggests a role in skin biology, potentially keratinocyte differentiation, epidermal barrier formation, or skin-specific metabolic processes. AlphaFold structure prediction (AF-Q9NWC5-F1) yields very high-confidence model (global pLDDT 91.9), indicating a well-folded stable architecture across all 275 residues—among the highest confidence predictions, suggesting evolutionary constraint and important structural integrity. ClinVar lists 9 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all large chromosomal copy number variations spanning chromosome 3q regions (e.g., chr3:93979547-124774010 deletion, chr3:95563096-102371126 deletion, chr3:93819623-103888749 triplication, chr3:93800620-145695381 triplication, chr3:97795369-115663349 deletion); no specific monogenic disease is attributed solely to TMEM45A point mutations, suggesting dosage sensitivity within larger chromosomal syndromes rather than haploinsufficiency. Conservation evidenced by TMEM45B paralog (Q96B21, 275 aa identical length) and panther family membership across vertebrates. Functional hypothesis: TMEM45A functions as an endoplasmic reticulum membrane protein involved in protein quality control, membrane trafficking, or lipid metabolism pathways. The DUF716 domain likely mediates protein-protein interactions or substrate recognition. High-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 91.9) suggests defined transmembrane topology with structured luminal/cytosolic domains. Interaction with GHITM (mitochondrial outer membrane protein) suggests potential ER-mitochondria contact site function. COPS7A and MINDY3 interactions implicate roles in ubiquitin-mediated protein degradation and COP9 signalosome regulation. Strong skin expression (449.0 nTPM) indicates specialized function in epidermal biology—potentially keratinocyte differentiation, cornified envelope formation, or skin barrier maintenance. Disease mechanism: TMEM45A haploinsufficiency or overexpression may contribute to chromosome 3q deletion/duplication syndromes. The 9 ClinVar CNVs suggest dosage sensitivity; triplication may cause aberrant protein accumulation or dominant-negative effects while deletion may impair ER function in skin and other tissues. No specific dermatological syndrome is currently attributed to TMEM45A, but skin-enriched expression predicts potential cutaneous phenotypes. Priority experiments include co-IP validation of STRING interactions (especially GHITM, COPS7A, MINDY3), ER localization confirmation via immunofluorescence, investigation of TMEM45A role in keratinocyte differentiation using siRNA knockdown, assessment of TMEM45A expression during epidermal development, structural analysis leveraging high-confidence AlphaFold model (pLDDT 91.9) to identify functional surfaces and transmembrane topology, and generation of knockout mice to assess skin phenotype.
