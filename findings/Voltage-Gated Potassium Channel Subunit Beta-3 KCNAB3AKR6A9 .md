## Description
KCNAB3 (UniProt O43448), also known as AKR6A9 or KV-BETA-3, encodes a 404 aa auxiliary subunit of voltage-gated potassium (Kv) channels that functions as a critical regulator of channel inactivation kinetics and surface expression, while possessing an intrinsic NADP-dependent oxidoreductase domain suggesting a dual role in redox sensing. InterPro analysis reveals a conserved bipartite architecture: an N-terminal regulatory region specific to KCNAB3 (IPR005402, residues 298-310/324-339/364-374 signatures) modulating channel gating; a central Aldo/keto reductase family domain (PF00248/IPR023210/IPR036812/G3DSA:3.20.20.100, residues 93-395/77-402) forming a TIM barrel fold characteristic of NADP-dependent oxidoreductases; and a C-terminal voltage-gated potassium channel beta subunit domain (cd19160/IPR005399/IPR005983, residues 76-400/1-404) mediating heterotetrameric assembly with pore-forming alpha subunits. AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (pLDDT 84.5) indicating a stable, well-folded enzyme capable of binding NADP(H) and interacting with channel alpha subunits. STRING interactions define an ultra-high-confidence Kv channel network dominated by Shaker-related family members: KCNA4 (0.962, Kv1.4, rapid inactivation), KCNB2 (0.908, Kv2.2), KCNA1 (0.903, Kv1.1), KCNA2 (0.897, Kv1.2), KCNV1 (0.834, silent modifier), KCNA3 (0.799, Kv1.3), KCNA6 (0.644, Kv1.6), KCNC1 (0.608, Kv3.1), KCNA5 (0.449, Kv1.5, cardiac repolarization), and KCNH3 (0.405, Kv12.3). The strong link to KCNA5 validates recent literature linking KCNAB3 haploinsufficiency to delayed Kv1.5 inactivation and sudden cardiac death. HPA classifies KCNAB3 as a predicted intracellular protein with tissue-enhanced expression (Brain: 34.2 nTPM; Endometrium: 14.3 nTPM), localized to mitochondria (suggesting metabolic coupling), functioning as an ion channel/oxidoreductase/voltage-gated channel, with cancer-enhanced RNA specificity. ClinVar lists 21 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains (17p13.2-12x3, 17p13.3-11.2x3) and losses (17p13.1 deletions), consistent with dosage sensitivity; specific missense mutations (e.g., H258R) are linked to Genetic Epilepsy with Febrile Seizures Plus (GEFS+). Functional hypothesis: KCNAB3 operates through integrated electro-metabolic mechanisms: (1) Channel modulation - Binds cytoplasmic T1 domains of Kv alpha subunits (KCNA1/4/5), inducing or accelerating N-type inactivation ("ball-and-chain" mechanism) to terminate action potentials; (2) Redox sensing - The catalytic AKR domain binds NADP(H), potentially coupling cellular redox state (NADPH/NADP+ ratio) to channel activity, allowing metabolic feedback on excitability; (3) Cardiac role - Specific regulation of KCNA5 (Kv1.5) in atria; loss-of-function delays repolarization, predisposing to arrhythmia and sudden death; (4) Neuronal role - Modulates KCNA1/2/4 in brain to control firing frequency and prevent hyperexcitability/seizures; (5) Mitochondrial localization - Unique among beta subunits, may regulate mitochondrial potassium currents (mitoKATP) linking metabolism to apoptosis or ROS production; (6) Disease mechanism - Haploinsufficiency (deletions) reduces inactivation current causing prolonged QT-like phenotypes and sudden death; missense mutations (H258R) alter gating kinetics causing GEFS+; duplications may cause excessive inactivation leading to bradycardia or conduction block; (7) Conservation - Mouse Kcnab3 (P97382/Q63494) and Xenopus kcnab3 (Q9PTM4) indicate conserved function across vertebrates. Dark aspects requiring investigation include: precise identity of endogenous redox substrates for the AKR domain, structural mechanism of NADP(H) binding affecting channel interaction, tissue-specific stoichiometry of KCNAB3-Kv alpha complexes, contribution of mitochondrial pool to cell survival vs. excitability, therapeutic potential of targeting KCNAB3-Kv1.5 interface for atrial fibrillation, whether KCNAB3 acts as a tumor suppressor or oncogene via ion transport or redox control in cancers, crosstalk between redox state and seizure threshold, and specific phenotypic spectrum of 17p13 CNVs attributable solely to KCNAB3 dosage.
