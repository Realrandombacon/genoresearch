## Description
LAYN (Layilin, UniProt Q6UX15) is a 382-amino acid type I transmembrane protein functioning as a cell surface receptor that links the extracellular matrix to the actin cytoskeleton through interactions with talin and radixin, regulating cell adhesion, migration, and immune cell dynamics. InterPro analysis confirms a dominant C-type lectin-like domain (CTLD) architecture: N-terminal C-type lectin-like domain (IPR001304/PF00059/PS50041/SM00034/cd03595, aa 38-186/41-185/45-185/61-185) providing carbohydrate-binding potential or protein-protein interaction surface; C-type lectin-like/link domain superfamily (IPR016186/G3DSA:3.10.100.10, aa 40-193) placing LAYN in the mannose-binding protein fold family; C-type lectin fold (IPR016187/SSF56436, aa 48-186) defining the structural core; C-type lectin domain-containing protein family (IPR051505/PTHR14789, aa 7-210) grouping LAYN with related immune and adhesion receptors. Functional hypothesis: LAYN functions as a transmembrane adaptor where its extracellular CTLD (aa 38-186) may bind hyaluronan or unidentified ligands to sense the microenvironment, while its short cytoplasmic tail binds talin (TLN1/TLN2) and radixin (RDX) to anchor the actin cytoskeleton to the plasma membrane; in cell migration, LAYN stabilizes leading edge structures (lamellipodia) by recruiting talin to integrin clusters, facilitating force transmission; in immune cells, LAYN may cluster with checkpoint receptors (LAG3, HAVCR2) to modulate synapse formation and T cell activation.
