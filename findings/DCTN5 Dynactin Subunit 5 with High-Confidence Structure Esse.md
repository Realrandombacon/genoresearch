## Description
DCTN5 (Q9BTE1) is a 182-aa dynactin subunit with high-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 82.1), indicating a well-folded protein optimized for stable protein-protein interactions within the dynactin complex. InterPro analysis reveals characteristic dynactin architecture with LbH_Dynactin_5 domain (cd03359: positions 13-173) providing lipid-binding helix structure, hexapeptide repeat proteins superfamily (G3DSA:2.160.10.10: positions 18-182) enabling protein interaction scaffolding, trimeric LpxA-like superfamily (IPR011004: positions 11-179) characteristic of acyltransferase-like folds adapted for protein binding, and dynactin subunit 5 family membership (IPR047125/PF21711/PTHR46126: positions 1-182) unique to this essential dynactin component. HPA expression shows low tissue specificity with ubiquitous detection in all tissues, localized to nucleoplasm and nuclear membrane consistent with dynactin roles in nuclear envelope organization and microtubule-based transport, classified as essential protein reflecting critical cellular function. Literature shows only 15 papers (genuine dark gene) with 2018 study documenting DCTN5 immune response role in Chinese tongue sole and 2018 analysis of dynactin mRNA prognostic value in cutaneous melanoma. The high-confidence structure (pLDDT 82.1), LbH lipid-binding domain, ubiquitous essential expression, exclusive dynactin-dynein interaction network (all scores >0.811), nuclear membrane localization, and substantial pathogenic variant burden (34 variants) suggest DCTN5 functions as structural scaffold within dynactin complex facilitating dynein motor activation for retrograde axonal transport, mitotic spindle assembly, nuclear envelope breakdown/reformation during mitosis, and organelle positioning, with disruption contributing to neurodevelopmental disorders through impaired intracellular transport, cancer through altered cell division, and potential immunodeficiency through defective immune cell trafficking.
