## Description
ANXA9 (Annexin A9; UniProt O76027) is a 345 amino acid calcium-dependent phospholipid-binding protein with 77 publications, representing a moderately studied annexin family member with emerging roles in cancer progression, immune cell function, and membrane trafficking.\n\nSTRUCTURAL ARCHITECTURE: InterPro analysis reveals canonical annexin architecture with four conserved annexin repeats (IPR018502/PF00191, residues 41-112/113-184/197-266) that form the characteristic curved solenoid structure for membrane binding. The protein belongs to the annexin superfamily (IPR037104/G3DSA:1.10.220.10, residues 3-344/32-270) with GO annotations for calcium ion binding (GO:0005509) and calcium-dependent phospholipid binding (GO:0005544). The annexin A9-specific family domain (IPR009116, residues 8-56) distinguishes it from other annexin paralogs. Conserved calcium-binding sites are present at positions 55-77, 95-111, and 122-143 within the annexin repeat core. AlphaFold predicts high confidence structure (global pLDDT 89.9), indicating a well-folded annexin core with stable calcium-coordinating loops—typical of functional annexins that undergo calcium-triggered membrane association.\n\nINTERACTION NETWORK: STRING-DB identifies medium-confidence interactions with cytoskeletal scaffolds, ubiquitination machinery, and membrane trafficking proteins: PPL (0.642, periplakin, desmosomal/plasma membrane scaffold), NEDD4 (0.523, E3 ubiquitin-protein ligase NEDD4, regulates annexin turnover), desmogleins DSG4 (0.497), DSG3 (0.448), DSG1 (0.448) (cadherin-family desmosomal adhesion proteins), S100A10 (0.479, S100 calcium-binding protein A10/p11, annexin partner), tetraspanins CD9 (0.453) and CD63 (0.420) (exosome/EV markers), TMEM60 (0.480, transmembrane protein 60), and C6orf132 (0.462). This interaction profile establishes ANXA9 as a membrane-cytoskeleton linker functioning at desmosomes and exosomes, with S100A10 partnership suggesting roles in plasminogen activation and fibrinolysis, while NEDD4 interaction implies ubiquitin-mediated regulation of membrane association.\n\nEXPRESSION & LOCALIZATION: HPA shows expression detected in many tissues with low tissue and cancer specificity but immune cell-enhanced expression, consistent with roles in leukocyte migration and immune synapse formation. Subcellular localization includes nuclear speckles (suggesting RNA processing roles) and cytosol (membrane-shuttling pool). Molecular function is annotated as receptor activity. Classified as a predicted intracellular protein with cell type-enriched single cell specificity.\n\nDISEASE LANDSCAPE: ClinVar contains 16 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number gains at 1q21.1-25.3, 1q21.1-44, 1q21.1-23.1, and 1q21.3 encompassing ANXA9, plus copy number losses at 1q21.3 and 1q21.2-21.3. No monogenic disease is yet definitively assigned, but 1q21 CNVs cause developmental anomalies and thrombocytopenia-absent radius (TAR) syndrome features. Recent 2024-2025 literature establishes oncogenic roles: ANXA9 facilitates S100A4 to promote breast cancer progression through STAT3 pathway modulation (2024, cited 24x); the ZMYM2-ANXA9 axis mediates oxaliplatin resistance in colorectal cancer, targetable by FLT3 inhibitor G749 (2025); ANXA9 expression correlates with immune evasion and drug resistance in CRC (2025). The S100A4 interaction is particularly significant—S100A4 is a well-established metastasis promoter, and ANXA9 may serve as its membrane anchor.\n\nCONSERVATION: Orthologs confirmed in mouse (Q9JHQ0, 345 aa), bovine (Q3ZC08, 345 aa), rat (D3ZSA0, 344 aa), and pig (A0A287AK76, 348 aa), indicating strong evolutionary constraint on the 345-amino-acid annexin core across mammals.\n\nFUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: ANXA9 functions as a calcium-regulated membrane-binding protein that translocates from cytosol to plasma membrane upon calcium influx, where it binds phosphatidylserine-rich inner leaflet membranes via its annexin core. At the membrane, ANXA9 serves multiple roles: (1) Desmosome stabilization through interactions with periplakin (PPL) and desmogleins (DSG1/3/4), linking intermediate filaments to cadherin adhesion complexes in epithelial tissues; (2) Exosome biogenesis and trafficking via tetraspanin partners CD9/CD63, potentially regulating tumor-derived extracellular vesicle release that promotes metastasis; (3) S100A10 heterotetramer formation for plasminogen activation and fibrinolysis, analogous to annexin A2; (4) STAT3 pathway modulation in cancer cells, where ANXA9-S100A4 complexes may enhance STAT3 phosphorylation and nuclear translocation, driving proliferation and immune evasion genes. NEDD4-mediated ubiquitination likely regulates ANXA9 membrane residence time. In breast cancer, ANXA9 overexpression amplifies S100A4-driven metastasis. In colorectal cancer, ANXA9 confers oxaliplatin resistance through ZMYM2 partnership. The 16 ClinVar variants warrant investigation for bleeding disorders (fibrinolysis defects), blistering skin diseases (desmosome dysfunction), and immune deficiencies.
