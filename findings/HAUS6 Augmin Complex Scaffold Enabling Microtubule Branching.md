# HAUS6: Augmin Complex Scaffold Enabling Microtubule Branching with Emerging Cancer Prognostic Value

**Confidence:** MEDIUM-HIGH

## Description
HAUS6 (UniProt Q7Z4H7) encodes a 955-amino acid subunit of the hetero-octameric augmin complex, essential for microtubule-dependent microtubule amplification during mitotic spindle assembly. InterPro analysis identifies HAUS6-specific domains including the N-terminal region (PF14661/IPR028163, aa 14-239) and a conserved family domain (IPR026797, aa 7-922) associated with spindle assembly (GO:0051225) and HAUS complex formation (GO:0070652). Recent 2025 literature confirms a calponin homology domain within HAUS6 anchors augmin to existing microtubules for branching nucleation. AlphaFold predicts a partially structured protein (pLDDT: 55.9), consistent with flexible scaffold proteins that undergo conformational changes during complex assembly.\n\nSTRING interactions reveal exclusive high-confidence partnerships with augmin complex members: HAUS5 (0.999), HAUS3 (0.755), HAUS1 (0.714), HAUS8 (0.703), HAUS2 (0.647), HAUS4 (0.637), HAUS7 (0.639), plus microtubule regulators NEDD1 (0.993), NUMA1 (0.924), and TUBG1 (0.816). This confirms HAUS6's role as an integral structural component of the γ-TuRC recruitment machinery.\n\nHPA data shows ubiquitous expression across all tissues with localization to nuclear speckles, centriolar satellites, and cytosol—consistent with cell cycle functions. ClinVar contains 81 pathogenic variants including copy number gains/losses and splice site mutations. With only 22 literature papers, HAUS6 remains underexplored despite clinical relevance: it suppresses colorectal cancer proliferation via p53/p21 pathway activation when downregulated (2022, 22 citations), serves as a prognostic biomarker in hepatocellular carcinoma and tongue squamous cell carcinoma (2024-2025), and represents a potential immunological marker in pan-cancer analysis. This positions HAUS6 as a genuine 'dark' mitotic regulator with untapped diagnostic potential.
