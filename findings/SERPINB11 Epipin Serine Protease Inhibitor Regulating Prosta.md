## Description
SERPINB11 (also known as Epipin) encodes a 392 aa serine protease inhibitor (UniProt Q96P15) located at 18q21.33, functioning as a critical regulator of epithelial homeostasis, cancer progression, and developmental processes. InterPro analysis confirms its canonical serpin architecture: N-terminal domain (G3DSA:3.30.497.10, aa 13-189/289-339) and C-terminal domain (G3DSA:2.30.39.10, aa 190-288/340-385) forming the characteristic alpha-1-antitrypsin fold (IPR000215/PF00079), with a conserved reactive center loop signature (PS00284, aa 365-375) essential for protease inhibition. AlphaFold predicts a very high-confidence structure (pLDDT 89.4), indicating a rigid, stable inhibitory conformation typical of functional serpins. STRING interactions reveal a specialized network involving metabolic and proteolytic regulators: ADH7 (0.491, alcohol dehydrogenase 7, epithelial differentiation), KLK14 (0.485, kallikrein-related peptidase 14, primary target protease), ZNF799 (0.479), DNLZ (0.468), CPE (0.461, carboxypeptidase E), and S100A12 (0.400, calcium-binding inflammatory mediator). HPA expression shows strong tissue enhancement in prostate (30.5 nTPM) and esophagus (25.7 nTPM), with cancer-enriched expression patterns, consistent with roles in epithelial integrity and tumorigenesis. ClinVar reports 55 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large 18q copy number gains (x3, x4) and losses (x1), suggesting dosage sensitivity in developmental syndromes; specific frameshift variants are linked to STEC-HUS liver injury and a novel hoof-specific phenotype in Connemara ponies. Literature (31 papers) details emerging mechanistic roles: (1) Ovarian Cancer Prognosis - SERPINB11 overexpression correlating with poor survival in high-grade serous and clear cell carcinomas, where its inhibition by eupatilin triggers ER-mitochondria calcium influx and apoptosis; (2) Protease Regulation - potential inhibition of KLK14 and other kallikreins to modulate epithelial shedding and inflammation; (3) Developmental Roles - critical for hoof integrity in horses and spermatogenesis in mice via apoptosis regulation. Functional hypothesis: SERPINB11 likely functions through: (1) Protease Inhibition - binding and inhibiting KLK14 or related serine proteases via its reactive center loop to prevent excessive extracellular matrix degradation and inflammation; (2) Calcium Signaling Modulation - interacting with S100A12 or ER-mitochondria axis components to regulate intracellular calcium fluxes, determining cell fate (survival vs. apoptosis) in cancer and development; (3) Epithelial Differentiation - cooperating with ADH7 to maintain prostate and esophageal barrier function; (4) Dosage Sensitivity - 18q amplification causing SERPINB11 overexpression, potentially suppressing apoptosis and promoting tumor growth, while deletion leads to developmental defects. Disease implications include ovarian cancer prognosis, STEC-HUS complications, hoof dysplasia, and potential roles in prostate/esophageal disorders. Despite 31 papers, the precise native protease targets, structural mechanism of inhibition, and therapeutic potential of SERPINB11 modulation in cancer remain underexplored.
