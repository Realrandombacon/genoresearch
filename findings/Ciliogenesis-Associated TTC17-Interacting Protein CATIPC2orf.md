## Description
CATIP (Ciliogenesis-associated TTC17-interacting protein, also known as C2orf62) is a 387 aa intracellular protein functioning as a critical structural regulator of actin cytoskeleton dynamics during spermiogenesis and ciliogenesis. With 17 literature references, CATIP represents a 'partially characterized' dark gene where its essential role in male fertility is established, but its molecular mechanisms in ciliary base assembly and potential roles in thyroid cancer metastasis are emerging (2020-2025). InterPro analysis reveals a unique bipartite domain architecture: a large, conserved N-terminal domain of unknown specific function but high structural constraint (IPR048777/PF21772, aa 31-263) comprising ~60% of the protein, and a C-terminal dimerization/docking (D/D) domain (IPR047501/cd22973/SSF47391, aa 316-354) homologous to the regulatory subunits of cAMP-dependent protein kinase (PKA), suggesting a role in anchoring signaling complexes or mediating protein-protein interactions. AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (pLDDT 85.9), indicating a rigid, well-folded scaffold where the N-terminal domain likely forms a stable core and the C-terminal D/D domain provides a specific interaction interface. HPA shows tissue-enhanced expression, highest in fallopian tube (18.9 nTPM) and choroid plexus (15.4 nTPM)—both ciliated epithelia—and localizes specifically to the sperm connecting piece and midpiece, consistent with a role in manchette (transient microtubule/actin structure) organization and flagellar formation. STRING interactions reveal a network enriched for cytoskeletal and ciliary components: CATIP associates with GAS2L2 (0.628, growth arrest-specific protein linking microtubules and actin), FOPNL (0.596, centriolar satellite protein), CABYR (0.523, calcium-binding tyrosine-phosphorylated protein of the fibrous sheath), and AK8 (0.576, adenylate kinase 8), supporting its function in coordinating cytoskeletal remodeling. ClinVar lists 27 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains on chromosome 2q33-37 and missense mutations (e.g., p.Phe35Ile in the N-terminal domain), definitively linking CATIP dysfunction to oligoteratoasthenozoospermia (OTA), a severe form of male infertility characterized by low sperm count, abnormal morphology, and reduced motility. Recent literature (2020-2025) establishes CATIP as: (1) Essential regulator of actin dynamics in the spermatid manchette, where mutation disrupts nuclear shaping and flagellar assembly; (2) Target of lncRNA CATIP-AS1, whose downregulation promotes thyroid cancer progression via the EMT pathway; (3) Interactor of TTC17, potentially forming a complex that regulates ciliary gate integrity.
