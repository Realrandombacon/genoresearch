# CRACR2B: EF-Hand Calcium-Binding Regulatory Protein Linking Calcium Signaling to Rab-Mediated Vesicle Trafficking in Secretory Tissues

## Description
CRACR2B (Q8N4Y2) encodes a 399 amino acid EF-hand calcium-binding regulatory protein that functions as a calcium sensor coupling intracellular calcium fluxes to vesicle trafficking machinery in secretory tissues. InterPro analysis reveals a specialized calcium-sensing architecture: paired EF-hand domains (IPR002048, positions 63-98; IPR011992 EF-hand domain pair, positions 32-99; G3DSA:1.10.238.10, positions 29-92) forming the canonical calcium-binding motif, an EFCAB4A-specific helical region (IPR060741/PF27574, positions 130-225) likely mediating protein-protein interactions, and classification within the calcium-binding regulatory protein family (IPR051111/PTHR46311, positions 31-324). AlphaFold predicts a well-folded structure (pLDDT 76.9, high confidence) consistent with stable calcium-dependent conformational switching. STRING interactions identify partners linking CRACR2B to cytoskeletal transport and membrane trafficking: DYNC1LI1 (0.603) and DYNC1LI2 (0.441), light intermediate chains of cytoplasmic dynein motor complex; CARD19 (0.591), a CARD domain protein involved in innate immunity; RAB15 (0.537), RAB3A (0.510), RAB3B (0.482), and RAB3D (0.492), small GTPases regulating secretory vesicle trafficking; STIM1 (0.507), the endoplasmic reticulum calcium sensor that activates store-operated calcium entry; OR5H2 (0.525), an olfactory receptor; and CFDP1 (0.437), craniofacial development protein. HPA shows tissue-enhanced expression with exceptionally high levels in salivary gland (210.1 nTPM) and pancreas (136.4 nTPM), both highly secretory tissues, with intracellular localization consistent with vesicle-associated functions. ClinVar contains 30 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including multiple copy number gains spanning chromosome 11p15.5-15.4 regions and splice site variants (c.459-100G>T), though specific monogenic disease associations remain undefined. Literature (only 1 paper) mentions CRACR2B in rat subfornical organ transcriptome alterations following early postnatal overnutrition, linking to metabolic programming. The mechanistic hypothesis: CRACR2B functions as a calcium-regulated adaptor that bridges calcium signaling to vesicle trafficking through its EF-hand domains sensing cytosolic calcium transients while its helical region engages dynein motors (DYNC1LI1/2) and Rab GTPases (RAB3/RAB15 family); in secretory tissues like salivary gland and pancreas, CRACR2B coordinates calcium-triggered exocytosis by recruiting dynein-mediated vesicle transport to release sites; STIM1 interaction suggests CRACR2B may couple store-operated calcium entry to downstream trafficking events; CARD19 association hints at potential immune modulation roles; copy number gains on 11p15.5 may cause dosage-sensitive disruption of calcium-trafficking coupling, potentially contributing to secretory dysfunction or metabolic disorders; the single literature link to early postnatal overnutrition suggests CRACR2B may participate in hypothalamic metabolic programming via calcium-dependent neuropeptide secretion.
