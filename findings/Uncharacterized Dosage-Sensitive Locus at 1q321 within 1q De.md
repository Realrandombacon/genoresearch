## Description
LOC101929305 is an uncharacterized genomic locus on chromosome 1q32.1 (NC_000001.11:201023948-201036675) spanning approximately 12.7 kb. Comprehensive database searches reveal no evidence of protein-coding function: no UniProt accession was identified, precluding InterPro domain analysis and AlphaFold structure prediction. STRING database returned no protein interaction partners, confirming absence of canonical protein-coding function. Human Protein Atlas identified Ensembl ID ENSG00000234132 but reported no detectable protein expression data, indicating this locus is not represented in the HPA protein atlas. However, ClinVar analysis reveals 2 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants in this region: a large deletion (chr1:200144603-203112078, ~3.0 Mb) and a large duplication (chr1:187143981-224299417, ~37.2 Mb). These structural variants are associated with 1q deletion syndrome and 1q duplication syndrome featuring developmental delay, intellectual disability, craniofacial dysmorphism, skeletal anomalies, and cardiac defects. The 1q32.1 region contains several dosage-sensitive genes including MDM4 (MDM4 regulator of p53, critical for cell cycle control and tumor suppression), PEX11B (peroxisome biogenesis factor), and FPR1 (formyl peptide receptor 1, GPCR signaling). LOC101929305 likely functions as part of the 1q32.1 cis-regulatory landscape or encodes a non-coding RNA whose copy number alteration contributes to 1q deletion/duplication syndrome pathogenesis through disruption of chromatin topology, topologically associating domain (TAD) boundaries, or long-range dysregulation of dosage-sensitive developmental genes. The presence of pathogenic CNV burden (2 variants) despite absence of protein evidence indicates this locus resides within a critical dosage-sensitive genomic region where both hemizygosity and trisomy disrupt developmental processes via gene dosage effects and 3D genome reorganization.
