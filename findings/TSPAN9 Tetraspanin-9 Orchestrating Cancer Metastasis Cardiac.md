## Description
TSPAN9 (Tetraspanin-9) is a 239 amino acid four-transmembrane domain protein that functions as a critical regulator of cancer metastasis, cardiac hypertrophy, and chondrocyte senescence through tetraspanin web assembly with integrins and signaling receptors, FAK-Ras-ERK1/2 pathway activation for epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT), and mitocytosis-mediated mitochondrial quality control in cardiomyocytes and chondrocytes. InterPro analysis identifies characteristic tetraspanin architecture including four transmembrane domains forming two extracellular loops (small EC1 and large EC2), tetraspanin animal family signature (IPR000301, PIRSF002419, PTHR19282, residues 3-235/11-215) defining membrane topology, tetraspanin EC2 domain superfamily (IPR008952, SSF48652, G3DSA:1.10.1450.10, cd03165, residues 105-202/105-201/107-200) containing conserved cysteines for disulfide bonding and partner protein binding, tetraspanin/peripherin family (IPR018499, PF00335, residues 9-229) providing structural scaffold, tetraspanin conserved site signature (IPR018503, PS00421, residues 61-83) with GQ motif for functional integrity, transmembrane 4 family signatures (PR00259, residues 11-34/50-76/77-105) defining four-pass membrane topology, and prokaryotic membrane lipoprotein lipid attachment site profile (PS51257, residues 1-25) suggesting potential lipid modification. AlphaFold structure (AF-O75954-F1) predicts very high confidence (pLDDT 89.6) across the 239 residue sequence, indicating a well-folded four-transmembrane protein with stable EC2 domain suitable for tetraspanin web assembly and partner protein recruitment. HPA data classifies TSPAN9 as a predicted membrane protein and transporter with nucleoplasmic, Golgi apparatus, and cytosolic localization reflecting trafficking between membrane compartments, tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in brain (148.5 nTPM) and heart muscle (120.6 nTPM), detected in many tissues, low cancer specificity, not detected in immune cells, and cell type-enhanced specificity consistent with regulated membrane organization function. STRING interactions reveal coupling to tetraspanin network and integrin signaling: CD9 (0.761) as primary tetraspanin web partner; TSPAN32 (0.715) as related family member; GP6 (Glycoprotein VI, 0.614) for platelet activation; ITGB1 (Integrin Beta-1, 0.603) as key adhesion receptor partner; CD151 (0.527) and CD63 (0.515) as tetraspanin family members; TSPAN15 (0.493) for web assembly; CTDSPL (0.466) for transcriptional regulation; SAMD14 (0.446) for sterile alpha motif signaling; and VWCE (0.436) for von Willebrand factor interaction. ClinVar lists 65 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including duplications (single allele), large copy number gains on 12p13.33-11.1, 12p13.33-q13.12, 12p13.33-13.2 (spanning 17-49 Mb regions), copy number losses on 12p13.33-13.31, 12p13.33-13.32 (multiple independent CNV events), though no specific monogenic syndrome is defined, suggesting roles in developmental disorders and cancer susceptibility. Literature (57 papers) establishes TSPAN9 as an emerging regulator: TSPAN9 exacerbates cardiac hypertrophy by impairing cardiac autophagy revealing heart failure mechanism; TSPAN9 inhibits malignant progression of hepatocellular carcinoma demonstrating tumor suppressor function in liver cancer; TSPAN9-mediated mitocytosis protects against interleukin-1β-induced chondrocyte senescence showing mitochondrial quality control role; TSPAN9 induces EMT and promotes osteosarcoma metastasis via activating FAK-Ras-ERK1/2 pathway establishing oncogenic signaling mechanism; and hsa-miR-9-5p-mediated TSPAN9 downregulation correlates with poor hepatocellular carcinoma prognosis and tumor immune infiltration confirming miRNA regulation in cancer. As an emerging tetraspanin with multifaceted disease associations, TSPAN9 represents a critical node connecting membrane organization to cancer metastasis, cardiac hypertrophy, and chondrocyte aging through tetraspanin web assembly with CD9/CD151/ITGB1 partners, FAK-Ras-ERK1/2 signaling activation for EMT induction, and mitocytosis-mediated mitochondrial quality control disrupted in osteosarcoma metastasis, hepatocellular carcinoma progression, heart failure, and cartilage degeneration.
