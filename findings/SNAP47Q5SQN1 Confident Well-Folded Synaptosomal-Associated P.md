## Description
SNAP47 (Synaptosome associated protein 47, also known as C1orf142, ESFI5812, HEL-S-290, HEL170, SNAP-47, SVAP1) is a 464 amino acid intracellular SNARE protein (UniProt Q5SQN1) with confident well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 70.4), characteristic of stable coiled-coil proteins that mediate membrane fusion events. The protein contains multiple defining domains: a C-terminal SNARE motif (cd15854, residues 404-462) and N-terminal SNARE motif (cd15888, residues 151-215) with t-SNARE coiled-coil homology (IPR000727/PS50192, residues 180-216/401-463), a GRAM domain (IPR004182/PF02893, residues 47-145) involved in lipid binding and membrane association, a PH-like domain superfamily (IPR011993/G3DSA:2.30.29.30, residues 40-145), a SNAP47 PH-like domain (IPR060052/PF28207, residues 256-353), and belongs to the synaptosomal-associated protein 47 family. SNAP47 is located on chromosome 1q42.13 and is predicted to enable SNAP receptor activity and syntaxin binding activity, involved in synaptic vesicle fusion to presynaptic active zone membrane and synaptic vesicle priming.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals a comprehensive network of SNARE complex proteins with high confidence scores: STX3 (0.960, Syntaxin-3, plasma membrane t-SNARE), STX12 (0.954, Syntaxin-12, endosomal t-SNARE), STX7 (0.954, Syntaxin-7, endosomal/lysosomal t-SNARE), VAMP2 (0.945, Vesicle-associated membrane protein 2, synaptic v-SNARE), SEC22B (0.927, Vesicle-trafficking protein SEC22b, ER-Golgi SNARE), SNAP29 (0.905, Synaptosomal-associated protein 29, plasma membrane t-SNARE), VAMP4 (0.815, Vesicle-associated membrane protein 4, endosomal v-SNARE), STX18 (0.690, Syntaxin-18, ER t-SNARE), and STX1A (0.582, Syntaxin-1A, neuronal t-SNARE). These interactions confirm SNAP47 functions within the core SNARE fusion machinery across multiple trafficking pathways.\n\nHPA expression reveals SNAP47 has low tissue specificity and is detected in all tissues, consistent with a ubiquitous housekeeping role in vesicle trafficking. Subcellular localization includes cytosol with predicted membrane association. The protein is classified as a predicted intracellular and membrane protein with cell type-enhanced specificity at the single-cell level. SNAP47 is located in the BLOC-1 complex (Biogenesis of Lysosome-related Organelles Complex 1), which is involved in trafficking to lysosome-related organelles.\n\nClinVar documents 41 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including large copy number gains (1q21.1-44, 1q42.11-43, 1q41-44 amplifications) and loss-of-function mutations: nonsense variant (c.61C>T p.Arg21Ter), splice site variants (c.554+4A>C, c.969+2T>C, c.970-50C>G), missense variant (c.1244C>T p.Ala415Val), and intronic SNVs. The variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity. While no specific Mendelian disease is annotated, 1q CNVs are associated with developmental delay, intellectual disability, congenital anomalies, and thrombocytopenia. Given SNAP47's role in vesicle fusion and BLOC-1 complex membership, loss-of-function may disrupt lysosome-related organelle biogenesis, potentially causing Hermansky-Pudlak syndrome-like phenotypes (bleeding disorders, oculocutaneous albinism).\n\nConservation is strong across mammals: human (Q5SQN1, 464 aa), mouse (Q8R570, 413 aa), rat (Q6P6S0, 419 aa), indicating an essential function in vesicle trafficking conserved in mammals.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: SNAP47 functions as a non-canonical SNARE protein that participates in multiple vesicle fusion pathways beyond classical neurotransmission. The dual SNARE motifs (N-terminal and C-terminal) suggest SNAP47 may form unusual SNARE complexes or act as a regulatory SNARE that modulates fusion kinetics. The GRAM and PH-like domains mediate membrane association through phosphoinositide binding, targeting SNAP47 to specific organelle membranes. Through interactions with STX3, STX12, STX7, and VAMP2, SNAP47 may participate in endosomal trafficking, lysosome-related organelle biogenesis, and synaptic vesicle priming. BLOC-1 complex membership links SNAP47 to Hermansky-Pudlak syndrome pathways, suggesting roles in platelet dense granule and melanosome biogenesis. The ubiquitous expression indicates SNAP47 performs essential housekeeping functions in constitutive secretion and endocytic recycling. 1q CNVs cause SNAP47 dosage imbalance, disrupting vesicle trafficking and contributing to neurodevelopmental phenotypes through impaired synaptic function and organelle biogenesis. SNAP47 represents a candidate gene for trafficking disorders and a potential modifier of Hermansky-Pudlak syndrome.
