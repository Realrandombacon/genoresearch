## Description
FAM90A10 (Gene ID: 441328, UniProt A6NDY2) is a 464 amino acid primate-specific protein located on chromosome 8p23.1 (NC_000008.11, positions 7768976-7771987, ~3kb region), belonging to subfamily II of the FAM90A gene family that originated from multiple duplications and rearrangements during primate evolution (Bosch et al., 2007). InterPro domain analysis reveals membership in the FAM90 family (IPR039213, positions 2-462, spanning nearly the entire protein), a conserved zinc knuckle domain (IPR041670/PF15288, positions 41-82) characteristic of nucleic acid-binding proteins, and classification within the PROTEIN FAM90A1 family (PTHR16035, positions 2-462). The zinc knuckle domain typically coordinates zinc ions through cysteine/histidine residues and mediates RNA or DNA binding in transcriptional regulation contexts. AlphaFold structure prediction (AF-A6NDY2-F1) shows very low confidence (global pLDDT 50.0), indicating the protein is predominantly intrinsically disordered with no stable globular fold - this is consistent with proteins functioning through flexible protein-protein or protein-nucleic acid interactions rather than enzymatic catalysis. Human Protein Atlas classifies FAM90A10 as a predicted intracellular protein localized to nucleoplasm and nucleoli, but RNA expression was not detected across surveyed tissues, cancers, blood cells, or single cell types, suggesting extremely restricted expression patterns possibly limited to specific developmental stages, cell types not captured in HPA panels, or condition-induced expression. STRING database identifies 10 protein-protein interactions revealing connections to deubiquitination, antimicrobial defense, and proline-rich signaling: USP17L7 (score 0.720, ubiquitin carboxyl-terminal hydrolase 17-like 7, highest confidence interaction - deubiquitinating enzyme), USP17L1 (0.661, related deubiquitinase), DEFB106A (0.559, beta-defensin 106A, antimicrobial peptide), DEFB107A (0.479, beta-defensin 107A), PRR23B (0.447, proline rich 23B), PRR23A (0.446), PRR23D2 (0.446), PRR23D1 (0.446), USP17L4 (0.408), and DEFB108B (0.405). The USP17L family interactions are particularly significant as these deubiquitinases regulate protein stability, cell cycle progression, and immune responses through removal of ubiquitin chains from target proteins. ClinVar contains 38 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variants affecting the FAM90A10 locus on chromosome 8p23.1, including large deletions (chr8:7296514-12636756x1 ~5.3Mb, chr8:449893-23854904x1 ~23.4Mb, chr8:241530-7895064x1 ~7.7Mb, chr8:2475295-27504279x1 ~25Mb, chr8:241530-10458484x1 ~10.2Mb) and duplications (chr8:241530-7895064x3, chr8:226452-12698554x3 ~12.5Mb, chr8:226452-145068712x3 ~144.8Mb whole arm, chr8:208048-145070385x3). All variants have uncertain clinical significance (?) but are classified as pathogenic, suggesting association with 8p23.1 deletion/duplication syndrome phenotypes including congenital heart defects, developmental delay, intellectual disability, and behavioral abnormalities. Conservation: FAM90A genes are primate-specific with no orthologs in mouse or other non-primate mammals, reflecting recent evolutionary emergence through gene duplication. Functional hypothesis: FAM90A10 functions as an intrinsically disordered nuclear protein that regulates gene expression through dynamic protein-protein interactions, potentially recruiting USP17 deubiquitinases to modulate chromatin-associated protein stability or serving as a scaffold for transcriptional complexes. The zinc knuckle domain suggests direct nucleic acid binding capability, positioning FAM90A10 as a potential transcriptional regulator or RNA-processing factor. Interaction with defensins (DEFB family) may indicate roles in linking nuclear processes to immune response pathways. Disease mechanism: Copy number alterations at 8p23.1 disrupt FAM90A10 dosage along with neighboring genes (including GATA4, SOX7, TNKS), contributing to congenital heart disease, diaphragmatic hernia, and neurodevelopmental disorders through combined haploinsufficiency effects. Priority experiments include identification of nucleic acid binding targets via SELEX or CLIP-seq, characterization of USP17 interaction interfaces, determination of expression patterns in primate-specific tissues, assessment of deubiquitination substrate effects, and investigation of developmental roles in primate models.
