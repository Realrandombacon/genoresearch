# GDAP1L1: Brain-Enriched Glutathione S-Transferase Regulating Mitochondrial Dynamics and Neuronal Differentiation via FIS1-MFN2-MFF Interaction Network

## Description
GDAP1L1 (Ganglioside-Induced Differentiation-Associated Protein 1-Like 1) is a 367 amino acid endoplasmic reticulum-associated protein (UniProt Q96MZ0) with a well-folded glutathione S-transferase (GST) architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 77.2, high confidence), functioning as a mitochondrial dynamics regulator essential for neuronal differentiation, neurite sprouting, and maintenance of mitochondrial fission-fusion balance in the central nervous system.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals GDAP1L1 contains a bipartite GST fold composed of an N-terminal thioredoxin-like domain (IPR004045/PF13409/PS50404, residues 45-126/58-120) with glutaredoxin superfamily homology (G3DSA:3.40.30.10, residues 44-152), and a C-terminal alpha-helical GST domain (IPR010987/PF13410/PS50405, residues 174-341/230-304). The protein belongs to the Mitochondrial Dynamics and Biodegradation GST-Related family (PTHR44188, residues 30-366) and adopts the canonical GST_N_GDAP1 fold (cd03052, residues 47-119). Unlike classical GSTs with enzymatic detoxification activity, GDAP1L1 likely functions as a scaffold or redox sensor, with the thioredoxin-like domain potentially coordinating glutathione or mediating disulfide exchange.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals GDAP1L1 functions within the mitochondrial fission-fusion machinery: strongest interactions with FIS1 (0.553, fission mitochondrial 1, outer membrane fission adaptor), MFN2 (0.546, mitofusin 2, mitochondrial fusion GTPase), MFF (0.530, mitochondrial fission factor, DRP1 receptor), TMEM151A (0.487, transmembrane protein 151A), DNM1L (0.455, dynamin-1-like protein/DRP1, mitochondrial fission GTPase), FBXL16 (0.453, F-box and leucine-rich repeat protein 16, ubiquitin ligase), DNM1 (0.441, dynamin-1, synaptic vesicle endocytosis), RUNDC3A (0.435), CPXM1 (0.433), and TMEM47 (0.428). The FIS1-MFN2-MFF-DNM1L network is the core mitochondrial dynamics apparatus, indicating GDAP1L1 directly regulates mitochondrial morphology.\n\nHPA expression shows strong brain enrichment (61.9 nTPM) with secondary expression in pituitary gland (17.5 nTPM), classified as group enriched with cell type-enhanced specificity. Subcellular localization includes endoplasmic reticulum, consistent with roles in ER-mitochondria contact sites (MAMs) where lipid transfer and calcium signaling occur. The protein is classified as predicted intracellular and membrane protein.\n\nClinVar documents 9 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, exclusively large chromosome 20 copy number variations including deletions (20q11.21-13.12, 20q13.12) and duplications (20p13-q13.33). These CNVs suggest dosage sensitivity potentially causing neurodevelopmental disorders, peripheral neuropathy, or mitochondrial encephalopathy through disrupted mitochondrial dynamics.\n\nConservation is evident from mouse (Q8VE33, 370 aa), zebrafish (B8A514, 367 aa), and other vertebrates, indicating ancient evolutionary conservation of mitochondrial regulation mechanisms.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: GDAP1L1 acts as a redox-sensitive scaffold at ER-mitochondria contact sites that coordinates mitochondrial fission-fusion balance. The GST fold, while catalytically degenerate, binds glutathione to sense cellular redox state. Upon oxidative stress or differentiation signals, GDAP1L1 recruits FIS1 and MFF to activate DRP1 (DNM1L)-mediated fission, or interacts with MFN2 to promote fusion. The ER localization suggests GDAP1L1 functions at MAMs to couple mitochondrial dynamics to calcium signaling and lipid metabolism. Brain enrichment reflects high neuronal demand for mitochondrial trafficking along axons and dendrites. During ganglioside-induced differentiation, GDAP1L1 upregulation drives neurite sprouting by fragmenting mitochondria for distribution to growing processes. CNV-mediated haploinsufficiency causes mitochondrial elongation, impaired axonal transport, and neurodegeneration reminiscent of GDAP1-associated Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease type 4A. The 20q13.12 locus is amplified in cancers, suggesting GDAP1L1 overexpression confers metabolic advantage through enhanced mitochondrial biogenesis.
