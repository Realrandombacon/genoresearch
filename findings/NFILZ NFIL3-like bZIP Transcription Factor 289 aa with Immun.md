## Description
NFILZ (Nuclear Factor Interleukin-3-regulated protein Like Zinc finger, alias E4 promoter-binding protein 4) is a 289-amino acid transcription factor encoded on chromosome 19p13.3, belonging to the bZIP (basic leucine zipper) transcription factor superfamily. InterPro analysis confirms NFILZ contains a canonical basic leucine zipper domain (IPR004827, residues 40-102; PF07716, residues 42-92; PS00036, residues 48-62; PS50217, residues 42-92) that mediates DNA binding and homo/heterodimerization, placing it in the NFIL3-like protein family (IPR047106, residues 41-98; IPR047229, residues 13-284; PTHR15284). The bZIP architecture consists of a basic region for DNA recognition (typically binding AP-1-like or E-box motifs) coupled with a leucine zipper coiled-coil for dimerization. AlphaFold predicts a medium-confidence structure (global pLDDT 58.6), consistent with a partially structured protein containing an ordered bZIP domain flanked by intrinsically disordered regions that may serve as transactivation domains or protein interaction modules.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals an unexpected but compelling interaction network centered on mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation, with highest confidence for COX5A (0.613, cytochrome c oxidase subunit 5A), MT-CO2 (0.592, mitochondrial cytochrome c oxidase II), COX7C (0.545, cytochrome c oxidase subunit 7C), COX5B (0.532, cytochrome c oxidase subunit 5B), C1orf198 (0.532, mitochondrial translation optimization factor), UQCRB (0.484, ubiquinol-cytochrome c reductase binding protein), COX4I2 (0.482, cytochrome c oxidase subunit 4I2), COX4I1 (0.482, cytochrome c oxidase subunit 4I1), MT-CO3 (0.475, mitochondrial cytochrome c oxidase III), and COX6A1 (0.465, cytochrome c oxidase subunit 6A1). This interaction profile is highly unusual for a bZIP transcription factor and suggests NFILZ may have a dual role: canonical nuclear transcriptional regulation AND direct mitochondrial association, potentially regulating oxidative phosphorylation gene expression or physically interacting with respiratory chain complexes. This mirrors emerging paradigms of 'moonlighting' transcription factors with extra-nuclear functions.\n\nHuman Protein Atlas classifies NFILZ as 'tissue enhanced' with top expression in testis (1.5 nTPM) and immune cell enrichment in blood cells. The testis specificity is notable, as this tissue expresses many transcription factors involved in spermatogenesis and meiotic regulation. The immune cell enrichment aligns with the NFIL3 family connection, as NFIL3 (E4BP4) is a well-characterized regulator of immune cell development (NK cells, dendritic cells, macrophages). The single-cell classification as 'group enriched' suggests expression in specific immune cell subsets rather than ubiquitous immune expression.\n\nClinVar reports 2 pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants, both structural variants on chromosome 19p13.3-13.2: a single allele duplication (VCV000545228) and a copy number gain spanning chr19:1972245-9648879 (VCV000059082). While no specific monogenic disease is named solely for NFILZ, the 19p13.3 region contains multiple dosage-sensitive genes, and CNVs in this locus are associated with neurodevelopmental disorders, growth abnormalities, and immune dysregulation. The pathogenic classification suggests NFILZ dosage may be clinically relevant.\n\nLiterature analysis (1 paper) confirms NFILZ as a genuine dark gene with minimal characterization. The single historical reference (1924, German language, vascular system pathology) provides no functional insight, indicating NFILZ remains functionally uncharacterized despite its clear domain architecture and clinical variants.\n\nWe hypothesize that NFILZ functions as a dual-compartment bZIP transcription factor with roles in immune regulation and metabolic control. The NFIL3 homology suggests it may regulate similar target genes (circadian rhythm, immune cytokines, cell survival) but with distinct specificity or in different cell types (testis, specific immune subsets). The mitochondrial protein interaction network raises two non-exclusive possibilities: (1) NFILZ transcriptionally regulates nuclear-encoded mitochondrial genes (COX subunits, UQCRB), coupling immune activation to metabolic reprogramming, or (2) NFILZ physically localizes to mitochondria, directly modulating respiratory chain assembly or activity—a phenomenon observed for some transcription factors (e.g., NF-κB, p53). The testis expression suggests a role in spermatogenesis, potentially regulating meiotic gene expression or germ cell metabolism. The 19p13.3 CNVs may cause dosage-sensitive phenotypes affecting immune function, fertility, or neurodevelopment. NFILZ represents a candidate regulator of immune-metabolic crosstalk, a potential mitochondrial-associated transcription factor, and an uncharacterized contributor to 19p13.3 deletion/duplication syndrome phenotypes.
