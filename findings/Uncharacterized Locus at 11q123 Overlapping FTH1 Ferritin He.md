## Description
LOC399900 is an uncharacterized genomic locus on chromosome 11q12.3 (NC_000011.10:61967293-61968878) spanning approximately 1.6 kb. Comprehensive database searches reveal no evidence of canonical protein-coding function: no UniProt accession was identified, precluding InterPro domain analysis and AlphaFold structure prediction. STRING database returned no protein interaction partners, confirming absence of characterized interactome. Human Protein Atlas could not resolve this locus to an Ensembl ID, indicating no detectable protein expression in surveyed tissues. However, ClinVar analysis reveals 1 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variant in this region: a single nucleotide variant (SNV) at FTH1 c.-164A>T (NM_002032.3), located in the 5′ untranslated region (5′ UTR) of FTH1 (Ferritin Heavy Chain 1). FTH1 encodes the heavy chain subunit of ferritin, the major intracellular iron storage protein complex critical for iron homeostasis, oxidative stress protection, and iron detoxification. Mutations in FTH1 cause neurodegeneration with brain iron accumulation (NBIA) and are associated with restless legs syndrome, hyperferritinemia-cataract syndrome, and Parkinson disease. The c.-164A>T variant in the 5′ UTR likely affects FTH1 mRNA translation efficiency, stability, or iron-responsive element (IRE) function, disrupting post-transcriptional iron regulation. LOC399900 likely represents: (1) an alternative FTH1 transcript isoform with distinct 5′ UTR, (2) a long non-coding RNA (lncRNA) overlapping FTH1 regulatory sequences, (3) a cis-regulatory element (enhancer/promoter) controlling FTH1 expression, or (4) an upstream open reading frame (uORF) regulating FTH1 translation. The presence of a pathogenic 5′ UTR variant despite absence of protein evidence indicates this locus functions as a critical post-transcriptional regulatory element for FTH1, where sequence alteration disrupts iron homeostasis through translational dysregulation rather than protein structure disruption.
