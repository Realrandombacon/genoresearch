# Olfactomedin-like Protein OLFML2B: Extracellular Matrix Modulator Driving Cancer EMT and Immune Infiltration

## Description
OLFML2B (Olfactomedin Like 2B) is a 750-amino acid protein belonging to the olfactomedin domain-containing family, characterized by a C-terminal olfactomedin-like domain (IPR003112, positions 492-750; PF02191, 492-748; PS51132, 493-750; SM00284, 496-750) within a larger olfactomedin-like domain-containing architecture (IPR050605, 66-748). The olfactomedin domain is known to mediate protein-protein interactions in extracellular matrix organization and cell adhesion contexts. STRING interactions reveal connections to extracellular matrix remodeling and cell adhesion regulators: ADAMTS2 (0.457, procollagen N-peptidase), COL5A2 (0.449, type V collagen), CTHRC1 (0.428, collagen triple helix repeat containing), MXRA5 (0.419, matrix remodeling associated), ISLR (0.432, immunoglobulin superfamily containing leucine-rich repeat), TOR3A/TOR1AIP1 (0.449/0.430, torsin-associated nuclear envelope proteins), alongside uncharacterized partners C1orf226 (0.462), TMEM220 (0.438), and NOS1AP (0.409, nitric oxide synthase adaptor), suggesting roles in ECM organization, cell migration, and potentially nuclear envelope dynamics. HPA data shows low tissue specificity with detection in many tissues, group-enriched expression in blood cells, cell type-enhanced specificity, cytosolic localization, and classification as both a predicted intracellular and secreted protein, indicating potential dual functionality. ClinVar contains 26 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number gains/losses spanning 1q21.1-25.3 regions and missense variants (p.Asn207Ser, p.Gln411His), though specific monogenic disease phenotypes remain under characterization (significance marked '?' for most); the genomic location overlaps with regions implicated in developmental disorders and cancer susceptibility. AlphaFold structure (AF-Q68BL8-F1) predicts medium confidence folding (pLDDT 62.4) consistent with a partially structured protein containing both ordered olfactomedin domains and flexible linker regions typical of extracellular matrix-interacting proteins. With 44 papers, OLFML2B represents a 'gray' gene transitioning from dark: pan-cancer analyses (2022) identify it as a robust prognostic biomarker across multiple tumor types including bladder cancer (7 cohorts validated), gastric cancer, lung adenocarcinoma, and squamous cell carcinoma, where overexpression promotes epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT), metastasis, invasion, and correlates with immune cell infiltration patterns. The mechanistic hypothesis posits that OLFML2B functions as an extracellular matrix scaffold or modulator that facilitates tumor cell migration and invasion through ADAMTS2-mediated collagen processing and integrin signaling, while its secreted form may regulate immune cell recruitment to the tumor microenvironment, making it a potential therapeutic target for blocking metastatic progression.
