## Description
FRRS1L encodes a 293 aa ferric chelate reductase 1 like protein (UniProt Q9P0K9), functioning as an auxiliary regulatory protein for AMPA-type glutamate receptors (AMPAR) with a DOMON domain architecture. The protein contains a DOMON domain (IPR005018, PF03351, PS50836, SM00664, aa118-234/143-232) characterized as a possible catecholamine-binding domain present in eukaryotic proteins, belongs to FRRS1L family (IPR042789, PTHR46902, aa13-293), and has GO annotation for regulation of glutamate receptor signaling pathway (GO:1900449). AlphaFold prediction shows high confidence structure (pLDDT 79.5), indicating a well-folded single-domain protein with stable architecture suitable for protein-protein interactions. HPA shows tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in brain (26.9 nTPM), classified as predicted membrane protein and disease-related gene with epilepsy involvement. STRING interactions reveal unexpected mitochondrial ribosome connections: MRPL23 (0.907, mitochondrial ribosomal protein L23—highest confidence), MRPL46 (0.886), ABITRAM (0.882, mitochondrial ribosome-associated membrane protein), MRPL16 (0.881), MRPL3 (0.879), MRPL19 (0.873), MRPL47 (0.872), MRPL4 (0.869), MRPL22 (0.865), MRPS15 (0.860, mitochondrial ribosomal protein S15). ClinVar contains 92 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including nonsense mutations (p.Trp181Ter in DOMON domain), frameshifts (p.Arg108_Tyr109insTer, p.Arg40fs, p.Phe107fs), deletions (c.327del, c.-99del, c.321del), duplications (c.116dup), and missense variants (p.Glu194Gly, p.Asp60Tyr, p.Leu228Pro, p.Asp243Tyr) distributed across the protein, though no specific disease name is assigned in ClinVar. Literature (23 papers) documents FRRS1L roles in: (1) developmental and epileptic encephalopathy-37 (DEE37) with biallelic pathogenic variants causing severe epileptic encephalopathy; (2) continuous spikes and waves during sleep (CSWS) syndrome; (3) choreoathetosis movement disorder associated with FRRS1L deficiency; (4) AMPAR auxiliary protein function regulating glutamate receptor signaling; (5) Purkinje cell morphological and functional disorders underlying movement phenotype; (6) ferriheme overload and hyperpigmentation in Lanping black bone sheep (cross-species iron metabolism role); (7) sulthiame treatment response in FRRS1L encephalopathy. FRRS1L functions as an AMPAR auxiliary protein—the DOMON domain (aa118-234) likely mediates protein-protein interactions with AMPAR subunits or trafficking machinery, modulating receptor surface expression, gating properties, or synaptic localization. Brain-enriched expression (26.9 nTPM) and epilepsy classification confirm CNS-specific function in glutamatergic neurotransmission. The 92 ClinVar variants demonstrate extreme mutational sensitivity—nonsense/frameshift variants cause loss-of-function leading to DEE37, while missense variants in the DOMON domain (p.Trp181Ter, p.Glu194Gly, p.Leu228Pro, p.Asp243Tyr) likely disrupt protein folding or AMPAR binding. Purkinje cell involvement explains choreoathetosis—FRRS1L deficiency may impair AMPAR-mediated synaptic transmission in cerebellar Purkinje cells, disrupting motor coordination circuits. The STRING mitochondrial ribosome network (MRPL23/46/16/3/19/47/4/22, MRPS15; 0.860-0.907) is highly unexpected for an AMPAR auxiliary protein—this may indicate: (1) FRRS1L has dual functions in mitochondrial translation and synaptic signaling; (2) mitochondrial dysfunction contributes to DEE37 pathogenesis through energy metabolism defects in neurons; (3) FRRS1L localizes to both synaptic and mitochondrial compartments. ABITRAM (0.882) connection supports mitochondrial membrane association. The ferriheme overload phenotype in sheep suggests FRRS1L may have ancestral iron metabolism functions retained in some species—the DOMON domain's catecholamine-binding capability could coordinate iron or heme groups. CSWS syndrome indicates FRRS1L regulates sleep-related neuronal excitability patterns. Sulthiame responsiveness provides therapeutic insight—carbonic anhydrase inhibition may compensate for FRRS1L deficiency by modulating neuronal pH and excitability. The DOMON domain architecture represents a unique AMPAR regulator distinct from known auxiliary proteins (TARP, CNIH, GSG1L)—FRRS1L may define a novel class of glutamate receptor modulators with mitochondrial connections.
