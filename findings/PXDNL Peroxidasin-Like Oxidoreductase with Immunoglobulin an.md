## Description
PXDNL (A1KZ92) encodes a 1463 amino acid probable oxidoreductase belonging to the peroxidasin family that functions as a multifunctional enzyme involved in extracellular matrix remodeling, cell motility regulation, and apoptosis modulation. InterPro analysis reveals complex multi-domain architecture characteristic of peroxidasin family proteins: an N-terminal ribonuclease inhibitor-like domain (G3DSA:3.80.10.10, positions 21-232), multiple leucine-rich repeats (IPR001611, positions 53-110 & 123-182; IPR003591 typical subtype at 53-72, 73-96, 97-120), three immunoglobulin subtype 2 domains (IPR003598, positions 246-312, 342-405, 431-495), a central animal heme peroxidase catalytic domain (cd09826, positions 851-1286; G3DSA:1.10.640.10 haem peroxidase superfamily at 705-1299), and a C-terminal VWFC domain (IPR001007, positions 1393-1451) mediating protein-protein interactions. AlphaFold predicts a well-folded structure (pLDDT 81.4) consistent with stable enzymatic activity and scaffold functions. STRING interactions identify high-confidence partners linking PXDNL to metabolism, cytoskeleton, and apoptosis: GAPDH (0.924), ACTB (0.924), AKT1 (0.875), CASP3 (0.858), CCK (0.856), PXDN (0.848, the canonical peroxidasin homolog), ANXA5 (0.821), ALB (0.733), MAPK3 (0.628), and CYCS (0.588). HPA shows tissue-enriched expression with highest levels in heart muscle (43.4 nTPM), localized to nucleoplasm and cytosol, with molecular functions including endonuclease, hydrolase, nuclease, and oxidoreductase activities. ClinVar contains 29 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number gains/losses on chromosome 8 and missense mutations (p.Pro819Arg), though specific disease associations remain undefined. Literature (34 papers) demonstrates PXDNL promotes invasive breast carcinoma progression, activates urothelial bladder carcinoma cell motility through Wnt/β-catenin pathway, acts as a fibroblast-specific driver of sarcopenia, and associates with acute primary angle closure. The mechanistic hypothesis: PXDNL functions as a heme-dependent peroxidase that catalyzes oxidative crosslinking of extracellular matrix components through its peroxidase domain while simultaneously engaging intracellular signaling via immunoglobulin and LRR domains; the protein bridges extracellular matrix remodeling with intracellular pathways (AKT1, MAPK3, CASP3) to regulate cell survival, motility, and apoptosis; in cancer contexts, PXDNL overexpression activates Wnt/β-catenin signaling to promote invasion and metastasis, while in muscle tissue it may contribute to age-related sarcopenia through fibroblast-mediated matrix stiffening; copy number alterations disrupt the balance between oxidoreductase activity and scaffold functions, contributing to developmental and degenerative phenotypes.
