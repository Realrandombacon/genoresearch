## Description
FOXR1 (Forkhead Box Protein R1) is a 292-amino acid nuclear transcription factor with 40 publications confirming its partially characterized status. The protein contains a conserved Forkhead/winged helix DNA-binding domain (IPR001766, positions 171-272; PF00250, positions 172-261; cd20036, positions 171-259) with winged helix-like DNA-binding domain superfamily architecture (G3DSA:1.10.10.10, IPR036388, IPR036390) that mediates sequence-specific DNA binding to forkhead recognition elements. AlphaFold predicts a partially structured protein (pLDDT 66.3), consistent with transcription factors containing ordered DNA-binding domains connected by intrinsically disordered transactivation regions. HPA reveals testis-enriched expression (4.1 nTPM), cancer-enhanced expression pattern, and nucleoplasm/cytosol localization with transcription regulation function. ClinVar documents 28 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants (primarily chromosome 11q23.3-24.2/25 copy number gains, deletions, and duplications), though specific disease phenotypes beyond neurodevelopmental disorders remain uncharacterized. STRING interactions reveal FOXR1 functions within the NuA4/TIP60 chromatin remodeling complex with very high confidence connections to MYC (0.847), KAT5/TIP60 (0.844), EP400 (0.843), MRGBP (0.842), BRD8 (0.837), MORF4L1 (0.836), EPC1 (0.834), MEAF6 (0.832), YEATS4/GAS41 (0.831), and EPC2 (0.809). Recent studies (2023-2025) established FOXR1 deletion causing microcephaly with cortical and hippocampal hypoplasia, FOXR1 required for normal early embryogenesis but dispensable for male fertility in mice, YAP1::FOXR1 fusion in intraosseous composite hemangioendothelioma, and PHF1::FOXR1/FOXR2 fusions in malignant ossifying fibromyxoid tumors. We hypothesize FOXR1 functions as a neurodevelopmental transcription factor that recruits the NuA4/TIP60 histone acetyltransferase complex to forkhead target gene promoters during cortical and hippocampal development, with KAT5-mediated H4 acetylation enabling chromatin opening for neuronal differentiation gene expression. The MYC interaction suggests involvement in proliferative transcription programs, while recurrent YAP1::FOXR1 and PHF1::FOXR1 fusions in rare mesenchymal tumors indicate oncogenic potential when FOXR1's DNA-binding domain is fused to strong transactivation domains (YAP1) or chromatin readers (PHF1), driving aberrant transcriptional programs in sarcomas. Testis enrichment hints at additional roles in germ cell development consistent with FOX family functions.
