## Description
PLPPR3 (Phospholipid phosphatase related 3), also known as PRG-2 or LPPR3, is a 718-amino acid integral membrane protein belonging to the lipid phosphate phosphatase (LPP) family. It functions as a critical regulator of bioactive lipid signaling in the nervous system, specifically modulating lysophosphatidic acid (LPA) and sphingosine-1-phosphate (S1P) levels to control neurite outgrowth, axonal guidance, and synaptic plasticity. Unlike canonical LPPs, PLPPR3 may act primarily through protein-protein interactions or non-catalytic scaffolding roles, as recent literature suggests its phosphorylation state controls synaptic vesicle release rather than direct lipid dephosphorylation.\n\nStructural analysis via InterPro reveals a conserved PAP2_wunen domain (cd03384, residues 129-278) and the Phosphatidic acid phosphatase type 2 superfamily (IPR000326, PF01569), characteristic of enzymes that dephosphorylate lipid substrates. However, the AlphaFold model (AF-Q6T4P5-F1) predicts a globally low-confidence structure (pLDDT 58.5), indicating significant intrinsically disordered regions flanking the core catalytic domain, which may facilitate dynamic interactions with synaptic machinery. STRING interaction data places PLPPR3 in a network with ADCY1 (adenylate cyclase 1, score 0.655), PLPPR1 (0.590), and GNAS (0.562), linking it to cAMP signaling pathways crucial for neuronal development. Additional partners include STX16 (syntaxin 16) and GRIN3B, suggesting a role in vesicle trafficking and glutamatergic transmission.\n\nClinVar records 26 pathogenic or likely pathogenic copy number variants (gains and losses) spanning the 19p13.3 locus, implicating dosage sensitivity in neurodevelopmental disorders, although specific syndromic phenotypes remain under-characterized compared to other LPP family members. While HPA tissue expression data is unavailable for this specific entry, literature confirms high enrichment in the central nervous system, particularly in developing neurons and presynaptic terminals. With only 9 publications, PLPPR3 represents a genuine 'dark gene' where the precise mechanistic switch between catalytic activity and scaffolding function in synaptic regulation remains to be fully elucidated.
