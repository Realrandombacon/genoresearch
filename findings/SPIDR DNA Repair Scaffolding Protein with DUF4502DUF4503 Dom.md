## Description
SPIDR (Scaffold Protein Involved in DNA Repair, also known as C8orf31, UniProt Q14159) is a 915 amino acid nucleoplasmic scaffolding protein with moderate characterization (119 papers) that functions as a DNA repair scaffold mediating homologous recombination through BLM-RAD51 complex assembly, oxidative stress response, and cell stress translational suppression. The protein contains two domains of unknown function: DUF4502 (IPR028026/PF14950, residues 11-369) and DUF4503 (IPR028032/PF14951, residues 521-905), belongs to the DNA repair-scaffolding protein family (IPR053054/PTHR34347, residues 11-908), placing it in the homologous recombination repair machinery. STRING interactions reveal high-confidence partnerships with DNA repair proteins: SWSAP1 (0.899, SWI5-SFR1 homolog 1-associated protein 1), RAD51 (0.884, DNA repair protein RAD51 homolog 1), FIGNL1 (0.870, fidgetin-like protein 1), RMI1 (0.680, RecQ-mediated genome instability protein 1), BLM (0.671, Bloom syndrome helicase), RMI2 (0.663, RecQ-mediated genome instability protein 2), TOP3A (0.647, DNA topoisomerase 3-alpha), ZSWIM7 (0.637, zinc finger SWIM-type containing protein 7), XRCC3 (0.628, X-ray repair cross-complementing protein 3), and PLA2G10 (0.617, phospholipase A2 group X), placing SPIDR in the BLM-RAD51 homologous recombination repair network. HPA data shows ubiquitous expression (detected in all tissues), low tissue and cancer specificity, nucleoplasmic localization, DNA damage/DNA recombination/DNA repair biological processes, and disease variant involvement. ClinVar contains 35 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including frameshift mutations (c.2104dup p.Cys702fs, c.902_903del p.Val301fs, c.1727dup p.Ala577fs), nonsense mutation (c.814C>T p.Arg272Ter), splice site variant (c.1685+3G>C), and copy number gains at 8p11.23-q11.23/8p21.2-q21.3/8p23.3-q24.3/8p11.23-q11.21, though no specific diseases are directly assigned—suggesting potential cancer predisposition, genomic instability, or DNA repair disorder phenotypes. AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 62.0) indicates medium confidence with partially structured protein containing both ordered domains and intrinsically disordered regions typical of scaffolding proteins. Functional hypothesis: SPIDR acts as a molecular scaffold localized to the nucleoplasm where it facilitates homologous recombination repair by bridging BLM helicase and RAD51 recombinase complexes through its DUF4502/DUF4503 domains, where recent 2024-2025 research demonstrates: (1) SPIDR enables multiplexed mapping of RNA-protein interactions and uncovers mechanism for selective translational suppression upon cell stress (2025, 13 citations)—revealing unexpected role in stress response beyond DNA repair, (2) Nuclear respiratory factor 1 (NRF1) regulates super enhancer-controlled SPIDR to protect hepatocellular carcinoma cells from oxidative stress (2024, 2 citations)—establishing role in cancer cell survival, (3) STRING partners (BLM helicase, RAD51 recombinase, RMI1/2, TOP3A, SWSAP1, FIGNL1, XRCC3) are all established homologous recombination repair proteins confirming SPIDR functions in the BTR complex (BLM-TOP3A-RMI1/2) pathway for dissolution of double Holliday junctions, with ubiquitous expression suggesting essential housekeeping DNA repair function and 35 ClinVar variants at 8p11.23-q24.3 potentially contributing to cancer predisposition, genomic instability syndromes, or DNA repair disorders through defective homologous recombination, making SPIDR a potential therapeutic target for hepatocellular carcinoma and cancers with defective DNA repair through scaffolding protein pathway modulation.
