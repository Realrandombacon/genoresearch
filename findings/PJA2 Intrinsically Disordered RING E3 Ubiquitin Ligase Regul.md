# PJA2: Intrinsically Disordered RING E3 Ubiquitin Ligase Regulating Ciliary Signaling, Interferon Response, and Cancer Progression via HDAC2 and KSR1 Degradation

**Date:** 2026-05-01T20:47:46.590160

**Quality Score:** 4/10 (MODERATE)

**Confidence:** LOW-MEDIUM

## Description
PJA2 (Praja ring finger ubiquitin ligase 2, also known as Praja-2) encodes a 708 amino acid E3 ubiquitin-protein ligase (UniProt O43164) characterized by extensive intrinsic disorder and a C-terminal catalytic RING domain. InterPro analysis reveals a canonical RING-type zinc finger domain (IPR001841, PF13639, cd16465, PS50089, SM00184, residues 633-678) belonging to the H2 subclass found in Praja proteins. This RING domain (G3DSA:3.30.40.10, SSF57850, residues 596-682) facilitates E2 ubiquitin-conjugating enzyme binding and substrate ubiquitination. The protein is classified within the NEP1-interacting protein-like 2 family (PTHR46151, residues 588-677). Notably, ~85% of the protein sequence lacks defined secondary structure, consistent with its function as a flexible scaffold for recruiting diverse substrates.\n\nAlphaFold prediction shows low global confidence (pLDDT 48.0) across all 708 aa, confirming PJA2 is predominantly an intrinsically disordered protein (IDP). The low confidence reflects the dynamic, unstructured nature of the N-terminal and central regions, which likely serve as interaction hubs. Only the C-terminal RING domain (residues ~630-680) is expected to be structured, enabling catalytic activity while the disordered regions confer substrate specificity and regulatory plasticity.\n\nSTRING interactions reveal PJA2 operates at the center of ubiquitin-dependent proteostasis and signaling networks with high confidence scores: UBE2D2 (0.821) and UBE2D3 (0.640) are E2 ubiquitin-conjugating enzymes that directly partner with RING ligases to transfer ubiquitin; MAGED1 (0.775) is a melanoma-associated antigen involved in neurodevelopment; MFHAS1 (0.738) is a related RING protein (Malignant Fibrous Histiocytoma Amplified Sequence); UBE2K (0.624) is an E2 enzyme specific for polyubiquitin chain elongation; UBXN7 (0.595) and UBXN1 (0.588) are UBX domain proteins linking ubiquitination to ERAD and mitochondrial quality control; FBXL17 (0.583) is an F-box ubiquitin ligase; ARHGAP36 (0.583) is a Rho GTPase activating protein; SERINC1 (0.659) is a serine incorporator. The strong UBE2D2/3 interactions confirm PJA2's active role in ubiquitin transfer.\n\nHPA expression shows PJA2 is ubiquitously detected in all tissues with low tissue specificity, indicating essential housekeeping functions. Subcellular localization is highly distinctive: microtubules, primary cilium, primary cilium transition zone, basal body, and cytosol. This ciliary enrichment suggests PJA2 regulates ciliary protein turnover and signaling. Biological processes include the Ubl (ubiquitin-like) conjugation pathway. PJA2 is classified as an enzyme, metabolic protein, and transferase.\n\nClinVar reports 27 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including missense (c.2003C>T p.Ser668Leu near the RING domain), splice deletions, and large 5q15-23.2 copy number losses/gains. While no specific monogenic disease is definitively assigned, the 5q locus overlaps with developmental disorder regions. The Ser668Leu variant lies within the critical RING domain, likely disrupting E2 binding or catalytic activity.\n\nLiterature (43 papers) reveals emerging roles in cancer suppression, interferon signaling, and ciliary regulation: Recent 2025 studies show PJA2 suppresses colorectal cancer progression by controlling HDAC2 degradation and stability—ubiquitinating HDAC2 reduces its levels, altering chromatin states to inhibit tumor growth. Another 2025 study identified aberrant PJA2-CHRM3 signaling creating therapeutic vulnerability in gastric tumors. A 2024 study demonstrated ZC3H13 promotes autophagy in bladder cancer through m6A methylation of PJA2 and subsequent ubiquitination of KSR1 (a MAPK scaffold), linking PJA2 to autophagy and MAPK signaling. A landmark 2023 study (11 citations) used TurboID proximity labeling to reveal PJA2 as a negative regulator of type I interferon signaling, likely by ubiquitinating key signaling components (e.g., STING, TBK1, or IRFs) to prevent excessive inflammation. Earlier work linked PJA2 to gastric cancer via the KDM5A-PJA2-KSR1 axis promoting M2 macrophage polarization.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: PJA2 functions as a master regulator of signal transduction through targeted ubiquitination of key signaling nodes, leveraging its intrinsically disordered architecture to engage diverse substrates. Mechanism 1: Ciliary gating—localized to the transition zone and basal body, PJA2 likely ubiquitinates ciliary membrane proteins or signaling components (e.g., GPCRs, Hedgehog pathway elements) to control ciliary entry/exit and signal amplitude. Dysfunction could lead to ciliopathies. Mechanism 2: Interferon braking—by ubiquitinating positive regulators of type I IFN signaling (potentially STING or TBK1), PJA2 prevents hyperinflammation and autoimmunity; loss of PJA2 would cause excessive IFN production. Mechanism 3: Cancer suppression via HDAC2/KSR1—PJA2 targets HDAC2 for degradation to maintain open chromatin at tumor suppressor loci, and ubiquitinates KSR1 to dampen MAPK/ERK oncogenic signaling. The m6A modification by ZC3H13 may regulate PJA2 stability or localization, adding a layer of post-transcriptional control. The intrinsic disorder allows PJA2 to act as a 'hub' protein, adapting its conformation to bind distinct substrates (HDAC2, KSR1, IFN components) in different cellular contexts. The 5q CNVs and RING domain mutations suggest PJA2 dosage and catalytic integrity are critical for neurodevelopment and cancer prevention. Therapeutic strategies could target PJA2-substrate interfaces or modulate its expression to restore balanced ubiquitination in cancer or inflammatory diseases.
