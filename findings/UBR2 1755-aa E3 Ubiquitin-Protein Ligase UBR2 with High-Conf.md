## Description
UBR2 (UniProt Q8IWV8), or ubiquitin protein ligase E3 component n-recognin 2, encodes a massive 1755-amino acid E3 ubiquitin ligase belonging to the UBR1-like E3 ligase family (IPR003126/IPR003769/IPR014719/IPR036390/IPR039164/cd16686/cd19679/G3DSA:1.10.10.2670/G3DSA:2.10.110.30/G3DSA:3.30.1390.10). The protein architecture features multiple functional domains: an N-terminal UBR-box zinc finger (IPR003126/cd19679, aa 98-167) that recognizes type-1 N-degrons (basic residues Arg/Lys at protein N-termini), a ClpS adaptor core domain (IPR003769/IPR014719, aa 223-300) for substrate binding, a winged helix DNA-binding domain superfamily region (IPR036390, aa 777-860) suggesting potential chromatin interactions, and a C-terminal RING finger H2 subclass (cd16686, aa 1107-1222) that recruits E2 ubiquitin-conjugating enzymes for catalytic activity. AlphaFold predicts high-confidence structure (global pLDDT: 84.6), indicating a well-folded multi-domain architecture consistent with complex substrate recognition and ubiquitin transfer mechanisms.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals UBR2's integration into ubiquitination and DNA repair networks: TRIP12 (0.964, HECT E3 ligase and ARF tumor suppressor regulator), RECQL4 (0.940, RecQ helicase mutated in Rothmund-Thomson syndrome), UBR4 (0.882, paralogous mega-Dalton E3 ligase), UBR1 (0.835, canonical N-end rule pathway E3), UBR5 (0.725, HECT E3 ligase), NTAN1 (0.611, N-terminal acetyltransferase competing with UBR recognition), ATE1 (0.562, arginyltransferase generating type-1 N-degrons), UBE2B (0.435, E2 conjugating enzyme), UBE2A (0.428, E2 conjugating enzyme), and WDYHV1 (0.418). This partner profile positions UBR2 at the intersection of the N-end rule pathway (UBR1/UBR4/ATE1/NTAN1), DNA repair (RECQL4), and transcriptional regulation (TRIP12).\n\nWith 146 papers but emerging mechanistic insights in immunology and genome stability, UBR2 qualifies as a dark gene with underappreciated regulatory complexity. Recent breakthrough studies reveal: (1) DUSP22 phosphatase inhibits UBR2-mediated K63-ubiquitination of Lck kinase downstream of TCR signaling, establishing UBR2 as a positive regulator of T cell activation; (2) alternative pocket discovery for N-degron binding shared between UBR1/UBR2 reveals previously unknown substrate recognition modes beyond classical type-1/type-2 degrons; (3) upregulation in acetaldehyde-treated myotubes links UBR2 to alcohol-induced fast-twitch muscle atrophy via selective protein degradation; (4) RECQL4 interaction suggests roles in DNA replication stress response and genome stability maintenance; (5) yeast Mub1 adaptor modulates UBR2 function in cell wall stress responses through multiple transcription factors. HPA data confirms ubiquitous expression across all tissues with low tissue specificity, consistent with housekeeping role in protein quality control; immune cell-enhanced expression reflects T cell signaling functions; dual nucleoplasmic and plasma membrane localization suggests both nuclear (transcription/DNA repair) and cytoplasmic (signaling) roles. ClinVar lists 14 pathogenic variants including splice site mutations (c.3242+2T>A, c.2873-4G>A) and 6p22.1-q14.1 deletions, confirming dosage sensitivity potentially linked to developmental disorders. We hypothesize UBR2 functions as a 'molecular switch' integrating multiple cellular states: the UBR-box recognizes destabilizing N-terminal residues generated by proteolytic cleavage or ATE1 arginylation, marking proteins for degradation; K63-linked ubiquitination (non-degradative) regulates signaling complexes like Lck in TCR pathways; RECQL4 interaction positions UBR2 at replication forks for DNA damage response; TRIP12 cooperation suggests coordinated regulation of tumor suppressor pathways; in muscle, UBR2 upregulation during alcohol exposure selectively degrades contractile proteins causing type II fiber atrophy; the 6p CNVs disrupt UBR2 stoichiometry relative to UBR1/UBR4, altering global N-end rule pathway flux and causing developmental defects.
