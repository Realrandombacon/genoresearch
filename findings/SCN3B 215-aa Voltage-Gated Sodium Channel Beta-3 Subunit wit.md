## Description
SCN3B (Sodium Voltage-Gated Channel Beta Subunit 3; UniProt Q9NY72) is a 215 amino acid auxiliary subunit of voltage-gated sodium channels with 120 publications, representing a moderately characterized ion channel regulator with emerging roles in cardiac arrhythmia, neurodevelopmental disorders, and cancer progression through modulation of Nav channel trafficking and gating.\n\nSTRUCTURAL ARCHITECTURE: InterPro analysis reveals compact immunoglobulin-fold architecture optimized for extracellular protein-protein interactions with Nav channel alpha subunits. The entire extracellular domain comprises an immunoglobulin V-set domain (IPR013106/PF07686/SM00409, residues 29-140/30-140) belonging to the immunoglobulin superfamily (G3DSA:2.60.40.10, residues 25-149; IPR003599, residues 30-140; IPR007110, residues 24-123; IPR036179, residues 28-125). The protein belongs to the sodium channel subunit beta-1/beta-3 family (IPR027098/PTHR10546, residues 1-215) with GO annotations for sodium channel regulator activity (GO:0017080), sodium ion transport (GO:0006814), and voltage-gated sodium channel complex (GO:0001518). AlphaFold predicts high confidence structure (global pLDDT 86.2), indicating a well-folded immunoglobulin domain typical of cell adhesion molecules—consistent with SCN3B's role in non-covalent association with Nav alpha subunits at the plasma membrane.\n\nINTERACTION NETWORK: STRING-DB identifies high-confidence interactions exclusively with voltage-gated sodium channel family members and cardiac conduction proteins: SCN7A (0.973, sodium channel protein type 7 subunit alpha, Nav1.7), SCN2A (0.950, Nav1.2), SCN4A (0.949, Nav1.4), SCN4B (0.926, beta-4 subunit paralog), SCN10A (0.901, Nav1.8), SCN5A (0.838, Nav1.5, cardiac sodium channel), SCN2B (0.676, beta-2 subunit), plus medium-confidence interactors GPD1L (0.603, glycerol-3-phosphate dehydrogenase 1-like, Brugada syndrome protein), KCNE3 (0.586, potassium voltage-gated channel subfamily E member 3), and TRPM4 (0.571, transient receptor potential cation channel subfamily M member 4). This interaction profile establishes SCN3B as a pan-Nav channel modulator that co-assembles with multiple alpha subunits (Nav1.2/1.4/1.5/1.7/1.8) to regulate surface expression, gating kinetics, and voltage dependence of activation/inactivation.\n\nEXPRESSION & LOCALIZATION: HPA shows tissue-enhanced expression detected in many tissues with cancer-enriched specificity. Single cell type specificity is cell type-enhanced.\n\nDISEASE LANDSCAPE: ClinVar contains 69 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number losses at 11q24.1-25, 11q23.3-25, 11q24.1-25, 11q23.3-24.2, plus copy number gains at 11q23.3-24.2, 11q23.3-25, plus the missense variant c.163G>A (p.Glu55Lys) within the immunoglobulin V-set domain that likely disrupts alpha subunit binding, plus single allele duplications. No monogenic disease is yet definitively assigned in ClinVar, but recent 2025 literature establishes biallelic truncating variants cause neurodevelopmental phenotype with/without epilepsy and ataxia. Additional 2025 papers describe: novel SCN3B in-frame codon deletion in Brugada syndrome patient disrupting Nav1.5 function; SCN3B as anti-breast cancer molecule with migration inhibition effect; enterotoxin-related genes PPFIA4 and SCN3B promoting colorectal cancer development. The 2024 highly-cited paper (64 citations) questions SCN3B as glioma biomarker.\n\nCONSERVATION: Orthologs confirmed in mouse (Q8BHK2, 215 aa), rat (Q9JK00, 215 aa), bovine (Q2KI11, 215 aa), and macaque (Q8HXJ7, 215 aa), indicating strong evolutionary constraint on the immunoglobulin domain architecture across mammals for conserved Nav channel regulation.\n\nFUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: SCN3B functions as a non-covalent auxiliary subunit that modulates voltage-gated sodium channel biophysics and trafficking through its extracellular immunoglobulin domain. Unlike beta-1/beta-3 subunits that lack transmembrane domains, SCN3B associates non-covalently with Nav alpha subunits via Ig-domain contacts, increasing surface expression by enhancing ER export and reducing endocytosis. The Ig V-set domain (residues 29-140) mediates heterophilic cell adhesion and trans-interactions with neighboring cells, potentially clustering Nav channels at nodes of Ranvier or intercalated discs. In brain, SCN3B co-assembles with Nav1.2 (SCN2A) to regulate action potential initiation and propagation—loss causes neurodevelopmental defects with epilepsy/ataxia via impaired neuronal excitability. In heart, SCN3B modulates Nav1.5 (SCN5A) gating kinetics and surface density—variants cause Brugada syndrome (ST elevation, ventricular arrhythmia) and atrial fibrillation through reduced sodium current (INa). The c.163G>A (p.Glu55Lys) mutation within the Ig domain likely disrupts alpha subunit binding or cell adhesion. In cancer, SCN3B shows context-dependent roles: it inhibits breast cancer migration (tumor suppressor) but promotes colorectal cancer progression (oncogene), possibly through differential Nav channel partnerships (Nav1.5 in CRC vs Nav1.7 in breast). The GPD1L interaction links SCN3B to Brugada syndrome pathogenesis via shared trafficking pathways. Therapeutic targeting of SCN3B-Nav interfaces could modulate excitability in arrhythmia and epilepsy while sparing other tissues.
