## Description
LGALS4 encodes a 323 aa galectin-4 (UniProt P56470) located at 19q13.2, functioning as a tandem-repeat galectin with two distinct carbohydrate recognition domains (CRDs) that regulate intestinal epithelial integrity, cell adhesion, and tumor progression. InterPro analysis reveals a characteristic tandem architecture: N-terminal CRD (cd00070/IPR001079/PF00337/PS51304/SM00276, aa 18-150) and C-terminal CRD (aa 193-322) connected by a flexible linker peptide, both adopting the Concanavalin A-like lectin fold (G3DSA:2.60.120.200/SSF49899, aa 4-156 and 177-323) essential for β-galactoside binding (GO:0030246). AlphaFold predicts high-confidence structure (pLDDT 89.6), indicating a well-folded tandem protein with stable CRDs capable of cross-linking glycoconjugates. STRING interactions reveal a diverse network linking metabolism, transcription, and adhesion: GTF2B (0.873, general transcription factor), PHGDH (0.857, phosphoglycerate dehydrogenase in serine biosynthesis), CHD7 (0.820, chromatin remodeler), PAICS (0.791, purine biosynthesis), SLCO6A1 (0.752, solute carrier), CDH17 (0.561, cadherin-17 intestinal adhesion), LGALS3 (0.474, galectin-3 partner), TP53 (0.418, tumor suppressor). ClinVar reports 5 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, exclusively large 19q copy number gains (x3) spanning the LGALS4 locus, suggesting dosage sensitivity in chromosomal disorder syndromes. Literature (97 papers) details emerging roles in cancer and immunity: (1) Colorectal cancer suppression - LGALS4 inhibiting glycolysis and promoting apoptosis via β-catenin signaling disruption (5 citations, 2025); (2) Tumor immune microenvironment - TP53-LGALS4 axis modulating TIME and synergizing with anti-PD-L1 therapy in CRC (0 citations, 2025); (3) Oxaliplatin resistance - LGALS4 identified as biomarker in acquired chemoresistance (systems biology analysis); (4) Prostate cancer - Mendelian randomization identifying LGALS4 as potential therapeutic target (5 citations, 2025); (5) Intestinal homeostasis - maintenance of epithelial barrier function through glycan-mediated cross-linking of membrane receptors. Functional hypothesis: LGALS4 likely functions through: (1) Glycan Cross-linking Scaffold - tandem CRDs binding distinct β-galactoside ligands on cell surface glycoproteins/glycolipids, forming lattices that stabilize lipid rafts and restrict receptor mobility; (2) Wnt/β-Catenin Modulation - cytoplasmic or nuclear interaction with β-catenin complex, promoting its degradation or sequestration to inhibit oncogenic transcription in colorectal epithelium; (3) Metabolic Regulation - physical or functional association with PHGDH/PAICS enzymes, coupling glycosylation status to serine/purine biosynthesis pathways in rapidly dividing cells; (4) Chemotherapy Response - modulation of drug efflux or DNA damage response pathways contributing to oxaliplatin sensitivity/resistance; (5) Immune Surveillance - regulation of tumor-infiltrating lymphocyte recruitment or macrophage polarization via glycan-dependent mechanisms, enhancing anti-PD-L1 efficacy. The tandem CRD architecture distinguishes LGALS4 from prototype (LGALS1, LGALS2) and chimera (LGALS3) galectins, enabling unique bivalent binding modes. Disease implications include: colorectal cancer progression/metastasis, prostate cancer, inflammatory bowel disease, chemoresistance, 19q duplication syndromes. Despite 97 papers, LGALS4's specific glycan ligands in vivo, structural basis for β-catenin regulation, and therapeutic exploitation in combination immunotherapy remain underexplored.
