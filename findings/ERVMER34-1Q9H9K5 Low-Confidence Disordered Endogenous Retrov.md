## Description
ERVMER34-1 (Endogenous retrovirus group MER34 member 1, envelope, also known as HEMO, envMER34) is a 563 amino acid protein (UniProt Q9H9K5) with low-confidence disordered architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 44.3), characteristic of intrinsically disordered viral envelope proteins that function through membrane fusion and protein-protein interactions rather than stable enzymatic activity. The protein belongs to the TLV/ENV coat polyprotein family (IPR018154/PTHR10424, residues 23-508) that defines retroviral envelope glycoproteins, contains a virus ectodomain structural fold (SSF58069/G3DSA:1.10.287.210, residues 374-463/378-471) typical of viral surface proteins, and is classified as a predicted membrane and secreted protein. ERVMER34-1 is located on chromosome 4q12 and is predicted to be located in extracellular region and plasma membrane, with observed subcellular localization in nucleoplasm and cytosol.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals a network centered on endogenous retroviral proteins and chromatin regulators: ERVV-1 (0.639, Endogenous retrovirus group V member 1, envelope protein), ERVV-2 (0.622, Endogenous retrovirus group V member 2, envelope/syncytin-2), USP46 (0.543, Ubiquitin specific peptidase 46, deubiquitination), FANCM (0.532, Fanconi anemia complementation group M, DNA repair), ZNF138 (0.462, Zinc finger protein 138, transcription), OR2A2 (0.447, Olfactory receptor 2A2), ZNF782 (0.437, Zinc finger protein 782), ZNF107 (0.437, Zinc finger protein 107), ZNF550 (0.435, Zinc finger protein 550), and MAPK1IP1L (0.434, MAPK1 interacting protein 1-like). These interactions confirm ERVMER34-1 functions within the endogenous retrovirus network, potentially cooperating with ERVV-1/ERVV-2 (syncytin family) in placental development and retroviral element regulation.\n\nHPA expression reveals ERVMER34-1 has tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in parathyroid gland (12.1 nTPM) and placenta (10.2 nTPM), detected in some tissues. This parathyroid/placenta-enriched expression pattern is consistent with endogenous retroviral envelope proteins that function in cell-cell fusion (syncytiotrophoblast formation) and hormone regulation. Subcellular localization includes nucleoplasm and cytosol, suggesting potential roles in nuclear signaling and cytoplasmic trafficking. The protein is classified as a predicted membrane and secreted protein. Group-enriched single cell type specificity suggests expression in endocrine and trophoblast cell types. Low cancer specificity indicates ERVMER34-1 is not strongly associated with malignancy. Not detected in immune cells suggests limited immune function.\n\nClinVar documents 16 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including copy number losses (4q11-13.1, 4q12-21.1, 4q11-12 deletions) and copy number gains (4p12-q35.2, 4p16.3-q13.1, 4p16.3-q35.2, 4q12-31.21 amplifications). The variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity. While no specific Mendelian disease is annotated, 4q CNVs are associated with developmental delay, growth abnormalities, and skeletal anomalies. Given ERVMER34-1's placental expression and retroviral envelope function, loss-of-function may cause placental insufficiency and pregnancy complications through impaired syncytiotrophoblast formation, similar to syncytin-1/2 deficiencies.\n\nConservation is observed across primates and mammals: human (Q9H9K5, 563 aa), chimpanzee (A0A6D2WUS7/A0A2J8PEJ9, 563 aa), orangutan (A0A6D2WYU5, 563 aa), cat (A0ABI7VYX2, 578 aa), indicating an ancient endogenous retroviral integration conserved in mammals.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: ERVMER34-1/HEMO functions as an endogenous retroviral envelope protein that mediates cell-cell fusion in placental trophoblasts, analogous to syncytin-1 (ERVW-1) and syncytin-2 (ERVV-2). The disordered architecture (pLDDT 44.3) is typical of viral fusion proteins that undergo conformational changes during membrane fusion. Through interactions with ERVV-1 and ERVV-2, ERVMER34-1 may form heteromeric complexes that regulate syncytiotrophoblast formation and placental barrier function. USP46 and FANCM interactions link ERVMER34-1 to ubiquitin-mediated protein turnover and DNA damage response, potentially regulating retroviral element silencing. Zinc finger protein interactions (ZNF138/782/107/550) suggest ERVMER34-1 may influence transcriptional regulation of endogenous retroviral loci. Parathyroid gland expression (12.1 nTPM) suggests potential roles in calcium homeostasis through PTH regulation. Placenta expression (10.2 nTPM) indicates critical functions in pregnancy maintenance. 4q CNVs cause ERVMER34-1 dosage imbalance, potentially disrupting placental development and causing pregnancy complications, intrauterine growth restriction, or preeclampsia. ERVMER34-1 represents a candidate gene for placental insufficiency disorders and may contribute to the evolutionary adaptation of viviparity in mammals through endogenous retrovirus domestication.
