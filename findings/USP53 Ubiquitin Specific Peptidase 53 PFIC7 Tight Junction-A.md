**Confidence:** HIGH

## Description
USP53 encodes a 1073 aa large deubiquitinating enzyme (UniProt Q70EK8), also designated PFIC7 (Progressive Familial Intrahepatic Cholestasis type 7), that functions as a specialized K63-linked deubiquitinase controlling cell death signaling, tight junction integrity, and sensory perception through selective polyubiquitin chain editing. Despite initial annotation as catalytically inactive, 2024 mechanistic studies confirmed USP53 possesses bona fide K63-linkage-directed deubiquitinase activity representing phylogenetically distant USP family member. The protein features a canonical USP catalytic architecture: an N-terminal USP domain (IPR001394/IPR028889/PF00443/PS50235/cd02257, residues 30-351) forming papain-like cysteine peptidase fold (G3DSA:3.90.70.10/SSF54001/IPR038765) with catalytic triad for ubiquitin C-terminal hydrolysis; extensive C-terminal regions of unknown function potentially mediating substrate specificity and subcellular targeting; classified within USP53/54 family (IPR052398/PTHR22975). STRING interactions reveal USP53 operates within tight junction and cell death networks: TJP2 (0.733), tight junction protein 2 anchoring USP53 to bicellular tight junctions critical for epithelial barrier function; CRK (0.614), adaptor protein linking to apoptotic signaling; USP50 (0.566), paralogous deubiquitinase; PAN2 (0.552), poly(A)-specific ribonuclease; AZGP1 (0.504), alpha-2-glycoprotein 1; and multiple USP family members (USP39/USP48/USP45/USP13/USPL1, scores 0.421-0.503) suggesting functional cooperation or redundancy. HPA confirms expression in many tissues with low specificity, plasma membrane and cytosol localization consistent with tight junction association, classified as enzyme/hydrolase/disease-related gene/potential drug target involved in Ubl conjugation pathway with disease involvement in deafness and intrahepatic cholestasis. ClinVar lists 72 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including frameshifts (c.209del p.Gly70fs, c.336del p.Gln113fs), nonsense mutations (c.941G>A p.Trp314Ter, c.9G>A p.Trp3Ter, c.1219A>T p.Lys407Ter), missense variants (c.431G>A p.Cys144Tyr, c.136G>T p.Ala46Ser), splice site mutations (c.823-5A>G), and large copy number gains (4q21.21-31.21 spanning 79-146Mb), demonstrating strong dosage sensitivity and loss-of-function pathogenicity. AlphaFold structure prediction yields medium confidence (global pLDDT 55.1), revealing partially structured protein with ordered USP catalytic domain and extensive flexible/disordered C-terminal regions characteristic of regulatory scaffolding. Mechanistically, USP53 operates through integrated modes: (1) K63-deubiquitination—2024 study demonstrates specific cleavage of K63-linked polyubiquitin chains regulating protein stability and signaling without affecting K48-linked proteasomal degradation signals; (2) RIPK1 activation—2025 study shows USP53 promotes ethanol-induced myocardial injury by K63 deubiquitination-dependent RIPK1 activation at K377 residue triggering necroptosis in alcoholic cardiomyopathy; (3) Tight junction maintenance—TJP2 interaction localizes USP53 to bicellular junctions regulating epithelial barrier integrity critical for bile acid containment; (4) PFIC7 pathogenesis—loss-of-function causes progressive familial intrahepatic cholestasis type 7 via disrupted tight junction function and bile acid transport; (5) Auditory function—gene ontology predicts involvement in response to auditory stimulus and outer hair cell apoptotic process, mutations cause sensorineural deafness; (6) Spinal cord injury—2025 study shows FOS/USP53/ubiquitination axis regulates pyroptosis and necroptosis following CNS trauma, juglone promotes recovery by suppressing this pathway; (7) Osteogenic differentiation—2025 study demonstrates EIF4A3 enhances BMSC viability/invasion/osteogenesis via USP53/SMAD5 pathway. Loss-of-function causes accumulated K63-ubiquitinated substrates, dysregulated RIPK1 signaling, enhanced necroptosis/pyroptosis, disrupted tight junction integrity leading to cholestasis, outer hair cell death causing deafness, and developmental defects.
