# ZNF835: C2H2 Zinc Finger Transcription Factor with Cell Division Localization and Disease-Associated CNVs

## Description
ZNF835 encodes a 537 amino acid C2H2-type zinc finger transcription factor (UniProt Q9Y2P0) with three canonical C2H2 zinc finger domains (IPR013087, PF00096) positioned at residues 110-193, forming a classic Krueppel-type DNA-binding module. The protein shows confident AlphaFold structure prediction (pLDDT 75.9) across the full-length sequence, indicating stable tertiary folding. HPA data reveals ubiquitous expression with low tissue specificity, detected in many tissues, and distinctive subcellular localization to microtubules, cytokinetic bridge, mitotic spindle, primary cilium, and basal body—suggesting a role in cell division and ciliary function. STRING interactions identify 9 protein partners including PHF3 (0.416), ADAL (0.439), and olfactory receptors OR5P3/OR13A1 (0.44-0.45), though these medium-confidence interactions may reflect chromatin co-regulation rather than direct physical binding. ClinVar documents 17 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly copy number gains and losses spanning 19q13.33-13.43, indicating dosage sensitivity and disease association. The combination of zinc finger DNA-binding domains, mitotic spindle localization, and CNV pathogenicity suggests ZNF835 functions as a cell cycle-regulated transcriptional repressor that binds specific genomic targets during mitosis, potentially silencing differentiation genes to maintain proliferative state. Disruption via CNVs alters gene dosage, leading to dysregulated cell division and oncogenic phenotypes.
