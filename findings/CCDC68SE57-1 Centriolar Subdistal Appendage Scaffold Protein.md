## Description
CCDC68 (alias SE57-1, coiled-coil domain containing 68) encodes a 335 amino acid well-folded cytosolic/Golgi protein (AlphaFold pLDDT 80.5) containing a Tuftelin/GRINL1A/MYZAP/CCD68 family domain (IPR051375, aa 1-307) and belonging to the PTHR23171 family (aa 1-307) with a cathgene3d homologous superfamily (G3DSA:1.10.287.1490, aa 154-321). HPA shows tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in placenta (30.5 nTPM) and intestine (23.3 nTPM), detected in many tissues distribution, Golgi apparatus/cytosol subcellular localization, cancer-enhanced specificity, not detected in immune cells, and cell type-enhanced single cell pattern. ClinVar contains 60 pathogenic variants predominantly consisting of 18q11.1-23, 18q21.2-23, and 18q21.2-22.1 copy number gains/losses associated with chromosomal duplication/deletion syndromes. STRING interactions reveal high-confidence network with centrosome/centriole scaffolding proteins: CCDC120 (coiled-coil domain containing 120, centriolar satellite organization, 0.976), CEP128 (centrosomal protein 128, centriole structure, 0.962), MIA2 (melanoma inhibitory activity 2, ERTMI complex, 0.960), CTAGE1 (cancer/testis antigen 1, ER-Golgi intermediate compartment, 0.955), CEP170 (centrosomal protein 170, microtubule binding, 0.896), ODF2 (outer dense fiber 2, centriole outer structure, 0.853), NIN (ninein, microtubule anchoring, 0.837), CNTRL (centriolin, centrosome maturation, 0.779), DCTN1 (dynactin subunit 1, microtubule motor adaptor, 0.767), and KIF2A (kinesin family member 2A, microtubule depolymerization, 0.763). Conservation across vertebrates indicates functional constraint. The CCD68 family domain architecture combined with high-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 80.5) and gene ontology (microtubule anchoring at centrosome, protein localization, centriole, centriolar subdistal appendage) strongly suggests CCDC68 functions as a centriolar subdistal appendage scaffold protein involved in microtubule anchoring and centrosome organization. CCDC120 interaction (0.976) is particularly informative—CCDC120 localizes to centriolar satellites and regulates centrosome assembly, placing CCDC68 at the centriolar satellite interface. CEP128 association (0.962) links CCDC68 to centriole structure—CEP128 is required for centriole integrity and ciliogenesis. NIN interaction (0.837) is mechanistically critical—ninein is the canonical microtubule anchoring protein at the subdistal appendage, suggesting CCDC68 functions as a ninein cofactor or scaffold recruiting microtubule minus ends to the centriole. CEP170 association (0.896) reinforces microtubule binding role—CEP170 directly binds microtubules at the centrosome. ODF2 interaction (0.853) places CCDC68 in the outer dense fiber network, structural components of the centriole wall. CNTRL association (0.779) links to centrosome maturation during cell cycle progression. DCTN1 interaction (0.767) connects to dynein-dynactin microtubule motor complex, suggesting role in microtubule-based cargo transport from the centrosome. KIF2A association (0.763) indicates coupling to microtubule dynamics regulation through depolymerization activity. Placenta enrichment (30.5 nTPM) indicates specialized function in trophoblast proliferation and placental development, potentially regulating mitotic spindle organization in rapidly dividing trophoblast cells. Intestine expression (23.3 nTPM) suggests role in intestinal epithelial cell division and barrier maintenance. Golgi apparatus/cytosol localization indicates potential dual function—centrosomal during mitosis and Golgi-associated during interphase, potentially coordinating centrosome-Golgi positioning. Cancer-enhanced expression suggests upregulation in proliferating tumor cells, consistent with centrosome amplification and mitotic defects in cancer. Cell type-enhanced pattern indicates specific upregulation in dividing cell lineages. High pLDDT score (80.5) indicates stable folded coiled-coil structure, consistent with scaffold proteins requiring defined helical interfaces for oligomerization and protein-protein interactions at the centriole. Pathogenic 18q CNVs likely cause dosage effects disrupting microtubule anchoring, centrosome organization, and mitotic spindle assembly, potentially contributing to 18q deletion/duplication syndrome (18q- syndrome, 18q+ syndrome) phenotypes including growth retardation, intellectual disability, and developmental defects through impaired cell division and ciliogenesis.
