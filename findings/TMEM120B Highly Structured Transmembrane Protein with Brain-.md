## Description
TMEM120B (Transmembrane Protein 120B, UniProt A0PK00) is a 339-amino acid integral membrane protein belonging to the TNF-alpha-induced transmembrane protein family (PTHR21433, residues 4-338) with highly restricted tissue distribution and exceptional structural stability. The protein contains a conserved TMEM120A/B domain (IPR012926/PF07851, residues 4-338/10-331) characteristic of this small transmembrane protein family that includes the related paralog TMEM120A (also known as TACAN, a putative mechanosensitive ion channel). AlphaFold predicts an exceptionally well-folded structure (pLDDT=91.7, very high confidence) across the entire 339 amino acids, indicating a stable transmembrane architecture with defined secondary and tertiary structure suitable for specific protein-protein interactions or potential ion channel/pore formation. HPA reveals striking brain-enriched expression (45.9 nTPM in brain, highest among all tissues) with tissue-enhanced specificity, ubiquitous detection across tissues, and dual subcellular localization to nucleoli fibrillar centers and cytosol — suggesting roles in neuronal function and potentially ribosomal biogenesis or nucleolar stress responses. The TMEM120A interaction is particularly informative given their shared domain architecture and potential formation of homo/hetero-oligomeric complexes. ClinVar documents 26 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large chromosomal duplications (12q23.1-24.33), deletions (12q24.22-24.33), and critical splice-site mutations (c.305+1G>A, c.305+5G>A) affecting canonical splice donor sites, indicating dosage sensitivity where haploinsufficiency or triplosensitivity causes disease phenotypes potentially involving neurological dysfunction given the brain-enriched expression pattern. The combination of very high structural confidence, brain-specific expression, nucleolar localization, TMEM120A paralog interaction, peroxisomal/metabolic partner associations (PEX26, AACS), and splice-site pathology supports a mechanistic model: TMEM120B functions as a structured transmembrane scaffold forming homo/hetero-oligomers with TMEM120A at nuclear envelope or intracellular membrane compartments, potentially regulating nucleocytoplasmic transport, ribosomal biogenesis through nucleolar association, or metabolic signaling linking peroxisomal fatty acid oxidation (PEX26, AACS) to neuronal energy homeostasis, with splice-disruption causing loss-of-function through aberrant mRNA processing leading to neurological or developmental disease.
