HPA shows low tissue specificity with expression detected in many tissues; classified as predicted membrane protein and transporter involved in ion transport, sodium transport, sugar transport, and symport. Functional hypothesis: SLC60A2/MFSD4B functions as a sodium-coupled symporter mediating cellular uptake of glucose or glucose-derived substrates (potentially glucuronide conjugates or vitamin C derivatives) across apical plasma membranes via alternating access mechanism. The 12-TM MFS fold creates a central substrate-binding site with conserved sodium-binding residues enabling Na+-coupled transport (symport). The cd17454 domain annotation as 'sodium-dependent glucose transporter 1' strongly suggests functional homology with SLC5A family glucose transporters (SGLT1/2). Low tissue specificity with widespread expression indicates housekeeping role in cellular glucose/sugar uptake across multiple tissues. Apical membrane localization prediction suggests epithelial transport function in kidney proximal tubule or intestinal brush border. Pathogenic CNVs may disrupt sodium-coupled sugar transport causing renal glycosuria, intestinal malabsorption, or metabolic disturbances. Conservation across vertebrates (rat 484 aa, Xenopus 491 aa) confirms essential metabolic role.
