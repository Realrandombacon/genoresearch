## Description

FUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: PLD5 functions as a catalytically inactive PLD family member that regulates lipid metabolism and membrane dynamics through protein-protein interactions rather than enzymatic activity. The dual PLD domain architecture (aa 111-273, 309-502) retains structural homology to active phospholipases but lacks critical catalytic residues (HKD motifs) required for phospholipid hydrolysis. PLD5 likely operates through: (1) Competitive inhibition - binding PLD substrates or interaction partners without catalysis, modulating active PLD enzyme function (PLD2, PLD6), (2) Scaffolding - organizing lipid metabolism complexes at mitochondrial membranes through interactions with PNPLA8 (mitochondrial phospholipase A2) and FAR2 (fatty acid elongase), (3) Signal transduction - coupling lipid metabolism to Rho GTPase signaling via ARHGAP25 interaction, (4) Neuropeptide signaling modulation through NPSR1 interaction. The choroid plexus-enriched expression (26.1 nTPM) suggests specialized roles in cerebrospinal fluid production, blood-CSF barrier function, or neuroendocrine regulation. Mitochondrial localization indicates involvement in mitochondrial lipid metabolism, membrane dynamics, or apoptosis regulation. The cancer-enhanced expression pattern and prostate/thyroid cancer literature suggest PLD5 promotes tumor progression through miRNA-regulated mechanisms - miR-145-5p suppression of PLD5 reduces proliferation and metastasis, indicating oncogenic function. The 83 ClinVar variants (1q43-44 CNVs) suggest PLD5 haploinsufficiency may cause undiagnosed neurological or metabolic disorders, consistent with choroid plexus/brain expression and mitochondrial function. The PLD5-PLD6 interaction (0.711) is particularly notable as both are inactive PLD family members with mitochondrial localization, suggesting coordinated regulatory functions. PLD5 represents a partially characterized regulatory protein with defined domain architecture, tissue-specific expression, cancer relevance, and potential neurological functions requiring mechanistic elucidation. Experimental priorities include: identifying PLD5 binding partners in mitochondrial lipid metabolism, determining structural basis for catalytic inactivity, characterizing choroid plexus-specific functions, evaluating PLD5 as therapeutic target in prostate/thyroid cancer, and investigating neurological phenotypes in 1q43-44 deletion patients.
