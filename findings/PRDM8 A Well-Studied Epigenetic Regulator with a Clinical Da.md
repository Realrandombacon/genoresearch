# PRDM8: A Well-Studied Epigenetic Regulator with a Clinical Data Gap

**Date:** 2026-05-08T00:34:16.455916

**Quality Score:** 8/10 (EXCELLENT)

**Confidence:** MEDIUM

## Description
PRDM8 (PR domain-containing protein 8) is a well-studied gene with a significant clinical data gap. Biochemically, PRDM8 is a bifunctional nuclear protein (UniProt Q9NQV8, 689 aa) containing a PR-SET domain and C2H2-type zinc fingers, functioning as a histone methyltransferase and transcriptional repressor. Structurally, AlphaFold predicts it is partially structured (pLDDT 53.5) with intrinsically disordered regions, consistent with its role in dynamic chromatin interactions. It is highly expressed in the nucleus, with tissue enhancement in the seminal vesicle and prostate, and is cancer-enhanced. STRING analysis identifies it as a key partner in the PR domain protein family (PRDM2, PRDM5, PRDM6, PRDM10, PRDM15) and interacts with NHLRC1 and EPM2A, linking it to protein degradation and telomere maintenance pathways. ClinVar lists 29 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants (mostly large copy number losses/gains on chr4), yet 0 specific human genetic syndromes are associated. Despite strong literature links to Dyskeratosis Congenita, Idiopathic Aplastic Anemia, and Borderline Personality Disorder via aberrant DNA methylation and neurogenesis, the specific disease phenotype remains uncataloged. This suggests PRDM8 acts as a master epigenetic regulator where CNVs cause broad developmental or aging defects rather than a discrete syndrome, creating a gap in clinical genetic databases.
