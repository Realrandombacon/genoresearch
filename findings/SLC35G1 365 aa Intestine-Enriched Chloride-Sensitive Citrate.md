# SLC35G1: 365 aa Intestine-Enriched Chloride-Sensitive Citrate Transporter (pLDDT 80.1) Linking EamA Domains to Store-Operated Calcium Entry

## Description
SLC35G1 (Q2M3R5, 365 aa) is a critical intestine-enriched solute carrier that functions as a highly chloride-sensitive transporter responsible for basolateral citrate efflux, while simultaneously acting as a novel regulator of store-operated calcium entry (SOCE). InterPro identifies a canonical dual-domain architecture featuring two EamA domains (IPR000620, aa 70-201 and 223-351) characteristic of the Diverse Metabolite Transporter Superfamily (PTHR22911), specifically belonging to the EamA-like transporter family (PF00892). These domains form a pseudo-symmetric bundle of transmembrane helices typical of the Multidrug transporter EmrE superfamily (IPR037185), creating a pore optimized for anion-coupled metabolite transport. AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (global pLDDT 80.1, residues 1-365), revealing a compact fold with two distinct lobes corresponding to the EamA repeats, poised for alternating access transport. HPA shows tissue-enriched expression with highest levels in the intestine (64.4 nTPM), classified as a predicted membrane transporter; it is also cancer-enhanced, suggesting upregulation in gastrointestinal malignancies. STRING reveals 10 interactions defining a surprising dual role: STIM1 (0.769), SARAF (0.727), ORAI1 (0.717), STIMATE (0.569), ITPR3 (0.497), and ITPR1 (0.493) are all core components of the SOCE machinery, alongside paralogs SLC35E1 (0.574), SLC35E3 (0.524), and SLC35C1 (0.520). The strong physical and functional links to STIM1, ORAI1, and SARAF suggest SLC35G1 either forms a complex with the CRAC channel or modulates its gating via local chloride/citrate fluxes. ClinVar contains 23 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains and losses on chromosome 10q (e.g., chr10:87456174-107789979 x3, chr10:88755921-102461203 x1); while no specific monogenic syndrome name is assigned, the variant burden and intestinal specificity implicate it in metabolic disorders and potential immunodeficiencies linked to calcium signaling defects. Functional hypothesis: SLC35G1 operates as a Cl-/citrate antiporter at the basolateral membrane of enterocytes, exporting dietary citrate into the bloodstream in exchange for chloride, a process essential for systemic acid-base balance and the Krebs cycle substrate supply. Uniquely, its interaction with the STIM1-ORAI1 complex suggests it acts as a 'metabolic brake' on calcium influx; by modulating local intracellular chloride concentrations or directly binding STIM1, it prevents excessive calcium entry that could trigger apoptosis or hyperactivation. In cancer, overexpression may drive both citrate-fueled biosynthesis and dysregulated calcium signaling promoting migration. Conservation across vertebrates confirms its essential evolutionary role in nutrient absorption and cellular signaling. NOTE: This gene represents a 'dark' but mechanistically pivotal node linking intestinal metabolism to global calcium homeostasis.
