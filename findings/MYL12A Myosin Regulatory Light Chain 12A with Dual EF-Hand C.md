## Description
MYL12A (Myosin Regulatory Light Chain 12A, UniProt P19105) is a 171 aa calcium-binding regulatory protein belonging to the myosin regulatory light chain family (IPR050403/PTHR23049, positions 1-169/1-169) that functions as an essential component of the myosin II motor complex regulating actomyosin contractility, cytokinesis, and cell migration. The molecular architecture consists of two canonical EF-hand calcium-binding domains (IPR002048/IPR011992/IPR015070/IPR018247, positions 28-76/25-164/101-159/28-63+97-132) forming a paired EF-hand superfamily structure (G3DSA:1.10.238.10/SSF47473/PF13499, positions 9-94+95-163/25-164/32-75) with conserved calcium-binding signature (PS00018/PS50222/SM00054, positions 41-53/28-63+97-132/32-60+101-129). AlphaFold predicts a well-folded structure (pLDDT 83.4) indicating stable EF-hand pair conformation typical of calcium-regulated light chains. Literature (39 papers) establishes MYL12A roles in: (1) diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) progression and immune evasion via OTUD3-mediated deubiquitination stabilizing MYL12A and PD-L1 (2024, 9 citations), (2) acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS) neutrophil function through liquid-liquid phase separation improving prognosis (2025), (3) Salmonella gut-vascular barrier penetration via SteC-mediated myosin light chain activation (2024, 20 citations), (4) brain lower-grade glioma purity and prognosis gene signature (2020, 26 citations), (5) USP6 gene fusion partners in bone and soft tissue lesions (2021, 16 citations). Functional hypothesis: MYL12A functions as a calcium-regulatory light chain that binds to the neck region of myosin II heavy chains (MYH9/MYH10/MYH11/MYH14) and modulates actomyosin ATPase activity and contractility through calcium-dependent conformational changes. The mechanistic model proposes: (1) MYL12A pairs with MYL6/MYL9/MYL6B as alternative regulatory light chains for different myosin II isoforms, (2) dual EF-hand domains bind calcium ions inducing conformational change that activates myosin motor function, (3) phosphorylation by MYLK/MYLK2 kinases provides additional regulatory layer for smooth muscle and non-muscle contractility, (4) MYL12A-MYH9/MYH10 complex drives non-muscle cell migration and cytokinesis, (5) MYL12A-MYH11 complex regulates smooth muscle contraction, (6) MYL12A-MYH14 complex functions in specialized tissues (heart/skeletal muscle), (7) ACTB interaction links MYL12A to actin filament sliding mechanism, (8) in cancer, MYL12A stabilization via OTUD3 deubiquitination enhances cell migration and immune evasion through PD-L1 co-regulation, (9) LLPS in neutrophils suggests MYL12A forms condensates regulating cytoskeletal dynamics during inflammation, (10) Salmonella SteC kinase hijacks MYL12A phosphorylation to promote bacterial dissemination. Despite 39 publications, MYL12A remains mechanistically incomplete—its calcium-binding affinity, phosphorylation sites, isoform-specific functions, and disease variant effects are unknown. The 133 ClinVar variants and muscle-enriched expression indicate clinical significance in cardiomyopathy and myopathy. MYL12A represents a partially characterized regulatory light chain at the intersection of muscle physiology, cancer metastasis, and infectious disease.
