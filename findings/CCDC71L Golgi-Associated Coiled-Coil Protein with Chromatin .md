# CCDC71L: Golgi-Associated Coiled-Coil Protein with Chromatin Regulation Links and 7q22 Dosage Sensitivity

## Description
CCDC71L (Gene ID: 168455, UniProt Q8N9Z2, alias C7orf74) is a 235 amino acid coiled-coil domain-containing protein located on chromosome 7q22.3 (NC_000007.14, positions 106654359-106661157, ~6.8kb genomic region). This gene encodes a paralog of CCDC71 with conserved structural domains suggestive of scaffolding or protein-protein interaction functions. InterPro domain analysis reveals a predominantly coiled-coil architecture: CCDC71/71L family signature (IPR026695, positions 1-152, spanning N-terminal two-thirds of protein), coiled-coil domain (PF15374, positions 37-113, central ~77 residue helical region), and PTHR14484 coiled-coil domain-containing protein 71 family (positions 1-152). The coiled-coil domain occupies the N-terminal region, suggesting dimerization or oligomerization capability typical of scaffold proteins. The C-terminal region (residues 153-235, ~82 residues) lacks defined domains and may be intrinsically disordered, providing flexibility for partner binding. STRING interaction network identifies 3 medium-confidence partners: ZNF280A (0.446, zinc finger protein 280A, transcriptional regulator), MTMR8 (0.423, myotubularin-related protein 8, phosphoinositide phosphatase involved in membrane trafficking), and CBX3 (0.420, chromobox homolog 3/HP1γ, heterochromatin protein 1 gamma, epigenetic silencing). The CBX3 interaction is particularly significant—HP1γ mediates heterochromatin formation and gene silencing through chromatin binding, suggesting CCDC71L may function in nuclear organization or transcriptional regulation despite cytoplasmic localization signals. MTMR8 interaction implicates phosphoinositide metabolism and membrane trafficking pathways. Human Protein Atlas classifies CCDC71L (ENSG00000253276) as a predicted intracellular protein with unexpected triple subcellular localization: Golgi apparatus, actin filaments, and cytosol. This multi-compartment distribution suggests dynamic shuttling or distinct functional pools. RNA expression shows low tissue specificity with detection in many tissues, low cancer specificity, low immune cell specificity, but cell type-enhanced single cell specificity—indicating ubiquitous housekeeping expression with cell type modulation. AlphaFold structure prediction (AF-Q8N9Z2-F1) yields medium-confidence model (global pLDDT 60.0), indicating a partially structured protein with mixed ordered coiled-coil regions and disordered segments across all 235 residues—consistent with intrinsically disordered protein (IDP) characteristics or flexible linker regions between structured domains. The medium confidence score reflects the challenging nature of coiled-coil prediction and potential conformational flexibility. ClinVar lists 18 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all large chromosomal copy number variations spanning chromosome 7q22-q36 regions (e.g., chr7:104536649-109624996 deletion, chr7:100676872-119156160 deletion, chr7:99417471-111586308 deletion, chr7:104506008-107408857 deletion, chr7:10365-159119707 triplication, chr7:98693388-159119707 triplication, chr7:43361-159119707 triplication); no specific monogenic disease is attributed solely to CCDC71L point mutations, suggesting dosage sensitivity within larger chromosomal syndromes rather than haploinsufficiency. Conservation is evidenced by CCDC71 paralog (Q8IV32, 467 aa) and orthologs in mouse (Q8VEG0, 433 aa), rat (Q4V7C4, 436 aa), and cattle (Q2HJ91, 452 aa)—though CCDC71L itself appears primate-specific or rapidly evolving given length differences. Functional hypothesis: CCDC71L functions as a multi-compartment scaffold protein linking Golgi membrane trafficking, cytoskeletal organization, and chromatin regulation. The N-terminal coiled-coil domain (residues 37-113) mediates homodimerization or heterodimerization with CCDC71 or other coiled-coil partners. Golgi localization suggests roles in vesicle trafficking, protein sorting, or Golgi structure maintenance. Actin filament association indicates potential involvement in cytoskeletal dynamics, cell motility, or Golgi-actin coordination. CBX3/HP1γ interaction unexpectedly links CCDC71L to heterochromatin organization—perhaps through nucleocytoplasmic shuttling or indirect complex assembly. MTMR8 interaction places CCDC71L within phosphoinositide signaling networks regulating membrane identity and vesicle budding. Medium-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 60.0) suggests conformational flexibility consistent with scaffold/adapter function requiring multiple binding partners. Ubiquitous expression reflects fundamental role in cellular infrastructure. Disease mechanism: CCDC71L haploinsufficiency or overexpression from chromosome 7q CNVs may contribute to developmental disorders or cancer. The 18 ClinVar CNVs suggest dosage sensitivity; 7q deletions/duplications cause diverse phenotypes including intellectual disability, growth abnormalities, and congenital malformations. CCDC71L may contribute through disrupted Golgi trafficking (affecting protein secretion, receptor recycling), altered cytoskeletal dynamics (impacting cell migration, division), or aberrant chromatin regulation (via CBX3 interaction affecting gene expression programs). Priority experiments include co-IP validation of CBX3, MTMR8, ZNF280A interactions, subcellular localization confirmation via immunofluorescence (Golgi vs nucleus vs cytosol), investigation of CCDC71L role in vesicle trafficking using cargo transport assays, assessment of CCDC71L involvement in heterochromatin organization via ChIP-seq if nuclear pool confirmed, structural analysis leveraging AlphaFold model (pLDDT 60.0) to identify coiled-coil interfaces and disordered regions, generation of knockout models to assess Golgi morphology and trafficking phenotypes, and analysis of CCDC71L expression in 7q syndrome patient cells.
