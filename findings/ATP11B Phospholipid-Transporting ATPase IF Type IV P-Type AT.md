## Description
ATP11B encodes a 1177 aa type IV P-type ATPase (UniProt Q9Y2G3), also known as ATPIF or ATPIR, that functions as an aminophospholipid translocase (flippase) responsible for ATP-dependent transport of phosphatidylserine and phosphatidylethanolamine from the outer to inner leaflet of plasma membrane and endosomal membranes—a critical process for maintaining membrane asymmetry, vesicle trafficking, and cell signaling. The protein features a canonical P-type ATPase architecture with multiple conserved domains: an N-terminal aminophospholipid translocase domain (cd02073, residues 41-979) characteristic of the Dnf/Drs2/ATP8A/ATP11 family; a P-type ATPase family domain (IPR001757, residues 89-207/334-427/784-904) containing the conserved phosphorylation site (IPR018303, residues 407-413) with the signature DKTGT motif; a P-type ATPase subfamily IV domain (IPR006539, residues 39-1105) specific for lipid transport; A-domain (IPR008250/G3DSA:2.70.150.10, residues 99-273), N-domain (G3DSA:3.40.1110.10, residues 518-649), and HAD superfamily catalytic domain (IPR023214/G3DSA:3.40.50.1000, residues 671-859). STRING interactions place ATP11B in phospholipid transport networks with high-confidence partnerships: TMEM30B (0.882) and TMEM30A (0.635), beta-subunits essential for flippase activity and plasma membrane localization; ATP11A (0.703), a paralogous flippase; ATP8A1 (0.635), ATP8A2 (0.632), ATP10B (0.622), ATP8B3 (0.603), ATP10D (0.590), and ATP8B4 (0.581), other type IV P-type ATPases forming the broader flippase family; SCGB1A1 (0.669), secretoglobin involved in inflammation.
