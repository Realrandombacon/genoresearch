## Description
STK26 (UniProt Q9P289), also known as MST4 (Mammalian Sterile 20-like kinase 4), encodes a 416-amino acid serine/threonine kinase belonging to the GCK group III family of Ste20-like kinases. The protein features a canonical N-terminal kinase domain (IPR000719/IPR035056, aa 19-295) with ATP binding site (IPR017441, aa 30-53), embedded within the protein kinase-like domain superfamily (IPR011009, aa 24-320). The C-terminus contains a PDCD10 dimerization domain (IPR048288/IPR046409, aa 345-413/353-406) mediating homodimerization and complex assembly. AlphaFold predicts a confident structure (pLDDT: 79.6) consistent with an active kinase conformation.\n\nSTRING interactions reveal STK26 as a core component of the STRIPAK (Striatin-Interacting Phosphatase and Kinase) complex: PDCD10 (0.999), STRIP1 (0.998), STK24 (0.998), CTTNBP2NL (0.996), MOB4 (0.996), STRN3 (0.996), STK25 (0.995), TRAF3IP3 (0.995), CTTNBP2 (0.994), and CAB39 (0.590). HPA classifies STK26 as tissue-enhanced with highest expression in bone marrow (66.1 nTPM) and epididymis (53.1 nTPM); subcellular localization to Golgi apparatus, nucleoplasm, centrosome, and cytosol; functional annotation in apoptosis.\n\nWith only 23 papers, STK26 represents a partially characterized dark gene with emerging oncogenic significance. Recent 2025 research demonstrates STK26 promotes colorectal cancer progression by stabilizing ATF6 (Activating Transcription Factor 6) within the unfolded protein response pathway, linking ER stress to tumorigenesis. In hepatocellular carcinoma, STK26 is targeted by miR-22-3p and regulates ATG4B-mediated autophagy to drive proliferation, migration, and invasion. The kinase's Golgi localization via GM130 binding positions it as a spatial regulator of membrane trafficking and stress signaling. ClinVar contains 177 pathogenic variants, predominantly Xq copy number alterations, suggesting dosage sensitivity. This positions STK26 as an understudied node connecting STRIPAK complex assembly, UPR signaling, and autophagy regulation in cancer progression.
