## Description
EXOC1 encodes an 894 aa peripheral membrane protein (UniProt Q9NV70), also known as SEC3 or SEC3L1, that serves as a critical spatial landmark subunit of the evolutionarily conserved exocyst complex essential for tethering post-Golgi secretory vesicles to specific plasma membrane docking sites before SNARE-mediated fusion. The protein features a sophisticated multi-domain architecture: an N-terminal PH-like domain (IPR028258, PF15277, SM01313, cd14683, residues 31-122) that binds phosphatidylinositol 4,5-bisphosphate (PIP2) for plasma membrane recruitment; a central coiled-coil region (IPR019160, PF09763, residues 185-316) mediating heterodimeric interactions with other exocyst subunits; and a large C-terminal domain (IPR048628, PF20654, residues 577-871) providing structural scaffold integrity. STRING interactions place EXOC1 at the absolute core of the exocyst machinery with ultra-high confidence partnerships: EXOC3 (0.999), EXOC5 (0.999), EXOC6B (0.991), and EXOC7 (0.968)—all essential exocyst subunits; CDC42 (0.992), a Rho GTPase regulating exocyst assembly and actin polymerization; plus EXOC6 (0.732), EXOC3L1 (0.678), EXOC2 (0.658), EXOC4 (0.631), and EXOC8 (0.562). HPA confirms ubiquitous expression across all tissues with localization to plasma membrane, cytosol, and microtubules, annotating roles in exocytosis, protein transport, antiviral defense, and host-virus interaction—reflecting the exocyst's involvement in immune synapse formation and viral particle release. ClinVar lists 20 pathogenic variants including large copy number gains/losses spanning chromosome 4p16.3-q35.2
