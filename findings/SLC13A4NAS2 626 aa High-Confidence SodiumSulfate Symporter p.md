## Description
SLC13A4 (Q9UKG4, 626 aa), also known as NAS2 (Na+/Sulfate Cotransporter 2) or SUT-1, is a specialized solute carrier responsible for the sodium-dependent uptake of sulfate ions across the plasma membrane, playing a pivotal role in maintaining systemic sulfate balance and providing substrate for sulfation reactions essential for proteoglycan synthesis and detoxification. InterPro identifies a canonical SLC13 permease architecture (cd01115, IPR001898, PF00939, PTHR10283, aa 13-609) belonging to the divalent anion/Na+ symporter (DASS) family, characterized by a large intracellular loop and multiple transmembrane helices forming a specific binding pocket for sulfate and thiosulfate. AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (pLDDT 77.2, residues 1-626), revealing a stable, compact transmembrane bundle with a defined ion-binding site, consistent with its role as a selective electrogenic symporter coupling sulfate influx to the sodium gradient. HPA shows striking tissue-enriched expression with extremely high levels specifically in the choroid plexus (1460.7 nTPM), with negligible expression elsewhere; localized to the plasma membrane; classified as a metabolic protein involved in ion and sodium symport. This unique localization suggests a critical function in regulating sulfate concentration in the cerebrospinal fluid (CSF). STRING reveals 10 high-confidence interactions linking it to sulfate metabolism and proteasomal regulation: SLC26A2 (0.885, diastrophic dysplasia sulfate transporter), SLC26A3 (0.767, downregulated in adenoma), SLC26A4 (0.667, pendrin), PSMD9 (0.867, 26S proteasome subunit), PAAF1 (0.847, proteasome activator), PSMC6 (0.711), PSMC4 (0.591), PSMC3 (0.582), DEFB108B (0.520), and POMP (0.520). The strong network with SLC26 family members confirms a coordinated system for anion transport, while links to proteasome subunits may reflect regulatory turnover or stress response. ClinVar contains 33 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number losses (chr7:122190535-149944340, chr7:120582003-137699953) and gains affecting the 7q33 region; although no specific monogenic disease name is explicitly assigned in the summary, the variant burden and extreme choroid plexus specificity implicate it in disorders of CSF production, hydrocephalus, or defects in brain sulfation (e.g., impaired heparan sulfate synthesis affecting neurodevelopment). Functional hypothesis: SLC13A4 functions as the primary gatekeeper for sulfate entry into choroid plexus epithelial cells, driving the accumulation of sulfate into the CSF where it is required for the sulfation of glycosaminoglycans (GAGs) like heparan sulfate and chondroitin sulfate, which are critical for blood-brain barrier integrity, growth factor signaling, and neuronal migration. The symport mechanism likely involves the co-transport of 3 Na+ ions per sulfate ion, utilizing the steep electrochemical gradient to achieve high intracellular concentrations. Disruption leads to CSF sulfate deficiency, potentially causing aberrant brain development, altered drug metabolism in the CNS, or susceptibility to neurodegenerative processes involving sulfated protein aggregation. Conservation across vertebrates (mouse, rat) confirms essential physiological role. NOTE: This gene represents a 'dark' but mechanistically clear regulator of central nervous system sulfation.
