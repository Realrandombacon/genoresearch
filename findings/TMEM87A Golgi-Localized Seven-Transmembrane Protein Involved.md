# TMEM87A: Golgi-Localized Seven-Transmembrane Protein Involved in Mechanotransduction and Retrograde Transport

**Date:** 2026-03-23T00:32:39.128357

**Quality Score:** 3.6/10  (E=6.2, D=1.0) [MODERATE]

## Description
TMEM87A (Transmembrane Protein 87A), also known as ELKIN1 or GOLPHCAT, encodes a 555 amino acid multi-pass membrane protein localized to the Golgi apparatus and plasma membrane. The protein architecture is defined by a conserved N-terminal GOLD domain (residues 36-188, IPR054101, PF21901), a domain often involved in glycolipid binding or protein-protein interactions within the secretory pathway. Uniquely, TMEM87A contains a "GOST" seven-transmembrane domain (residues 225-467, IPR053937, PF06814), structurally homologous to G-protein coupled receptors (GPCRs) but classified within the GPR107/GPR108-like family (IPR009637). This suggests a potential role as an orphan receptor or ion channel modulator.

**Expression & Localization**: Human Protein Atlas confirms ubiquitous expression across all tissues with low specificity, consistent with a housekeeping role in fundamental cellular processes. Subcellular localization is strictly Golgi apparatus, with additional presence in plasma membrane ruffles, linking it to membrane dynamics and trafficking.

**Functional Context**: Gene ontology annotations indicate involvement in "cellular response to mechanical stimulus" and "retrograde transport, endosome to Golgi." This positions TMEM87A as a candidate mechanotransducer that may sense physical forces at the cell surface or within the Golgi, triggering signaling cascades or adapting vesicular trafficking rates. The alias "ELKIN1" implies a relationship to mechanosensitive ion channels (e.g., PIEZO1), potentially acting as a regulatory subunit or downstream effector.

**Clinical Significance**: ClinVar reports 10 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including splice site mutations (c.460-19A>T, c.1240+5G>C) and large copy number variations spanning chromosome 15q15.1-q26.3. The presence of truncating/splicing variants alongside large CNVs suggests haploinsufficiency or dominant-negative mechanisms. Disruption of TMEM87A likely impairs Golgi integrity and retrograde trafficking, potentially leading to defective processing of mechanosensitive cargo or glycosylation enzymes, contributing to developmental or neurological phenotypes associated with 15q15.1 rearrangements.

**HYPOTHESIS**: TMEM87A functions as a Golgi-resident seven-transmembrane scaffold that couples mechanical stress signals to vesicular trafficking machinery. The GOST domain may form a pore or scaffold for lipid second messengers, while the GOLD domain anchors the complex to Golgi membranes. Upon mechanical stimulation, TMEM87A could modulate the rate of retrograde transport to adjust cell surface receptor composition, thereby tuning cellular sensitivity to touch or pressure. Pathogenic variants disrupt this mechanotransduction-to-trafficking coupling, leading to systemic defects in tissue mechanohomeostasis.
