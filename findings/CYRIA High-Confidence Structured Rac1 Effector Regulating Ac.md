## Description
CYRIA encodes a 323 amino acid CYFIP-related Rac1 interactor A (UniProt Q9H0Q0), a compact Rac1-binding protein regulating actin cytoskeleton dynamics. Despite 92 publications, direct functional characterization remains limited, with most literature focused on related family members.\n\nSTRUCTURAL FEATURES: AlphaFold predicts exceptionally high confidence structure (pLDDT 92.8) across all 323 residues, indicating a well-folded, stable protein. InterPro analysis reveals a single dominant domain architecture: Rac1 binding domain (IPR009828, PF07159, residues 18-319) spanning nearly the entire protein, and CYFIP-related Rac1 interactor family domain (IPR039789, PTHR12422, residues 1-323). GO annotations confirm small GTPase binding (GO:0031267) and regulation of actin filament polymerization (GO:0030833).\n\nINTERACTION NETWORK: STRING-DB identifies 10 interaction partners with medium confidence (0.40-0.70), suggesting dynamic regulatory associations. Family paralog: FAM49B/CYRIB (0.698)—the closest homolog with overlapping function. Actin regulators: LRATD1 (0.559), RHPN2 (0.539)—both Rho GTPase-activating proteins linking to cytoskeletal remodeling. Oncogenic transcription factor: MYCN (0.506)—suggesting roles in neuroblastoma or proliferative signaling. Chromatin/DNA metabolism: FAM227B (0.496), DDX1 (0.473). Signaling regulators: SPATS2L (0.420), DCAF4L2 (0.418), PIK3C2G (0.411), PPP1R1A (0.402).\n\nEXPRESSION & LOCALIZATION: HPA confirms tissue-enhanced expression detected in all tissues with highest levels in brain (46.1 nTPM). Cancer-enhanced expression pattern suggests upregulation in tumors. Subcellular localization is nucleoplasmic—unusual for actin regulators which typically localize to cytoplasm/membrane, suggesting potential nuclear roles in transcription-coupled cytoskeletal regulation or chromatin remodeling. Low immune cell specificity.\n\nCLINICAL SIGNIFICANCE: ClinVar reports 25 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number gains (2p25.3-23.1, 2p25.3-22.3, 2p24.3-24.2) and losses (2p25.1-q13, 2p24.3-24.1) encompassing the CYRIA locus, plus 3' UTR variants (c.*2633G>T). While no specific monogenic disease is yet annotated, the variant burden at 2p suggests potential contribution to developmental disorders or cancer predisposition.\n\nFUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: CYRIA functions as a specialized Rac1 effector with dual cytoplasmic and nuclear roles: (1) Actin dynamics regulation—the conserved Rac1 binding domain (residues 18-319) binds active GTP-bound Rac1, potentially competing with or modulating WAVE complex recruitment to control Arp2/3-mediated actin polymerization. The high structural confidence (pLDDT 92.8) suggests a rigid binding interface optimized for specific Rac1 recognition. (2) Nuclear functions—nucleoplasmic localization implies non-canonical roles in chromatin-associated actin polymerization, transcriptional regulation, or DNA repair. MYCN interaction suggests involvement in neuroblastoma pathogenesis where Rac1 signaling drives proliferation. (3) Cancer enhancement—elevated expression in tumors may reflect oncogenic Rac1 pathway activation. (4) Paralog compensation—FAM49B/CYRIB interaction suggests functional redundancy, potentially explaining lack of monogenic disease annotation. Mechanistically, CYRIA likely acts as a Rac1 sink or modulator, fine-tuning spatial/temporal activation of downstream effectors. Loss-of-function would impair actin-dependent processes (cell migration, neurite outgrowth); gain-of-function could drive invasive phenotypes in cancer.
