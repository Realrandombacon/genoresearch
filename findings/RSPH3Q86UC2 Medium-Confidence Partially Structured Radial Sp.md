## Description
RSPH3 (Radial spoke head protein 3 homolog, also known as RSHL2, RSP3, CILD32, dJ111C20.1) is a 560 amino acid intracellular protein (UniProt Q86UC2) with medium-confidence partially structured architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 64.6), characteristic of proteins with ordered domains interspersed with flexible linkers that function in multi-protein complexes. The protein contains a radial spoke 3 family domain (IPR009290/PTHR21648, residues 149-558) and a radial spoke protein 3 Pfam domain (PF06098, residues 245-468) that define its role in ciliary/flagellar radial spoke assembly. RSPH3 is located on chromosome 6q25.3 and acts as a protein kinase A (PKA) anchoring protein that localizes to the flagellar centriole, mid piece, and annulus of sperm flagella.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals a comprehensive network of radial spoke and ciliary proteins with high confidence scores: NANP (0.956, N-acetylneuraminate pyruvate lyase homolog, ciliary function), PPP1R21 (0.955, Protein phosphatase 1 regulatory inhibitor subunit 21, PKA signaling), ING3 (0.954, Inhibitor of growth family member 3, chromatin regulation), WTAP (0.954, WT1-associated protein, RNA processing), RSPH4A (0.916, Radial spoke head protein 4A, direct radial spoke component), RSPH9 (0.870, Radial spoke head protein 9, direct radial spoke component), DTNA (0.840, Dystrobrevin alpha, cytoskeletal anchoring), DTNB (0.840, Dystrobrevin beta), TAGAP (0.837, T-cell activation RhoGTPase activating protein, immune cell function), and RSPH6A (0.418, Radial spoke head protein 6A). These interactions confirm RSPH3 functions within the radial spoke complex and ciliary motility machinery.\n\nHPA expression reveals RSPH3 has low tissue specificity and is detected in all tissues, with immune cell-enhanced expression in blood cells. Subcellular localization includes plasma membrane, cytosol, flagellar centriole, mid piece, and annulus. The ubiquitous expression is consistent with the presence of motile cilia in multiple tissues (respiratory epithelium, ependymal cells, fallopian tubes) and sperm flagella. RSPH3 is classified as a human disease-related gene and predicted intracellular protein.\n\nClinVar documents 60 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including large copy number gains (6q25.2-27, 6q24.2-27 amplifications), copy number losses (6q25.3-27, 6q25.2-27 deletions), and loss-of-function mutations: splice site deletions (c.346+1del), splice site SNVs (c.117-1G>C), 5'UTR deletions (c.-261_-260del), frameshift deletions (c.330del p.His110fs), and frameshift duplications (c.454dup p.Thr152fs). These variants cause primary ciliary dyskinesia (PCD), specifically CILD32 (Ciliary Dyskinesia, Primary, 32). PCD is characterized by defective axonemal structure in motile cilia and sperm flagella, leading to chronic respiratory infections, situs inversus, and male infertility.\n\nConservation is strong across vertebrates: human (Q86UC2, 560 aa), bovine (A8E4N3, 606 aa), indicating an ancient and essential function in ciliary motility. The radial spoke is a conserved axonemal structure present from Chlamydomonas to humans.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: RSPH3 functions as a structural component of the radial spoke, a T-shaped multiprotein complex that projects from the outer doublet microtubules toward the central pair apparatus in the 9+2 axoneme of motile cilia and flagella. The radial spoke transmits mechanical and chemical signals between the central pair and dynein arms, regulating ciliary beat frequency and waveform. As a PKA anchoring protein, RSPH3 localizes cAMP-dependent protein kinase to specific axonemal locations, enabling phosphorylation-dependent regulation of dynein motor activity. The partially structured architecture (pLDDT 64.6) allows RSPH3 to form flexible connections between radial spoke subunits (RSPH4A, RSPH9, RSPH6A) and the axonemal scaffold. Loss-of-function mutations disrupt radial spoke assembly, causing immotile or dyskinetic cilia, impaired mucociliary clearance, and defective sperm motility. The immune cell-enhanced expression suggests additional roles in leukocyte migration or signaling. RSPH3 represents a well-established PCD gene with diagnostic value in ciliopathy panels.
