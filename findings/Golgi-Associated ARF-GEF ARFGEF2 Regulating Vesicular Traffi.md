## Description
ARFGEF2 (ADP Ribosylation Factor Guanine Nucleotide Exchange Factor 2, alias BIG2, UniProt Q9Y6D5) is a 1785-amino acid Golgi apparatus-localized guanine nucleotide exchange factor (GEF) activating ARF family GTPases for vesicular trafficking. InterPro analysis reveals complex multidomain architecture: Sec7 catalytic domain (IPR000904/cd00171, aa 636-827) responsible for GDP-GTP exchange on ARF proteins with GO annotations for guanyl-nucleotide exchange factor activity (GO:0005085) and regulation of ARF protein signal transduction (GO:0032012); HUS domain (IPR032691, aa 371-529) for protein interactions; dimerization and cyclophilin-binding domain (IPR032629, aa 23-200); HDS domain (IPR015403, aa 1167-1248) characteristic of BIG family GEFs; and extensive armadillo-type fold regions (IPR016024/IPR011989, aa 402-1755) plus leucine-rich repeat variant domain (G3DSA:1.25.10.10, aa 1140-1480) for protein-protein interactions. STRING interactions confirm high-confidence partnerships with trafficking/cytoskeletal regulators: FLNA (0.885, filamin A actin crosslinker), PPP1CC (0.741, protein phosphatase 1), EXOC7 (0.632, exocyst complex component), MYCBP (0.632, c-Myc binding protein), ARF1 (0.632, ARF GTPase substrate), ARFGEF1 (0.583, paralogous GEF BIG1), ITCH (0.583, E3 ubiquitin ligase), ARF6 (0.551, ARF GTPase substrate), DCHS1 (0.507, protocadherin), and ERMARD (0.451). HPA shows tissue-enhanced expression (detected in all tissues) with highest expression in parathyroid gland (76.7 nTPM) and Golgi apparatus localization consistent with vesicular trafficking function. AlphaFold predicts high-confidence structure (pLDDT 76.8) for the full 1785 aa, indicating a well-folded multi-domain protein with structured catalytic and interaction domains. ClinVar documents 87 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including nonsense mutations (p.Gln1163Ter, p.Glu487Ter, p.Pro449_Asp450insTer), missense variants (p.Pro211Gln, p.Met1743Leu, p.Thr673Lys, p.Lys1574Asn, p.Glu426Lys), and splice site mutations (c.3121+5G>C), causing periventricular nodular heterotopia, progressive microcephaly, epilepsy, cardiomyopathy, and movement disorders. Literature (79 papers) reveals emerging functions: (1) Exosomal miR-1277-3p promotes AML progression via ARFGEF2/KLHL2 suppression (2025); (2) Hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis triggered by influenza B in child with ARFGEF2 mutation (2025); (3) Arf1 and ARFGEF2/Sec71 control neuroblast polarity by anchoring nonmuscle myosin II for asymmetric neural stem cell division (2025); (4) Expanding phenotypic spectrum including cardiomyopathy and movement disorder (21 citations, 2016); (5) Recessive mutations cause progressive microcephaly, epilepsy, and distinct MRI pattern with periventricular heterotopia (2018). Functional hypothesis: ARFGEF2 serves as a master regulator of Golgi-to-plasma membrane vesicular trafficking by catalyzing GDP-GTP exchange on ARF1 and ARF6 GTPases, with critical roles in neuronal migration, cortical development, and cell polarity during neurogenesis. Mechanistic proposal: The Sec7 domain (aa 636-827) binds ARF-GDP substrates and catalyzes nucleotide exchange via glutamic finger mechanism, converting ARF1/ARF6 to active GTP-bound states; active ARF-GTP recruits coat proteins (COPI, clathrin adaptors) and lipid-modifying enzymes to initiate vesicle budding from Golgi membranes; armadillo repeat regions (aa 402-1755) provide scaffolding for protein complex assembly including filamin A (FLNA) for cytoskeletal coupling; in neural progenitors, ARFGEF2-ARF1 signaling anchors nonmuscle myosin II to establish apical-basal polarity required for asymmetric division; loss-of-function mutations disrupt vesicular trafficking of adhesion molecules and signaling receptors, causing neuronal migration defects (periventricular heterotopia), reduced cortical size (microcephaly), and epileptogenesis; cardiomyopathy and movement disorder phenotypes suggest ARFGEF2 roles in cardiac development and neuromuscular junction maintenance via trafficking of ion channels and receptors.
