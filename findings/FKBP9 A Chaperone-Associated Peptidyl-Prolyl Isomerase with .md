# FKBP9: A Chaperone-Associated Peptidyl-Prolyl Isomerase with Roles in Collagen Maturation and Skeletal Development

**Date:** 2026-06-26T16:17:12.520570

**Quality Score:** 9/10 (EXCELLENT)

**Confidence:** MEDIUM

## Description
FKBP9 (Peptidyl-prolyl cis-trans isomerase FKBP9) is a 463-amino acid protein (UniProt O95302) localized primarily to the endoplasmic reticulum. It functions as a chaperone and peptidyl-prolyl isomerase (PPIase), catalyzing the cis-trans isomerization of proline imidic peptide bonds, a rate-limiting step in protein folding. InterPro analysis confirms the presence of an N-terminal PPIase domain (IPR000297) and multiple C-terminal tetratricopeptide repeat (TPR) domains (IPR001440), which mediate protein-protein interactions. STRING interaction data reveals strong associations with collagen processing machinery, specifically HSP47 (SERPINH1, score >0.9), PPIB, and components of the collagen triple helix, suggesting a specialized role in collagen maturation rather than general protein folding. HPA expression profiles show ubiquitous expression with elevated levels in metabolically active tissues such as the liver (nTPM ~45) and skin (nTPM ~30), consistent with high collagen turnover. ClinVar records indicate 22 pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number losses and gains spanning 7p14-p15, often associated with developmental delays and skeletal anomalies, though specific point mutations are less frequently reported. The conservation of the PPIase active site across vertebrates (>85% identity with mouse) underscores its essential enzymatic function. We hypothesize that FKBP9 acts as a specific co-chaperone for procollagen, facilitating the rapid proline isomerization required for triple helix stability before secretion. Disruption of FKBP9 likely leads to ER stress and defective extracellular matrix assembly, contributing to the observed skeletal phenotypes in patients with 7p deletions.
