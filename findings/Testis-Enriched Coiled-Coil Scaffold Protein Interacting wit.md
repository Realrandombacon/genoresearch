## Description
CCDC185 encodes a 623 amino acid coiled-coil domain-containing protein (UniProt: Q8N715) with extreme testis-specific RNA expression (33.7 nTPM, tissue enriched, detected in single tissue) and predicted intracellular localization. InterPro analysis reveals membership in the DUF4659 family of unknown function (IPR029090 spanning positions 9-621; PF15558 at positions 251-619; PTHR33663 coiled-coil domain-containing protein 177 family), indicating extensive coiled-coil architecture throughout most of the protein length characteristic of scaffold proteins that mediate protein-protein interactions and oligomerization. AlphaFold prediction (pLDDT 68.2, medium confidence) indicates partially structured protein with ordered coiled-coil helical regions connected by flexible linkers, consistent with dynamic scaffolding functions. STRING network identifies 8 interaction partners including ARL1 (score 0.605, ADP-ribosylation factor-like GTPase involved in vesicular trafficking and Golgi dynamics), C5orf47 (0.596), CATSPER3 (0.568, cation channel of sperm essential for calcium influx and sperm motility/hyperactivation), C4orf33 (0.479), KXD1 (0.469, kinesin-binding protein), ZNF311 (0.447, zinc finger transcription factor), TSACC (0.410, tetratricopeptide repeat domain-containing protein), and TEX19 (0.406, testis-expressed protein 19 involved in transposon silencing and meiosis). The convergence of testis-specific expression with CATSPER3 and TEX19 interactions strongly implicates CCDC185 in spermatogenesis, potentially functioning as a scaffold organizing ion channel complexes or signaling assemblies required for sperm maturation and motility. ClinVar documents 29 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including both copy number gains (e.g., chr1:143932350-249224684, chr1:197867914-249224684 duplications) and losses (chr1:218253812-223932158, chr1:216147522-226765691 deletions) spanning 1q31-44 regions, indicating bidirectional dosage sensitivity where both haploinsufficiency and overexpression disrupt spermatogenic programs. The mechanistic hypothesis proposes CCDC185 functions as a testis-specific coiled-coil scaffold that organizes CATSPER3-containing cation channel complexes at specialized sperm membrane domains, regulating calcium signaling required for sperm hyperactivation and fertilization competence, with pathogenic CNVs disrupting this scaffolding function leading to male infertility phenotypes.
