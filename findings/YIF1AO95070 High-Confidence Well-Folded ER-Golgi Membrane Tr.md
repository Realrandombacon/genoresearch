## Description
YIF1A (Yip1 interacting factor homolog A, membrane trafficking protein, also known as YIF1, YIF1P, or 54TM) is a 293 amino acid intracellular membrane protein localized to the Golgi apparatus, vesicles, and endoplasmic reticulum-Golgi intermediate compartment (UniProt O95070) with high-confidence well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 73.6). The protein contains the canonical Yif1 family domain (IPR005578/PF03878/PTHR14083, residues 12-288/57-288) forming a ~277-232 aa core module (~95-79%) that defines its role in endoplasmic reticulum to Golgi vesicle-mediated transport (GO:0006888) and endoplasmic reticulum membrane localization (GO:0005789). The YIF1 family belongs to the YIP1 interacting factor homologs, which are multi-pass transmembrane proteins that function as cargo receptors or vesicle coat components. Molecular functions include protein transport, ER-Golgi transport, and general transport processes, placing YIF1A within the fundamental secretory pathway machinery.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places YIF1A at the heart of ER-Golgi trafficking machinery: strongest interaction with RAB1A (0.771, Ras-related protein Rab-1A, a key GTPase regulating ER-to-Golgi vesicle budding and fusion), confirming its role in COPII vesicle dynamics. High confidence links include VAPB (0.738, Vesicle-associated membrane protein-associated protein B, an ER membrane protein involved in lipid transfer and membrane contact sites) and YIPF3 (0.704, Yip1 domain family member 3, a paralog involved in Golgi trafficking). Connections to other YIPF family members (YIPF5 at 0.691, YIPF7 at 0.518, YIPF4 at 0.435) indicate functional redundancy or cooperative assembly into multi-subunit complexes. VTI1B (0.659, Vesicle transport through interaction with t-SNAREs 1B) is a SNARE protein essential for vesicle fusion. XBP1 (0.585, X-box binding protein 1) is a transcription factor activated during ER stress, suggesting YIF1A may participate in the unfolded protein response. GOLPH3 (0.416, Golgi phosphoprotein 3) and RABAC1 (0.407, Rab acceptor 1) further link YIF1A to Golgi structure and Rab GTPase regulation.\n\nHPA expression reveals low tissue specificity with detection in all tissues, consistent with a fundamental housekeeping role in the secretory pathway. Subcellular location in the Golgi apparatus and vesicles confirms its trafficking function. The 'low cancer specificity' and 'low immune cell specificity' indicate it is not a tissue-specific oncogene but may be essential for general cellular function. The 'cell type enhanced' status suggests higher expression in secretory cell types.\n\nClinVar documents 12 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains and losses affecting the 11q12.1-11q13.5 region: CNV gains (11q12.1-13.3, 11q12.2-13.5, 11q13.1-13.2 duplications) and CNV losses (11p13-q25, 11q13.1-13.2 deletions). The moderate variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity. Haploinsufficiency would likely impair ER-Golgi trafficking, causing accumulation of misfolded proteins in the ER, activation of the unfolded protein response, and secretory defects, potentially leading to neurodevelopmental disorders or metabolic diseases. Duplications may cause aberrant vesicle formation or cargo sequestration, contributing to 11q deletion/duplication syndrome phenotypes (developmental delay, intellectual disability, autism spectrum disorder, facial dysmorphism).\n\nConservation is strong across vertebrates: human (O95070, 293 aa), mouse (Q91XB7, 293 aa), cow (Q3T196, 293 aa), zebrafish (Q6PC24, 307 aa), indicating evolutionary pressure to maintain the Yif1 family architecture.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: YIF1A functions as a multi-pass transmembrane cargo receptor or vesicle coat component in the ER-Golgi secretory pathway. The Yif1 family domain likely forms a transmembrane bundle that recognizes specific cargo proteins for packaging into COPII vesicles. Through interaction with RAB1A, YIF1A may regulate vesicle budding or tethering at the Golgi. The VAPB connection suggests it may also participate in membrane contact sites between ER and Golgi, facilitating lipid transfer. Cooperation with YIPF3/YIPF5/YIPF7 indicates it functions as part of a heteromeric complex that recognizes diverse cargo classes. During ER stress, YIF1A may modulate XBP1 signaling by affecting cargo flux. Dysregulation via 11q CNVs disrupts secretory pathway homeostasis: loss causes ER stress and trafficking failure (neurodegeneration, metabolic defects), while gain causes vesicle dysregulation and cargo mis-sorting, contributing to 11q syndrome phenotypes.
