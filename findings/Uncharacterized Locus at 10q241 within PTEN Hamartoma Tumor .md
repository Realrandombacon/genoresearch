## Description
LOC102723665 is an uncharacterized genomic locus on chromosome 10q24.1 (NC_000010.11:96832457-96830631) spanning approximately 1.8 kb. Comprehensive database searches reveal no evidence of canonical protein-coding function: no UniProt accession was identified, precluding InterPro domain analysis and AlphaFold structure prediction. STRING database returned no protein interaction partners, confirming absence of characterized interactome. Human Protein Atlas could not resolve this locus to an Ensembl ID, indicating no detectable protein expression in surveyed tissues. However, ClinVar analysis reveals 3 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants: two copy number losses spanning 10q23.33-24.2 (chr10:92626680-97755102, ~5.1 Mb deletion) and 10q23.33-24.32 (chr10:93181201-101356779, ~8.2 Mb deletion), plus one copy number gain spanning 10q23.33-25.3 (chr10:95112607-116776637, ~21.7 Mb duplication). The 10q24 region is the critical locus for PTEN hamartoma tumor syndrome (PHTS), including Cowden syndrome and Bannayan-Riley-Ruvalcaba syndrome, caused by germline mutations in PTEN (phosphatase and tensin homolog, tumor suppressor). The 10q24 region also contains NKX2-3 (homeobox transcription factor, intestinal development), MGMT (O-6-methylguanine-DNA methyltransferase, DNA repair), and ADORA2A (adenosine A2a receptor, inflammation). LOC102723665 likely functions as: (1) a cis-regulatory enhancer or silencer controlling PTEN expression, (2) a long non-coding RNA (lncRNA) modulating 10q24 tumor suppressor gene transcription, (3) a chromatin boundary element within the 10q24 topologically associating domain (TAD), or (4) an alternative transcript isoform of neighboring genes. The moderate pathogenic CNV burden (3 variants) despite absence of protein evidence indicates this locus resides within a critical 10q24 dosage-sensitive genomic region where copy number alterations disrupt: (1) PTEN haploinsufficiency leading to PI3K-AKT-mTOR pathway hyperactivation, (2) 10q24 chromatin architecture affecting tumor suppressor expression, (3) cell cycle control and DNA damage response, or (4) developmental processes mediated by NKX2-3, leading to hamartoma formation, macrocephaly, intellectual disability, and cancer predisposition phenotypes.
