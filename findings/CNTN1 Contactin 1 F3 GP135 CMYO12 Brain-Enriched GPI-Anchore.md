## Description
CNTN1 encodes a 1018 aa glycosylphosphatidylinositol (GPI)-anchored neuronal membrane protein (UniProt Q12860), also known as F3 or GP135, that functions as a critical cell adhesion molecule in the immunoglobulin superfamily mediating axon guidance, node of Ranvier formation, and neuronal connectivity during nervous system development while exhibiting oncogenic properties in multiple cancers. This well-studied gene (299 publications) has emerging clinical significance for ovarian and gastric cancer metastasis, CNTN1 antibody-associated neuropathy with membranous glomerulonephritis, coronary artery disease, and pulmonary arterial hypertension. The protein features extensive extracellular domain architecture: six immunoglobulin-like domains (IPR003598/IPR003599/IPR007110/cd05849/cd05727/cd05851/cd05852/cd04970, residues 40-134/142-230/241-328/413-501/503-601 with I-set classification) providing homophilic and heterophilic binding interfaces; three fibronectin type III domains (IPR003961/cd00063, residues 604-704/707-806/809-906) mediating protein-protein interactions; classified within immunoglobulin superfamily (G3DSA:2.60.40.10, residues 38-131/132-236/237-329) with GO terms for cell adhesion, protein binding, and Notch signaling pathway. STRING interactions reveal CNTN1 operates within high-confidence neuronal adhesion and signaling networks: PTPRZ1 (0.992), protein tyrosine phosphatase receptor type Z1 forming receptor complex; NRCAM (0.923), neuron-glia cell adhesion molecule paralog; NOTCH1 (0.793)/NOTCH2 (0.533), notch receptors linking to developmental signaling; NFASC (0.754), neurofascin in node of Ranvier assembly; DTX1 (0.751), deltex E3 ubiquitin ligase in Notch pathway; PTPRB (0.577), protein tyrosine phosphatase receptor type B; APP (0.504), amyloid precursor protein; L1CAM (0.459), L1 cell adhesion molecule; CNTNAP1 (0.409), contactin-associated protein 1. HPA confirms tissue-enhanced expression with brain enrichment (120.8 nTPM), detected in many tissues, cancer-enhanced expression, immune cell-enhanced in blood, classified as disease-related plasma protein involved in cell adhesion and Notch signaling. ClinVar lists 64 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large-scale copy number gains (12p13.33-q13.12 0.08-49Mb x3, 12q12-13.11 38-48Mb x3), missense mutation in Ig domain region (c.1517G>A p.Arg506Gln within cd04970 sixth Ig domain), splice site variant (c.2711-1G>A), nonsense mutations causing truncation (c.2512G>T p.Glu838Ter, c.1362G>A p.Trp454Ter, c.739C>T p.Gln247Ter), frameshift duplication (c.482dup p.Tyr162fs), missense in Ig domain (c.623A>G p.Asn208Ser), and exon deletions (EX2-15DEL, EX18-19DEL), demonstrating strong dosage sensitivity and loss-of-function mechanisms though specific monogenic diseases remain undefined beyond antibody-mediated neuropathy. AlphaFold structure prediction yields high confidence (global pLDDT 87.2), revealing a well-folded elongated structure with properly arranged Ig and FN3 domains characteristic of cell adhesion molecules requiring extended conformation for trans-interactions. Mechanistically, CNTN1 operates through integrated modes: (1) Axon guidance—homophilic CNTN1-CNTN1 interactions and heterophilic binding to PTPRZ1/NRCAM guide growing axons during development; (2) Node of Ranvier formation—complex with NFASC and CNTNAP1 clusters voltage-gated sodium channels at nodes enabling saltatory conduction; (3) Ovarian cancer metastasis—2025 study demonstrates CNTN1 promotes cell proliferation and metastasis through PSEN1 (presenilin-1) interaction enhancing gamma-secretase activity; (4) Gastric cancer progression—2025 study shows PFKM upregulates CNTN1 via H3K18 lactylation modification driving tumor progression; (5) Antibody-associated neuropathy—2024-2025 case reports identify anti-CNTN1 antibodies causing acute sensorimotor neuropathy with membranous glomerulonephritis, establishing autoimmune pathogenesis; (6) Notch signaling—interaction with NOTCH1/NOTCH2 and DTX1 links CNTN1 to developmental patterning; (7) Cardiovascular biomarker—2024 study identifies CNTN1 as co-pathogenic target in coronary artery disease and pulmonary arterial hypertension with diagnostic potential. Loss-of-function causes defective axon guidance, impaired node formation, peripheral neuropathy, while overexpression drives cancer metastasis through enhanced cell adhesion, Notch activation, and gamma-secretase modulation.
