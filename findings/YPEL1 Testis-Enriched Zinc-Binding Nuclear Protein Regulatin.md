## Description
YPEL1 (UniProt O60688), also known as Yippee-Like 1, encodes a compact 119 aa nuclear protein belonging to the evolutionarily conserved Yippee/Mis18/Cereblon family, functioning as a critical regulator of cell cycle progression, cellular senescence, and craniofacial development through zinc-dependent chromatin interactions. InterPro analysis reveals a highly conserved single-domain architecture: a canonical Yippee zinc-binding/DNA-binding domain (aa 21-111, IPR004910, PF03226, PS51792) characterized by a cysteine-rich motif capable of coordinating zinc ions to stabilize a fold essential for DNA or chromatin binding; classification within the Yippee family (IPR039058, aa 8-117) links it functionally to Mis18 (centromere assembly) and Cereblon (E3 ligase substrate receptor), suggesting roles in epigenetic regulation or protein quality control. AlphaFold predicts an exceptionally high-confidence structure (global pLDDT 90.7), indicating a rigid, well-folded globular domain with precise geometry for metal coordination and molecular recognition—a hallmark of stable nuclear factors involved in essential cellular processes. STRING interactions define YPEL1 as a node connecting cell cycle regulation, ubiquitination, and neuronal adhesion: WDR26 (0.630, G-beta-like scaffold), FBXL2 (0.624, F-box ubiquitin ligase), STRN3/STRN4 (0.589/0.545, striatins regulating PP2A phosphatase), CTTNBP2NL (0.574), FBXL20 (0.511), C1QL4/C1QL2 (0.454/0.404, complement C1q-like proteins), ZNF564 (0.436), and NCAM1 (0.416, neural cell adhesion molecule), suggesting diverse roles from protein degradation (FBXLs) to neuronal connectivity (C1QLs, NCAM1). HPA expression shows testis enrichment (149.8 nTPM), classified as tissue-enriched and detected in all tissues but highest in reproductive organs, consistent with roles in gametogenesis or meiotic regulation; also classified as an essential protein, implying non-redundant functions in cell viability. ClinVar reports 47 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains and losses on chromosome 22q11.21-11.23 (spanning the YPEL1 locus), indicating genomic instability at this region potentially linked to 22q11.2 deletion syndrome (DiGeorge/Velocardiofacial syndrome) phenotypes including craniofacial dysmorphology, though no specific monogenic disease has been definitively assigned to YPEL1 alone. Literature (26 papers) highlights: (1) Cancer Suppression—YPEL1 inhibits gemcitabine resistance in NK/T cell lymphomas and is downregulated in pancreatic cancer, acting as a tumor suppressor; (2) Gastric Cancer Progression—MiR-885-5p promotes gastric cancer proliferation by targeting and suppressing YPEL1, confirming its anti-proliferative role; (3) Craniofacial Development—overexpression in avian embryos causes mandibular dysmorphogenesis via upregulation of apoptosis, demonstrating a dosage-sensitive role in facial patterning; (4) Senescence Induction—YPEL1 is a known mediator of oncogene-induced senescence, arresting cell cycle progression to prevent malignant transformation. Functional hypothesis: YPEL1 functions as a: (1) Chromatin Stability Factor—binds DNA or histones via its zinc finger to maintain centromeric or pericentromeric integrity, preventing chromosomal instability; (2) Senescence Effector—accumulates in response to oncogenic stress to enforce cell cycle arrest, potentially by stabilizing p53 or inhibiting E2F targets; (3) Developmental Morphogen—regulates apoptosis gradients in craniofacial mesenchyme to sculpt facial structures, with dosage sensitivity explaining 22q11.2 syndrome phenotypes; (4) Ubiquitination Coordinator—interacts with F-box proteins (FBXL2/20) to target specific substrates for degradation, linking chromatin state to protein turnover. Mechanistic proposal: In the nucleus, YPEL1 utilizes its zinc-coordinating domain to bind specific DNA sequences or modified histones, recruiting chromatin remodelers or blocking transcription factor access to induce senescence. Under oncogenic stress, YPEL1 levels rise, interacting with WDR26 or striatins to modulate signaling pathways (e.g., MAPK, PP2A) that halt proliferation. In development, precise YPEL1 levels are required to balance survival and apoptosis in neural crest-derived mesenchyme; haploinsufficiency (via 22q11.2 deletion) reduces apoptotic pruning, leading to dysmorphic features, while overexpression triggers excessive cell death. The very high-confidence structure (pLDDT 90.7) supports a stable platform for zinc binding and protein-protein interactions with FBXLs and striatins. Chromosome 22q CNVs disrupt YPEL1 dosage, contributing to the pleiotropic effects of 22q11.2 deletion syndrome. The combination of a rigid zinc-binding fold, interactions with cell cycle and ubiquitination machinery, testis-enriched essential expression, high clinical variant burden, and demonstrated roles in senescence and craniofacial morphogenesis positions YPEL1 as a genuine dark gene requiring mechanistic dissection of its contribution to cancer suppression and congenital heart/craniofacial defects.
