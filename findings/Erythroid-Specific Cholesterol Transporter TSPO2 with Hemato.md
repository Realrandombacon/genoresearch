# Erythroid-Specific Cholesterol Transporter TSPO2 with Hematopoietic Maturation Role

**Quality Score:** 9/10 (EXCELLENT)

**Confidence:** MEDIUM

## Description
TSPO2 (Translocator Protein 2) is a 170-amino acid membrane protein specifically expressed in late-stage erythroblasts, functioning as a cholesterol-binding transporter essential for erythroid maturation and proliferation. InterPro analysis confirms its classification within the TspO/MBR family with domains IPR004307 (TspO/MBR-related protein, positions 4-156), PF03073 (TspO/MBR family, 8-154), and PIRSF005859 (Peripheral-type benzodiazepine receptor), sharing structural homology with the mitochondrial TSPO but restricted to the plasma membrane in erythroid lineage. STRING interactions reveal high-confidence associations with erythroid markers: GYPA (Glycophorin A, 0.710), VDAC1 (0.675), GYPB (0.645), TRIM10 (0.582), and transcription factor KLF1 (0.486), suggesting a functional complex coordinating cholesterol redistribution during terminal differentiation. HPA data shows strict tissue-enriched expression in bone marrow (47.3 nTPM) with cancer-enhanced patterns in hematologic malignancies, consistent with its role in erythropoiesis. ClinVar lists 6 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variants (losses and gains on 6p), though specific monogenic disease phenotypes remain under characterization; literature links TSPO2 mutations to impaired erythroid maturation in canine models and potential human analogs. AlphaFold structure (AF-Q5TGU0-F1) predicts a medium-confidence fold (pLDDT 68.2) with five transmembrane helices characteristic of the MBR family, indicating a stable pore-like architecture for lipid transport. With only 28 papers, TSPO2 represents a partially characterized 'gray' gene: while its general function in cholesterol transport is known, its specific regulatory mechanisms, interaction partners beyond GYPA/VDAC1, and precise contribution to human anemias or erythroleukemias remain largely unexplored.
