# Centrosomal Protein 85 kDa (CEP85/CCDC21): Coiled-Coil Scaffold Regulating Centriole Duplication and PLK4-STIL Recruitment Pathway

**Date:** 2026-06-17T10:35:15.776904

**Quality Score:** 9/10 (EXCELLENT)

**Confidence:** HIGH

## Description
CEP85 encodes a 762 aa intracellular scaffold protein (UniProt Q6P2H3) characterized by a conserved CC4 coiled-coil domain (IPR058190, PF24555, residues 567-650) that mediates homo- or hetero-oligomerization typical of centrosomal structural proteins. CEP85 belongs to the Cep85/Cep85L family (IPR040210, PTHR31075) and is also known as CCDC21 (coiled-coil domain-containing protein 21). STRING interactions place CEP85 at the heart of the centriole duplication machinery with high-confidence partnerships to core centrosomal regulators: CEP192 (0.847), a master scaffold recruiting PLK1 and CDK2 to centrosomes; STIL (0.845), the key initiator of procentriole assembly; PLK4 (0.733), the master kinase controlling centriole number; CEP152 (0.672), the receptor anchoring PLK4 at the proximal end of mother centrioles; OFD1 (0.613), mutated in oral-facial-digital syndrome; RTTN (0.588), required for centriole elongation; and CEP131 (0.582), involved in ciliogenesis. HPA shows tissue-enhanced expression peaking in skeletal muscle (124.5 nTPM) and tongue (61.6 nTPM) with complex subcellular localization including centrosome, nucleoli, Golgi apparatus, and vesicles—suggesting dynamic trafficking during cell cycle progression. ClinVar lists 7 pathogenic variants including large copy number gains/deletions spanning chromosome 1p36 and a missense variant (p.Ser19Ile), implicating CEP85 dosage sensitivity in developmental disorders though no specific disease phenotype has been definitively assigned. AlphaFold structure prediction yields medium confidence (global pLDDT 66.9), consistent with a predominantly coiled-coil architecture interspersed with flexible linker regions—a hallmark of centrosomal scaffolds requiring conformational adaptability for multi-protein complex assembly. Mechanistically, CEP85 likely functions as a regulatory scaffold in the PLK4-STIL-CEP152 axis that controls centriole duplication: (1) The CC4 coiled-coil domain enables CEP85 oligomerization, creating a platform for recruiting multiple partners simultaneously; (2) CEP85 may stabilize STIL at the centriole, preventing its premature degradation and ensuring proper procentriole assembly; (3) The interaction with CEP192 suggests CEP85 participates in the early recruitment cascade that licenses centriole duplication once per cell cycle; (4) Localization to Golgi and vesicles hints at a potential role in trafficking centrosomal components or in ciliary vesicle formation during ciliogenesis. Disruption of CEP85 would cause centriole amplification or loss, spindle assembly defects, chromosome missegregation, and potentially ciliopathy phenotypes—consistent with the pathogenic CNVs observed in patients with 1p36 deletion syndrome.
