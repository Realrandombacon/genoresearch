## Description
MBOAT1 (Membrane bound glycerophospholipid O-acyltransferase 1, also known as LPEAT1, LPLAT1, LPSAT, OACT1) is a 495 amino acid multi-pass membrane enzyme (UniProt Q6ZNC8) with high-confidence well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 89.6), characteristic of the membrane-bound O-acyltransferase (MBOAT) superfamily. The protein defines the MBOAT family (IPR004292/PF03062/PTHR13906, residues 126-436) and Lysophospholipid acyltransferase 7/Porcupine-like family (IPR049941, residues 17-480). MBOAT1 catalyzes the transfer of acyl groups from acyl-CoA donors to lysophospholipids, specifically preferring oleoyl-CoA to esterify lysophosphatidylethanolamine (LPE) and lysophosphatidylserine (LPS). This Lands cycle remodeling maintains membrane phospholipid composition and fluidity. Molecular functions include acyltransferase activity, lipid biosynthesis, and phospholipid metabolism, placing MBOAT1 within the glycerophospholipid remodeling pathway.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals an exceptionally tight network of phospholipid biosynthesis enzymes: strongest interactions with CEPT1 (0.940, Choline/ethanolamine phosphotransferase 1, final step in PC/PE synthesis), AGPAT1 (0.938, 1-acylglycerol-3-phosphate O-acyltransferase 1, phosphatidic acid synthesis), AGPAT2 (0.931, AGPAT2, congenital generalized lipodystrophy gene), PISD (0.925, Phosphatidylserine decarboxylase, PS to PE conversion), PLB1 (0.921, Phospholipase B1, phospholipid degradation), and LCLAT1 (0.911, Lysocardiolipin acyltransferase 1, cardiolipin remodeling). Additional links include AGPAT3 (0.636), AGPAT5 (0.630), AGPAT4 (0.601), and LPCAT4 (0.551). This profile confirms MBOAT1 as a central node in phospholipid metabolism, physically and functionally coupled to enzymes that synthesize, remodel, and degrade membrane lipids.\n\nHPA expression reveals low tissue specificity, detected in all tissues, consistent with the universal requirement for phospholipid homeostasis in all cell membranes. Low cancer and immune cell specificity indicate MBOAT1 is a housekeeping enzyme rather than a disease driver, though dysregulation may contribute to metabolic disorders.\n\nClinVar documents 13 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, exclusively large copy number gains and losses affecting chromosome 6p (6p24.1-22.3 deletions, 6p25.3-22.3 duplications). The variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity. While no specific Mendelian disease is annotated, MBOAT1 haploinsufficiency or overexpression likely causes phospholipid metabolism disorders. 6p CNVs are associated with developmental delay, craniofacial abnormalities, and brachydactyly syndactyly syndrome (noted in gene summary); MBOAT1 disruption may impair membrane composition in developing tissues, contributing to skeletal and neurological phenotypes.\n\nConservation is strong across vertebrates: human (Q6ZNC8, 495 aa), mouse (Q8BH98, 492 aa), tilapia (I3JJ69, 509 aa), medaka (H2LJR3, 515 aa), indicating evolutionary pressure to maintain the acyltransferase catalytic machinery.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: MBOAT1 functions as a lysophospholipid acyltransferase that remodels membrane phospholipids by reacylating lysophospholipid intermediates generated by phospholipase activity. The enzyme likely localizes to the endoplasmic reticulum, where it cooperates with AGPAT enzymes to maintain phosphatidylethanolamine and phosphatidylserine pools. CEPT1 interaction suggests MBOAT1 provides substrate for final phospholipid synthesis steps. PISD interaction indicates coordination between phospholipid synthesis and decarboxylation pathways. Loss of MBOAT1 disrupts the Lands cycle, causing accumulation of lysophospholipids (detergent-like, membrane-disrupting) and depletion of mature phospholipids, leading to membrane instability, ER stress, and impaired vesicle trafficking. Overexpression may alter membrane fatty acid composition, affecting fluidity and protein function. MBOAT1 represents a potential biomarker for 6p CNV syndromes and a target for modulating membrane lipid composition in metabolic diseases.
