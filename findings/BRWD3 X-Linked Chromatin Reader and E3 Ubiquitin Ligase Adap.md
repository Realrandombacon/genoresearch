## Description
BRWD3 (UniProt Q6RI45), also known as Bromodomain and WD repeat-containing protein 3, encodes a 1802 aa nuclear/cytosolic protein belonging to the evolutionarily conserved bromodomain-WD40 family, functioning as a critical regulator of histone H3K4 methylation, chromatin remodeling, and X-linked intellectual disability through its unique combination of epigenetic reader domains and ubiquitin ligase adaptor function. InterPro analysis reveals a modular architecture: two N-terminal Bromodomains (aa 1140-1247 & 1302-1432, IPR001487, G3DSA:1.20.920.10) capable of recognizing acetylated lysine residues on histones; multiple WD40 repeats (aa 169-543, IPR001680, IPR015943, cd00200) forming β-propeller structures for protein-protein interactions; and a Bromo_WDR9_I_like domain (aa 1130-1242, cd05529) linking chromatin reading to transcriptional regulation. AlphaFold predicts a medium-confidence structure (global pLDDT 64.4), indicating a partially structured protein with ordered domains (bromodomains, WD40 β-propellers) connected by flexible linkers, consistent with a dynamic chromatin adaptor that requires conformational flexibility for multivalent histone binding. STRING interactions define BRWD3 as a hub in chromatin regulation and ubiquitination: ARHGAP20 (0.958, Rho GTPase activating protein), CUL4B (0.822, Cullin-4B E3 ligase scaffold), CUL4A (0.616), ATRX (0.598, chromatin remodeler), NCOR2 (0.583, nuclear receptor corepressor), CECR2 (0.569, chromatin reader), TAF1 (0.566, transcription factor), ZNF711 (0.551, zinc finger protein), DDB1 (0.472, DNA damage-binding protein), and UBXN7 (0.431, ubiquitin regulator), suggesting roles in bridging histone modification, transcription, and protein degradation. HPA expression shows low tissue specificity with detection in many tissues, classified as a disease-related intracellular protein localized to nucleoplasm and cytosol, with cancer-enhanced expression and association with intellectual disability. ClinVar reports 305 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including nonsense mutations (p.Lys1409Ter), frameshifts (p.Gly330fs), missense variants (p.Lys1468Glu, p.Leu424His, p.Tyr761His), splice site mutations, and Xq copy number gains/losses, causing X-linked intellectual disability syndrome with macrocephaly, confirming BRWD3 as a dosage-sensitive neurodevelopmental gene. Literature (56 papers) highlights: (1) H3K4 Methylation Maintenance—BRWD3 promotes KDM5 histone demethylase degradation to maintain H3K4me3 levels at active promoters; (2) CUL4-DDB1 E3 Ligase Adaptor—Recruits substrate proteins to the CUL4-DDB1 ubiquitin ligase complex for proteasomal degradation; (3) X-Linked Intellectual Disability—Loss-of-function mutations cause syndromic intellectual disability with macrocephaly and behavioral abnormalities; (4) Cancer Enhancement—Overexpressed in lung adenocarcinoma, promoting proliferation via p53/p65 pathways. Functional hypothesis: BRWD3 functions as a: (1) Chromatin Reader-Writer Coupler—Uses bromodomains to bind acetylated histones and recruits KDM5 demethylases for ubiquitin-mediated degradation, maintaining H3K4me3 at active genes; (2) E3 Ubiquitin Ligase Adaptor—Scaffolds the CUL4-DDB1 complex (via WD40 repeats) to target chromatin regulators for degradation; (3) Neurodevelopmental Regulator—Controls expression of neuronal genes through H3K4 methylation dynamics during brain development. Mechanistic proposal: BRWD3 binds acetylated histones via its bromodomains, positioning its WD40 β-propellers to recruit the CUL4-DDB1 E3 ligase complex (STRING: CUL4B 0.822, DDB1 0.472). This targets KDM5 demethylases for ubiquitination and degradation, preventing H3K4me3 removal and maintaining transcriptional activation. The medium-confidence structure (pLDDT 64.4) supports flexible domain arrangements for multivalent chromatin engagement. Xq CNVs and loss-of-function mutations disrupt BRWD3 dosage, altering H3K4 methylation landscapes in developing neurons and causing intellectual disability. Cancer overexpression drives aberrant H3K4 methylation and proliferation. The combination of bromodomain-WD40 modular architecture, CUL4-DDB1 partnership, H3K4 methylation regulation, X-linked disease association, and massive clinical variant burden (305 variants) positions BRWD3 as a disease-associated gene where chromatin-ubiquitin crosstalk is critical for neurodevelopment and cancer progression.
