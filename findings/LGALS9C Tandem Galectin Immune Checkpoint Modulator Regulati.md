## Description
LGALS9C (UniProt Q6DKI2) encodes a 356 aa tandem-repeat galectin protein that functions as a critical immune checkpoint modulator in gastrointestinal mucosal immunity and tumor immune evasion. InterPro analysis reveals a specialized domain architecture characteristic of tandem-repeat galectins: two carbohydrate recognition domains (CRDs) positioned at aa 16-146 and 227-355 (IPR001079, PF00337, SM00276, cd00070 GLECT domains), connected by a flexible linker region (~80 aa), enabling bivalent carbohydrate binding and cross-linking of cell surface glycoproteins; classification within the galectin family (IPR013320, IPR044156, PTHR11346) with GO annotations for carbohydrate binding (GO:0030246) and concanavalin A-like lectin fold (G3DSA:2.60.120.200, SSF49899). AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (pLDDT 84.2), confirming a well-folded tandem galectin architecture capable of stable glycan recognition and protein-protein interactions. STRING interactions define LGALS9C as a central hub in the immune checkpoint network with remarkably high confidence scores: CLEC7A/Dectin-1 (0.983), a C-type lectin receptor on dendritic cells and macrophages; CTLA4 (0.973), the critical T cell inhibitory receptor; TIGIT (0.951), an emerging checkpoint receptor on exhausted T cells and NK cells; PDCD1/PD-1 (0.948), the canonical checkpoint receptor targeted by immunotherapy; CD44 (0.935), a hyaluronan receptor involved in T cell trafficking and activation; CD274/PD-L1 (0.875), the PD-1 ligand expressed on tumor cells; TNFRSF9/4-1BB (0.805), a costimulatory TNF receptor; BTLA (0.805), another inhibitory checkpoint receptor; HAVCR2/TIM-3 (0.661), a known galectin-9 binding partner; and LAG3 (0.589), an additional checkpoint receptor. This interaction profile strongly suggests LGALS9C functions as an endogenous ligand for multiple inhibitory checkpoints, analogous to LGALS9 (galectin-9) binding TIM-3. HPA expression shows tissue-enhanced expression in gastrointestinal tissues: stomach (10.8 nTPM), esophagus (10.3 nTPM), and intestine (9.2 nTPM), with cancer-enhanced expression pattern and cytosolic subcellular localization, suggesting roles in mucosal immune surveillance and gastrointestinal tumor immunity. ClinVar reports 52 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large 17p11.2 copy number losses/gains (Potocki-Lupski/Smith-Magenis syndrome regions), though no monogenic disease is yet assigned solely to LGALS9C point mutations, suggesting dosage sensitivity in immune regulation. Literature (only 6 papers) is remarkably sparse despite the compelling interaction data, with emerging links to: (1) Rectal adenocarcinoma prognosis signatures; (2) DNA methylation associations with lipid metabolism; (3) EGFR-mutated lung adenocarcinoma PD-L1 stratification; (4) Multiple sclerosis radiologically isolated syndrome transcriptomics. Functional hypothesis: LGALS9C functions through: (1) Multi-Checkpoint Ligand—acts as an endogenous glycan-binding ligand simultaneously engaging PD-1, CTLA-4, TIGIT, and TIM-3 on exhausted T cells, amplifying inhibitory signaling beyond single-checkpoint blockade; (2) T Cell Exhaustion Driver—by cross-linking multiple inhibitory receptors, LGALS9C promotes terminal T cell exhaustion in chronic infection and cancer, limiting anti-tumor immunity; (3) Mucosal Immune Tolerance—gastrointestinal expression suggests LGALS9C maintains tolerance to commensal microbiota and dietary antigens via checkpoint-mediated T cell suppression; (4) Tumor Immune Evasion—cancer-enhanced expression indicates tumors exploit LGALS9C to suppress infiltrating lymphocytes, analogous to PD-L1 upregulation; (5) Therapeutic Target—LGALS9C blockade could synergize with PD-1/CTLA-4 inhibitors by preventing compensatory checkpoint engagement, particularly in gastrointestinal cancers. The high-confidence tandem galectin structure, exceptional immune checkpoint interaction network, gastrointestinal tissue specificity, and minimal literature (confirming genuine dark gene status despite functional importance) position LGALS9C as a high-priority therapeutic target in cancer immunotherapy and autoimmune disease.
