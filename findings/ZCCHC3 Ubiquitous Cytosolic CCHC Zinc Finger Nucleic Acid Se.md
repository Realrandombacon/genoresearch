## Description
ZCCHC3 (Zinc Finger CCHC-Type Containing 3, also C20orf99) is a 403 amino acid cytoplasmic protein (UniProt Q9NUD5) with a partially structured conformation (AlphaFold pLDDT 65.8, medium confidence), functioning as a pattern recognition receptor (PRR) that detects cytosolic double-stranded DNA and RNA to activate innate immune responses and antiviral defense pathways.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals ZCCHC3 contains multiple nucleic acid-binding domains: two novel RNA-binding domains (IPR057810/PF23057, residues 164-242; IPR057811/PF23058, residues 257-328) unique to this protein family, and C-terminal CCHC-type zinc fingers (IPR001878/IPR036875/PF00098/PS50158, residues 334-387) with zinc knuckle motifs (residues 352-367/371-387) that coordinate zinc ions (GO:0008270) for nucleic acid recognition (GO:0003676). The protein belongs to the Zinc Finger CCHC Domain-Containing Protein 3 family (IPR042509, residues 25-395) and mediates double-stranded DNA binding (GO:0003690), RNA binding (GO:0003723), and activation of innate immune response (GO:0002218). The medium-confidence AlphaFold structure reflects flexible linkers between domains enabling adaptation to diverse nucleic acid structures.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals ZCCHC3 functions within the cytosolic nucleic acid sensing network: strongest interactions with IFIH1 (0.767, interferon-induced helicase C domain-containing protein 1/MDA5, viral RNA sensor), CGAS (0.699, cyclic GMP-AMP synthase, cytosolic DNA sensor), SERBP1 (0.680, SERPINE1 mRNA-binding protein 1), UPF1 (0.569, up-frameshift protein 1, RNA surveillance), PAIP1 (0.507, poly(A)-binding protein-interacting protein 1), PABPC1 (0.499), DDX58 (0.473, RIG-I, viral RNA sensor), TRIM56 (0.428, E3 ubiquitin ligase), MOV10 (0.418, RNA helicase), and TRIM25 (0.400, E3 ligase that activates RIG-I). The IFIH1-CGAS-DDX58 triad represents the core cytosolic PRR machinery, indicating ZCCHC3 acts as a co-sensor or signal amplifier.\n\nHPA expression shows ubiquitous detection in all tissues with low tissue and cancer specificity, consistent with a housekeeping immune surveillance protein. Subcellular localization includes vesicles, suggesting association with endosomal compartments where nucleic acid sensing occurs. The protein is classified under antiviral defense, innate immunity, DNA-binding, and RNA-binding functions.\n\nClinVar documents 49 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, exclusively large chromosome 20p13 copy number variations including deletions (20p13, 61569-1091477 del) and duplications (20p13-11.21 gains). The high variant count suggests extreme dosage sensitivity, with haploinsufficiency potentially causing immunodeficiency, increased viral susceptibility, or autoinflammatory disorders through disrupted nucleic acid sensing.\n\nConservation is evident from mouse (Q8BPK2, 400 aa) and other mammals, indicating ancient evolutionary conservation of cytosolic surveillance mechanisms.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: ZCCHC3 acts as a co-receptor that enhances sensitivity of primary PRRs (MDA5, RIG-I, cGAS) to cytosolic nucleic acids. The two novel RNA-binding domains recognize specific structural motifs in viral RNA or mislocalized self-RNA, while CCHC zinc fingers stabilize binding through zinc coordination. Upon nucleic acid engagement, ZCCHC3 oligomerizes and recruits IFIH1 or DDX58 via protein-protein interactions, lowering the activation threshold for interferon production. CGAS interaction suggests ZCCHC3 also participates in DNA sensing, potentially bridging RNA and DNA pathways. TRIM25/TRIM56 interactions indicate ZCCHC3 undergoes ubiquitination for signal amplification, similar to RIG-I activation. Vesicle localization suggests ZCCHC3 monitors endosomal compartments for viral entry. The medium-confidence structure (pLDDT 65.8) reflects conformational flexibility required for recognizing diverse nucleic acid structures. CNV-mediated haploinsufficiency impairs viral detection, causing recurrent infections, while overexpression may trigger autoinflammation through aberrant self-nucleic acid recognition. The 20p13 locus is a hotspot for developmental disorders, suggesting ZCCHC3 dosage affects both immunity and neurodevelopment.
