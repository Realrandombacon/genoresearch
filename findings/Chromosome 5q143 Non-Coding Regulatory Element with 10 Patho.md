The 5q14.3 chromosomal region contains MEF2C (myocyte enhancer factor 2C, MADS-box transcription factor, neurodevelopment, cortical development, GABAergic neuron differentiation, synaptic function) causing MEF2C haploinsufficiency syndrome (severe intellectual disability, epilepsy, movement disorders, autism spectrum disorder, stereotypic movements, hypotonia, absent speech, delayed motor development), as well as CABLES1 (Cdk5 and Abl enzyme substrate 1, cell cycle regulation, tumor suppressor), ARRDC3 (arrestin domain containing 3, ubiquitin ligase adaptor, metabolic regulation), CCNH (cyclin H, CDK7 holoenzyme, transcription initiation, cell cycle), and MIR9-2 (microRNA-9-2, neuronal development, neurogenesis). Functional hypothesis: LOC101929380 likely represents a critical cis-regulatory enhancer or chromatin organizer controlling MEF2C expression in developing cortex and GABAergic interneurons. The 10 pathogenic CNVs indicate strong dosage sensitivity where heterozygous deletion causes MEF2C haploinsufficiency leading to severe neurodevelopmental phenotypes. Disease mechanism involves reduced MEF2C transcriptional activity disrupting cortical progenitor proliferation, neuronal migration, and GABAergic synapse formation. MEF2C regulates activity-dependent gene expression programs essential for excitatory-inhibitory balance in cortical circuits. Heterozygous loss causes reduced dendritic arborization, impaired synaptic plasticity, and altered neuronal network activity manifesting as intellectual disability, epilepsy, and autism. The 5q14.3 deletion syndrome phenotype includes severe developmental delay, hypotonia, seizures, and behavioral abnormalities. The absence of protein-coding features combined with ClinVar evidence (10 CNV variants) and MEF2C neurodevelopmental gene neighborhood suggests this locus functions as a long-range enhancer regulating MEF2C transcription through chromatin looping, enhancer RNA production, or topologically associating domain (TAD) boundary maintenance critical for cortical development.
