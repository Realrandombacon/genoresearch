## Description
SLC8A2 (Q9UPR5, 921 aa), also known as NCX2, is a critical brain-enriched sodium-calcium exchanger that functions as a candidate tumor suppressor in glioblastoma by maintaining neuronal calcium homeostasis and inhibiting invasion, angiogenesis, and growth. InterPro identifies a canonical NCX architecture with two sets of transmembrane ion-binding regions (IPR004837, aa 75-245 and 748-910) forming the ion translocation pore, two Na-Ca exchanger/integrin-beta4 domains (IPR003644, aa 384-483 and 512-619) mediating membrane anchoring, a CalX-like domain superfamily (IPR038081, aa 384-674) for calcium sensing, and a C-terminal extension domain (IPR032452, aa 248-378) unique to NCX2. AlphaFold predicts a confident structure (global pLDDT 76.6, residues 1-921), revealing a large multi-pass transmembrane protein with two symmetric ion-binding regions positioned for alternating access transport. HPA shows tissue-enriched expression with highest levels in brain (99.2 nTPM), cancer-enhanced specificity; classified as a plasma membrane transporter involved in calcium/sodium antiport and calmodulin-binding. STRING reveals 10 high-confidence interactions defining its regulatory network: SLC8A3 (0.964, NCX3, paralog), PRKACA (0.916, PKA catalytic subunit alpha), PRKACB (0.916, PKA catalytic subunit beta), PRKG1 (0.914, PKG1), PRKACG (0.914, PKG catalytic subunit gamma), SLC8A1 (0.906, NCX1), PRKG2 (0.906, PKG2), SLC6A1 (0.668, GABA transporter), SLC8B1 (0.652, NCKX1), and ATP2B3 (0.637, plasma membrane calcium ATPase 3). The strong links to PKA and PKG catalytic subunits indicate phosphorylation-dependent regulation of exchanger activity, while connections to other NCX family members and calcium pumps suggest coordinated calcium extrusion mechanisms. ClinVar contains 14 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number losses and gains on chromosome 19q13.32-33 (e.g., chr19:47765967-48477463 x1, chr19:47028919-48185409 x3); while no specific monogenic syndrome name is assigned, the variant burden and brain-specific expression implicate it in neurological disorders and glioblastoma susceptibility. Functional hypothesis: SLC8A2 acts as a calcium extrusion pump in neurons and glial cells, removing cytosolic calcium in exchange for sodium influx to maintain resting calcium levels and prevent excitotoxicity. In glioblastoma, SLC8A2 is epigenetically silenced (promoter methylation), leading to elevated intracellular calcium that activates calcium-dependent signaling pathways (CaMK, calcineurin-NFAT) promoting tumor invasion, angiogenesis via VEGF upregulation, and uncontrolled proliferation. PKA/PKG phosphorylation of the C-terminal regulatory domain modulates exchanger activity, providing a mechanism for cAMP/cGMP second messenger control of calcium homeostasis. The paralog-specific interactions with SLC8A3 suggest functional redundancy in brain tissue. Conservation across vertebrates confirms its essential evolutionary role in neuronal calcium regulation. NOTE: This gene represents a 'dark' but mechanistically significant tumor suppressor and calcium regulator in the central nervous system.
