## Description
GPR146 (333 aa, UniProt Q96CH1), also known as PGR8, is an orphan class A rhodopsin-like G protein-coupled receptor (GPCR) residing in the plasma membrane that functions as a critical regulator of cholesterol metabolism, blood pressure homeostasis, and vascular remodeling through Gi/o-mediated adenylate cyclase inhibition and ERK1/2 signaling pathway modulation. The protein architecture features canonical 7-transmembrane helix topology characteristic of rhodopsin-family GPCRs: G protein-coupled receptor 146 class A family domain (cd14990, aa39-307) containing the ligand-binding pocket; rhodopsin 7-helix transmembrane superfamily (G3DSA:1.20.1070.10, aa20-314); GPCR rhodopsin-like family (IPR000276/PF00001, aa45-296/aa45-296) with seven conserved transmembrane segments; GPCR rhodopsin-like 7TM domain (IPR017452, aa44-296); G-protein coupled receptor 146-specific family signature (IPR037487, aa39-307); G-protein coupled estrogen receptor 1-like family (IPR047143/PTHR24226, aa1-322) suggesting structural relationship to GPER1; PS50262 GPCR family 1 profile (aa44-296); PR00237 GPCRRHODOPSN fingerprints at aa62-83, aa109-131, aa231-255 representing conserved motifs across TM helices; SSF81321 Family A GPCR-like homologous superfamily. HPA confirms tissue-enhanced expression with adipose tissue enrichment (43.1 nTPM), ubiquitous detection across all tissues, low cancer specificity, group-enriched blood cell expression, cell type-enhanced single cell specificity, plasma membrane localization, and classification as a G-protein coupled receptor/receptor/transducer with molecular function in adenylate cyclase-inhibiting GPCR signaling pathway and cholesterol biosynthetic process regulation. Mechanistically, GPR146 forms medium-confidence interactions within the orphan GPCR network: GPR107 (STRING 0.665) is a Golgi-localized orphan GPCR involved in toxin retrograde transport; GPR139 (0.619) is an orphan GPCR activated by amino acids L-tryptophan and L-phenylalanine regulating opioid withdrawal; GPR153 (0.473), GPR142 (0.453), GPR82 (0.445), GPR148 (0.438), and GPR162 (0.411) represent orphan class A GPCRs with unknown ligands; PSMG3 (0.449) is a proteasome assembly chaperone; TRH (0.426) is thyrotropin-releasing hormone neuropeptide; NMU (0.424) is neuromedin U neuropeptide ligand for GPR66/NMU receptors. AlphaFold prediction (pLDDT 78.1) reveals high-confidence well-folded 7-transmembrane helix bundle structure with extracellular N-terminus and intracellular C-terminus consistent with crystallographic structures of class A GPCRs showing characteristic helical arrangement forming ligand-binding cavity and G protein coupling interface. Clinical significance includes 41 pathogenic/likely pathogenic ClinVar variants: large copy number gains spanning 7p22.3-21.3/7p22.3-14.3/7p22.3-22.1/7p22.3-21.1 (chr7:41312-12292675, 158725-29918785, 43361-5965440, 43360-9649794, 43360-19485604 amplifications), copy number losses at 7p22.3-22.2/7p22.3 (chr7:41421-3873619, 43360-2020306 deletions), large deletion (NC_000007.13:g.(?_193200)_(1498962_?)del), and complex rearrangements; reduced GPR146 expression associates with reductions in both LDL and HDL cholesterol in humans. Conservation across mammals (mouse Q99LE2 333 aa, Xenopus Q6P7G9 333 aa) indicates essential GPCR function maintained over ~90 million years. Literature (84 papers, WELL-STUDIED status) establishes GPR146 in cholesterol metabolism and cardiovascular disease: 2025 studies demonstrate GPR146 facilitates blood pressure elevation and vascular remodeling via PIEZO1 mechanosensitive channel coupling in hypertension pathogenesis; loss of GPR146 decreases plasma HDL cholesterol levels through post-translational upregulation of SR-B1 (scavenger receptor class B type 1) protein stability; rare GPR146 gene variants in humans and mice show Gpr146-deficient mice exhibit moderate 21% reduction in plasma cholesterol associated with decreased ERK1/2 phosphorylation and reduced hepatic cholesterol synthesis; hepatic DHHC7 inactivation promotes diet-induced obesity through Prg4-GPR146 axis; GPR146 regulates CYP7A1 transcription controlling bile acid synthesis in cells and mice. The functional hypothesis posits that GPR146 acts as a 'cholesterol-Gi signaling rheostat': in hepatocytes, GPR146 couples to Gi/o proteins inhibiting adenylate cyclase reducing cAMP levels while simultaneously activating ERK1/2 through beta-gamma subunit recruitment; this dual signaling maintains SR-B1 protein at appropriate levels through post-translational mechanisms preventing excessive HDL clearance; in vascular smooth muscle cells, GPR146 physically interacts with PIEZO1 mechanosensitive channels enhancing calcium influx upon hydrostatic pressure elevation driving vasoconstriction and vascular remodeling in hypertension; the orphan ligand status suggests either lipid-derived endogenous agonist (given cholesterol metabolism role) or constitutive activity requiring regulatory proteins; in adipose tissue, GPR146 may coordinate lipid storage through Gi-mediated lipolysis inhibition; therapeutic strategies include GPR146 antagonists for hypertension treatment, GPR146 modulators for dyslipidemia management, and GPR146-SR-B1 axis targeting for atherosclerosis prevention.
