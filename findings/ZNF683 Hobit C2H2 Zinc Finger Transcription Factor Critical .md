# ZNF683 (Hobit): C2H2 Zinc Finger Transcription Factor Critical for Tissue-Resident T Cell Differentiation

## Description
ZNF683 (Zinc Finger Protein 683), also known as Hobit (Homolog of BLIMP1 in T cells), is a 524-amino acid C2H2-type zinc finger transcription factor encoded on chromosome 1p36.11 (UniProt Q8IZ20). InterPro analysis reveals three canonical C2H2 zinc finger motifs (IPR013087, PF00096, PS00028, SM00355) spanning residues 322-425, adopting the classic beta-beta-alpha fold (G3DSA:3.30.160.60) for sequence-specific DNA binding. Notably, ZNF683 lacks a KRAB repressor domain, distinguishing it from canonical KRAB-ZNF proteins. The protein belongs to the PRDM4/PRDM1/PRDM14-like zinc finger family (IPR050331, PTHR16515), suggesting functional homology with BLIMP1/PRDM1 in transcriptional regulation. Human Protein Atlas reveals tissue-enriched expression with testis showing highest expression (76.5 nTPM), cancer-enhanced expression pattern, and immune cell-enhanced specificity, consistent with its established role in tissue-resident memory T cell (Trm) differentiation and maintenance. ClinVar documents 6 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including a frameshift nonsense mutation (c.749_750insA, p.Trp250Ter) that truncates the protein within the zinc finger array, and large-scale copy number gains on chromosome 1 spanning 1p36.33-q44. The C2H2 zinc finger architecture without KRAB domain suggests ZNF683 functions as a sequence-specific transcriptional regulator that may recruit alternative corepressor complexes distinct from TRIM28. Given immune cell-enriched expression, testis specificity, and clinical variant burden, ZNF683 likely regulates tissue-resident lymphocyte differentiation programs through direct DNA binding and transcriptional repression of effector genes, with haploinsufficiency potentially impairing mucosal immunity and tumor surveillance.
