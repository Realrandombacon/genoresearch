## Description
C1QTNF2 (UniProt Q9BXJ5), or complement C1q tumor necrosis factor-related protein 2 (also known as CTRP2), encodes a 285-amino acid secreted adipokine belonging to the C1q/TNF-related protein family (IPR001073/IPR008160/IPR008983/IPR050392/PF00386/PF01391/PTHR15424/SM00110/G3DSA:2.60.120.40/SSF49842). The protein architecture features a modular organization characteristic of complement system proteins: an N-terminal signal peptide for secretion, followed by collagen triple helix repeats (IPR008160/PF01391, aa 82-140) that mediate trimerization and structural stability, and a C-terminal globular C1q domain (IPR001073/IPR050392/PF00386/PS50871/SM00110, aa 143-281) belonging to the TNF-like superfamily that mediates receptor binding. This partner profile positions C1QTNF2 at the intersection of adipokine signaling (ADIPOR1), collagen matrix remodeling (COL2A1/COL6A2), and vascular homeostasis (CD93).\n\nWith moderate literature coverage but incomplete mechanistic understanding, C1QTNF2 qualifies as a dark gene with emerging metabolic disease implications. Recent insights suggest: (1) C1QTNF2 enhances insulin sensitivity and glucose uptake in skeletal muscle via AMPK activation through ADIPOR1; (2) protective role in cardiovascular disease through endothelial cell maintenance and anti-inflammatory effects; (3) collagen-like repeats enable trimerization essential for receptor activation, analogous to adiponectin; (4) CD93 interaction suggests complement-independent roles in vascular integrity; (5) dysregulation linked to metabolic syndrome and type 2 diabetes. HPA data confirms tissue-enhanced expression with blood vessel (13.2 nTPM) as top tissue, reflecting vascular endothelial secretion; Golgi apparatus and centrosome localization indicate active secretory pathway trafficking; classified as predicted secreted protein with immune cell enrichment. ClinVar lists 18 pathogenic variants including 5q33.1-35.2 copy number losses/gains, confirming dosage sensitivity potentially linked to 5q35 Sotos syndrome region disorders. We hypothesize C1QTNF2 functions as a metabolic-vascular coupling factor: the collagen repeats mediate homotrimerization creating a stable platform for C1q domain presentation to ADIPOR1 on muscle/endothelial cells, activating AMPK/PI3K pathways that enhance glucose disposal and suppress inflammation; the COL2A1/COL6A2 interactions suggest C1QTNF2 integrates into extracellular matrix to modulate tissue stiffness and mechanotransduction; CD93 binding links C1QTNF2 to complement clearance pathways independent of classical complement activation; loss of C1QTNF2 leads to insulin resistance and endothelial dysfunction, while the 5q33 CNVs disrupt dosage-sensitive metabolic regulation contributing to syndromic features.
