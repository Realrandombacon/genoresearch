## Description
SPSB3 (SplA/ryanodine receptor domain and SOCS box containing 3, also known as C16orf31 or SSB3) is a 355 amino acid intracellular protein localized to mitochondria (UniProt Q6PJ21) with high-confidence well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 76.7). The protein features a canonical bipartite E3 ubiquitin ligase adaptor architecture comprising an N-terminal B30.2/SPRY domain (IPR001870/IPR003877/PF00622, residues 85-274/149-264) forming a ~190-180 aa module (~53-51%) that mediates substrate recognition via protein-protein interactions (GO:0005515), and a C-terminal SOCS box domain (IPR001496/PF07525, residues 272-312/273-312) forming a ~40-40 aa module (~11%) that recruits the elongin BC-cullin 5 ubiquitin ligase complex. The SPRY domain adopts a concanavalin A-like lectin/glucanase fold (IPR013320/G3DSA:2.60.120.920), while the SOCS box belongs to the SOCS box-like superfamily (IPR036036/G3DSA:1.10.750.20). Molecular functions include ubiquitin-like ligase-substrate adaptor activity, protein K48-linked ubiquitination, and negative regulation of the cGAS/STING signaling pathway, placing SPSB3 within the F-box/SPRY and SOCS box protein family (IPR050672).\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places SPSB3 at the core of an E3 ubiquitin ligase machinery: strongest interaction with ELOC (0.806, Elongin-C, a component of the VHL E3 ligase complex), confirming its role in recruiting the elongin BC complex. High confidence links include CUL5 (0.791, Cullin-5, the scaffold protein of the ECS E3 ligase), RNF7 (0.507, Ring finger protein 7, an E2 ubiquitin-conjugating enzyme), and ELOB (0.508, Elongin-B), completing the elongin BC heterodimer. Connections to UBE2F (0.511) and UBE2M (0.508) are ubiquitin-conjugating enzymes, while NEDD8 (0.506) is involved in cullin activation. The interaction with CCDC22 (0.499) and COMMD3 (0.499) suggests a potential link to the CCC (COMMD/CCDC22/CCDC93) complex, which regulates endosomal trafficking and NF-κB signaling, possibly coordinating ubiquitination with membrane dynamics. CISH (0.562), another SOCS family member, indicates functional redundancy or cooperative regulation.\n\nHPA expression reveals low tissue specificity with detection in all tissues, consistent with a fundamental regulatory role. The 'mitochondrial' subcellular location is particularly significant, suggesting SPSB3 may regulate mitochondrial signaling or quality control. The 'ubiquitous expression' and 'low cancer specificity' indicate it is not a tissue-specific oncogene but may play a housekeeping role in proteostasis. The 'cell type enhanced' status suggests higher expression in specific cell populations.\n\nClinVar documents 42 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including both single nucleotide variants (e.g., c.595+23C>T, c.615C>T) and large copy number gains and losses affecting the 16p13.3 region: CNV gains (16p13.3, 16p13.3-13.13 duplications) and CNV losses (16p13.3 deletions). The high variant burden indicates strong dosage sensitivity. Haploinsufficiency would likely impair substrate degradation, causing accumulation of pro-inflammatory mediators (e.g., cGAS/STING pathway components), potentially leading to autoinflammatory or autoimmune disorders. Duplications may cause hyper-degradation of signaling proteins, contributing to immunodeficiency or 16p deletion/duplication syndrome phenotypes (developmental delay, intellectual disability, autism spectrum disorder).\n\nConservation is strong across mammals: human (Q6PJ21, 355 aa), mouse (Q571F5, 354 aa), indicating evolutionary pressure to maintain the SPRY-SOCS architecture.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: SPSB3 functions as a mitochondrial-localized substrate-specific adaptor for the ECS (Elongin-Cullin-SOCS) E3 ubiquitin ligase complex. The SPRY domain recognizes specific substrates—likely components of the cGAS/STING innate immune pathway—through protein-protein interactions, while the SOCS box recruits the ELOC-ELOB heterodimer and CUL5 scaffold to assemble the active E3 ligase. This complex then recruits E2 ubiquitin-conjugating enzymes (RNF7, UBE2F) to polyubiquitinate the substrate with K48-linked chains, targeting it for proteasomal degradation. By negatively regulating cGAS/STING signaling, SPSB3 prevents excessive type I interferon production and autoinflammation. Its mitochondrial localization suggests it may monitor mitochondrial DNA leakage, a key activator of cGAS. Dysregulation via 16p CNVs disrupts immune homeostasis: loss causes chronic interferon signaling (autoimmunity), while gain causes immunosuppression (infection susceptibility), contributing to 16p syndrome phenotypes.
