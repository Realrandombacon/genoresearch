## Description
TMEM62 (Gene ID: 80021, UniProt Q0P6H9) is a 643 amino acid transmembrane protein located on chromosome 15q15.2 (NC_000015.10, positions 43133114-43185143, ~52kb region). InterPro domain analysis reveals a multi-domain architecture characteristic of regulatory phosphatases: an N-terminal metallophosphatase domain (cd07401/IPR041871, positions 57-345) containing a calcineurin-like phosphoesterase catalytic core (IPR004843/PF00149, positions 59-259) with GO-annotated hydrolase activity (GO:0016787), membership in the metallo-dependent phosphatase superfamily (IPR029052/SSF56300, positions 59-305), a TMEM62-specific Ig-like domain (IPR056229/PF24384, positions 309-411) likely mediating protein-protein interactions, and a TMEM62-specific C-terminal domain (IPR056230/PF24394, positions 433-599). AlphaFold structure prediction (AF-Q0P6H9-F1) shows very high confidence (global pLDDT 91.8), indicating a well-folded stable protein with defined tertiary structure suitable for enzymatic function. Human Protein Atlas classifies TMEM62 as a predicted intracellular and membrane protein localized to nucleoplasm, nucleoli fibrillar center, nuclear bodies, and cytoplasmic bodies. Expression shows low tissue specificity with detection in all surveyed tissues, suggesting ubiquitous housekeeping or fundamental cellular functions. STRING database identifies 7 medium-confidence protein-protein interactions predominantly with other transmembrane proteins: TMEM164 (score 0.488), ST8SIA5 (0.483), TMEM131L (0.467), SPRYD4 (0.429), TMEM132A (0.415), TMEM63C (0.405), and CDHR2 (0.404). These partners include sialyltransferases (ST8SIA5), cadherins (CDHR2), and multiple uncharacterized transmembrane proteins, suggesting TMEM62 may function in membrane trafficking, cell adhesion, or glycosylation regulation. ClinVar contains 13 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number gains (15q11.2-21.2 chr15:22770421-50347130x3, 15q11.2-26.3 chr15:22770422-102429112x3/x4), copy number losses (15q15.2-21.2 chr15:42850434-49592633, 15q15.2-15.3 chr15:43135272-43744542x1, 15q15.1-21.2 chr15:41689327-52446981x1), and single nucleotide variants (NM_024956.4:c.431-7A>T), though clinical significance remains uncertain for most entries. Conservation across vertebrates is evidenced by orthologs in mouse (Q8BXJ9, 643 aa) and northern pike (A0A3P8YNW3, 634 aa; A0AAY5K9X0, 1006 aa). Functional hypothesis: TMEM62 functions as a metal-dependent phosphoesterase/hydrolase that dephosphorylates specific protein or lipid substrates, potentially regulating signal transduction pathways through reversible phosphorylation. The calcineurin-like catalytic domain suggests similarity to PPP-family phosphatases that require divalent metal ions (Fe²⁺, Zn²⁺, or Mn²⁺) for catalysis. The Ig-like domain may mediate substrate recognition or recruitment of regulatory partners, while the C-terminal domain could provide membrane anchoring or additional interaction surfaces. Nuclear and cytoplasmic localization suggests TMEM62 may shuttle between compartments to regulate distinct substrate pools. Disease mechanism: Dosage sensitivity from 15q15.2 copy number variations disrupts TMEM62 expression levels, potentially causing phosphatase activity imbalance that dysregulates phosphorylation-dependent signaling cascades. Overexpression from duplications may cause excessive dephosphorylation of target substrates, while haploinsufficiency from deletions may lead to hyperphosphorylation states. The 15q15.2 region's involvement in neurodevelopmental disorders suggests TMEM62 dysfunction may contribute to cognitive impairment, developmental delay, or neuropsychiatric phenotypes observed in patients with chromosome 15 CNVs. Priority experiments include identification of endogenous substrates via phosphoproteomics in TMEM62 knockout cells, biochemical characterization of metal ion requirements and catalytic kinetics, determination of crystal structure with bound substrates/inhibitors, investigation of nucleocytoplasmic shuttling mechanisms, and assessment of neurodevelopmental phenotypes in Tmem62 conditional knockout mice.
