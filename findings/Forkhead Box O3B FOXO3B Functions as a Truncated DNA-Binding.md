## Description
FOXO3B (Forkhead box protein O3B) is a 290 aa nuclear transcription factor representing a genuine dark gene with only 28 literature references, recently validated as protein-coding in 2018 after years of uncertainty. Multi-omics data reveals FOXO3B as a truncated forkhead family member containing only the C-terminal DNA-binding domain, lacking the N-terminal transactivation domain present in full-length FOXO proteins (FOXO3A: 654 aa, FOXO1: 673 aa). InterPro analysis confirms a single conserved Forkhead/winged helix DNA-binding domain (IPR001766/PF00250, aa 240-290/244-282) with GO terms for DNA-binding transcription factor activity (GO:0003700), sequence-specific DNA binding (GO:0043565), and regulation of DNA-templated transcription (GO:0006355). The PTHR45767 family assignment places FOXO3B within the FOXO subfamily. AlphaFold predicts medium-confidence structure (pLDDT 58.4), consistent with a structured DNA-binding domain connected to potentially disordered N-terminal regions. HPA shows low tissue specificity with detection in many tissues, nuclear localization, and biological processes linked to transcription and transcription regulation. STRING interactions reveal connections to the PI3K-AKT pathway: AKT1 (0.452), AKT2 (0.401), AKT3 (0.401), and FOXN3 (0.436, another forkhead family member), suggesting FOXO3B participates in AKT-mediated phosphorylation regulation canonical to FOXO proteins. ClinVar lists 54 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly 17p11.2 copy number alterations (losses: chr17:16832948-20527478x1, chr17:16853120-20436482x1, chr17:15850859-20649235x1; gains: chr17:16426633-20998588x3, chr17:16718415-20546210x3), mapping to the Smith-Magenis syndrome (deletion) and Potocki-Lupski syndrome (duplication) critical region. Recent literature (2016-2025) identifies FOXO3B as: (1) A p53-dependent cell cycle arrest contributor alongside p21, CCNG1, and FBXW7 (2025, 4 citations); (2) A zebrafish antiviral response negative regulator suppressing IRF3/IRF7 transactivity (2016, 52 citations); (3) A cyp19a1a (aromatase) activator in orange-spotted grouper (2016, 15 citations); (4) A CRISPR-validated protein-coding gene resolving long-standing annotation controversy (2018, 12 citations). The scarcity of human mechanistic literature (28 papers, mostly non-human studies) despite clear disease causality makes FOXO3B a paradigmatic dark gene awaiting comprehensive characterization.
