## Description
RAPGEF5 (Rap Guanine Nucleotide Exchange Factor 5, UniProt Q92565) is a 580 aa cytoplasmic/nuclear guanine nucleotide exchange factor representing a partially characterized dark gene with 44 literature references and significant clinical relevance. Structural analysis reveals canonical RasGEF architecture: N-terminal regulatory region (aa 1-75), Ras exchange motif (REM) domain (aa 76-199, cd06224) mediating protein-protein interactions, central Ras-like GEF N-terminal domain (aa 67-201, IPR000651), and C-terminal catalytic RasGEF domain (aa 341-575, cd00155/IPR001895/PF00617) containing the catalytic core for nucleotide exchange. AlphaFold predicts high-confidence structure (pLDDT=77.6) consistent with well-folded Ras-like guanine nucleotide exchange factor superfamily (IPR008937, aa 53-579) containing characteristic RasGEF catalytic domain fold (G3DSA:1.10.840.10, aa 333-580). STRING interactions place RAPGEF5 within Ras/Rap GTPase signaling network with very high confidence: RAP1A (0.986, small GTPase substrate), MRAS (0.984, muscle RAS oncogene homolog), HRAS (0.942, Harvey RAS), NRAS (0.913, neuroblastoma RAS), RRAS (0.905, related RAS viral oncogene), SLC35C1 (0.838, GDP-fucose transporter), KRAS (0.703, Kirsten RAS), RAP1B (0.553, RAP1 paralog), RAP2A (0.425, RAP2 paralog), and RRAS2 (0.416, RAS related 2) — indicating RAPGEF5 functions as multi-specific GEF activating Rap and Ras family GTPases through GDP/GTP exchange. HPA expression confirms tissue-specific pattern: parathyroid gland-enriched (364.8 nTPM), brain-enriched (158.3 nTPM), group-enriched specificity, detected in many tissues, low cancer specificity, nucleoplasm and nuclear bodies localization consistent with nuclear signaling function. ClinVar identifies 37 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including multiple copy number gains (7p22.3-14.3, 7p21.2-15.2) and losses (7p21.1-15.3, 7p21.3-15.2) affecting RAPGEF5 locus on chromosome 7p, plus splice site variants (c.1636+5_1636+6del) and synonymous variants (c.1332G>A/p.Pro444=), though specific RAPGEF5-associated disease phenotypes remain incompletely defined. Literature establishes RAPGEF5 function in: (1) colorectal cancer — circ-RAPGEF5 downregulation inhibits CRC progression by reducing GALNT3 expression (4 citations), (2) intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma — circ-RAPGEF5 promotes ICC progression by stabilizing SAE1 to facilitate SUMOylation (15 citations), (3) myeloid neoplasia — RAPGEF5 identified as novel tumor suppressor in 7q/del7q myeloid malignancies (0 citations, 2024), (4) idiopathic epilepsy — variant RAPGEF5 protein associated with epilepsy risk in Belgian shepherd dogs via Wnt signaling pathway (1 citation), (5) molecular function — GEF activity for RAP1A, RAP2A, and MRAS with MRAS association inhibiting Rap1 activation. Functional hypothesis: RAPGEF5 acts as multi-specific guanine nucleotide exchange factor activating Rap1/Rap2/MRas GTPases through catalytic RasGEF domain, with REM domain mediating regulatory protein interactions and subcellular localization. The parathyroid/brain-enriched expression suggests roles in calcium homeostasis signaling (parathyroid) and neuronal development/synaptic plasticity (brain), while nuclear localization indicates transcriptional regulation functions. Disease mechanism: RAPGEF5 exhibits context-dependent oncogenic/tumor-suppressive roles — promotes cholangiocarcinoma via circRNA-mediated SAE1/SUMOylation pathway, suppresses myeloid neoplasia in 7q deletion syndromes, and modulates colorectal cancer through GALNT3 O-glycosylation regulation. The 37 ClinVar CNVs on 7p suggest dosage sensitivity where copy number alterations disrupt Rap/Ras signaling homeostasis affecting cell proliferation, differentiation, and neuronal function. Epilepsy association suggests RAPGEF5 variants disrupt Wnt signaling in neural development. Priority experiments: (a) identify RAPGEF5 substrate specificity via GTPase activation assays, (b) characterize RAPGEF5 nuclear localization signals and nuclear body partners, (c) test RAPGEF5 role in parathyroid hormone secretion via knockout models, (d) determine RAPGEF5-associated disease phenotypes in 7p CNV patients. RAPGEF5 exemplifies 'dark RasGEF' where catalytic mechanism and cancer associations are defined but tissue-specific functions, nuclear signaling roles, and disease mechanisms remain unexplored despite 37 ClinVar variants.
