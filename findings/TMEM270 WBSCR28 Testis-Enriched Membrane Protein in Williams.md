## Description
TMEM270 (Gene ID: 135886, UniProt Q6UE05, alias WBSCR28) is a 265 amino acid transmembrane protein located on chromosome 7q11.23 (NC_000007.14, positions 73860847-73865889, ~5kb genomic region). This gene resides within the Williams-Beuren syndrome critical region (WBSCR), a ~1.5Mb deletion hotspot causing Williams-Beuren syndrome (WBS, OMIM 194050), though TMEM270's specific contribution to WBS phenotype remains undefined. InterPro domain analysis reveals membership in Williams-Beuren syndrome chromosomal region 28 protein homologue family (IPR029166/PF15164, positions 1-265; PTHR37369), indicating evolutionary conservation of this WBS-associated protein. STRING interaction network identifies 10 protein partners with medium confidence scores: TMEM225 (0.690), TRIM50 (0.587), FAM71F1 (0.584), GTSF1L (0.573), SPATS1 (0.562), SPATA3 (0.544), TBL2 (0.534), DNAJC30 (0.511), METTL27 (0.493), and FSCN3 (0.433). Notably, several partners (SPATS1, SPATA3, FAM71F1) are testis-expressed spermatogenesis-associated proteins, suggesting potential role in germ cell biology. Human Protein Atlas classifies TMEM270 (ENSG00000175877) as a predicted membrane protein with vesicular subcellular location and striking testis-enriched expression pattern (74.6 nTPM in testis, detected in single tissue only), indicating highly restricted tissue specificity. RNA not detected in immune cells. AlphaFold structure prediction (AF-Q6UE05-F1) yields medium-confidence model (global pLDDT 66.1), suggesting partially structured protein with possible intrinsically disordered regions—consistent with membrane-associated proteins that may have flexible domains for protein-protein interactions. ClinVar lists 164 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains/losses and deletions spanning 7q11.23 (e.g., chr7:73338336-74772490x3, chr7:73240749-74727155x1); these overlap the classic WBS deletion region (~1.5Mb encompassing ~26 genes including ELN, LIMK1, RFC2). No specific monogenic disease is attributed solely to TMEM270 point mutations, suggesting haploinsufficiency contributes to WBS phenotype rather than causing independent disorder. Conservation evidenced by mouse (Q6UJB9, 264 aa) and cattle (Q2TBQ4, 262 aa) orthologs with nearly identical lengths. Functional hypothesis: TMEM270 functions as a vesicle-associated membrane protein involved in intracellular trafficking, potentially in testis-specific processes such as spermatid differentiation or acrosome biogenesis. The interaction network suggests possible roles in (1) vesicular transport between Golgi and endosomal compartments, (2) protein complex assembly via TRIM50 (E3 ubiquitin ligase) interactions, or (3) cytoskeletal organization through FSCN3 (fascin actin-bundling protein). Disease mechanism: TMEM270 haploinsufficiency likely contributes to Williams-Beuren syndrome phenotype as part of the contiguous gene deletion syndrome. Specific contributions may include neurodevelopmental abnormalities (given WBS cognitive profile), cardiovascular defects (WBS characteristic supravalvular aortic stenosis), or connective tissue abnormalities. Testis-enriched expression suggests potential male fertility effects when deleted, though this remains unconfirmed. The medium-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 66.1) indicates flexible architecture suitable for dynamic protein interactions in membrane trafficking. Priority experiments include generation of Tmem270 knockout mice to assess WBS-relevant phenotypes, co-IP/MS validation of STRING-predicted interactions, detailed subcellular localization studies in testis germ cells, investigation of TMEM270 role in vesicle trafficking using live-cell imaging, and assessment of sperm parameters in TMEM270-deficient models.
