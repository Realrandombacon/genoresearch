## Description
FCF1 (rRNA-processing protein FCF1 homolog, also known as UTP24, C14orf111, CGI-35, Bka) is a 198 amino acid nucleoplasmic protein (UniProt Q9Y324) with high-confidence well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 83.8), characteristic of stable enzymes that function in ribonucleoprotein complexes. The protein contains a PIN domain (IPR002716/PF04900/cd09864/SM00670/SSF88723, residues 57-187/67-166) that forms a nuclease fold for RNA cleavage, belongs to the Fcf1/UTP23 family (IPR006984/PTHR12416, residues 92-188/6-192) involved in small-subunit processome (GO:0032040), and is part of the 5'-nuclease superfamily (G3DSA:3.40.50.1010, residues 50-193). FCF1 is located on chromosome 14q24.3 and enables RNA binding activity, functioning in ribosomal small subunit biogenesis and rRNA processing as part of the small-subunit processome.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals an exceptionally high-confidence network of small-subunit processome and ribosome biogenesis factors: WDR46 (0.999, WD repeat-containing protein 46, ribosome assembly), EMG1 (0.999, Essential for mitotic growth 1, rRNA methyltransferase), UTP11 (0.999, UTP small-subunit processome component 11), DNTTIP2 (0.999, Deoxynucleotidyltransferase-interacting protein 2), BMS1 (0.998, GTP-binding protein BMS1, ribosome assembly), UTP3 (0.997, UTP small-subunit processome component 3), KRR1 (0.996, KRR1 small-subunit processome component), NOP58 (0.996, Nucleolar protein 58, box C/D snoRNP), IMP3 (0.991, IMP U3 small-subunit processome component), and UTP20 (0.989, UTP small-subunit processome component 20). These interactions confirm FCF1 functions as a core endonuclease within the small-subunit processome that cleaves pre-rRNA during 18S rRNA maturation.\n\nHPA expression reveals FCF1 has low tissue specificity and is detected in all tissues, consistent with an essential housekeeping role in ribosome biogenesis. Subcellular localization includes nucleoplasm where ribosome assembly occurs. The protein is classified as an essential predicted intracellular protein involved in ribosome biogenesis and rRNA processing biological processes. Cell type-enhanced specificity at the single-cell level suggests specialized functions in highly proliferative cells with high ribosome demand.\n\nClinVar documents 17 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including copy number gains (14q23.1-32.33, 14q13.3-32.33, 14q22.2-24.3, 14q11.2-32.33 amplifications), copy number loss (14q24.3 deletion), and single nucleotide variants (c.*11A>T 3' UTR, c.549-26C>T intronic, c.342G>T p.Gly114= synonymous). The variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity. While no specific Mendelian disease is annotated, 14q CNVs are associated with developmental delay, intellectual disability, growth retardation, and congenital anomalies. Given FCF1's essential role in ribosome biogenesis, loss-of-function may cause ribosomopathy phenotypes similar to Diamond-Blackfan anemia or Treacher Collins syndrome through impaired 18S rRNA production.\n\nConservation is strong across eukaryotes: human (Q9Y324, 198 aa), mouse (Q9CTH6, 198 aa), budding yeast (Q05498, 189 aa), fission yeast (O13610, 192 aa), indicating an ancient and essential function in ribosome biogenesis conserved from yeast to humans.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: FCF1/UTP24 functions as an endonuclease within the small-subunit processome (SSU processome) that cleaves pre-rRNA at the A0 and A1 sites during 18S rRNA maturation. The PIN domain forms the catalytic center for RNA cleavage, coordinating divalent metal ions for phosphodiester bond hydrolysis. Through interactions with UTP11, UTP3, UTP20, and IMP3, FCF1 is positioned within the SSU processome to access specific cleavage sites on the 35S/47S pre-rRNA transcript. WDR46 and EMG1 interactions link FCF1 to later stages of small-subunit assembly and rRNA modification. BMS1 interaction connects FCF1 to GTP-dependent conformational changes during processome assembly. The well-folded structure (pLDDT 83.8) provides a stable nuclease domain optimized for precise RNA cleavage. Nucleoplasmic localization ensures FCF1 accesses nascent pre-rRNA transcripts. 14q CNVs cause FCF1 dosage imbalance, disrupting 18S rRNA production and potentially causing ribosomopathy phenotypes through impaired translation capacity. FCF1 represents a candidate gene for congenital ribosomopathies and a potential target for cancer therapy given its essential role in cell proliferation.
