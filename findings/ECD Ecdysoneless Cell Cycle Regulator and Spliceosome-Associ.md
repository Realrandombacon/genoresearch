## Description
ECD (Ecdysoneless Homolog, UniProt O95905) is a 644 amino acid nucleoplasmic/cytosolic protein with moderate characterization that functions as a cell cycle regulator and spliceosome-associated factor critical for mRNA processing and transcriptional regulation. The protein contains an Ecdysoneless family domain (IPR010770, residues 1-637), an SGT1 protein domain (PF07093, residues 14-597) involved in protein complex assembly and stability, and belongs to the SGT1 suppressor of GCR2 family (PTHR13060, residues 1-637), placing it in the chaperone/co-chaperone superfamily with roles in kinetochore assembly and cell cycle checkpoint control. STRING interactions reveal exceptionally high-confidence partnerships with core spliceosome components: AAR2 (0.955, U5 snRNP assembly factor), PRPF8 (0.901, U5 snRNP core protein), SNRNP200 (0.884, U5 snRNP helicase), TSSC4 (0.851, U5 snRNP assembly), SNRNP40 (0.805, U5 snRNP component), EFTUD2 (0.762, U5 snRNP GTPase), EAPP (0.697, E2F-associated phosphoprotein), ZNHIT2 (0.570), PIH1D1 (0.513, R2TP complex), and KIAA1143 (0.423), placing ECD in the pre-mRNA splicing machinery alongside U5 snRNP assembly factors. HPA data shows ubiquitous expression (detected in all tissues), low tissue specificity, nucleoplasmic and cytosolic localization, with biological processes including mRNA processing, mRNA splicing, transcription, and transcription regulation. ClinVar contains 16 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number gains/losses across chromosome 10q regions, though no specific diseases are directly assigned—suggesting potential developmental, hematologic, or cancer phenotypes. AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 76.0) indicates high confidence with a well-folded SGT1-like domain structure. Functional hypothesis: ECD acts as a cell cycle regulator that couples mRNA splicing fidelity to cell cycle progression through its SGT1 domain-mediated interactions with U5 snRNP assembly factors (AAR2, PRPF8, SNRNP200, TSSC4), where ECD stabilizes spliceosome assembly during G1/S transition, ensures proper splicing of cell cycle regulators (cyclins, CDKs, E2F targets), and coordinates transcriptional output with splicing capacity, with ubiquitous expression supporting essential housekeeping functions in all cell types, SGT1 domain homology suggesting chaperone-like activity for spliceosome assembly, and chromosomal 10q CNVs potentially contributing to developmental disorders or cancer through splicing dysregulation of tumor suppressors and oncogenes, making ECD a potential biomarker for cancers with splicing factor mutations and a therapeutic target for spliceosome-dependent malignancies.
