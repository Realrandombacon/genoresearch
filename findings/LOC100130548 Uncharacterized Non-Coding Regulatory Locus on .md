## Description
LOC100130548 is an uncharacterized genomic locus located on chromosome 9q34.2 (GRCh38: 134054289-134058804, ~4.5kb region). This locus lacks protein-coding evidence: no UniProt accession, no InterPro domains, no STRING protein interactions, and Human Protein Atlas has no expression data (ENSG00000235138 not in HPA database). The absence of coding signatures suggests this is a non-coding regulatory element (lncRNA, enhancer, silencer, or insulator) rather than a protein-coding gene. ClinVar analysis reveals 27 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variants disrupting this region, including recurrent 9q34.13-34.3 microdeletions (~0.2-1.1Mb, chr9:137552082-138059181) and large 9p24.3-q34.3 duplications spanning the entire chromosome 9q arm (~138Mb). These recurrent CNVs are associated with 9q34 deletion syndrome (Kleefstra syndrome-like phenotype) featuring developmental delay, intellectual disability, hypotonia, distinctive facial features, and congenital heart defects.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: LOC100130548 functions as a dosage-sensitive cis-regulatory element critical for 9q34.2 topologically associating domain (TAD) architecture and gene regulation. The locus may encode a long non-coding RNA (lncRNA) that acts in cis to regulate neighboring developmental genes such as EHMT1 (Kleefstra syndrome gene), STAC, or ABL1 through chromatin looping or transcriptional interference mechanisms. Alternatively, it may serve as a boundary/insulator element maintaining TAD integrity, with deletion causing ectopic enhancer-promoter contacts and misexpression of dosage-sensitive developmental regulators. The high number of recurrent pathogenic CNVs (27 variants) suggests strong selective pressure against locus disruption, indicating essential regulatory function. Haploinsufficiency (deletions) likely causes loss of repressive or activating regulatory activity, while duplications may create dominant-negative effects or alter chromatin topology. The 9q34.2 location adjacent to EHMT1 supports a potential role in Kleefstra syndrome pathogenesis through dysregulation of this critical chromatin modifier.
