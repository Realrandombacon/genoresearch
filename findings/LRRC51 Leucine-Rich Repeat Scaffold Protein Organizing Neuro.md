# LRRC51: Leucine-Rich Repeat Scaffold Protein Organizing Neurotransmitter Metabolism Enzyme Complexes

**Date:** 2026-03-23T01:03:38.437476

**Quality Score:** 4.8/10  (E=7.5, D=2.1) [MODERATE]

## Description
LRRC51 encodes a 192 amino acid leucine-rich repeat protein (UniProt Q96E66) containing multiple LRR domains (IPR001611, PF14580, residues 86-175; IPR032675 LRR superfamily, residues 3-186) within the PTHR46545 family (residues 7-189). AlphaFold predicts an exceptionally well-folded structure with very high confidence (pLDDT 91.5), indicating stable tertiary structure optimized for protein-protein interactions typical of LRR scaffolds. The protein localizes to microtubules, suggesting cytoskeletal association. Human Protein Atlas data shows tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in testis (93.3 nTPM) and fallopian tube (63.4 nTPM), indicating enrichment in reproductive tissues. ClinVar documents 9 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including nonsense mutations (p.Arg8Ter), frameshift deletions (p.His110fs, p.Ile114fs), and 11q13.4-14.1 copy number losses, demonstrating clinical relevance and dosage sensitivity. STRING interaction analysis reveals high-confidence partnerships with neurotransmitter metabolism enzymes: MAOB (0.990), MAOA (0.989), DDC (0.976), CYP1B1 (0.966), DBH (0.965), CYP1A1 (0.957), ALDH3A1 (0.931), ALDH3B2 (0.927), ALDH3B1 (0.925), and PNMT (0.732). This interaction network strongly suggests LRRC51 functions as a scaffold organizing catecholamine and serotonin metabolism complexes. Orthologs are conserved across mammals: mouse Q9DAK8 (192 aa), rat B6CZ61 (192 aa), bovine Q5EAD8 (192 aa), demonstrating strong evolutionary preservation. Functional hypothesis: LRRC51 acts as a microtubule-associated scaffold protein that spatially organizes neurotransmitter metabolism enzyme complexes (MAO, DDC, DBH, PNMT, CYPs, ALDHs), facilitating substrate channeling and metabolic efficiency. The LRR domains mediate specific protein-protein interactions, positioning enzymes in proximity for coordinated catecholamine/serotonin biosynthesis and degradation. Mechanistic proposal: LRRC51 anchors monoamine oxidase complexes (MAOA/MAOB) and downstream enzymes (DDC, DBH, PNMT) to microtubule tracks, enabling directed transport of neurotransmitter precursors and products within axons or specialized cellular compartments. High testis expression suggests particular importance in spermatogenic cells where catecholamine signaling regulates sperm maturation and motility. Pathogenic variants disrupt scaffold integrity, impairing neurotransmitter metabolism coordination and causing disease through metabolic dysregulation.
