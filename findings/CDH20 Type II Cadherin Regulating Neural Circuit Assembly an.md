## Description
CDH20 encodes an 801 aa type II classical cadherin (UniProt Q9HBT6), a calcium-dependent cell-cell adhesion glycoprotein with five extracellular cadherin repeats and a conserved cytoplasmic tail. InterPro analysis reveals: (1) Cadherin Y-type LIR-motif (IPR000233, aa 733-792) for catenin binding; (2) Cadherin-like domains (IPR002126, aa 67-389) with three tandem repeats for homophilic adhesion; (3) Cadherin-like superfamily fold (IPR015919, aa 79-383); (4) Catenin binding domain superfamily (IPR027397, aa 700-796) linking to cytoskeleton. AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (pLDDT 77.3), indicating stable folding with defined extracellular adhesion interface and intracellular signaling domain. STRING interactions confirm functional clustering with type II cadherin family: CDH2 (0.843), CDH15 (0.840), CDH10 (0.834), CDH6 (0.796), CDH4 (0.784), CDH8 (0.762), CDH11 (0.744), CDH3 (0.737), CDH12 (0.734), plus CTNNB1/β-catenin (0.767) for downstream signaling. HPA shows tissue-enhanced expression: tongue (22.4 nTPM), brain (17.3 nTPM), retina (10.2 nTPM), with cancer-enriched RNA patterns. ClinVar reports 46 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly 18q copy number losses/gains, though specific disease associations remain undefined. Literature (29 papers) reveals: (1) CDH20-β-catenin interaction inhibits cervical cancer cell migration/invasion via TGF-β/Smad/SNAIL-mediated EMT suppression (2020, 17 citations); (2) Cdh20 mRNA demarcates somatotopic subregions and spiny projection neuron subpopulations in rat dorsolateral striatum (2021), suggesting role in motor circuit assembly; (3) Differential spatiotemporal expression during CNS segmentation and brain nuclei formation (2021, 28 citations); (4) GWAS associations with obsessive-compulsive disorder. Mechanistically, CDH20 likely functions through: (1) Homophilic adhesion between neurons expressing CDH20, enabling selective cell sorting during brain development; (2) β-catenin recruitment to cytoplasmic tail, linking adhesion to Wnt signaling and cytoskeletal dynamics; (3) EMT suppression in epithelial tissues by maintaining cell-cell contacts and repressing SNAIL transcription factors. In disease, CDH20 dysfunction may contribute to: neurodevelopmental disorders via disrupted neural circuit assembly; cancer metastasis through EMT dysregulation; psychiatric disorders via altered striatal connectivity. The high-confidence structure and dual neural/cancer roles suggest CDH20 as a therapeutic target for metastatic disease and neurodevelopmental conditions.
