## Description
LST1 (Leukocyte-Specific Transcript 1) is a 97-amino acid small protein encoded within the MHC class III region on chromosome 6p21.33, a genomic neighborhood densely populated with immune regulatory genes. InterPro analysis confirms LST1 belongs to the leukocyte-specific transcript 1 family (IPR007775, residues 1-97; PF05083, residues 16-96; PIRSF037638, residues 1-97; PTHR15452, residues 4-97), indicating it represents a distinct protein family without homology to characterized structural domains. A prokaryotic membrane lipoprotein lipid attachment site profile (PS51257, residues 1-30) suggests potential membrane association, though this may reflect N-terminal signal peptide features. AlphaFold predicts a confident structure (pLDDT 70.4) for this small protein, indicating a stable folded conformation despite its compact size.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places LST1 within the immune signaling network with highest confidence for LTB (0.830, lymphotoxin beta, a TNF family cytokine), TYROBP (0.792, DAP12, an immune signaling adaptor protein), ABHD16A (0.762, alpha/beta hydrolase domain-containing protein), LTA (0.760, lymphotoxin alpha/TNF-beta), RALGPS2 (0.733, Ral GEF with PH domain and SH3 binding motif), NFKBIL1 (0.716, NF-kappa-B inhibitor-like protein 1), DDX39B (0.697, DEAD-box helicase), MYOF (0.653, myoferlin), NCR3 (0.613, NKp30, natural killer cell receptor), and AIF1 (0.466, allograft inflammatory factor 1/IBA1, macrophage/microglia marker). This interaction profile strongly supports a role in immune cell activation, particularly in myeloid lineage cells and NK cells. The LTB/LTA connections suggest involvement in TNF superfamily signaling pathways, while TYROBP and NCR3 interactions indicate roles in activating receptor signaling cascades.\n\nHuman Protein Atlas has no expression data for LST1, suggesting it may be expressed in cell types not comprehensively profiled (e.g., specific immune cell subsets, activated states) or at levels below detection thresholds in bulk tissue. This absence from HPA is consistent with immune-restricted expression patterns.\n\nClinVar reports 7 pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants, all large copy number variations spanning 6p21.33-25.3 (coordinates: chr6:30944923-31867966 deletion, chr6:28005012_31683185 deletion, chr6:31036397-34088832 gain, chr6:29455465-81447367 gain, chr6:156975-170919482 gain). The 6p21.33 region contains the MHC class III locus with numerous immune genes, and CNVs in this region are associated with autoimmune and inflammatory disorders, though no specific LST1 monogenic disease is named.\n\nLiterature analysis (150 papers) indicates LST1 is moderately studied with emerging functional insights. Recent 2025 work identified LST1 expression as correlating with immune infiltration and predicting poor prognosis in acute myeloid leukemia, suggesting oncogenic or immunomodulatory functions in myeloid malignancies. Additional 2025 research identified LST1 as a novel biomarker for efferocytosis in the co-occurrence of type 2 diabetes and clear cell renal cell carcinoma, linking it to metabolic-inflammatory crosstalk. Earlier studies established LST1 as leukocyte-specific with alternative splicing generating multiple isoforms.\n\nWe hypothesize that LST1 functions as a small immune modulator protein that regulates myeloid cell activation and cytokine signaling within the MHC class III region network. The LTB/TYROBP/NCR3 interaction network suggests LST1 may function at the intersection of TNF superfamily signaling and activating immune receptor pathways. Its small size (97 aa) and confident AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 70.4) suggest it may act as a protein-protein interaction hub or adaptor rather than an enzyme. The membrane lipoprotein attachment profile hints at potential membrane localization, possibly anchoring signaling complexes. In AML, LST1 expression may modulate leukemic cell-immune microenvironment interactions, explaining its prognostic value. The 6p21.33 CNVs may cause dosage-sensitive immune dysregulation, potentially contributing to autoimmune phenotypes or altered infection responses. LST1 represents a candidate biomarker for myeloid malignancies, a potential regulator of efferocytosis in metabolic-inflammatory disease, and an uncharacterized component of MHC class III immune regulation.
