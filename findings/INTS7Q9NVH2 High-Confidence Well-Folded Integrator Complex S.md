## Description
INTS7 (Integrator complex subunit 7, also known as C1orf73, INT7) is a 962 amino acid nuclear protein (UniProt Q9NVH2) with high-confidence well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 88.1), characteristic of stable scaffold proteins that form multi-subunit complexes. The protein contains an Armadillo-type fold (IPR016024/SSF48371, residues 23-498) that mediates protein-protein interactions through stacked alpha-helical repeats, an INTS7-specific N-terminal domain (IPR056516/PF24436, residues 26-537), a helical bundle domain (IPR056517/PF24437, residues 539-719) for structural stability, and a C-terminal domain (IPR054519/PF22965, residues 805-918) unique to this subunit. INTS7 belongs to the integrator complex subunit 7 family (IPR033060/PTHR13322, residues 19-934) with molecular functions in snRNA processing (GO:0016180) as part of the integrator complex (GO:0032039).\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals a comprehensive network of integrator complex subunits with very high confidence scores: INTS6 (0.999), INTS3 (0.999), INTS5 (0.999), INTS8 (0.999), INTS1 (0.999), INTS2 (0.998), INTS11 (0.998), INTS4 (0.996), INTS9 (0.995), and INTS10 (0.988). These interactions confirm INTS7 functions as an integral component of the 12-subunit integrator complex that associates with the C-terminal domain (CTD) of RNA polymerase II. The integrator complex mediates 3'-end processing of small nuclear RNAs (snRNAs) U1 and U2, which are essential for spliceosome assembly and pre-mRNA splicing.\n\nHPA expression reveals INTS7 is an essential protein with low tissue specificity, detected in all tissues, with nuclear bodies localization. The ubiquitous expression pattern is consistent with the fundamental role of snRNA processing in all cell types. INTS7 is involved in DNA damage response pathways and shows cell type-enhanced expression at the single-cell level. The nuclear bodies localization suggests INTS7 concentrates in nuclear subcompartments where snRNA transcription and processing occur.\n\nClinVar documents 20 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including large copy number gains (1q21.1-44, 1q25.3-32.3, 1q31.3-44, 1q32.1-44, 1q32.2-44 amplifications), deletions (1q32.3 region), duplications, and single nucleotide variants (c.372-7T>A intronic, c.1500A>C synonymous). The variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity. While no specific Mendelian disease is annotated, 1q CNVs are associated with developmental delay, intellectual disability, congenital anomalies, and cancer susceptibility. Given INTS7's essential role in snRNA processing, haploinsufficiency or overexpression may disrupt spliceosome biogenesis, causing widespread splicing defects and cellular dysfunction.\n\nConservation is strong across vertebrates and invertebrates: human (Q9NVH2, 962 aa), bovine (Q1RMS6, 962 aa), zebrafish (Q8JGR7, 964 aa), Drosophila (Q9VT41/defl, 1001 aa), indicating an ancient and essential function in RNA processing conserved from flies to humans.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: INTS7 functions as a structural scaffold subunit of the integrator complex, using its Armadillo-type fold to mediate protein-protein interactions with other INTS subunits and RNA polymerase II. The integrator complex binds the phosphorylated CTD of RNA polymerase II during snRNA gene transcription, recruiting endonucleases (INTS11/INTS9) that cleave nascent snRNA transcripts at the 3'-end. INTS7's helical bundle and C-terminal domains may stabilize the complex architecture or recruit additional factors. Disruption of INTS7 function impairs snRNA 3'-end processing, leading to accumulation of aberrant snRNA precursors, defective spliceosome assembly, and global splicing defects. This explains the essential nature of INTS7 and the developmental phenotypes associated with 1q CNVs. The DNA damage association suggests INTS7 may also participate in transcription-coupled repair or coordinate snRNA processing with genome integrity surveillance. INTS7 represents a core component of the RNA processing machinery with implications for developmental disorders and cancer.
