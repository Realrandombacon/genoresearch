## Description
LOC129389913 is a 200bp cis-regulatory element (CRE) located on chromosome 7q36.1 (GRCh38: 145443362-145443562), functionally validated as a transcriptional silencer through STARR-seq massively parallel reporter assays (MPRAs) in K562 erythroleukemia cells. This locus lacks canonical promoter, enhancer, or CTCF-bound insulator chromatin signatures but demonstrates robust repressive activity against the super core promoter SCP1. As a non-coding regulatory sequence, it has no UniProt accession, protein domains, STRING interaction partners, or Human Protein Atlas expression data (query failed to resolve Ensembl ID).\n\nClinVar analysis reveals a striking burden of 24 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variants disrupting this region. The variant spectrum is overwhelmingly dominated by duplications/copy number gains (approximately 23:1 ratio vs deletions), including massive arm-level events spanning 7q32.3-36.3 (~28 Mb), 7q33-36.3 (~22 Mb), 7q34-36.3 (~20 Mb), and whole 7q35-36.3 regions (~24 Mb). A single large-scale deletion (7q33-36.1, ~13.8 Mb) and one tetrasomy event (7q35-36.1, x4, ~6.5 Mb) are also present. This extreme duplication bias suggests that "hyper-silencing" or triplication-mediated position effects are the primary pathogenic mechanism. These CNVs are associated with 7q36 deletion/duplication syndromes, characterized by severe intellectual disability, holoprosencephaly-like features, polydactyly, and distinctive facial dysmorphisms. The locus resides in the critical 7q36 region containing SHH (Sonic Hedgehog), a master regulator of embryonic patterning.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: LOC129389913 functions as a critical distal silencer within the 7q36 topologically associating domain (TAD), specifically regulating the SHH locus or neighboring developmental genes. Given its validation in K562 cells, it likely acts as a general repression module. The mechanism involves recruitment of co-repressor complexes (e.g., PRC2, NuRD) to maintain target genes in a silenced state during specific developmental windows or in non-target tissues. The extreme duplication bias indicates that triplication of this silencer causes profound downregulation of SHH or other targets, mimicking loss-of-function mutations and leading to holoprosencephaly-like phenotypes. The single deletion event may cause derepression (overexpression) of targets, also disrupting the precise morphogen gradient required for forebrain and limb development. The presence of a tetrasomy event (x4) further supports the dosage-sensitive nature of this regulatory element, where increased copy number linearly increases repressive activity. This silencer likely functions as part of a complex enhancer-silencer balance that fine-tunes SHH expression levels during embryogenesis.
