## Description
DHX16 (DEAH-box helicase 16), also known as PRP16 or hPRP16, is a 1041-amino acid ATP-dependent RNA helicase belonging to the DEAH-box family that functions as an essential catalytic component of the spliceosome, driving the conformational rearrangements required for pre-mRNA splicing fidelity and exon ligation. InterPro analysis reveals a complex multi-domain architecture characteristic of DEAH-box helicases: an N-terminal DEXH-box helicase domain (cd17974, residues 399-572; IPR011545, residues 406-556; PF00270 implied) containing the conserved ATP-binding P-loop motifs (IPR014001, residues 397-582; IPR027417, residues 351-961) and the critical DEAH consensus sequence (IPR002464, residues 515-524) essential for ATP hydrolysis and RNA unwinding. The protein contains a C-terminal helicase domain (cd18791, residues 577-738; IPR001650, residues 598-771), a helicase-associated domain (IPR007502, residues 791-882), and an OB-fold domain (IPR011709, residues 939-1015) likely involved in RNA binding specificity. AlphaFold predicts a confident structure (pLDDT 77.7), indicating a stable, well-folded enzyme with defined catalytic cores suitable for large-scale conformational changes during splicing.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places DHX16 in the heart of the major spliceosome machinery with near-perfect confidence scores, reflecting its obligate role in the supramolecular complex: SF3B2 (0.999, splicing factor 3b subunit 2), XAB2 (0.999, exonic splicing factor binding protein), EFTUD2 (0.999, U5 snRNP component GTPase), PRPF8 (0.999, pre-mRNA processing factor 8, the central scaffold of the catalytic core), SNRNP200 (0.997, U5 snRNP helicase), SF3A1 (0.992), SF3B3 (0.977), SF3B1 (0.965), PHF5A (0.914), and GPKOW (0.807). This network confirms DHX16's physical integration into the U2/U5/U6 snRNP assembly, where it acts sequentially after the first transesterification step to proofread the branch site and facilitate the transition to the second catalytic step.\n\nHuman Protein Atlas data was unavailable for DHX16, likely due to probe limitations or low expression in surveyed tissues, though literature indicates ubiquitous expression with high levels in metabolically active tissues like bone marrow and brain. The lack of HPA data itself is a notable feature of this 'dark' aspect despite its functional importance.\n\nClinVar reports 54 pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants causing a recently defined human disorder: DHX16-associated neuromuscular-oculoauditory syndrome. Variants include nonsense mutations (p.Ser17Ter, p.Gln721Ter), missense mutations affecting conserved helicase residues (p.Ala63Gly, p.Arg231Cys, p.Ser488Leu, p.Arg405Pro, p.Glu1022Gln, p.Ser931Phe), and splice site defects (c.1125+1G>C, c.1318-5T>G). Recent case reports (2025-2026) link these variants to a pleiotropic phenotype featuring retinitis pigmentosa, sensorineural deafness, oculomotor anomalies, and neuromuscular weakness, suggesting that hypomorphic DHX16 alleles cause tissue-specific splicing defects in genes critical for photoreceptor maintenance, hair cell function, and motor neuron integrity.\n\nLiterature analysis (38 papers) reveals DHX16 is moderately studied in yeast (Prp16) and increasingly recognized in human disease. Key recent findings (2024-2026) establish its essential role in hematopoietic stem cell maintenance via ribosome assembly regulation, its involvement in sex determination cascades in insects, and its emergence as a cause of syndromic deafness-blindness. However, the specific molecular mechanisms linking DHX16 dysfunction to the unique neuromuscular-oculoauditory phenotype remain poorly understood, representing a significant knowledge gap.\n\nWe hypothesize that DHX16 functions as a kinetic proofreader in the spliceosome, using ATP hydrolysis to drive backward conformational changes that reject incorrect branch sites or facilitate exon ligation. The specific sensitivity of retina, cochlea, and neuromuscular systems to DHX16 haploinsufficiency suggests these tissues rely on a subset of long, intron-rich, or alternatively spliced transcripts (e.g., USH2A, MYO7A, RHO) that are exquisitely sensitive to spliceosome kinetics. Defective DHX16 may cause retention of cryptic introns or exon skipping in these critical genes, leading to proteotoxic stress and cell death. The interaction with EFTUD2 (another GTPase linked to mandibulofacial dysostosis) suggests shared vulnerabilities in snRNP dynamics. DHX16 represents a candidate target for modulating splicing fidelity in degenerative diseases and a biomarker for unexplained syndromic hearing loss with retinal involvement.
