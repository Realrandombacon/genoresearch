## Description
PPP1R42 (Protein phosphatase 1 regulatory subunit 42, 309 aa, UniProt Q7Z4L9) is a leucine-rich repeat (LRR)-containing regulatory subunit of protein phosphatase 1 (PP1) that controls centrosome duplication, separation, and primary cilia dynamics. Structural analysis reveals: (1) N-terminal LRR domain architecture (IPR001611, 3 LRR repeats at aa 51-72, 73-94, 95-116) for protein-protein interactions; (2) SDS22/Internalin LRR-containing superfamily (IPR050836, aa 2-239) suggesting homology to PP1 regulatory subunit SDS22; (3) C-terminal PP1-binding region (cd21340, aa 27-246) for catalytic subunit recruitment. AlphaFold structure shows high confidence (pLDDT 85.9), indicating stable LRR solenoid fold typical of protein interaction scaffolds. STRING interactions identify proteasome and COP9 signalosome partners: FAU (0.768), GPS1 (0.762), COPS4 (0.758), PSMD12 (0.738), COPS6 (0.734), PSMD6 (0.714), COPS2 (0.550), COPS3 (0.503), suggesting roles in protein complex assembly and ubiquitin-mediated regulation. HPA shows testis-enriched expression (8.5 nTPM) and fallopian tube expression (5.1 nTPM), consistent with centrosome/cilia functions in germ cells and ciliated epithelia. ClinVar contains 13 pathogenic variants including multiple copy number gains/losses at 8p23.3-q24.3, indicating clinical relevance though specific diseases not yet defined. Literature (5 papers) reveals emerging roles: (1) centrosome dynamics - PPP1R42 binds PP1 and regulates centrosome duplication cycle in ARPE-19 cells; (2) cilia formation - positive regulator of cilia dynamics through PP1-mediated dephosphorylation of centrosomal substrates; (3) kinase interaction - novel kinase activities regulate cilia formation via PPP1R42-PP1 complex. Functional hypothesis: PPP1R42 functions as a PP1 targeting subunit that directs phosphatase activity to centrosomal and ciliary substrates through LRR-mediated protein interactions, controlling phosphorylation states of proteins required for centrosome duplication and ciliogenesis. Mechanistic proposal: N-terminal LRR domain serves as scaffold binding centrosomal proteins (potentially including γ-tubulin, pericentrin, or CEP family members) while C-terminal region recruits PP1 catalytic subunit, creating holoenzyme complex that dephosphorylates key regulators of centrosome cycle (e.g., NEDD1, CP110) and cilia assembly/disassembly—positioning PPP1R42 as critical link between PP1 signaling and microtubule organizing center function with implications for ciliopathies and centrosome amplification in cancer.
