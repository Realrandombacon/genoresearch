# FAM124B: Mitochondrial-Nuclear Scaffold Protein Linking Chromatin Remodeling to Placental Development

**Date:** 2026-03-23T00:12:07.551937

**Quality Score:** 3.9/10  (E=7.0, D=0.8) [MODERATE]

## Description
FAM124B encodes a 455 amino acid protein (UniProt Q9H5Z6) with a conserved FAM124 domain (IPR046365/PF15067, residues 10-244) and medium-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 63.1), suggesting partial intrinsic disorder. The protein dual-localizes to nucleoplasm and mitochondria per HPA, with tissue-enhanced expression peaking in placenta (8.0 nTPM) and cancer-enriched transcription patterns. STRING interactions reveal high-confidence binding to chromatin remodelers CHD8 (score 0.760) and CHD7 (0.570), plus paralogs FAM124A (0.433) and FAM174B (0.521), indicating membership in a chromatin regulatory complex. ClinVar documents 29 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly copy number gains/losses on chromosome 2q36.2, implicating FAM124B dosage sensitivity in disease. Conservation extends to mouse (Q8BLQ0, 456 aa, ~98% identity) and bovine orthologs.

FUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: FAM124B functions as a mitochondrial-nuclear scaffold protein that bridges chromatin remodeling complexes (via CHD8/CHD7 interactions) with mitochondrial metabolism, particularly during placental development where expression peaks. The FAM124 domain mediates protein-protein interactions within a multi-subunit complex regulating transcription of genes involved in trophoblast differentiation and embryonic growth.

MECHANISTIC PROPOSAL: FAM124B recruits CHD8/CHD7 ATP-dependent chromatin remodelers to specific genomic loci via its structured N-terminal domain (residues 10-244), while its disordered C-terminal region (low pLDDT) enables dynamic, context-dependent interactions with mitochondrial outer membrane proteins. This dual localization allows FAM124B to coordinate nuclear gene expression with mitochondrial metabolic state, explaining placental enrichment (high metabolic demand tissue) and cancer elevation (Warburg effect reprogramming). Pathogenic CNVs disrupt this scaffolding function, causing developmental disorders through impaired chromatin accessibility at growth-regulatory enhancers.
