# RPAIN (RPA-Interacting Protein, HRIP): Nuclear Scaffold Protein Coordinating DNA Replication Stress Response and Ribosome Biogenesis via Tripartite Domain Architecture Mediating Replication Protein A Complex Recruitment to Nucleolar Fibrillar Centers

**Date:** 2026-06-17T12:05:26.645139

**Quality Score:** 9/10 (EXCELLENT)

**Confidence:** HIGH

## Description
RPAIN encodes a 219 aa nuclear protein (UniProt Q86UA6), also known as HRIP (HRad51 Interacting Protein), that functions as a critical coordinator of DNA replication stress response and nucleolar function through direct interaction with the Replication Protein A (RPA) complex. The protein features a compact tripartite domain architecture unique to the RPAIN family: an N-terminal RPA-interacting domain (IPR028158/PF14766, residues 8-47) mediating binding to RPA heterotrimer; a central domain of unknown specific function (IPR028155/PF14767, residues 60-126) potentially involved in protein oligomerization or additional partner recruitment; and a C-terminal domain (IPR028159/PF14768, residues 136-216) completing the functional unit. STRING interactions confirm RPAIN operates within DNA replication and repair networks: RPA2 (0.804) and RPA1 (0.770), the essential single-stranded DNA-binding complex protecting exposed DNA during replication/recombination; XPO1 (0.606), exportin-1 mediating nuclear-cytoplasmic transport; ETAA1 (0.524), Ewing tumor-associated antigen 1 activating ATR kinase at stalled replication forks; EPS15 (0.525), endocytosis adaptor potentially linking to membrane trafficking; POLD2 (0.458), DNA polymerase delta subunit for lagging strand synthesis; and ANAPC16 (0.486), anaphase-promoting complex component regulating cell cycle progression. HPA confirms ubiquitous expression (detected in all tissues) with low tissue specificity, nucleoplasmic and nucleoli fibrillar center subcellular localization, and classification as an essential protein consistent with fundamental role in genome maintenance. ClinVar lists 27 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number losses (17p13.3-13.2 spanning 0.5-5.7Mb, 17p13.2-13.1 spanning 5.1-7.4Mb, 17p13.2 spanning 4.4-5.4Mb) and gains (17p13.2-12 spanning 4.7-14.7Mb, 17p13.3-11.2 spanning 0.0005-21.5Mb), plus point mutations (c.501G>C p.Leu167Phe, c.330C>T p.Ser110=), demonstrating dosage sensitivity though specific monogenic diseases remain undefined. AlphaFold structure prediction yields high confidence (global pLDDT 81.9), revealing well-folded compact structure consistent with stable protein-protein interaction scaffold. Mechanistically, RPAIN operates through integrated modes: (1) RPA recruitment—N-terminal domain binds RPA heterotrimer targeting it to specific genomic loci or nucleolar structures; (2) Replication stress response—coordinates with ETAA1-ATR pathway sensing stalled forks and activating checkpoint signaling; (3) Nucleolar function—fibrillar center localization suggests role in ribosomal DNA replication or rRNA processing coupling genome maintenance to ribosome biogenesis; (4) UV damage response—gene ontology indicates involvement in UV response potentially facilitating nucleotide excision repair at damaged sites; (5) Nuclear import—acts upstream of protein import into nucleus suggesting regulatory role in nuclear trafficking. The 2016 literature identifies long non-coding RNA RPAIN regulating trophoblast invasion/apoptosis via complement C1q in preeclampsia, suggesting additional transcriptional regulatory functions. Loss-of-function causes defective RPA recruitment, impaired replication fork stability, accumulated DNA damage triggering cell cycle arrest or apoptosis, disrupted nucleolar integrity affecting ribosome production, and developmental defects reflected in CNV-associated phenotypes.
