## Description
SLC16A9 (Q7RTY1, 509 aa), also known as Monocarboxylate Transporter 9 (MCT9), is a specialized solute carrier belonging to the Major Facilitator Superfamily (MFS) within the proton-linked monocarboxylate transporter family (PTHR11360). InterPro identifies a canonical MFS architecture comprising two domains forming a central substrate-binding cavity: Monocarboxylate transporter 9 domain (cd17428, aa 12-482), MFS general substrate transporter-like domains (G3DSA:1.20.1250.20, aa 9-200, 297-490), and multiple MFS family signatures (IPR011701, IPR020846, IPR036259, PF07690, PS50850, aa 8-490). The structure supports symport activity driven by proton gradients. AlphaFold predicts a confident structure (pLDDT 77.3, residues 1-509) consistent with a well-folded transmembrane protein featuring the characteristic 12-transmembrane helix bundle of MFS transporters, with ordered N- and C-terminal cytoplasmic tails. HPA shows tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in kidney (178.4 nTPM) and adrenal gland (142.9 nTPM); localized to plasma membrane (though HPA notes nucleoplasm/cell junctions, functional consensus places it at the membrane); classified as a metabolic protein and transporter involved in symport. STRING reveals 10 interactions forming a renal solute transport network: SLC17A3 (0.714, sodium-dependent phosphate transporter 4), SLC17A4 (0.659, sodium-dependent phosphate transporter 3), SLC22A5 (0.566, organic cation/carnitine transporter OCTN2), PDZK1 (0.558, PDZ domain-containing protein 1, a scaffold for apical transporters), SLC22A11 (0.549, organic anion transporter OAT4), SLC17A1 (0.538, sodium-dependent phosphate transporter 1), SLC22A12 (0.536, urate transporter URAT1), SLC2A9 (0.513, glucose transporter GLUT9/urate transporter), CARMIL1 (0.446), and INHBC (0.416). The strong clustering with urate (SLC22A12, SLC2A9) and carnitine (SLC22A5) transporters confirms its role in renal reabsorption of these metabolites. ClinVar contains 17 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number losses (chr10:51735638-70791246, chr10:56031210-65660398, chr10:50250603-69256083) and gains (chr10:42709645-100834951), plus an inversion (chr10:43611191_61663279), suggesting dosage sensitivity, though no specific monogenic disease is yet explicitly named. Functional hypothesis: SLC16A9 functions as a proton-coupled symporter primarily responsible for the renal reabsorption of creatine and carnitine from the glomerular filtrate, preventing their urinary loss. The high expression in kidney (178.4 nTPM) and interaction with URAT1 (SLC22A12) and GLUT9 (SLC2A9) place it within the apical reabsorption machinery of the proximal tubule. It may also contribute to urate handling, either directly or via complex formation with other SLC22 members. Disruption via CNVs likely leads to renal wasting of creatine (potentially causing muscle energy deficits) and carnitine (leading to secondary carnitine deficiency and impaired fatty acid oxidation), and possibly hyperuricosuria or gout susceptibility. Conservation across vertebrates (mouse 508 aa, rat 508 aa) confirms essential metabolic role. NOTE: This gene is a key but under-appreciated regulator of renal metabolite homeostasis.
