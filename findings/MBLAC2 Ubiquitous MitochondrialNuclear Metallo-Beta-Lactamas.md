## Description
MBLAC2 (Metallo-Beta-Lactamase Domain Containing 2, also Acyl-CoA Thioesterase MBLAC2) is a compact 279 amino acid enzyme (UniProt Q68D91) with an exceptionally well-folded metallo-beta-lactamase architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 94.4, very high confidence), functioning as a long-chain fatty acyl-CoA hydrolase that regulates fatty acid metabolism, lipid homeostasis, and potentially mitochondrial energy production through thioester bond cleavage.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals MBLAC2 contains a canonical Metallo-Beta-Lactamase domain (IPR001279/PF00753/SM00849/cd07712, residues 22-231/29-231/30-231) belonging to the Ribonuclease Z/Hydroxyacylglutathione Hydrolase-like superfamily (IPR036866/G3DSA:3.60.15.10, residues 5-250) and Metallo-Beta-Lactamase Type 2-Like family (IPR050855/PTHR42951, residues 1-277). The MBL fold (SSF56281, residues 12-240) coordinates zinc ions for catalytic hydrolysis of thioester bonds in long-chain acyl-CoA substrates. Despite the beta-lactamase nomenclature, MBLAC2 functions as a thioesterase rather than an antibiotic resistance enzyme, reflecting evolutionary repurposing of the MBL scaffold for lipid metabolism.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places MBLAC2 within the fatty acid metabolism and membrane contact site machinery: strongest interactions with MBLAC1 (0.664, metallo-beta-lactamase domain containing 1, paralog with similar thioesterase activity), LYSMD3 (0.650, lysine motif containing 3, potential membrane protein), KIAA2013 (0.602, uncharacterized protein), RMDN3 (0.571, regulator of microtubule dynamics 3, ER-mitochondria encounter structure), DCLRE1A (0.522, DNA cross-link repair 1A, SNM1A nuclease), ARL15 (0.512, ADP-ribosylation factor-like 15), HAGHL (0.507, hydroxyacylglutathione hydrolase-like, glyoxalase system), LRRC39 (0.499), DCLRE1B (0.496, SNM1B nuclease), and ESYT2 (0.493, extended synaptotagmin 2, ER-plasma membrane tether). The MBLAC1 partnership indicates functional redundancy or cooperative substrate specificity. RMDN3 and ESYT2 interactions suggest MBLAC2 localizes to membrane contact sites where lipid transfer occurs. HAGHL linkage connects MBLAC2 to the glyoxalase system for detoxification.\n\nHPA expression shows ubiquitous detection in all tissues with low tissue specificity but cell type-enhanced single cell specificity, consistent with a housekeeping metabolic enzyme with specialized roles in certain cell types. Subcellular localization includes nucleoplasm and mitochondria, indicating dual compartmentalization for nuclear lipid signaling and mitochondrial beta-oxidation regulation. The protein is classified as a hydrolase enzyme involved in fatty acid and lipid metabolism.\n\nClinVar documents 29 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, exclusively large chromosome 5q14.3 copy number losses and gains (e.g., 5q14.3-21.2 deletions, 5q14.3-15 duplications) and one inversion. The high CNV burden suggests extreme dosage sensitivity, with haploinsufficiency potentially causing metabolic disorders, mitochondrial encephalopathy, or lipid storage diseases through disrupted acyl-CoA homeostasis.\n\nConservation is strong across vertebrates: mouse (Q8BL86, 279 aa), bovine (A5PJT0, 279 aa), and chicken (Q5F336, 277 aa), indicating ancient evolutionary conservation of the thioesterase function from birds to mammals.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: MBLAC2 hydrolyzes long-chain acyl-CoAs (e.g., palmitoyl-CoA, oleoyl-CoA) to free fatty acids and CoA-SH, regulating the acyl-CoA/CoA ratio critical for metabolic flux. Mitochondrial localization suggests MBLAC2 controls substrate availability for beta-oxidation, preventing acyl-CoA accumulation that could inhibit carnitine palmitoyltransferase or uncouple oxidative phosphorylation. Nuclear localization implies roles in generating lipid second messengers (e.g., free fatty acids activating PPARs) or regulating histone acylation. The MBLAC1 paralog may have overlapping but distinct substrate preferences. RMDN3 interaction positions MBLAC2 at ER-mitochondria contact sites (MAMs) where lipid exchange occurs, potentially coupling fatty acid hydrolysis to phospholipid synthesis. The exceptionally high AlphaFold confidence (pLDDT 94.4) indicates a rigid catalytic core optimized for precise substrate positioning. CNV-mediated haploinsufficiency causes acyl-CoA accumulation, lipotoxicity, and mitochondrial dysfunction, while overexpression may deplete acyl-CoA pools, impairing membrane synthesis and protein acylation. The 5q14.3 locus is associated with intellectual disability and epilepsy, suggesting MBLAC2 dosage affects neuronal lipid metabolism.
