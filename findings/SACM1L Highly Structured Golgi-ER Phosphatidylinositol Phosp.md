## Description
SACM1L (Q9NTJ5, SAC1, 587 aa) is an exceptionally well-folded (AlphaFold pLDDT: 90.8) phosphatidylinositol phosphatase that localizes to Golgi apparatus and nucleoplasm, serving as a critical regulator of phosphoinositide homeostasis at ER-Golgi membrane contact sites. InterPro analysis confirms membership in the SAC phosphatase family (IPR002013, PF02383, PS50275, PTHR45662) with the catalytic SAC domain spanning residues 58-451, containing the signature CX5R(T/S) active site motif essential for dephosphorylating PI4P and PI3P. HPA data shows ubiquitous expression across all tissues with low specificity, consistent with fundamental roles in lipid metabolism and vesicular trafficking. While STRING returned no direct interactors (suggesting transient membrane associations), established literature positions SACM1L in complex with VAP proteins at ER-Golgi contact sites, where it counterbalances PI4-kinase activity to maintain phosphoinositide gradients required for COPII vesicle formation and Golgi-to-ER retrograde transport. ClinVar documents 9 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains/losses spanning chromosome 3p regions, implicating SACM1L dosage sensitivity in developmental disorders though specific monogenic phenotypes remain undefined. With only 21 literature citations despite its fundamental role in lipid signaling, SACM1L represents a genuine 'dark' enzyme whose dysregulation likely contributes to congenital disorders of glycosylation, neurodegenerative diseases involving lipid accumulation, and cancer metabolism rewiring.
