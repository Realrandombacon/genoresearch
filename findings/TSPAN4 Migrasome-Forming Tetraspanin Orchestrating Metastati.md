# TSPAN4: Migrasome-Forming Tetraspanin Orchestrating Metastatic Niche Assembly, VSMC Phenotypic Switching, and EGFR Stability via Tetraspanin Web Interactions

**Quality Score:** 9/10 (EXCELLENT)

**Confidence:** MEDIUM

## Description
TSPAN4 (Tetraspanin-4, UniProt O14817) is a 238-amino acid integral membrane protein belonging to the transmembrane 4 superfamily (tetraspanins), functioning as a master organizer of membrane microdomains ('tetraspanin web') and the specific structural marker of migrasomes—large vesicular organelles mediating intercellular communication during cell migration. The protein architecture features four conserved transmembrane domains forming a tight bundle, connected by a short intracellular N-terminus, a large extracellular loop (LEL, aa 105-202) containing the signature CCG motif and variable regions determining partner specificity, and a short C-terminal cytoplasmic tail. InterPro analysis confirms membership in the tetraspanin family (IPR000301, PF00335, PIRSF002419, IPR018499) with the characteristic LEL domain (cd03165, G3DSA:1.10.1450.10, IPR008952) essential for homo- and hetero-oligomerization. The AlphaFold predicted structure shows high confidence (pLDDT 89.7), indicating a well-folded stable transmembrane scaffold with a rigid LEL domain projecting from the membrane surface to engage partner proteins. STRING interaction network places TSPAN4 within the cell adhesion and migration machinery with medium-confidence associations to: CD81 (0.698) and CD9 (0.628), canonical tetraspanin partners forming the core tetraspanin web; ITGB1 (0.649) and ITGA6 (0.590), integrin beta-1 and alpha-6 subunits linking to extracellular matrix and cytoskeleton; CHD7 (0.649), chromodomain helicase DNA binding protein 7 suggesting nuclear crosstalk or developmental regulation; NISCH (0.574), nisch regulator of cell adhesion; TMEM8B (0.571), another tetraspanin-like protein; ADCY10 (0.531), soluble adenylyl cyclase linking to cAMP signaling; GDF15 (0.422), growth differentiation factor 15 stress cytokine; and SERPINA7 (0.422), thyroxine-binding globulin. HPA expression profiles demonstrate low tissue specificity with detection in all tissues, group-enriched expression in blood cells, cell type-enhanced specificity, and classification as a predicted membrane protein with low cancer specificity at the protein level despite strong mRNA associations in tumors. ClinVar records document 29 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants consisting primarily of large copy number gains and duplications on chromosome 11p15.5-15.4 (spanning 198kb-3.4Mb to 230kb-8.8Mb regions), suggesting dosage sensitivity where overexpression may contribute to developmental disorders or cancer predisposition, though no specific monogenic syndrome has been definitively assigned yet. Literature analysis (77 papers) reveals emerging critical roles: TSPAN4 is the defining marker of migrasomes, driving metastatic niche assembly in pancreatic adenocarcinoma through migrasome-mediated metabolic reprogramming and stromal-immune crosstalk; regulates vascular smooth muscle cell (VSMC) phenotypic switching and intimal hyperplasia by stabilizing TPM1 (tropomyosin-1) to organize the actin cytoskeleton; controls glioblastoma progression by maintaining EGFR stability preventing lysosomal degradation; serves as a prognostic signature correlating with immune infiltrates and metabolic disturbances in hepatocellular carcinoma; and shapes the suppressive tumor immune microenvironment across pan-cancer types. We hypothesize that TSPAN4 functions through a spatial organization mechanism: (1) In migrating cells, TSPAN4 accumulates at the rear retraction fibers where it nucleates migrasome formation by clustering integrins (ITGB1/ITGA6) and recruiting cytoskeletal regulators, facilitating the release of signaling cargo (cytokines, miRNAs, metabolites) to precondition distant metastatic sites; (2) In VSMCs, TSPAN4-TPM1 interaction maintains contractile phenotype, and loss of TSPAN4 triggers synthetic switching promoting proliferation and intimal hyperplasia in atherosclerosis/restenosis; (3) In glioblastoma, TSPAN4 binds EGFR within tetraspanin-enriched microdomains (TEMs) shielding it from ubiquitination and lysosomal sorting, sustaining oncogenic signaling; (4) The high-confidence LEL structure suggests specific hydrophobic patches mediating selective partner recruitment distinct from other tetraspanins like CD9/CD81; (5) ClinVar CNVs may cause disease via TSPAN4 overexpression enhancing migrasome production and pathological cell migration or immune evasion; (6) Interaction with CHD7 implies a potential non-canonical role in chromatin regulation during development. Therapeutic targeting of TSPAN4-migrasome axis could block metastasis or restenosis by disrupting this unique vesicular communication system.
