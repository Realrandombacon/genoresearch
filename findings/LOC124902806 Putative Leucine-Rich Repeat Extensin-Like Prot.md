## Description
LOC124902806 (leucine-rich repeat extensin-like protein 5) represents a genuinely uncharacterized genomic locus at chromosome 11p15.4 (NC_000011.10, positions 7538538-7508018) annotated as a putative protein-coding gene but lacking experimental protein evidence. Despite the descriptive name suggesting homology to plant leucine-rich repeat extensin proteins (LRX family), comprehensive UniProt searches returned zero human protein entries, with all LRX homologs identified exclusively in Arabidopsis thaliana (LRX1-Q9SN46, LRX2-O48809, LRX3-Q9T0K5, LRX5-Q9SN46), indicating this may represent a lineage-specific annotation or pseudogene rather than a conserved mammalian protein. Human Protein Atlas could not resolve LOC124902806 to an Ensembl ID, confirming absence of transcript/protein evidence in major expression databases. STRING interaction network returned no protein partners, consistent with lack of characterized protein product. However, ClinVar contains 2 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number gain variants spanning the 11p15 region (GRCh38/hg38 11p15.5-15.4 chr11:61793-10727969 x3 gain; GRCh38/hg38 11p15.5-15.1 chr11:446754-18904742 x3 gain), demonstrating clinical significance where chromosomal duplication encompassing this locus contributes to disease phenotypes. The 11p15.4 chromosomal region is known to harbor imprinted genes involved in growth regulation (including CDKN1C, KCNQ1, IGF2), and duplications in this region are associated with Beckwith-Wiedemann syndrome and other overgrowth disorders. Given the complete absence of protein evidence across UniProt/HPA/STRING databases despite the descriptive gene name, combined with ClinVar CNV pathogenicity in a clinically significant chromosomal region, LOC124902806 likely represents either: (1) a non-coding RNA or regulatory element misannotated as protein-coding, (2) a processed pseudogene derived from ancestral LRX-like sequences, or (3) a genuine but extremely low-abundance transcript whose pathogenicity reflects disruption of neighboring imprinted gene regulation rather than loss of LRREXL5 function itself. The leucine-rich repeat extensin nomenclature suggests potential extracellular matrix or cell wall-related functions if translated, but human LRX homologs remain unconfirmed.
