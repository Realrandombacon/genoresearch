## Description
USF3 (Upstream Transcription Factor Family Member 3, also KIAA2018) is a 2245 amino acid nuclear transcription factor (UniProt Q68DE3) with an intrinsically disordered architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 36.6) except for a structured N-terminal basic helix-loop-helix (bHLH) DNA-binding domain, functioning as an epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) regulator that controls gene expression programs during development, tissue homeostasis, and cancer progression through bHLH-mediated DNA binding and protein-protein interactions.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals USF3 contains a canonical Myc-type basic helix-loop-helix domain (IPR011598/PF00010/PS50888/SM00353/cd18910, residues 15-79/18-75/20-70/24-75) embedded within the helix-loop-helix DNA-binding domain superfamily (IPR036638/G3DSA:4.10.280.10/SSF47459, residues 11-104/17-104). The bHLH domain mediates DNA binding to E-box sequences (CANNTG) and protein dimerization (GO:0046983). USF3 belongs to the Upstream Transcription Factor 3 family (IPR048064) and is classified as an Epithelial-Mesenchymal Transition Regulator (IPR053252/PTHR46970, residues 1-2245). The low-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 36.6) indicates extensive intrinsic disorder outside the bHLH domain, enabling binding to multiple transcriptional co-regulators.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places USF3 within the transcriptional regulation and chromatin remodeling network: strongest interactions with TAB3 (0.606, TGF-beta activated kinase 1 and MAP3K7 binding protein 3, NF-kappaB signaling scaffold), ASTE1 (0.543, asterless homolog 1, centrosome protein), ANKAR (0.480, ankyrin repeat and KH domain-containing protein, RNA-binding regulator), ADNP2 (0.461, activity-dependent neuroprotector homeobox protein 2, chromatin regulator), ZNF236 (0.457, zinc finger protein 236, transcriptional repressor), CCDC150 (0.455, coiled-coil domain-containing protein 150, centrosome-associated), CAPN12 (0.455, calpain-12, calcium-dependent protease), TCF20 (0.449, transcription factor 20, high-mobility group box transcription factor), TMEM244 (0.447, transmembrane protein 244), and MRPL34 (0.432, mitochondrial ribosomal protein L34). The TAB3-TCF20-ADNP2 cluster links USF3 to NF-kappaB signaling, chromatin remodeling, and neurodevelopment. The ASTE1-CCDC150 partnership connects USF3 to centrosome function and cell division.\n\nHPA expression shows low tissue specificity with detection in all tissues, indicating ubiquitous housekeeping expression consistent with fundamental transcriptional regulation. Subcellular localization includes nucleoplasm, nucleoli, and centrosome, confirming USF3 operates in both transcriptional and cell division contexts. The protein is classified as a predicted intracellular protein with DNA-binding molecular function.\n\nClinVar documents 32 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including one 3' UTR variant (c.*977C>G), multiple chromosome 3q11.1-13.31 copy number losses and gains, and deletions. The high CNV burden indicates dosage sensitivity, with haploinsufficiency or overexpression causing developmental disorders, cancer predisposition, or EMT-related pathologies through disrupted transcriptional programs.\n\nConservation is evident across vertebrates: human (Q68DE3, 2245 aa), goldfish (A0A671W8J6, 2354 aa), Asian bonytongue (A0A8C9VDI1, 2223 aa), indicating strong evolutionary conservation of EMT regulation functions.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: USF3 functions as a bHLH transcription factor that regulates EMT gene expression programs through E-box binding and recruitment of chromatin modifiers. The TAB3 interaction (0.602) links USF3 to NF-kappaB and TGF-beta signaling pathways that drive EMT. TCF20 and ADNP2 partnerships position USF3 within chromatin remodeling complexes that modulate accessibility of EMT target genes. The ASTE1-CCDC150 centrosome association suggests USF3 may coordinate transcriptional programs with cell cycle progression, ensuring EMT occurs during appropriate cell division phases. The intrinsically disordered C-terminal region (pLDDT 36.6, residues 105-2245) enables binding to multiple co-activators and co-repressors, allowing context-dependent regulation. The nucleolar localization suggests roles in ribosome biogenesis coordination with EMT. The 3' UTR variant (c.*977C>G) may disrupt mRNA stability or miRNA regulation. CNV-mediated haploinsufficiency causes developmental defects through insufficient EMT during organogenesis. USF3 overexpression in cancers promotes metastasis through enhanced EMT. USF3 represents a master regulator linking transcriptional control, centrosome function, and cell fate decisions.
