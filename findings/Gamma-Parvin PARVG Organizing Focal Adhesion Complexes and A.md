## Description
PARVG (Parvin Gamma, UniProt Q9HBI0) is a 331-amino acid focal adhesion scaffold protein functioning in integrin-mediated cell adhesion, actin cytoskeleton organization, and cell migration. InterPro analysis confirms canonical parvin architecture: two calponin homology (CH) domains (IPR001715/PF00307, first CH aa 45-150, second CH aa 196-317/212-314) characteristic of actin-binding proteins, parvin family membership (IPR028433/PTHR12114/PIRSF039131, aa 18-319), and GO annotations for actin binding (GO:0003779), cell adhesion (GO:0007155), and actin cytoskeleton organization (GO:0030036). STRING interactions reveal exceptionally high-confidence partnerships with focal adhesion complex components: ILK (0.928, integrin-linked kinase), PARVA (0.918, alpha-parvin), PARVB (0.911, beta-parvin), ACTB (0.905, beta-actin), ACTG1 (0.901, gamma-actin), TLN2 (0.864, talin-2), TLN1 (0.623, talin-1), PXN (0.590, paxillin), LIMS1 (0.580, LIM and senescent cell antigen-like domains 1), and RSU1 (0.572, Ras suppressor protein 1), confirming integration into the ILK-PINCH-parvin (IPP) complex and focal adhesion machinery. HPA shows tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in bone marrow (46.9 nTPM) and lymphoid tissue (28.1 nTPM), with cell type-enhanced pattern consistent with roles in hematopoietic cell migration and immune cell trafficking. AlphaFold predicts high-confidence structure (pLDDT 81.7) for the full 331 aa, indicating a well-folded tandem CH domain architecture typical of actin-binding proteins. ClinVar documents 53 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including extensive copy number losses on chromosome 22q13.2-13.33 and gains on 22q13.1-13.31, though specific disease phenotypes remain incompletely defined. Literature (24 papers) reveals emerging functions: (1) PARVG differential expression and immune infiltration prognostic in uterine corpus endometrial carcinoma (2 citations, 2022, retracted 2023) - cancer biomarker potential; (2) PARVG gene polymorphism associated with operational renal allograft tolerance (9 citations, 2012) - transplant immunology relevance; (3) PARVG evaluated as candidate tumor suppressor gene for colorectal and breast cancer (16 citations, 2003) - cancer genetics. Functional hypothesis: PARVG serves as a focal adhesion scaffold protein linking integrin signaling to actin cytoskeleton dynamics through the IPP complex, regulating cell migration, adhesion turnover, and mechanotransduction, with roles in cancer metastasis, immune cell trafficking, and transplant tolerance. Mechanistic proposal: The tandem CH domain architecture (aa 45-150, aa 196-317) enables direct actin filament binding, anchoring the IPP complex to the cytoskeleton; PARVG binds ILK (integrin-linked kinase) through its N-terminal region, forming the core IPP complex with PINCH; this complex localizes to focal adhesions where it connects integrin receptors to actin stress fibers; PARVG coordinates with talins (TLN1/TLN2) and paxillin (PXN) to regulate focal adhesion assembly/disassembly during cell migration; in hematopoietic cells (high bone marrow/lymphoid expression), PARVG mediates immune cell trafficking through endothelial barriers; copy number losses on 22q13 may disrupt focal adhesion integrity affecting cell migration and cancer metastasis; PARVG polymorphisms influencing allograft tolerance suggest roles in leukocyte adhesion and immune synapse formation; as a tumor suppressor candidate, PARVG loss may promote metastatic behavior through enhanced cell motility and reduced adhesion; the parvin paralogs (PARVA, PARVB) show functional redundancy but tissue-specific expression patterns.
