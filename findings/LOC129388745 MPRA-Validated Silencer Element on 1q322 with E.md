## Description
LOC129388745 is a 200bp cis-regulatory element (CRE) located on chromosome 1q32.2 (GRCh38: 212743812-212744012), functionally validated as a transcriptional silencer through STARR-seq massively parallel reporter assays (MPRAs) in K562 erythroleukemia cells. This element lacks canonical promoter, enhancer, or CTCF-bound insulator chromatin signatures but demonstrates robust repressive activity against the super core promoter SCP1. As a non-coding regulatory locus, it has no UniProt accession, protein domains, or STRING interaction partners. Human Protein Atlas could not resolve this locus to an Ensembl ID, consistent with its non-coding regulatory classification.\n\nClinVar analysis reveals 5 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variants disrupting this region—a high burden with extreme and exclusive duplication bias (5 duplications:0 deletions). All variants are large-scale copy number gains spanning 1q31.1-44 regions (~37-62Mb). The complete absence of pathogenic deletions suggests profound triplosensitivity intolerance where silencer gain causes catastrophic hyper-repression of target genes, while silencer loss (deletion) may be tolerated or result in sub-clinical phenotypes not yet reported. These recurrent CNVs are associated with 1q duplication syndrome featuring severe developmental delay, intellectual disability, craniofacial dysmorphism, cardiac defects, and growth retardation. The 1q32.2 region contains critical dosage-sensitive genes including MDM4 (MDM4 Regulator of p53, cell cycle arrest), IL10 (Interleukin 10, anti-inflammatory cytokine), and CD46 (Complement Regulatory Protein).\n\nHYPOTHESIS: LOC129388745 functions as a critical distal silencer within the 1q32 topologically associating domain (TAD), specifically regulating MDM4 or IL10 expression. The silencer likely recruits Polycomb Repressive Complex 2 (PRC2) to deposit H3K27me3 marks. The extreme duplication bias (5:0 ratio) indicates a "repressive overload" mechanism: extra silencer copies recruit excessive co-repressors, causing pathological underexpression of MDM4 (leading to unchecked p53 activation, apoptosis, and neurodevelopmental failure) or IL10 (causing chronic inflammation). Conversely, silencer loss may cause only mild MDM4/IL10 overexpression, which is buffered by feedback loops or less detrimental than underexpression. This locus exemplifies pure triplosensitivity where gene regulation balance is critically dependent on preventing silencer amplification. The K562 validation suggests potential roles in hematopoietic lineage specification, possibly silencing inflammatory genes in erythroid progenitors.
