# ACAD9: Dual-Function Mitochondrial Acyl-CoA Dehydrogenase Serving as Complex I Assembly Factor and Fatty Acid β-Oxidation Enzyme in Primary Mitochondrial Disease, Ovarian Cancer Metabolism, and Osteoporosis Prevention

## Description
ACAD9 (Acyl-CoA Dehydrogenase Family Member 9, Q9H845) encodes a 621 aa mitochondrial protein functioning as a moonlighting enzyme with dual roles: (1) essential assembly factor for mitochondrial Complex I (NADH:ubiquinone oxidoreductase) biogenesis, and (2) acyl-CoA dehydrogenase catalyzing the first step of fatty acid β-oxidation. Structural architecture reveals canonical acyl-CoA dehydrogenase fold with N-terminal domain (G3DSA:1.10.540.10, residues 61-175), middle domain (IPR006091, residues 177-277), and C-terminal domain (IPR009075, residues 290-437/486-609) forming three-domain structure characteristic of acyl-CoA dehydrogenases; conserved catalytic sites (IPR006089, residues 179-191/399-418) enable FAD-dependent dehydrogenation; AlphaFold prediction (pLDDT 91.9) confirms very high confidence well-folded structure consistent with stable enzymatic function and protein-protein interactions. STRING interactions define Complex I assembly machinery with ultra-high confidence: TMEM126B (0.999) and TIMMDC1 (0.999) as core Complex I assembly factors, NDUFAF3 (0.998)/NDUFAF1 (0.899) as Complex I assembly chaperones, catalytic subunits NDUFS2 (0.998)/NDUFS3 (0.882)/NDUFS7 (0.719), MT-ND6 (0.997) mitochondrial-encoded subunit, NDUFA13 (0.997), and ECSIT (0.623) linking to TLR signaling. HPA localizes ACAD9 to mitochondria as oxidoreductase involved in primary mitochondrial disease with ubiquitous expression detected in all tissues. ClinVar documents 269 pathogenic variants including nonsense mutations (p.Glu260Ter), frameshift deletions (p.Met1fs, p.Leu98fs, p.Phe425fs), splice site mutations (c.633+1G>A, c.151-1G>C, c.1485+2T>G, c.1279-1G>C), and missense variants (p.Ile222Asn, p.Val384Met). Literature (120 papers) demonstrates disease mechanisms: ACAD9 deficiency causes mitochondrial Complex I deficiency presenting as infantile cardiomyopathy, lactic acidosis, and neuromuscular syndrome responsive to riboflavin/bezafibrate/nicotinamide riboside therapy; moonlighting cytosolic function suppresses TRAF6-mediated osteoclastogenesis protecting against osteoporosis; metabolic gatekeeper role coordinates linoleic acid metabolism and redox homeostasis driving high-grade serous ovarian cancer progression. Mechanistic hypothesis: ACAD9 functions as homodimer with FAD cofactor bound at interface between N-terminal and C-terminal domains; catalytic glutamate (Glu260) acts as base abstracting proton from substrate α-carbon while FAD accepts hydride forming enoyl-CoA product; as Complex I assembly factor, ACAD9 interacts with TMEM126B-TIMMDC1 module stabilizing membrane arm assembly during biogenesis; dual functionality explained by distinct cellular pools: mitochondrial matrix pool participates in β-oxidation of very long-chain fatty acids while inner membrane-associated pool scaffolds Complex I assembly intermediates; pathogenic variants disrupt either catalytic activity (missense in active site) or protein stability (nonsense/frameshift causing haploinsufficiency); therapeutic response to riboflavin reflects FAD cofactor supplementation stabilizing mutant proteins; in ovarian cancer, ACAD9 upregulation enhances fatty acid oxidation supporting tumor growth in lipid-rich microenvironment while maintaining redox balance through Complex I function; cytosolic ACAD9 pool inhibits TRAF6 ubiquitination preventing NF-κB activation and osteoclast differentiation.
