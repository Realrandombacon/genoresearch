## Description

This gene remains profoundly uncharacterized: no UniProt accession exists, preventing InterPro domain analysis and AlphaFold structure prediction. STRING database found no protein interaction partners, and the Human Protein Atlas lacks expression data, suggesting either extremely low abundance, tissue-restricted expression, or absence of validated antibodies. PubMed search returned zero publications, confirming its status as a true dark gene. However, ClinVar reports 4 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variants encompassing the FAM242B locus, all large-scale duplications (CNV gains) on chromosome 20 ranging from 20p13-q13.33 to 20p12.2-q12. These CNVs are associated with developmental phenotypes, implying that FAM242B dosage may be clinically relevant. The gene belongs to the FAM242 family, with a paralog FAM242A also on chromosome 20, suggesting potential functional redundancy or co-evolution. Given the complete absence of molecular characterization data combined with clinical CNV associations, FAM242B likely encodes a non-essential but dosage-sensitive regulatory factor, possibly functioning in chromatin organization or transcriptional regulation at the 20q11 locus. The lack of conservation signals (no orthologs in standard databases) suggests this may be a primate-specific or human-specific gene with emerging function.
