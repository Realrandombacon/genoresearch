# CMTM2: Testis-Specific MARVEL Domain Membrane Protein Regulating Chemokine Signaling and Spermatogenesis

## Description
CMTM2 (CKLF-like MARVEL transmembrane domain containing 2, alias CKLFSF2) encodes a 248-amino acid type III transmembrane protein belonging to the CMTM chemokine-like factor superfamily (IPR050578/PTHR22776, residues 63-198) that bridges chemokine and tetraspanin signaling. The protein contains a canonical MARVEL domain (IPR008253/PS51225, residues 82-204) characteristic of membrane-association and vesicular trafficking proteins, with four predicted transmembrane helices. AlphaFold predicts medium confidence structure (pLDDT=66.3), indicating a partially folded transmembrane protein with structured membrane-spanning regions and flexible termini. HPA shows remarkable testis-enriched expression (616.8 nTPM, >100-fold higher than other tissues), with cancer-enhanced and immune cell-enriched patterns, consistent with roles in spermatogenesis and immune modulation. Gene ontology annotations include chemotaxis and cytokine function, positioning CMTM2 within chemokine signaling networks. STRING interactions reveal strong homophilic and heterophilic partnerships within the CMTM family: CMTM3 (0.968), CMTM4 (0.963), CMTM7 (0.946), CMTM5 (0.848), CMTM8 (0.740), CMTM6 (0.577), CMTM1 (0.536), and the founding member CKLF (0.440). Notably, interactions with CXCR1 (0.668, chemokine receptor) and PROK2 (0.648, prokineticin-2, neuropeptide/angiogenesis factor) suggest CMTM2 modulates GPCR signaling and reproductive neuropeptide pathways. ClinVar documents 26 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including 16q12.2-22.1 copy number gains/losses, indicating dosage sensitivity that may contribute to male infertility or developmental disorders. Conservation is evident across mammals. Mechanistically, CMTM2 likely functions as a tetraspanin-like scaffold that organizes chemokine receptor signaling microdomains in spermatogenic cells, potentially regulating germ cell migration, blood-testis barrier integrity, or sperm maturation. The MARVEL domain mediates membrane curvature sensing and vesicle fusion, while the chemokine-like extracellular loops may directly bind chemokine receptors or ligands. Testis-specific overexpression suggests CMTM2 may be a therapeutic target for male infertility or testicular cancer.
