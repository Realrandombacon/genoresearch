## Description
TINAGL1 (Tubulointerstitial Nephritis Antigen-Like 1) is a 467-amino acid secreted/intracellular protein (UniProt Q9GZM7) that represents a genuine dark gene with only 74 publications despite emerging roles in cancer drug resistance, fibrosis, and reproductive health. Structurally, TINAGL1 possesses a unique hybrid architecture combining an N-terminal Somatomedin B domain (IPR001212/PS50958, residues 50-98) typically found in vitronectin and involved in cell adhesion, fused to a C-terminal papain-like cysteine protease domain (IPR000668/IPR013128/PF00112, residues 203-456) containing the catalytic histidine active site (IPR025660/PS00639, residues 398-408). This places it in the Peptidase C1A family (IPR038765/PTHR12411). AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (pLDDT 86.2), supporting a stable two-domain fold where the SMB domain may regulate protease activity or mediate specific protein-protein interactions. Functionally, TINAGL1 is tissue-enhanced with highest expression in blood vessels (305.7 nTPM, HPA), detected in many tissues, and immune cell enhanced. STRING interactions reveal connections to HSPG2 (0.819, heparan sulfate proteoglycan/perlecan), SEC23A (0.594, COPII vesicle coat), GADD45GIP1 (0.499), EGFR (0.485, Epidermal Growth Factor Receptor), TMEM184A (0.479), LOXL2 (0.479, lysyl oxidase-like 2 involved in fibrosis), PRMT5 (0.465), FIGLA (0.463), CD59 (0.451), and KAT5 (0.449), linking it to extracellular matrix remodeling, vesicular transport, and growth factor signaling. Critically, ClinVar documents 9 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants (large copy number gains/losses spanning 1p36-35) yet ZERO formally associated Mendelian diseases. Recent literature highlights multifaceted roles: TINAGL1 restores tamoxifen sensitivity in breast cancer by blocking EGFR and β1-integrin/FAK signaling to inhibit EMT (2 citations, 2025); mesenteric adipose-derived exosomal TINAGL1 enhances intestinal fibrosis in Crohn's Disease via SMAD4 (13 citations, 2024); YTHDF1 facilitates esophageal cancer progression via m6A-dependent TINAGL1 translation (10 citations, 2024); and TINAGL1 levels in follicular fluid correlate with diminished ovarian reserve (2024). The mechanistic hypothesis: TINAGL1 functions as a dual-domain modulator where the Somatomedin B domain mediates binding to extracellular matrix components (e.g., perlecan/HSPG2) or integrins, while the cysteine protease domain potentially cleaves specific substrates to regulate signaling cascades. In cancer, TINAGL1 acts as a tumor suppressor by inhibiting EGFR/FAK-driven EMT and metastasis, sensitizing cells to chemotherapy; conversely, in fibrotic contexts (Crohn's), exosomal TINAGL1 promotes fibroblast activation via SMAD4/TGF-β pathways. The blood vessel enrichment suggests roles in vascular integrity or angiogenesis. The absence of formal disease nomenclature despite pathogenic variants implies haploinsufficiency tolerance, compensation by related proteases, or context-dependent phenotypes requiring specific environmental triggers (inflammation, hormonal changes). Future work should identify native protease substrates and clarify whether the SMB domain inhibits or targets the catalytic site.
