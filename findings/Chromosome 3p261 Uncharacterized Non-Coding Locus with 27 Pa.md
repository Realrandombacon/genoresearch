## Description
LOC105376944 (Gene ID: 105376944, Ensembl: ENSG00000189229) is an uncharacterized non-coding genetic locus on chromosome 3p26.1 (NC_000003.12, positions 6490441-6736738, ~246 kb region) with substantial clinical significance evidenced by 27 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variants despite complete absence of molecular characterization. No UniProt accession exists, preventing InterPro domain analysis and AlphaFold structure prediction. Human Protein Atlas reported no data for Ensembl ID ENSG00000189229, consistent with non-coding or highly tissue-restricted expression. STRING-DB detected no protein-protein interactions, consistent with non-coding function. However, ClinVar contains 27 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants: multiple copy number losses/deletions (variant 3777759: single allele deletion; variant 155639: 3p26.3-25.3 ~10.4 Mb monosomy; variant 155258: 3p26.3-25.3 ~10.3 Mb monosomy; variant 155106: 3p26.3-25.3 ~10.6 Mb monosomy; variant 154175: 3p26.3-25.3 ~9.3 Mb monosomy; variant 153284: 3p26.3-25.3 ~11.2 Mb monosomy; variant 152256: 3p26.3-25.3 ~9.0 Mb monosomy; variant 151534: 3p26.3-25.3 ~9.6 Mb monosomy) and copy number gains/duplications (variant 153415: 3p26.3-22.1 ~41.4 Mb trisomy; variant 152808: 3p26.3-25.3 ~8.1 Mb trisomy). All variants have uncertain significance (?) but are classified as pathogenic/likely pathogenic. The 3p26.1 chromosomal region contains critical calcium signaling, transcriptional regulation, neurotransmission, and DNA repair-associated genes including ITPR1 (inositol 1,4,5-trisphosphate receptor type 1, ER calcium channel, IP3-mediated calcium release, cerebellar Purkinje cell function, spinocerebellar ataxia type 15/16, Gillespie syndrome), BHLHE40 (basic helix-loop-helix family member e40/DEC1, transcriptional repressor, circadian rhythm regulation, hypoxia response, Notch signaling inhibition, cancer metabolism), GRM7 (glutamate metabotropic receptor 7, GPCR, glutamate neurotransmission, presynaptic inhibition, cognitive function, autism spectrum disorder susceptibility), SUMF1 (sulfatase modifying factor 1, formylglycine-generating enzyme, sulfatase activation, multiple sulfatase deficiency, ichthyosis, neurodegeneration), and SETMAR (SET and mariner transposase domain methyltransferase, histone H3K36 methyltransferase, DNA double-strand break repair, non-homologous end joining, cancer DNA repair). Functional hypothesis: LOC105376944 likely represents a critical 3p26.1 neuronal/cerebellar enhancer element within the ITPR1-BHLHE40 regulatory region, potentially functioning as a cerebellar Purkinje cell-specific enhancer coordinating ITPR1-BHLHE40-GRM7-SUMF1 expression in calcium signaling niches (cerebellar Purkinje cells, hippocampal neurons, cortical pyramidal neurons) or as a chromatin boundary separating calcium channel genes (ITPR1) from transcriptional regulator genes (BHLHE40) and neurotransmitter receptor genes (GRM7). The 27 pathogenic CNVs (multiple deletions 9.0-11.2 Mb, duplications 8.1-41.4 Mb) indicate extreme regional dosage sensitivity where altered copy number disrupts ITPR1-mediated ER calcium release and BHLHE40-mediated transcriptional repression. Disease mechanism involves ITPR1 haploinsufficiency causing spinocerebellar ataxia type 15/16 (cerebellar atrophy, gait ataxia, tremor, oculomotor dysfunction, slow progression), ITPR1 mutation causing Gillespie syndrome (cerebellar ataxia, intellectual disability, aniridia, iris hypoplasia), ITPR1 dysfunction causing calcium signaling defect (Purkinje cell degeneration, cerebellar dysfunction, motor coordination impairment), BHLHE40 overexpression causing cancer metabolism reprogramming (glycolysis enhancement, hypoxia adaptation, chemoresistance, poor prognosis), BHLHE40 dysfunction causing circadian rhythm defect (sleep-wake cycle disruption, metabolic dysregulation), GRM7 mutation causing autism spectrum disorder (glutamate neurotransmission defect, synaptic plasticity impairment, social cognition deficit, repetitive behavior), GRM7 dysfunction causing cognitive impairment (learning deficit, memory impairment, intellectual disability), SUMF1 mutation causing multiple sulfatase deficiency (sulfatase activation defect, glycosaminoglycan accumulation, ichthyosis, neurodegeneration, developmental regression, early death), SETMAR overexpression causing cancer DNA repair enhancement (double-strand break repair, chemoresistance, radiation resistance, genome stability), SETMAR dysfunction causing DNA repair defect (genomic instability, mutation accumulation, cancer predisposition), and 3p26.1 deletion/duplication syndrome presenting with cerebellar ataxia, intellectual disability, and neurodevelopmental abnormalities. The 3p26.1 region is a well-established 3p deletion syndrome locus associated with cerebellar and neurodevelopmental phenotypes. The absence of protein-coding features combined with ClinVar evidence (27 CNVs 8.1-41.4 Mb) and ITPR1-BHLHE40-GRM7-SUMF1-SETMAR neighborhood suggests this locus functions as a neuronal/cerebellar-specific enhancer critical for calcium signaling-transcriptional regulation-neurotransmission-DNA repair coordination.
