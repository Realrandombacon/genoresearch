## Description
C11orf96 (alias AG2, UniProt Q7Z7L8) is a 122-amino acid protein encoded on chromosome 11p11.2. InterPro analysis identifies a single conserved domain, DUF4695 (IPR031521/PF15766), spanning residues 14-118, defining it as the founding member of the PTHR40250 family. AlphaFold predicts a confident, well-folded structure (global pLDDT 70.4), suggesting a stable globular fold despite its small size. HPA data reveals ubiquitous expression with significant enrichment in blood vessels (1697.4 nTPM), and dual localization to the nucleoplasm and focal adhesion sites, hinting at a role linking nuclear signaling to cytoskeletal dynamics. STRING interaction analysis identifies three medium-confidence partners: SEC14L6 (0.512, lipid transport), L1TD1 (0.471, RNA binding/stemness), and PTP4A3 (0.435, oncogenic phosphatase). This network suggests potential involvement in cell migration or proliferation pathways. ClinVar records 15 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, exclusively large copy number losses and gains spanning 11p, indicating dosage sensitivity, although specific monogenic phenotypes remain unannotated. Literature is sparse (8 papers, DARK status), with recent work focusing on its expression in gingival fibroblasts and potential as a cancer biomarker. We hypothesize C11orf96 functions as a structural or regulatory module at the interface of the nucleus and focal adhesions, potentially modulating cell motility or mechanotransduction in vascular endothelial cells. The high expression in blood vessels combined with focal adhesion localization suggests a specific role in vascular integrity or angiogenesis. Interaction with SEC14L6 implies a possible link to lipid metabolism or membrane trafficking, while association with L1TD1 and PTP4A3 points to roles in stemness or oncogenic signaling. The DUF4695 domain likely provides a specific protein-protein interaction surface essential for these functions.
