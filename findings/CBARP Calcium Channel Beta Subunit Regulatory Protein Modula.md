# CBARP: Calcium Channel Beta Subunit Regulatory Protein Modulating Neuronal Excitability

**Quality Score:** 2.77/10  (E=4.55, D=1.0) [WEAK]

## Description
CBARP (C19orf26, BARP) encodes a 705 amino acid voltage-dependent calcium channel beta subunit-associated regulatory protein. The protein contains two conserved domains: a CBARP regulatory domain (PF27937, residues 42-477) and a C-terminal regulatory region (residues 528-629) with GO annotations for transmembrane transporter binding and negative regulation of voltage-gated calcium channel activity. STRING analysis reveals interactions with calcium channel beta subunits CACNB1 (0.686) and CACNB3 (0.605), plus SHOC2 (0.610), FAM174C (0.532), and other partners. This interaction profile places CBARP as a negative regulator of calcium channel trafficking or surface expression. HPA shows group-enriched expression in pituitary gland (19.8 nTPM) and brain (16.7 nTPM), with nucleoplasmic localization and cancer-enhanced expression pattern. ClinVar documents 30 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number gains and losses on chromosome 19p13.3, though no specific disease phenotype is yet assigned. AlphaFold predicts a partially structured protein (pLDDT 51.3) with mixed ordered and intrinsically disordered regions, consistent with regulatory scaffold function. Mouse ortholog Q66L44 (698 aa) demonstrates strong evolutionary conservation. The mechanistic hypothesis: CBARP binds calcium channel beta subunits (CACNB1/3) to retain channels in intracellular compartments or promote degradation, thereby reducing surface calcium current density. This regulatory function is critical in neuronal and endocrine tissues where calcium signaling controls neurotransmitter release and hormone secretion. Dysregulation may contribute to neurological disorders or endocrine dysfunction.
