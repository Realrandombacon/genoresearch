## Description
NOL9 (Nucleolar protein 9, also known as Grc3, NET6) is a 702 amino acid large nucleolar protein (UniProt Q5SY16) with high-confidence well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 77.9), characteristic of the polynucleotide 5'-hydroxyl-kinase family. The protein defines the Polynucleotide 5'-hydroxyl-kinase family (IPR045116/PTHR12755, PF16575, residues 114-673), which catalyzes the hydroxylation of the 5' end of RNA substrates. NOL9 functions as a subunit of the CLP1/CLP2/CLP3 holoenzyme complex, essential for the maturation of 5.8S rRNA and other small nuclear RNAs. Molecular functions include polynucleotide 5'-hydroxyl-kinase activity and RNA processing, placing NOL9 within the ribosome biogenesis pathway.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals a tight network of nucleolar and nuclear proteins: strongest interactions with WDR18 (0.988, WD repeat domain 18, nucleolar scaffold), CREBZF (0.846, CREB-binding protein zinc finger, transcriptional regulator), WDR36 (0.845, WD repeat domain 36, nucleolar scaffold), and NCBP1 (0.824, Nucleolar protein 1, nucleolar matrix). This profile confirms NOL9 as a central nucleolar organizer interacting with structural scaffolds (WDRs), transcriptional regulators (CREBZF), and stress/apoptosis effectors (PDCD11, HEATR1), suggesting roles in nucleolar integrity, stress response, and cell cycle progression.\n\nHPA expression reveals low tissue specificity, detected in all tissues, consistent with its essential role in ribosome biogenesis. Low cancer specificity suggests NOL9 is not a primary oncogene but may be dysregulated in contexts requiring rapid protein synthesis or stress adaptation. Low immune cell specificity indicates non-hematopoietic roles.\n\nClinVar documents 51 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, exclusively large copy number losses affecting chromosome 1p36 (1p36.32-36.23 deletions, 1p36.33-36.22 deletions). The variant burden indicates extreme dosage sensitivity. While no specific Mendelian disease is annotated, NOL9 haploinsufficiency likely causes developmental delay, intellectual disability, and growth retardation. 1p36 deletion syndrome is characterized by severe intellectual disability, seizures, and distinctive facial features; NOL9 disruption may impair ribosome biogenesis, leading to global protein synthesis defects and neurodevelopmental phenotypes.\n\nConservation is strong across vertebrates: human (Q5SY16, 702 aa), mouse (Q3TZX8, 714 aa), zebrafish (E7F654, 713 aa), indicating evolutionary pressure to maintain the polynucleotide kinase function and nucleolar localization.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: NOL9 functions as a catalytic subunit of the CLP1 holoenzyme that hydroxylates the 5' end of 5.8S rRNA, a critical step in rRNA processing and ribosome assembly. The interaction with WDR18 and WDR36 suggests NOL9 is anchored to the nucleolar fibrillar center, coordinating rRNA synthesis with nucleolar integrity. CREBZF interaction implies NOL9 may be regulated by stress-induced transcription factors, linking ribosome biogenesis to cellular stress responses. PDCD11 and HEATR1 interactions suggest NOL9 may act as a checkpoint in apoptosis or stress-induced cell cycle arrest, potentially preventing ribosome dysfunction from triggering cell death. Loss of NOL9 impairs ribosome maturation, causing global protein synthesis defects, nucleolar stress, and neurodevelopmental disorders. NOL9 represents a potential biomarker for 1p36 deletion syndrome and a target for understanding nucleolar stress in cancer.
