## Description
OR11H4 (Olfactory receptor family 11 subfamily H member 4, UniProt Q8NGC9) is a 324-amino acid integral membrane protein located on chromosome 14q11.2 within the large olfactory receptor gene cluster. InterPro analysis reveals canonical class A G protein-coupled receptor (GPCR) architecture: olfactory receptor domain (IPR000725/PF13853, aa 41-315) embedded within rhodopsin-like 7-transmembrane helix bundle (IPR000276/IPR017452/G3DSA:1.20.1070.10, aa 36-60/114-136/282-308 and 31-323/51-300) forming the characteristic serpentine fold with extracellular N-terminus and intracellular C-terminus. GO annotations confirm olfactory receptor activity (GO:0004984), G protein-coupled receptor activity (GO:0004930), G protein-coupled receptor signaling pathway (GO:0007186), and membrane localization (GO:0016020). AlphaFold structure prediction achieves high confidence (pLDDT 87.6), indicating a well-folded stable 7TM structure optimal for odorant binding and G protein activation. Human Protein Atlas has no data for OR11H4 — consistent with highly restricted expression in olfactory sensory neurons of the nasal epithelium where individual ORs exhibit monoallelic, neuron-specific expression patterns below detection thresholds of bulk RNA-seq platforms. STRING interaction network reveals 10 partners representing the canonical olfactory signaling cascade: RTP2 (0.715, receptor transporter protein 2 — highest confidence interaction confirming chaperone-assisted trafficking to cell surface), GNAL (0.686, Gαolf subunit — direct G protein coupling), GNB1 (0.682, Gβ1 subunit), ARRB2/ARRB1 (0.679 each, arrestins mediating receptor desensitization and internalization), GNG7 (0.678, Gγ subunit), PRKACA (0.650, cAMP-dependent protein kinase catalytic subunit), GRK2/GRK3 (0.569/0.547, G protein-coupled receptor kinases), and GNG13 (0.510, Gγ13 subunit). This interactome confirms OR11H4 functions within the canonical cAMP-mediated olfactory transduction pathway. ClinVar contains 20 pathogenic variants consisting predominantly of large copy-number gains and losses across 14q11.2-32.33, demonstrating dosage sensitivity where genomic rearrangements disrupting chromosome 14q cause disease phenotypes potentially affecting olfactory function and neurodevelopment. Conservation across mammals (bovine F1MU73, 314 aa) indicates ancient evolutionary origin tied to fundamental chemosensory mechanisms. OR11H4 likely functions as an odorant-activated GPCR, using its 7TM bundle to bind specific volatile ligands in the olfactory epithelium while RTP2 assists proper folding and trafficking to the ciliary membrane, then activating Gαolf to stimulate adenylyl cyclase, increase cAMP, open cyclic nucleotide-gated channels, and depolarize olfactory sensory neurons — initiating action potentials that transmit smell information to the olfactory bulb, with potential extra-nasal functions in non-olfactory tissues where ectopic OR expression regulates processes like sperm chemotaxis, wound healing, and cancer metastasis.
