## Description
ATXN2L (Ataxin-2-Like Protein; UniProt Q8WWM7) is a 1075 amino acid intrinsically disordered RNA-binding protein with 53 publications, representing a moderately characterized stress granule component with emerging roles in mRNA translation regulation, neurodegeneration, and cancer progression through liquid-liquid phase separation mechanisms.\n\nSTRUCTURAL ARCHITECTURE: InterPro analysis reveals modular domain architecture embedded within largely disordered sequence optimized for dynamic protein-RNA interactions. The N-terminal region contains an SM domain (IPR025852/IPR047575/PF14438/PS52002, residues 120-195/122-199) belonging to the Sm/Lsm fold family that mediates RNA binding (GO:0003723). A central LsmAD domain (IPR009604/PF06741/SM01272, residues 264-333/264-332) provides additional RNA-binding capacity through Lsm-associated fold. A C-terminal PAM2 motif (IPR009818/PF07145, residues 654-669) mediates interaction with poly(A)-binding proteins. The full-length protein belongs to the Ataxin2-like family (IPR045117/PTHR12854, residues 102-890). AlphaFold predicts low confidence structure (global pLDDT 48.5), indicating extensive intrinsic disorder—characteristic of RNA-binding proteins that undergo liquid-liquid phase separation (LLPS) to form membraneless organelles like stress granules and P-bodies. This disordered architecture enables conformational flexibility for multivalent interactions with RNA and protein partners.\n\nINTERACTION NETWORK: STRING-DB identifies high-confidence interactions with stress granule machinery, RNA metabolism factors, and calcium signaling components: LSM12 (0.986, LSm12 homolog, stress granule assembly), ATXN2 (0.953, Ataxin-2, closest paralog, SCA2 disease protein), DDX6 (0.952, DEAD-box helicase 6, P-body component, translation repression), ATXN3 (0.917, Ataxin-3, SCA3 disease protein, deubiquitinase), ITPR3 (0.908, Inositol 1,4,5-trisphosphate receptor type 3, ER calcium channel), ATXN3L (0.903, Ataxin-3-like), ITPR1 (0.900, IP3 receptor type 1), ITPR2 (0.900, IP3 receptor type 2), NUFIP2 (0.816, NUFIP family member 2, recent 2025 paper shows primary ATXN2L interactor whose depletion occurs without ATXN2L), and PABPC1 (0.638, Poly(A)-binding protein cytoplasmic 1, translation initiation via PAM2 motif). This interaction profile establishes ATXN2L as a core stress granule component that bridges RNA metabolism (ATXN2-DDX6-PABPC1 axis) with calcium signaling (ITPR1/2/3) and disease pathways (ATXN2/ATXN3 in spinocerebellar ataxias).\n\nEXPRESSION & LOCALIZATION: HPA shows ubiquitous expression detected in all tissues with low tissue and cancer specificity, consistent with fundamental requirements for mRNA metabolism across all cell types. Subcellular localization is cytosolic where stress granules and P-bodies assemble. Protein class includes plasma proteins and predicted intracellular proteins. Classified with low cell type specificity.\n\nDISEASE LANDSCAPE: ClinVar contains 120 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number losses at 16p11.2 (multiple independent submissions: chr16:28824858-30321320, chr16:28802398-29070000, chr16:28371467-29379768), copy number gains at 16p12.1-11.2 and 16p11.2, the splice donor variant c.2172+1G>T that would disrupt proper splicing, plus 3' UTR variant c.*820G>A and intronic variant c.1588+119A>G. No monogenic disease is yet definitively assigned, but 16p11.2 CNVs cause neurodevelopmental disorders including autism spectrum disorder, developmental delay, and obesity. Recent 2025 literature establishes prominent mechanistic roles: (1) ATXN2L primarily interacts with NUFIP2—absence of ATXN2L results in NUFIP2 depletion, while ATXN2-polyQ expansion triggers NUFIP2 accumulation (8 citations, linking to SCA2 pathogenesis); (2) Liquid-liquid phase separation of ATXN2L enhances mRNA translation in hepatocellular carcinoma (2 citations, oncogenic mechanism); (3) Exosomal FOXL1 from bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells activates METTL3/ATXN2L pathway to ameliorate high glucose-induced retinal microvascular endothelial cell injury in diabetic retinopathy (5 citations); (4) Conditional ATXN2L-null in adult frontal cortex CamK2a+ neurons does not cause cell death but restricts spontaneous mobility and affects alternative splicing pathway (1 citation).\n\nCONSERVATION: Orthologs confirmed in mouse (Q7TQH0, 1049 aa) indicating strong evolutionary constraint on the modular LsmAD-SM-PAM2 architecture across mammals despite length variation.\n\nFUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: ATXN2L functions as an intrinsically disordered scaffold protein that nucleates stress granule assembly and regulates mRNA translation through multivalent RNA-protein interactions and liquid-liquid phase separation. The SM and LsmAD domains bind RNA directly, while the PAM2 motif recruits PABPC1 to link with poly(A) tails of mRNAs. In normal conditions, ATXN2L promotes formation of dynamic ribonucleoprotein condensates that sequester untranslated mRNAs during cellular stress, coordinating with DDX6 (helicase), LSM12 (granule assembly), and ATXN2 (paralog). The ITPR1/2/3 interactions suggest ATXN2L couples calcium signaling to translational control—ER calcium release may trigger stress granule disassembly for rapid translation restart. In disease, ATXN2L is hijacked or dysregulated: (1) In hepatocellular carcinoma, ATXN2L undergoes LLPS to enhance oncogenic mRNA translation, driving tumor progression; (2) In diabetic retinopathy, the METTL3/ATXN2L axis (activated by exosomal FOXL1 from BMSCs) protects retinal endothelial cells from high glucose injury, suggesting therapeutic potential; (3) In spinocerebellar ataxia, ATXN2-polyQ expansions alter NUFIP2 homeostasis through ATXN2L interaction, contributing to neurodegeneration; (4) The c.2172+1G>T splice variant would disrupt proper isoform production, potentially causing haploinsufficiency. The 16p11.2 CNVs encompassing ATXN2L contribute to neurodevelopmental phenotypes through dosage effects on stress granule dynamics. Therapeutic targeting of ATXN2L LLPS could modulate oncogenic translation in cancer while preserving neuronal homeostasis.
