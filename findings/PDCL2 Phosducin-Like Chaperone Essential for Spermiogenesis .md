## Description
PDCL2 (Phosducin-Like Protein 2, UniProt Q8N4E4) is a 241 amino acid intracellular chaperone with moderate characterization that functions as a critical regulator of spermiogenesis and male fertility. The protein contains a phosducin thioredoxin-like domain (IPR024253/PF02114, residues 34-202) within the thioredoxin-like superfamily (IPR036249/SSF52833, residues 7-204), placing it in the phosducin-like chaperone and apoptosis regulator family (IPR051498/PTHR45809, residues 1-233) with a glutaredoxin-like fold (G3DSA:3.40.30.10, residues 81-215) and a VIAF-like domain (cd02988, residues 8-200). STRING interactions reveal high-confidence partnerships with PLP2 (0.894, phosducin-like protein 2 paralog), REST (0.640, transcriptional repressor), C11orf42 (0.507), SPATA3 (0.497, spermatogenesis-associated), SRD5A3 (0.490, steroid 5-alpha-reductase), SHCBP1L (0.484), CCT4 (0.482, chaperonin containing TCP1 subunit 4), TMEM165 (0.480), LRRC66 (0.479), and PDCL3 (0.478), placing PDCL2 in chaperone networks and spermatogenesis pathways. Recent 2022 literature demonstrates PDCL2 is essential for spermiogenesis and male fertility in mice, with patients exhibiting teratozoospermia showing low PDCL2 expression, and DNA-encoded library selections identifying novel PDCL2 ligands for potential non-hormonal male contraception. HPA data shows testis-enriched expression (61.8 nTPM, tissue enriched), cancer-enhanced pattern, and subcellular localization to microtubules and centrosome, consistent with roles in sperm flagella formation and cell division. ClinVar contains 20 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large deletions and copy number gains/losses at 4p12-q35.2 and 4q11-31.21, though no specific diseases are directly assigned—suggesting potential male infertility, developmental, or cancer phenotypes. AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 80.8) indicates high confidence with a well-folded thioredoxin-like domain. Functional hypothesis: PDCL2 acts as a co-chaperone that facilitates the folding of tubulin and other cytoskeletal proteins through its thioredoxin-like domain, interacting with the CCT/TRiC chaperonin complex (CCT4) to ensure proper assembly of microtubules in the sperm flagella and centrosome during spermiogenesis, where PDCL2 deficiency leads to defective sperm morphology (teratozoospermia) and male infertility, while its cancer-enhanced expression and centrosomal localization suggest roles in tumor cell division and potential as a target for male contraceptives that reversibly inhibit spermatogenesis without affecting hormonal axes.
