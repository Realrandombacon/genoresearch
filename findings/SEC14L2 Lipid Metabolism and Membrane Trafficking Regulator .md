## Description
SEC14L2 (UniProt O76054), also known as SEC14-like lipid binding protein 2, encodes a 403 aa cytosolic/nuclear protein belonging to the evolutionarily conserved CRAL-TRIO/GOLD superfamily, functioning as a critical regulator of lipid metabolism, membrane trafficking, and cell signaling through its unique multi-domain architecture. InterPro analysis reveals a complex, modular structure: an N-terminal CRAL-TRIO lipid-binding domain (aa 16-275, IPR001251, PF00650) capable of binding phospholipids and phosphatidylinositol phosphates; a central GOLD domain (aa 276-383, IPR009038, G3DSA:2.60.120.680) involved in protein-protein interactions and potentially enzymatic activity; and a C-terminal Sec14p-like lipid-binding domain (aa 77-244, IPR001251, cd00170) essential for lipid transfer. AlphaFold predicts an exceptionally high-confidence structure (global pLDDT 95.6), indicating a rigid, well-folded protein with precise geometry for lipid coordination and complex assembly, consistent with stable membrane-associated factors. STRING interactions define SEC14L2 as a node connecting lipid metabolism, cell adhesion, and oncogenesis: PI4KA (0.528, phosphatidylinositol 4-kinase), CD81 (0.506, tetraspanin), CLDN1 (0.500, tight junction protein), SEC14L3 (0.492, homolog), PPIA (0.473, ribosomal protein), SCARB1 (0.471, scavenger receptor), DVL2 (0.439, Wnt signaling), and APOE (0.414, apolipoprotein), suggesting roles in coordinating lipid transport with cell adhesion and Wnt signaling. HPA expression shows liver enrichment (227.8 nTPM) and immune cell enhancement, classified as tissue-enhanced and detected in many tissues, consistent with roles in metabolic homeostasis and immune response; also classified as an activator in transcription and transport processes. ClinVar reports 12 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains and losses on chromosome 22q11.1-13.33 (spanning the SEC14L2 locus), indicating genomic instability at this region potentially linked to 22q11.2 deletion syndrome (DiGeorge/Velocardiofacial syndrome) phenotypes including cardiac and craniofacial defects, though no specific monogenic disease has been definitively assigned to SEC14L2 alone. Literature (74 papers) highlights: (1) Oral Cancer Progression—SEC14L2 expression correlates with poor prognosis in oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC) and drives neoplastic progression via the CD24-SEC14L2 axis; (2) Cholesterol Transport—SEC14L2 regulates cholesterol transport in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) through interaction with SCARB1, suggesting a mechanism for lipid accumulation in cancer cells; (3) Evolutionary Conservation—Sequence conservation observed in Chinese tree shrews (Tupaia belangeri chinensis), implying ancient functional importance. Mechanistic Hypothesis: SEC14L2 acts as a scaffold that recruits PI4KA to generate PI4P lipids, which are then transferred to SCARB1 or CD81 via the CRAL-TRIO/GOLD domains to facilitate cholesterol and phospholipid trafficking. In OSCC, this axis promotes membrane fluidity and survival signaling, while in 22q11.2 syndrome, copy number variations disrupt the stoichiometry of the CRAL-TRIO/GOLD complex, leading to aberrant lipid metabolism and developmental defects in craniofacial structures and cardiac outflow tracts. This positions SEC14L2 as a 'dark gene' candidate where lipid trafficking interfaces with developmental signaling and oncogenesis.
