## Description

Located in the AZFb (azoospermia factor b) interval of Yq11.223 in the nonrecombining portion of the Y chromosome, RBMY1J is one of multiple closely related paralogs in a gene cluster with six highly similar functional copies identified. The encoded protein contains an RNA-binding motif in the N-terminus and four SRGY (serine, arginine, glycine, tyrosine) boxes in the C-terminus, and likely functions as a splicing factor during spermatogenesis. The three literature reports (2024, 2019, 2017) describe cytogenetics in infertile males, Y-chromosome gene transcript alterations in cryptorchidism responding to hormone treatment, and copy number variations in testicular maturation arrest. Based on its 496 aa length, RRM domain architecture (residues 8-85), low AlphaFold confidence (pLDDT 49.8), testis-specific expression (1.4 nTPM), nucleoplasmic/nucleolar localization, high-confidence STRING interactions with HNRNPK/RBM3/TSPY proteins, and AZFb location, RBMY1J functions as an RNA-binding protein facilitating pre-mRNA splicing during spermatogenesis. The structured RRM domain binds specific RNA sequences or structures in germ cell transcripts, while the disordered C-terminal region with SRGY boxes enables dynamic protein-protein interactions with splicing factors. The SRGY boxes are characteristic of germ cell-specific RNA-binding proteins and likely mediate assembly of large ribonucleoprotein complexes. RBMY1J likely operates in concert with HNRNPK (major splicing regulator) and RBM3 (cold-shock RNA chaperone) to process pre-mRNAs required for germ cell differentiation. The nucleolar localization suggests involvement in ribosome biogenesis or rRNA processing in addition to mRNA splicing. AZFb deletions removing RBMY1J cause spermatogenic arrest, indicating essential function in meiosis or post-meiotic germ cell development. RBMY1J exemplifies the emerging class of Y-chromosome RNA-binding proteins that coordinate male germ cell gene expression through RNA processing rather than transcriptional regulation, with clinical significance in male infertility diagnosis.
