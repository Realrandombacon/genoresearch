## Description
MRPS10 (Mitochondrial Ribosomal Protein S10, also designated uS10m) is a 201 aa structural component of the mitochondrial ribosome small subunit (28S) essential for mitochondrial protein synthesis and oxidative phosphorylation. InterPro analysis reveals a conserved ribosomal protein S10 domain architecture: a central uS10m domain (IPR027486, aa 77-173) characteristic of mitochondrial small subunit proteins, a Ribosomal protein S10p/S20e domain (IPR00338/PF00338, aa 80-173) shared with bacterial and eukaryotic cytoplasmic ribosomes, and membership in the mitochondrial 28S ribosomal protein S10 family (IPR040055, aa 1-200) with GO annotation for mitochondrial small ribosomal subunit (GO:0005763). The G3DSA:3.30.70.600 superfamily (aa 74-176) indicates an alpha-beta fold typical of ribosomal proteins. AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (pLDDT 78.7) consistent with a well-folded ribosomal protein that integrates into the 28S subunit. STRING interactions place MRPS10 at the core of the mitochondrial ribosome with ultra-high confidence partners (all 0.999): MRPS9, MRPS16, MRPS11, MRPS2, MRPS5, MRPS35, MRPS7, MRPS15 (all small subunit proteins), DAP3 (Death-Associated Protein 3, a mitochondrial ribosomal protein), and MRPL19 (large subunit protein), reflecting tight co-assembly of the mitoribosome. HPA expression shows ubiquitous detection across all tissues with low tissue and cancer specificity, subcellular localization to mitochondria and actin filaments, and classification as an essential protein, consistent with housekeeping roles in cellular energetics. ClinVar contains 9 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including a splice region variant (c.186+43A>G) and multiple copy number gains/losses on chromosome 6p25.3-q27, though no specific MRPS10-associated Mendelian disease is definitively established despite the clinical significance. Literature (18 papers) establishes MRPS10 as: elevated in diabetic retinopathy (2025, bioinformatics); associated with mitochondrial apoptosis in rheumatoid arthritis (2025, 6 citations); linked to intensive care unit-acquired weakness via miR-542 regulation (2017, 68 citations); and required for mitochondrial translation fidelity.
