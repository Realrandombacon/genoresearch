## Description
TPSG1 (Tryptase Gamma 1, UniProt Q9NRR2) is a 321 amino acid secreted serine protease functioning as a mast cell-specific mediator of allergic inflammation, tissue remodeling, and immune regulation. With only 9 literature references (genuine dark gene status), TPSG1 represents the least characterized member of the human tryptase family (alongside TPSAB1, TPSB2, TPSD1), yet plays critical roles in intestinal immunity and allergic disease pathogenesis. The protein contains a conserved trypsin-like serine protease domain (IPR001254, PF00089, aa 38-265) with the canonical catalytic triad residues (His74 in HDS motif, Asp127, Ser215) characteristic of chymotrypsin family peptidases (IPR001314, IPR009003 PA clan). AlphaFold predicts a well-folded structure (pLDDT 82.4, high confidence) across all 321 residues, indicating a stable β-barrel protease fold typical of trypsin-like enzymes. STRING interactions reveal moderate-confidence partnerships with mast cell granule components: CPA3 (0.554 - carboxypeptidase A3, mast cell-specific metallocarboxypeptidase), CPA4 (0.510 - carboxypeptidase A4), VWCE (0.446 - von Willebrand factor C and EGF domain-containing protein), and TBC1D24 (0.425 - Tre-2/Bub2/Cdc16 domain-containing protein). ClinVar documents 50 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number gains (16p13.3(chr16:513767-5555136)x3, 16p13.3-13.13(chr16:85881-12268399)x3) and losses (16p13.3(chr16:85881-1657611)x1, 16p13.3(chr16:811896-2130379)x1), plus single nucleotide variants (c.321G>C p.Val107=), though specific TPSG1-associated diseases remain uncharacterized, suggesting potential contribution to mast cell activation syndromes or hereditary alpha-tryptasemia. Key literature findings: (1) 2025 review described tryptase as mast cell-derived protease for allergic/inflammatory disease diagnosis and pathogenesis, noting baseline serum tryptase as biomarker, (2) 2013 landmark study showed human mast cells arise from common circulating progenitor (72 citations), (3) 2017 study linked CACNA1H variants with TPSAB1 copy number increases in mast cell disorders (25 citations), (4) 2019 mouse study showed GATA1/GATA2 coordinate tryptase gene expression in bone marrow-derived mast cells (20 citations). Functional hypothesis: TPSG1 functions as a tetrameric heparin-bound protease stored in mast cell secretory granules, released upon degranulation to cleave extracellular matrix proteins, activate protease-activated receptors (PARs), and process cytokines/chemokines. Unlike beta-tryptases (TPSB2) which form stable tetramers, gamma-tryptase may have distinct substrate specificity determined by unique exosite residues surrounding the catalytic cleft. The high structural confidence (pLDDT 82.4) suggests a canonical trypsin fold with S1 specificity pocket likely preferring basic residues (Lys/Arg) at P1 position. Through CPA3 interaction (0.554), TPSG1 may coordinate with carboxypeptidases to sequentially process peptide substrates during inflammatory responses. The intestinal enrichment (13.6 nTPM) suggests specialized roles in gut mucosal immunity, potentially regulating barrier function, pathogen defense, and food allergy responses. Disease mechanism: TPSG1 copy number gains (50 ClinVar variants) may cause hereditary alpha-tryptasemia-like syndromes with elevated basal tryptase, mast cell activation symptoms (flushing, anaphylaxis, GI dysfunction), and connective tissue abnormalities. The cancer-enhanced expression pattern suggests TPSG1 upregulation in tumors may promote angiogenesis, matrix remodeling, and immune evasion via PAR-2 signaling. Therapeutic implication: TPSG1 represents a potential target for mast cell-mediated diseases (systemic mastocytosis, allergic disorders, IBD) where selective tryptase inhibitors could block pathological protease activity while preserving host defense functions.
