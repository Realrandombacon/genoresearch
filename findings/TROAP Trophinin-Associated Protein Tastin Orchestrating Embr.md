## Description
TROAP (Trophinin-Associated Protein, also known as Tastin) is a 778 amino acid intracellular scaffolding protein that functions as a critical regulator of embryonic implantation adhesion complex assembly, mitotic spindle dynamics, and cell division through interaction with the trophinin-bystin adhesion system and kinesin-centromere machinery for chromosome segregation and cytokinesis in proliferating tissues and early embryonic development. InterPro analysis identifies characteristic tastin architecture including Tastin family (IPR026133, PTHR15289, residues 1-551/1-613) defining evolutionary relationship to implantation-associated proteins with cell adhesion function (GO:0007155) and intracellular localization (GO:0005622), and Tastin Pfam domain (PF31001, residues 1-613) forming the structured core for trophinin complex engagement and mitotic regulator binding. AlphaFold structure (AF-Q12815-F1) predicts low confidence (pLDDT 46.7) across the 778 residue modeled sequence, indicating a predominantly intrinsically disordered protein (IDP) with flexible regions suitable for dynamic protein-protein interactions, scaffold assembly, and conformational adaptability during mitosis and embryonic implantation. HPA data classifies TROAP as an essential protein and predicted intracellular protein with tissue-enhanced expression, detected in many tissues with top expression in bone marrow (48.3 nTPM), lymphoid tissue (34.3 nTPM), and testis (27.2 nTPM), low cancer specificity, immune cell-enhanced distribution, and cell type-enhanced pattern consistent with regulated cell division and adhesion function; biological process annotated as cell adhesion reflecting dual roles in implantation and mitosis. STRING interactions reveal tight coupling to adhesion and mitotic machinery: TRO (Trophinin, 0.999) as primary binding partner for embryonic implantation adhesion complex; BYSL (Bystin-Like, 0.998) for trophinin complex stabilization; HJURP (Holliday Junction Recognition Protein, 0.918) for centromere assembly and CENP-A loading; KIF18B (0.906), KIF4A (0.836), KIF2C (0.833), KIF20A (0.831) as kinesin motor proteins for mitotic spindle dynamics; CDCA3 (Cell Division Cycle Associated 3, 0.857) for G2/M transition control; ESPL1 (Separase, 0.846) for sister chromatid separation; and AURKB (Aurora Kinase B, 0.836) for spindle assembly checkpoint and cytokinesis. ClinVar lists 8 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number gains on 12p13.33-q24.33 (chr12:173787-133777902x3, chr12:1-133851895x3, chr12:121271-133196807x3), 12p13.33-q13.12 (chr12:82453-49847230x3), 12q11-13.12 (chr12:37857750-49791459), 12p11.21-q13.12 (chr12:31886971-50360461x3), and 3' UTR variant (c.*24C>A), though no specific monogenic syndrome is defined, suggesting roles in developmental disorders at the 12p locus. Literature (72 papers) establishes TROAP as an emerging cancer and cell cycle regulator: FBXW5 promotes epithelial-mesenchymal transition in lung adenocarcinoma through KLF13/TROAP signaling pathway revealing EMT mechanism; TROAP aggravates chemoresistance of colorectal cancer cells via lipogenesis by PI3K/Akt pathway and histone acetylation establishing metabolic-epigenetic axis; TROAP promotes esophageal squamous cell carcinoma progression via PI3K/AKT pathway confirming oncogenic signaling; CDCA8 and TROAP as prognostic biomarkers of postoperative metastatic progression in clear cell renal cell carcinoma linking to metastasis; E2F1-induced upregulation of TROAP contributes to endometrial cancer progression defining transcriptional regulation. As an emerging scaffolding protein with cancer and cell cycle associations, TROAP represents a critical node connecting embryonic implantation to mitotic regulation through TRO-BYSL-HJURP-kinesin network integration for adhesion complex assembly and spindle dynamics, intrinsically disordered structure-mediated protein-protein interactions for scaffold flexibility, 12p copy number variation disorder pathogenesis via dosage sensitivity, and cancer progression/chemoresistance/metastasis disruption in lung adenocarcinoma EMT, colorectal cancer lipogenesis-PI3K/Akt-histone acetylation axis, esophageal squamous cell carcinoma, renal cell carcinoma metastasis, and endometrial cancer E2F1-driven proliferation.
