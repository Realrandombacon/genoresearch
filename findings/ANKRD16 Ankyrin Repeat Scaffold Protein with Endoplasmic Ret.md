# ANKRD16: Ankyrin Repeat Scaffold Protein with Endoplasmic Reticulum Localization, Cell Cycle and DNA Repair Interactions, and 10p15.3 Deletion Syndrome Association

## Description
ANKRD16 encodes a 361 amino acid ankyrin repeat domain-containing scaffold protein (UniProt Q6P6B7) with a highly structured tandem repeat architecture. InterPro analysis reveals extensive ankyrin repeat domains (IPR002110, IPR036770, PF12796, PF13606, PF13637, SM00248, PS50088, residues 4-344) comprising three canonical ankyrin repeat modules that mediate protein-protein interactions through a curved solenoid fold—this domain architecture places ANKRD16 in the PTHR24173 family (Cul2-RING E3 ligase substrate recognition). AlphaFold structure prediction shows very high confidence (pLDDT 93.9), indicating a well-folded stable tertiary structure typical of ankyrin repeat scaffolds. HPA data indicates ubiquitous expression across all tissues with low specificity and endoplasmic reticulum subcellular localization—suggesting roles in ER-associated protein quality control, unfolded protein response, or membrane protein scaffolding. STRING interactions identify 10 protein partners including FBH1 (0.639, F-box DNA helicase 1 involved in DNA replication fork regulation), NFKBIA (0.501, NF-kappa-B inhibitor alpha), CDKN2C (0.485, cyclin-dependent kinase inhibitor 2C/p18-INK4c cell cycle regulator), RFXANK (0.497, MHC class II transcription regulator), and RPP14 (0.516, RNase P subunit)—this interaction pattern suggests ANKRD16 functions as a molecular scaffold coordinating DNA damage response, cell cycle progression, and transcriptional regulation through ankyrin-mediated protein complex assembly. ClinVar documents 27 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including single nucleotide variants (c.535+144G>T, c.929-3C>A) and large copy number gains/losses spanning 10p15.3-13 (chr10:100026-15273144) and 10p15.3-q11.23 regions, indicating strong dosage sensitivity and disease association through chromosomal rearrangement mechanisms affecting the 10p terminal region. The combination of ankyrin repeat scaffolding, ER localization, cell cycle/DNA repair interaction partners, and pathogenic CNVs suggests ANKRD16 functions as an ER-localized molecular adaptor that recruits cell cycle regulators and DNA repair factors to specific protein complexes, with haploinsufficiency via 10p deletions disrupting this regulatory circuit contributing to 10p15.3 deletion syndrome phenotypes including developmental delay and growth retardation.
