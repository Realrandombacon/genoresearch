## Description

While no specific monogenic disease is yet annotated, the variant burden and recent cancer literature suggest contributions to glioma progression and potentially neuromuscular disorders given myogenesis annotations.\n\nFUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: VAMP5 functions as a specialized v-SNARE coordinating three distinct membrane fusion pathways: (1) Exosome release—recent literature (2023, 17 citations) demonstrates VAMP5 forms distinct SNARE complexes with specific Q-SNARE sets to mediate exosome secretion from endomembrane compartments, separate from plasma membrane-derived small EVs. This positions VAMP5 as a regulator of intercellular communication via exosomal cargo delivery. (2) Phagosome maturation—recent literature (2024, 3 citations) shows VAMP5 promotes Fcγ receptor-mediated phagocytosis in macrophages by regulating phagosome maturation through sequential membrane fusion events with SNAP23. This suggests immune surveillance functions. Mechanistically, the N-terminal transmembrane anchor (residues 1-5) embeds VAMP5 in vesicle membranes while the central SNARE motif (residues 3-70) forms parallel four-helix bundles with cognate t-SNAREs (STX4/SNAP23 for plasma membrane fusion; STX16/SNAP29 for endosomal fusion). The high-confidence AlphaFold structure suggests stable helical packing enabling rapid SNARE complex assembly. Loss-of-function would impair exosome secretion, phagocyte function, and potentially muscle differentiation; gain-of-function could drive oncogenic membrane trafficking in gliomas.
