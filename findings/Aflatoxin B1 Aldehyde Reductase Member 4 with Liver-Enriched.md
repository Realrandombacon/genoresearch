## Description
AKR7L encodes a 331 amino acid aldo-keto reductase (UniProt Q8NHP1) that functions as an NADPH-dependent oxidoreductase involved in xenobiotic detoxification. The protein contains a canonical aldo/keto reductase domain (PF00248, positions 13-318) and belongs to the AKR7A family (cd19075). AlphaFold predicts a highly confident 3D structure (pLDDT 95.9) consistent with the NADP-dependent oxidoreductase fold (G3DSA:3.20.20.100, IPR023210).\n\nHPA expression data shows AKR7L is enriched in metabolically active tissues: intestine (58.4 nTPM), liver (34.7 nTPM), and stomach (23.5 nTPM), consistent with a detoxification role at environmental exposure sites. The gene is classified as 'group enriched' with intracellular localization in Golgi apparatus and cytosol.\n\nClinVar reports 16 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly copy number variations (deletions, duplications) across chromosome 1p36, though no specific disease associations are yet established. This suggests haploinsufficiency may cause phenotypic effects.\n\nOnly 3 publications exist for AKR7L (gene_literature confirms VERY DARK status), making this a genuine dark gene despite its clear enzymatic function. The protein likely protects against aflatoxin B1 carcinogenicity by reducing the reactive dialdehyde form to non-binding dialcohol, analogous to characterized AKR7A family members.
