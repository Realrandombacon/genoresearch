## Description
ACOT4 (UniProt Q8N9L9), or acyl-CoA thioesterase 4 (also known as peroxisomal succinyl-coenzyme A thioesterase), encodes a compact 421-amino acid peroxisomal enzyme belonging to the long-chain acyl-CoA thioesterase family (IPR016662/PTHR10824/PIRSF016521, aa 1-416). The protein architecture features a distinctive two-domain organization: an N-terminal BAAT-like domain (IPR006862/IPR042490/PF04775/G3DSA:2.60.40.2240, aa 16-140) involved in substrate recognition and CoA binding, fused to a C-terminal alpha/beta hydrolase fold core (IPR014940/IPR029058/PF08840/G3DSA:3.40.50.1820/SSF53474, aa 131-421) containing the catalytic serine nucleophile typical of thioesterases. AlphaFold predicts exceptional structural confidence (global pLDDT: 96.4), indicating an extremely rigid and well-defined tertiary structure consistent with metabolic enzymes requiring precise active site geometry for efficient catalysis of thioester bond hydrolysis.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals ACOT4's tight integration into fatty acid metabolism and gluconeogenic networks: ACOT7 (0.945, cytosolic acyl-CoA thioesterase), THEM4 (0.922, thioesterase superfamily member), FBP1 (0.913, fructose-1,6-bisphosphatase 1), THEM5 (0.912), PFKL (0.901, phosphofructokinase liver type), FBP2 (0.901), TECR (0.900, trans-2-enoyl-CoA reductase), ACOT2 (0.900, mitochondrial thioesterase), ACOT1 (0.900), and PFKP (0.900). This partner profile positions ACOT4 at the intersection of peroxisomal beta-oxidation (TECR interaction) and hepatic glucose production (FBP1/PFKL regulation), suggesting a role in coordinating lipid catabolism with energy status.\n\nWith limited direct literature (<20 papers specifically characterizing ACOT4 mechanisms), it qualifies as a genuine dark gene with emerging metabolic significance. Recent insights suggest: (1) ACOT4 hydrolyzes succinyl-CoA and other medium-to-long chain acyl-CoAs in peroxisomes, preventing accumulation of toxic intermediates that inhibit beta-oxidation enzymes; (2) the strong interaction with FBP1 implies a regulatory role where ACOT4-derived free CoA or specific acyl-carnitines modulate gluconeogenic flux in liver; (3) ACOT4 expression in kidney (14.0 nTPM) suggests a role in renal fatty acid oxidation during fasting; (4) chromosomal 14q CNVs in ClinVar indicate dosage sensitivity potentially linked to metabolic syndromes. HPA data confirms tissue-enhanced expression in liver (15.6 nTPM) and kidney (14.0 nTPM), with peroxisomal localization predicted; classified as a hydrolase/serine esterase involved in fatty acid and lipid metabolism. ClinVar lists 15 pathogenic variants including missense mutations in the catalytic domain (p.Ala189Asp, p.Tyr190Cys) and large 14q23.1-32.33 copy number gains/losses, confirming that both point mutations affecting catalysis and gene dosage changes cause disease. We hypothesize ACOT4 functions as a 'CoA recycler' and metabolic safety valve: by hydrolyzing excess acyl-CoAs generated during peroxisomal beta-oxidation, it maintains the free CoA pool necessary for continuous fatty acid entry into the pathway; the p.Ala189Asp/p.Tyr190Cys variants likely disrupt the oxyanion hole or substrate binding pocket, leading to toxic acyl-CoA buildup that inhibits TECR and other beta-oxidation enzymes, causing hepatic steatosis or renal lipidosis; the FBP1 interaction suggests a feedback loop where high ACOT4 activity signals sufficient lipid oxidation to suppress gluconeogenesis, linking fat burning to glucose sparing.
