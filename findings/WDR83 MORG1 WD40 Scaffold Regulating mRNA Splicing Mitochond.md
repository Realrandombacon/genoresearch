## Description
WDR83 (UniProt Q9BRX9), also known as MORG1 (Mitogen-activated protein kinase Organizer 1), is a 315 amino acid WD40 repeat protein functioning as a critical scaffold for mRNA processing and cellular signaling. InterPro analysis confirms a canonical WD40 architecture (IPR001680, IPR015943, PF00400) with conserved protein-binding sites (GO:0005515), specifically highlighting the MORG1 family domain (IPR051980) essential for its role in organizing the spliceosome and mitochondrial translation machinery. AlphaFold structure (AF-Q9BRX9-F1) predicts a highly stable, well-folded 3D structure with very high confidence (global pLDDT 93.3), consistent with a multi-subunit scaffold protein. HPA expression reveals low tissue specificity but cell-type enhanced expression, with a biological process profile centered on mRNA splicing and mRNA processing in the nucleoplasm and vesicles. STRING interactions identify 10 high-confidence partners (0.796–0.994) exclusively from the spliceosome and mitochondrial translation complex, including PRPF8 (0.994, spliceosome core), LAMTOR3 (0.956, mTORC1 regulator), EGLN3 (0.948, HIF-1α prolyl hydroxylase), SF3B4 (0.845, spliceosome), and MAP2K1/MAPK3 (0.813/0.781, MAPK signaling), forming a coherent network linking RNA processing to mitochondrial function and stress response. ClinVar contains 31 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly single nucleotide variants and copy number variations on chromosome 19p13.2 (loss/gain), confirming significant clinical relevance despite no specific disease phenotype being currently annotated. Literature (13 papers) identifies WDR83 as essential for neuronal development (p.L218P variant), DNA damage repair splicing, basal autophagy via RRAG-MTORC1 inhibition, and gastric cancer regulation via antisense transcript interactions. The combination of WD40 domains, high-confidence AlphaFold structure, broad cellular expression, exclusive spliceosome/mitochondrial interaction network, 19p CNV burden, and neurodevelopmental/autophagy functions suggests WDR83 functions as a master scaffold coordinating mRNA splicing fidelity with mitochondrial biogenesis and metabolic signaling, with copy number variations potentially contributing to neurodevelopmental disorders and cancer progression.
