# RHBDD3: Rhomboid-UBA Fusion Protein Regulating Innate Immunity via NEMO K27-Linked Ubiquitination and TLR Signaling Suppression

**Confidence:** MEDIUM

## Description
RHBDD3 (Rhomboid domain-containing protein 3) encodes a 386 amino acid membrane-associated protein (UniProt Q9Y3P4) with a unique domain architecture combining rhomboid protease and ubiquitin-binding modules. InterPro analysis reveals dual functional domains: an N-terminal rhomboid-like superfamily fold (G3DSA:1.20.1540.10, residues 14-181; IPR035952) containing the peptidase S54 rhomboid domain (IPR022764, PF01694, residues 48-183) with predicted serine-type endopeptidase activity, and a C-terminal ubiquitin-associated domain (UBA, IPR015940, PF00627, residues 326-359; IPR009060, SSF46934) that mediates protein-protein interactions with ubiquitinated substrates. This rhomboid-UBA fusion architecture is distinctive among rhomboid family members and suggests RHBDD3 functions as a ubiquitin-dependent regulatory scaffold rather than a canonical protease.\n\nAlphaFold prediction shows medium global confidence (pLDDT 69.5) across all 386 aa, consistent with a structured rhomboid core connected to a flexible UBA domain via an unstructured linker region. The medium confidence suggests conformational flexibility that may be functionally important for substrate recognition.\n\nSTRING interactions reveal RHBDD3 operates within the rhomboid protease and ubiquitin signaling networks: RHBDL1 (0.559) and RHBDL3 (0.475) are active rhomboid proteases; UBAC2 (0.534) is an ERAD ubiquitin-binding protein; RHBDF1 (0.494), RHBDD1 (0.490), and RHBDD2 (0.436) are rhomboid pseudoprotease paralogs; FAM118A (0.508) is a family with unknown function; LETM2 (0.460) is a mitochondrial enzyme; TMEM115 (0.451) is a Golgi transmembrane protein; RNF112 (0.448) is a RING finger E3 ubiquitin ligase. The UBAC2 and RNF112 interactions strongly support a role in ubiquitin-mediated signaling.\n\nHPA expression shows RHBDD3 localizes to microtubules with low tissue specificity but is detected in many tissues, indicating ubiquitous expression. Single cell type specificity shows cell type-enhanced expression pattern. Blood cell expression shows low immune cell specificity, consistent with its role in innate immunity across multiple immune cell types.\n\nClinVar reports 29 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly 22q12.1-12.2 copy number losses and gains, plus splice site mutations (e.g., c.148+3G>T). While no specific monogenic disease is definitively assigned, the chromosomal region overlaps with susceptibility loci for autoimmune and inflammatory conditions. The CNV sensitivity suggests RHBDD3 dosage affects immune homeostasis.\n\nLiterature (12 papers) reveals critical immune regulatory functions: The landmark 2014 study (136 citations) demonstrated RHBDD3 controls autoimmunity by suppressing IL-6 production in dendritic cells via K27-linked ubiquitination of NEMO (NF-κB essential modulator), a key adapter in TLR and IL-1R signaling. RHBDD3 negatively regulates TLR signaling to prevent excessive innate inflammatory responses. A 2023 fish study showed Rhbdd3 positively regulates IFN response through RIG-I signaling, suggesting evolutionary conservation of antiviral functions. RHBDD3 is widely expressed in immune cells and controls innate inflammatory diseases.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: RHBDD3 functions as a rhomboid pseudoprotease-UBA fusion protein that regulates innate immune signaling through ubiquitin-dependent mechanisms. Unlike canonical rhomboid proteases that cleave transmembrane substrates, RHBDD3 likely uses its rhomboid domain as a scaffold to recruit specific substrates while the UBA domain recognizes K27-linked polyubiquitin chains on NEMO. This positions RHBDD3 as a negative regulator of NF-κB activation: by binding K27-ubiquitinated NEMO, RHBDD3 may recruit deubiquitinases or prevent K63-linked ubiquitination required for NF-κB activation, thereby suppressing IL-6 production and preventing autoimmunity. The microtubule localization suggests RHBDD3 may traffic ubiquitinated signaling complexes or coordinate immune signaling with cytoskeletal dynamics. The RNF112 interaction hints at E3 ligase recruitment for targeted ubiquitination. In disease, RHBDD3 deficiency or CNV loss would cause unchecked NEMO ubiquitination, hyperactive TLR signaling, elevated IL-6, and autoimmune/inflammatory phenotypes. The conserved antiviral role (RIG-I pathway in fish) suggests RHBDD3 balances pro-inflammatory and antiviral responses across vertebrates.
