## Description
KAT14 (UniProt Q9H8E8) encodes a 782 aa Histone Acetyltransferase (HAT) belonging to the GNAT (Gcn5-related N-acetyltransferase) family, functioning as a critical regulator of spiral artery remodeling in preeclampsia and DNA damage response. InterPro analysis identifies a canonical GNAT domain (aa 638-782, IPR000182, PF00583, PS51186) characteristic of the GNAT family, catalyzing the transfer of acetyl groups to lysine residues on histones and other substrates (GO:0016747); the protein also contains an N-terminal domain (aa 70-225) and a C-terminal domain (aa 675-727) within the N-Acyltransferase superfamily (cd04301, SSF55729), suggesting multifunctional regulatory capabilities. Functional hypothesis: KAT14 functions through: (1) Chromatin Remodeling—acetylating histones and non-histone proteins (e.g., WDR5, ZZZ3) to regulate gene expression during development and stress; (2) Vascular Integrity—acetylating CDH5 or interacting with SRF to maintain spiral artery structure in pregnancy; (3) DNA Repair—cooperating with WDR5 to facilitate DNA damage response and prevent genomic instability. Mechanistic proposal: In preeclampsia, KAT14 acetylates CDH5 or interacts with SRF to ensure proper spiral artery remodeling; loss of function leads to vascular dysfunction and placental insufficiency. In Diamond-Blackfan anemia, CNVs or mutations disrupt KAT14-WDR5 complex assembly, impairing ribosome biogenesis or chromatin regulation. The combination of a GNAT domain, high-confidence interactions with chromatin factors (WDR5, ZZZ3), ubiquitous expression, significant clinical variant burden (17 variants), and emerging literature on vascular and developmental phenotypes positions KAT14 as a key regulator of cellular homeostasis and a genuine dark gene requiring further mechanistic study.
