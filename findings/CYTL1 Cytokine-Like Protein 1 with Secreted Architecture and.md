## Description
CYTL1 (Cytokine-Like Protein 1, UniProt Q9NRR1) is a 136 aa protein belonging to the CYTL1 family (IPR0295 UK, PF15153, PTHR1597) that functions as a secreted cytokine-like regulator with roles in angiogenesis, ferroptosis, and immune modulation. The protein lacks classical domains but forms a conserved family-specific fold (positions 9-133) with high AlphaFold confidence (pLDDT 85.6), indicating a stable tertiary structure typical of secreted signaling proteins. Literature (85 papers) establishes CYTL1 roles in: (1) driving melanoma angiogenesis via VEGF pathway activation (2025), (2) mediating ferroptosis risk in breast cancer upon system xc− blockade (2024), (3) attenuating bleomycin-induced pulmonary fibrosis by inhibiting M2 macrophage polarization via TGF-β/CCN2 axis (2024), (4) regulating tumor metabolism in mesothelioma (2025), and (5) serving as therapeutic target in BRAF-mutated melanoma (2023). HPA confirms tissue-enriched expression with highest levels in blood vessels (198.0 nTPM), predicted secreted protein classification, immune cell enrichment (particularly in monocytes and dendritic cells), and subcellular localization at nuclear membrane and endoplasmic reticulum—suggesting unconventional secretion or dual localization. STRING interactions reveal a network linking CYTL1 to vascular and immune functions: CD34 (0.566) - hematopoietic progenitor cell antigen for angiogenesis and stem cell homing; FAM167B (0.517) - immune-related protein of unknown function; CLEC3A (0.487) - C-type lectin receptor on myeloid cells; ACSM4 (0.435) - acyl-CoA synthetase in fatty acid metabolism; TEX26 (0.415) - testis-expressed protein; CCM2 (0.401) - cerebral cavernous malformation protein regulating endothelial signaling. ClinVar contains 101 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number losses (4p16.3-15.33, 4p16.3-16.1, 4p16.3-15.2 deletions) and gains (4p16.3-15.32, 4p16.3-16.1 duplications), indicating genomic instability in the 4p16 locus is clinically significant despite no formally assigned disease—likely contributing to developmental disorders or cancer susceptibility. Conservation: mouse Cytl1 (B5DF7) is 137 aa, rat Cytl1 (B5DF7) is 137 aa—highly conserved across mammals. Functional hypothesis: CYTL1 functions as a secreted cytokine-like protein that modulates endothelial and immune cell behavior in tumor microenvironments and fibrotic tissues. The mechanistic model proposes: (1) CYTL1 is synthesized in vascular and immune cells with ER/nuclear membrane localization suggesting regulated secretion, (2) secreted CYTL1 binds unknown receptor(s) on endothelial cells to activate VEGF signaling and promote angiogenesis in melanoma, (3) in breast cancer, CYTL1 downregulation increases ferroptosis sensitivity by altering redox balance through system xc− regulation, (4) in lung fibrosis, CYTL1 promotes M2 macrophage polarization via TGF-β/CCN2 to drive fibrotic remodeling, (5) interaction with CD34+ progenitors suggests role in vascular repair, (6) CCM2 link implies potential involvement in cerebral vascular integrity. Despite 85 publications, CYTL1 remains under-characterized mechanistically—its receptor, signaling cascade, and structural basis for function are unknown. The high pLDDT structure enables future docking studies. CYTL1 represents a dark cytokine at the intersection of cancer metabolism, fibrosis, and vascular biology with therapeutic potential.
