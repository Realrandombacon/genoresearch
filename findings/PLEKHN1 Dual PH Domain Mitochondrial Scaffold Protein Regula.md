## Description
PLEKHN1 encodes a 611 amino acid pleckstrin homology domain-containing family N member 1 (UniProt Q494U1), a partially characterized scaffold protein with emerging roles in apoptosis regulation and host-pathogen interactions. With only 14 publications, PLEKHN1 remains understudied despite significant clinical variant burden.\n\nSTRUCTURAL FEATURES: AlphaFold predicts medium confidence structure (pLDDT 53.4) across all 611 residues, consistent with a scaffold protein containing ordered domains connected by flexible linkers. InterPro analysis reveals dual PH domain architecture: N-terminal PH domain (IPR001849, SM00233, residues 96-192; G3DSA:2.30.29.30, residues 84-191) and C-terminal PH domain (IPR001849, SM00233, residues 222-319; IPR037839, residues 216-336; G3DSA:2.30.29.30, residues 216-320). The PLEKHN1-specific family domain (IPR042835, PTHR46882, residues 1-611) spans the entire protein. GO annotations confirm cardiolipin binding (GO:1901612) and positive regulation of apoptotic process (GO:0043065).\n\nINTERACTION NETWORK: STRING-DB identifies 10 interaction partners with medium confidence (0.42-0.51), suggesting transient/conditional interactions typical of signaling scaffolds. Transcriptional regulators: ZNF396 (0.509). Elongator complex: ELP1 (0.505), ELP3 (0.438)—linking to tRNA modification and transcriptional elongation. Muscle-specific: PERM1 (0.492). Phosphatase: PTPDC1 (0.470). Olfactory receptors: OR4F5 (0.451), OR4F17 (0.451). Uncharacterized proteins: C3orf67 (0.447), TMEM212 (0.442). Glycosidase: MANEA (0.420). The modest interaction scores suggest context-dependent binding rather than stable complex formation.\n\nEXPRESSION & LOCALIZATION: HPA confirms tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in esophagus (58.4 nTPM), skin (48.4 nTPM), vagina (25.0 nTPM), and cervix (23.9 nTPM). Detected in many tissues with cell type-enhanced specificity. Subcellular localization includes plasma membrane and cytosol—consistent with PH domain-mediated membrane recruitment. Not detected in immune cells.\n\nCLINICAL SIGNIFICANCE: ClinVar reports 124 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number losses (1p36.33-36.23, 1p36.33-36.22, 1p36.33-35.1) encompassing the critical 1p36 deletion syndrome region, copy number gains (1p36.33-36.32), and splice variants (c.1434+39C>A). While no specific monogenic disease is yet annotated, PLEKHN1 lies within the 1p36 deletion syndrome critical region, suggesting potential contribution to developmental phenotypes.\n\nFUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: PLEKHN1 functions as a mitochondrial apoptosis scaffold with dual regulatory roles: (1) Apoptotic amplification—literature (2018, 19 citations) demonstrates PLEKHN1 binds pro-apoptotic BH3-only protein Bid, enhancing Bax-Bak hetero-oligomerization at mitochondrial outer membrane to trigger cytochrome c release. Dual PH domains bind cardiolipin (GO:1901612), anchoring PLEKHN1 to mitochondrial membranes where apoptosis execution occurs. (2) Host-pathogen interface—Legionella pneumophila effector AnkX directly interacts with PLEKHN1 (2018, 16 citations), suggesting bacterial exploitation of apoptotic machinery. (3) Transcriptional coupling—ELP1/ELP3 interactions link PLEKHN1 to Elongator complex-mediated tRNA modification and stress responses. Mechanistically, PH domains recruit PLEKHN1 to cardiolipin-rich mitochondrial membranes upon apoptotic stimuli; flexible linker regions (low pLDDT) enable conformational switching to present Bid to Bax/Bak, accelerating oligomerization. Loss-of-function would impair apoptosis execution (cancer susceptibility); gain-of-function could drive excessive cell death (neurodegeneration). The 1p36 deletion syndrome association suggests developmental roles requiring fine-tuned apoptotic regulation.
