## Description
ZNF101 encodes a 436 amino acid KRAB-domain zinc finger protein (UniProt Q8IZC7) functioning as a sequence-specific DNA-binding transcriptional repressor. The protein contains an N-terminal KRAB repression domain (PF01352, IPR001909, residues 3-82) that recruits corepressor complexes, followed by multiple C2H2-type zinc finger domains (PF00096, IPR013087) at positions 102-129, 169-196, 197-224, and additional fingers extending to residue 416, enabling sequence-specific DNA recognition. AlphaFold predicts a medium-confidence structure (pLDDT 66.2) consistent with folded zinc finger domains linked by flexible regions. Tissue expression profiling (HPA) shows ubiquitous expression with enrichment in lymphoid tissue (25.7 nTPM), classified as tissue-enhanced with detection across all tissues examined. Protein interaction mapping (STRING) reveals 10 partners including TRIM28 (score 0.522), the canonical KRAB-associated corepressor KAP1, supporting the mechanistic model of transcriptional silencing via heterochromatin formation. Additional interactors ZNF766 (0.568), YJEFN3 (0.525), TAF8 (0.472), and HINFP (0.456) suggest roles in transcriptional complexes and chromatin regulation. ClinVar documents 8 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number gain variants spanning chromosome 19p13.11-q13.43 regions encompassing ZNF101, though specific disease phenotypes remain unassigned. The conserved KRAB-ZNF architecture across vertebrates indicates ancient evolutionary origin for transcriptional control functions.
