## Description
C2orf72 (UniProt A6NCS6) is a 295-amino acid protein encoded on chromosome 2q37.1. InterPro analysis identifies a single conserved domain of unknown function, DUF4630 (IPR027868/PF15443), spanning residues 139-293, defining it as the founding member of the PTHR35675 family. AlphaFold predicts a partially structured protein (global pLDDT 65.5), suggesting a mix of ordered and disordered regions that may enable conformational flexibility for regulatory functions. HPA data reveals group-enriched expression with highest levels in liver (18.9 nTPM) and brain (5.9 nTPM), with dual localization to both nucleoplasm and plasma membrane, indicating potential roles in nucleocytoplasmic signaling or membrane-associated processes. STRING interaction analysis identifies two medium-confidence partners: CLBA1 (0.583, clathrin-binding protein involved in endocytosis) and ENTPD5 (0.487, ectonucleoside triphosphate diphosphohydrolase linked to ER stress and UDP metabolism). This interaction profile strongly suggests involvement in vesicle trafficking, endocytic pathways, or membrane dynamics. ClinVar records 29 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains and losses spanning 2q, indicating dosage sensitivity though no specific monogenic phenotype is yet defined. Literature is sparse (4 papers, DARK status), with associations to prostate cancer, preeclampsia, glioblastoma, and osteosarcoma in bioinformatics studies. We hypothesize C2orf72 functions as a regulatory adaptor in clathrin-mediated vesicle trafficking or endocytic pathways, potentially linking ER stress responses (via ENTPD5) to membrane dynamics in liver and brain tissues. The dual nucleoplasmic and plasma membrane localization suggests it may shuttle between compartments or function at membrane-cytosol interfaces. The DUF4630 domain likely provides a protein-protein interaction surface for recruiting trafficking machinery. Given its expression in metabolically active tissues (liver) and neuronal tissue (brain), C2orf72 may coordinate vesicle-mediated transport of metabolic enzymes or neurotransmitter receptors. The high pathogenic variant burden despite minimal mechanistic characterization indicates C2orf72 is clinically important but functionally unexplored, representing a genuine dark gene priority for experimental validation.
