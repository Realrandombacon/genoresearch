## Description
PLEKHG7 (formerly C12orf74) encodes a 691 amino acid intracellular protein functioning as a Rho guanine nucleotide exchange factor (RhoGEF). The protein architecture features an N-terminal domain of unknown function DUF4675 (PF15720, residues 2-187), a central Dbl homology (DH) domain (IPR000219/PF00621, residues 288-488) responsible for catalytic GEF activity, and a C-terminal Pleckstrin Homology (PH) domain (IPR001849/PF00169, residues 535-670) mediating membrane localization via phosphoinositide binding. AlphaFold predicts a medium-confidence structure (pLDDT 62.8), consistent with modular signaling proteins containing flexible linkers between structured domains. HPA data localizes PLEKHG7 to the centrosome, with tissue-enhanced expression highest in fallopian tube (2.5 nTPM) and epididymis (1.8 nTPM), and detection in specific blood cell groups. STRING interactions reveal connections to other RhoGEFs (ARHGEF33 score 0.464, ARHGEF38 score 0.457) and scaffolding proteins (WDFY3 score 0.418, FER1L6 score 0.470), supporting a role in cytoskeletal regulation and vesicle trafficking. ClinVar documents 8 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, exclusively large copy number losses and gains spanning 12q21-22, indicating dosage sensitivity where haploinsufficiency or triplosensitivity disrupts development. The mechanistic hypothesis: PLEKHG7 acts as a spatially regulated activator of Rho GTPases at the centrosome, coupling membrane signals (via PH domain) to cytoskeletal reorganization (via DH domain). Its centrosomal localization suggests involvement in mitotic spindle orientation or ciliogenesis. Disruption of PLEKHG7 dosage likely causes developmental defects through aberrant Rho signaling dynamics during cell division or migration.
