## Description
STX8 encodes a 236 aa type IV t-SNARE protein (UniProt Q9UNK0), also known as CARB, that functions as a membrane fusion machinery component regulating protein trafficking from early to late endosomes through vesicle fusion and exocytosis. The protein features a canonical SNARE architecture: an N-terminal regulatory region (residues 1-139); a central SNARE motif/target SNARE coiled-coil homology domain (IPR000727/PF05739/SM00397/cd15852, residues 140-224/152-204) forming the four-helix bundle for membrane fusion; and a C-terminal transmembrane anchor anchoring the protein to endosomal membranes. The SNARE domain adopts the characteristic G3DSA:1.20.5.110 fold (SSF58038 SNARE fusion complex superfamily) essential for complex assembly. STRING interactions place STX8 at the core of endosomal SNARE networks with exceptional confidence: VTI1B (0.999) and STX7 (0.999), forming the canonical STX7-STX8-VTI1B-VAMP8 tetrameric complex for early-to-late endosome fusion; VTI1A (0.996), an alternative v-SNARE partner; STX12 (0.995), another endosomal syntaxin; VAMP8 (0.993), the cognate v-SNARE for late endosome/lysosome fusion; STX3 (0.986), a plasma membrane syntaxin suggesting cross-compartment regulation; VAMP7 (0.966), involved in lysosomal exocytosis; NAPA (0.956), alpha-SNAP mediating SNARE complex disassembly; YKT6 (0.890), a lipid-anchored v-SNARE; and VAMP4 (0.714), recently linked to STX8-mediated autophagic mitochondria secretion. HPA confirms ubiquitous expression across all tissues with subcellular localization to vesicles and cytosol, annotated for transport function—consistent with housekeeping endosomal trafficking roles. ClinVar lists 10 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all copy number gains spanning chromosome 17p13.3-p13.2 (ranging from 165kb to 81Mb duplications), demonstrating dosage sensitivity though specific disease associations remain undefined. AlphaFold structure prediction yields high confidence (global pLDDT 76.3), revealing a well-folded SNARE domain with proper coiled-coil geometry for four-helix bundle formation. Mechanistically, STX8 functions through multiple integrated modes: (1) As a t-SNARE, STX8 forms heterotypic complexes with STX7, VTI1B, and VAMP8 to drive membrane fusion between early and late endosomes—the four-helix coiled-coil bundle brings membranes into close apposition, overcoming repulsive forces and enabling cargo transfer; (2) Recent studies (2026) reveal STX8-VAMP4 complexes mediate autophagic secretion of mitochondria under hypoxic conditions in head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC), promoting tumor-associated macrophage (TAM) polarization toward immunosuppressive phenotypes; (3) Mendelian randomization studies (2025) link STX8 variants to pneumoconiosis susceptibility through lung-gut axis mechanisms, suggesting roles in particle clearance and inflammatory responses; (4) Evolutionary analysis (2021) demonstrates divergence in the retromer-sorting motif, indicating species-specific regulation of STX8 recycling from endosomes; (5) Interaction with NAPA suggests regulated SNARE complex disassembly via NSF ATPase activity for recycling. Disruption of STX8 would cause defective endosomal maturation, impaired lysosomal degradation, aberrant autophagic mitochondria secretion, altered TAM polarization in tumors, and increased pneumoconiosis susceptibility—phenotypes consistent with the 17p13 duplication syndromes observed in patients.
