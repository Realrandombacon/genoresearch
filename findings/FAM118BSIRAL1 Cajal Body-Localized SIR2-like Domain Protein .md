# FAM118B/SIRAL1: Cajal Body-Localized SIR2-like Domain Protein Regulating Transcriptional Coactivation and snRNP Biogenesis via YAP1-Hippo Pathway Integration

## Description
FAM118B (Family With Sequence Similarity 118 Member B, also SIRAL1, SIR2 Antiphage Like 1) is a 351 amino acid nuclear protein (UniProt Q9BPY3) with a well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 83.9, high confidence), localized to Cajal bodies where it functions in snRNP biogenesis and transcriptional regulation through protein-protein interactions mediated by a degenerate SIR2-like domain.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals FAM118B contains a C-terminal SIR2-like domain (PF13289, residues 158-292) belonging to the sirtuin/deacetylase superfamily, though likely catalytically inactive due to absence of conserved NAD+-binding residues. The protein defines the Protein FAM118 family (IPR038916/PTHR28623, residues 1-348/1-348). The SIR2-like fold typically adopts a Rossmann-fold topology for cofactor binding, but in FAM118B likely serves as a protein interaction scaffold rather than enzymatic catalyst.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals FAM118B functions within transcriptional coactivation and nuclear organization networks: strongest interactions with C11orf95 (0.824, chromosome 11 open reading frame 95, nuclear protein), YAP1 (0.590, Yes-associated protein 1, Hippo pathway transcriptional coactivator), MAMLD1 (0.512, mastermind-like domain-containing protein 1, Notch signaling coactivator), ACTR8 (0.484, actin-related protein 8, ARP2/3 complex subunit), NUTM1 (0.472, NUT midline carcinoma family member 1, chromatin reader), MFAP1 (0.463, microfibrillar-associated protein 1, Cajal body component), RELA (0.461, NF-kappa-B p65 subunit, inflammatory transcription factor), INTS4 (0.461, integrator complex subunit 4, snRNA 3'-end processing), SLC25A17 (0.455, solute carrier family 25 member 17, peroxisomal transporter), and MED7 (0.451, mediator complex subunit 7, RNA polymerase II transcription). The YAP1, RELA, and MED7 interactions link FAM118B to transcriptional coactivation, while INTS4 and MFAP1 connections suggest Cajal body/snRNP processing roles.\n\nHPA expression shows low tissue specificity with detection in all tissues and predicted intracellular localization, consistent with housekeeping nuclear functions. Single cell type specificity is cell type enhanced.\n\nClinVar documents 63 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all large chromosome 11q23.3-25 copy number variations (gains and losses spanning 11q23.3-25 to 11p13-q25 regions). These CNVs suggest dosage sensitivity potentially causing developmental disorders through impaired transcriptional regulation and snRNP biogenesis.\n\nConservation is evident from mouse (Q8C569, 351 aa), rat (Q4QQT2, 350 aa), and cow (Q5E977, 351 aa) orthologs, indicating ancient evolutionary conservation of nuclear scaffold functions.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: FAM118B acts as a Cajal body-localized scaffold protein that integrates Hippo pathway signaling (via YAP1) with snRNP biogenesis and transcriptional coactivation. The SIR2-like domain, while catalytically inactive, provides a stable protein interaction platform that recruits transcriptional machinery (MED7, RELA) and RNA processing factors (INTS4). Cajal body localization via MFAP1 interaction positions FAM118B at sites of snRNP assembly and telomerase RNP maturation. YAP1 interaction suggests FAM118B modulates Hippo pathway output, potentially affecting cell proliferation and organ size control. The C11orf95 partnership (0.824 score) indicates a stable complex, possibly forming a heterodimeric scaffold. NUTM1 interaction links FAM118B to chromatin regulation through bromodomain-mediated histone acetylation reading. ACTR8 connection suggests roles in actin nucleation for nuclear architecture. CNV-mediated haploinsufficiency disrupts transcriptional coactivation complexes and snRNP assembly, causing developmental defects through impaired RNA polymerase II/III transcription and pre-mRNA splicing fidelity. The 11q24 deletion syndrome region overlaps FAM118B, suggesting contribution to Jacobsen syndrome phenotypes.
