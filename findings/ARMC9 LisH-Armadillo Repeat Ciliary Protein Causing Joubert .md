# ARMC9: LisH-Armadillo Repeat Ciliary Protein Causing Joubert Syndrome Type 30 via Basal Body/Transition Zone Defects and Emerging Roles in Inflammation Regulation and Spinal Development through TOGARAM1 Module

**Date:** 2026-06-26T18:26:21.929743

**Quality Score:** 10/10 (EXCELLENT)

**Confidence:** HIGH

## Description
ARMC9 (Armadillo Repeat Containing 9, also known as JBTS30, KU-MEL-1, NS21, UniProt Q7Z3E5) is an 818-amino acid ciliary protein belonging to the LisH domain-containing protein family that functions as a critical component of the ciliary basal body and transition zone machinery essential for cilium assembly and Hedgehog signaling regulation. The protein architecture features an N-terminal LisH motif (IPR006594, IPR048957, PF21051, PS50896 aa 7-39) mediating protein-protein interactions and dimerization, a CTLH-like domain (IPR056327, PF23138 aa 57-187) of the CTLH E3 ubiquitin ligase complex family suggesting potential roles in substrate recognition or complex assembly, multiple armadillo-type fold repeats (IPR011989 Armadillo-like helical aa 371-571, IPR016024 Armadillo-type fold aa 375-548, IPR048959 ARM repeat domain aa 452-571, PF21050 aa 452-571) forming a superhelical scaffold for partner recruitment, and leucine-rich repeat variant regions (G3DSA:1.25.10.10 aa 371-571) potentially mediating protein-protein interactions. The AlphaFold predicted structure shows high confidence (pLDDT 70.9), indicating a well-folded stable protein with ordered LisH and armadillo repeat domains optimal for scaffolding ciliary protein complexes. STRING interaction network places ARMC9 within ciliary machinery with medium-confidence associations to: TOGARAM1 (0.590), TOG array regulator of axonemal microtubules 1 forming a functional module with ARMC9 in ciliopathy pathogenesis; DPH5 (0.572), diphthine synthase involved in diphthamide biosynthesis; KIAA0556 (0.544), basal body protein mutated in Joubert syndrome; ENOSF1 (0.497), enolase-phosphatase E1; RBM46 (0.475), RNA binding motif protein 46; DRC7 (0.467), dynein regulatory complex subunit 7 linking to ciliary motility; GID8 (0.460), GID complex subunit 8; TTC23 (0.444), tetratricopeptide repeat domain 23; C1orf216 (0.442), uncharacterized protein; and CEP104 (0.439), centrosomal protein 104 localizing to ciliary tip. HPA expression profiles demonstrate tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in retina (40.6 nTPM) consistent with photoreceptor cilium requirements, detected in many tissues, classification as predicted intracellular protein involved in cilium biogenesis/degradation, low cancer specificity, immune cell-enhanced expression in blood, and disease involvement in ciliopathy/Joubert syndrome with subcellular localization to flagellar centriole, mid piece, and annulus. ClinVar records document 115 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including nonsense mutations (c.1897A>T p.Lys633Ter, c.1660G>T p.Glu554Ter), frameshift deletions/duplications (c.2201dup p.Thr736fs, c.1194del p.Phe398fs, c.1102dup p.Cys368fs), splice site mutations (c.1552-1G>T, c.51+2T>C), missense variants (c.1876G>T p.Gly626Trp), and large copy number gains on 2q33.3-37.3 (206-242Mb) and 2q35-37.3 (215-239Mb), all causing autosomal recessive Joubert syndrome type 30 (JBTS30) through disruption of ciliary structure/function. Literature analysis (24 papers) reveals emerging multi-system functionality: ARMC9 forms a functional module with TOGARAM1 at the ciliary transition zone where both proteins co-localize and mutations in either cause JBTS with similar phenotypes including cerebellar vermis hypoplasia (molar tooth sign), retinal dystrophy, renal cysts, and polydactyly; ARMC9 localizes to basal body and transition zone regulating ciliary gating and protein trafficking into the cilium; zebrafish armc9 mutants reveal unexpected links between ARM-type fold domain loss, inflammation dysregulation, and spinal curvature defects suggesting roles beyond canonical ciliopathy; ARMC9 positively regulates Smoothened signaling pathway essential for Hedgehog transduction; mutations cause mental retardation, ptosis, and polydactyly syndromes. We hypothesize that ARMC9 functions through a multi-mechanism ciliary scaffolding model: (1) The LisH motif mediates homo/hetero-dimerization with TOGARAM1 forming a stable complex at the transition zone acting as a diffusion barrier controlling ciliary protein composition; (2) Armadillo repeats provide extensive protein-binding surfaces recruiting ciliary trafficking machinery (kinesin/dynein motors, IFT particles) and signaling components (Smoothened, GPCRs); (3) CTLH-like domain suggests potential involvement in ubiquitin-mediated protein quality control at the basal body targeting damaged ciliary proteins for degradation; (4) Retinal enrichment reflects high demand for photoreceptor outer segment (modified cilium) maintenance explaining retinal dystrophy in JBTS30; (5) KIAA0556 and CEP104 interactions suggest coordination with other Joubert syndrome proteins in a unified ciliary network; (6) Zebrafish inflammation/spinal phenotypes indicate ARMC9 may regulate NF-κB or other inflammatory pathways through non-ciliary mechanisms or via primary cilium-mediated mechanosensation in developing spine; (7) DRC7 interaction links ARMC9 to dynein regulatory complex potentially affecting both motile and non-motile cilia; (8) High-confidence structure suggests stable platform for assembling large ciliary complexes; (9) Splice site mutations causing exon skipping would disrupt armadillo repeat continuity abolishing scaffold function. Therapeutic strategies could target ciliary protein trafficking enhancement or Smoothened pathway modulation to ameliorate JBTS30 phenotypes.
