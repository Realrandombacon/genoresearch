## Description
ZNF815P (zinc finger protein 815 pseudogene, Gene ID: 401303) is a 130-amino acid putative protein encoded on chromosome 7p22.1 that represents a genuine dark gene with minimal molecular characterization and an unusual structural profile. Despite its annotation as a zinc finger protein, AlphaFold structure prediction shows very low confidence (global pLDDT 47.7), indicating the protein is mostly disordered and lacks a stable globular fold—highly atypical for canonical C2H2 zinc finger proteins which normally display well-defined beta-beta-alpha zinc-coordinating structures. This low structural confidence suggests either: (1) the pseudogene annotation is accurate and ZNF815P does not produce a stable folded protein, (2) the protein functions as an intrinsically disordered protein (IDP) mediating protein-protein interactions rather than DNA binding, or (3) it functions primarily at the RNA level as a long non-coding RNA. UniProt accession A8K554 exists but InterPro domain scanning failed, preventing confirmation of zinc finger domains. STRING database returned no interaction partners (no protein found in species 9606), and HPA has no expression data (ENSG00000235944 not in database), reflecting its understudied nature. ClinVar contains zero variants—no known pathogenic mutations or disease associations. Literature analysis shows only 4 total papers (DARK status), none directly characterizing ZNF815P function; instead, papers describe 7p22.1 microdeletion syndromes involving ACTB (beta-actin) as the causative gene for developmental delay, short stature, and microcephaly. The chromosome 7p22.1 location places ZNF815P within a clinically relevant genomic region, though haploinsufficiency of ZNF815P itself has not been implicated in disease. The combination of pseudogene annotation, disordered structure prediction, lack of domain confirmation, and absence from interaction/expression databases suggests ZNF815P may represent a transcriptional artifact, a recently evolved gene without conserved function, or a non-coding RNA misannotated as protein-coding. If functional, the disordered nature suggests potential roles in scaffold functions, phase separation, or regulatory RNA mechanisms rather than sequence-specific DNA binding typical of zinc finger transcription factors. This represents a genuine dark gene requiring experimental validation to determine if it produces a functional protein product.
