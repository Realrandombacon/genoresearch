## Description
CPSF4 encodes a 269 amino acid cleavage and polyadenylation specificity factor subunit 4 (UniProt O95639), an essential component of the mammalian mRNA 3' end processing machinery that recognizes the AAUAAA polyadenylation signal and coordinates pre-mRNA cleavage and poly(A) tail addition. The protein contains multiple RNA-binding zinc finger domains: three CCCH-type zinc fingers (IPR000571 at positions 35-61, 62-89, 90-117; IPR036855 at 63-87, 144-172; IPR041686 at 40-118) and one CCHC-type zinc knuckle (IPR001878/PF00098 at positions 244-260; IPR036875 at 224-260). These domains confer RNA-binding capability (GO:0003723) and metal ion binding (GO:0046872), with the CPSF4-like family signature (IPR045348 at positions 1-262) defining this specific subunit.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals CPSF4 forms an exceptionally tight complex with core cleavage/polyadenylation machinery: FIP1L1 (score 0.999), WDR33 (0.999), CPSF1 (0.999), CPSF2 (0.999), CSTF2 (0.997), CSTF3 (0.995), NUDT21/CPSF5 (0.990), CPSF3 (0.983), CPSF7 (0.979), and SYMPK (0.875). This positions CPSF4 at the heart of the ~800 kDa CPSF complex responsible for recognizing poly(A) signals on thousands of human transcripts. Recent work indicates CPSF4 also regulates alternative splicing decisions, particularly of HMG20B in triple-negative breast cancer.\n\nHPA expression data classifies CPSF4 as an essential protein with low tissue specificity, detected in all tissues with cell type-enhanced expression patterns. Subcellular localization includes nucleoplasm (consistent with transcription-coupled mRNA processing) and vesicles (potentially reflecting mRNA export or quality control compartments). Biological processes include host-virus interaction (suggesting roles in viral mRNA processing or antiviral responses) and mRNA processing.\n\nClinVar documents 20 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number losses (7q22.1 deletions spanning megabases), deletions (NC_000007.13:g.(?_94024344)_(100860555_?)del), copy number gains (7q21.12-22.1 duplications), and complex rearrangements. Critically, ClinVar lists 0 associated diseases—a profound knowledge gap given CPSF4's essential role in mRNA biogenesis. This suggests CPSF4 haploinsufficiency may cause undiagnosed developmental disorders, immunodeficiency (given host-virus interaction annotation), or synthetic lethal relationships in cancer.\n\nAlphaFold structure prediction (AF-O95639-F1) yields a global pLDDT score of 75.9 (high confidence) across all 269 residues, indicating a well-folded structure with ordered zinc finger domains suitable for RNA recognition.\n\nLiterature analysis (37 papers) reveals emerging but incomplete understanding. Recent 2024-2026 studies link CPSF4 to hepatocellular carcinoma progression via miR-98-5p axis, lung cancer brain metastasis through NFκB/COX2 pathway, and triple-negative breast cancer via HMG20B alternative splicing regulation. However, no Mendelian disease phenotype has been defined despite 20 pathogenic variants. Key unknowns include: specific disease phenotypes from loss-of-function mutations, contribution to developmental disorders (given essential protein classification), role in viral infection susceptibility, and therapeutic potential for cancers with CPSF4-dependent splicing programs.
