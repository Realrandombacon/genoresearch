## Description
ACAP2 (UniProt Q15057), also known as CENTB2 or CNT-B2, encodes a 778 aa multi-domain Arf GTPase-activating protein functioning as a critical regulator of membrane trafficking and actin cytoskeleton dynamics. InterPro analysis reveals conserved modular architecture: N-terminal BAR domain (cd07638/G3DSA:1.20.1270.60, residues 18-217/1-255) sensing and inducing membrane curvature for vesicle formation; central Pleckstrin Homology (PH) domain (cd13250/G3DSA:2.30.29.30/IPR001849, residues 269-365/261-376/266-363) binding phosphoinositides (PI(4,5)P2) for membrane recruitment; catalytic ArfGAP domain (cd08851/G3DSA:1.10.220.150/IPR001164, residues 399-513/377-518/399-520) mediating GTPase activator activity (GO:0005096) to hydrolyze ARF-GTP to ARF-GDP terminating vesicle budding; and C-terminal Ankyrin repeat domain (G3DSA:1.25.40.20/IPR002110, residues 608-763/610-705/706-735) providing protein-protein interaction surfaces (GO:0005515). AlphaFold predicts high confidence structure (pLDDT 81.4) indicating a well-folded protein with stable domain interfaces suitable for coordinated membrane binding and catalytic activity. STRING interactions define a high-confidence endosomal trafficking network: ARF6 (0.808, primary substrate regulating endocytic recycling and actin remodeling), ARF1 (0.725, Golgi trafficking regulator), OCRL (0.624, Lowe syndrome protein, phosphatase acting on PI(4,5)P2), ACAP3 (0.615, paralog), ARF5 (0.583), TBC1D10A (0.577, Rab GAP), ASAP1 (0.575, another ArfGAP), RUSC2 (0.571), PLEK (0.508, pleckstrin), and RAB35 (0.412, Rab GTPase). The strong ARF6/ARF1/OCRL links confirm ACAP2 role in phosphoinositide-dependent endosomal recycling. HPA classifies ACAP2 as plasma protein with low tissue specificity (detected in all tissues), immune cell enhanced expression, localized to endosomes consistent with trafficking function, involved in GTPase activation. ClinVar lists 42 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants predominantly consisting of large copy number gains (3q22.1-29x3, 3q29x3, 3q26.31-29x3) and duplications involving the 3q29 region; while no single-gene disorder is named, 3q29 duplication syndrome is a known neurodevelopmental disorder where ACAP2 dosage may contribute to phenotype. Functional hypothesis: ACAP2 operates through integrated membrane trafficking mechanisms: (1) Membrane recruitment - PH domain binds PI(4,5)P2 on endosomal membranes while BAR domain senses curvature, positioning the protein at budding sites; (2) Catalytic cycle - ArfGAP domain stimulates GTP hydrolysis on ARF6 (and ARF1), causing dissociation of coat proteins and termination of vesicle budding; (3) Actin coupling - ARF6 inactivation by ACAP2 regulates downstream effectors (e.g., WAVE complex) controlling actin polymerization at the cell cortex; (4) Endosomal recycling - Specific role in recycling integrins and transferrin receptor from endosomes to plasma membrane affecting cell migration and nutrient uptake; (5) Cancer mechanism - Recent literature (2021-2025) establishes ACAP2 as a tumor suppressor or oncogene depending on context: degraded by RNF126 in ovarian cancer promoting lipid metabolism reprogramming; targeted by CAF-derived exosomal miR-3656 in esophageal cancer activating PI3K-AKT pathway; sponged by circ-ACAP2 in colorectal cancer and neuroblastoma affecting miR-143-3p targets (FZD4, HK2); (6) Neurodevelopmental mechanism - 3q29 duplications including ACAP2 may disrupt neuronal migration or synaptic plasticity via altered ARF6 signaling; (7) Conservation - Mouse Acap2 (Q6ZQK5, 770 aa) and Rat Acap2 (Q5FVC7, 770 aa) indicate essential conserved function. Dark aspects requiring investigation include: precise structural mechanism of BAR-PH-ArfGAP coordination, specific lipid substrates for PH domain beyond general PI(4,5)P2, contribution of ACAP2 haploinsufficiency vs. overexpression to 3q29 syndrome phenotypes, therapeutic potential of targeting ACAP2-ARF6 axis in cancers with dysregulated recycling, role in immune cell function (HPA immune enhanced), crosstalk with OCRL phosphatase in Lowe syndrome pathology, involvement in neuronal development and synaptic vesicle recycling, regulation by post-translational modifications, and potential synthetic lethality with other ArfGAPs (ASAP1, ACAP3).
