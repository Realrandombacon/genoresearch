# ERMAP: Scianna Blood Group Antigen with Emerging Immune Checkpoint Functions Regulating Macrophage Phagocytosis, T Cell Activation, Colitis, EAE, and Liver Metastasis Restriction via Galectin-9-Dectin-2 Axis

## Description
ERMAP (Erythroblast Membrane-Associated Protein), also known as the Scianna blood group antigen, is a 475-amino acid type I transmembrane protein (UniProt Q96PL5) representing a genuine dark gene with only 49 publications despite its critical roles as an emerging immune checkpoint molecule regulating macrophage phagocytosis, T cell function, inflammatory bowel disease, experimental autoimmune encephalomyelitis, and liver metastasis restriction. Structurally, ERMAP contains canonical immune receptor architecture: Immunoglobulin V-set domain (IPR013106, residues 42-143) mediating ligand binding and protein-protein interactions, B30.2/SPRY domain (IPR001870/cd15813, residues 220-418/230-412) functioning as a protein interaction module, SPRY domain (IPR003877, residues 290-415) involved in pathogen recognition and immune signaling, Butyrophylin-like SPRY domain (IPR003879, residues 236-253+253-270+275-299) linking to butyrophilin family immune regulators, SPRY-associated domain (IPR006574, residues 237-289) stabilizing the SPRY fold, Immunoglobulin-like domain superfamily (IPR007110/IPR013783/IPR036179, residues 17-140/36-150) defining the extracellular recognition module, Immunoglobulin domain subtype (IPR003599, residues 35-144), and Concanavalin A-like lectin/glucanase domain superfamily (IPR013320, residues 230-413) suggesting carbohydrate-binding capacity. AlphaFold predicts high-confidence structure (pLDDT 81.3), consistent with a well-folded type I membrane protein containing stable Ig V-set and SPRY domains forming defined ligand-binding surfaces. Functionally, ERMAP localizes to Golgi apparatus and cytosol with ubiquitous expression across all tissues (HPA: detected in all, low tissue specificity, low cancer specificity, low immune cell specificity, cell type enhanced), classified as a blood group antigen protein functioning in blood group antigen determination—Golgi localization confirms roles in glycoprotein processing and trafficking. STRING interactions reveal connections to erythroid and metabolic proteins: XDH (0.969, xanthine dehydrogenase—purine metabolism enzyme), TECR (0.918, trans-2,3-enoyl-CoA reductase—very long chain fatty acid elongation), MUC1 (0.608, mucin 1—cell surface glycoprotein), MUC15 (0.541, mucin 15—cell surface glycoprotein), LTF (0.531, lactotransferrin—iron-binding antimicrobial protein), KEL (0.521, Kell blood group glycoprotein—endopeptidase/blood group antigen), PGM1 (0.514, phosphoglucomutase 1—glycolysis enzyme), MFGE8 (0.495, milk fat globule-EGF factor 8—phagocytosis mediator), PLIN2 (0.448, perilipin 2—lipid droplet protein), and GOT2 (0.446, glutamate oxaloacetate transaminase 2—mitochondrial transaminase). This interaction network positions ERMAP at the intersection of blood group biology, erythroid function, and immune regulation. Critically, ClinVar documents 14 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large deletions at 1p34.2 (NC_000001.10:g.(?_42922237)_(43916151_?)del, NC_000001.10:g.(?_43200757)_(43424429_?)del), copy number losses at 1p34.2 (GRCh38/hg38 chr1:42278946-43598390, chr1:40834404-43123071, chr1:42653385-43093829), copy number gains at 1p35.1-33 (GRCh37/hg19 chr1:33285582-47891811), whole chromosome arm gains at 1p36.33-q44 (chr1:849467-249224684)x3, inversions, and 1p34.2-34.1 losses (chr1:42914303-45001279)x1, yet ZERO diseases are formally associated—a profound gap given the blood group antigen status and emerging immune functions. Recent literature establishes emerging specialized roles beyond blood group determination: ERMAP attenuates DSS-induced colitis in mice by regulating macrophage and T cell functions, establishing protective role in inflammatory bowel disease through immune modulation (1 citation, 2025); endogenous ERMAP affects T-cell function in EAE mice, revealing role in multiple sclerosis pathogenesis by modulating myelin-reactive CD4+ T cells (2025); ligation between ERMAP, galectin-9 and dectin-2 promotes Kupffer cell phagocytosis and antitumor immunity, establishing ERMAP as a novel immune checkpoint ligand engaging galectin-9-dectin-2 axis in liver macrophages (64 citations, 2023); ERMAP activates phagocytosis in Kupffer cells to restrict liver metastases, demonstrating tumor-suppressive function in hepatic microenvironment (2023); and French literature documents ERMAP dans l'érythropoïèse humaine (ERMAP in human erythropoiesis). The mechanistic hypothesis: ERMAP functions as a type I transmembrane immune checkpoint molecule through its extracellular Ig V-set domain engaging galectin-9, which bridges to dectin-2 on Kupffer cells and macrophages, triggering phagocytic activation and antitumor immunity. In colitis, ERMAP likely modulates macrophage polarization (M1/M2 balance) and T cell activation (Th1/Th17 vs Treg), preventing excessive inflammation through checkpoint-like inhibition of pro-inflammatory signaling. In EAE/MS, ERMAP regulates myelin-reactive CD4+ T cell activation and CNS infiltration, potentially through PD-1/CTLA-4-like inhibitory signaling. In liver metastasis, ERMAP-galectin-9-dectin-2 axis activates Kupffer cell phagocytosis of circulating tumor cells, restricting metastatic seeding—the Ig V-set domain binds galectin-9 (β-galactoside-binding lectin), inducing conformational changes that promote dectin-2 clustering and Syk kinase activation. The SPRY/B30.2 domain may mediate intracellular signaling or protein-protein interactions with downstream effectors. The blood group antigen function suggests ERMAP polymorphisms define Scianna blood group phenotypes (Sc1, Sc2, Sc3 antigens), potentially affecting transfusion compatibility and hemolytic disease of newborn. The Golgi localization indicates roles in glycoprotein trafficking and maturation beyond surface expression. The absence of formal disease associations despite 14 pathogenic variants suggests ERMAP dysfunction contributes to Scianna blood group anomalies, inflammatory bowel disease susceptibility, multiple sclerosis risk, liver metastasis predisposition, or hemolytic transfusion reactions not yet mapped to Mendelian phenotypes. Future work should define ERMAP's complete ligand repertoire beyond galectin-9, determine crystal structure of Ig V-set domain bound to galectin-9, investigate whether ERMAP polymorphisms cause Scianna blood group phenotypes with clinical transfusion implications, develop ERMAP-blocking antibodies for cancer immunotherapy (similar to PD-1/PD-L1), explore ERMAP agonists for IBD treatment, and determine if germline ERMAP mutations cause immunodeficiency or autoimmunity.
