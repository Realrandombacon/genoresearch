## Description
HNRNPA1L2 (Heterogeneous Nuclear Ribonucleoprotein A1-Like 2) is a 320 amino acid intracellular RNA-binding protein (UniProt Q32P51) with a well-folded architecture containing two RNA Recognition Motifs (RRMs) and a C-terminal low-complexity domain (AlphaFold pLDDT 70.2, confident), functioning as a core component of the spliceosome and mRNA transport machinery that regulates pre-mRNA splicing, nuclear export, and mRNA stability through sequence-specific RNA binding.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals HNRNPA1L2 contains two canonical RNA recognition motifs: RRM1 (IPR000504/PF00076/PS50102/cd12762, residues 14-97/16-81) and RRM2 (IPR000504/PF00076/PS50102, residues 105-182/107-168), belonging to the hnRNP A1/A2 family (IPR034845/IPR035979). The C-terminal region contains an hnRNP A1/A2-like domain (IPR021662/PF11627, residues 255-292) characteristic of low-complexity prion-like domains involved in phase separation and protein-protein interactions. GO annotations include RNA binding (GO:0003723), nucleic acid binding (GO:0003676), mRNA processing, splicing, and transport. The confident AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 70.2) confirms stable RRM folds optimized for single-stranded RNA recognition.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places HNRNPA1L2 within the mRNA splicing and transport network: strongest interactions with RAB1A (0.623, Ras-related protein Rab-1A, vesicular trafficking), TRA2B (0.622, Transformer-2 protein homolog beta, splicing regulator), HNRNPF (0.543, heterogeneous nuclear ribonucleoprotein F), CSTF2T (0.524, cleavage stimulation factor subunit 2 testis-specific), ALYREF (0.523, THO complex subunit 4, mRNA export adaptor), SRSF2 (0.517, serine/arginine-rich splicing factor 2), HNRNPH1 (0.515), TNPO1 (0.510, transportin-1, nuclear import receptor), TRA2A (0.442), and HNRNPH3 (0.441). The TRA2B-SRSF2-HNRNPF-HNRNPH1 cluster confirms HNRNPA1L2 operates within the spliceosomal machinery, potentially regulating alternative splicing. ALYREF and TNPO1 partnerships link HNRNPA1L2 to mRNA nuclear export and import cycles.\n\nHPA expression shows low tissue specificity with detection in all tissues, indicating ubiquitous housekeeping expression consistent with fundamental roles in gene expression. Low cancer and immune cell specificities suggest broad constitutive function. Subcellular localization in the nucleoplasm confirms nuclear function in transcription and splicing. Classification as an RNA-binding ribonucleoprotein confirms its role in mRNA metabolism.\n\nClinVar documents 59 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number losses and gains across chromosome 13q (e.g., 13q13.1-21.32 del, 13q11-31.3 dup). The high CNV burden indicates extreme dosage sensitivity, where haploinsufficiency or overexpression likely causes developmental disorders, intellectual disability, or cancer predisposition through global disruption of mRNA splicing and transport.\n\nConservation is strong across vertebrates: human (Q32P51, 320 aa), mouse (B7ZWG9, 275 aa), indicating evolutionary conservation of splicing functions.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: HNRNPA1L2 functions as a dual-RRM RNA-binding protein that recognizes specific AU-rich or UAGGG(A/U) sequences in pre-mRNAs, recruiting spliceosomal components (TRA2B, SRSF2) to regulate exon inclusion/skipping. The C-terminal low-complexity domain (residues 255-292) likely mediates liquid-liquid phase separation, concentrating splicing factors in nuclear speckles. Interaction with ALYREF and TNPO1 suggests HNRNPA1L2 escorts mature mRNAs from the nucleus to the cytoplasm, coupling splicing completion with export. RAB1A partnership (0.623) may link mRNA processing to vesicular trafficking pathways. The ubiquitous expression pattern implies HNRNPA1L2 is essential for basal cellular function, explaining the severe phenotypic consequences of 13q CNVs. Dysregulation of HNRNPA1L2 likely contributes to neurodegeneration or cancer via aberrant splicing of key regulatory transcripts. HNRNPA1L2 represents a critical hub linking RNA recognition, spliceosome assembly, and nucleocytoplasmic transport.
