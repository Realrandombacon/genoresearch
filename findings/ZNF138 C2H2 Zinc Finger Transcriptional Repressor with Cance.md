# ZNF138: C2H2 Zinc Finger Transcriptional Repressor with Cancer-Enhanced Expression and Clinical Variant Burden

## Description
ZNF138 (Zinc Finger Protein 138) is a 262-amino acid C2H2-type zinc finger transcription factor encoded on chromosome 7q11.21 (UniProt P52744). InterPro analysis reveals three canonical C2H2 zinc finger motifs (IPR013087, PF00096, PF13912) spanning residues 109-242, characteristic of the Krueppel C2H2-type zinc-finger protein family (PTHR24390). These zinc finger domains adopt a beta-beta-alpha fold (G3DSA:3.30.160.60) that coordinates zinc ions for sequence-specific DNA binding in the major groove. Human Protein Atlas confirms ubiquitous expression across all tissues with low tissue specificity, nuclear localization, and classification as a DNA-binding transcriptional repressor. Notably, ZNF138 shows cancer-enhanced expression, suggesting potential oncogenic or tumor-suppressive roles depending on cellular context. ClinVar documents 17 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants encompassing ZNF138, including large-scale copy number gains and losses on chromosome 7 (7p22.3-q36.3 and 7q11.21-11.22 regions), single nucleotide variants, and a TMEM106B-BRAF fusion deletion, indicating dosage sensitivity and potential involvement in chromosomal rearrangement disorders. The C2H2 zinc finger architecture strongly suggests ZNF138 functions as a sequence-specific DNA-binding repressor that recruits corepressor complexes to silence target genes through chromatin modification. Given ubiquitous expression, cancer-enhanced expression pattern, and extensive clinical variant burden, ZNF138 likely regulates fundamental cellular processes such as cell cycle control or differentiation, with haploinsufficiency or overexpression contributing to developmental phenotypes and potentially cancer progression through dysregulation of downstream target gene networks.
