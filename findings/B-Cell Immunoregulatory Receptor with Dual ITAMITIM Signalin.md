## Description
FCRL2 (Fc receptor-like protein 2, CD307b) is a 508 amino acid type I transmembrane receptor of the immunoglobulin superfamily expressed predominantly on B cells. The extracellular region contains four C2-type immunoglobulin domains (IPR003598, IPR003599, positions 23-387) that likely mediate ligand binding or receptor dimerization. The cytoplasmic tail harbors one immunoreceptor tyrosine-based activation motif (ITAM) and two immunoreceptor tyrosine-based inhibitory motifs (ITIMs), enabling bidirectional signaling regulation. AlphaFold prediction shows high structural confidence (pLDDT 80.8), indicating a well-folded receptor architecture. HPA data reveals group-enriched expression in lymphoid tissue (33.2 nTPM) and intestine (19.5 nTPM), with blood cell specificity confirming B-cell restriction. STRING interaction network identifies 10 partners in B-cell receptor signaling: PIGR (0.756), CD20 marker MS4A1 (0.673), related family member FCRL1 (0.663), complement regulator CD5L (0.630), Fc gamma receptors FCGR2A (0.628) and FCGR2B (0.547), B-cell co-receptor CD19 (0.561), and sialic acid-binding SIGLEC5 (0.581). ClinVar reports 11 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variants though specific disease associations remain undefined. Literature (66 papers) establishes FCRL2 as a novel prognostic marker in chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL) that refines risk stratification based on IGHV mutation status; ligation by monoclonal antibodies downregulates BCR-mediated signaling, suggesting therapeutic potential. FCRL2 likely functions as a rheostat for B-cell activation, balancing activating and inhibitory signals through differential phosphorylation of ITAM versus ITIM motifs, thereby modulating BCR signal strength, B-cell tolerance, and malignant transformation in CLL.
