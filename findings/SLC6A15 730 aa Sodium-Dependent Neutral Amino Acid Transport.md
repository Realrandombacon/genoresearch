## Description
SLC6A15 (Q9H2J7, 730 aa), also known as B(0)AT2 (Sodium-dependent Neutral Amino Acid Transporter B(0)AT2), NTT73, SBAT1, or V7-3, is a sodium-dependent symporter belonging to solute carrier family 6 (neurotransmitter:sodium symporter family, NSS). InterPro identifies comprehensive NSS transporter architecture: sodium:neurotransmitter symporter family (IPR000175, PF00209, aa 45-646), neutral amino acid SLC6 transporter (IPR002438, aa 196-208/209-220/358-368), B(0)AT2 solute-binding domain (IPR042934, cd11522, aa 61-640), NSS superfamily (IPR037272, aa 61-641), sodium-dependent transporter (NF037979, aa 64-538), and conserved signatures (PS00610 aa 85-99, PS00754 aa 168-188, PS50267). AlphaFold predicts high-confidence structure (pLDDT 79.7, residues 1-730) consistent with canonical 12-transmembrane helix NSS fold with substrate-binding site in membrane core. HPA shows brain-enriched expression (retina 14.6 nTPM, brain 13.0 nTPM, tissue enhanced); localized to nucleoli and vesicles; classified as metabolic protein, transporter involved in amino-acid transport, ion transport, neurotransmitter transport, sodium transport, and symport. Functional hypothesis: SLC6A15/B(0)AT2 functions as a sodium-coupled symporter mediating cellular uptake of neutral amino acids (branched-chain amino acids: leucine, isoleucine, valine; also methionine, phenylalanine) across neuronal plasma membranes via alternating access mechanism. The 12-TM NSS fold creates a central substrate-binding site with conserved sodium-binding residues (PS00610, PS00754 signatures) enabling Na+-coupled transport (symport). Brain (13.0 nTPM) and retina (14.6 nTPM) enrichment reflects critical role in neuronal amino acid homeostasis, neurotransmitter precursor supply, and synaptic function. Strong SLC7 family interactions (SLC7A6: 0.468, SLC7A8: 0.462) indicate coordination with cationic/neutral amino acid exchangers. SLC1A4 interaction (0.420) connects to alanine/serine/cysteine transport. Nucleolar localization suggests role in ribosomal biogenesis via amino acid supply. Pathogenic CNVs disrupt neuronal amino acid transport causing susceptibility to major depression (PMID: 21521612), altered stress response, and potential neurodevelopmental disorders. Conservation across vertebrates (mouse, rat, primate orthologs 712-730 aa) confirms essential neuronal role.
