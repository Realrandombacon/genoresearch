## Description
TRMT13 (tRNA methyltransferase 13 homolog, UniProt Q9NUP7) is a 481-amino acid nuclear enzyme located on chromosome 1p22.1. InterPro analysis reveals a multi-domain architecture characteristic of tRNA modification enzymes: an N-terminal CCCH-type zinc finger (IPR021721/PF11722, residues 17-45) for RNA binding, a central CHHC-type zinc finger (IPR022776/PF05253, residues 56-83) structurally related to U11-48K spliceosomal proteins, and a C-terminal methyltransferase catalytic domain (IPR007871/PF05206, residues 165-469) responsible for S-adenosylmethionine-dependent methylation. AlphaFold structure prediction achieves high confidence (pLDDT 79.2), indicating a well-folded enzyme with defined catalytic pocket. Human Protein Atlas shows ubiquitous expression across all tissues with low tissue specificity and subcellular localization to nucleoplasm, nuclear bodies, and cytosol, consistent with roles in both nuclear tRNA processing and cytoplasmic translation quality control. STRING interaction network reveals 10 partners exclusively comprising tRNA modification enzymes: TRMT44 (0.597), TRMT61A (0.565), TRMT11 (0.518), CMTR2 (0.491), DIMT1 (0.415), RTCA (0.574), and LRRC39 (0.609), placing TRMT13 within a coordinated tRNA epitranscriptomic machinery. ClinVar contains 9 pathogenic variants including copy-number losses and gains across 1p21-q44 and a synonymous SNV (c.471C>T, p.Gly157=), suggesting haploinsufficiency or overexpression disrupts tRNA modification homeostasis. Conservation across mouse (Q8BYH3, 481 aa) and Xenopus (A0A8J1MV26, 485 aa) indicates ancient evolutionary origin. TRMT13 likely catalyzes N4-methylcytidine (m4C) or related modifications at specific tRNA positions, affecting codon recognition efficiency and translational fidelity. Disruption may cause ribosome stalling, proteotoxic stress, and developmental anomalies through impaired protein synthesis.
