## Description
WDR13 (Q9H1Z4, 485 aa), also known as MG21, is an X-linked member of the WD repeat protein family functioning as a signal transduction regulator. InterPro identifies a canonical beta-propeller architecture composed of multiple WD40 repeats (IPR001680, PF00400, SM00320, PS50082, aa 160-201, 206-289, 392-437, 446-480) forming a stable seven-bladed structure typical for protein-protein interaction hubs. The protein belongs to the WD40/YVTN repeat-like superfamily (IPR015943, G3DSA:2.130.10.10, aa 161-485) and the specific WD repeat-containing signal transduction regulator family (IPR051350, PTHR22838, aa 120-481). AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (pLDDT 80.0, residues 1-485), indicating a rigid, well-folded single-domain propeller suitable for scaffolding multiprotein complexes. HPA shows low tissue specificity with expression detected in all tissues; localized to the nucleoplasm, plasma membrane, and centriolar satellites, suggesting roles in both nuclear signaling and cytoskeletal organization. STRING reveals 10 interactions linking it to diverse pathways: TBC1D25 (0.644, Rab-GAP involved in endocytosis), FAM3A (0.543), WDR45 (0.528, beta-propeller protein linked to neurodegeneration), JUN (0.506, AP-1 transcription factor), TRAPPC5 (0.506, trafficking complex), GATA1 (0.497, erythroid transcription factor), B3GAT3 (0.482), ZNF182 (0.471), FTSJ1 (0.470, rRNA methyltransferase), and EBPL (0.458). The connection to JUN and GATA1 suggests a role in modulating transcriptional responses, while links to TBC1D25 and TRAPPC5 imply involvement in vesicular trafficking. ClinVar contains 162 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number losses (chrX:48286848-48520104, chrX:15392463-48777470, chrX:36355238-54106257) and gains (chrX:2757837-156030895, chrX:48224455-52832596) affecting the Xp11.23 region, plus deletions, indicating strong dosage sensitivity; although no specific monogenic disease is explicitly named, the location and variant burden suggest potential contributions to X-linked intellectual disability or developmental disorders. Functional hypothesis: WDR13 functions as a versatile scaffold protein that integrates signals between the nucleus and cytoplasm, potentially regulating the stability or activity of transcription factors like JUN and GATA1, or facilitating vesicle trafficking via interactions with TBC1D25 and TRAPPC5. Its localization to centriolar satellites hints at a role in ciliogenesis or centrosome function, consistent with other WD40 proteins. The X-linked nature and high number of CNVs suggest that haploinsufficiency or overexpression disrupts critical signaling nodes, leading to developmental phenotypes. Conservation across mammals (mouse 485 aa, chimp 485 aa) confirms essential function.
