## Description
APOLD1 (Apolipoprotein L Domain-Containing Protein 1, UniProt Q96LR9) is a 279 aa endothelial cell early response protein functioning as a modulator of endothelial barrier permeability, required for proper organization of endothelial cell-cell junctions and cytoskeleton through its conserved apolipoprotein L domain. Structurally, AlphaFold predicts a partially ordered architecture (pLDDT=60.2) featuring a canonical apolipoprotein L domain (IPR008405/PF05461, aa 46-256/61-139) within the apolipoprotein L family (PTHR14096, aa 46-256), enabling lipid binding (GO:0008289), lipid transport (GO:0006869), and lipoprotein metabolic process (GO:0042157) characteristic of apolipoprotein family members. Functionally, STRING interactions reveal coupling to vascular and signaling machinery: DNA repair helicase FANCM (0.532), fibroblast growth factors FGF13 (0.513) and FGF2 (0.452) mediating angiogenesis, transcriptional regulator ZNF516 (0.463), G-protein coupled receptor GPRC5A (0.447), cell adhesion molecule CDH4 (0.409), and various membrane transporters. HPA shows tissue-enhanced expression with adipose tissue highest at 116.6 nTPM, cancer-enhanced specificity, nucleoplasmic and plasma membrane localization, classified as predicted membrane protein with disease variant involvement. ClinVar identifies 47 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number gains at 12p13.33-11.1 (chr12:173787-34835837), chr12:64621-34650483, chr12:82453-49847230, splice variant c.96+1G>A, and indel p.Arg49Tyr, though no specific Mendelian disease is annotated - these CNVs overlap with vascular bleeding disorder and endothelial dysfunction regions. Literature (44 papers) reveals emerging mechanistic insights: APOLD1 loss causes endothelial dysfunction involving cell junctions, cytoskeletal architecture, and Weibel-Palade bodies while disrupting hemostasis (2022-2023, 24 citations each), is dispensable for normal development but controls angiogenesis under pathological conditions (2022, 24 citations), associates with increased arterial thrombosis in vivo (2019, 13 citations), and represents a potential new vascular bleeding disorder (2022, 3 citations). The gene represents a 'semi-dark' vascular regulator where canonical endothelial barrier function is established, but specific mechanisms linking APOLD1-mediated junction organization to lipid transport functions, tissue-specific vulnerability in adipose and vascular beds, and therapeutic targeting for pathological angiogenesis and thrombosis remain underexplored.
