## Description
NT5DC1 (5'-Nucleotidase Domain-Containing Protein 1; UniProt Q5TFE4) is a 455 amino acid HAD-superfamily hydrolase with only 16 publications, classifying it as a genuine dark gene despite essential roles in nucleotide metabolism, cancer prognosis, and respiratory disease susceptibility.\n\nSTRUCTURAL ARCHITECTURE: InterPro analysis reveals canonical HAD (haloacid dehalogenase) superfamily hydrolase architecture. The protein contains the 5'-nucleotidase family domain (IPR008380/PF05761, residues 3-343/4-339) with catalytic hydrolase activity (GO:0016787). The core catalytic domain adopts the HAD-like fold (IPR023214/IPR036412/G3DSA:3.40.50.1000/SSF56784, residues 2-435/167-357), characteristic of phosphatases that dephosphorylate nucleotide monophosphates. The protein belongs to the 5'-nucleotidase domain-containing family (PTHR12103, residues 3-343). AlphaFold predicts high confidence structure (global pLDDT 88.4), indicating a well-folded enzymatic protein with stable catalytic core.\n\nINTERACTION NETWORK: STRING-DB identifies medium-confidence interactions with calcium homeostasis modulators and nucleotide metabolism proteins: CALHM4 (0.547, calcium homeostasis modulator 4), TSPYL4 (0.518, testis-specific Y-encoded-like protein 4, paralog co-associated in COPD SNPs), CALHM5 (0.499, calcium homeostasis modulator 5), OR6C65 (0.480, olfactory receptor), CCDC152 (0.475, coiled-coil domain containing 152), SEC23IP (0.469, SEC23 interacting protein), NT5DC4 (0.465, 5'-nucleotidase domain-containing 4, family paralog), CTXN2 (0.444, cortactin-binding protein 2), BCKDHB (0.437, branched-chain ketoacid dehydrogenase E1b, metabolic enzyme), and PKLR (0.435, pyruvate kinase L/R, glycolytic enzyme). This interaction profile suggests NT5DC1 functions in nucleotide metabolism linked to cellular energy status and calcium signaling.\n\nEXPRESSION & LOCALIZATION: HPA shows tissue-enhanced expression with dramatically elevated levels in choroid plexus (130.5 nTPM)—the highest expression of any tissue, suggesting specialized roles in cerebrospinal fluid production, blood-CSF barrier function, or brain nucleotide homeostasis. Expression is detected in all tissues with low cancer specificity. Subcellular localization includes nucleoplasm and cytosol, consistent with nucleotide pool regulation in both compartments. Molecular function is annotated as hydrolase activity. Classified as a predicted intracellular protein with cell type-enhanced single cell specificity.\n\nDISEASE LANDSCAPE: ClinVar contains 141 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants at 6q22.1-22.33, including copy number losses spanning this region encompassing NT5DC1, plus numerous coding variants. The chromosomal region 6q22 also contains COL10A1 (collagen X), and many ClinVar entries reference COL10A1 mutations—suggesting NT5DC1 variants may be co-inherited in 6q22 deletion syndromes. Recent 2025 literature reviews the NT5DC family (NT5DC1-4) as evolutionarily-conserved 5'-nucleotidases catalyzing intracellular nucleotide monophosphate dephosphorylation. 2023 studies establish NT5DC family prognostic significance in breast cancer and pancreatic adenocarcinoma, where expression correlates with immune infiltration and patient survival. A 2012 study identified NT5DC1 SNPs associated with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) susceptibility in Chinese Han populations, alongside TSPYL4 variants.\n\nCONSERVATION: Orthologs confirmed in mouse (Q8C5P5, 467 aa), bovine (Q2TBU5, 452 aa), and Xenopus laevis (Q6GN17, 455 aa), indicating strong evolutionary constraint on the HAD hydrolase architecture across vertebrates.\n\nFUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: NT5DC1 functions as a cytosolic/nucleoplasmic 5'-nucleotidase that dephosphorylates nucleotide monophosphates (NMPs) to nucleosides, regulating intracellular nucleotide pools for DNA/RNA synthesis and energy metabolism. The choroid plexus enrichment suggests specialized roles in CSF nucleotide homeostasis, potentially affecting neurogenesis or blood-CSF barrier integrity. In cancer contexts, NT5DC1 expression may influence tumor proliferation by modulating nucleotide availability for DNA replication—explaining its prognostic value in breast and pancreatic cancers. The CALHM4/5 interactions suggest potential coupling between nucleotide metabolism and calcium signaling, possibly affecting ion channel regulation. In COPD, NT5DC1 SNPs may alter nucleotide metabolism in airway epithelial cells, affecting inflammatory responses or mucociliary clearance. The 141 ClinVar variants at 6q22 warrant investigation for haploinsufficiency syndromes combining skeletal abnormalities (from COL10A1) with neurological phenotypes (from NT5DC1 choroid plexus dysfunction).
