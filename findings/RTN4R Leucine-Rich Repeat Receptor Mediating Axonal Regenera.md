## Description
RTN4R (UniProt Q9BZR6) encodes a 473 aa Leucine-Rich Repeat (LRR) transmembrane receptor functioning as the primary inhibitor of axonal regeneration in the CNS and a critical regulator of neuronal plasticity. InterPro analysis reveals a sophisticated multi-domain architecture optimized for protein-protein interactions: multiple LRR domains (aa 60-128, 156-213; IPR001611, IPR003591, PF13855, SM00369) forming a curved solenoid structure for ligand binding; a C-terminal cysteine-rich flanking region (aa 260-310, IPR000483, SM00082) stabilizing the LRR cap; and classification within the LRR and transmembrane domain-containing protein family (IPR050541, PTHR24369). The protein belongs to the Ribonuclease Inhibitor-like superfamily (G3DSA:3.80.10.10, SSF52058), indicating an evolutionarily conserved fold adapted for high-affinity ligand recognition. AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (global pLDDT 75.1), confirming a well-folded extracellular LRR domain capable of precise ligand discrimination. STRING interactions define RTN4R as a central hub in the axonal guidance network with extremely high confidence partners: RTN4/Nogo-A (0.999), MAG (0.999), NGFR/p75NTR (0.998), ANPEP (0.996), LINGO1 (0.995), TNFRSF19 (0.994), OMG (0.993), KIAA0319L (0.867), ADGRB1/BAI1 (0.820), and EMG1 (0.812), establishing its role as a co-receptor in the Nogo signaling complex. HPA expression shows brain-enriched specificity (82.6 nTPM) with plasma membrane, actin filament, and focal adhesion localization, consistent with synaptic and growth cone functions; disease involvement includes schizophrenia. ClinVar reports 210 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly copy number gains/losses on chromosome 22q11.21 (DiGeorge/velocardiofacial syndrome region), indicating dosage sensitivity and strong association with neurodevelopmental disorders. Literature (76 papers) highlights: (1) Axonal Regeneration—RTN4R binds Nogo-A, MAG, and OMG to inhibit neurite outgrowth via RhoA/ROCK pathway activation; (2) Schizophrenia—22q11.21 CNVs disrupting RTN4R dosage contribute to psychosis risk; (3) Spinal Cord Injury—RTN4R antagonism promotes axonal regeneration and functional recovery; (4) Obesity—recent evidence links RTN4R loss to hyperphagia. Functional hypothesis: RTN4R functions through: (1) Ligand Recognition—LRR domains form a curved surface binding myelin-derived inhibitors (Nogo-66, MAG, OMG) with high specificity; (2) Signal Transduction—complex assembly with LINGO1 and p75NTR/NGFR activates RhoA GTPase, collapsing growth cones and inhibiting regeneration; (3) Dosage Sensitivity—22q11.21 CNVs alter RTN4R expression, disrupting synaptic plasticity and contributing to schizophrenia pathogenesis. Mechanistic proposal: In CNS injury, myelin-derived ligands bind RTN4R's LRR domain, inducing conformational changes that recruit LINGO1 and p75NTR, activating RhoA/ROCK signaling to collapse actin cytoskeleton and halt axonal growth. In schizophrenia, RTN4R haploinsufficiency or overexpression disrupts synaptic pruning and dendritic spine maturation during development, leading to circuit dysfunction. The combination of a well-folded LRR structure, exclusive high-confidence interactions with myelin proteins, brain-enriched expression, massive clinical variant burden (210 variants), and therapeutic relevance in spinal cord injury positions RTN4R as a critical but under-characterized regulator of neuronal plasticity and a genuine dark gene requiring mechanistic dissection.
