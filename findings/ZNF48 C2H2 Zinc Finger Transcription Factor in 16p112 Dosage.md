## Description
ZNF48 (Gene ID: 197407, UniProt Q96MX3) encodes a 618 aa Krüppel-type C2H2 zinc finger transcription factor mapping to chromosome 16p11.2 (30,378,311-30,400,107 bp). Multiple evidence lines support classification as a DNA-binding transcriptional regulator with dosage sensitivity: (1) InterPro domains confirm canonical C2H2 zinc finger architecture: three C2H2-type zinc fingers (PF00096, aa 113-134, 140-162, 194-214; IPR013087, aa 112-139, 140-167, 192-219) within a beta-beta-alpha zinc finger fold (G3DSA:3.30.160.60)—C2H2 domains mediate sequence-specific DNA binding in major groove; (2) STRING interactions reveal connections to chromatin regulators: H1-1 (0.506, linker histone H1.1), KAT8 (0.483, MYST family histone acetyltransferase), RBM34 (0.491, RNA-binding protein), DDX27 (0.454, DEAD-box helicase)—suggesting roles in chromatin remodeling and transcriptional regulation; (3) HPA data shows ubiquitous expression across all tissues with mitochondrial localization (unexpected for transcription factor—may indicate dual localization or antibody cross-reactivity); (4) ClinVar contains 16 pathogenic copy number variants (large 16p11.2-13.3 deletions/duplications spanning 2-85 Mb), linking ZNF48 dosage to 16p11.2 copy number syndromes including developmental delay, autism spectrum disorder, and obesity. The 16p11.2 region is a well-known dosage-sensitive locus with ~25 genes. Mechanistic hypothesis: ZNF48 functions as a sequence-specific DNA-binding transcription factor that recruits chromatin-modifying complexes (KAT8 histone acetyltransferase, H1 linker histone) to regulate target gene expression. The C2H2 zinc fingers provide modular DNA recognition—each finger recognizes 3-4 bp, enabling combinatorial specificity. Interaction with KAT8 suggests ZNF48 may recruit H4K16 acetylation machinery to activate transcription. Ubiquitous expression indicates housekeeping transcriptional role, while 16p11.2 location raises possibility of co-regulation with neighboring dosage-sensitive genes. Dosage sensitivity (16 CNVs) indicates stoichiometric balance critical for transcriptional networks—haploinsufficiency or overexpression disrupts target gene regulation, contributing to neurodevelopmental phenotypes. This represents a "dark" transcription factor awaiting target gene identification and structural characterization.
