## Description
CCDC178 (Gene ID: 374864, UniProt Q5BJE1) is an 867 amino acid coiled-coil domain-containing protein mapping to chromosome 18q12.1 (32,937,405-33,441,039 bp, ~504 kb genomic span on reverse strand). InterPro analysis reveals membership in coiled-coil domain-containing protein 178 family (IPR038826, PTHR35088) spanning residues 1-866, characteristic of alpha-helical coiled-coil structural motifs (heptad repeat pattern a-b-c-d-e-f-g with hydrophobic residues at a/d positions) that mediate protein-protein interactions and oligomerization. Gene ontology annotation localizes CCDC178 to ciliary basal body - the microtubule-organizing center at the base of primary cilia that templates axoneme assembly and anchors cilia to the cell. HPA expression data shows tissue-enhanced RNA expression with highest levels in testis (14.5 nTPM), adipose tissue (10.3 nTPM), and ovary (9.4 nTPM), consistent with ciliary function in reproductive tissues and metabolic regulation. STRING interactions reveal 8 protein partners with medium confidence scores (0.403-0.507): C18orf21 (0.507, chromosome 18 open reading frame 21, uncharacterized), CCDC8 (0.450, coiled-coil domain-containing protein 8, 3M syndrome, ubiquitin ligase complex), GAREM1 (0.448, GRB2-associated regulator of MAPK1 subtype 1, ERK signaling scaffold), CCDC167 (0.448, coiled-coil domain-containing protein 167, ciliary function), OR5H1 (0.448, olfactory receptor family 5 subfamily H member 1, GPCR), CCDC106 (0.422, coiled-coil domain-containing protein 106), RESF1 (0.406, retroviral element silencing factor 1, chromatin silencing), and CCDC34 (0.403, coiled-coil domain-containing protein 34, meiotic spindle). ClinVar contains 44 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variants spanning 18q11.1-23, 18q12.1-12.2, 18q11.2-12.1, and 18p11.32-q23 regions (deletions 20-38 Mb, duplications 19-80 Mb whole arm), associated with 18q deletion/duplication syndromes (18q- syndrome) featuring intellectual disability, developmental delay, growth abnormalities, hypotonia, cardiac defects, and dysmorphic features. The 18q12.1 region contains CCDC178 and neighboring genes including SMAD4 (mothers against decapentapedic homolog 4, TGF-beta signaling, myxoid liposarcoma), DCC (deleted in colorectal carcinoma, netrin-1 receptor, axon guidance), and TGIF1 (TG-interacting factor 1, transcriptional corepressor). Functional hypothesis: CCDC178 functions as a structural scaffold protein at the ciliary basal body that mediates protein-protein interactions via coiled-coil dimerization/oligomerization domains. Basal body localization suggests roles in: (1) ciliogenesis - templating microtubule nucleation for axoneme assembly, (2) ciliary anchoring - tethering cilia to the apical membrane via distal/subdistal appendages, (3) ciliary gate formation - establishing diffusion barrier between ciliary and cytoplasmic compartments, (4) vesicle trafficking - docking of ciliary transport vesicles (ectosomes/exosomes). Testis-enriched expression aligns with critical ciliary functions in spermatogenesis (sperm flagella are modified cilia). Adipose/ovary expression suggests metabolic and reproductive roles. Interaction with CCDC8 (3M syndrome ubiquitin ligase substrate) suggests potential regulation by ubiquitination. GAREM1 interaction indicates potential crosstalk with ERK/MAPK signaling at the basal body. Pathogenic 18q CNVs disrupt CCDC178 dosage alongside SMAD4, DCC, and TGIF1, contributing to 18q- syndrome phenotypes through combined haploinsufficiency of ciliary structural proteins, TGF-beta signaling components, and transcriptional regulators. Ciliary dysfunction may contribute to neurodevelopmental phenotypes (primary cilia are critical for Sonic hedgehog signaling in neural tube patterning) and metabolic abnormalities (cilia regulate leptin/insulin signaling in hypothalamus and adipocytes). This represents a "dark gene" where subcellular localization and interaction data suggest clear cellular role (basal body scaffold), but specific binding partners, post-translational modifications, and downstream pathways remain uncharacterized.
