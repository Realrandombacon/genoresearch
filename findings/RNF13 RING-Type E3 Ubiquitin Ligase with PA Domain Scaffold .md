# RNF13: RING-Type E3 Ubiquitin Ligase with PA Domain Scaffold Function in Vesicular Trafficking and Neurological Disease

**Date:** 2026-03-23T03:54:04.733789

**Quality Score:** 3.48/10  (E=5.15, D=1.8) [WEAK]

## Description
RNF13 encodes a 381 amino acid E3 ubiquitin-protein ligase (UniProt O43567) containing two critical functional domains: an N-terminal PA domain (residues 66-158, PF02225, IPR003137) and a C-terminal RING-type zinc finger (residues 239-282, PF13639, IPR001841). The PA domain mediates membrane association and vesicular sorting, while the RING finger (C3HC4 motif) coordinates zinc and catalyzes ubiquitin transfer from E2 conjugating enzymes to substrate proteins.\n\nSTRING interaction network reveals RNF13 functions within the ubiquitin-proteasome system with high-confidence partners: UBE2D2 (0.930), UBB (0.907), UBC (0.904), RPS27A (0.901), and UBA52 (0.900) — all core ubiquitin machinery components. Additional interactions with AP3S1 (0.783), AP3D1 (0.666), and CD63 (0.698) link RNF13 to adaptor protein 3 (AP-3) complex-mediated vesicular trafficking to lysosomes and melanosomes.\n\nHPA data shows ubiquitous expression across all tissues (low tissue specificity) with subcellular localization in nucleoplasm, vesicles, and cytosol. ClinVar documents 46 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants associated with developmental and epileptic encephalopathy (DEE73/EIEE73), indicating critical neurological function. AlphaFold structure prediction (pLDDT 74.4) confirms a well-folded protein with stable domain architecture.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: RNF13 serves as a dual-function protein coupling ubiquitination to endolysosomal sorting. The PA domain anchors RNF13 to vesicular membranes where it ubiquitinates cargo proteins (potentially neurotransmitter receptors or ion channels), marking them for AP-3-dependent lysosomal degradation. Loss-of-function variants disrupt this quality control pathway, causing accumulation of toxic substrates in neurons and triggering epileptogenesis.
