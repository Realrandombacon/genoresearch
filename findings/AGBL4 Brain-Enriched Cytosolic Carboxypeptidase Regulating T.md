# AGBL4: Brain-Enriched Cytosolic Carboxypeptidase Regulating Tubulin Polyglutamylation and Glioblastoma Progression

## Description
AGBL4 (AGBL Carboxypeptidase 4, also known as Cytosolic Carboxypeptidase 6 or CCP6, UniProt Q5VU57) is a 503 aa metallocarboxypeptidase belonging to the peptidase M14 family that functions in post-translational modification of tubulin and other cytoskeletal proteins through deglutamylation. The molecular mechanism involves zinc-dependent carboxypeptidase activity (IPR000834/PF00246, positions 167-438) with an N-terminal regulatory domain (IPR040626/PF18027, positions 47-134) that removes polyglutamate side chains from tubulin, regulating microtubule stability and dynamics. Literature (29 papers) establishes AGBL4 roles in: (1) AGBL4 promoting malignant progression of glioblastoma via modulation of MMP-1 and inflammatory pathways (2024, 3 citations), (2) AGBL4 genetic polymorphisms associated with antituberculosis drug-induced hepatotoxicity susceptibility in Chinese Han population (2019, 7 citations), (3) cytosolic carboxypeptidase family members regulating tubulin polyglutamylation critical for neuronal function and cilia biology. HPA confirms brain-enriched expression (4.5 nTPM in brain, 4.4 nTPM in retina, 3.5 nTPM in choroid plexus) with tissue-enhanced specificity, consistent with neuronal tubulin regulation function. STRING interactions reveal tubulin modification network: BEND5 (0.871), TTLL4 (0.808), TTLL6 (0.741), TTLL1 (0.466), TTLL7 (0.418) - all tubulin tyrosine ligase-like enzymes that add glutamate chains, positioning AGBL4 as the counterbalancing deglutamylase. Additional interactions with complement system (CFH 0.697, C3 0.610) and innate immunity (CGAS 0.631) suggest broader roles. ClinVar contains 9 pathogenic variants including copy number gains/losses at 1p34-32, though specific disease phenotypes remain undefined. AlphaFold shows high-confidence structure (pLDDT 87.0) with well-folded catalytic domain. Conservation: mouse Agbl4 (Q09LZ8) is 540 aa with high sequence identity. Functional hypothesis: AGBL4 regulates microtubule dynamics in neurons and glial cells by removing polyglutamate modifications, with dysregulation contributing to glioblastoma invasion through MMP-1 upregulation and altered cell migration. The brain-specific expression pattern and interaction with TTLL family enzymes suggests AGBL4 is critical for neuronal microtubule homeostasis, cilia function, and potentially neurodevelopmental processes.
