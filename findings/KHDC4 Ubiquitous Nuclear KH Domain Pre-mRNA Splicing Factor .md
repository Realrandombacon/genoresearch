# KHDC4: Ubiquitous Nuclear KH Domain Pre-mRNA Splicing Factor Regulating Splice Site Recognition via LUC7L3-CDC5L-SF1 Spliceosome Interaction Network

## Description
KHDC4 (KH Domain Containing 4, also BLOM7 or KIAA0907) is a 614 amino acid nuclear protein (UniProt Q7Z7F0) with a partially structured conformation (AlphaFold pLDDT 62.2, medium confidence), functioning as a pre-mRNA splicing factor that recognizes splice sites through its K homology (KH) RNA-binding domains, playing critical roles in mRNA processing and spliceosome assembly.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals KHDC4 contains two canonical Type I KH RNA-binding domains (IPR047890/cd22385, residues 99-182; IPR047889/cd22386, residues 229-330) belonging to the KH Domain Type 1 superfamily (IPR036612/G3DSA:3.30.1370.10, residues 101-191/230-332). These domains adopt a βααββα fold that binds single-stranded RNA sequences, typically recognizing 4-nucleotide motifs. KHDC4 belongs to the RIK/BLOM7 family (IPR031121, residues 7-575) and shares homology with ATP-dependent RNA helicase PRP5/DDX46 KH domains (IPR056149, residues 104-187), suggesting functional connections to spliceosome remodeling. The medium-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 62.2) reflects ordered KH domains connected by flexible linkers, characteristic of RNA-binding proteins that require conformational adaptability for diverse RNA targets.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places KHDC4 within the core spliceosome machinery: strongest interactions with LUC7L3 (0.623, LUC7-like 3, U1 snRNP component regulating 5' splice site selection), CDC5L (0.606, cell division cycle 5-like, Prp19 complex scaffold essential for spliceosome activation), SF1 (0.573, splicing factor 1, branch point recognition protein), STARD7 (0.502, StAR-related lipid transfer domain 7), PUF60 (0.499, poly(U)-binding splicing factor 60, 3' splice site recognition), FUBP3 (0.485, far upstream element-binding protein 3, RNA-binding), YAE1 (0.474), ZNF529 (0.474, zinc finger protein), PRPF19 (0.427, pre-mRNA processing factor 19, Prp19 complex component), and LUC7L (0.425). The LUC7L3-CDC5L-SF1-PRPF19 network represents the core spliceosome assembly pathway, with LUC7L3/SF1 recognizing splice sites and CDC5L/PRPF19 catalyzing spliceosome activation.\n\nHPA expression shows ubiquitous detection in all tissues with low tissue specificity, consistent with a housekeeping splicing factor required for constitutive splicing in all cell types. Subcellular localization is exclusively nucleoplasmic, where spliceosome assembly occurs. The protein is classified under mRNA processing, mRNA splicing, and RNA-binding functions.\n\nClinVar documents 17 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including one splice site variant (c.1553+5G>C) and multiple chromosome 1q21.1-23.1 copy number gains and losses. The CNV burden suggests dosage sensitivity, with haploinsufficiency potentially causing splicing defects leading to neurodevelopmental disorders, as 1q21.1 microdeletion/duplication syndromes are associated with intellectual disability and autism.\n\nConservation is strong across vertebrates: mouse (Q3TCX3, 612 aa), zebrafish (Q6NZ18, 570 aa), Xenopus tropicalis (A0JM64, 612 aa), and Xenopus laevis (Q2NLB0, 585 aa), indicating ancient evolutionary conservation of splicing machinery from fish to mammals.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: KHDC4 functions as a splice site recognition factor that binds pre-mRNA through its tandem KH domains, positioning the spliceosome at correct exon-intron boundaries. The two KH domains may recognize bipartite RNA motifs flanking splice sites or cooperate to increase binding affinity and specificity. LUC7L3 interaction suggests KHDC4 participates in 5' splice site selection, potentially stabilizing U1 snRNP binding. CDC5L and PRPF19 partnerships indicate KHDC4 recruits or stabilizes the Prp19 complex during spliceosome activation, bridging early recognition and catalytic steps. SF1 interaction implies KHDC4 cooperates with branch point recognition, coordinating 5' and 3' splice site pairing. PUF60 linkage suggests roles in 3' splice site selection and exon definition. The medium-confidence structure (pLDDT 62.2) reflects flexible linkers between KH domains enabling adaptation to diverse RNA sequences and spacing requirements. The c.1553+5G>C splice variant may disrupt KHDC4's own splicing, causing haploinsufficiency. CNV-mediated dosage changes alter splice site selection fidelity, causing widespread splicing errors particularly in neurons where alternative splicing is extensive, contributing to 1q21.1 syndrome phenotypes including intellectual disability, developmental delay, and autism spectrum disorders.
