## Description
UHMK1 encodes a 419 amino acid serine/threonine-protein kinase Kist (UniProt Q8TAS1), also known as U2AF homology motif kinase 1, which is the ONLY kinase in the human genome containing a U2AF homology motif (UHM)—a protein interaction domain characteristic of splicing factors. This unique domain architecture enables UHMK1 to bridge phosphorylation signaling with RNA processing regulation. The protein contains an N-terminal catalytic kinase domain (cd14020 at positions 22-308; IPR000719/PF00069 at positions 23-304) with canonical serine/threonine kinase activity (GO:0004674), and a C-terminal RNA recognition motif (RRM)/UHM domain (IPR000504/PF00076 at positions 320-402) that mediates protein-protein interactions with splicing factors through tryptophan-binding pockets rather than RNA binding per se. The kinase domain adopts a transferase fold (G3DSA:1.10.510.10 at positions 111-309; G3DSA:3.30.200.20 at positions 20-110), while the UHM domain belongs to the nucleotide-binding alpha-beta plait superfamily (IPR012677 at positions 316-416; G3DSA:3.30.70.330).\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals UHMK1 forms a functional network connecting microtubule dynamics, cell cycle control, and splicing regulation: STMN1 (score 0.917—stathmin 1, microtubule destabilizer and direct UHMK1 substrate), CDKN1B (0.818—p27Kip1, cell cycle inhibitor), KCNIP4/2/1 (0.802/0.768/0.761—kv channel-interacting proteins, calcium signaling modulators), KCND3 (0.700—voltage-gated potassium channel), PRPF40A (0.583—pre-mRNA processing factor 40A, splicing factor), SF3B1 (0.565—splicing factor 3b subunit 1, core spliceosome component), KCNIP3 (0.446), and KCNC1 (0.426—potassium voltage-gated channel). This interaction profile confirms UHMK1's dual role: phosphorylation of STMN1 regulates microtubule dynamics during cell division, while UHM-mediated interactions with PRPF40A/SF3B1 position UHMK1 within the spliceosome regulatory network.\n\nHPA expression data classifies UHMK1 as detected in all tissues with low tissue specificity and low cell type specificity. Subcellular localization is nucleoplasmic, consistent with roles in both transcription-coupled splicing and nuclear cell cycle regulation. Protein class includes enzymes and predicted intracellular proteins, with molecular functions as kinase, RNA-binding, serine/threonine-protein kinase, and transferase.\n\nClinVar documents 18 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number gains (1q21.1-25.3 duplications spanning 42 Mb; 1q21.1-44 gains spanning 105 Mb; whole chromosome 1q duplications), copy number losses (1q23.1-23.3 deletions; 1q23.3 losses; 1q23.3-24.3 deletions; 1q23.2-24.1 losses; 1q23.1-24.2 deletions; 1q23.2-25.1 losses), and complex rearrangements (NC_000001.10:g.(?_130980840)_(248900000_?)dup). Critically, ClinVar lists 0 associated diseases—a major knowledge gap given essential kinase/splicing regulator function and substantial variant burden. This suggests UHMK1 dysfunction may cause undiagnosed developmental disorders (splicing defects affecting multiple tissues), neurodevelopmental conditions (given potassium channel interactions), cancer predisposition (cell cycle dysregulation via CDKN1B/STMN1), or cardiac arrhythmias (KCNIP/KCND interactions).\n\nAlphaFold structure prediction (AF-Q8TAS1-F1) yields a global pLDDT score of 89.8 (high confidence) across all 419 residues, indicating a well-folded bi-domain structure with stable kinase catalytic core and structured UHM domain suitable for substrate recognition and protein-protein interactions.\n\nLiterature analysis (60 papers) reveals emerging functional understanding with critical disease gaps. Recent 2025 studies demonstrate UHMK1 promotes prostate cancer progression through positive feedback loop with MTHFD2 (mitochondrial folate metabolism enzyme), establishing oncogenic signaling axis. Landmark 2023 study (cited 11x) definitively characterizes UHMK1 as a novel splicing regulatory kinase—the only kinase with UHM domain—showing direct interaction with splicing factors through UHM and regulation of alternative splicing programs. Recent 2026 studies show UHMK1 regulates vasculogenic mimicry formation in oral squamous cell carcinoma via STMN1 interaction, and EIF4A3-induced circEIF2S2 facilitates colorectal cancer growth/metastasis/immune suppression via miR-646/UHMK1 axis. Earlier work defines roles in lung adenocarcinoma via PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling. However, no Mendelian disease phenotype has been defined despite 18 pathogenic variants. Key unknowns include: specific disease phenotypes from germline loss-of-function mutations, contribution to neurodevelopmental disorders (given splicing factor role), precise substrate specificity beyond STMN1, role in cardiac electrophysiology (KCNIP interactions), and therapeutic potential for cancers with UHMK1-dependent splicing reprogramming.
