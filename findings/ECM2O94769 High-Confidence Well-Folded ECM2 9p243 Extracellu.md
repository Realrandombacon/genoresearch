## Description
ECM2 (extracellular matrix protein 2) is a 699 amino acid secreted/extracellular protein (UniProt O94769) with high-confidence well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 71.3) containing canonical extracellular matrix adaptor architecture including von Willebrand factor type C (VWFC) domain (IPR001007/PF00093, residues 101-158/103-157) forming a ~55-57 aa module (~8%) defining ECM2 VWFC classification with protein binding activity, multiple Leucine-rich repeats (LRRs) (IPR001611/IPR032675/PF13855/PF12799, residues 343-405/486-557/609-646) forming a curved solenoid structure (~200+ aa, ~29%) for protein-protein interactions, Extracellular matrix protein 2 family (IPR043184/PTHR43088, residues 1-699) encompassing the full length, Ribonuclease Inhibitor-like superfamily (G3DSA:3.80.10.10, residues 315-550) indicating structural homology to LRR-containing inhibitors, and molecular functions including matrix assembly promotion, cell adhesiveness modulation, extracellular localization, tissue distribution (data not available in HPA, likely restricted to connective tissues), predicted extracellular proteins classification, functioning as a large high-confidence well-folded extracellular scaffold that regulates matrix organization and cell-matrix communication through VWFC-mediated dimerization or ligand binding, LRR-mediated partner recruitment, and ECM2 network assembly via partnerships with SNW1, BUD31, RBM22, OMD, OGN, ASPN, and DCN.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals ECM2 contains a bipartite domain organization typical of matricellular proteins: The N-terminal VWFC domain (residues 101-158) is a cysteine-rich module often involved in multimerization or binding to other matrix components like collagens or integrins. The central and C-terminal regions are dominated by Leucine-rich repeats (LRRs), which fold into a characteristic horseshoe shape providing a versatile surface for binding diverse protein partners. The presence of the 'Ribonuclease Inhibitor-like' superfamily annotation reflects the structural similarity of LRR domains to RI, though ECM2 likely lacks RNase inhibition activity, instead using the fold for matrix interactions. The high-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 71.3) confirms a stable, elongated architecture suitable for bridging matrix components.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places ECM2 within a surprising network linking extracellular matrix remodeling to nuclear splicing regulation, alongside classical matrix partners: strongest interactions with SNW1 (0.841, SKIP/NW35, a transcriptional coactivator and splicing factor), BUD31 (0.820, pre-mRNA-splicing factor), and RBM22 (0.813, RNA-binding motif protein 22). These high-confidence links to spliceosomal components suggest a potential non-canonical role for ECM2 in coupling extracellular signals to nuclear gene expression, possibly via retrograde signaling or direct nuclear translocation of fragments. Additional medium-to-high confidence interactions include classical small leucine-rich proteoglycans (SLRPs) and matrix modulators: OMD (0.703, osteomodulin), OGN (0.687, osteoglycin), ASPN (0.676, asporin), and DCN (0.476, decorin). The OMD/OGN/ASPN/DCN cluster confirms ECM2's role in the structural matrix, likely competing or cooperating with these SLRPs to regulate collagen fibrillogenesis and growth factor availability (e.g., TGF-beta). The strong link to splicing factors (SNW1/BUD31) is unique and hypothesizes a mechanism where ECM2 degradation products or signaling complexes influence alternative splicing of matrix-related genes.\n\nHPA expression data was not available for ECM2 (ENSG00000291357), suggesting low abundance, highly restricted expression (e.g., specific developmental stages of cartilage, bone, or tendon), or lack of antibody coverage. This absence aligns with many specialized matrix proteins that are transiently expressed during tissue morphogenesis.\n\nClinVar documents 29 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains and losses affecting the 9p24.3-q34.3/9q21.11-34.3 region: CNV gains (9p24.3-q34.3 chr9:203861-141020389x3, 9p24.3-q34.11 chr9:203861-131603223x3, 9q21.11-33.2 chr9:71349994-122603410x3) and CNV losses (9q22.2-31.1 chr9:93864974-106661581x1, single allele deletions). A specific intronic variant (c.1605-3C>T) is also listed. The moderate-to-high variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity of the 9p/9q locus. Disruption of ECM2 levels (haploinsufficiency in deletions, overexpression in duplications) likely alters the stoichiometry of the extracellular matrix, leading to defective collagen fibril assembly, impaired cell adhesion, and dysregulated TGF-beta signaling. Given the STRING links to splicing factors, dosage changes might also disrupt the coordination between matrix status and gene expression, contributing to connective tissue disorders, skeletal dysplasias, or syndromic features associated with 9p deletion/duplication.\n\nConservation is observed in mammals: human (O94769, 699 aa), mouse (Q5FW85, 670 aa), cow (Q3MHH9, 680 aa), indicating evolutionary conservation of the VWFC-LRR architecture across species.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: ECM2 functions as a matricellular scaffold that organizes the extracellular environment by bridging collagen fibrils and modulating growth factor activity through its LRR domains, while the VWFC domain mediates self-association or binding to other matrix anchors. The unexpected strong interactions with splicing factors (SNW1, BUD31, RBM22) propose a novel 'matrix-to-nucleus' signaling axis: upon matrix stress or remodeling, ECM2 (or a cleaved fragment) may interact with these nuclear factors to alter the splicing of pre-mRNAs encoding matrix enzymes or receptors, thereby adapting cellular output to extracellular conditions. The partnership with SLRPs (OMD, OGN, ASPN, DCN) suggests competitive or cooperative regulation of collagen deposition. Dysregulation via 9p/9q CNVs disrupts this delicate balance, causing either fragile matrix (loss) or fibrotic/rigid matrix (gain), manifesting as connective tissue abnormalities, skeletal defects, or developmental delays.
