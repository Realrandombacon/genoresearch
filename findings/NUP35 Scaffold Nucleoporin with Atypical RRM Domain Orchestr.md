## Description
NUP35 encodes a 326 amino acid nucleoporin (UniProt Q8NFH5), a critical scaffold component of the nuclear pore complex (NPC) inner ring. With 35 publications, it is partially characterized but retains 'dark' aspects regarding its specific regulatory roles in tissue-specific contexts like the heart and colon.\n\nSTRUCTURAL FEATURES: AlphaFold predicts a medium confidence structure (pLDDT 63.2) across residues 1-326, consistent with a protein containing an ordered RNA recognition motif (RRM) embedded in flexible, intrinsically disordered regions typical of nucleoporins. InterPro analysis reveals a unique domain architecture: an atypical RRM domain (IPR007846, PF05172, cd12722, residues 168-251) distinct from canonical RNA-binding RRMs, and the NUP53/NUP35 family domain (IPR017389, PIRSF038119, residues 3-326). GO annotations confirm structural constituent of nuclear pore (GO:0017056) and nucleocytoplasmic transport (GO:0006913).\n\nINTERACTION NETWORK: STRING-DB identifies 10 interaction partners with exceptionally high confidence (0.971-0.999), defining NUP35 as a central hub in the NPC scaffold. Inner ring scaffold: NUP93 (0.999), NUP188 (0.999), NUP205 (0.993)—core structural components. Transmembrane anchors: NDC1 (0.999)—links NPC to nuclear envelope. FG-nucleoporins: NUP98 (0.997), NUP88 (0.996), NUP62 (0.995), NUP153 (0.995), NUP155 (0.992)—forming the selective permeability barrier. Transport regulator: RANBP2 (0.971). This network places NUP35 at the interface of scaffold stability and channel formation.\n\nEXPRESSION & LOCALIZATION: HPA confirms ubiquitous expression detected in all tissues with low tissue and cancer specificity. Subcellular localization includes nucleoplasm, nuclear membrane, and plasma membrane. Biological processes include mRNA transport, protein transport, and translocation. Recent literature highlights group-enriched expression in cardiomyocytes where it regulates pathological remodeling.\n\nCLINICAL SIGNIFICANCE: ClinVar reports 27 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number losses (2q31.1-32.2, 2q31.3-33.1, 2q32.1) and gains (2p25.3-q37.3, 2q31.1-35, 2q31.2-37.3), plus splice variants (c.212-9A>T). While no specific monogenic disease is yet annotated, the variant burden and mouse models (Nup35 mutation causes degenerative colonic smooth muscle myopathy) suggest roles in tissue-specific structural integrity.\n\nFUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: NUP35 functions as a dual-role scaffold and regulator: (1) NPC assembly coordinator—the atypical RRM domain (residues 168-251) likely mediates protein-protein rather than RNA interactions, bridging the NUP93-NUP188-NUP205 scaffold subcomplex to transmembrane anchor NDC1 during post-mitotic NPC reassembly. (2) Selective barrier modulation—interactions with FG-nups (NUP98/62/153) suggest NUP35 helps organize the central transport channel. (3) Tissue-specific regulation—recent literature (2025-2026) reveals novel roles: driving angiogenesis and poor prognosis in pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma via vascular invasion signatures; regulating pathological cardiac remodeling through Wif1 suppression in cardiomyocytes; and maintaining colonic smooth muscle integrity (mouse model). Mechanistically, NUP35's flexible structure allows conformational adaptation during NPC assembly, while its RRM domain may recruit specific cargo or regulators in a tissue-dependent manner. Loss-of-function would impair NPC biogenesis and nucleocytoplasmic transport, leading to tissue-specific degeneration (colon, heart); gain-of-function could drive oncogenic transcriptional programs in cancer.
