# CCDC86: Nuclear Coiled-Coil Scaffold Protein with Parathyroid-Specific Expression and Disease-Associated Variants

**Date:** 2026-03-23T10:54:08.836334

**Quality Score:** 3.3/10  (E=5.8, D=0.8) [WEAK]

## Description
Coiled-coil domain-containing protein 86 (CCDC86) is a 360 amino acid nuclear protein localized to the nucleoplasm, nucleoli, nucleoli rim, and mitotic chromosomes. The protein contains a conserved coiled-coil domain (InterPro IPR026570, PTHR13557) spanning residues 181-347, which likely mediates oligomerization and protein-protein interactions typical of scaffold proteins. AlphaFold structure prediction yields a medium confidence score (pLDDT 61.5), suggesting a partially ordered structure with potential intrinsically disordered regions flanking the coiled-coil core. Human Protein Atlas data reveals striking tissue-specific expression: CCDC86 is highly enriched in parathyroid gland (355.2 nTPM) compared to other tissues, indicating a specialized role in parathyroid hormone regulation or calcium homeostasis. The protein is classified as essential and predicted intracellular, with involvement in host-virus interactions. ClinVar database documents 10 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains on chromosome 11 spanning the CCDC86 locus, suggesting that gene dosage alterations cause disease phenotypes. Conservation across vertebrates is evident: orthologs exist in mouse (426 aa, Q9JJ89), rat (341 aa, Q5XIB5), bovine (354 aa, Q2TBX7), and orangutan (360 aa, Q5RB69), indicating functional constraint despite length variation. The nuclear localization combined with coiled-coil architecture suggests CCDC86 functions as a structural scaffold organizing nucleolar or chromatin complexes, potentially recruiting transcriptional machinery or ribosome biogenesis factors. Parathyroid-specific upregulation implies tissue-restricted interaction partners or post-translational modifications. Disease-associated copy number variants may disrupt stoichiometric balance of nuclear complexes, leading to developmental or endocrine pathology.
