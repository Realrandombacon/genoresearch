## Description
GPATCH2 (G-patch domain-containing protein 2, also known as PPP1R30, Pfa1, CT110) is a 528 amino acid nuclear RNA-binding protein (UniProt Q9NW75) with medium-confidence partially-structured architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 55.7), characteristic of splicing factors and ribonucleoprotein complex components that contain both ordered RNA-binding domains and intrinsically disordered regions for dynamic protein-protein interactions. The protein contains a G-patch domain (IPR000467/PF01585/PS50174/SM00443, residues 465-513/467-513/468-509) enabling nucleic acid binding activity (GO:0003676), and belongs to the splicing-associated domain-containing protein family (IPR051189/PTHR14195, residues 8-510/8-510) involved in pre-mRNA splicing and ribosome biogenesis. These interactions confirm GPATCH2 functions within the ribosome biogenesis and spliceosome networks, with UTP25 and DHX15 as the highest-confidence partners linking GPATCH2 to small subunit processome assembly and spliceosome activation.\n\nHPA expression reveals GPATCH2 has low tissue specificity and is detected in all tissues surveyed, indicating ubiquitous expression consistent with housekeeping roles in RNA processing and ribosome biogenesis. Subcellular localization to nuclear speckles suggests GPATCH2 functions in pre-mRNA splicing and spliceosome assembly at sites of active transcription. Single cell type specificity shows cell type enriched expression, suggesting elevated levels in cells with high transcriptional activity. The protein is classified as a plasma protein and predicted intracellular protein involved in nucleic acid binding.\n\nClinVar documents 34 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including extensive copy number gains (1q21.1-44, 1q31.3-44, 1q32.2-44 amplifications) and losses (1q41-42.12, 1q41 deletions), and single allele deletions. The variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity. While no specific Mendelian disease is annotated, 1q CNVs are associated with developmental delay, growth abnormalities, and intellectual disability. Given GPATCH2's G-patch RNA-binding function and UTP25/DHX15 interactions, loss-of-function may cause ribosomopathy or spliceopathy syndromes through impaired pre-mRNA splicing and 18S rRNA processing.\n\nConservation is present across vertebrates: human (Q9NW75, 528 aa), mouse (Q7TQC7, 527 aa), indicating the G-patch domain architecture is conserved in mammals with nearly identical protein length.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: GPATCH2 functions as a splicing factor and ribosome biogenesis cofactor that coordinates pre-mRNA splicing with small subunit processome assembly. Ubiquitous expression (detected in all tissues) indicates GPATCH2 is essential for basal RNA processing in all cell types. 1q CNVs cause GPATCH2 haploinsufficiency or overexpression, potentially disrupting spliceosome stoichiometry and causing developmental abnormalities through aberrant splicing of critical developmental genes.
