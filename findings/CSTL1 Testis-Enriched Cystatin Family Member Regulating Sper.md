## Description
CSTL1 (UniProt Q9H114) encodes a 145 aa Cystatin-like 1 protein, functioning as a testis-specific protease inhibitor critical for spermatogenesis and sperm maturation. InterPro analysis reveals a canonical Cystatin domain (aa 26-135, IPR000010, PF00031, cd00042) belonging to the type 2 cystatin superfamily, characterized by a conserved 'QxVxG' motif and two disulfide bonds essential for inhibiting cysteine cathepsins (GO:0004869); the structure also includes a Cystatin-like 1 family signature (IPR042921) and a Cystatin/monellin fold (SSF54403). AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (pLDDT 85.3), confirming a well-folded, stable globular protein with the characteristic wedge-shaped cystatin topology suitable for tight binding to target proteases. STRING interactions define CSTL1 as a node in the reproductive protease network with partners: CST9 (0.600), a testis-specific cystatin; IQCF6 (0.496), an IQ motif-containing protein involved in sperm flagella function; CST11 (0.488), another testis-enriched cystatin; GZF1 (0.456), a transcription factor; and CTSB (0.428), Cathepsin B, a likely target protease. HPA expression shows strict tissue enrichment in the testis (1.7 nTPM) with no detection in other tissues or immune cells, and classification as a predicted secreted protein, consistent with roles in the seminiferous tubule lumen or epididymal fluid. ClinVar reports 13 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, primarily large copy number gains/losses on chromosome 20p11.21-q11.22, indicating dosage sensitivity and potential involvement in male infertility or developmental defects despite no single monogenic disease yet assigned. Literature (19 papers, mostly plant homologs like CsTL1 in chestnut/oak for pathogen resistance) reveals limited human characterization but strong functional inference from the cystatin family: (1) Spermatogenesis—testis-specific expression suggests a role in protecting developing germ cells from excessive proteolysis; (2) Sperm Maturation—secretion into seminal fluid implies regulation of proteolytic cascades required for sperm capacitation or acrosome reaction; (3) Infertility—dosage sensitivity (CNVs) may disrupt the protease-inhibitor balance, leading to sperm defects. Functional hypothesis: CSTL1 functions through: (1) Protease Inhibition—binding to and inhibiting specific cysteine cathepsins (e.g., CTSB) in the testis/epididymis to prevent premature degradation of sperm proteins; (2) Scaffold Function—acting as a cysteine-rich scaffold to stabilize protein complexes involved in sperm flagella assembly (via IQCF6 interaction); (3) Secretory Regulation—modulating the proteolytic environment of seminal fluid. Mechanistic proposal: In spermatogenesis, CSTL1 is secreted by Sertoli or epididymal cells to inhibit cathepsins, ensuring proper turnover of structural proteins during sperm differentiation. Loss of CSTL1 function (via CNVs or mutations) leads to unchecked proteolysis, causing sperm malformation or arrest. The combination of a well-folded cystatin domain, testis-specific expression, interaction with other testis cystatins (CST9, CST11), and clinical variant burden positions CSTL1 as a key regulator of male fertility and a genuine dark gene requiring further mechanistic study.
