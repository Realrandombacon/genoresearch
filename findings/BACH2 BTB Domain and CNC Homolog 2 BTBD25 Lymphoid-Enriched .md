## Description
BACH2 encodes an 841 aa transcriptional regulator protein (UniProt Q9BYV9), also known as BTBD25 or IMD60, that functions as a master transcription factor controlling B cell and T cell lineage commitment, memory cell differentiation, and immune homeostasis through heterodimerization with small MAF proteins and recruitment of corepressor complexes. This extensively studied gene (766 publications) has critical clinical significance for immunodeficiency, HIV persistence, and autoimmune disease. The protein features bipartite transcription factor architecture: BTB/POZ domain (IPR000210/cd18278, residues 6-129/27-133) mediating homodimerization, heterodimerization, and NCOR corepressor recruitment; basic leucine zipper bZIP domain, Maf-type (IPR004826/IPR004827/cd14719, residues 618-708/640-710) enabling DNA binding and heterodimerization with MAFG/MAFK/MAFF; Skn-1-like DNA-binding domain superfamily (IPR008917/G3DSA:1.10.880.10, residues 585-671) providing sequence-specific DNA recognition; classified within CNC (cap'n'collar) bZIP transcription factor family with GO terms for DNA-binding transcription factor activity, regulation of transcription, and protein binding. STRING interactions reveal BACH2 operates within extremely high-confidence B cell differentiation and transcriptional regulation networks: MAF (0.993), MAFG (0.981), MAFK (0.980), MAFF (0.955) forming heterodimeric DNA-binding complexes; NCOR1 (0.890)/NCOR2 (0.851), nuclear receptor corepressors mediating transcriptional repression; IRF4 (0.853), interferon regulatory factor 4 antagonizing BACH2 in plasma cell differentiation; PRDM1/BLIMP1 (0.809), B lymphocyte-induced maturation protein 1 competing with BACH2 for target genes; PAX5 (0.803), paired box 5 B cell identity factor; AICDA (0.672), activation-induced cytidine deaminase in somatic hypermutation. HPA confirms group-enriched expression with lymphoid tissue enrichment (16.3 nTPM), bone marrow (6.9 nTPM), detected in many tissues, immune cell enhanced, low cancer specificity, nucleoplasm/cytosol subcellular localization, classified as disease-related transcription factor with activator/repressor molecular functions and disease variant involvement. ClinVar lists 28 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including missense mutations affecting BTB domain (c.194C>T p.Ala65Val within cd18278), bZIP/DNA-binding regions (c.2416G>C p.Glu806Gln, c.1935G>T p.Glu645Asp, c.979G>A p.Ala327Thr, c.1189G>C p.Val397Leu, c.1534C>A p.Pro512Thr, c.2332G>A p.Gly778Arg, c.2320G>A p.Ala774Thr), 3-prime UTR variant (c.*1546C>A), and copy number loss (6q15-16.1 88-94Mb x1), demonstrating strong mutational sensitivity causing immunodeficiency 60 though specific disease mechanisms remain under investigation. AlphaFold structure prediction yields medium confidence (global pLDDT 53.6), indicating a partially structured protein with ordered BTB and bZIP domains connected by extensive intrinsically disordered regions characteristic of transcription factors requiring conformational flexibility for partner switching and chromatin engagement. Mechanistically, BACH2 operates through integrated modes: (1) B cell memory vs plasma cell fate—2024 study (33 citations) demonstrates epigenetic recording reveals BLIMP1-BACH2 balance determines memory B cell fate upon recall challenge, with BACH2 repressing PRDM1/BLIMP1 to maintain memory state; (2) HIV-1 reservoir persistence—2025 studies (16+7 citations) show BACH2 shapes tissue resident memory T cell programs promoting HIV-1 persistence and facilitates seeding of long-lived viral reservoir in memory CD4+ T cells near therapy initiation time; (3) Immunodeficiency 60—gene summary confirms BACH2 mutations cause IMD60 with defective class switch recombination, somatic hypermutation, and impaired antibody responses; (4) Regulatory T cell expansion in atherosclerosis—2025 study (6 citations) demonstrates low-dose IL-2 induces clonal expansion of BACH2-repressed effector regulatory T cells following acute coronary syndrome, targeting inflammation in cardiovascular disease; (5) Lens epithelial cell apoptosis—2025 study (6 citations) shows METTL3 mediates CPB1 expression by regulating BACH2 promoting apoptosis and oxidative stress in lens epithelial cells; (6) Transcriptional repression mechanism—BTB domain recruits NCOR1/NCOR2 corepressors while bZIP-MAF heterodimers bind antioxidant response elements (AREs) and MAF recognition elements (MAREs); (7) Oxidative stress response—CNC family membership suggests role in cytoprotective gene regulation. Loss-of-function causes defective B cell class switch recombination, impaired somatic hypermutation, accelerated plasma cell differentiation at expense of memory B cells, defective tissue resident memory T cell formation, reduced HIV-1 reservoir establishment, immunodeficiency 60, and altered regulatory T cell homeostasis.
