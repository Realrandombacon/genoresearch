## Description
PLAC9 (Placenta Associated 9) encodes a small 97-amino acid protein of unknown molecular function, originally identified as placenta-enriched but now revealed to have striking choroid plexus specificity. This LIGHT GRAY gene has only 19 literature references, with the seminal 2003 discovery paper (124 citations) remaining the primary characterization, and recent 2025 work focusing on early pregnancy establishment. InterPro analysis confirms PLAC9 as a distinct family (IPR027941, PF15205, PTHR37355) spanning residues 1-96, with no homology to known functional domains, suggesting a unique structural role. AlphaFold predicts a confident, well-folded structure (pLDDT 77.6), indicating a stable scaffold rather than an intrinsically disordered peptide. Human Protein Atlas reveals unexpected tissue specificity: while classified as 'tissue enhanced', expression is overwhelmingly dominated by the choroid plexus (215.8 nTPM), far exceeding placental levels, with additional enhancement in immune cells. This choroid plexus localization suggests a potential role in blood-CSF barrier function or CSF production. STRING interactions link PLAC9 to EMILIN1 (0.756, high confidence), an extracellular matrix glycoprotein involved in elastic fiber assembly, and DUXB/DUXA/DUX4 (0.44-0.61), double homeobox transcription factors implicated in facioscapulohumeral muscular dystrophy (FSHD), hinting at a regulatory or structural connection to chromatin organization or developmental pathways. ClinVar documents 31 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number losses and gains spanning 10q22.3-23.2, indicating dosage sensitivity and potential involvement in genomic disorders, though specific monogenic disease phenotypes remain undefined. Functional hypothesis: PLAC9 acts as a specialized scaffold protein at the blood-CSF barrier, potentially stabilizing extracellular matrix interactions via EMILIN1 or modulating chromatin dynamics through DUX family interactions. Mechanistic proposal: The high choroid plexus expression and structural stability suggest PLAC9 maintains barrier integrity or facilitates CSF secretion; copy number variations disrupt this delicate balance, leading to neurodevelopmental or barrier dysfunction phenotypes yet to be fully elucidated.
