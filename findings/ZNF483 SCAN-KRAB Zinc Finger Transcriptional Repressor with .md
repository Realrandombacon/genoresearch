# ZNF483: SCAN-KRAB Zinc Finger Transcriptional Repressor with Nucleolar Localization and Cancer Association

**Date:** 2026-03-23T10:55:31.085914

**Quality Score:** 2.0/10  (E=3.0, D=1.0) [WEAK]

## Description
Zinc finger protein 483 (ZNF483) is a 744 amino acid nuclear transcription factor localized to nucleoli. The protein architecture features three functional domains: an N-terminal SCAN oligomerization domain (InterPro IPR003309, cd07936, aa 48-156) that mediates homo- and heterodimerization with other SCAN-containing zinc finger proteins; a central KRAB repressor domain (InterPro IPR001909, cd07765, aa 169-241) that recruits KAP1/TRIM28 co-repressor complexes for epigenetic silencing via histone methylation and heterochromatin formation; and multiple C-terminal C2H2-type zinc fingers (InterPro IPR013087, at least 7 fingers spanning aa 351-644) that confer sequence-specific DNA binding to target gene promoters. Human Protein Atlas data shows ZNF483 is expressed at 19.7 nTPM in brain tissue with cancer-enhanced expression patterns, suggesting dysregulation in oncogenesis. STRING interaction network reveals ZNF483 forms complexes with related zinc finger proteins: ZNF446 (score 0.798), ZKSCAN4 (0.777), ZNF462 (0.692), ZKSCAN3 (0.684), and SCAND1 (0.684), indicating participation in a transcriptional regulatory network where SCAN domains mediate combinatorial dimerization. ClinVar documents 33 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number losses and gains on chromosome 9q31-q32 spanning the ZNF483 locus, suggesting that altered gene dosage disrupts transcriptional repression programs. The nucleolar localization combined with KRAB domain architecture suggests ZNF483 may silence ribosomal RNA genes or nucleolar organizer regions, or recruit repressive chromatin modifiers to specific genomic loci. Cancer-enhanced expression implies potential oncogenic or tumor suppressor roles depending on cellular context, possibly through dysregulation of cell cycle or apoptosis target genes. The extensive interaction network with other KRAB-ZNF proteins suggests functional redundancy and cooperative gene regulation.
