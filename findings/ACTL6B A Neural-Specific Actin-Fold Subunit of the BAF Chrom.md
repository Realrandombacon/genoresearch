# ACTL6B: A Neural-Specific Actin-Fold Subunit of the BAF Chromatin Remodeling Complex Essential for Neurodevelopment and Synaptic Plasticity

**Date:** 2026-06-26T17:35:53.715712

**Quality Score:** 10/10 (EXCELLENT)

**Confidence:** HIGH

## Description
ACTL6B (Actin Like 6B, UniProt O94805), also known as BAF53B or SMARCN2, is a 426-amino acid actin-related protein that serves as a neuron-specific subunit of the BAF (BRG1/BRM-associated factor) chromatin remodeling complex, replacing the ubiquitous ACTL6A (BAF53A) paralog during neuronal differentiation to enable activity-dependent gene expression programs required for dendritic morphogenesis and synaptic plasticity. ACTL6B belongs to the actin superfamily but has diverged from conventional actins to function primarily in chromatin regulation rather than cytoskeletal dynamics. InterPro analysis reveals a canonical actin-fold architecture: the actin family domain (IPR004000, PF00022, SM00268, aa 8-426) encompassing the entire protein; an actin conserved site (IPR004001, PS00432, aa 407-415) containing the signature motif critical for nucleotide binding; an ATPase nucleotide-binding domain (IPR043129, aa 9-167/161-425) forming the core catalytic cleft; structural superfamilies including G3DSA:2.30.36.70 (actin domain 2, aa 41-84), G3DSA:3.90.640.10 (actin domain 4, aa 200-321), and SSF53067 (actin-like ATPase domain); the protein belongs to the actin family (PTHR11937, PR00190). The AlphaFold predicted structure exhibits very high confidence (pLDDT 91.0), indicating a well-folded stable protein with the characteristic two-lobed actin fold containing a central ATP-binding cleft, though ACTL6B has lost catalytic ATPase activity and functions structurally within the BAF complex. STRING interaction data places ACTL6B at the core of the mammalian BAF chromatin remodeling machinery with extremely high confidence associations to: DPF1 (0.999) and DPF3 (0.999), double PHD finger proteins recognizing histone modifications; PHF10 (0.992), PHD finger protein involved in complex assembly; SMARCC2 (0.991) and SMARCD1 (0.990), core scaffold subunits; SMARCB1 (0.989), SNF5 tumor suppressor subunit; SMARCA2 (0.982) and SMARCA4 (0.980), catalytic ATPase subunits BRM and BRG1; SMARCE1 (0.982), BAF57 subunit; and ARID1A (0.978), AT-rich interaction domain subunit frequently mutated in cancer. HPA expression profiles confirm brain-enriched expression (51.2 nTPM) with group-enriched tissue specificity, pituitary gland expression (14.6 nTPM), nuclear localization at nucleoli rim and mitotic chromosomes, classification as a chromatin regulator involved in neurogenesis and transcription regulation, and disease associations including epilepsy and intellectual disability. ClinVar records document 109 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including frameshift mutations causing truncation (p.Gly50fs, p.Ala13fs), missense variants affecting critical structural residues (p.Gly3Arg near N-terminus, p.Cys425Tyr at C-terminus, p.Glu273Lys in domain core, p.Arg363Gln), and large genomic deletions spanning the locus; biallelic mutations cause autosomal recessive developmental and epileptic encephalopathy (DEE76/EIEE76) while heterozygous mutations cause autosomal dominant intellectual disability with speech and language delays. We hypothesize that ACTL6B functions as a molecular switch enabling neuronal identity through BAF complex specialization: during neural progenitor proliferation, ACTL6A-containing npBAF complexes maintain stemness by repressing neuronal differentiation genes; upon cell cycle exit, ACTL6B expression is upregulated and replaces ACTL6A in the complex forming nBAF; this subunit exchange alters chromatin targeting specificity through interactions with neuron-specific transcription factors (NEUROD1, MEF2C); the actin-fold domain provides structural scaffolding rather than enzymatic activity, positioning adjacent BAF subunits for optimal nucleosome engagement; ATP binding (but not hydrolysis) may regulate conformational states controlling complex assembly/disassembly; loss of ACTL6B traps cells in progenitor-like chromatin states preventing activity-dependent gene expression required for dendritic arborization and synapse formation; the high-confidence AlphaFold structure suggests rigid domain architecture intolerant to mutation explaining severe neurodevelopmental phenotypes; cancer associations (colorectal cancer biomarker, TDP-43 loss biomarker) indicate dysregulated ACTL6B may contribute to oncogenic chromatin states; the brain-specific expression pattern explains why mutations cause neurological rather than systemic disease despite BAF complex ubiquity.
