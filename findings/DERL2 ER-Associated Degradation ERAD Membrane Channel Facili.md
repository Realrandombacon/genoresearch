# DERL2: ER-Associated Degradation (ERAD) Membrane Channel Facilitating Retrotranslocation of Misfolded Glycoproteins and Chemotherapy Resistance in Cholangiocarcinoma

## Description
DERL2 (Derlin-2, UniProt Q9GZP9) is a 239-amino acid integral membrane protein of the endoplasmic reticulum (ER) that serves as a core component of the ER-associated degradation (ERAD) machinery, specifically mediating the retrotranslocation of misfolded glycoproteins from the ER lumen to the cytosol for proteasomal destruction. DERL2 belongs to the Derlin family (IPR007599, PF04511, aa 13-203) within the Rhomboid-like superfamily (IPR035952, SSF144091, aa 10-191), characterized by six transmembrane helices forming a hydrophilic channel that allows polypeptide passage while maintaining membrane integrity. The AlphaFold predicted structure shows high confidence (pLDDT 81.6), indicating a well-folded, stable transmembrane architecture consistent with a functional retrotranslocon pore. STRING interaction network places DERL2 at the heart of the ERAD-L (lumenal) complex with ultra-high confidence associations to: SEL1L (0.999), adaptor protein bridging substrate recognition to the retrotranslocation channel; OS9 (0.999), lectin recognizing misfolded N-glycans on substrates; SYVN1 (0.998), E3 ubiquitin ligase (Hrd1) catalyzing substrate ubiquitination; FAF2 (0.996) and AUP1 (0.992), UBX-domain proteins recruiting the VCP/p97 ATPase complex; AMFR (0.995), gp78 E3 ligase alternative to SYVN1; VCP (0.994), valosin-containing protein providing mechanical extraction force; ERLEC1 (0.990), EDEM-like chaperone initiating substrate delivery; DERL1 (0.989), paralog with overlapping but distinct substrate specificity; and SELENOS (0.977), selenoprotein S regulating ER stress and inflammation. HPA expression profiles demonstrate ubiquitous detection across all tissues with low tissue specificity, ER subcellular localization confirming its role in protein quality control, classification under unfolded protein response and host-virus interaction biological processes, and low cancer/immune cell specificity suggesting a housekeeping function essential for cellular homeostasis. ClinVar records document 24 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants predominantly consisting of large copy number losses on chromosome 17p13.3-13.2 (spanning 0.005-7.4 Mb regions) and gains, though specific point mutations are not yet cataloged; the chromosomal region 17p13 is frequently deleted in cancers and neurodevelopmental disorders (Miller-Dieker syndrome), but whether DERL2 haploinsufficiency contributes to these phenotypes via ER stress accumulation remains unknown. Literature analysis (29 papers) identifies DERL2 as an emerging oncogene in cholangiocarcinoma where it stabilizes BAG6 (BAG cochaperone 6) promoting chemotherapy resistance; essential for West Nile virus-induced cell death suggesting viral exploitation of ERAD for replication; upregulated in coronary artery disease as an ER stress biomarker; and critical for MHC class I antigen presentation by facilitating retrotranslocation of viral peptides. We hypothesize that DERL2 functions through a coordinated multi-step mechanism: (1) recognition of misfolded glycoproteins by luminal chaperones (BiP, OS9, ERLEC1) identifying exposed hydrophobic patches or immature glycans; (2) recruitment to the DERL2-SYVN1-SEL1L complex via adaptor interactions; (3) insertion of the misfolded substrate into the DERL2 transmembrane channel formed by its six helices; (4) polyubiquitination by SYVN1 or AMFR on cytosolic lysine residues marking the substrate for degradation; (5) engagement of the VCP/p97-UFD1-NPL4 ATPase complex via FAF2/AUP1 adaptors providing mechanical force to extract the polypeptide from the membrane; (6) delivery to the 26S proteasome for degradation preventing toxic aggregate formation; (7) in cancer, overexpression enhances clearance of misfolded oncoproteins or chemotherapeutic targets (via BAG6 stabilization) conferring drug resistance; (8) during viral infection, DERL2 may be hijacked to retrotranslocate viral proteins or modulate ER stress responses to favor viral replication. The high-confidence AlphaFold structure suggests a rigid pore capable of accommodating diverse polypeptide sequences, while the ubiquitous expression implies fundamental importance in proteostasis. ClinVar variants in the 17p13 region suggest dosage sensitivity where loss could lead to ER stress-induced apoptosis contributing to neurodegeneration or developmental defects.
