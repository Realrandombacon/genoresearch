## Description
TAGLN3 (Transgelin-3, UniProt Q9UI15) is a 199-amino acid neuron-specific actin-binding protein belonging to the calponin family that stabilizes actin filaments and modulates cytoskeletal dynamics. The protein contains a conserved calponin homology (CH) domain (IPR001715, PF00307, positions 24-136) responsible for actin binding, followed by a C-terminal calponin repeat (IPR000557, PF00402, positions 174-199) involved in regulatory interactions. AlphaFold predicts a highly confident structure (pLDDT 87.5) with a compact CH domain fold typical of actin regulators. TAGLN3 exhibits extreme brain specificity (HPA: 474.6 nTPM in brain, undetectable in most other tissues), consistent with its role in neuronal morphogenesis and synaptic plasticity. STRING interactions confirm functional partnerships with cytoskeletal effectors: WDR90 (0.779), LIM2 (0.771), MYH11 (0.632), CFL1/cofilin-1 (0.628), TAGLN2 (0.603), ACTA2 (0.578), SCIN/scinderin (0.578), CALD1 (0.540), and TPM1 (0.524). Mechanistically, TAGLN3 is a critical downstream effector in Alzheimer's disease pathology: APOE4 drives neuroinflammation via HDAC-dependent repression of TAGLN3, leading to NF-κB activation and astrocyte dysfunction (79 citations). In cancer, TAGLN3 acts as a metastasis suppressor; its loss promotes epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) and invasion in glioblastoma and nasopharyngeal carcinoma. ClinVar documents 16 pathogenic variants (mostly 3q CNVs), though no specific monogenic syndrome is yet defined. With only 18 literature references despite clear roles in AD, cancer, and cytoskeletal regulation, TAGLN3 represents an underexplored neuronal actin regulator with therapeutic potential in neurodegeneration.
