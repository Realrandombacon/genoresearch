## Description
DDX42 (DEAD-box helicase 42) is a 938 amino acid ATP-dependent RNA helicase critical for spliceosome assembly and antiviral defense. InterPro analysis reveals canonical DEAD-box helicase architecture: an N-terminal Q motif (IPR014014, residues 253-281) unique to DEAD-box proteins for ATP sensing, followed by two RecA-like domains forming the catalytic core - the DEAD/DEAH-box helicase domain (IPR000629, IPR011545, PF00270, residues 277-447) containing the conserved DEAD motif (IPR000629, positions 405-413), and a C-terminal helicase domain (IPR001650, PF00271, residues 488-593). The protein belongs to the P-loop NTPase superfamily (G3DSA:3.40.50.300, IPR027417) spanning residues 215-636. AlphaFold structure (AF-Q86XP3-F1) predicts medium confidence (pLDDT 66.1) across all 938 residues, consistent with structured catalytic domains connected by flexible linkers typical of RNA remodeling enzymes. HPA data confirms ubiquitous expression with specific localization to nuclear speckles, the subnuclear compartments where splicing factors concentrate. STRING interactions reveal exclusive coupling to the U2 snRNP complex: SF3B5 (0.994), SF3B1 (0.987), SF3B3 (0.978), SF3A2 (0.969), SF3B2 (0.964), SF3B4 (0.961), DHX15 (0.951), SF3B6 (0.940), PHF5A (0.915), and SF3A1 (0.903), placing DDX42 at the heart of branch site recognition during spliceosome assembly. Recent structural work (PMID: 37845197) reveals DDX42 cooperates with DDX46 in human U2 snRNP assembly precursors. ClinVar lists 16 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including splice site mutations (c.1152+5G>T, c.372+6T>C) and large copy number gains on chromosome 17q, though specific monogenic diseases remain undefined. Beyond splicing, DDX42 functions as an intrinsic restriction factor against positive-strand RNA viruses including HIV-1 and alphaviruses, likely through RNA helicase activity that disrupts viral replication complexes. With only 33 publications despite essential cellular functions, DDX42 represents an undercharacterized node linking RNA metabolism to antiviral immunity.
