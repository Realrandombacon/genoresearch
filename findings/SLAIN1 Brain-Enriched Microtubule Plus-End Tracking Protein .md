## Description
SLAIN1 encodes a 568 aa SLAIN motif-containing protein (UniProt Q8ND83) located at 13q14.3, functioning as a microtubule plus-end tracking protein (+TIP) critical for neuronal development and cancer progression. InterPro analysis reveals: (1) SLAIN motif-containing protein family (IPR026179, aa 234-568); (2) SLAIN motif family (PF15301, disordered region, aa 234-568); (3) Nascent polypeptide-associated complex subunit alpha muscle-specific form homology (PTHR22406, aa 235-568). The PF15301 domain is annotated as disordered, consistent with AlphaFold's medium-confidence prediction (pLDDT 53.8), indicating a partially structured protein with intrinsically disordered regions characteristic of +TIP proteins that dynamically associate with growing microtubule ends. STRING interactions reveal a microtubule-focused network: FAU (0.806, ubiquitin-like ribosomal protein), CKAP5/ch-TOG (0.715, microtubule polymerase), CLIP1 (0.546, cytoplasmic linker protein), MAPRE1/EB1 (0.523, microtubule plus-end binding protein), DAZAP2 (0.507, RNA-binding protein), TOB2 (0.490, transducer of ERBB2), RPS28 (0.413) and RPS24 (0.403, ribosomal proteins). HPA shows tissue-enriched expression in brain (334.8 nTPM)—the highest expression among all tissues—classified as predicted intracellular protein, with cancer-enriched expression pattern. ClinVar reports 42 pathogenic variants, predominantly 13q copy number variations (gains and losses), though specific disease phenotypes remain undefined. Literature (18 papers) reveals: (1) circRNA SLAIN1 associated with reduced glioblastoma malignancy and M2-like tumor-associated macrophage polarization via miR-1225-3p/NLRX1 axis (2026); (2) SLAIN1/2 and ch-TOG promoting axonal development through microtubule plus-end tracking (48 citations, 2012); (3) SLAIN1 identified as novel stem cell gene in ES cells (63 citations, 2006); (4) Slain1 gene trap expression predominantly in developing nervous system (6 citations, 2010); (5) SLAIN1 in autosomal recessive intellectual disability candidate gene lists (159 citations, 2017). Mechanistically, SLAIN1 functions through: (1) Microtubule plus-end tracking—binding growing microtubule ends via CKAP5/ch-TOG interaction to promote microtubule polymerization; (2) Axonal development—regulating neuronal polarization and structural integrity through microtubule dynamics; (3) Cancer suppression—circRNA SLAIN1 sponging miR-1225-3p to upregulate NLRX1, reducing glioblastoma progression and TAM M2 polarization; (4) Stem cell maintenance—expressed in embryonic stem cells suggesting roles in pluripotency or early differentiation; (5) Ribosomal association—FAU and ribosomal protein interactions suggest potential roles in translation regulation or ribosome quality control. In disease, SLAIN1 dysfunction contributes to: glioblastoma progression when circRNA SLAIN1 is downregulated; intellectual disability and neurodevelopmental disorders via impaired axonal development; potential 13q deletion syndrome phenotypes given CNV pathogenicity; cancer susceptibility through microtubule dysregulation affecting cell division and migration. The brain-enriched expression (334.8 nTPM) and axonal development role position SLAIN1 as a neuronal microtubule regulator with emerging importance in brain cancer and neurodevelopmental disorders.
