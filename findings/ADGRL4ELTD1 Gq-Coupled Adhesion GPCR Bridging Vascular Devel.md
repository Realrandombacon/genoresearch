## Description
ADGRL4/ELTD1 (Q9HBW9, 690 aa) is a well-folded (AlphaFold pLDDT: 81.0) adhesion G protein-coupled receptor (aGPCR) functioning as an orphan receptor with emerging roles in vascular biology and oncology. InterPro analysis reveals a complex extracellular architecture featuring multiple EGF-like calcium-binding domains (IPR001881, residues 58-108), a GPS autoproteolysis motif (IPR000203, residues 253-418) characteristic of aGPCRs, and a class B2 secretin-like 7TM domain (IPR000832/IPR017981, residues 427-668) coupling to Gq signaling. HPA data confirms adipose tissue enrichment (64.3 nTPM) with cancer-enhanced expression patterns and cell type specificity, consistent with roles in metabolic and proliferative contexts. STRING interactions define a vascular signaling network linking ADGRL4 to endothelin receptors EDNRA (0.635) and EDNRB (0.620), angiogenic factors TEK/Tie2 (0.509) and KDR/VEGFR2 (0.485), endothelial markers EMCN (0.516) and CLEC14A (0.433), Notch ligand DLL4 (0.429), and paralog ADGRF5 (0.480), positioning it at the intersection of vasculogenesis and tumor angiogenesis. Recent literature (37 papers) establishes groundbreaking mechanistic insights: the first high-resolution Gq-coupled structure reveals canonical aGPCR activation mechanisms; DNA repair factor KU80 directly binds and activates ADGRL4, linking genome maintenance to GPCR signaling; ADGRL4 drives neuroblastoma aggressiveness, EMT, and angiogenesis via ERK/STAT3 pathway activation; and melatonin induces apoptosis in ER+ breast cancer through ADGRL4 promoter methylation. ClinVar documents 17 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number losses on chromosome 1p regions, implicating ADGRL4 haploinsufficiency in developmental disorders though specific monogenic phenotypes remain undefined. The convergence of GPS-mediated autoproteolysis, Gq coupling, KU80 binding, and vascular interaction partners suggests ADGRL4 functions as a mechanosensitive switch translating extracellular matrix cues into pro-angiogenic signaling, with therapeutic potential in neuroblastoma, breast cancer, and vascular malformations.
