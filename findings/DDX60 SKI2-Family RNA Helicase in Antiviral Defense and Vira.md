## Description
DDX60 (DExD/H-box Helicase 60) is an emerging antiviral RNA helicase with 137 publications, recently characterized (2024-2025) as a critical mediator of dsRNA accumulation and viral mimicry that is suppressed by oncogenic KRAS in colorectal cancer, and protects against ischemic myocardial injury through Arl2 mRNA translation regulation. This large 1712-amino acid cytosolic protein (UniProt Q8IY21) contains multiple functional domains: N-terminal PIN-like domain (IPR055124, positions 7-228), middle domain (IPR061284, positions 583-711), DEXH-box helicase domain (cd18025, positions 763-952), DEAD/DEAH-box helicase domain (IPR011545/PF00270, positions 768-924), C-terminal Ski2-family helicase domain (cd18795, positions 1246-1340), and winged helix domain (IPR059032, positions 1358-1440). AlphaFold predicts high-confidence structure (pLDDT 82.4), indicating stable multi-domain architecture typical of SKI2-subfamily helicases.\n\nSTRING interactions place DDX60 at the core of antiviral innate immunity: IFIH1/MDA5 (0.987), DDX58/RIG-I (0.944), IFIT1 (0.944), IFIT3 (0.936), ISG15 (0.939), OAS1 (0.933), OAS2 (0.907), FAU (0.931), IFI44 (0.913), RSAD2/viperin (0.911). This interaction profile confirms DDX60 operates within the RIG-I/MDA5 cytosolic RNA sensing pathway and interferon-stimulated gene network.\n\nHPA shows tissue-enhanced expression detected in all tissues with highest levels in stomach (38.5 nTPM). Subcellular localization to cytosol, intermediate filaments, and centrosome supports roles in RNA surveillance and cell division. ClinVar contains 57 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number gains/losses on 4q28-35 and missense mutations (p.Arg1028Gln in helicase region), though specific diseases remain uncharacterized. Conservation across vertebrates indicates ancient evolutionary origin.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: DDX60 is a SKI2-family RNA helicase that functions as a cytosolic dsRNA sensor and processor coordinating antiviral defense with tumor immunogenicity. Unlike canonical RIG-I/MDA5 receptors that trigger interferon production, DDX60 may process viral or endogenous dsRNA to maintain basal interferon tone and enable viral mimicry - the phenomenon where accumulated dsRNA triggers innate immune responses against cancer cells. The KRAS-mediated suppression of DDX60 in colorectal cancer suggests it acts as a tumor suppressor by maintaining immunogenic dsRNA levels. Recent evidence linking DDX60 to Arl2 mRNA translation in cardioprotection indicates broader roles in post-transcriptional regulation beyond antiviral defense. Pathogenic variants may disrupt RNA helicase activity, potentially causing immunodeficiency or increased cancer susceptibility through defective viral mimicry.
