## Description
TTC21B/IFT139 (Q7Z4L5, 1316 aa) is a well-folded (AlphaFold pLDDT: 83.1) tetratricopeptide repeat-containing scaffold protein functioning as an essential component of the retrograde intraflagellar transport (IFT-A) complex required for primary cilium assembly and maintenance. InterPro analysis reveals an elaborate architecture dominated by multiple TPR helical domains (IPR011990/IPR019734/PF13181, residues 41-456/479-605/606-960) mediating protein-protein interactions within the IFT machinery, complemented by five ARM repeat domains (IPR056832-836, residues 10-232/269-539/758-911/952-1068/1103-1311) providing additional binding surfaces for cargo recognition, classifying it within the TTC21A/21B family (IPR040364). HPA data confirms ubiquitous expression across all tissues with low tissue specificity but cell type-enhanced patterns and definitive localization to primary cilium, centriolar satellite, basal body, and connecting piece, consistent with fundamental roles in ciliary biology. STRING interactions define an exceptionally high-confidence IFT-A complex network linking TTC21B to WDR35 (0.999), IFT122 (0.999), WDR19 (0.999), IFT140 (0.999), TULP3 (0.989), IFT88 (0.971), IFT27 (0.966), IFT74 (0.938), IFT43 (0.838), and IFT81 (0.542), positioning it as a core structural component of retrograde transport machinery. Literature (111 papers) establishes clinically critical mechanistic insights: compound heterozygous TTC21B mutations cause nephronophthisis type 12 (NPHP12) leading to pediatric end-stage renal disease through ciliary dysfunction; biallelic variants identified in premature infant nephronophthisis cases; TTC21B is required for proper neural progenitor cell proliferation with perdurant protein essential for forebrain development in early mouse embryos; variants disrupt left-right asymmetry and pronephric development in zebrafish models; and preimplantation genetic testing (PGT) strategies developed for genetic prevention. ClinVar documents 289 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including frameshifts (p.Glu540fs, p.Ala1164fs, p.Leu336fs), nonsense mutations (p.Cys937Ter, p.Arg926Ter), splice site variants, and large copy number losses on chromosome 2q24.3, strongly implicating TTC21B haploinsufficiency in ciliopathy spectrum disorders including Joubert syndrome, Meckel syndrome, intellectual disability, and isolated nephronophthisis. The convergence of TPR-mediated IFT-A complex assembly, retrograde ciliary transport function, renal tubular epithelial ciliation requirements, and neural progenitor proliferation roles suggests TTC21B functions as a molecular bridge coordinating cargo loading/unloading during ciliary turnover, with therapeutic implications for ciliopathy management and understanding developmental brain-kidney axis disorders.
