## Description
ADCY3 encodes adenylate cyclase type 3 (AC-III/AC3), a 1144 aa membrane-associated enzyme (UniProt O60266) belonging to the class-III adenylyl cyclase family that serves as critical hub for cAMP-dependent signaling with unique primary cilium localization. InterPro reveals characteristic multi-domain architecture: N-terminal transmembrane domain region (IPR032628/PF16214, residues 42-297) anchoring protein to membrane compartments including primary cilium, two cyclase homology domains CHD1 (cd07302/G3DSA:3.30.70.1230, residues 317-472) and CHD2 (cd07302/G3DSA:3.30.70.1230, residues 921-1119) forming the catalytic core where ATP binding and conversion to cAMP occurs, adenylyl cyclase class-3/4 domains (IPR001054/PF00211, residues 270-472/883-1121) defining family membership with conserved catalytic residues, class-4/guanylyl cyclase conserved sites (IPR018297, residues 423-446/1052-1075) containing metal-coordinating residues essential for catalysis. AlphaFold predicts high confidence structure (pLDDT 76.4) consistent with well-folded enzyme containing structured catalytic domains connected by regulatory regions typical of membrane-integrated signaling enzymes requiring conformational changes during G-protein activation. STRING reveals functional clustering with cAMP signaling cascade components: GNAL (0.975, olfactory-specific G-protein alpha subunit direct activator), PRKACG (0.946) and PRKACA (0.827)/PRKACB (0.824) (cAMP-dependent protein kinase catalytic subunits downstream effectors), CALM3 (0.945)/CALML4 (0.944)/CALML3 (0.943)/CALML5 (0.942) (calmodulin family calcium sensors regulating AC3 activity), GNAS (0.814, stimulatory G-protein alpha subunit universal AC activator), RGS2 (0.485, regulator of G-protein signaling terminating GPCR signals). HPA shows low tissue specificity with ubiquitous expression across all tissues, cell type-enhanced pattern particularly in olfactory sensory neurons and adipocytes, multiple subcellular localizations including plasma membrane (canonical signaling), primary cilium (compartmentalized cAMP microdomains), Golgi apparatus (trafficking/processing), nucleoplasm/nucleoli (nuclear cAMP signaling), classified as enzyme/metabolic protein with calmodulin-binding/lyase molecular functions, disease involvement in obesity through BMIQ19 locus association. ClinVar lists 61 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including frameshift mutation disrupting catalytic domain (c.2267_2268del/p.Tyr756fs), nonsense mutation truncating protein (c.187G>T/p.Glu63Ter), missense variants affecting catalytic residues (c.970G>A/p.Asp324Asn, c.2179T>C/p.Cys727Arg), splice site mutations (c.1968-3C>T, c.2433-86G>A, c.2577+266A>G), and extensive copy number gains spanning 2p25.3-23.1/2p24.1-22.2 affecting gene dosage though specific monogenic phenotypes emerging as severe early-onset obesity with hyperphagia based on recent human genetics studies. Functional mechanism: ADCY3 operates through compartmentalized cAMP production distinct from other AC isoforms: (1) Olfactory transduction - GNAL binding following odorant receptor activation stimulates AC3 in olfactory cilia generating cAMP that opens CNGA2 channels triggering neuronal depolarization, explaining why ADCY3 knockout mice are anosmic; (2) Primary cilium signaling - exclusive localization to primary cilium creates spatially restricted cAMP microdomains regulating Hedgehog pathway, Wnt signaling, and mechanosensation independent of bulk cytoplasmic cAMP; (3) Energy homeostasis - adipocyte ADCY3 produces cAMP activating PKA phosphorylating hormone-sensitive lipase and perilipin controlling lipolysis, while hypothalamic ADCY3 regulates appetite circuits via MC4R pathway explaining BMIQ19 obesity association; (4) Calmodulin regulation - Ca2+/calmodulin binding provides feedback inhibition linking calcium signaling to cAMP production creating oscillatory dynamics essential for signal encoding; (5) Nuclear functions - unexpected nucleoplasmic/nucleolar localization suggests roles in transcriptional regulation via CREB phosphorylation or ribosomal biogenesis control. Recent research reveals expanded disease relevance: monogenic obesity syndrome with bi-allelic loss-of-function variants causing severe hyperphagia and reduced energy expenditure, type 2 diabetes susceptibility through impaired insulin secretion modulation, psychiatric disorders via altered dopaminergic signaling in striatum, cancer progression through cAMP-PKA-CREB axis affecting proliferation/apoptosis balance, ciliopathy phenotypes overlapping with Bardet-Biedl syndrome given primary cilium dysfunction. Despite mechanistic understanding of catalytic activity and G-protein regulation, complete interactome beyond identified partners especially tissue-specific complexes in hypothalamus versus adipose versus olfactory epithelium, regulatory phosphorylation/acetylation sites controlling catalytic efficiency versus subcellular trafficking, precise contribution of 2p CNVs to metabolic versus neurodevelopmental phenotypes, genotype-phenotype correlations for missense variants affecting CHD1 versus CHD2 folding versus catalytic residue integrity, isoform-specific functions from alternative splicing across tissues, evolutionary conservation distinguishing AC3 from AC1/AC5/AC6 paralogs explaining unique cilium targeting, therapeutic potential for ADCY3 agonists enhancing lipolysis versus antagonists suppressing appetite remain incompletely characterized - representing genuine dark aspects of this critical cAMP generator bridging sensory biology with metabolic disease.
