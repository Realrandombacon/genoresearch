# SPICE1: Centrosomal Scaffold Protein Coordinating Centriole Duplication and Mitotic Spindle Assembly with Emerging Roles in Osteosarcoma Metabolism

## Description
SPICE1 (855 aa, UniProt Q8N0Z3), also known as CCDC52, is a spindle and centriole-associated scaffold protein that orchestrates centriole duplication and metaphase chromosome alignment during cell division. The protein contains a large N-terminal centriole duplication and mitotic chromosome congression domain (PF15678, aa36-434) within the SPICE1 family signature (IPR031387, aa1-855), positioning it as a structural organizer at the centrosome. HPA confirms centrosome and basal body localization with ubiquitous expression across all tissues, consistent with fundamental cell cycle functions. Mechanistically, SPICE1 forms the core of a centriole biogenesis complex through high-confidence interactions with CEP120 (STRING 0.994), a critical centriole assembly factor, and CEP295 (0.840), which recruits CEP152 to initiate procentriole formation. Additional interactions with TACC1 (0.786) and TACC3 (0.746) link SPICE1 to microtubule stabilization at spindle poles, while connections to CENPJ/CPAP (0.765), PCNT/pericentrin (0.758), POC5 (0.718), PCM1 (0.635), and HAUS5 (0.627) embed it within the pericentriolar material network that nucleates microtubules. The AlphaFold predicted structure (pLDDT 56.6) reveals a predominantly disordered architecture with scattered structured regions, characteristic of flexible scaffold proteins that undergo conformational changes during centrosome maturation. Clinical significance is demonstrated by 31 pathogenic/likely pathogenic ClinVar variants including copy number losses at 3q13.2-13.31 and splice site mutations (c.1156-11T>C), though no specific monogenic disease has been definitively established. Conservation across vertebrates (mouse Q8C804, rat Q5RKG1, bovine Q2T9X8, xenopus Q6DF94) indicates essential function maintained over ~450 million years of evolution. Recent 2025 research reveals unexpected non-mitotic functions: SPICE1 promotes osteosarcoma growth by enhancing USP10-mediated deubiquitination of FASN (fatty acid synthase), linking centrosomal proteins to cancer metabolic reprogramming; bioinformatics analysis also implicates SPICE1 in keloid pathogenesis via abnormal fibroblast proliferation. The functional hypothesis posits that SPICE1 acts as a 'centrosomal timing coordinator': during G1/S transition, SPICE1 recruits CEP120-CEP295 to license centriole duplication while simultaneously phosphorylation by Aurora kinases triggers conformational changes that expose TACC-binding sites for spindle assembly; in cancer contexts, overexpressed SPICE1 aberrantly stabilizes metabolic enzymes like FASN through competitive inhibition of ubiquitination machinery, driving lipid biosynthesis independent of its canonical centrosomal role.
