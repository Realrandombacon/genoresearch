## Description
MYEF2 (Myelin Expression Factor 2, alias MEF-2) is a 600-amino acid nuclear RNA-binding protein containing three canonical RNA recognition motif (RRM) domains (RRM1: residues 101-176, RRM2: residues 233-308, RRM3: residues 524-600) that confer broad RNA-binding capacity. InterPro analysis confirms membership in the RNA-binding domain superfamily (IPR035979) and the MEF-2 specific RRM subfamilies (IPR034630 for RRM1, IPR034631 for RRM3). The nucleotide-binding alpha-beta plait domain superfamily (IPR012677) indicates conserved RNA-binding architecture across all three RRMs. AlphaFold predicts a medium-confidence structure (pLDDT 61.1), consistent with a modular protein containing structured RRM domains connected by flexible linkers, typical for splicing factors that require conformational adaptability during spliceosome assembly.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places MYEF2 within the spliceosome and RNA processing machinery with highest confidence for CTXN2 (0.751, cortactin-binding protein), XAB2 (0.734, XPA-binding protein involved in transcription-coupled repair and splicing), HNRNPM (0.682, heterogeneous nuclear ribonucleoprotein M), and SOX2 (0.682, pluripotency transcription factor). Additional partners include CRNKL1 (0.677, splicing factor), TFIP11 (0.630, Tuftelin-interacting protein regulating mRNA splicing), SYF2 (0.589, pre-mRNA splicing factor), and ISY1 (0.543, spliceosomal component). This interaction profile strongly supports a role in constitutive and alternative splicing, potentially linking splicing to DNA damage response via XAB2.\n\nHuman Protein Atlas classifies MYEF2 as having low tissue specificity with expression detected in many tissues, localized to nucleoplasm and cytosol. The ubiquitous expression pattern is consistent with a housekeeping splicing factor, while the dual localization suggests potential roles in both nuclear RNA processing and cytoplasmic mRNA regulation. Cancer specificity is low, though literature indicates prognostic value in glioblastoma.\n\nClinVar reports 54 pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number losses spanning 15q21.1-21.2 (coordinates: chr15:47392800-52877953 and chr15:45043912-51196595), splice site mutations (c.1138+1G>T), and frameshift deletions. While no specific monogenic syndrome is named, the burden of CNVs and truncating variants suggests haploinsufficiency contributes to developmental disorders or cancer susceptibility associated with 15q deletions.\n\nLiterature analysis (31 papers) establishes MYEF2 as a multifunctional RNA-binding protein with roles in X-chromosome inactivation (via CRISPR-Cas9 knockout studies), erythroid gene repression through a novel RUNX1-MYEF2 complex, and glioblastoma prognosis as an immune infiltration-related factor. A pivotal 2012 study demonstrated that MYEF2 forms a complex with RUNX1 to repress hematopoietic genes during erythroid differentiation, preventing premature expression of lineage-inappropriate transcripts. In glioblastoma, MYEF2 expression correlates with immune cell infiltration and serves as a prognostic biomarker for IDH-wildtype tumors. Recent work (2020) dissected MYEF2 function in neural stem cells, suggesting roles in neurogenesis. The protein is also found in melanoma-derived extracellular vesicles, indicating potential intercellular RNA regulatory functions.\n\nWe hypothesize that MYEF2 functions as a versatile RNA-processing hub that coordinates splicing with transcriptional regulation during cell differentiation. The three RRM domains enable binding to diverse RNA targets, with RRM1 and RRM3 potentially recognizing specific sequence motifs while RRM2 provides auxiliary binding affinity. In erythroid progenitors, MYEF2 is recruited by RUNX1 to nascent transcripts of hematopoietic genes, where it recruits splicing repressors or promotes exon skipping to generate non-functional isoforms, effectively silencing these genes post-transcriptionally. During X-chromosome inactivation, MYEF2 may bind Xist RNA or other lncRNAs to facilitate chromatin silencing complex assembly. The XAB2 interaction suggests MYEF2 participates in transcription-coupled splicing, potentially linking RNA polymerase II elongation rate to splice site selection. In glioblastoma, altered MYEF2 expression may drive oncogenic splicing programs that promote immune evasion or tumor progression. The 15q21 CNVs likely cause haploinsufficiency, disrupting global splicing fidelity and contributing to developmental phenotypes. MYEF2 represents a candidate gene for 15q-associated syndromes and a potential therapeutic target for splicing modulation in cancer.
