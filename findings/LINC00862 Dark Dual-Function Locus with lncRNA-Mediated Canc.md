## Description
LINC00862 (long intergenic non-protein coding RNA 862, Gene ID: 554279, aliases C1orf98, SMIM16) is a dual-function locus on chromosome 1q32.1 that represents a dark gene with 6 publications revealing complex regulatory roles in cancer biology. Literature analysis shows LINC00862 functions primarily as a lncRNA with two key mechanisms: (1) a 2025 study demonstrates a positive feedback loop between LINC00862 and RBM47 (RNA binding motif protein 47) in hepatocellular carcinoma suppression, and (2) a 2024 pan-cancer analysis (cited 8x) identifies LINC00862 as a super-enhancer-induced diagnostic/prognostic biomarker that promotes SIRT1 expression by competitively binding miR-200 in cervical and gastric cancer, with predictive value for immunotherapy efficacy. Despite lncRNA annotation, UniProt entry A6NCI5 exists for a 91-aa putative transmembrane protein (SMIM16), suggesting potential dual coding/non-coding function. InterPro scan failed, indicating no conserved domains detectable. AlphaFold structure prediction shows medium confidence (global pLDDT 50.1), indicating a partially structured small membrane protein with significant disordered regions. STRING database returned no interactions, consistent with primary lncRNA function. HPA has no expression data available. ClinVar contains 3 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including a deletion (n.551-23_551-22del) and two copy number variations spanning 1q31-1q42 regions, though no specific monogenic disease is attributed solely to LINC00862; these CNVs are associated with 1q deletion/duplication syndromes. GWAS studies link this locus to olfactory function (sense of smell). The cancer suppressor function via RBM47 interaction and SIRT1 upregulation through miRNA sponging suggests LINC00862 functions as a competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) in multiple tumor types. The putative 91-aa transmembrane microprotein may have independent function or represent alternative translation. This represents a dark gene with emerging mechanistic understanding of lncRNA-mediated cancer regulation but unexplored microprotein potential.
