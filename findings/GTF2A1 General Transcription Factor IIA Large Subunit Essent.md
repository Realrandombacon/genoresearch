## Description
GTF2A1 (General transcription factor IIA subunit 1) encodes a 376 aa nuclear protein (P52655) functioning as the large subunit of transcription factor IIA (TFIIA), an essential component of the RNA polymerase II pre-initiation complex. With only 22 publications, this core transcription machinery gene is PARTIALLY CHARACTERIZED at the molecular level but remains understudied regarding human disease mechanisms. FUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: GTF2A1 functions as a stabilizer of TBP-DNA interactions during transcription initiation, preventing inhibitory factors from binding TATA-box sequences and facilitating recruitment of additional general transcription factors (GTF2B, GTF2E, GTF2F, GTF2H) to form the pre-initiation complex. MECHANISTIC PROPOSAL: GTF2A1 operates through: (1) TBP-DNA complex stabilization - the beta-barrel domain (IPR009088, G3DSA:2.30.18.10, positions 318-376) and alpha-helical domain (SSF47396, positions 7-51) form a bipartite structure that binds TBP-DNA complex, preventing NC2/DR1 inhibitory factor binding; (2) Pre-initiation complex assembly - high-confidence STRING interactions with GTF2B (0.999), GTF2A2/small subunit (0.999), GTF2F1 (0.996), GTF2E2 (0.993), TBP (0.941) indicate essential role in recruiting RNA polymerase II holoenzyme; (3) Oncogenic fusion partner - GTF2A1-NCOA2 fusion in uterine tumor resembling ovarian sex cord tumor (UTROSCT) shows aggressive behavior with focal rhabdoid morphology (19 citations, 2021), suggesting transcriptional dysregulation mechanism; (4) Reproductive trait modulation - polymorphisms associated with litter size in sheep (3 citations, 2023), conserved role in reproductive biology. GTF2A1 represents a partially characterized essential transcription factor with well-defined molecular function but incompletely understood disease mechanisms and emerging oncogenic roles.
