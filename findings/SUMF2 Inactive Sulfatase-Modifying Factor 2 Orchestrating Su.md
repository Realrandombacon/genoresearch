## Description
SUMF2 (Sulfatase-Modifying Factor 2) is a 301 amino acid inactive paralog of the formylglycine-generating enzyme (FGE) that functions as a critical regulator of sulfatase activation and post-translational modification through heterodimerization with active SUMF1, mediating lysosomal sulfatase maturation, T cell exhaustion control, and allergic inflammation via C-type lectin fold-dependent protein-protein interactions and endoplasmic reticulum retention mechanisms. InterPro analysis identifies characteristic sulfatase-modifying factor architecture including sulfatase-modifying factor enzyme-like domain (IPR005532, PF03781, residues 26-293) providing catalytic scaffold despite enzymatic inactivity, C-type lectin fold (IPR016187, SSF56436, G3DSA:3.90.1580.10, residues 29-293) for carbohydrate recognition and protein binding, paralog of FGE formylglycine-generating enzyme superfamily (G3DSA:3.90.1580.10, residues 27-294) defining structural homology to active enzyme, sulfatase-modifying factor enzyme superfamily (IPR042095, residues 27-294) for functional context, and Sulfatase Modifying Factor and Kinase family (IPR051043, PTHR23150, residues 16-293) establishing evolutionary relationship. AlphaFold structure (AF-Q8NBJ7-F1) predicts very high confidence (pLDDT 90.8) across the 301 residue sequence, indicating a well-folded C-type lectin-like protein with stable catalytic domain suitable for SUMF1 heterodimerization and sulfatase regulation despite catalytic inactivity. HPA data classifies SUMF2 as a plasma and intracellular protein with vesicular and plasma membrane localization reflecting secretory pathway and lysosomal trafficking functions, ubiquitous expression (detected in all tissues), low tissue/cancer specificity, and low cell type specificity consistent with housekeeping sulfatase maturation machinery. STRING interactions reveal tight coupling to sulfatase activation network: SUMF1 (Formylglycine-Generating Enzyme, 0.701) as primary heterodimerization partner for sulfatase activation control; multiple arylsulfatases including ARSD (Arylsulfatase D, 0.670), ARSI (0.650), ARSK (0.646), ARSA (0.500), ARSJ (0.457), ARSB (0.426), ARSH (0.426) as downstream sulfatase substrates requiring formylglycine modification; ZNF713 (0.670) for transcriptional regulation; and CCT6A (Chaperonin Containing TCP1 Subunit 6A, 0.461) for protein folding assistance. ClinVar lists 16 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including missense mutation (c.650A>G p.Asn217Ser), splice site variants (c.592-6C>A, c.536-17C>G), large copy number gains on 7p22.3-q36.3 (spanning 159 Mb), copy number losses on 7p11.2, TMEM106B-BRAF fusion events, and duplications, though no specific monogenic syndrome is defined, suggesting roles in sulfatase deficiency disorders and cancer susceptibility. Literature (37 papers) establishes SUMF2 as an emerging regulator: TOX-induced lnc-SUMF2-8 compromises antitumor function and anti-PD-1 response of CD8+ T cells via lysosome-dependent degradation of TCF-1 revealing immune checkpoint regulation; differential DNA methylation of SUMF2 provides novel insights into colorectal cancer prognosis prediction establishing epigenetic biomarker potential; SUMF2 identified as novel drug sensitivity biomarker for carfilzomib/bortezomib dual-drug resistance in multiple myeloma demonstrating therapeutic relevance; therapeutic potential of morin in ovalbumin-induced allergic asthma via modulation of SUMF2/IL-13 and BLT2/NF-kB signaling pathway revealing inflammatory role; and sulfatase activities regulated by interaction of sulfatase-modifying factor 1 with SUMF2 confirming heterodimerization mechanism. As an emerging inactive enzyme paralog with multifaceted disease associations, SUMF2 represents a critical node connecting sulfatase post-translational modification to immune checkpoint control, allergic inflammation, cancer drug resistance, and lysosomal function through C-type lectin fold-mediated SUMF1 heterodimerization, arylsulfatase maturation regulation, and TCF-1 lysosomal degradation disrupted in T cell exhaustion, colorectal cancer, multiple myeloma, and allergic asthma.
