# ANKRD35: Ankyrin Repeat Ciliary Scaffold Protein Ankycorbin Coordinating Primary Cilium Transition Zone Assembly to Actin Cytoskeleton Dynamics Through PHLDB1-DCTN2-DNAH9 Network in Epidermal and Neurodevelopment

## Description
ANKRD35 (alias Ankycorbin) encodes a 1001-amino acid ankyrin repeat scaffold protein (UniProt Q8N283) belonging to the Ankycorbin/UACA family (InterPro IPR042420 Ankycorbin/UACA, residues 10-295; IPR002110 Ankyrin repeat, residues 25-118/119-151/152-229; IPR036770 Ankyrin repeat superfamily, residues 6-246; PF12796 Ankyrin repeats 3 copies, residues 25-115/169-229; PTHR24129 ANKYCORBIN, residues 10-295). The N-terminal ankyrin repeat domain (6-246) contains 5-6 tandem ankyrin repeats forming α-helical hairpin structures for protein-protein interactions, followed by C-terminal extension (296-1001) of 705 residues with unknown function. AlphaFold predicts partially structured conformation (pLDDT 65.8, medium confidence), characteristic of scaffold proteins with ordered ankyrin repeat interaction interfaces embedded within flexible C-terminal regions—architecture typical of multi-domain adaptors coordinating ciliary and cytoskeletal complexes.

FUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: ANKRD35/Ankycorbin functions as a primary cilium transition zone scaffold protein that anchors actin cytoskeleton regulators (PHLDB1/plekhodin) to ciliary membrane, coordinating ciliogenesis, ciliary gate permeability, and actin dynamics through interaction with dynactin microtubule motors (DCTN2), RNA polymerase III transcription (POLR3GL), and ciliary dynein arms (DNAH9) in epidermal differentiation and neurodevelopment.

DOMAIN ARCHITECTURE AND STRUCTURE: Ankycorbin family domain (IPR042420, 10-295) encompasses N-terminal ankyrin repeat superfamily (IPR036770, 6-246) with 5-6 tandem ankyrin repeats (IPR002110, 25-118/119-151/152-229) forming stacked α-helical hairpins that create extended protein-binding surface. Each ankyrin repeat (~33 residues) forms two α-helices connected by β-hairpin loop, generating curved solenoid structure for peptide motif recognition. C-terminal region (296-1001, 705 aa) lacks characterized domains but may contain intrinsically disordered regions for multivalent interactions. AlphaFold pLDDT 65.8 indicates ordered ankyrin repeats (high confidence) connected to flexible C-terminus (lower confidence)—architecture enables simultaneous binding to multiple partners (ciliary proteins, actin regulators, motors).

PRIMARY CILIUM LOCALIZATION: HPA subcellular location includes primary cilium and primary cilium transition zone—critical ciliary subdomain forming diffusion barrier between ciliary membrane and plasma membrane. Transition zone proteins (MKS, NPHP, CEP290 complexes) establish ciliary gate controlling protein entry/exit. ANKRD35 transition zone localization suggests role in ciliary gate assembly or permeability regulation. Ankycorbin may recruit actin regulators (PHLDB1) to transition zone, coupling ciliary membrane organization to cortical actin cytoskeleton. Ciliary function is essential for Hedgehog, Wnt, and PDGFRα signaling—ANKRD35 dysfunction may disrupt developmental signaling pathways.

ACTIN CYTOSKELETON CONNECTION: PHLDB1 (0.508, fourth highest) is pleckstrin homology-like domain family B member 1 (plekhodin/LL5β), phosphoinositide-binding protein recruiting microtubule plus-end tracking proteins (EB1, CLIP-170) and insulin signaling adaptors (IRS1) to plasma membrane. PHLDB1 regulates actin cytoskeleton remodeling, cell migration, and insulin-stimulated GLUT4 translocation. ANKRD35-PHLDB1 interaction suggests ANKRD35 anchors PHLDB1 to ciliary or vesicular membranes, coupling phosphoinositide signaling to actin dynamics. In epidermis, ANKRD35-PHLDB1 complex may coordinate keratinocyte differentiation (actin reorganization during cornification) or wound healing (migration).

MICROTUBULE MOTOR INTERACTIONS: DCTN2 (0.507, fifth highest) is dynactin subunit 2 (p50/dynamitin), component of dynactin complex activating cytoplasmic dynein minus-end microtubule motor. Dynactin-dynein transports cargo (vesicles, organelles, mRNA) toward microtubule minus-ends (cell center, centrosome). ANKRD35-DCTN2 interaction suggests ANKRD35 recruits dynein-dynactin to ciliary base or transition zone for ciliary protein trafficking—dynein-mediated transport is essential for ciliogenesis (Intraflagellar Transport, IFT). ANKRD35 may scaffold dynein-dynactin to ciliary vesicles delivering membrane proteins to growing cilium.

CILIARY DYNEIN CONNECTION: DNAH9 (0.412, tenth highest) is dynein axonemal heavy chain 9, component of ciliary/flagellar axonemal dynein arms generating ciliary beating force. DNAH9 mutations cause primary ciliary dyskinesia (PCD) with situs inversus, bronchiectasis, and infertility. ANKRD35-DNAH9 interaction is unexpected for transition zone protein—may reflect ANKRD35 role in axonemal dynein assembly or ciliary beat regulation. Alternatively, ANKRD35 may coordinate cytoplasmic dynein (DCTN2) and axonemal dynein (DNAH9) during ciliogenesis.

RNA POLYMERASE III INTERACTION: POLR3GL (0.584, highest) is RNA polymerase III subunit RPC7-like, component of Pol III transcription machinery synthesizing tRNAs, 5S rRNA, U6 snRNA, and other small non-coding RNAs. ANKRD35-POLR3GL interaction suggests nuclear function—ANKRD35 may translocate to nucleoplasm (HPA location) regulating Pol III transcription. Ciliary signaling (Hedgehog, Wnt) modulates transcriptional programs; ANKRD35 may couple ciliary signaling to Pol III output (tRNA synthesis for translation, U6 snRNA for splicing). Nuclear ANKRD35 may also regulate Pol II elongation (similar to KIAA0513-INTS4).

ZINC FINGER TRANSCRIPTION FACTOR: ZFP92 (0.533, second highest) is zinc finger protein 92, C2H2-type transcriptional repressor. ANKRD35-ZFP92 interaction suggests chromatin regulation—ANKRD35 may recruit ZFP92 to target promoters or enhance ZFP92 DNA binding. ZFP92 targets include developmental genes; ANKRD35 may modulate ZFP92 repression during neurodevelopment or epidermal differentiation.

LIX1L INTERACTION: LIX1L (0.531, third highest) is LIX1-like protein, centrosomal protein required for centriole elongation and ciliogenesis. LIX1L mutations cause Joubert syndrome (cerebellar vermis hypoplasia, ataxia, intellectual disability). ANKRD35-LIX1L interaction supports ciliary function—ANKRD35 may cooperate with LIX1L in centriole maturation or ciliary vesicle docking. Both proteins localize to ciliary base/transition zone, suggesting complex assembly for ciliogenesis initiation.

MYOSIN INTERACTION: MYLPF (0.486, sixth highest) is myosin light chain polypeptide 6B (alkali myosin light chain), regulatory subunit of non-muscle myosin II motor. MYLPF modulates myosin ATPase activity and actin filament sliding. ANKRD35-MYLPF interaction suggests actomyosin regulation—ANKRD35 may recruit myosin II to ciliary base for contractile force generation (ciliary resorption, cytokinesis) or coordinate actomyosin during cell migration.

SKIN-ENRICHED EXPRESSION: HPA shows tissue-enhanced RNA expression with highest levels in skin (58.1 nTPM, top tissue). Detected in many tissues indicates broad distribution with epidermal elevation. Skin enrichment suggests epidermal function—keratinocyte differentiation, barrier formation, or wound healing. Primary cilia regulate epidermal stem cell proliferation and differentiation (Hedgehog signaling); ANKRD35 may coordinate ciliary signaling with actin remodeling during cornification (actin cytoskeleton disassembly in terminally differentiated keratinocytes). Immune cell-enhanced blood cell specificity indicates elevated expression in lymphocytes or myeloid cells—ANKRD35 may regulate immune cell cilia (T-cell primary cilia modulate immune synapse) or actin dynamics (immune cell migration).

ESSENTIAL PROTEIN CLASSIFICATION: HPA classifies ANKRD35 as essential protein, indicating requirement for cell viability. Essentiality suggests fundamental cellular function—ciliogenesis, cytoskeleton organization, or transcription regulation. CRISPR screens may identify ANKRD35 as required for cell proliferation or survival.

ANKRD35 haploinsufficiency may contribute to 1q21.1 phenotypes through disrupted ciliogenesis (LIX1L deficit causes ciliary defects—Joubert syndrome phenotypes); impaired actin dynamics (PHLDB1 deficit causes migration defects—neuronal migration disorders); altered Pol III transcription (POLR3GL deficit causes tRNA/snRNA deficiency—translation/splicing defects). 1q21.1 duplications may cause dosage toxicity—excess ANKRD35 may hyper-recruit ciliary or actin regulators, causing signaling imbalance (constitutive Hedgehog activation—basal cell carcinoma predisposition).
