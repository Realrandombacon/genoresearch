## Description
PFKFB2 (6-Phosphofructo-2-Kinase/Fructose-2,6-Bisphosphatase 2) is a 505 amino acid bifunctional metabolic enzyme that catalyzes both the synthesis and degradation of fructose-2,6-bisphosphate (F2,6BP), the most potent allosteric activator of phosphofructokinase-1 (PFK-1), serving as a critical rheostat for glycolytic flux and cellular energy metabolism. InterPro analysis identifies characteristic bifunctional architecture including N-terminal 6-phosphofructo-2-kinase domain (IPR013079, residues 27-249) with kinase activity (GO:0003873) and ATP binding capability (GO:0005524), C-terminal fructose-2,6-bisphosphatase domain (IPR003094, residues 15-451) with phosphatase activity (GO:0003824) and fructose 2,6-bisphosphate metabolic process capability (GO:0006003), histidine phosphatase superfamily clade-1 (IPR013078, cd07067, residues 251-437) with catalytic histidine residue phosphorylation during reaction, phosphoglycerate/bisphosphoglycerate mutase active site (IPR001345, residues 254-263), P-loop containing nucleotide triphosphate hydrolase domains (IPR027417, G3DSA:3.40.50.300, residues 37-250), and phosphoglycerate mutase-like structural homology (G3DSA:3.40.50.1240, residues 248-464). AlphaFold structure (AF-O60825-F1) predicts high confidence (pLDDT 84.9) across the 505 residue sequence, indicating a stable bifunctional enzyme with distinct kinase and phosphatase domains suitable for reciprocal regulation of F2,6BP levels. HPA data classifies PFKFB2 as a metabolic enzyme and multifunctional hydrolase/kinase/transferase involved in glycolysis, with nucleoplasmic localization reflecting nuclear signaling roles, retina-enriched expression (77.2 nTPM, tissue enhanced), detected in all tissues, group-enriched blood cell specificity, and implications in metabolic and immune disorders. STRING interactions reveal tight functional coupling to the glycolytic regulatory network: PFKM (Phosphofructokinase muscle type, 0.981), PFKFB3 (0.980), FBP2 (Fructose-1,6-bisphosphatase 2, 0.980), FBP1 (0.964), PFKP (Phosphofructokinase platelet type, 0.961) as core glycolytic enzymes; AKT1 (0.936), AKT2 (0.916), AKT3 (0.914) as upstream kinases phosphorylating and activating PFKFB2; TIGAR (TP53-induced glycolysis and apoptosis regulator, 0.443) as an opposing fructose-2,6-bisphosphatase; and PFKL (Phosphofructokinase liver type, 0.425). ClinVar lists 20 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number gains on 1q21.1-44, 1q25.3-32.3, 1q31.3-44, and 1q31.3-32.2 (multiple independent CNV events spanning 130-249 Mb), deletions, duplications, and splice site variants (c.309-165T>G, c.-15A>T), though no specific monogenic syndrome is defined, suggesting potential roles in metabolic disorder or cancer susceptibility. Literature (169 papers) establishes PFKFB2 as a context-dependent metabolic regulator: PFKFB2-driven glycolysis promotes dendritic cell maturation and exacerbates acute lung injury through enhanced immune activation; PSMD14-mediated PFKFB2 deubiquitination activates H3K27 lactylation to drive cancer stemness in gastric adenocarcinoma linking metabolism to epigenetic regulation; PFKFB2-mediated glycolysis promotes lactate-driven continual efferocytosis by macrophages for inflammation resolution; Ginsenoside Rk1 suppresses epithelial-mesenchymal transition of hepatic stellate cells via PFKFB2-mediated aerobic glycolysis inhibition in liver fibrosis; and comprehensive reviews detail PFKFB2 regulatory mechanisms in glycolysis and tumor metabolism as therapeutic targets. As a well-studied metabolic enzyme with emerging roles in immune regulation and cancer epigenetics, PFKFB2 represents a critical node connecting cellular energy status to immune cell function, tumor stemness, and inflammatory resolution through precise control of fructose-2,6-bisphosphate levels and downstream PFK-1 activation.
