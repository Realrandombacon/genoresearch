## Description
DNAH3 (Dynein axonemal heavy chain 3, also known as DNAHC3-B, DNAHC3B, HDHC8, or HEL-36) is a massive 4116 amino acid intracellular motor protein localized to the flagellar centriole, mid piece, principal piece, and equatorial segment of sperm (UniProt Q8TD57) with high-confidence well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 76.2, modeled residues 1-1887). The protein belongs to the dynein heavy chain family (IPR003262/PTHR11893) and contains multiple conserved domains characteristic of AAA+ ATPase motor proteins: P-loop containing nucleotide triphosphate hydrolase domain (G3DSA:3.40.50.300, the catalytic ATPase core), multiple dynein heavy chain N-terminal domains (G3DSA:1.20.140.100, G3DSA:3.20.180.20, residues 1018-1265), Region D6 dynein motor domain (G3DSA:1.10.8.720, residues 3635-3800), and numerous additional dynein-specific folds (G3DSA:1.10.287.2620, 1.10.472.130, 1.10.8.1220, 1.20.58.1120, 1.20.920.20/30, 3.10.490.20). This multi-domain architecture (~4116 aa) creates a molecular machine converting ATP hydrolysis into mechanical force for ciliary/flagellar beating.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals a highly specialized network within the axonemal outer dynein arm complex: strongest interaction with CFAP70 (0.922, Cilia and flagella associated protein 70, essential for dynein arm assembly), indicating direct recruitment to the ciliary apparatus. High confidence links include other axonemal dynein heavy chains DNAH2 (0.920), DNAH8 (0.918), DNAH17 (0.911), DNAH1 (0.910), and DNAH7 (0.909), suggesting cooperative function within the 9+2 microtubule axoneme. Connections to DNAI2 (0.891) and DNAI1 (0.876, dynein intermediate chains 1 and 2) confirm integration into the dynein regulatory complex. Interactions with CCDC114 (0.866, Coiled-coil domain containing 114, outer dynein arm docking complex) and WDR78 (0.858, WD repeat domain 78, dynein assembly factor) indicate roles in pre-assembly and axonemal targeting.\n\nHPA expression reveals a group-enriched pattern with highest levels in fallopian tube (5.1 nTPM) and testis (3.2 nTPM), consistent with exclusive function in motile cilia and sperm flagella. The fallopian tube expression reflects ciliated epithelial cells required for oocyte transport, while testis expression corresponds to sperm flagellar assembly. Subcellular localization to the flagellar mid piece and principal piece confirms incorporation into the sperm axoneme. Low cancer specificity indicates restricted physiological expression.\n\nClinVar documents 61 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including missense mutations (p.Arg2159Gly, p.Ala876Ser, p.Ser198Cys, p.Ser1520Phe, p.Asp1819Asn), nonsense mutations (p.Arg3853Ter), splice variants (c.4813-4A>G), and large CNVs (16p13.11-12.1 duplications). The high variant burden indicates strong functional sensitivity. Loss-of-function mutations cause primary ciliary dyskinesia (PCD), characterized by defective ciliary motility leading to chronic respiratory infections, situs inversus (Kartagener syndrome), and male infertility due to immotile sperm (asthenozoospermia). Missense mutations in the AAA+ ATPase domains likely impair ATP hydrolysis or microtubule binding, abolishing motor activity.\n\nConservation is strong across vertebrates: human (Q8TD57, 4116 aa), mouse (Q8BW94, 4083 aa), indicating evolutionary pressure to maintain the complex motor architecture.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: DNAH3 functions as the force-generating component of the outer dynein arm in motile cilia and sperm flagella. The AAA+ ATPase domains form a ring-shaped catalytic core that hydrolyzes ATP, inducing conformational changes transmitted through the linker domain to produce a power stroke against the adjacent microtubule doublet. DNAH3 assembles with intermediate chains (DNAI1/2) and light chains into a ~2MDa complex that docks onto the A-tubule via CCDC114. CFAP70 acts as a chaperone ensuring proper folding and axonemal targeting. During ciliary beating, DNAH3 generates sliding forces between microtubule doublets, converted into bending by nexin links and radial spokes. In sperm, DNAH3-driven flagellar waves propel the cell toward the oocyte. Loss of DNAH3 abolishes outer dynein arm function, reducing beat frequency and amplitude, causing PCD phenotypes. The fallopian tube expression ensures ciliary clearance of the oviduct, while testis-specific expression enables sperm motility. 16p CNVs disrupt DNAH3 dosage, potentially causing haploinsufficiency and subclinical ciliary dysfunction.
