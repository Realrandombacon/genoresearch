## Description
RHOXF1 (Rhox Homeobox Family Member 1) is a 184 aa nuclear protein representing a partially characterized dark gene with only 23 literature references, functioning as an X-linked transcription factor essential for spermatogenesis and male fertility. Multi-omics data reveals RHOXF1 as a member of the reproductive homeobox (Rhox) cluster, a primate-specific expansion of the paired homeobox family with specialized roles in germ cell development. InterPro analysis confirms the defining Paired Homeobox architecture: Homeodomain (IPR001356/PF00046/SM00389, aa 101-165) with the canonical helix-turn-helix motif (IPR000047, aa 132-157) responsible for sequence-specific DNA binding (GO:0003677); Homeodomain-like superfamily (G3DSA:1.10.10.60/IPR009057, aa 97-166) indicating the conserved three-helix bundle structure; and classification within the Paired Homeobox Transcription Factors family (IPR050649/PTHR24329, aa 64-165), distinguishing it from typical homeodomain proteins by its extended N-terminal region and specific DNA recognition code. The conserved homeobox signature site (IPR017970/PS00027, aa 136-159) confirms functionality as a DNA-binding transcription factor (GO:0000981). AlphaFold predicts a medium-confidence structure (pLDDT 64.2) across the full 184 aa length, consistent with a structured homeodomain core embedded in flexible, potentially disordered N- and C-terminal regions that may serve as transcriptional activation/repression domains or protein interaction interfaces. HPA shows tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in testis (15.0 nTPM) and brain (12.3 nTPM), nucleoplasm/cytosol localization, and immune cell enrichment, aligning with its dual roles in germline development and potential neuro-regulatory functions. STRING interactions reveal low-to-medium confidence partners: AR (Androgen Receptor, 0.532), ZNF354C (0.472), and UNC5C (0.461). The AR interaction is biologically plausible given RHOXF1's testis expression and the known crosstalk between homeobox factors and steroid hormone signaling in spermatogenesis. ClinVar lists a massive burden of 162 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, exclusively large X-chromosome copy number variations (losses: chrX:113417246-155233731x1, chrX:92712119-129831493x1, chrX:119071609-155233731x1; gains: chrX:71017904-140066710x4, chrX:118228490-133002510x3, chrX:119043046-123885987x2), implicating RHOXF1 dosage sensitivity in X-linked syndromes. Recent literature (2024) explicitly links deleterious RHOXF1 variants to male infertility phenotypes (oligozoospermia, azoospermia), while earlier studies (2012) show RHOXF1 silencing affects BCL2 expression in cancer lines, suggesting anti-apoptotic functions. The 23 papers confirm RHOXF1 as a genuine 'emerging' dark gene—known to cause disease but with poorly defined molecular mechanisms and target genes.
