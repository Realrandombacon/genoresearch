## Description
PPDPFL (Pancreatic progenitor cell differentiation and proliferation factor-like protein, UniProt Q8WWR9) is a small 84-amino acid intracellular protein located on chromosome 8p11.23 within a region prone to extensive copy-number variations and amplifications. InterPro analysis reveals membership in the pancreatic progenitor cell differentiation and proliferation factor family (IPR026754/PTHR14572, aa 1-81) with a differentiation and proliferation regulator domain (PF15060, aa 1-61) and PRINTS signature motifs (PR02071, aa 1-19/45-60). GO annotations confirm roles in multicellular organism development (GO:0007275) and cell differentiation (GO:0030154), consistent with the protein's named function in progenitor cell regulation. AlphaFold structure prediction achieves medium confidence (pLDDT 61.3), indicating a partially structured small protein with potential intrinsically disordered regions — architecture characteristic of regulatory proteins that function through flexible protein-protein interactions rather than stable enzymatic domains. Human Protein Atlas reveals striking tissue-enriched expression with exceptionally high expression in skeletal muscle (741.2 nTPM — among the highest values observed for dark genes), detected in some tissues, cancer-enriched pattern, not detected in immune cells, and group-enriched single cell type specificity — this muscle enrichment contrasts with the pancreatic progenitor name, suggesting potential extra-pancreatic functions in muscle development, regeneration, or metabolism. STRING interaction network reveals 3 partners: LYPD2 (0.484, Ly6/PLAUR domain-containing protein 2 involved in epithelial cell signaling), NAT8L (0.462, N-acetyltransferase 8-like protein involved in metabolite conjugation), and C9orf135 (0.412, uncharacterized chromosome 9 protein). This limited but specific interactome suggests PPDPFL functions within differentiation and metabolic regulatory networks. ClinVar contains 23 pathogenic variants consisting predominantly of large copy-number gains and losses across 8p11.23-q24.3, deletions, and duplications, demonstrating extreme dosage sensitivity where genomic rearrangements disrupting chromosome 8 cause disease phenotypes including developmental disorders and cancer predisposition (8p amplifications are frequent in multiple cancer types). Conservation across vertebrates (bovine A8E653, 141 aa; Xenopus Q0IHF8, 113 aa) indicates ancient evolutionary origin tied to fundamental developmental mechanisms. PPDPFL likely functions as a differentiation regulatory protein, potentially acting as a transcriptional co-regulator or signaling modulator in skeletal muscle progenitor cells (satellite cells) during muscle development and regeneration — the high muscle expression suggests primary function in myogenesis rather than pancreatic development, with the 'pancreatic progenitor' name reflecting historical annotation based on sequence homology. Dysfunction may contribute to muscle disorders, developmental defects, and cancers where chromosome 8p rearrangements are common, particularly given the cancer-enriched expression pattern suggesting potential oncogenic or tumor suppressor roles depending on cellular context.
