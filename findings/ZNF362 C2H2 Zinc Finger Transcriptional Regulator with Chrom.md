## Description
ZNF362 encodes a 420 amino acid C2H2-type zinc finger transcription factor localized to the nucleus/nucleoplasm. InterPro analysis reveals three canonical C2H2 zinc finger domains (IPR013087, PF00096) at positions 227-310, forming a beta-beta-alpha zinc finger fold (G3DSA:3.30.160.60) characteristic of sequence-specific DNA-binding proteins. The AlphaFold structure (pLDDT=56.6) shows medium confidence with partially disordered regions, suggesting an intrinsically disordered N-terminal activation domain coupled to structured DNA-binding C-terminal zinc fingers—a common architecture for transcriptional regulators.

STRING interaction network identifies 10 high-confidence protein partners, notably TRIM71 (score 0.734), an E3 ubiquitin ligase regulating mRNA stability and stem cell maintenance; LIN28A (0.546), an RNA-binding protein controlling developmental timing; and RAD21 (0.480), a cohesin complex component mediating chromatin looping. Additional interactions with ZNF451, ZNF512, and NAB1 suggest ZNF362 functions within multi-zinc finger transcriptional complexes, potentially recruiting corepressors or coactivators to target gene promoters.

HPA data confirms ubiquitous expression across all tissues with low specificity (nuclear localization), consistent with a housekeeping transcriptional regulator. ClinVar documents 10 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly copy number gains/losses spanning 1p35.1, implicating ZNF362 dosage sensitivity in disease. Conservation analysis shows orthologs in primates (Pan troglodytes, Pongo abelii) and amphioxus (Branchiostoma lanceolatum), indicating ancient evolutionary origin.

FUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: ZNF362 acts as a sequence-specific DNA-binding transcriptional regulator that recruits chromatin-modifying complexes (via TRIM71, RAD21 interactions) to regulate genes involved in cell differentiation and developmental timing. The disordered N-terminus likely serves as a transactivation domain recruiting histone modifiers, while the C-terminal zinc fingers recognize GC-rich promoter elements. Dysregulation through CNVs may disrupt target gene networks contributing to developmental disorders or cancer.
