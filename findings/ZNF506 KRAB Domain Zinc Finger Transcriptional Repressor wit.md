## Description
ZNF506 (Gene ID: 9349, UniProt Q5JVG8) is a 444 amino acid Krüppel-associated box (KRAB) domain-containing zinc finger protein located on chromosome 19p13.12 (NC_000019.10). This protein belongs to the C2H2-type zinc finger transcription factor family, one of the largest gene families in the human genome involved in sequence-specific DNA binding and transcriptional regulation. InterPro domain analysis reveals a canonical KRAB-ZFP architecture: N-terminal KRAB domain (IPR001909/PF01352, positions 4-75/4-44) functioning as a transcriptional repression module, followed by multiple C2H2-type zinc finger DNA-binding domains (IPR013087/PF00096/PF13912, positions 152-363 with at least 7 distinct zinc finger motifs at positions 201-223, 229-251, 257-279, 341-363). The KRAB domain recruits TRIM28/KAP1 corepressor complex which mediates heterochromatin formation through HP1 binding and histone methyltransferase recruitment. AlphaFold structure prediction (AF-Q5JVG8-F1) yields medium-confidence model (global pLDDT 67.6), consistent with KRAB-ZFP architecture featuring structured zinc finger clusters connected by flexible linkers—characteristic of DNA-binding proteins requiring conformational adaptability for target recognition. Human Protein Atlas classifies ZNF506 as a predicted intracellular transcription factor with DNA-binding molecular function; RNA expression shows low tissue specificity with detection across all surveyed tissues, low cancer specificity, and cell type-enhanced expression at single-cell resolution, suggesting ubiquitous but regulated expression pattern typical of housekeeping transcriptional regulators. STRING database identifies 8 interaction partners revealing connections to chromatin regulation and other zinc finger proteins: TRIM28 (score 0.522, tripartite motif-containing protein 28/KAP1, essential KRAB corepressor), ZNF844 (0.518), ZNF766 (0.493), ZNF347 (0.490), ZNF493 (0.476), ZNF431 (0.454), ZNF320 (0.423), and TMEM54 (0.405). The TRIM28 interaction is mechanistically critical—TRIM28 serves as universal scaffold for KRAB-ZFP-mediated repression, recruiting SETDB1 histone methyltransferase, NuRD complex, and HP1 to establish H3K9me3 heterochromatin marks. Other ZNF partners suggest potential cooperative DNA binding or functional redundancy within the KRAB-ZFP network. ClinVar lists 8 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all large copy number gains spanning extensive 19p-q regions (multi-megabase duplications/triplications from ~2-59Mb affecting hundreds of genes); no specific monogenic disease is attributed solely to ZNF506, but 19p13 duplications cause various developmental syndromes. Conservation is evident across primates with orthologs in Pan troglodytes (444 aa), Pongo abelii (444 aa), and Nomascus leucogenys (444 aa), indicating strong evolutionary constraint on KRAB-ZFP architecture. Functional hypothesis: ZNF506 functions as a sequence-specific transcriptional repressor that binds genomic DNA through its C2H2 zinc finger array recognizing specific nucleotide sequences, then recruits TRIM28/KAP1 corepressor complex via its KRAB domain to establish localized heterochromatin through H3K9me3 deposition and HP1 recruitment. This silences target genes involved in development, differentiation, or cellular homeostasis. Like other KRAB-ZFPs, ZNF506 may regulate endogenous retroviral elements, imprinted loci, or developmental gene networks. Disease mechanism: Given absence of specific ZNF506 point mutations in ClinVar (only large 19p CNVs), this gene is unlikely to cause monogenic disease when disrupted alone; however, ZNF506 dosage alterations within 19p13 duplication syndromes may contribute to neurodevelopmental phenotypes through dysregulation of target gene networks. Priority experiments include ChIP-seq to identify genomic binding sites, CRISPR knockout followed by RNA-seq to identify derepressed targets, co-IP validation of TRIM28/ZNF844/ZNF766 interactions, and investigation of ZNF506 role in retrotransposon silencing.
