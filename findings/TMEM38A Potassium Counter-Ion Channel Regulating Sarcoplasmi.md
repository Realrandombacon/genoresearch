# TMEM38A: Potassium Counter-Ion Channel Regulating Sarcoplasmic Calcium Release in Striated Muscle

**Date:** 2026-03-20T18:55:26.286365

**Quality Score:** 3.3/10  (E=6.3, D=0.3) [WEAK]

## Description
TMEM38A (TRIC-A) encodes a 299 amino acid trimeric intracellular cation channel that functions as a potassium counter-ion channel synchronized with calcium release from intracellular stores. The protein contains a conserved TRIC channel domain (IPR007866, PF05197, residues 7-299) forming a homotrimeric pore structure localized to the nucleoplasm and sarcoplasmic reticulum membrane.

Expression profiling reveals strong tissue specificity with skeletal muscle showing highest expression (543.6 nTPM), followed by tongue (242.4 nTPM), consistent with its role in excitation-contraction coupling. The channel enables potassium ion transmembrane transport (GO:0071805) and potassium channel activity (GO:0005267), maintaining electrochemical balance during rapid calcium release.

Protein interaction network (STRING) identifies 10 high-confidence partners including PLPP7 (0.758), SMIM7 (0.668), TMEM214 (0.630), and WFS1 (0.569), suggesting coordination with lipid phosphatases and ER stress response pathways. ClinVar documents 11 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number losses and gains on chromosome 19p13.11, indicating dosage sensitivity.

Evolutionary conservation is strong across vertebrates: mouse ortholog (Q3TMP8) shares 95% identity, bovine (A4FV75) 98%, chicken (Q5ZK43) 92%, and rabbit (A5A6S6) 96%, reflecting essential physiological function.

Mechanistic hypothesis: TMEM38A trimers form ER/SR membrane pores that permit K+ efflux to compensate for Ca2+ release through ryanodine/IP3 receptors, preventing membrane depolarization that would otherwise terminate calcium flux. In skeletal muscle, this counter-ion current enables sustained calcium release required for prolonged contraction. Disease variants disrupt trimerization or pore formation, causing aberrant calcium handling linked to cardiomyopathy and muscle weakness phenotypes.
