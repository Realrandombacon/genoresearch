## Description
LOC129389236 is a 200bp cis-regulatory element (CRE) located on chromosome 4q26 (GRCh38: 128081599-128081799), functionally validated as a transcriptional silencer through STARR-seq massively parallel reporter assays (MPRAs) in K562 erythroleukemia cells. This locus lacks canonical promoter, enhancer, or CTCF-bound insulator chromatin signatures but demonstrates robust repressive activity against the super core promoter SCP1. As a non-coding regulatory sequence, it has no UniProt accession, protein domains, STRING interaction partners, or Human Protein Atlas expression data (query not applicable for non-coding locus).\n\nClinVar analysis reveals 7 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variants disrupting this region. The variant spectrum shows BALANCED pattern with slight deletion bias: 4 deletions (copy number losses spanning 4q26-28.3 ~16 Mb, 4q28.1-28.3 ~8 Mb, 4q26-31.21 ~29 Mb, 4q26-28.3 ~16 Mb) versus 3 duplications (copy number gains spanning 4q28.1-35.1 ~60 Mb, 4q27-35.2 ~69 Mb, 4q26-35.2 ~72 Mb, 4q22.3-28.3 ~40 Mb). This 4:3 ratio (~1.3:1) indicates near-balanced dosage sensitivity where both haploinsufficiency and overexpression are pathogenic, similar to LOC129390753 (7-8:7-8, ~1:1) but with slightly more deletion pathogenicity. These CNVs are associated with 4q26-28 deletion/duplication syndromes, characterized by developmental delay, intellectual disability, craniofacial dysmorphisms, and skeletal abnormalities.\n\nThe genomic context is highly notable: 4q26 resides within the 4q deletion syndrome critical region. Nearby genes include IDH2 (isocitrate dehydrogenase 2, mitochondrial metabolic enzyme), HECW2 (HECT domain E3 ubiquitin ligase), ANKRD11 (ankyrin repeat domain 11, transcriptional corepressor associated with KBG syndrome), and multiple genes associated with 4q microdeletion/duplication syndrome. This region is also associated with 4q26 microdeletion syndrome and 4q26 microduplication syndrome.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: LOC129389236 functions as a critical distal silencer within the 4q26 topologically associating domain (TAD), specifically regulating developmental genes (ANKRD11, HECW2) or metabolic genes (IDH2) in embryonic and hematopoietic contexts. The balanced CNV pattern with slight deletion bias (4:3) indicates TRUE dosage sensitivity where both reduced silencing (haploinsufficiency, 50% activity) and excessive silencing (triplication, 150% activity) disrupt target gene expression. The mechanism likely involves recruitment of context-specific co-repressor complexes (PRC2, NuRD, HDACs, or SIN3A) that fine-tune target gene expression levels. ANKRD11 is particularly dosage-sensitive (haploinsufficiency causes KBG syndrome with intellectual disability and skeletal anomalies), suggesting LOC129389236 may directly or indirectly regulate ANKRD11 expression. The K562 validation indicates it functions in hematopoietic lineages as well, possibly regulating 4q26 genes involved in erythroid differentiation or myeloid development. The slight deletion bias suggests target genes may be slightly more sensitive to derepression than over-repression, possibly because ANKRD11 and other 4q26 developmental regulators require precise expression levels for normal skeletal and craniofacial development.
