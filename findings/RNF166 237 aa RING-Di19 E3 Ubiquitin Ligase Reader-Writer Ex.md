## Description
RNF166 (UniProt Q96A37, 237 aa) is a partially characterized E3 ubiquitin-protein ligase with emerging roles in antibacterial autophagy, cancer progression, and diabetic nephropathy, yet 68 ClinVar pathogenic variants lack defined disease associations. InterPro reveals a bipartite domain architecture: an N-terminal RING-type zinc finger (IPR001841, PF13445, aa 33-73) providing E3 ligase catalytic activity, followed by a C-terminal Di19 zinc-binding domain (IPR008598, PF05605, aa 149-209) likely mediating substrate recognition. Additional C2HC zinc finger (PF18574, aa 93-124) suggests nucleic acid or protein interaction capability. AlphaFold predicts high-confidence structure (global pLDDT 84.4), confirming stable folding of both RING and Di19 domains with well-defined secondary structure. HPA classifies RNF166 as tissue-enhanced with highest expression in lymphoid tissue (98.5 nTPM) and bone marrow (86.0 nTPM), localized to nucleoplasm, plasma membrane, and cytosol, consistent with roles in innate immunity and autophagy. STRING interactions reveal functional partnerships with PARP11 (0.711, ADP-ribosyltransferase), LRSAM1 (0.707, E3 ligase), KLHL28 (0.676, substrate adaptor), RNF114 (0.643, related E3 ligase), and multiple zinc finger proteins (ZNF653, ZNRD2, ZNF827), placing RNF166 in ubiquitination and transcriptional regulation networks. ClinVar contains 68 pathogenic variants including large copy number losses (16q24.2-24.3 deletions), duplications, and splice site mutations (c.649-26G>A), but zero specific disease names—indicating clinical significance without established genotype-phenotype correlations. Recent literature (2024-2025) reveals RNF166 as a 'reader-writer' E3 ligase that extends K11-linked polyubiquitin chains onto sites of MARUbylation (mono-ADP-ribosylated ubiquitin), promotes colorectal cancer by destabilizing poly-ADP-ribosylated angiomotins (Hippo pathway regulators), and podocyte deficiency alleviates diabetic nephropathy via CYLD signaling. Functional hypothesis: RNF166 functions as a ubiquitin code editor that recognizes ADP-ribosylated substrates via its Di19 domain and extends K11-linked polyubiquitin chains via its RING domain, targeting proteins for proteasomal degradation or autophagic clearance. In antibacterial autophagy, RNF166 recruits adaptor proteins (SQSTM1, NDP52) to ubiquitinated bacteria. In cancer, it destabilizes tumor suppressors (angiomotins) when they are PARP-modified. The 68 pathogenic variants likely cause immune dysregulation, cancer susceptibility, or renal disease through loss of ubiquitin editing activity. The high-confidence structure (pLDDT 84.4) and conserved domain architecture (mouse Q3U9F6, rat Q6J1I7) suggest the RING-Di19 arrangement is essential for coordinating substrate recognition with catalytic activity—a mechanism still being elucidated.
