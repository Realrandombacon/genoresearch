# HCFC1R1: Small Beta-Propeller-Interacting Nuclear Regulator of HCFC1-Mediated Transcriptional Activation and Viral Immediate-Early Gene Expression with Skeletal Muscle-Specific Functions

**Date:** 2026-06-14T18:27:42.100946

**Quality Score:** 8/10 (EXCELLENT)

**Confidence:** HIGH

## Description
HCFC1R1 (Q9NWW0) encodes a 138 amino acid small nuclear regulatory protein that functions as a beta-propeller-interacting modulator of Host Cell Factor C1 (HCFC1), a critical transcriptional coactivator controlling cell cycle progression, viral gene activation, and chromatin remodeling. InterPro analysis reveals HCFC1R1 belongs to the HCF-1 beta-propeller-interacting protein family (IPR029195/PF15226, positions 2-134) with a compact HCFC1R1-specific domain (PTHR16246, positions 2-93) optimized for protein-protein interactions rather than enzymatic activity. AlphaFold predicts a partially structured protein (pLDDT 61.8, medium confidence) consistent with intrinsically disordered regions enabling conformational flexibility for multi-partner binding. STRING interactions demonstrate specific partnerships linking HCFC1R1 to transcriptional regulation and mRNA processing: HCFC1 (0.599), the primary target host cell factor; YIPF3 (0.542), a Golgi trafficking protein; THOC6 (0.534), a TREX complex subunit for mRNA export; FLYWCH2 (0.517), a zinc finger transcriptional regulator; E4F1 (0.462), an E2F transcription factor family member controlling cell cycle; PP2D1 (0.457), a phosphatase; BICDL2 (0.455), a coiled-coil protein; C1QTNF12 (0.448), a complement C1q tumor necrosis factor-related protein; SH3TC1 (0.428), an SH3 domain protein; and ANKEF1 (0.420), an ankyrin repeat protein. HPA shows tissue-enhanced expression with exceptionally high levels in skeletal muscle (479.9 nTPM), nucleoplasmic localization consistent with transcriptional regulation roles, and detection across all tissues. ClinVar contains 37 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number gains spanning chromosome 16p13.3 regions (e.g., chr16:513767-5555136 duplications), deletions (chr16:2550259-3154076), and regulatory variants (c.-57G>T promoter variant, c.281+9C>G splice variant), though specific monogenic disease associations remain undefined. Literature (only 2 papers) demonstrates HCFC1R1 deficiency blocks herpes simplex virus-1 (HSV-1) infection by inhibiting nuclear translocation of HCFC1 and viral protein 16 (VP16), preventing immediate-early gene expression required for viral replication; this reveals HCFC1R1 as a host dependency factor for HSV-1 hijacking of cellular transcriptional machinery. The mechanistic hypothesis: HCFC1R1 functions as a nuclear adaptor that binds the HCFC1 kelch/beta-propeller domain through its PF15226 interface, modulating HCFC1's recruitment of transcriptional coactivators (E4F1), chromatin remodelers, and viral transactivators (VP16) to target gene promoters; in skeletal muscle, high HCFC1R1 expression suggests specialized roles in muscle-specific transcriptional programs potentially involving myogenic differentiation or metabolism; during HSV-1 infection, viral VP16 exploits the HCFC1-HCFC1R1 complex to access immediate-early promoters, making HCFC1R1 essential for viral replication; THOC6 interaction suggests HCFC1R1 may couple transcriptional activation to mRNA export via the TREX complex; copy number alterations on 16p13.3 disrupt HCFC1R1 dosage, potentially causing combined defects in cell cycle control, muscle function, and antiviral defense; the small size (138 aa) and partial disorder enable HCFC1R1 to act as a flexible molecular bridge rather than structured enzyme.
