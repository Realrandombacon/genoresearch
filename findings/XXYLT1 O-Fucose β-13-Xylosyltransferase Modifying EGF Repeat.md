FUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: XXYLT1 functions as a Golgi-resident O-fucose β-1,3-xylosyltransferase that modifies EGF-like repeats in NOTCH receptors and other EGF-repeat-containing proteins through sequential glycosylation. The enzymatic mechanism involves: (1) POFUT1 first adds O-fucose to conserved Ser/Thr residues in EGF repeats (C2-X(3-5)-C3 motif), (2) XXYLT1 then adds β-1,3-xylose to the O-fucose using UDP-xylose as donor substrate, forming the xylose-fucose disaccharide. This modification regulates NOTCH receptor trafficking, ligand binding affinity, and downstream signaling. The GT8 family catalytic domain (aa 200-355) contains the conserved DXD motif for metal ion coordination and UDP-sugar binding. High-confidence interactions with POGLUT1/2/3 and EOGT indicate XXYLT1 operates within a coordinated EGF repeat glycosylation network. In cancer, XXYLT1 shows context-dependent roles: it inhibits NOTCH1 activation in T-cell leukemia (Jurkat cells) while promoting proliferation, and β-elemene suppression of XXYLT1 inhibits NSCLC tumor progression. The lncRNA XXYLT1-AS2 (antisense to XXYLT1) promotes hepatocellular carcinoma through TFEB degradation and autophagy inhibition, suggesting XXYLT1 locus regulation in cancer. The 39 ClinVar variants at 3q26-29 suggest XXYLT1 copy number alterations may contribute to developmental disorders or cancer susceptibility through disrupted NOTCH signaling and EGF-repeat protein glycosylation. The well-folded AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 83.9) supports stable enzymatic function. XXYLT1 represents a partially characterized gene with emerging roles in NOTCH regulation, cancer biology, and congenital disorders of glycosylation warranting further investigation.
