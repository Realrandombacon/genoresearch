## Description
KANTR (KANTR Integral Membrane Protein) is a poorly characterized 76-amino acid protein encoded on the X chromosome (Xp22.33-q28 region) with only 17 total publications, most of which are non-biological or tangential references, leaving its molecular function entirely unknown. The protein is predicted to be an integral membrane component, consistent with its UniProt annotation, and AlphaFold predicts a well-folded structure (global pLDDT 80.8) despite its small size, suggesting a stable transmembrane helix or compact fold critical for function. InterPro scan failed to return domain annotations, indicating it lacks recognizable domains in current databases, classifying it as an orphan protein family. STRING interaction analysis returned zero partners, confirming its isolation from known protein networks and highlighting the complete gap in mechanistic understanding.\n\nHuman Protein Atlas reveals brain-enriched expression (4.7 nTPM) with 'tissue enriched' specificity and detection in single cell types, suggesting a specialized role in specific neuronal subpopulations rather than ubiquitous housekeeping functions. This restricted expression pattern aligns with its X-linked location, raising hypotheses about roles in neurodevelopment or sex-specific brain functions. ClinVar reports 71 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly massive copy number gains and losses spanning the entire X chromosome arm (Xp22.33-q28), though no specific Mendelian disease is yet assigned to KANTR alone. The sheer volume of CNVs suggests that dosage imbalance of the Xq/Xp region including KANTR contributes to X-linked intellectual disability, developmental delay, or epilepsy syndromes, but the specific contribution of KANTR haploinsufficiency or overexpression remains undefined.\n\nWe hypothesize that KANTR functions as a small transmembrane regulator of synaptic vesicle trafficking, ion channel modulation, or membrane receptor stability in specific brain circuits. Its small size (76 aa) and high structural confidence (pLDDT 80.8) are characteristic of regulatory micro-proteins or peptide hormones that modulate larger complexes. The brain-enriched expression and X-linkage suggest a potential role in male-specific neurodevelopmental processes or X-inactivation escape mechanisms in females. The lack of interaction data may reflect technical limitations in detecting transient membrane interactions or indicate that KANTR functions as a lipid-embedded modifier rather than a protein-binding adaptor. Future work should focus on determining its subcellular localization (e.g., synaptic membrane, ER, Golgi) and identifying binding partners via co-immunoprecipitation in brain tissue.
