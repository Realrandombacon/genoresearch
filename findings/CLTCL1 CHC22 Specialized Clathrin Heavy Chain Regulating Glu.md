## Description
CLTCL1 (Clathrin Heavy Chain Like 1, also known as CHC22, UniProt P53675) is a 1640-amino acid specialized clathrin isoform functioning as a structural component of coat complexes that regulate intracellular trafficking, specifically distinguished from the canonical CHC17 (CLTC) by its critical role in glucose metabolism and GLUT4 sequestration in insulin-responsive tissues. InterPro analysis confirms canonical clathrin architecture with unique regulatory features: N-terminal beta-propeller domain (G3DSA:2.130.10.110/IPR016025, aa 1-364) mediating protein-protein interactions with adaptor complexes; extensive tetratricopeptide-like helical domains (G3DSA:1.25.40.10/IPR011990, aa 444-768/888-1041/1042-1183) providing scaffolding surface for light chain binding; seven-fold repeat regions (IPR000547/PF00637, aa 537-972) forming the trimerization hub essential for clathrin cage assembly; linker core motifs (IPR015348/PF09268, aa 331-421) connecting propeller to leg domains; and C-terminal ankle region (G3DSA:1.25.40.730, aa 1528-1624) involved in lattice curvature. GO annotations include clathrin light chain binding (GO:0032051), vesicle-mediated transport (GO:0016192), and intracellular protein transport (GO:0006886). STRING interactions reveal high-confidence partnerships with endocytic machinery: AP2B1 (0.992, adaptor-related protein complex 2 subunit beta 1 - core clathrin adaptor), AP2S1 (0.955, adaptor complex 2 subunit sigma), CLTA (0.854, clathrin light chain A), CLTB (0.732, clathrin light chain B), EPN2 (0.697, epsin 2 - membrane curvature inducer), EPN3 (0.659, epsin 3), CLINT1 (0.652, clathrin interacting protein 1), GAK (0.610, cyclin G-associated kinase - uncoating ATPase), AP2A1 (0.454), and DNAJC6 (0.426, auxilin - co-chaperone for uncoating) - confirming integration into clathrin-coated vesicle formation and uncoating cycles. HPA shows group-enriched expression with highest levels in skeletal muscle (180.9 nTPM), followed by tongue (57.3 nTPM) and testis (45.3 nTPM), consistent with its specialized role in insulin-responsive striated muscle; subcellular localization to vesicles; low cancer specificity but group-enriched blood cell pattern; cell type enhanced specificity. AlphaFold predicts high-confidence structure (pLDDT 77.4) for the full 1640 aa, indicating a well-folded trimeric scaffold with rigid leg domains and flexible linker regions typical of clathrin heavy chains capable of assembling into polyhedral lattices. ClinVar documents 387 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number variations on chromosome 22q11.21 including deletions (e.g., chr22:18929330-20686544 del, chr22:19017218-21105423 del) and duplications (e.g., chr22:18919477-21459713 dup) associated with 22q11 deletion/duplication syndromes, though specific phenotypes attributable solely to CLTCL1 dosage remain confounded by neighboring genes; the high variant count suggests strong dosage sensitivity. Literature (53 papers) reveals emerging metabolic functions distinct from CHC17: (1) Evolution of CLTCL1 revealing selection influencing CHC22's role in human glucose metabolism (2018) - highlighting human-specific adaptation; (2) Genetic variation in CLTCL1 associating with whole-body glucose control (2019) - metabolic trait; (3) Down-regulation of CLTCL1 as early event in preinvasive ductal carcinoma of breast progression (18 citations, 2012) - cancer biomarker. Functional hypothesis: CLTCL1 (CHC22) forms a specialized clathrin coat distinct from the ubiquitous CHC17, evolving in humans to regulate the retrograde trafficking and sequestration of the glucose transporter GLUT4 (SLC2A4) in skeletal muscle and adipose tissue; unlike CHC17 which mediates constitutive endocytosis, CHC22 facilitates the formation of a specialized intracellular compartment where GLUT4 is stored in the basal state, preventing its surface expression until insulin stimulation triggers release; this mechanism allows humans to maintain tight glycemic control through regulated glucose uptake in muscle. Mechanistic proposal: The N-terminal beta-propeller (aa 1-364) interacts with specific adaptors (potentially distinct from AP2) to recognize GLUT4 sorting signals; the trimerization hub (aa 537-972) assembles into a clathrin lattice that buds GLUT4-containing vesicles from the trans-Golgi network or endosomal recycling compartment; CHC22-coated vesicles traffic GLUT4 to a specialized storage compartment (GSC) in muscle cells; upon insulin signaling, CHC22 function is bypassed or modulated to allow GLUT4 translocation to the plasma membrane; high expression in skeletal muscle (180.9 nTPM) correlates with tissue-specific demand for regulated glucose uptake; evolutionary selection on CLTCL1 (2018) suggests adaptation to human dietary shifts requiring precise glucose homeostasis; genetic variation affecting CLTCL1 expression or function alters whole-body glucose control (2019), positioning it as a modifier of diabetes risk; downregulation in breast cancer (2012) may disrupt normal trafficking of growth factor receptors or nutrient transporters, contributing to malignant progression; 22q11 CNVs encompassing CLTCL1 contribute to the metabolic and developmental phenotypes of DiGeorge/velocardiofacial syndromes through haploinsufficiency or triplosensitivity; high-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 77.4) supports stable lattice formation comparable to CHC17 but with potential sequence divergences in adaptor-binding interfaces conferring functional specificity; CLTCL1 represents a human-specialized trafficking regulator critical for metabolic health and a potential target for improving insulin sensitivity.
