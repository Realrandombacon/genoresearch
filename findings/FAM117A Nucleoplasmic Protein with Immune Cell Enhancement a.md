## Description
FAM117A encodes a 453-amino acid nucleoplasmic protein containing a conserved FAM117 family domain (IPR026642/PF15388, residues 86-398, full protein 25-410) classified as disordered in Pfam, suggesting conformational flexibility. AlphaFold structure prediction (pLDDT=55.5) indicates medium-confidence folding with potentially disordered regions, consistent with intrinsically disordered proteins that mediate dynamic protein interactions. Protein interaction analysis reveals functionally coherent partnerships: SLC35B1 (0.660, solute carrier family 35 member B1, nucleotide-sugar transporter), DYRK1A (0.682, dual-specificity tyrosine phosphorylation-regulated kinase 1A), RNF169 (0.541, E3 ubiquitin ligase involved in DNA damage response), LZTS2 (0.532, leucine zipper tumor suppressor 2), and PICK1 (0.424, PDZ domain-containing trafficking regulator). The SLC35B1 interaction suggests FAM117A may regulate nucleotide-sugar transport or glycosylation processes indirectly through transcriptional or post-translational control. Tissue expression is detected in all tissues with immune cell enhancement and highest expression in bone marrow (72.2 nTPM), indicating roles in hematopoietic development or immune function. Subcellular localization in nucleoplasm supports transcriptional regulatory or chromatin-associated functions. Clinical significance includes 13 pathogenic/likely pathogenic ClinVar variants, including copy number losses (17q21.31-25.3) and gains (17p13.3-q25.3), as well as single nucleotide variants in regulatory regions, indicating dosage sensitivity and potential regulatory disruption. Evolutionary conservation is evident across vertebrates: mouse ortholog (451 aa) maintains similar length and domain architecture. The combination of nucleoplasmic localization, immune cell enhancement, interaction with SLC35B1 and DYRK1A, and CNV dosage sensitivity strongly suggests FAM117A functions as a regulatory scaffold in the nucleus that integrates signaling (via DYRK1A phosphorylation), ubiquitination (via RNF169), and potentially glycosylation-related pathways to modulate gene expression in hematopoietic cells.
