# RBM33: Intrinsically Disordered m6A Reader Regulating ALKBH5 and Microglial Senescence

**Date:** 2026-04-16T12:20:28.667310

**Quality Score:** 2.5/10  (E=4.7, D=0.3) [WEAK]

## Description
RBM33 (PRR8) encodes a large, 1170-amino acid RNA-binding protein characterized by a single C-terminal RNA Recognition Motif (RRM, IPR000504, residues 1099-1166) embedded within a predominantly intrinsically disordered scaffold (AlphaFold pLDDT 46.6). This structural architecture suggests a mechanism where the disordered N-terminal region (residues 1-1098) acts as a flexible tether or hub for protein-protein interactions, while the structured RRM provides specific RNA binding. RBM33 functions as a critical regulator of epitranscriptomic dynamics, specifically acting as an m6A reader that modulates the substrate selectivity and demethylase activity of ALKBH5. STRING interactions confirm associations with nuclear export machinery (THOC7, score 0.507; NCBP3, score 0.609) and DNA repair factors (EME1, score 0.583), supporting its role in mRNA stability and nuclear export. HPA data indicates ubiquitous nucleoplasmic expression with low tissue specificity. ClinVar reports 93 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, primarily large copy number gains/losses at 7q36.3, suggesting dosage sensitivity. Recent literature links RBM33 to microglial senescence via liquid-liquid phase separation (LLPS) driving CDKN1A activation, proposing a mechanistic link between its disordered nature, phase separation propensity, and aging-related neuroinflammation.
