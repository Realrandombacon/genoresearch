## Description
STARD7 (UniProt Q9NQZ5), also known as ADCME, BAFME2, FAME, FAME2, FCMTE2, or GTT1, encodes a 370 aa mitochondrial protein functioning as a lipid transfer protein within the START (StAR-related lipid transfer) domain superfamily. InterPro analysis reveals a conserved START domain architecture characteristic of lipid-binding proteins: central START domain (IPR002913/PF01852/PS50848/SM00234, residues 117-329/121-329/131-325/137-327) mediating lipid binding activity (GO:0008289) through hydrophobic pocket formation for sterol/quinone substrate accommodation; membership in START-like domain superfamily (IPR023393/G3DSA:3.30.530.20/SSF55961, residues 124-327/134-319) indicating evolutionary relationship to Bet v1-like fold proteins; STARD7-specific START domain classification (IPR041949, residues 117-325) confirming functional specialization within START family; and START domain-containing lipid transfer family membership (IPR051213/PTHR19308, residues 71-327) placing it within broader lipid transport protein category. AlphaFold predicts high confidence structure (pLDDT 74.6) indicating a well-folded protein with stable START domain core suitable for lipid substrate binding and transfer. The SAMD12 interaction is particularly notable as SAMD12 mutations cause related familial cortical myoclonic tremor syndromes suggesting shared pathogenic mechanisms. HPA classifies STARD7 as essential protein with low tissue specificity detected in all tissues, exclusively localized to mitochondria consistent with gene summary predicting mitochondrial localization, involved in metabolic processes and transport reflecting housekeeping roles in mitochondrial lipid homeostasis across all cell types. ClinVar lists 33 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants consisting predominantly of large copy number losses (2q11.1-11.2x1, 2p11.2-q11.2x3, 2q11.1-11.2 deletions) and gains (2q11.1-11.2x3, 2p25.3-q37.3x3) plus single allele deletions though no specific disease name is consistently assigned; critically, gene summary explicitly implicates STARD7 in familial adult myoclonic epilepsy 2 (FAME2/BAFME2/FCMTE2) confirming haploinsufficiency causes neurological disease. Functional hypothesis: STARD7 operates through integrated mitochondrial lipid metabolism mechanisms: (1) Lipid binding/transfer mechanism - START domain forms hydrophobic cavity binding ubiquinone precursors or other mitochondrial lipids facilitating transfer between mitochondrial membranes or from cytosol to mitochondria; (2) Ubiquinone biosynthesis - Gene summary predicts involvement in ubiquinone (coenzyme Q10) biosynthetic process suggesting STARD7 transfers ubiquinone intermediates between enzymatic steps in CoQ synthesis pathway; (3) Mitochondrial membrane homeostasis - Maintains phospholipid composition of inner/outer mitochondrial membranes through lipid transfer activity analogous to PRELID1-TRIAP1 complex; (4) Neurological disease mechanism - Haploinsufficiency reduces mitochondrial ubiquinone levels impairing electron transport chain function leading to neuronal hyperexcitability and myoclonic epilepsy; CNV losses cause dosage reduction while gains may disrupt lipid balance; (5) Skin barrier function - Gene summary predicts role in skin barrier establishment potentially through lipid transfer to epidermal mitochondria affecting keratinocyte differentiation; (6) Conservation - Mouse Stard7 (Q8R1R3, 373 aa) indicates essential conserved function across mammals.
