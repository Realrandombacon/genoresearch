## Description
DENND6A (Q8IWF6, 608 aa) is a highly structured (AlphaFold pLDDT: 83.9) guanine nucleotide exchange factor (GEF) essential for coordinating lysosomal positioning and autophagic flux. InterPro analysis reveals a unique architecture dominated by a tripartite DENN domain (IPR037516, residues 63-528) flanked by an AVL9-specific domain (IPR018307, residues 64-200) and a distinct C-terminal domain (IPR063156, residues 542-604), defining it as a specialized regulator within the DENN family. HPA data confirms ubiquitous expression with immune cell enrichment and vesicular localization, consistent with roles in intracellular trafficking. STRING interactions validate its function as a molecular bridge, showing high-confidence links to paralogs DENND10 (0.936) and DENND11 (0.934), the homolog AVL9 (0.920), and critically, the small GTPases RAB14 (0.576) and RPS6KC1 (0.573). Recent literature (only 5 papers) establishes a novel mechanistic role: DENND6A physically couples the lysosomal GTPase Arl8b to a Rab34/RILP/dynein motor complex, driving retrograde transport of lysosomes toward the perinuclear region to facilitate autophagosome fusion. ClinVar documents 10 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number losses on chromosome 3p, implicating DENND6A haploinsufficiency in developmental disorders, though specific phenotypes remain undefined. The convergence of structural stability, specific GTPase coupling, and dosage sensitivity positions DENND6A as a critical but underexplored node in lysosomal dynamics, with potential implications for neurodegenerative diseases characterized by trafficking defects.
