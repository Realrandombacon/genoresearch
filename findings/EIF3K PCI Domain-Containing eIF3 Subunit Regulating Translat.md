## Description
EIF3K encodes a 218 aa eukaryotic translation initiation factor 3 subunit K (UniProt Q9UBQ5) located at 19q13.33, functioning as a core component of the 43S preinitiation complex that regulates cap-dependent translation initiation, ribosome content, and innate immune signaling. InterPro analysis reveals a PCI domain architecture (IPR000717/PS50250/PF10075, aa 42-204) characteristic of the CSN8/PSMD8/EIF3K family (IPR033464, aa 62-200), with N-terminal ARM-type fold (IPR016024/G3DSA:1.25.40.250, aa 3-130) for protein-protein interactions and C-terminal winged helix DNA-binding domain superfamily (IPR036388/SSF46785, aa 123-218) suggesting nucleic acid binding capacity. AlphaFold predicts high-confidence structure (pLDDT 87.1), indicating a well-folded PCI domain protein with stable ARM and winged helix regions. STRING interactions reveal exclusive association with the eIF3 complex core: EIF3A/B/C/D/E/F/G/H/I/J (all 0.999 scores), confirming its role as an integral translation initiation factor subunit. ClinVar reports 9 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including missense (c.128A>G p.Asp43Gly), splice site (c.421+4A>C, c.354+29G>T), and 19q11-13.33 copy number gains, with recent 2025 literature documenting homozygous variants causing neurodevelopmental delay, microcephaly, and growth retardation in four unrelated individuals. Literature (59 papers) details emerging multifunctional roles: (1) Neurodevelopmental disorders - recessive homozygous variants causing global developmental delay with microcephaly (1 citation, 2025); (2) NF-κB regulation - eIF3K inhibiting NF-κB signaling by targeting MyD88 for ATG5-mediated autophagic degradation in teleost models (27 citations, 2022); (3) Cancer biology - eIF3K as cancer-relevant regulator of ribosome content through mRNA-selective translation control (19 citations, 2022); (4) Fungal pathogenesis - eIF3K domain proteins regulating conidiogenesis, appressorium turgor, and virulence in Magnaporthe oryzae (21 citations, 2021). Functional hypothesis: EIF3K likely functions through: (1) Translation Initiation Scaffold - PCI domain mediating eIF3 complex assembly and 43S preinitiation complex stability, coordinating mRNA recruitment to 40S ribosomal subunit; (2) Ribosome Content Regulation - selective translation of ribosome biogenesis mRNAs, coupling protein synthesis capacity to cellular growth demands; (3) Innate Immune Modulation - MyD88 interaction promoting ATG5-dependent autophagic degradation, limiting NF-κB activation and inflammatory responses; (4) Neurodevelopment - essential for neural progenitor proliferation and brain growth, with loss causing microcephaly through impaired protein synthesis in developing neurons; (5) Nucleic Acid Recognition - winged helix domain potentially binding mRNA or regulatory RNAs for translation selectivity. The PCI-ARM-winged helix architecture parallels other eIF3 subunits and COP9 signalosome components, suggesting conserved scaffolding mechanisms across translation and ubiquitin signaling pathways. Disease implications include: neurodevelopmental disorders (microcephaly, developmental delay), cancer (ribosome content dysregulation), inflammatory diseases (NF-κB hyperactivation), potential viral susceptibility. Despite 59 papers, EIF3K's neurodevelopmental role, mRNA selectivity mechanisms, and therapeutic targeting potential remain underexplored.
