# SNED1: A Multi-Domain Extracellular Matrix Scaffold Protein Mediating Cell-Matrix Adhesion and Notch Signaling via Modular EGF-Sushi-Nidogen Architecture

## Description
SNED1 (Sushi, Nidogen and EGF-like Domain-containing protein 1, UniProt Q8TER0) is a large 1,413-amino acid extracellular matrix (ECM) glycoprotein that functions as a modular scaffold for cell-matrix adhesion and developmental signaling. InterPro analysis reveals a complex multi-domain architecture optimized for ECM organization and protein-protein interactions: an N-terminal nidogen domain (IPR003886, aa 70-260) that mediates basement membrane assembly by bridging laminin and collagen IV networks; three calcium-binding EGF-like domains (IPR000742, IPR001881, G3DSA:2.10.25.10, aa 268-385) with conserved hydroxylation sites (IPR000152) for post-translational modification; a central Sushi/CCP/SCR complement control module (IPR000436, cd00033, aa 696-753) typically involved in regulatory protein interactions; and three C-terminal fibronectin type III domains (FN3, cd00063, G3DSA:2.60.40.10, aa 908-1197) that provide structural rigidity and integrin-binding surfaces. The AlphaFold predicted structure shows confident folding (pLDDT 71.6), consistent with a stable ECM scaffold protein. STRING interaction data places SNED1 within ECM and endocrine signaling networks with moderate confidence associations to somatostatin SST (0.559) and its receptors SSTR1/SSTR2 (0.421-0.511); cortistatin CORT (0.581); the complement-related protein SUSD5 (0.463); the ECM-associated protein CCDC80 (0.417); and membrane metalloendopeptidases MME/MMEL1 (0.405-0.410) that may process SNED1 or its binding partners. HPA expression profiles confirm ubiquitous detection across tissues with immune cell enhancement, consistent with roles in tissue homeostasis and inflammation; the protein is predicted to be secreted into the ECM. ClinVar records document 108 pathogenic variants including large copy number losses at 2q37.3 (associated with Albright hereditary osteodystrophy-like phenotypes and brachydactyly), copy number gains, and missense mutations (p.Asp793Tyr), implicating SNED1 in developmental disorders affecting skeletal morphogenesis. We hypothesize that SNED1 functions as a bifunctional ECM organizer: the nidogen domain anchors SNED1 to basement membranes where it stabilizes laminin-collagen networks; the EGF and FN3 domains present binding surfaces for cell surface receptors (integrins, Notch) to mediate adhesion and mechanotransduction; and the Sushi domain recruits regulatory proteins or growth factors to modulate local signaling microenvironments. The interaction with somatostatin pathway components suggests SNED1 may concentrate or present neuropeptides to their receptors in specific tissue contexts. Loss of SNED1 would disrupt ECM integrity, impair cell-matrix signaling, and potentially dysregulate Notch pathway activation during development, contributing to the skeletal and soft tissue abnormalities observed in 2q37.3 deletion syndrome.
