# COMMD5: COMM Domain Scaffold Protein in Copper Metabolism Complex Regulating RUNX2 Degradation, Autophagy, and Renal/Vascular Pathology

## Description
COMMD5 encodes a 224-amino acid COMM domain-containing protein 5 (UniProt Q9GZQ3), also known as HCaRG (hypertension-associated calcium-regulated gene), a well-folded scaffold protein functioning in the CCC (COMMD/CCDC22/CCDC93) complex for endosomal trafficking and protein degradation. The protein contains an N-terminal HN domain (PF21672, positions 28-129) characteristic of COMMD2-7/10 family members, plus a C-terminal COMM domain (IPR017920, positions 148-215; PF07258, positions 148-205; PS51269, positions 151-215) that mediates protein-protein interactions within the COMMD complex, classified within the COMMD5 family (IPR037357, positions 4-224; PTHR15666, positions 4-224) with Commd5_HCaRG domain (cd04753, positions 115-224). AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (pLDDT 84.0, residues 1-224) indicating stable folding consistent with scaffold protein architecture. STRING interaction analysis reveals COMMD5 forms part of the COMMD complex network with ultra-high confidence interactions exclusively with other COMMD family members: COMMD2 (0.980), COMMD10 (0.970), COMMD8 (0.962), COMMD1 (0.958), COMMD4 (0.957), COMMD6 (0.954), COMMD9 (0.950), COMMD3 (0.949), plus FAU (0.967, ubiquitin-like protein) and CCDC22 (0.956, coiled-coil domain-containing protein 22 that anchors COMMD complex to endosomes), confirming its role as a stoichiometric component of the 10-member COMMD heteromeric complex. HPA data shows ubiquitous expression with low tissue specificity (detected in all tissues), nucleoplasm and cytosol subcellular localization consistent with endosomal trafficking and nuclear signaling functions, annotated for transcription regulation and Ubl (ubiquitin-like) conjugation pathway biological processes. ClinVar contains 56 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number gains (8q24.13-24.3, 8q23.2-24.3, 8p23.3-q24.3, 8q23.3-24.3, 8q24.3, 8q13.2-24.3, 8q21.2-24.3) though specific Mendelian disorders remain undefined, suggesting COMMD5 dosage sensitivity. With only 25 publications, COMMD5 has emerging roles in renal and vascular pathology: COMMD5 inhibits vascular calcification in chronic kidney disease by promoting lysosomal degradation of RUNX2 (osteogenic transcription factor) via cytoskeletal stabilization (2025), COMMD5 counteracts cisplatin-induced nephrotoxicity by maintaining tubular epithelial integrity and autophagy flux (2024, 6 cit), COMMD5 involved in hypotension after parathyroidectomy in hemodialysis patients (2023, 3 cit), contrasting tumor suppression versus metastatic enhancement roles in renal cell carcinoma (2025). The functional hypothesis: COMMD5 serves as a scaffold component of the CCC (COMMD/CCDC22/CCDC93) complex that regulates endosomal sorting and cargo trafficking by recruiting ubiquitinated substrates to the retromer complex; the COMM domain mediates heteromeric assembly with other COMMD proteins while the HN domain may bind specific cargo or regulatory factors; FAU interaction suggests links to ubiquitin signaling and proteasomal/lysosomal degradation pathways; RUNX2 degradation mechanism indicates COMMD5-CCC complex targets osteogenic transcription factors for lysosomal destruction, preventing vascular smooth muscle cell osteogenic transdifferentiation and calcification; cisplatin nephrotoxicity protection suggests COMMD5 maintains autophagy flux in renal tubular epithelial cells, potentially by regulating autophagosome-lysosome fusion or cargo recognition; hypotension association implies COMMD5 affects vascular tone regulation through endothelial or smooth muscle cell signaling; renal cell carcinoma duality suggests context-dependent roles where COMMD5 may suppress primary tumor growth through copper homeostasis or NF-κB regulation but enhance metastasis through endosomal trafficking of integrins or growth factor receptors; 8q CNVs may disrupt COMMD5 dosage causing endosomal trafficking defects affecting multiple organ systems; ubiquitous expression indicates constitutive cellular housekeeping function in protein quality control and membrane receptor recycling.
