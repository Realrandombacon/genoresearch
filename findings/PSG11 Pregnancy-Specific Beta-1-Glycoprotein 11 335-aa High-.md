## Description
PSG11 (Pregnancy-Specific Beta-1-Glycoprotein 11) is a partially characterized dark gene with only 26 publications, representing a critical but understudied component of the placental secretome essential for maintaining pregnancy. UniProt accession Q9UQ72 identifies it as a 335-amino acid secreted glycoprotein. InterPro scan reveals a classic immunoglobulin superfamily architecture composed of three tandem C2-set Ig-like domains: an N-terminal V-set domain (IPR013106, residues 41-130) likely involved in specific ligand binding, followed by two C2-set domains (IPR003598, residues 160-224 and 252-308) that provide structural stability and dimerization interfaces. This domain arrangement classifies PSG11 as a member of the carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA) family, specialized for cell-cell adhesion and immune modulation at the maternal-fetal interface.\n\nAlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (global pLDDT 88.0), indicating a rigid, well-folded elongated molecule. The high confidence across all three domains suggests precise geometric constraints are required for its interaction with maternal immune cells or trophoblast receptors, preventing misfolding that could trigger inflammatory responses.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals 10 partners with medium-to-high confidence, defining a functional network centered on placental hormone production and remodeling: CSH2 (0.804 - Chorionic Somatomammotropin Hormone 2), PSG3 (0.787), PSG4 (0.681), PAPPA (0.669 - Pregnancy-Associated Plasma Protein A), CSH1 (0.586), SDC3 (0.548 - Syndecan 3), SDC4 (0.527), ALPP (0.519 - Placental Alkaline Phosphatase), GPC1 (0.505), and TGFB1 (0.500). The strong link to CSH2/CSH1 (growth hormones) and PAPPA (a protease critical for IGF bioavailability) suggests PSG11 acts as a scaffold or co-factor in the endocrine regulation of fetal growth. Interactions with Syndecans (SDC3/4) imply a role in organizing the extracellular matrix or presenting growth factors to trophoblasts.\n\nHuman Protein Atlas confirms extreme tissue specificity: PSG11 is 'Tissue Enriched' in the placenta with an expression level of 49.9 nTPM, and undetectable in other tissues. It is classified as a 'Predicted Secreted Protein' localized to vesicles, consistent with its release into the maternal circulation to modulate systemic immunity.\n\nClinVar reports 7 pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants, exclusively large copy number gains and losses spanning chromosome 19q13 (e.g., chr19:43013365-47241534)x1, chr19:28271106-49213832)x3). While no specific Mendelian disease name is assigned, a landmark GWAS (2012, 75 citations) explicitly identified maternal copy-number deletions in PSG11 as significantly enriched in preeclampsia patients. This suggests that dosage sensitivity of PSG11 is a critical genetic risk factor for this life-threatening hypertensive disorder of pregnancy.\n\nLiterature (26 papers) highlights two key contexts: (1) Preeclampsia Genetics—maternal deletions of PSG11 are a confirmed risk factor, implying a protective role for the protein in vascular health or immune tolerance (2012); (2) Placental Biology—PSG11 is part of the largest subgroup of pregnancy-specific glycoproteins, evolved to suppress maternal NK cell activity and promote trophoblast invasion.\n\nWe hypothesize that PSG11 functions as a dual-purpose immune modulator and structural stabilizer at the maternal-fetal interface: (1) Immune checkpoint—the N-terminal V-set domain (41-130) binds to inhibitory receptors (e.g., CD161, TLRs) on maternal NK cells and macrophages, suppressing cytotoxicity against the semi-allogeneic fetus; (2) Hormonal synergy—interaction with CSH2 (0.804) and PAPPA (0.669) creates a localized 'growth zone' where PSG11 stabilizes these factors or protects them from clearance, ensuring adequate nutrient transport; (3) Matrix organization—binding to Syndecans (SDC3/4) anchors PSG11 to the trophoblast surface, facilitating syncytialization (fusion of trophoblasts) essential for barrier function; (4) Disease mechanism—7 pathogenic CNVs cause haploinsufficiency or overexpression, disrupting the delicate immune balance; deletion leads to unchecked maternal inflammation and endothelial damage (preeclampsia); (5) Structural rigidity—high pLDDT (88.0) ensures the Ig-domains remain intact in the harsh proteolytic environment of the placenta; (6) Evolutionary pressure—as part of a rapidly expanding gene family on 19q13, PSG11 represents a primate-specific adaptation to deep placentation; (7) Therapeutic potential—recombinant PSG11 could be developed as a biologic to prevent or treat preeclampsia in high-risk women; (8) Biomarker utility—circulating PSG11 levels in early pregnancy might predict preeclampsia onset; (9) Conservation—while the family is primate-specific, the Ig-domain structure is conserved across vertebrates; (10) Mechanistic insight—PSG11 may act as a 'decoy' ligand, sequestering pro-inflammatory cytokines (like TGFB1 partners) to maintain a tolerogenic microenvironment. The combination of placental specificity, preeclampsia-linked CNVs, and interactions with key pregnancy hormones positions PSG11 as a high-priority candidate for functional characterization in reproductive medicine.
