## Description
MOB3B (MOB Kinase Activator 3B; UniProt Q86TA1) is a compact 216 amino acid member of the MOB/phocein family with only 24 publications, classifying it as a genuine dark gene despite essential roles in Hippo signaling, prostate cancer suppression, and neurodevelopment.\n\nSTRUCTURAL ARCHITECTURE: InterPro analysis reveals canonical MOB kinase activator architecture with the Mob1/phocein family domain (IPR005301/PF03637/SM01388, residues 33-207) forming the core scaffold. The protein belongs to the MOB kinase activator superfamily (IPR036703/G3DSA:1.20.140.30/SSF101152, residues 4-216) with a conserved α/β fold optimized for kinase binding and activation. AlphaFold predicts high confidence structure (global pLDDT 89.8), indicating a well-folded, stable activator protein capable of conformational changes upon binding to NDR/LATS kinases.\n\nINTERACTION NETWORK: STRING-DB identifies medium-confidence interactions with Hippo pathway components and signaling regulators: IFNK (0.646, interferon kappa), LINGO2 (0.614, leucine-rich repeat protein), CNKSR2 (0.587, connector enhancer of kinase suppressor of Ras 2), EQTN (0.554, equatorin), C9orf72 (0.546, chromosome 9 ORF 72, linked to ALS/FTD), LATS1 (0.524, large tumor suppressor kinase 1, core Hippo pathway), LRRC19 (0.506, leucine-rich repeat containing 19), LATS2 (0.488, large tumor suppressor kinase 2), MAPK14 (0.475, p38α MAP kinase), and FBXO30 (0.473, F-box protein). The LATS1/LATS2 connections establish MOB3B as a potential Hippo pathway regulator, analogous to MOB1/MOB2 activators of NDR kinases.\n\nEXPRESSION & LOCALIZATION: HPA shows tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in brain (30.9 nTPM), consistent with neurodevelopmental roles. Expression is detected in many tissues with low cancer specificity. Subcellular localization includes cytosol and intermediate filaments, suggesting roles in cytoskeletal organization and signal transduction scaffolding. Classified as a predicted intracellular protein.\n\nDISEASE LANDSCAPE: ClinVar contains 74 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains at 9p24.3-q21.13 (chr9:208455-72054336 duplications) encompassing MOB3B, plus coding mutations including p.Ser91Ter (nonsense truncation at residue 91). No monogenic disease is yet definitively assigned, but 9p deletions/duplications cause developmental anomalies. Recent 2015-2020 literature establishes tumor suppressor roles in prostate cancer: lower MOB3B levels associate with increased prostate cancer susceptibility and aggressive clinicopathological characteristics. The KDM5A/miRNA-495/YTHDF2/m6A-MOB3B axis facilitates prostate cancer progression, where KDM5A epigenetically represses miR-495, which normally targets YTHDF2 to destabilize m6A-modified MOB3B mRNA—creating an oncogenic cascade that suppresses MOB3B expression.\n\nCONSERVATION: Orthologs confirmed in mouse (Q8VE04, 216 aa) and bovine (Q29RK9, 216 aa), indicating strong evolutionary constraint on the MOB activator function across mammals.\n\nFUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: MOB3B functions as a kinase activator scaffold that binds and activates NDR-family kinases (potentially LATS1/LATS2 in the Hippo pathway), regulating cell proliferation, apoptosis, and centrosome duplication. The brain-enriched expression suggests roles in neuronal development and synaptic plasticity. In prostate cancer, MOB3B acts as a tumor suppressor whose downregulation via the KDM5A/miR-495/YTHDF2/m6A epigenetic axis enables uncontrolled proliferation. The 74 ClinVar CNVs warrant investigation for haploinsufficiency effects on neurodevelopment, potentially contributing to intellectual disability or autism spectrum disorders via disrupted Hippo signaling in neural progenitors. The C9orf72 interaction suggests potential links to ALS/FTD pathogenesis through shared regulatory mechanisms.
