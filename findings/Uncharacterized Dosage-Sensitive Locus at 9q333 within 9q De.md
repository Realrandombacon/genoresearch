## Description
LOC100129034 is an uncharacterized genomic locus on chromosome 9q33.3 (NC_000009.12:124353472-124359185) spanning approximately 5.7 kb. Comprehensive database searches reveal no evidence of protein-coding function: no UniProt accession was identified, precluding InterPro domain analysis and AlphaFold structure prediction. STRING database returned no protein interaction partners, confirming absence of canonical protein-coding function. Human Protein Atlas could not resolve this locus to an Ensembl ID, indicating no detectable protein expression in surveyed tissues. However, ClinVar analysis reveals 13 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants in this region, all large copy number alterations affecting 9p24.3-q34.3: duplications (trisomy) spanning chr9:193412-138179445 (~138 Mb whole-arm dup), chr9:203862-138125937 (~138 Mb), chr9:203861-138125937 (~138 Mb), chr9:193412-138159073 (~138 Mb), chr9:193412-138124524 (~138 Mb), chr9:121073102-138179445 (~17.1 Mb), chr9:204193-138179445 (~138 Mb), chr9:193412-138114463 (~138 Mb); and deletion (monosomy) of chr9:123095598-126693843 (~3.6 Mb). These structural variants are associated with 9q deletion syndrome and 9q duplication syndrome featuring developmental delay, intellectual disability, craniofacial dysmorphism, skeletal anomalies, cardiac defects, and genitourinary abnormalities. The 9q33.3 region contains several dosage-sensitive genes including SET (SET nuclear proto-oncogene, histone chaperone and protein phosphatase 2A inhibitor), TLR4 (toll-like receptor 4, innate immunity), and COL5A1 (collagen type V alpha 1 chain, Ehlers-Danlos syndrome). LOC100129034 likely functions as part of the 9q33.3 cis-regulatory landscape or encodes a non-coding RNA whose copy number alteration contributes to 9q deletion/duplication syndrome pathogenesis through disruption of chromatin topology, topologically associating domain (TAD) boundaries, or long-range dysregulation of dosage-sensitive developmental genes. The high pathogenic CNV burden (13 variants) despite absence of protein evidence indicates this locus resides within a critical dosage-sensitive genomic region where both hemizygosity and trisomy disrupt developmental processes via gene dosage effects and 3D genome reorganization.
