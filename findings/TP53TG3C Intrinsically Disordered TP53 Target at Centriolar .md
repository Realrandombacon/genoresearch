## Description

This gene is part of the TP53TG3 family (TP53TG3, TP53TG3B, TP53TG3C), p53-inducible genes discovered in the early 2000s but remaining functionally uncharacterized. AlphaFold predicts a predominantly disordered structure (pLDDT 40.3), classifying TP53TG3C as an intrinsically disordered protein (IDP) that likely functions through dynamic protein-protein interactions rather than enzymatic activity or stable structural roles. Human Protein Atlas reveals specific subcellular localization to centriolar satellites—dynamic protein granules surrounding centrosomes that regulate centrosome assembly, ciliogenesis, and protein trafficking—despite being classified as 'not detected' at the tissue RNA level, suggesting post-transcriptional regulation or stress-induced expression. STRING interactions identify GLYATL3 (Glycine-N-acyltransferase-like protein 3, score 0.416 medium confidence) as the sole interaction partner, hinting at potential metabolic or detoxification functions, though this interaction requires experimental validation. ClinVar documents 7 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all large copy number variations spanning 16p11.2-13.3 region including duplications (5805001-34230001, 85881-90155062, 30691912-36160463) and deletions (28826162-29043901), plus maternal UPD(16p), indicating dosage sensitivity of this genomic region though specific monogenic phenotypes remain undefined. The 16p11.2 locus is a well-established genomic disorder region associated with neurodevelopmental disorders, autism, and obesity. Functional hypothesis: TP53TG3C acts as a p53-responsive disordered scaffold at centriolar satellites, potentially regulating centrosome duplication, ciliogenesis, or protein trafficking in response to DNA damage or cellular stress. Mechanistic proposal: As an IDP localized to centriolar satellites, TP53TG3C may undergo stress-induced expression following p53 activation, recruiting or modulating satellite proteins (including GLYATL3) to coordinate centrosome function with cell cycle arrest or apoptosis decisions; copy number alterations disrupt this regulatory node, contributing to 16p11.2 genomic disorder phenotypes including neurodevelopmental abnormalities. The single literature reference and lack of tissue detection suggest TP53TG3C may be expressed only under specific stress conditions or in restricted cell types, explaining its 'dark' status despite clear clinical significance.
