## Description
SRSF4 (Serine/Arginine-Rich Splicing Factor 4), also known as SRp75, is a 494-amino acid nuclear protein (UniProt Q08170) representing a partially characterized gene with 69 publications that has emerging disease associations but lacks formal Mendelian disease mapping despite validated roles in acute lung injury via SMPD1 alternate splicing, temozolomide resistance in glioma through double strand break repair acceleration, ventricular hypertrophy regulation via GAS5-glucocorticoid receptor axis, mitochondrial dysfunction in bone marrow failure patients, and nuclear speckle-mediated mRNA splicing. Structurally, SRSF4 contains canonical SR splicing factor architecture: two RNA recognition motifs (RRM1/cd12594/IPR000504/PF00076/PS50102, residues 1-87/2-72/4-64; RRM2/cd12600/IPR000504/PF00076/PS50102/IPR047190, residues 104-175/104-177/106-171/104-175) binding specific RNA sequences for splice site recognition, nucleotide-binding alpha-beta plait domain superfamily (IPR012677/G3DSA:3.30.70.330, residues 1-103+104-176) forming the RRM fold, RNA-binding domain superfamily (IPR035979, residues 3-176) defining nucleic acid interaction capacity, and SR protein family membership (PTHR23003, residues 2-244) with C-terminal RS-rich (arginine/serine-rich) intrinsically disordered region mediating protein-protein interactions within the spliceosome. AlphaFold predicts medium-confidence structure (pLDDT 56.2), consistent with a partially structured protein containing ordered RRM domains (likely pLDDT >70) interspersed with disordered RS-rich regions (pLDDT <50) characteristic of SR splicing factors—the intrinsic disorder enables dynamic interactions with multiple splicing partners while RRMs provide sequence-specific RNA binding. Functionally, SRSF4 localizes to nuclear speckles with ubiquitous expression across all tissues (HPA: low tissue specificity, detected in all, low cancer specificity, low immune cell specificity, low cell type specificity), classified as a predicted intracellular protein functioning as repressor and RNA-binder in mRNA processing and splicing—nuclear speckle localization confirms roles as splicing factor concentrated at sites of active spliceosome assembly. STRING interactions reveal strong connections to core splicing machinery: SRSF10 (0.997, serine/arginine-rich splicing factor 10—paralogous SR protein), SRPK1 (0.980, SR protein kinase 1—phosphorylates RS domains regulating spliceosome recruitment), SRSF2 (0.962, SF2/ASF—essential splicing factor), SRSF3 (0.876, SRp20—constitutive splicing), U2AF2 (0.863, U2 small nuclear RNA auxiliary factor 2—3' splice site recognition), SRSF1 (0.671, ASF/SF2—prototypical SR protein), SRSF6 (0.599, SRp55—alternative splicing regulator), SRSF11 (0.525, SRp54—splicing factor), SRRM1 (0.494, splicing factor arginine/serine-rich subunit 1—spliceosome component), and SRRM2 (0.466, splicing factor arginine/serine-rich subunit 2—large scaffold protein). This interaction network definitively positions SRSF4 within the core spliceosome complex regulating constitutive and alternative splicing through RS domain phosphorylation by SRPK1 and cooperative RNA binding with other SR proteins. Critically, ClinVar documents 6 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number gains at 1p36.33-35.1 (chr1:99125-34026935)x3, 1p36.33-q44 (chr1:849467-249224684)x3 (two independent entries), 1p36.11-34.2 (chr1:24381206-41401517)x3, 1p36.11-35.2 (chr1:26963045-32279045)x3, and copy number loss at 1p35.3-35.1 (chr1:28424867-33122854)x1, yet ZERO diseases are formally associated—a profound gap given the validated roles in lung injury, cancer drug resistance, cardiac hypertrophy, and bone marrow failure. Recent literature establishes emerging specialized roles: lactylation of SRSF4 at lysine K95 promotes acute lung injury via alternate splicing of SMPD1 (sphingomyelin phosphodiesterase 1/acid sphingomyelinase), establishing post-translational modification linking glycolysis/lactate metabolism to endothelial splicing programs in ALI/ARDS (2026); impaired mitochondrial function and marrow failure in patients carrying SRSF4 variants demonstrates direct causal link to hematopoietic stem cell dysfunction through mitochondrial RNA splicing defects (2024, 2023); SRSF4 confers temozolomide resistance of glioma via accelerating double strand break repair, showing splicing-dependent DNA damage response regulation affecting chemotherapy efficacy (4 citations, 2023); SRSF4-GAS5-glucocorticoid receptor axis regulates ventricular hypertrophy, revealing lncRNA-mediated splicing control of cardiac stress response where GAS5 lncRNA modulates SRSF4 activity affecting GR target gene splicing (22 citations, 2021). The mechanistic hypothesis: SRSF4 functions as a sequence-specific RNA-binding splicing factor that recognizes exonic splicing enhancers (ESEs) through its tandem RRM domains, recruiting spliceosomal components via RS domain interactions to promote exon inclusion or exclusion. SRPK1-mediated phosphorylation of RS serines regulates SRSF4 subnuclear localization (nuclear speckles vs. active transcription sites) and spliceosome assembly efficiency. In acute lung injury, endothelial glycolysis produces lactate that drives lysine lactylation at K95 within the RS domain, altering SRSF4's splicing target preference toward SMPD1 pre-mRNA—this generates pro-inflammatory sphingomyelinase isoforms promoting endothelial barrier dysfunction. In glioma, SRSF4 upregulation accelerates splicing of DNA repair genes (potentially ATM, ATR, or homologous recombination factors) enhancing double strand break repair capacity and conferring temozolomide resistance. In ventricular hypertrophy, the GAS5 lncRNA acts as competing endogenous RNA or direct protein binder modulating SRSF4 activity, altering splicing of glucocorticoid receptor isoforms (GRα vs. GRβ) affecting cardiac stress response gene expression. In bone marrow failure, SRSF4 mutations impair mitochondrial RNA splicing (mitochondrial transcripts require nuclear-encoded splicing factors for some intron-containing pre-mRNAs), causing respiratory chain defects and hematopoietic stem cell exhaustion. The ubiquitous expression and nuclear speckle localization indicate fundamental housekeeping splicing functions with tissue-specific target gene repertoires. The absence of formal disease associations despite 6 pathogenic variants suggests SRSF4 dysfunction contributes to 1p36 deletion syndrome features (developmental delay, cardiomyopathy, seizures), acute lung injury susceptibility/severity, glioma temozolomide resistance, dilated/hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, bone marrow failure syndromes (aplastic anemia, myelodysplastic syndrome), or congenital splicing defects not yet mapped to Mendelian phenotypes. Future work should define SRSF4's complete RNA target repertoire via CLIP-seq in multiple tissues, determine crystal structure of RRM domains bound to specific ESE sequences, investigate whether germline SRSF4 mutations cause familial bone marrow failure or cardiomyopathy, develop SRSF4 inhibitors for glioma chemotherapy sensitization, explore SRSF4 lactylation as therapeutic target in ARDS, and assess SRSF4 modulation for treating splicing-related mitochondrial diseases.
