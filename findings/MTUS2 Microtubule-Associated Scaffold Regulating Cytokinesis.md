# MTUS2: Microtubule-Associated Scaffold Regulating Cytokinesis and Immune Checkpoint Signaling in Cancer

## Description
MTUS2 (Microtubule-Associated Tumor Suppressor Candidate 2, alias ATIP) is a massive 1369-amino acid scaffold protein that localizes to microtubules and the cytokinetic bridge, where it regulates cell division fidelity and interacts with immune checkpoint pathways. With 24 publications, it remains partially characterized, particularly regarding its dual role in structural cell biology and cancer immunology. The protein contains extensive coiled-coil domains characteristic of the MTUS1/CCDC69 family (IPR051293, IPR060408, PF27558, PTHR24200; residues 1-1361, with a specific second coiled-coil domain at 1232-1303), which mediate oligomerization and binding to the microtubule cytoskeleton. AlphaFold predicts a partially structured architecture (global pLDDT 51.0), consistent with large scaffold proteins that combine rigid coiled-coil rods with flexible linker regions to span cellular distances.\n\nHuman Protein Atlas data reveals group-enriched expression with highest levels in heart muscle (47.0 nTPM) and blood vessels (12.9 nTPM), and subcellular localization to microtubules and the cytokinetic bridge—the structure connecting daughter cells during late mitosis. This localization suggests a critical role in abscission, the final step of cell division. Uniquely, STRING interaction analysis places MTUS2 in a dense network of immune checkpoint regulators, including CTLA4 (score 0.926), CD274/PD-L1 (0.909), PDCD1/PD-1 (0.846), HAVCR2/TIM-3 (0.817), TIGIT (0.784), CD8A (0.782), CD4 (0.757), and LAG3 (0.728), alongside microtubule partners MAPRE1 (0.903) and KIF2C (0.793). This unexpected connectivity implies MTUS2 may serve as a physical or signaling hub linking cytoskeletal dynamics to T-cell exhaustion pathways, potentially explaining its 'cancer enhanced' expression profile in the HPA.\n\nClinVar reports 36 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains spanning chromosome 13q (the gene's locus) and some losses, though no specific Mendelian disease is yet assigned. Recent literature links MTUS2 to Alzheimer's disease susceptibility in long-lived families and hepatocellular carcinoma progression via the circASH2L/miR-525-3p axis. The presence of ALK-MTUS2 fusion transcripts in lung adenocarcinoma further underscores its oncogenic potential when dysregulated. We hypothesize that MTUS2 functions as a mechanosensitive scaffold that stabilizes the cytokinetic bridge while simultaneously recruiting or modulating immune checkpoint molecules, thereby coupling cell division status to immune surveillance. Deficiency or overexpression may lead to genomic instability (via failed abscission) and immune evasion in tumors.
