## Description
ABCD4 (ATP-Binding Cassette Subfamily D Member 4) is a 606 aa lysosomal membrane protein functioning as the essential transporter for cobalamin (vitamin B12) export from lysosomes to the cytosol. InterPro analysis reveals a canonical ABC transporter architecture with an N-terminal transmembrane domain (IPR011527, aa 27-311) containing 6 predicted helices and a C-terminal ATP-binding cassette domain (IPR003439, aa 389-603) featuring Walker A/B motifs and the ABC signature sequence (IPR017871). AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (pLDDT 86.5) consistent with a half-transporter that likely homodimerizes or heterodimerizes to form a functional pump. STRING interactions identify LMBRD1 (score 0.990) as the primary obligate partner, forming the active transport complex, alongside other ABC transporters (ABCG5, ABCA2) and surprisingly RAD51 (0.854), suggesting a potential link to DNA repair pathways. HPA expression shows ubiquitous detection across all tissues with low specificity, consistent with a housekeeping role in vitamin metabolism. ClinVar contains 89 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants (including frameshifts like p.Gln215fs and missense like p.Thr341Met) causing cblJ type methylmalonic aciduria and homocystinuria, a severe recessive disorder characterized by developmental delay, megaloblastic anemia, and neurological deficits due to intracellular B12 trapping. Literature (93 papers) confirms ABCD4's critical role in B12 trafficking, with recent work highlighting its indispensability for transmembrane helix 6 function and associations with vertebral phenotypes in livestock models.
