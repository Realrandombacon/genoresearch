## Description
PYCR3 (Pyrroline-5-Carboxylate Reductase 3) is a 274-amino acid oxidoreductase enzyme belonging to the PYCR family that catalyzes the NAD(P)H-dependent reduction of Δ1-pyrroline-5-carboxylate (P5C) to L-proline, the final step in proline biosynthesis. InterPro analysis reveals a canonical two-domain architecture characteristic of the PYCR enzyme family: an N-terminal catalytic domain containing a Rossmann-fold NAD(P)-binding domain (IPR036291, residues 9-165; G3DSA:3.40.50.720, residues 3-169; PF03807, residues 10-104) that binds the NAD(P)H cofactor, and a C-terminal dimerization domain (IPR029036, residues 167-271; G3DSA:1.10.3730.10, residues 170-274; PF14748) that mediates homodimerization essential for catalytic activity. The enzyme contains the conserved PYCR catalytic site (IPR053790, residues 227-249) and belongs to the PYCR-like family (IPR000304, residues 9-273; MF_01925, residues 9-271) with GO annotations for pyrroline-5-carboxylate reductase activity (GO:0004735) and L-proline biosynthetic process (GO:0055129). AlphaFold predicts a very high-confidence structure (pLDDT 94.8), consistent with a well-folded, stable enzyme with defined active site architecture suitable for substrate binding and catalysis.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places PYCR3 at the center of the proline metabolism network with exceptionally high confidence interactions: PYCR1 (0.984) and PYCR2 (0.980), its paralogous family members that perform the same enzymatic reaction in different cellular compartments; PRODH (0.969, proline dehydrogenase) and PRODH2 (0.924), which catalyze the reverse reaction (proline oxidation to P5C) in the proline cycle; ALDH4A1 (0.968, aldehyde dehydrogenase 4 family member A1), which converts P5C to glutamate; ALDH18A1 (0.918, aldehyde-dehydrogenase 18 family member A1/P5CS), which catalyzes the rate-limiting step converting glutamate to P5C; P4HA1 (0.911) and P4HA2 (0.918), prolyl 4-hydroxylases that hydroxylate proline residues in collagen; and LAP3 (0.901, leucine aminopeptidase 3). This interaction profile reveals PYCR3's integration into the complete proline metabolic cycle: synthesis (ALDH18A1→PYCR3), degradation (PRODH→ALDH4A1), and utilization (P4HA1/2 for collagen maturation). The PYCR1/2 interactions suggest potential hetero-oligomerization or functional redundancy among family members.\n\nHuman Protein Atlas classifies PYCR3 as a metabolic enzyme with low tissue specificity (detected in many tissues), consistent with a housekeeping role in amino acid biosynthesis. Subcellular localization includes cytosol, cytokinetic bridge, and mitotic spindle, suggesting potential roles beyond metabolism in cell division. The immune cell enhanced expression pattern indicates elevated proline metabolism in immune cells, potentially supporting rapid proliferation or specialized metabolic demands during immune activation.\n\nClinVar reports 61 pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number variations spanning 8q24 (coordinates include chr8:126446968-146295771 gain, chr8:142893048-144990940 loss, chr8:111432348-146295771 gain, chr8:113392581-146364022 gain, chr8:141419599-146295771 gain). The 8q24 region is a well-known cancer susceptibility locus, and CNVs in this region are associated with various malignancies. While no specific monogenic disease is named solely for PYCR3, the pathogenic variant classification suggests PYCR3 dosage is clinically relevant, potentially contributing to 8q24 duplication/deletion syndrome phenotypes or cancer predisposition through altered proline metabolism.\n\nLiterature analysis (23 papers) confirms PYCR3 as a partially characterized dark gene with emerging functional insights in cancer metabolism. Recent 2024 work demonstrated that PYCR3 modulates mitochondrial DNA copy number to drive proliferation and doxorubicin resistance in triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC), establishing a direct link between proline biosynthesis and mitochondrial function. A 2025 study characterized PYCR3 expression in TNBC and its role in prognosis, tumor progression, and immune regulation. A 2022 study provided expression and kinetic characterization of PYCR3, confirming its enzymatic activity. Pan-cancer analysis (2024) deciphered PYCR family effects on cell function, prognostic value, and immune infiltration in clear cell renal cell carcinoma. Multi-omics analysis (2025) revealed a protective role of endogenous proline in sheep oocyte vitrification, linking PYCR3 to reproductive biology.\n\nWe hypothesize that PYCR3 functions as a metabolic hub connecting proline biosynthesis to mitochondrial homeostasis, collagen production, and cancer progression. The canonical role is P5C reduction to proline using NAD(P)H, regenerating NAD(P)+ and maintaining redox balance. However, emerging evidence suggests additional functions: (1) mtDNA copy number regulation—PYCR3 may localize to mitochondria or interact with mitochondrial nucleoid proteins to maintain mtDNA levels, explaining the doxorubicin resistance phenotype (doxorubicin targets mtDNA); (2) collagen biosynthesis support—via P4HA1/2 interactions, PYCR3 may channel newly synthesized proline directly to prolyl hydroxylases for collagen maturation, critical for tumor stroma formation and metastasis; (3) proline cycle coupling—PYCR3 works with PRODH/ALDH4A1 to create a futile cycle that generates ATP and ROS, supporting cancer cell survival under stress; (4) immune modulation—elevated expression in immune cells suggests proline metabolism supports immune cell proliferation or function, potentially affecting tumor immune microenvironment. The 8q24 CNVs may cause dosage-sensitive phenotypes affecting proline metabolism, potentially contributing to developmental disorders (proline is critical for collagen, neurotransmission) or cancer (altered metabolism supports tumor growth). PYCR3 represents a candidate metabolic oncogene in TNBC, a potential biomarker for doxorubicin resistance, a regulator of mitochondrial genetics, and an uncharacterized contributor to 8q24 syndrome phenotypes through proline metabolism disruption.
