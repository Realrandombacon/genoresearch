## Description
CCDC27 (Coiled-Coil Domain Containing 27, UniProt Q2M243, Gene ID: 148870) is a 656 amino acid protein encoded on chromosome 1p36.32 (GRCh38: 3752449-3771644). InterPro analysis identifies a large coiled-coil domain characteristic of the CCDC27/FHAD1 family (IPR060375, PF28447, residues 93-643) and membership in the coiled-coil and Forkhead-associated (FHA) domain-containing protein superfamily (IPR052642, PTHR18853, residues 48-654). The enrichment for coiled-coil partners (C1orf174, C10orf120, C5orf47, C2orf83) suggests CCDC27 functions as a scaffold in large oligomeric complexes, potentially involved in cytoskeletal organization or nuclear architecture. The TRIM35 and TP73 links hint at roles in ubiquitination pathways or cell cycle regulation.\n\nHuman Protein Atlas shows striking testis-specific expression (16.2 nTPM), classified as "tissue enriched" and "detected in single" tissue, with no significant expression in other tissues or immune cells. Subcellular localization includes nucleoplasm and cytosol, consistent with dual roles in nuclear scaffolding and cytoplasmic complex assembly. This restricted expression pattern strongly implicates CCDC27 in spermatogenesis, meiosis, or sperm flagella formation.\n\nClinVar analysis reveals an extreme burden of 88 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants—the highest observed in this study cohort. Variants are overwhelmingly large copy number alterations spanning 1p36, including massive deletions (1p36.33-36.22, ~8-9Mb) and duplications (1p36.33-35.1, ~34Mb), alongside rare SNVs (e.g., c.504C>T p.Gly168=). The sheer volume of CNVs reflects the high density of dosage-sensitive genes in the 1p36 region (the most common terminal deletion syndrome locus), though CCDC27 itself may contribute to haploinsufficiency phenotypes. Associated conditions include 1p36 deletion syndrome featuring severe intellectual disability, hypotonia, distinctive facial features, and cardiac defects.\n\nAlphaFold structure prediction unavailable (500 server error).\n\nHYPOTHESIS: CCDC27 functions as a testis-specific coiled-coil scaffold protein essential for spermatogenesis, potentially organizing meiotic chromosomes or assembling sperm flagellar axonemes. The extensive coiled-coil domain (residues 93-643) mediates homo-oligomerization or hetero-oligomerization with partners like C1orf174 and TERB1 (critical for telomere-led chromosome movement in meiosis). The FHAD family association suggests a role in phospho-dependent signaling cascades regulating germ cell development. The extreme CNV burden (88 variants) indicates that CCDC27 resides in a genomic "hotspot" where large structural variants are frequent; while many phenotypes are driven by neighboring genes (e.g., PRDM16, GABRD), CCDC27 loss may specifically contribute to male infertility or subtle neurodevelopmental deficits masked by broader 1p36 syndrome features. The testis-restricted expression (16.2 nTPM) supports a specialized role in gametogenesis, possibly stabilizing microtubule arrays via interactions with SAXO1 or TRIP11.
