## Description
RPAP1 (RNA Polymerase II Associated Protein 1; UniProt Q9BWH6), also known as Rba50, is a massive 1393 amino acid nuclear protein with only 21 publications, representing a genuine dark gene functioning as an essential scaffold that coordinates the assembly of the RNA Polymerase II (Pol II) pre-initiation complex and facilitates the nuclear import of Pol II subunits through its C-terminal TPR-repeat domain and interactions with the GPN GTPase machinery.\n\nSTRUCTURAL ARCHITECTURE: InterPro analysis reveals a multi-domain architecture optimized for scaffolding large transcription complexes. The N-terminal region contains a conserved RPAP1 N-terminal domain (IPR013930/PF08621, residues 225-267) of unknown specific function but high conservation across metazoa. The central region contains a conserved RPAP1 C-terminal domain (IPR013929/PF08620, residues 356-421). The C-terminal region is dominated by multiple Tetratricopeptide Repeat (TPR) motifs (IPR057989/PF25766, residues 1102-1322), classic protein-protein interaction modules forming a superhelical structure ideal for binding partner proteins. The full-length protein belongs to the RPAP1/Rba50 family (IPR039913/PTHR21483, residues 1-1388) defined by roles in Pol II transcription. AlphaFold predicts high confidence structure (global pLDDT 74.4), indicating a well-folded scaffold with stable TPR repeats suitable for assembling multi-protein complexes, though with some flexible linkers typical of large scaffolding proteins.\n\nINTERACTION NETWORK: STRING-DB identifies very high-to-medium confidence interactions exclusively within the Pol II assembly and import machinery: GPN2 (0.958, GPN-loop GTPase 2—highest confidence; essential for Pol II nuclear import), RPAP2 (0.932, RNA Polymerase II Associated Protein 2—phosphatase regulating Pol II CTD), RUVBL1 (0.854, RuvB-like AAA ATPase 1—chromatin remodeling/TIP60 complex), POLR2M (0.841, DNA-directed RNA polymerase II subunit M—common Pol II subunit), INTS3 (0.829, Integrator complex subunit 3—RNA processing), RUVBL2 (0.808, RuvB-like AAA ATPase 2—partner of RUVBL1), GPN1 (0.777, GPN-loop GTPase 1—Pol II import), POLR2J (0.666, DNA-directed RNA polymerase II subunit J), RPAP3 (0.595, RNA Polymerase II Associated Protein 3—PIH1D2, chaperone for snoRNPs), and POLR2C (0.542, DNA-directed RNA polymerase II subunit C). This interaction profile establishes RPAP1 as a central hub connecting the GPN GTPase import system (GPN1/2) to the core Pol II enzyme (POLR2M/J/C) and regulatory factors (RPAP2, RUVBL1/2, INTS3).\n\nEXPRESSION & LOCALIZATION: HPA shows ubiquitous expression (detected in all tissues) with low tissue specificity, low cancer specificity, low immune cell specificity, and low cell type specificity. (2) 2004 characterization (71 cites) identifies RPAP1 as a novel Pol II-associated protein affinity purified with wild-type and mutated polymerase subunits; (3) Emerging evidence suggests RPAP1 acts as a chaperone facilitating the nuclear import of Pol II via GPN1/2 interaction, a rate-limiting step in transcription capacity.\n\nCONSERVATION: Orthologs confirmed in mouse (Q80TE0, 1409 aa—101.1% length conservation), rat (Q3T1I9, 1400 aa), cattle (A0JN53, 1395 aa), and Schizosaccharomyces pombe (homologous function in rba50), indicating extreme evolutionary constraint on the scaffold architecture from yeast to humans for conserved Pol II assembly functions.\n\nFUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: RPAP1 functions as an essential Pol II assembly and import scaffold through a coordinated mechanism: (1) GPN coupling—the C-terminal TPR repeats (residues 1102-1322) bind GPN1 and GPN2 (0.777-0.958), the GTPases responsible for escorting Pol II into the nucleus; Therapeutic strategies could target RPAP1-TPR interfaces to modulate Pol II availability in hyper-transcriptional cancers.
