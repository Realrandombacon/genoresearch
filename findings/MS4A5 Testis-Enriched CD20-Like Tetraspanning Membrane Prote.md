# MS4A5: Testis-Enriched CD20-Like Tetraspanning Membrane Protein Regulating Spermatogenesis and Calcium Signaling via MS4A Family Oligomerization Network

**Date:** 2026-04-06T14:14:10.358695

**Quality Score:** 4.05/10  (E=6.0, D=2.1) [MODERATE]

## Description
MS4A5 (Membrane Spanning 4-Domains Subfamily A Member 5, also CD20-L2 or TETM4) is a 200 amino acid tetraspanning membrane protein (UniProt Q9H3V2) with a well-folded transmembrane architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 76.0, high confidence), functioning as a testis-specific receptor potentially involved in spermatogenesis, sperm maturation, and calcium-dependent signaling pathways characteristic of the CD20/MS4A family.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals MS4A5 contains a canonical CD20-Like Transmembrane Domain (IPR007237/PF04103, residues 49-189) belonging to the Membrane-Spanning 4-Domains Subfamily A (IPR030417/PTHR23320, residues 25-196). This domain topology comprises four transmembrane helices with intracellular N- and C-termini, creating a pore-like structure characteristic of MS4A family members. The CD20-like fold (GO:0016020) typically forms calcium-permeable channels or scaffolds for signaling complexes. MS4A5 shares structural features with CD20 (MS4A1), the B-cell marker and therapeutic target, suggesting conserved ion channel or signaling functions repurposed for reproductive biology.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places MS4A5 within the MS4A family oligomerization network: strongest interactions with OOSP4B (0.695, oocyte-specific protein 4B, female gamete protein), TMEM176A (0.597, transmembrane protein 176A, lysosomal cation channel), FAM71D (0.579, family with sequence similarity 71 member D), MS4A4E (0.579, membrane-spanning 4-domains A4E, mast cell regulator), OOSP2 (0.557, oocyte-specific protein 2), MS4A18 (0.532), MS4A15 (0.519), MS4A1 (0.503, CD20, B-cell marker), MS4A2 (0.418, FcεRIβ subunit), and MS4A6E (0.415). The MS4A1-MS4A2-MS4A4E-MS4A6E cluster represents the core immune MS4A network, while OOSP interactions suggest unexpected links to gamete biology. TMEM176A partnership indicates potential roles in intracellular calcium homeostasis.\n\nHPA expression shows strong testis enrichment (41.5 nTPM) with tissue-enriched specificity and single-tissue distribution, indicating exclusive or near-exclusive expression in male germ cells. This pattern suggests specialized roles in spermatogenesis, sperm maturation, or fertilization. The protein is classified as a receptor and predicted membrane protein, consistent with signal transduction functions at the plasma membrane.\n\nClinVar documents 10 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, exclusively chromosome 11q12.2 copy number gains and one deletion. These CNVs (e.g., 11q12.1-13.3 duplications, 11p11.12-q12.2 gains) suggest dosage sensitivity, with overexpression potentially causing male infertility, testicular dysgenesis, or disrupted spermatogenesis through altered calcium signaling or membrane dynamics.\n\nConservation is evident in primates (chimpanzee A0A6D2XG89, 200 aa) and mammals (hyena A0A6G1A7V8, 195 aa; hamster A0AAU9ZNQ4, 156-199 aa), indicating evolutionary conservation of reproductive functions across placental mammals.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: MS4A5 functions as a testis-specific calcium channel or signaling scaffold regulating spermatogenesis and sperm function. The tetraspanning topology creates a pore analogous to CD20, potentially mediating calcium influx critical for sperm capacitation, acrosome reaction, or motility. OOSP interactions are intriguing given OOSP proteins are oocyte-specific; this may reflect co-evolution of male-female gamete recognition systems or shared ancestral functions in germ cell development. MS4A family oligomerization suggests MS4A5 forms heteromers with other MS4A proteins (MS4A1, MS4A2, MS4A4E) to modulate channel properties or signaling specificity. TMEM176A interaction links MS4A5 to lysosomal calcium stores, potentially coordinating intracellular calcium release during sperm maturation. The high-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 76.0) confirms stable transmembrane helices optimized for membrane insertion. CNV-mediated overexpression may cause constitutive calcium influx, triggering premature capacitation or apoptosis of germ cells, while haploinsufficiency impairs calcium-dependent sperm functions, causing male infertility. The 11q12.2 locus is a hotspot for reproductive disorders, and MS4A5 represents a candidate gene for idiopathic male infertility.
