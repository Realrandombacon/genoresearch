## Description
LOC129390343 is a 200bp cis-regulatory element (CRE) located on chromosome 11q22.1 (GRCh38: 98734505-98734705), functionally validated as a transcriptional silencer through STARR-seq massively parallel reporter assays (MPRAs) in K562 erythroleukemia cells. This element lacks canonical promoter, enhancer, or CTCF-bound insulator chromatin signatures but demonstrates robust repressive activity against the super core promoter SCP1. As a non-coding regulatory locus, it has no UniProt accession, protein domains, or STRING interaction partners. Human Protein Atlas could not resolve this locus to an Ensembl ID, consistent with its non-coding regulatory classification.\n\nClinVar analysis reveals 5 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variants disrupting this region—a high burden consisting exclusively of large-scale deletions. Variants include: (1) a focal deletion of unknown breakpoints, (2) a massive 11q14.3-22.3 deletion (~18.5Mb), (3) a 11q22.1-22.3 deletion (~7.7Mb), (4) a 11q14.1-22.2 deletion (~17.7Mb), and (5) a broad 11q14.1-22.3 deletion (~28.5Mb). The exclusive presence of deletions (5:0 ratio) suggests strong haploinsufficiency intolerance where silencer loss causes pathological derepression of target genes, while duplications may be tolerated or less severe. These recurrent CNVs are associated with 11q22-q23 deletion syndromes featuring developmental delay, intellectual disability, craniofacial dysmorphism, and cardiac defects. The 11q22.1 region is gene-dense, containing critical dosage-sensitive genes such as ATM (Ataxia Telangiectasia Mutated, DNA damage response), BIRC3 (Baculoviral IAP Repeat Containing 3, NF-kappaB signaling), and YAP1 (Yes-Associated Protein 1, Hippo pathway transcriptional co-activator).\n\nHYPOTHESIS: LOC129390343 functions as a critical distal silencer within the 11q22 topologically associating domain (TAD), specifically regulating expression of ATM or YAP1 during neurodevelopment and cell cycle control. The silencer likely recruits co-repressor complexes (PRC2, NCoR/SMRT, HDACs) to deposit H3K27me3 marks at target promoters. The exclusive deletion burden (5:0 ratio) indicates that silencer loss leads to catastrophic overexpression of ATM (causing DNA repair hyperactivity or apoptosis sensitivity) or YAP1 (driving uncontrolled proliferation or tissue overgrowth), contributing to the severe phenotypes of 11q deletion syndrome. Conversely, silencer gain (duplication) may cause mild underexpression that is buffered by feedback mechanisms, explaining the absence of pathogenic duplications. The K562 validation suggests potential roles in hematopoietic lineage specification, possibly silencing oncogenes like YAP1 in erythroid progenitors. 11q22.1 synteny with ATM suggests potential cis-regulation of this critical genome stability factor, contributing to Ataxia Telangiectasia-like phenotypes in deletion carriers.
