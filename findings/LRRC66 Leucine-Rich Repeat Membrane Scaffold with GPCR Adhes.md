## Description
LRRC66 encodes an 880-amino acid leucine-rich repeat (LRR) transmembrane protein (UniProt Q68CR7) located on chromosome 4q12. The protein belongs to the LRR and transmembrane domain-containing family (IPR050541, PTHR24369, residues 36-711), suggesting membrane anchoring with extracellular protein-protein interaction capability.

InterPro domain analysis reveals multiple LRR motifs: canonical LRR domains (IPR001611, PF00560, PF13855) at positions 86-236, plus LRR typical subtype repeats (IPR003591, SM00369) at positions 108-193. The N-terminal region contains ribonuclease inhibitor-like folds (G3DSA:3.80.10.10, residues 23-291), indicating structural similarity to LRR solenoid architectures that mediate ligand binding.

STRING interaction network shows LRRC66 associates with adhesion GPCR family members: ADGRL1/LPHN1 (0.514), ADGRL2/LPHN2 (0.514), ADGRL3/LPHN3 (0.514), suggesting roles in synaptic adhesion or GPCR signaling scaffolding. Additional partners include SUSD3 (0.526, sushi domain-containing tumor suppressor), GLOD4 (0.529, glioma tumor suppressor), PDCL2 (0.479, photoreceptor protein), and DCUN1D4 (0.476, cullin neddylation factor).

HPA expression shows tissue-enhanced pattern with highest expression in intestine (20.8 nTPM) and skeletal muscle (7.4 nTPM). Cancer-enhanced expression suggests potential oncogenic or tumor suppressor roles. AlphaFold structure prediction yields low confidence (pLDDT 46.9), indicating intrinsically disordered regions or flexible LRR solenoid architecture typical of protein interaction scaffolds.

ClinVar contains 15 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all copy number losses or gains affecting 4q12, indicating dosage sensitivity. BLAST analysis shows 47.7% sequence identity to mouse ortholog (E-value: 0.0, score: 686), demonstrating moderate evolutionary conservation.

Functional hypothesis: LRRC66 functions as a membrane-anchored LRR scaffold protein that tethers adhesion GPCR complexes (ADGRL1-3/LPHN family) to regulatory partners (SUSD3, GLOD4, PDCL2) at synaptic or cell-cell junctions. The LRR solenoid mediates protein-protein interactions while the transmembrane domain anchors the complex. Dosage alterations disrupt synaptic adhesion signaling, potentially causing neurodevelopmental or muscular phenotypes.
