# CCDC12: U2-Type Spliceosomal Coiled-Coil Protein with Cwf18 Domain, Ubiquitous Expression, and 10 ClinVar Variants

## Description
CCDC12 encodes a 166 amino acid coiled-coil domain-containing protein that functions as a component of the U2-type spliceosomal complex and post-mRNA release spliceosomal complex. The protein contains the mRNA splicing factor Cwf18-like domain (IPR013169, residues 1-166) and the cwf18 pre-mRNA splicing factor Pfam family (PF08315, residues 11-143), placing it within the PTHR31551 pre-mRNA splicing factor CWF18 family. AlphaFold prediction shows confident folding (pLDDT 75.7) across the full 166 aa length, consistent with a stable coiled-coil structure. HPA data indicates low tissue specificity with expression detected in all tissues, nucleoplasmic localization, and cell type-enhanced specificity at the single-cell level. STRING interactions reveal strong associations with core spliceosomal components: CDC40 (0.997), CRNKL1 (0.997), CDC5L (0.981), EFTUD2 (0.973), PPIL1 (0.903), TCERG1 (0.900), PRPF19 (0.852), SRRM1 (0.852), RNF113A (0.848), and SYF2 (0.773). These partners form the Nineteen Complex (NTC) and related spliceosomal assemblies that catalyze pre-mRNA splicing. ClinVar contains 10 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly copy number gains/losses spanning 3p21.31, though no specific monogenic disease phenotype is yet assigned. Mouse ortholog Q8R344 (166 aa) shows high conservation. Functional hypothesis: CCDC12 serves as a structural scaffold within the U2-type spliceosome, stabilizing protein-protein interactions through its coiled-coil domain during spliceosome assembly, catalytic activation, or post-catalytic release. The Cwf18 homology suggests functional conservation with yeast Cwf18p, which participates in NTC recruitment and spliceosome fidelity. Ubiquitous expression indicates essential housekeeping splicing function across all cell types. ClinVar CNVs may disrupt splicing fidelity genome-wide, potentially contributing to developmental disorders or cancer through aberrant mRNA processing.
