## Description
LOC126860000 is an uncharacterized cis-regulatory element (CRE) at chromosome 7p14.1 (genomic coordinates 37376201-37377400, ~1.2kb span). This locus is NOT a protein-coding gene but a functional regulatory DNA element validated through STARR-seq massively parallel reporter assays (MPRAs). The gene_info summary identifies this as a P300/CBP STRONGLY-DEPENDENT GROUP 1 ENHANCER that was experimentally validated in HCT116 colorectal carcinoma cells. Critically, this enhancer depends on BRD2, BRD4, P300/CBP, MED14, and CDK7 cofactors with STRONG DEPENDENCE on P300/CBP (histone acetyltransferases that acetylate histone H3K27 to open chromatin and recruit transcriptional machinery). This cofactor profile suggests it operates through chromatin remodeling and histone acetylation mechanisms. ClinVar contains 6 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants affecting this locus through large-scale chromosomal rearrangements. STRIKINGLY, this locus shows LOSS-DOMINANT pathogenicity: 4 copy number LOSSES (chr7:32678391-41044983x1, chr7:35460776-42013800x1, chr7:37303478-48660738x1, chr7:54185-159282390x1) versus only 2 copy number GAINS (chr7:33328312-62377476x3, chr7:54185-41875885x3). This 4:2 loss-to-gain ratio (67% deletions) contrasts with amplification-dominant enhancers and suggests tumor suppressor or developmental gene regulation where haploinsufficiency drives disease. The 7p region contains EGFR (7p12.1, epidermal growth factor receptor, frequently amplified in glioblastoma), BRAF (7q34, serine/threonine kinase), and multiple developmental transcription factors. No UniProt accession exists (non-coding DNA), STRING found no interactions (not a protein), HPA could not resolve (not protein-coding), and PubMed has zero publications for this specific locus. This represents a genuine dark regulatory element with clinical relevance — the P300/CBP-dependent enhancer activity combined with loss-dominant pathogenicity suggests LOC126860000 functions as a dosage-critical enhancer controlling 7p tumor suppressor or developmental gene expression, where deletion causes haploinsufficiency.
