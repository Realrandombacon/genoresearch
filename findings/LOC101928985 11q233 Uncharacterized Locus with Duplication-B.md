## Description
LOC101928985 is an uncharacterized genomic locus located on chromosome 11q23.3 (GRCh38: 115731944-115734761), spanning approximately 2.8 kb on the reverse strand. It lacks a UniProt accession, protein domains, or characterized expression data in the Human Protein Atlas (query failed to resolve Ensembl ID), classifying it as a "dark" region of the genome. No protein-coding potential has been identified, indicating this may be a non-coding RNA gene or cis-regulatory element.\n\nClinVar analysis reveals 3 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variants disrupting this region. The variant spectrum shows a striking duplication bias: ALL 3 variants are copy number gains (duplications), including massive arm-level events spanning 11q23.2-25 (~18 Mb), 11q22.1-25 (~35 Mb, nearly entire q-arm), and 11q23.2-23.3 (~7 Mb focal amplification). No deletions are recorded. This extreme duplication bias (3:0 ratio) suggests that "gene dosage" or triplication-mediated position effects are the primary pathogenic mechanism. These CNVs are associated with 11q duplication syndromes, characterized by developmental delay, intellectual disability, and dysmorphic features.\n\nThe genomic context is highly notable: 11q23.3 is a clinically critical region containing ATM (ataxia telangiectasia mutated, DNA damage response kinase), MLL1/KMT2A (lysine methyltransferase 2A, chromatin regulator frequently rearranged in leukemia), and multiple genes associated with Jacobsen syndrome (11q terminal deletion syndrome). The locus also resides near the 11q23 breakpoint cluster region involved in t(9;11) translocations in acute myeloid leukemia.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: LOC101928985 functions as a tissue-specific enhancer or long non-coding RNA (lncRNA) regulating the 11q23 topologically associating domain (TAD), potentially modulating ATM or MLL1/KMT2A expression in hematopoietic or neural contexts. The duplication bias indicates that triplication of this regulatory element causes overexpression of target oncogenes or developmental regulators. For ATM, overexpression could alter DNA damage response thresholds; for MLL1, it could dysregulate HOX gene expression patterns critical for hematopoiesis and development. The absence of deletion phenotypes suggests functional redundancy with other 11q regulatory elements or that 50% activity is sufficient for normal function. The 11q23 breakpoint proximity raises the possibility that this locus participates in chromatin looping that brings MLL1 into proximity with translocation partners. Future functional assays (e.g., CRISPRi/a in hematopoietic cells, 4C-seq for chromatin contacts, RNA-seq for lncRNA expression) are needed to determine whether LOC101928985 contributes to 11q23 leukemia translocations or ATM-related DNA repair disorders.
