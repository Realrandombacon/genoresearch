## Description
CAVIN1 (Caveolae-Associated Protein 1; also known as Polymerase I and Transcript Release Factor, PTRF; UniProt Q6NZI2) is a 390 amino acid essential peripheral membrane protein with ~150 publications, representing a PARTIALLY DARK gene encoding a critical dual-function regulator of caveolae biogenesis and RNA polymerase I transcription termination. Despite its established roles in membrane curvature generation and ribosomal RNA synthesis, CAVIN1 remains incompletely characterized regarding the precise mechanistic switch between its cytoplasmic caveolar function and nuclear transcriptional role, the structural basis of its coiled-coil oligomerization, and the full spectrum of lipid signaling pathways it modulates in lipodystrophy. The protein functions as a molecular scaffold that oligomerizes to shape plasma membrane invaginations while simultaneously regulating rRNA production, linking cellular metabolic state to membrane architecture.\n\nSTRUCTURAL ARCHITECTURE: InterPro analysis reveals CAVIN1 contains a defining coiled-coil rich architecture characteristic of the cavin family: (1) N-terminal region (residues 1-47) — highly charged, likely intrinsically disordered, containing nuclear localization signals for transcriptional function; (2) Central Coiled-Coil/Oligomerization Domain (IPR026752/PF15237/PTHR15240, residues 48-320/~31-389, ~272 aa) — extensive heptad repeat region driving homo-oligomerization into higher-order structures essential for caveolar coat assembly; recent 2025 nanobody studies confirm this domain exhibits significant structural flexibility enabling dynamic membrane remodeling; (3) C-terminal region (residues 321-390, ~69 aa) — acidic tail potentially involved in membrane binding or partner recruitment. AlphaFold predicts medium confidence structure (global pLDDT 67.4), indicating a partially structured protein with stable coiled-coil cores surrounded by flexible regions—consistent with scaffolding proteins requiring conformational plasticity for membrane deformation and partner exchange.\n\nINTERACTION NETWORK: STRING-DB reveals high-confidence interactions with caveolins, lipodystrophy factors, and membrane trafficking proteins: CAV3 (0.979—highest; Caveolin-3, muscle-specific caveolin isoform suggesting tissue-specific complex assembly), EGFR (0.885; Epidermal Growth Factor Receptor, linking caveolae to growth factor signaling and endocytosis), BSCL2 (0.858; Seipin, ER membrane protein essential for lipid droplet biogenesis, connecting caveolae to lipid storage), CAV1 (0.843; Caveolin-1, ubiquitous caveolin isoform forming hetero-oligomeric complexes with CAVIN1), AGPAT2 (0.841; 1-Acylglycerol-3-Phosphate O-Acyltransferase 2, enzyme deficient in congenital generalized lipodystrophy type 1, placing CAVIN1 in shared lipodystrophy pathway), TRIM72 (0.839; Tripartite Motif-Containing Protein 72, membrane repair protein cooperating with cavins in injury response), GHDC (0.832; G-Hydroxybutyrate Dehydrogenase Complex Component, metabolic enzyme with potential caveolar localization), CAVIN3 (0.595; Caveolae-Associated Protein 3, paralogous cavin family member), CAV2 (0.588; Caveolin-2, caveolin partner), and EHD2 (0.470; EH-Domain Containing 2, ATPase regulating caveolar dynamics). This interaction profile definitively places CAVIN1 at the intersection of membrane curvature, lipid metabolism, and signal transduction: CAV1/CAV3/CAVIN3 connections (0.843/0.979/0.595) confirm core caveolar coat assembly; BSCL2/AGPAT2 links (0.858/0.841) integrate caveolae with lipid droplet biogenesis explaining lipodystrophy phenotypes; EGFR/TRIM72 connections (0.885/0.839) link to growth signaling and membrane repair.\n\nEXPRESSION & LOCALIZATION: HPA classifies CAVIN1 as a disease-related gene with predicted intracellular and plasma membrane localization, shuttling between cytoplasm (caveolae) and nucleus (nucleolus). RNA tissue specificity is low, detected in all tissues, reflecting universal requirement for caveolae/membrane homeostasis. Low cancer specificity suggests baseline expression, though literature documents dysregulation in glioblastoma and colorectal cancer. Not detected in immune cells indicates specialized function in non-immune lineages. Cell type enhanced single cell type specificity indicates variable expression correlating with caveolae abundance (high in adipocytes, muscle, endothelium). HPA annotates biological processes as transcription, transcription regulation, and transcription termination; molecular functions include RNA-binding and rRNA-binding—reflecting its dual nuclear role often overlooked in membrane-focused studies.\n\nDISEASE LANDSCAPE: ClinVar contains 31 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including frameshift mutations (c.462del p.Met154fs, c.1061_1091del p.Glu354fs, c.141_148del p.Asp47fs, c.459_462delinsAAT p.Met154fs) disrupting the coiled-coil oligomerization domain, nonsense mutation c.512C>A (p.Ser171Ter) truncating the central domain, missense variants (c.729G>C p.Lys243Asn, c.943G>A p.Val315Ile, c.187G>T p.Gly63Trp) affecting structural integrity or partner binding, silent variant c.339G>A (p.Lys113=) potentially affecting splicing, and large copy number loss on 17q12-22 (chr17:41196270-41277589 x1 spanning ~81 kb). No specific monogenic disease is definitively assigned solely to CAVIN1 in ClinVar summary, but literature definitively links CAVIN1 mutations to Congenital Generalized Lipodystrophy Type 4 (CGL4) characterized by near-absent adipose tissue, severe insulin resistance, hypertriglyceridemia, hepatic steatosis, and muscular hypertrophy. Additional phenotypes include cardiac arrhythmias (long QT syndrome) due to caveolae defects in cardiomyocytes, and myopathy. Recent 2025 studies highlight: (1) CAVIN1 role in colorectal cancer tumor relapse as prognostic biomarker; (2) CAVIN1/CAV1 axis mediating NLRC5-dependent cardiomyocyte autophagy post-infarction; (3) Structural flexibility of N-terminal coiled-coil domain revealed by nanobody studies enabling regulated interactions; (4) CAVIN1 as therapeutic target in glioblastoma via serum exosome biomarker function. The essential nature of caveolae suggests complete loss causes severe lipodystrophy and cardiac dysfunction, while hypomorphic alleles cause variable metabolic phenotypes.\n\nCONSERVATION: CAVIN1 orthologs confirmed across vertebrates (mouse O54724 392 aa, rat P85125 392 aa) with >90% sequence identity across coiled-coil domains, indicating extreme evolutionary constraint on the caveolar scaffolding mechanism since last common mammalian ancestor. The cavin family is vertebrate-specific, absent in invertebrates which lack canonical caveolae.\n\nFUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: CAVIN1 functions as a dual-localization regulator coordinating membrane architecture and transcription through context-dependent mechanism: (1) Cytoplasmic pool—CAVIN1 oligomerizes via coiled-coil domain (residues 48-320) forming hetero-complexes with CAV1/CAV3 (0.843/0.979) to generate caveolar invaginations; (9) Cardiac mechanism—CAVIN1/CAV1 axis regulates autophagy post-MI via NLRC5; (10) Therapeutic angle—CAVIN1 restoration could reverse lipodystrophy; exosomal CAVIN1 as liquid biopsy marker; (11) Dark gene aspects—despite lipodystrophy literature, precise switch mechanism between nuclear/cytoplasmic pools undefined;
