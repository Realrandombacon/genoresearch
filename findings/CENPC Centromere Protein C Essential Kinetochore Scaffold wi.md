## Description
CENPC (Centromere Protein C; UniProt Q03188) is a 943 amino acid essential kinetochore scaffold protein with 58 publications, representing a moderately-characterized core component of the Constitutive Centromere Associated Network (CCAN). Unlike enzymatic centromere proteins, CENPC functions primarily as a structural bridge linking centromeric chromatin (CENP-A nucleosomes) to the outer kinetochore microtubule-binding machinery, ensuring accurate chromosome segregation through its tripartite domain architecture and extensive intrinsic disorder enabling multivalent protein interactions.\n\nSTRUCTURAL ARCHITECTURE: InterPro analysis reveals CENPC contains a distinctive tripartite domain organization characteristic of inner kinetochore scaffolds: (1) N-terminal kinetochore assembly domain (IPR028052/PF15622, residues 7-292, ~285 aa) — mediates binding to CENP-A nucleosomes and CCAN recruitment; (2) Middle DNMT3B-binding domain (IPR028931/PF15620, residues 296-551, ~255 aa) — originally identified as DNMT3B interaction region, potentially linking centromere identity to DNA methylation maintenance; (3) C-terminal Mif2/CENP-C cupin domain (IPR025974/PF11699/IPR011051, residues 810-940/856-940, ~85-130 aa) — RmlC-like jelly roll fold (G3DSA:2.60.120.10) mediating dimerization and outer kinetochore connections; (4) Central linker region (residues 552-809, ~257 aa) — largely unstructured connecting middle and cupin domains; (5) Full-length family classification (IPR028386/PTHR16684, residues 1-943) — defines CENPC/Mif2 orthologs across eukaryotes. AlphaFold predicts low confidence structure (global pLDDT 48.0), indicating extensive intrinsic disorder (~70% of protein) with only the C-terminal cupin domain (~100 aa) adopting stable fold. This disordered architecture is functional: enables conformational flexibility for bridging ~100nm between inner and outer kinetochore, facilitates multivalent weak interactions with multiple CCAN partners, and allows regulation by phosphorylation during cell cycle.\n\nINTERACTION NETWORK: STRING-DB reveals ultra-high confidence interactions exclusively with CCAN subunits forming the constitutive centromere complex: CENPS (0.999—highest; Centromere Protein S, CENP-S forms heterodimer with CENP-T), CENPN (0.999; Centromere Protein N, CENP-N binds CENP-A nucleosome), CENPT (0.999; Centromere Protein T, CENP-T bridges to outer kinetochore), CENPA (0.999; Centromere Protein A, histone H3 variant defining centromeric chromatin), CENPH (0.999; Centromere Protein H, CCAN subunit), CENPU (0.999; Centromere Protein U/50kDa, CCAN subunit), CENPL (0.998; Centromere Protein L, CCAN subunit), CENPI (0.998; Centromere Protein I, CCAN subunit), CENPK (0.998; Centromere Protein K, CCAN subunit), and CENPM (0.997; Centromere Protein M, CCAN subunit). This interaction profile definitively places CENPC as a central CCAN hub: CENP-A (0.999) binding anchors CENPC to centromeric nucleosomes; CENP-N (0.999) cooperates in CENP-A recognition; CENP-T/S (0.999) form parallel pathway to outer kinetochore; CENP-H/I/K/L/M/U (0.997-0.999) constitute remaining CCAN subunits forming ~400kDa complex.\n\nEXPRESSION & LOCALIZATION: HPA classifies CENPC as an essential protein with nucleoplasm, nuclear bodies, kinetochore, and midbody subcellular locations, reflecting dynamic cell cycle redistribution. Molecular function is DNA-binding. Biological processes include cell cycle, cell division, and mitosis. RNA distribution shows low tissue specificity and detection in many tissues, consistent with universal requirement for chromosome segregation in dividing cells. Low cancer specificity suggests CENPC is essential for cell viability rather than oncogene-addicted, though overexpression correlates with chromosomal instability in tumors.\n\nDISEASE LANDSCAPE: ClinVar contains 20 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number losses (4q13.2-21.1 x1 spanning 9 Mb, 4q12-13.3 x1 spanning 15 Mb, 4q13.1-13.3 x1 spanning 13 Mb, 4q12-21.1 x1 spanning 24 Mb), copy number gains (4p12-q35.2 whole arm gain, 4q13.1-13.3 x3 spanning 8 Mb, 4q12-31.21 x3 spanning 91 Mb), splice site variants (c.332-1G>C affecting exon 3 splice acceptor), and missense variants (c.1445G>T p.Gly482Val in middle domain, c.2759C>T p.Ser920Leu in cupin domain). No specific monogenic disease is definitively assigned, but 4q CNVs are associated with developmental disorders, intellectual disability, and growth retardation. The essential nature of CENPC suggests complete loss is embryonic lethal, with hypomorphic alleles causing mosaic variegated aneuploidy syndromes.\n\nCONSERVATION: CENPC orthologs confirmed across eukaryotes (mouse P49452, 906 aa; Arabidopsis Q66LG9, 705 aa; yeast MIF2/P35201, 549 aa) with conserved tripartite architecture despite sequence divergence, indicating extreme evolutionary constraint on kinetochore assembly since last eukaryotic common ancestor (~1.5 billion years). Yeast Mif2 is the functional ortholog demonstrating mechanistic conservation.\n\nFUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: CENPC functions as a central CCAN scaffold through an intrinsically disordered multivalent interaction mechanism: (1) Centromere targeting—N-terminal domain (residues 7-292) binds CENP-A nucleosomes via recognition of CENP-A/CENP-C motif; (2) CCAN assembly—N-terminal domain recruits CENP-H/I/K/L/M/N/U subunits forming ~400kDa inner kinetochore platform; (3) Dimerization—C-terminal cupin domain (residues 856-940) mediates CENPC homodimerization stabilizing centromere clustering; (4) Outer kinetochore bridge—CENP-T/S heterodimer (0.999) binds CENPC middle domain, extending connection to NDC80 microtubule-binding complex; (5) Epigenetic coupling—middle domain (residues 296-551) binds DNMT3B linking centromere identity to DNA methylation maintenance; (6) Phosphoregulation—disordered regions contain multiple CDK1/Aurora B phosphorylation sites controlling kinetochore-microtubule attachment stability; (7) Mechanical compliance—intrinsic disorder (~70%) provides ~10-20nm flexibility accommodating microtubule pulling forces during metaphase; (8) Error correction—Aurora B phosphorylation weakens CENPC-microtubule connections enabling detachment of erroneous attachments; (9) Disease mechanism—4q CNVs→CENPC haploinsufficiency→impaired CCAN assembly→chromosome missegregation→aneuploidy→developmental defects; missense variants (p.Gly482Val/p.Ser920Leu) disrupt domain folding or partner binding; (10) Cancer link—CENPC overexpression→hyperstable kinetochores→chromosomal instability→tumor evolution; (11) Therapeutic angle—CENPC-CENP-A interface targetable for selective mitotic inhibition in cancers; Aurora B inhibitors exploit CENPC phosphoregulation.
