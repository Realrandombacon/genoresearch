## Description
MOB1B (MOB kinase activator 1B, 216 aa, UniProt Q7L9L4) is a conserved MOB family co-activator protein that functions as a critical regulator of the Hippo signaling pathway, controlling organ size, cell proliferation, and apoptosis through YAP/TAZ transcriptional co-activators. Structural analysis reveals: (1) MOB kinase activator family domain (IPR005301, aa 9-214) with conserved MOB fold; (2) Mob1/phocein family domain (PF03637, aa 33-204) for kinase binding; (3) Complete MOB fold architecture (G3DSA:1.20.140.30, aa 1-216) enabling protein-protein interactions. AlphaFold structure shows high confidence (pLDDT 88.6), indicating stable α-helical bundle typical of MOB family scaffolds. STRING interactions identify core Hippo pathway partners: LATS1 (0.999), LATS2 (0.973), STK3/MST2 (0.972), STK38L/NAK (0.941), STK4/MST1 (0.896), MOB1A (0.820), WWTR1/TAZ (0.790), YAP1 (0.786), SAV1 (0.484), confirming role as LATS kinase activator and YAP/TAZ suppressor. HPA shows ubiquitous expression (detected in all tissues) with cancer-enriched pattern and subcellular localization to nucleoplasm, nucleoli, and cytosol, consistent with dual cytoplasmic signaling and nuclear transcriptional roles. ClinVar contains 17 pathogenic variants including multiple copy number losses at 4q12-21.1 and deletions, indicating clinical relevance though specific diseases not yet defined. Literature (44 papers) reveals emerging roles: (1) cardiomyocyte proliferation - CAND1 promotes heart regeneration by enhancing ubiquitinated degradation of MOB1B; (2) obesity-related endometrial cancer - miR-548ag suppresses MOB1B promoting oncogenesis; (3) spinal cord injury - GLIS3 regulates MOB1B inhibiting neuronal apoptosis; (4) multiple myeloma - PINK1-dependent mitophagy regulates cell migration via MOB1B-mediated Hippo-YAP/TAZ pathway; (5) adipose browning - miR-26b-5p/MOB1B axis modulates white adipose tissue browning. Functional hypothesis: MOB1B functions as paralog-specific Hippo pathway regulator with tissue-selective roles distinct from MOB1A, controlling LATS1/2 kinase activation to phosphorylate and inhibit YAP/TAZ, thereby suppressing proliferation and promoting apoptosis in context-dependent manner. Mechanistic proposal: MOB1B binds activated MST1/2 or STK3/4 kinases via Mob1/phocein domain, undergoes phosphorylation enabling recruitment and activation of LATS1/2 kinases, which then phosphorylate YAP/TAZ triggering cytoplasmic retention and degradation—this cascade suppresses transcription of proliferation genes (CTGF, CYR61, ANKRD1) while promoting apoptotic programs, with paralogue-specific functions (MOB1B vs MOB1A) determined by tissue expression patterns and differential protein interactions—positioning MOB1B as therapeutic target for cancer (YAP/TAZ-driven tumors), heart regeneration (cardiomyocyte proliferation), and metabolic disease (adipose browning).
