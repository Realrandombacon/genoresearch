## Description

The mechanistic hypothesis: JMJD4 functions as a 2-oxoglutarate and Fe(II)-dependent dioxygenase that hydroxylates specific protein substrates, targeting them for ubiquitin-proteasome degradation or altering their activity. The JmjC domain (residues 134-301) contains conserved HxD/E...H motif coordinating Fe(II) and binding 2-oxoglutarate co-substrate. Upon oxidative decarboxylation of 2-oxoglutarate to succinate and CO2, JMJD4 generates high-valent Fe(IV)=O species that abstracts hydrogen from substrate C-H bonds, enabling hydroxylation. In cardiomyocytes, JMJD4 hydroxylates PKM2 (pyruvate kinase M2) at specific proline residues, marking it for proteasomal degradation—this reduces glycolytic flux and promotes oxidative phosphorylation, protecting against metabolic stress-induced dilated cardiomyopathy. JMJD4 knockout mice develop DCM under stress due to PKM2 accumulation and metabolic inflexibility. In cancer, JMJD4 inhibits the PDCD5-TP53 tumor suppressor pathway: JMJD4 hydroxylates PDCD5 (programmed cell death 5) or TP53, preventing their interaction and nuclear translocation, thereby blocking apoptosis and enabling tumor progression.
