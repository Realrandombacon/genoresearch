# WDR24: GATOR2 Complex Scaffold Regulating mTORC1 Signaling via WD40-Mediated Protein Interactions and Lysosomal Localization

**Date:** 2026-04-06T13:12:50.801532

**Quality Score:** 3.95/10  (E=6.1, D=1.8) [MODERATE]

## Description
WDR24 (WD Repeat Domain 24, also C16orf21, JFP7) is a 790 amino acid cytosolic and lysosomal membrane-associated protein (UniProt Q96S15) with a well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 73.4, high confidence), characteristic of large scaffolding proteins containing multiple WD40 repeats for protein-protein interaction networks.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals WDR24 contains an N-terminal region with WD40/YVTN repeat-like domains (IPR001680/IPR015943/IPR036322, residues 19-326/68-103/109-192/196-245; G3DSA:2.130.10.10, residues 19-326) conferring protein binding (GO:0005515) activity. The protein belongs to the GATOR2 complex protein WDR24 family (IPR037590, residues 22-788) involved in positive regulation of TOR signaling (GO:0032008). A unique Nucleolar protein 10-like N-terminal domain (IPR056551/PF23098, residues 68-242) is present. The C-terminus contains a modified RING finger H2 subclass (C3H3C2-type) (cd16693, residues 742-787), potentially mediating ubiquitin ligase activity or zinc coordination.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals WDR24 functions as a core component of the GATOR2 complex with extremely high confidence interactions: SEH1L (0.999, SEH1 like nucleoporin), NPRL3 (0.999, nitrogen permease regulator 3), KPTN (0.999, kaputin), DEPDC5 (0.999, DEP domain-containing protein 5), MIOS (0.999, micronuclei associated and oocyte synthesis defective), RRAGC (0.999, Ras-related GTP-binding protein C), SEC13 (0.999, SEC13 homolog), NPRL2 (0.983), WDR59 (0.848), and RRAGB (0.713). These interactions define the GATOR2 complex (WDR24, SEH1L, MIOS, WDR59, KPTN) which regulates the GATOR1 complex (DEPDC5, NPRL2, NPRL3) and Rag GTPases (RRAGC, RRAGB) to control mTORC1 activation.\n\nHPA expression shows ubiquitous detection across all tissues with low tissue specificity and cytosolic/vesicular subcellular localization, consistent with housekeeping nutrient sensing functions. The protein is classified as predicted intracellular with involvement in autophagy and Ubl conjugation pathways, functioning as a transferase.\n\nClinVar documents 48 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all large chromosome 16p13.3 copy number variations (gains and losses spanning 16p13.3 and 16p13.3-13.13 regions). These CNVs suggest dosage sensitivity potentially causing developmental disorders, epilepsy, or intellectual disability through dysregulated mTORC1 signaling, a pathway critical for neuronal development and synaptic plasticity.\n\nConservation is evident from mouse (Q8CFJ9, 790 aa), zebrafish (Q7ZVL2, 779 aa), fruit fly (Q9XZ25, 776 aa), and Xenopus (Q7ZX22, 780 aa) orthologs, indicating ancient evolutionary conservation of the GATOR2 complex.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: WDR24 acts as a essential scaffold within the GATOR2 complex that inhibits the GATOR1 GAP complex towards RagA/B GTPases, thereby promoting mTORC1 activation at the lysosomal surface in response to amino acid sufficiency. The WD40 repeats form a beta-propeller structure that mediates assembly of the pentameric GATOR2 complex (WDR24-SEH1L-MIOS-WDR59-KPTN). Upon amino acid starvation, GATOR2 dissociates or changes conformation, allowing GATOR1 to hydrolyze RagA/B-GTP to GDP, inactivating mTORC1 and inducing autophagy. The C-terminal modified RING finger may recruit E2 ubiquitin-conjugating enzymes to ubiquitinate GATOR1 components or Rag GTPases, fine-tuning the switch. Lysosomal localization places WDR24 at the site of mTORC1 activation. CNV-mediated haploinsufficiency disrupts GATOR2 stoichiometry, leading to constitutive GATOR1 activity, suppressed mTORC1 signaling, and neurodevelopmental defects (epilepsy, intellectual disability) reminiscent of focal cortical dysplasia or tuberous sclerosis spectrum disorders.
