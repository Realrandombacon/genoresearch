# Flavin-Containing Monooxygenase 5 (FMO5/DIMETHYLAMINEMONOOXYGENASE 5): Hepatic ER-Resident N-Oxidase Regulating Lipid Metabolism, Gut Barrier Integrity, and Xenobiotic Detoxification via FAD-Dependent Monooxygenase Activity

## Description
FMO5 encodes a 533 aa integral membrane enzyme (UniProt P49326), also known as hBVMO1, that functions as an NADPH-dependent flavin monooxygenase catalyzing the N-oxygenation of soft nucleophilic heteroatom-containing xenobiotics and endogenous metabolites. The protein features a sophisticated bi-domain architecture characteristic of the FMO family: two FAD/NAD(P)-binding Rossmann fold domains (G3DSA:3.50.50.60, residues 2-178 and 297-458) that coordinate flavin adenine dinucleotide and NADP+ cofactors for redox catalysis, embedded within the conserved FMO5 family scaffold (IPR002257, residues 88-100/207-217/237-256; IPR000960, residues 3-532; PF00743, residues 3-532). STRING interactions place FMO5 at the intersection of drug metabolism and lipid homeostasis with ultra-high confidence partnerships: CYP3A4 (0.929), CYP2D6 (0.925), and CYP2C9 (0.922)—the three major cytochrome P450 enzymes responsible for phase I xenobiotic metabolism; plus GJA5 (0.724), a gap junction protein; ACP6 (0.704), an acyl carrier protein; RB1 (0.692), the retinoblastoma tumor suppressor; CHD1L (0.679), a chromatin remodeler at 1q21; and GPR89B (0.672), a Golgi pH regulator. HPA confirms extreme liver-specific expression (638.8 nTPM—among the highest tissue enrichment values observed), with subcellular localization to the endoplasmic reticulum and cytosol, annotated for lipid metabolism, monooxygenase, and oxidoreductase functions—consistent with roles in hepatic detoxification and metabolic homeostasis. ClinVar lists 294 pathogenic variants including extensive copy number losses/gains spanning chromosome 1q21.1-q21.2, deletions, and duplications, demonstrating extreme dosage sensitivity and linking FMO5 disruption to developmental disorders and metabolic disease susceptibility. AlphaFold structure prediction yields exceptional confidence (global pLDDT 94.9—very high), revealing a precisely folded bi-domain enzyme architecture optimized for FAD binding and substrate access. Mechanistically, FMO5 functions through multiple integrated modes: (1) As a hepatic N-oxygenase, FMO5 oxidizes dietary trimethylamine (TMA) to trimethylamine N-oxide (TMAO), a metabolite linked to cardiovascular disease risk and atherosclerosis; (2) Recent studies (2024-2025) reveal sex-specific roles in intestinal goblet cell maturation and mucus barrier formation, suggesting FMO5 regulates gut-liver axis signaling; (3) Interaction with CYP450 enzymes indicates coordinated phase I metabolism, potentially modulating drug clearance and xenobiotic toxicity; (4) Association with ACP6 and lipid metabolism annotations suggest roles in fatty acid oxidation or lipoprotein processing; (5) RB1 interaction implies potential cell cycle regulatory functions beyond metabolism; (6) Knockout mice exhibit a lean, slow-ageing phenotype with elevated 2,3-butanediol isomers, indicating FMO5 influences systemic metabolism and longevity. Disruption of FMO5 would cause defective TMA/TMAO metabolism, altered drug clearance, impaired gut barrier function, and metabolic dysregulation—phenotypes consistent with the 1q21.1 deletion/duplication syndromes and NAFLD susceptibility observed in patients.
