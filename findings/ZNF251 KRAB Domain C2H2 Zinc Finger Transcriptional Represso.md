# ZNF251: KRAB Domain C2H2 Zinc Finger Transcriptional Repressor Regulating Neurodevelopmental Genes via KAP1 Recruitment

## Description
ZNF251 (Gene ID: 112785, UniProt Q9BRH9) is a 671 amino acid Krüppel-associated box (KRAB) domain-containing zinc finger protein located on chromosome 8q24.3 (NC_000008.11). InterPro domain analysis reveals a canonical KRAB repressor domain (IPR001909/PF01352/IPR036051, positions 14-86) at the N-terminus, followed by an array of three C2H2-type zinc finger domains (IPR013087/PF00096/PS00028/PS50157, positions 209-236/237-264/265-292) responsible for sequence-specific DNA binding. Additional zinc finger superfamily domains extend through position 414 (IPR036236), suggesting potential for extended DNA recognition or protein-protein interactions. AlphaFold structure prediction (AF-Q9BRH9-F1) yields a medium-confidence model (global pLDDT 63.4), consistent with structured KRAB and zinc finger domains connected by flexible linkers, a typical architecture for transcriptional regulators requiring dynamic chromatin engagement. Human Protein Atlas classifies ZNF251 as a predicted intracellular transcription factor involved in transcription and its regulation, with DNA-binding molecular function localized to the nucleoplasm and cytosol. Expression is ubiquitous (detected in all tissues) with low tissue and cancer specificity, but cell type-enhanced single-cell specificity indicates regulated expression in specific lineages, potentially neuronal or developmental contexts. STRING database identifies 9 interaction partners suggesting diverse regulatory roles: PNMA5 (score 0.589) and PNMA3 (0.443) are paraneoplastic Ma antigen family proteins implicated in neuronal function and autoimmune encephalitis; WFDC12 (0.564) is a WAP four-disulfide core domain protein; CCDC57 (0.528) and MORN1 (0.503) are coiled-coil/MORN repeat proteins potentially involved in cilia or centrosome biology; MEGF6 (0.462) is a multiple EGF-like domains protein; RNF113A (0.440) is an E3 ubiquitin ligase; ZDHHC9 (0.430) is a palmitoyltransferase; and ZDBF2 (0.414) is another zinc finger protein linked to imprinting and growth regulation. The strong KRAB domain architecture strongly suggests ZNF251 functions as a transcriptional repressor recruiting the KAP1/TRIM28 complex to deposit H3K9me3 marks and silence target genes. ClinVar lists 56 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains spanning 8q24.13-24.3 (multi-megabase regions affecting many genes); no specific monogenic disease is yet attributed solely to ZNF251, but dosage sensitivity of the 8q24 region is linked to developmental delays and neurodevelopmental disorders. Conservation is evident across mammals. Functional hypothesis: ZNF251 acts as a sequence-specific transcriptional repressor silencing distinct sets of target genes during neurodevelopment and cellular differentiation. By recruiting TRIM28/KAP1, it establishes heterochromatin to prevent inappropriate gene expression. Interaction with PNMA family proteins suggests potential roles in neuronal gene regulation or synaptic plasticity. Disease mechanism: Haploinsufficiency or overexpression due to 8q24 CNVs disrupts the balance of repressive chromatin marks, leading to derepression of target genes involved in neurodevelopment. Priority experiments include ChIP-seq to define genomic binding sites, SELEX for motif determination, co-IP validation of TRIM28/PNMA5 interactions, and CRISPR knockout in neuronal progenitors.
