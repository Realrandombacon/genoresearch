## Description
FBLN5 (Fibulin-5; UniProt Q9UBX5) is a 448 amino acid secreted extracellular matrix protein with extensive literature coverage, representing a well-characterized but mechanistically rich elastin-binding scaffold that orchestrates elastic fiber assembly through integrin-mediated cell-matrix communication and lysyl oxidase recruitment.\n\nSTRUCTURAL ARCHITECTURE: InterPro analysis reveals a modular architecture dominated by calcium-binding EGF-like repeats optimized for protein-protein interactions and matrix organization. The protein contains multiple cbEGF domains: IPR001881/IPR000742 (residues 42-86, 127-167, 168-206, 207-246) providing rigid calcium-dependent structural modules that resist proteolysis. EGF-type aspartate/asparagine hydroxylation sites (IPR000152, residues 144-155, 181-192, 221-232) enable post-translational modifications critical for stability. The N-terminal region belongs to the growth factor receptor cysteine-rich domain superfamily (IPR009030, residues 15-288) mediating receptor interactions. Complement C1r-like EGF domains (IPR026823, residues 187-210, 227-250, 269-291) suggest complement system homology. GO annotations include calcium ion binding (GO:0005509). AlphaFold predicts high confidence structure (global pLDDT 83.3), indicating a well-folded elongated molecule with rigid cbEGF repeats connected by flexible linkers—consistent with its role as a molecular bridge between cells and elastin.\n\nINTERACTION NETWORK: STRING-DB identifies exceptionally high-confidence interactions with elastic fiber components and crosslinking enzymes: ELN (0.999, Elastin, near-perfect score indicating direct physical binding), FN1 (0.865, Fibronectin-1, provisional matrix scaffold), LOXL2 (0.859, Lysyl oxidase-like 2, elastin crosslinking enzyme), MFAP2 (0.857, Microfibrillar-associated protein 2, elastin-associated glycoprotein), MFAP4 (0.842, Microfibrillar-associated protein 4), ATP6V0A2 (0.612, V-type proton ATPase subunit a2, cutis laxa gene), FBN1 (0.516, Fibrillin-1, microfibril scaffold), LTBP2 (0.495, Latent TGF-beta binding protein 2), LOXL1 (0.455, Lysyl oxidase-like 1), and LOX (0.430, Lysyl oxidase). This interaction profile establishes FBLN5 as a central organizer that bridges elastin (ELN) to the cell surface while recruiting crosslinking enzymes (LOX/LOXL1/LOXL2) for covalent maturation.\n\nEXPRESSION & LOCALIZATION: HPA shows striking tissue specificity with extraordinary blood vessel enrichment (668.4 nTPM—the highest vascular expression observed in this research session, ~16x higher than TJP3 intestine at 40.5 nTPM). This reflects FBLN5's essential role in arterial elasticity. Protein class includes disease-related genes, plasma proteins, and predicted secreted proteins. Subcellular location is plasma membrane (cell surface binding). Biological process is cell adhesion. RNA cancer specificity is cancer-enhanced, suggesting roles in tumor stroma remodeling. Single cell type specificity is group-enriched (vascular smooth muscle cells, endothelial cells). Disease involvement explicitly includes age-related macular degeneration, Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease, neurodegeneration, and neuropathy.\n\nDISEASE LANDSCAPE: ClinVar contains 75 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including truncating mutations that abolish function: p.Arg54Ter (early nonsense in N-terminal region), p.Trp160Ter (mid-protein truncation), p.Glu291Ter (C-terminal truncation), plus missense variants affecting critical EGF domain cysteines: p.Cys131Arg (disrupts cbEGF disulfide bond), p.Arg77Gly, p.Pro70Ser, p.Thr86Asn, p.Ile4Val, p.Arg319Pro. Large copy number losses at 14q31.1-32.2 cause haploinsufficiency. While no single monogenic disease name is assigned in ClinVar, FBLN5 mutations cause autosomal recessive cutis laxa type IIB (loose, sagging skin due to elastic fiber defects), age-related macular degeneration (drusen accumulation), pelvic organ prolapse (loss of vaginal wall elasticity), and vascular stiffness (arterial rigidity leading to hypertension). Mouse knockout models demonstrate severe elastic fiber fragmentation resembling emphysema and vascular aging.\n\nCONSERVATION: Orthologs confirmed in mouse (Q9WVH9, 448 aa—identical length) and rat (Q9WVH8, 448 aa), indicating strong evolutionary constraint on the cbEGF repeat architecture across mammals for conserved elastic fiber assembly.\n\nFUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: FBLN5 functions as a molecular bridge that organizes elastic fiber assembly through a coordinated multi-step mechanism: (1) Secreted FBLN5 binds cell surface integrins (αvβ3, α5β1, α4β1) via its N-terminal RGD motif, anchoring the protein to vascular smooth muscle cells and fibroblasts; (2) The cbEGF repeats bind soluble tropoelastin monomers, concentrating them at the cell surface; (3) FBLN5 recruits lysyl oxidase enzymes (LOX, LOXL1, LOXL2) through direct protein-protein interactions, positioning them adjacent to tropoelastin substrates; (4) LOX enzymes catalyze oxidative deamination of lysine residues, forming desmosine crosslinks that polymerize tropoelastin into insoluble elastin; (5) FBLN5 simultaneously binds microfibrillar components (MFAP2, MFAP4, fibrillin-1) to integrate elastic fibers into the broader ECM network. In disease, FBLN5 loss causes: (a) Cutis laxa—failure to assemble elastic fibers in dermis leads to sagging skin; (b) AMD—impaired elastin turnover in Bruch's membrane causes drusen accumulation and choroidal neovascularization; (c) Pelvic organ prolapse—loss of vaginal wall elasticity during childbirth; (d) Vascular stiffness—arterial rigidity increases systolic blood pressure and cardiac afterload. The 668.4 nTPM blood vessel expression reflects continuous demand for elastic fiber maintenance throughout life. Therapeutic FBLN5 supplementation or LOX activation could treat cutis laxa, prevent AMD progression, or restore vascular compliance in hypertension.
