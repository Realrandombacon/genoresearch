## Description
ELMO3 (UniProt Q96BJ8), also known as Engulfment and cell motility protein 3 or CED-12 homolog, encodes a 720 aa high-confidence intracellular protein functioning as an essential regulator of RAC1 GTPase activation through formation of heterodimeric complexes with DOCK family guanine nucleotide exchange factors (GEFs), mediating phagocytosis of apoptotic cells, cell migration, and neuronal development. InterPro analysis reveals canonical ELMO family domain architecture: N-terminal ubiquitin-like domain (IPR060267, residues 6-79) potentially mediating protein-protein interactions or regulation; Armadillo-like helical domain superfamily (G3DSA:1.25.10.10/IPR011989/IPR016024, residues 6-272/86-253) forming a scaffold for complex assembly; ELMO-specific armadillo-like helical domain (IPR024574, residues 116-268) unique to this protein family; central ELMO domain (IPR006816, residues 292-479) critical for DOCK binding and functional activity; C-terminal Pleckstrin homology domain (IPR001849/G3DSA:2.30.29.30/IPR011993/cd13359, residues 535-663/546-664/534-663) mediating membrane recruitment via phosphoinositide binding; and family membership in Engulfment and cell motility domain-containing proteins (IPR050868, residues 62-599) and ELMO/CED-12 family (PF04727). AlphaFold predicts high confidence structure (pLDDT 88.9) indicating a well-folded stable protein with distinct modular architecture suitable for precise multi-protein complex assembly. STRING interactions reveal an exceptionally tight functional network centered on DOCK-ELMO-RAC1 signaling: DOCK1 (0.999, highest confidence interaction confirming obligate partnership), ELMO2 (0.938, paralog suggesting potential heterodimerization or compensatory function), GULP1 (0.907, engulfment adapter protein linking to apoptotic cell clearance), ELMO1 (0.893, closest paralog), DOCK4 (0.844, GEF for RAC1 in neuronal migration), WDR35 (0.768, intraflagellar transport protein linking to ciliopathy), DOCK3 (0.645, neuronal GEF), RHOG (0.596, small GTPase upstream of RAC1), CRK (0.473, adapter protein in integrin signaling), and DOCK5 (0.455), confirming ELMO3 operates within the core ELMO-DOCK-RAC1 phagocytosis and motility pathway. HPA classifies ELMO3 as a predicted intracellular protein with biological processes in apoptosis and phagocytosis, subcellular localization to plasma membrane and cell junctions consistent with migratory and engulfment functions, low tissue specificity (detected in many tissues), low cancer specificity, not detected in immune cells (suggesting non-professional phagocyte roles), and cell type enhanced single cell specificity. ClinVar lists 28 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including splice region variants (c.1262-8C>G, c.666-16C>G) and large copy number gains on chromosome 16q (16q11.2-24.3 chr16:46432879-90294753x3, 16q21-24.1 chr16:62746020-84485022x3, 16q21-24.3 chr16:61524229-90155062x3, multiple other duplications) and losses (16q22.1 deletions); critically, literature confirms biallelic ELMO3 mutations cause autosomal recessive intellectual disability through loss of DOCK-mediated RAC1 activation, establishing a named neurodevelopmental disorder phenotype. Literature (26 papers, PARTIALLY CHARACTERIZED) reveals: biallelic ELMO3 mutations causing intellectual disability via impaired DOCK-mediated RAC1 activation (5x citations, 2021 - definitive disease mechanism); comparative analysis of elmo1/elmo2/elmo3 zebrafish mutants demonstrating functional non-redundancy despite structural homology (4x citations, 2022); miR-181c-5p inhibition rescuing diabetes-impaired angiogenesis through ELMO3 and TRIB1 activation (0x citations, 2024); COX2 inhibitor downregulation of ELMO3 suppressing tumor growth and metastasis in non-small-cell lung cancer (20x citations, 2019); indicating roles in neurodevelopment, vascular biology, and cancer progression. Functional hypothesis: ELMO3 operates through integrated multi-domain scaffolding and allosteric activation mechanisms: (1) DOCK complex formation - ELMO domain (292-479) binds DOCK1/4/5 GEFs inducing conformational change that activates RAC1 GDP/GTP exchange; (2) Membrane recruitment - PH domain (535-663) binds PI(3,4,5)P3 or related phosphoinositides localizing ELMO3-DOCK complex to plasma membrane; (3) Armadillo scaffold - N-terminal armadillo repeats (6-272) provide structural platform for multi-protein assembly including GULP1 for apoptotic cell recognition; (4) Ubiquitin-like regulation - N-terminal UBL domain (6-79) may mediate ubiquitination-dependent regulation or proteasomal interactions; (5) RAC1 activation cascade - RHOG upstream activation leads to ELMO3-DOCK recruitment and RAC1 activation driving actin polymerization for phagocytosis/migration; (6) Conservation - mouse Q8BYZ7 (720 aa), rat Q499U2 (720 aa), bovine A6QR40 (652 aa) indicate highly conserved vertebrate mechanism. Dark aspects requiring investigation include: specific phosphoinositide ligands for PH domain, mechanism of autoinhibition relief upon DOCK binding, impact of splice variants c.1262-8C>G and c.666-16C>G on protein function, precise neurodevelopmental phenotypes in ELMO3-deficient patients (beyond intellectual disability - autism? epilepsy? motor deficits?), whether homozygous loss causes complete phagocytosis defects or partial impairment, tissue-specific thresholds for ELMO3 dosage in brain vs. vasculature, crosstalk between ELMO1/2/3 paralogs and compensation mechanisms, biomarker utility in NSCLC prognosis or diabetic angiopathy, therapeutic targeting of ELMO3-DOCK interface in cancer metastasis, WDR35 interaction suggesting ciliary role in neuronal development, and evolutionary divergence of ELMO3 from ELMO1/2 despite conserved domain architecture.
