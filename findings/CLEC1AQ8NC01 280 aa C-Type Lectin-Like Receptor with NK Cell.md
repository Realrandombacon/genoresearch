## Description
CLEC1A (C-Type Lectin Domain Family 1 Member A, UniProt Q8NC01) is a 280 aa membrane-associated immune receptor representing a genuine dark gene with only 22 literature references despite clinical significance. Structural analysis reveals canonical C-type lectin-like architecture: N-terminal cytoplasmic region (aa 1-78) lacking signaling motifs, central C-type lectin-like domain (CTLD, aa 137-259, IPR001304/PF00059/SM00034) forming the carbohydrate recognition core with NK cell receptor-like fold (IPR033992, cd03593), and C-terminal extension (aa 260-280) for membrane anchoring. Functional hypothesis: CLEC1A acts as inhibitory C-type lectin receptor lacking cytoplasmic signaling motifs, potentially functioning through sequestration of ligands (HRG, carbohydrate structures) or heterodimerization with signaling-competent partners (CLEC4E, KLRC4) to modulate immune activation thresholds. The NK cell receptor-like CTLD fold suggests recognition of glycosylated ligands on target cells, while placental expression indicates role in maternal-fetal immune tolerance. Disease mechanism: CLEC1A dysregulation may contribute to autoimmune diseases (EAE/MS via IL-17 pathway), inflammatory disorders (neutrophil recruitment defects), and pregnancy complications (placental immune tolerance failure) through altered lectin-mediated immune modulation. The 37 ClinVar CNVs suggest dosage sensitivity where copy number gains disrupt immune homeostasis. Priority experiments: (a) identify CLEC1A carbohydrate/ligand specificity via glycan array screening, (b) characterize CLEC1A-HRG binding interface via SPR/BLI, (c) test CLEC1A role in NK cell cytotoxicity via knockout models, (d) determine CLEC1A-associated disease phenotypes in 12p CNV patients. CLEC1A exemplifies 'dark immune lectin' where domain architecture and expression pattern are defined but ligand specificity, signaling mechanism, and disease associations remain unexplored despite 37 ClinVar variants.
