## Description
TIMM10 (Translocase of Inner Mitochondrial Membrane 10, also known as Tim10) is a 90 aa small hydrophilic protein functioning as an essential chaperone subunit of the TIM9-TIM10 complex in the mitochondrial intermembrane space. With only 12 literature references, TIMM10 represents a 'partially characterized' dark gene where its specific role beyond general carrier import—particularly in innate immunity—is emerging. InterPro analysis confirms a canonical Tim10-like domain (IPR004217/PF02953, aa 7-71) containing a conserved twin CX3C zinc-finger motif characteristic of the mitochondrial import machinery, which stabilizes the protein's fold and mediates substrate binding. AlphaFold predicts a very high-confidence structure (pLDDT 93.4), indicating a rigid, well-folded helix-turn-helix architecture typical of small chaperones that must resist proteolysis in the intermembrane space. HPA shows ubiquitous expression with low tissue specificity, detected in all tissues, consistent with its fundamental housekeeping role in maintaining mitochondrial metabolism by importing nuclear-encoded carrier proteins. STRING interactions reveal an extremely tight functional cluster dedicated to mitochondrial biogenesis: TIMM10 binds almost exclusively to its partner TIMM9 (0.999) to form the hexameric chaperone complex, and interacts strongly with the inner membrane insertase TIMM22 (0.998), the oxidoreductase CHCHD4 (0.993), and the deafness-dystonia associated TIMM8A (0.999). This network confirms TIMM10's role in shuttling hydrophobic multi-pass carriers (e.g., AAC, phosphate carrier) from the TOM complex to the TIM22 complex. ClinVar lists 11 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, exclusively large copy number gains/losses on chromosome 11q, implicating TIMM10 dosage sensitivity in developmental disorders, likely through mechanisms of mitochondrial failure. Recent literature (2025) uniquely identifies TIMM10 as a positive regulator of MAVS-mediated innate immune signaling against influenza A virus, suggesting a moonlighting function where mitochondrial import efficiency modulates antiviral responses, while earlier studies focus on its conservation across species (giant panda, mouse) and role in Mohr-Tranebjoerg syndrome-related pathways (via TIMM8A interaction).
