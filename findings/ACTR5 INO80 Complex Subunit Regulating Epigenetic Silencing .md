## Description
ACTR5 (Actin-Related Protein 5, alias Arp5) is a 607-amino acid essential subunit of the INO80 chromatin remodeling complex, functioning as a structural scaffold that integrates ATP-dependent nucleosome sliding with epigenetic regulation of cell cycle genes. With only 7 publications, it remains a 'dark gene' despite its critical role in cancer progression and DNA repair. The protein contains a canonical actin family fold (IPR004000, PF00022, residues 32-570) featuring a conserved nucleotide-binding domain (NBD, cd10211, residues 32-568) and actin signature motifs (PS00432), yet unlike conventional actins, it lacks polymerization capability and instead serves as a rigid structural module within large chromatin complexes. AlphaFold predicts a highly stable, well-folded structure (global pLDDT 82.8), consistent with its role as a core architectural component.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals an exceptionally tight functional network restricted to the INO80 complex machinery, with maximum confidence scores (0.999) for partners RUVBL1, RUVBL2, INO80, INO80B, INO80C, INO80E, NFRKB, and ACTR8, alongside the deubiquitinase UCHL5 (0.990). This exclusive interactome confirms ACTR5's identity as an obligate complex member rather than a free-standing actor. Recent literature (2022) establishes a specific mechanistic role: ACTR5 controls the epigenetic silencing of the tumor suppressor CDKN2A (p16INK4a) via H3K9 methylation in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), driving tumor progression in an INO80 ATPase-independent manner. This suggests ACTR5 acts as a specificity factor recruiting repressive histone modifiers to the CDKN2A locus.\n\nHuman Protein Atlas data indicates ubiquitous expression across all tissues and immune cells ('detected in all'), consistent with a housekeeping role in genome maintenance. ClinVar reports 7 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, exclusively large copy number gains and losses spanning chromosome 20q (the gene's locus), implicating dosage sensitivity in developmental disorders or cancer predisposition, though no specific Mendelian disease is yet assigned. Given its structural homology to actin but functional divergence into chromatin remodeling, we hypothesize that ACTR5 evolved to mechanically couple nucleosome dynamics to cell cycle checkpoint control, with dysregulation leading to unchecked proliferation via CDKN2A silencing.
