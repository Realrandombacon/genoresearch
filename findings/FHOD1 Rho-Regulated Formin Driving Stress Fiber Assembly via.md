# FHOD1: Rho-Regulated Formin Driving Stress Fiber Assembly via ROCK-Dependent Phosphorylation Switch

## Description
FHOD1 (Formin Homology 2 Domain Containing 1, Q9Y613) encodes a 1164 aa Diaphanous-related formin (DRF) functioning as a critical actin polymerization machinery component that nucleates and elongates unbranched actin filaments for stress fiber formation. Structural architecture reveals multi-domain organization: N-terminal GTPase-binding domain (GBD/FH3, IPR014768/PF18382/IPR041387, residues 17-101/53-458) for Rho-family GTPase regulation, central FH3 domain (IPR056771/PF24959, residues 174-308) for autoinhibition, and C-terminal FH2 domain (IPR015425/PF02181/G3DSA:1.20.58.2220, residues 616-1068/618-987/612-1000) forming the processive actin polymerization engine. AlphaFold prediction (pLDDT 68.4) reveals partially structured architecture with ordered FH2 catalytic domain embedded in flexible linkers characteristic of DRF autoinhibition mechanisms. STRING interactions confirm cytoskeletal regulatory network with nuclear envelope spectrins SYNE2 (0.994) and SYNE1 (0.975), Rho-associated kinase ROCK2 (0.919) for activating phosphorylation, intermediate filament desmin DES (0.695), profilins PFN1/PFN4 (0.549/0.616), and fascin FSCN1 (0.591). HPA shows tissue-enhanced expression in skeletal muscle (119.0 nTPM) and lymphoid tissue (93.9 nTPM) with cytosolic localization and actin-binding molecular function. ClinVar documents 28 pathogenic variants including missense (p.Ile529Met) and 16q CNVs disrupting the locus. Literature (137 papers) demonstrates roles in colorectal cancer prognosis (2025), glioblastoma temozolomide resistance via ferroptosis regulation (8x citations, 2024), and structural autoinhibition mechanism (2025). Mechanistic hypothesis: FHOD1 exists in autoinhibited state via intramolecular GBD-FH2 interaction; Rho-GTP binding and ROCK2-mediated phosphorylation of the N-terminal region releases autoinhibition, enabling FH2 dimer to nucleate actin filaments and recruit profilin-actin complexes for processive elongation, driving stress fiber assembly required for cell migration, invasion, and mechanotransduction.
