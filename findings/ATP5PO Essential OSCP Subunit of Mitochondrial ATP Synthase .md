# ATP5PO: Essential OSCP Subunit of Mitochondrial ATP Synthase Peripheral Stalk Regulating Rotational Catalysis and Linked to Leigh Syndrome and Hirschsprung Disease

## Description
ATP5PO (UniProt P48047) encodes a 213 aa essential subunit of the mitochondrial ATP synthase (Complex V), specifically functioning as the Oligomycin Sensitivity Conferral Protein (OSCP) within the peripheral stalk. This subunit acts as the critical mechanical stator, anchoring the catalytic F1 head (alpha3beta3gamma) to the membrane-embedded Fo rotor, thereby preventing futile rotation of the alpha3beta3 hexamer during proton-driven ATP synthesis. InterPro analysis confirms a highly conserved domain architecture: an N-terminal domain (aa 36-129, G3DSA:1.10.520.20) mediating F1 binding, a central OSCP/delta family domain (aa 14-211, IPR000711, PF00213) forming the elongated helical bundle of the peripheral stalk, and a C-terminal conserved site (aa 171-190, IPR020781, PS00389) crucial for Fo interaction and oligomycin sensitivity. AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (global pLDDT 84.0), revealing a rigid, elongated conformation consistent with its role as a structural brace against rotational torque. STRING interactions define ATP5PO as a hub of maximum confidence (0.999) within the intact ATP synthase holo-complex, interacting exclusively with core subunits: ATP5F1D, ATP5F1E, ATP5F1B, MT-ATP6, ATP5PD, ATP5PB, ATP5MG, ATP5MF, ATP5F1C, and ATP5ME, indicating it functions strictly as an integral structural component rather than a regulatory switch. HPA expression shows ubiquitous tissue distribution with exceptional enrichment in tongue (1494.1 nTPM) and high levels in metabolically active tissues, localized strictly to mitochondria; disease involvement is explicitly noted as 'Primary mitochondrial disease'. ClinVar reports 30 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains/losses on chromosome 21q (Down syndrome region) and specific homozygous splice variants causing Leigh syndrome (a severe neurodegenerative disorder) and linking ATP5PO deficiency to Hirschsprung disease via impaired enteric nervous system development in zebrafish models. Literature (21 papers) highlights: (1) Leigh Syndrome—homozygous splice variants disrupt Complex V assembly, leading to lactic acidosis and early death; (2) Hirschsprung Disease—reduced ATP5PO levels impair neural crest cell migration/survival in the gut; (3) Down Syndrome—trisomy 21 leads to ATP5PO overexpression, potentially contributing to metabolic phenotypes; (4) mPTP Regulation—recent evidence suggests ATP5PO deglutathionylation by GLRX2 regulates the mitochondrial permeability transition pore in AML. Functional hypothesis: ATP5PO serves as the indispensable mechanical linchpin of ATP synthase, transmitting conformational changes between the proton gradient-driven Fo rotor and the catalytic F1 head while preventing energy dissipation; its dosage sensitivity (haploinsufficiency causes Leigh syndrome, overexpression links to DS) underscores its role as a rate-limiting factor in oxidative phosphorylation efficiency. Mechanistic proposal: In Leigh syndrome, splice mutations truncate the C-terminal Fo-binding domain, destabilizing the peripheral stalk, uncoupling proton flow from ATP synthesis, and triggering bioenergetic failure in neurons. In Hirschsprung disease, subtle reductions in ATP5PO compromise the high energy demands of migrating neural crest cells, leading to aganglionosis. The combination of a rigid high-confidence structure, exclusive integration into the ATP synthase machine, ubiquitous high expression, severe clinical phenotypes (Leigh syndrome), and emerging roles in developmental disorders positions ATP5PO as a critical but under-appreciated determinant of mitochondrial health and a genuine dark gene requiring mechanistic dissection beyond its canonical structural role.
