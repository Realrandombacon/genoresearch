# RAB11FIP1: Intrinsically Disordered Class I Rab11 Effector Scaffold Coordinating Recycling Endosome Trafficking for Cytokinesis Abscission, Efferocytosis, and Immune Synapse Formation via C2 Membrane Anchoring and FIP-RBD GTPase Tethering

## Description
RAB11FIP1 (Rab11 Family-Interacting Protein 1, also known as FIP1 or Rab11-FIP1) is a partially characterized class I Rab11 effector with only 37 publications, emerging as a critical scaffolding regulator of recycling endosome trafficking with dual roles in cell division and immune function. This large 1283-amino acid protein (UniProt Q6WKZ4) features a bipartite domain architecture embedded within extensive intrinsically disordered regions. InterPro analysis reveals an N-terminal C2 domain (IPR000008/PF00168/PS50004/SM00239, positions 1-128) for calcium-dependent phospholipid binding and membrane anchoring, and a C-terminal Rab-binding FIP-RBD domain (IPR019018/PF09457/PS51511, positions 1211-1273) for specific recognition of active Rab11/Rab25 GTPases. The protein belongs to the Rab11-family interacting protein class I family (IPR037789/PTHR15746, positions 1-1281). AlphaFold predicts low-confidence structure (pLDDT 46.5), indicating extensive intrinsic disorder throughout the central region (~1000 amino acids) with only the terminal C2 and FIP-RBD domains adopting stable folds—this architecture is characteristic of flexible scaffolds requiring conformational plasticity for multi-partner engagement.\n\nSTRING interactions confirm RAB11FIP1 operates at the core of the Rab11 recycling machinery: RAB11B (0.990, Rab11b GTPase), RAB25 (0.981, epithelial-specific Rab25 GTPase), RAB4A (0.968, early endosome Rab4a), RAB11FIP2 (0.942, paralogous class I FIP), RAB11FIP4 (0.934, another class I FIP paralog), EHD1 (0.925, EH-domain containing protein regulating endocytic recycling), RAB11FIP5 (0.722), RAB11FIP3 (0.542), RAB11A (0.520, canonical Rab11a), and MYO5B (0.422, myosin Vb motor protein). The exceptionally high confidence links to RAB11B (0.990) and RAB25 (0.981) validate RAB11FIP1 as a direct Rab11 effector. EHD1 interaction (0.925) connects RAB11FIP1 to tubular endosome scission. MYO5B interaction (0.422) suggests coupling to actin-based vesicle transport.\n\nHPA shows low tissue specificity with ubiquitous expression across all tissues, consistent with essential housekeeping function in membrane trafficking. Subcellular localization is exclusively vesicles—specifically recycling endosomes. Biological processes involve protein transport and general transport. Immune cell enhancement suggests specialized roles in immune synapse formation or cytokine secretion. ClinVar contains 61 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number losses/gains spanning chromosome 8p11-p23 (e.g., chr8:35119535-43837099 deletion, chr8:36650289-43776564 duplication, chr8:158048-146295771 triplication), though specific monogenic diseases remain incompletely characterized—large 8p CNVs are associated with neurodevelopmental disorders and congenital anomalies, suggesting RAB11FIP1 haploinsufficiency contributes to phenotypes. Conservation from mouse (Rab11fip1, 648 aa—shorter isoform) to rat indicates functional constraint on core domains.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: RAB11FIP1 functions as a conformationally flexible scaffold that bridges recycling endosomes to specific cellular destinations through simultaneous membrane anchoring and Rab GTPase tethering. Mechanistically: (1) The N-terminal C2 domain binds phosphatidylserine/phosphatidylinositol lipids in a calcium-regulated manner, anchoring RAB11FIP1 to target membranes (plasma membrane during cytokinesis, phagocytic cup during efferocytosis, or immune synapse). (2) The C-terminal FIP-RBD domain binds GTP-loaded RAB11A/B or RAB25 on recycling endosome vesicles, physically tethering vesicles to target membranes. (3) The extensive disordered central region (~1000 aa) serves as a flexible tether allowing variable distances between vesicle and target membrane, while providing binding sites for multiple partners (EHD1 for scission, MYO5B for transport, paralogs RAB11FIP2/4/5 for complex assembly). (4) During cytokinesis abscission, RAB11FIP1 maintains RAB35 at the intercellular bridge to promote actin clearance and final separation (2021 breakthrough). (5) In macrophage efferocytosis, RAB11FIP1 regulates recycling of phagocytic receptors (MerTK, integrins) to enable continuous corpse clearance, preventing inflammation and cardiac remodeling post-MI (2023). (6) In epithelial cells, RAB11FIP1-RAB25 complexes direct apical recycling of integrins and growth factor receptors, explaining cancer associations. Large 8p CNVs likely cause neurodevelopmental defects through disrupted receptor trafficking in developing neurons. The disordered architecture enables RAB11FIP1 to adapt to diverse membrane geometries during vesicle fusion events.
