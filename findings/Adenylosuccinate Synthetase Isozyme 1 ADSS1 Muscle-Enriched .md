## Description
ADSS1 (Adenylosuccinate Synthetase Isozyme 1) is a 457-amino acid metabolic enzyme with 33 publications confirming its recently disease-associated dark gene status. The protein belongs to the adenylosuccinate synthetase family (IPR001114, positions 1-457; PTHR11873, positions 1-457) with chordate-specific isozyme 1 classification (IPR027509, positions 1-457), containing GTP-binding site (IPR018220, positions 40-47), active site residues (IPR033128, positions 166-177), and P-loop NTP hydrolase superfamily domain (IPR027417, positions 32-455). CATH-Gene3D reveals three structural domains: subunit A domain 1 (G3DSA:3.40.440.10, positions 35-131 and 232-297), domain 2 (G3DSA:1.10.300.10, positions 132-231), and domain 3 (G3DSA:3.90.170.10, positions 298-457). AlphaFold predicts a well-folded enzyme structure (pLDDT 92.7, very high confidence), consistent with stable metabolic enzymes. HPA reveals highly muscle-enriched expression with skeletal muscle (1192.9 nTPM top tissue) and tongue (569.7 nTPM), group enriched RNA specificity, detected-in-many distribution, cancer-enriched RNA specificity, not detected in immune cells, with annotated functions in purine biosynthesis and ligase activity. ClinVar documents 101 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants (including c.948+2T>C splice site, c.414dup p.Asp139Ter nonsense, c.1073+1G>T splice site, c.986G>A p.Trp329Ter nonsense, c.794G>A p.Gly265Glu missense, and chromosome 14q32.2-32.33 copy number gains/losses), causing autosomal recessive ADSS1 myopathy characterized by progressive cardiac and skeletal muscle degeneration with childhood to adolescent onset. STRING interactions reveal ADSS1 functions within purine biosynthesis pathway with very high confidence connections to ADSL (0.999) - adenylosuccinate lyase converting adenylosuccinate to AMP, IMPDH1 (0.977) and IMPDH2 (0.811) - inosine monophosphate dehydrogenases converting IMP to XMP in GMP pathway, GART (0.969) - trifunctional purine biosynthesis protein, AMPD1 (0.962) and AMPD3 (0.957) - AMP deaminases, ITPA (0.955) - inosine triphosphatase, ATIC (0.947) - bifunctional purine biosynthesis protein, and medium confidence connections to HPRT1 (0.697) - hypoxanthine-guanine phosphoribosyltransferase, GMPR (0.640) - GMP reductase. Recent studies (2025) established ADSS1 myopathy as ultra-rare disorder with consistent MRI patterns, clinical/radiological/autopsy findings in affected siblings, patient/caregiver-reported insights, and mouse models recapitulating muscle degeneration features. We hypothesize ADSS1 functions as the muscle-enriched isozyme catalyzing the GTP-dependent conversion of IMP to adenylosuccinate in the AMP biosynthetic pathway, with tissue-specific expression (1192.9 nTPM skeletal muscle) explaining muscle-selective pathology. The enzyme's position in purine metabolism (ADSL 0.999 interaction confirms sequential pathway relationship) suggests that ADSS1 deficiency disrupts AMP production while potentially accumulating IMP or shunting toward GMP pathway (IMPDH1/2 connections). The 101 ClinVar variants indicate strong purifying selection and dosage sensitivity. The well-folded structure (pLDDT 92.7) suggests loss-of-function mechanisms through catalytic site disruption (active site IPR033128, positions 166-177) or GTP-binding impairment (IPR018220, positions 40-47). ADSS1 represents an uncharacterized metabolic enzyme where recent disease discovery (2025) revealed critical role in muscle energy metabolism through purine nucleotide homeostasis.
