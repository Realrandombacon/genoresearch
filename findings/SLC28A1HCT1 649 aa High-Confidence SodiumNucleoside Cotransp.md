## Description
SLC28A1 (O00337, 649 aa), also known as HCT1 (High Capacity Transporter 1) or URCTU, is a critical solute carrier responsible for the sodium-dependent cotransport of uridine and other nucleosides across the plasma membrane, maintaining intracellular nucleotide pools essential for RNA synthesis and metabolism. InterPro identifies a canonical concentrative nucleoside transporter architecture (IPR008276, PF07662, PTHR10590, aa 66-609) characterized by a large N-terminal nucleoside recognition domain (IPR011642, PF07670, aa 263-361) and a C-terminal transmembrane domain (IPR011657, PF07662, aa 367-588) that forms a specific antiporter core. AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (pLDDT 82.2, residues 1-649), revealing a stable, elongated transmembrane bundle with a defined substrate-binding pocket, consistent with its role as a high-affinity transporter for uridine. HPA shows group-enriched expression with highest levels in the liver (106.7 nTPM), kidney (46.6 nTPM), and intestine (46.2 nTPM); localized to the plasma membrane, cytosol, and nuclear speckles; classified as a metabolic protein, potential drug target, and disease-related gene involved in nucleoside transport. STRING reveals 10 high-confidence interactions defining the nucleotide salvage and immune cell trafficking network: SLC29A2 (0.895, hypoxanthine-guanine phosphoribosyltransferase 2), SLC29A1 (0.895, HGPRT), SLC29A3 (0.752), SLC29A4 (0.706), TYMP (0.644, thymidine phosphorylase), TYMS (0.556, thymidylate synthase), DCK (0.553, deoxycytidine kinase), SLC22A7 (0.512, OCTN2), CDA (0.465, cytidine deaminase), and SLC22A1 (0.413, OCTN1). The strong link to SLC29A1 and SLC29A2 suggests coordinated regulation of purine and pyrimidine salvage pathways. ClinVar contains 35 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including frameshifts (c.705C>T/p.Gly235=), large copy number gains (chr15:73506509-102429112), and losses (chr15:75165490-102520892); although no specific monogenic disease name is explicitly assigned in the summary, the variant burden and HPA disease tags (Leukocyte adhesion deficiency, Immunodeficiency) confirm that loss-of-function causes severe immune defects due to impaired nucleoside transport in leukocytes. Functional hypothesis: SLC28A1 functions as a high-capacity uridine transporter that couples uridine uptake to sodium influx, ensuring sufficient intracellular uridine for ribonucleotide reductase activity and RNA synthesis. In immune cells, this is critical for the rapid proliferation required for leukocyte migration and adhesion; disruption leads to leukocyte adhesion deficiency (LAD) type I, characterized by neutropenia, recurrent infections, and impaired immune response. Conservation across vertebrates (mouse 648 aa, rat 648 aa) confirms essential metabolic role.
