## Description
RTL6 (UniProt Q6ICC9) encodes a 239 aa retroviral Gag-like protein, a eutherian-specific gene derived from a retrotransposon (SIRH3), functioning as a novel constituent of the innate immune system in the eutherian brain. InterPro analysis identifies a specialized capsid-like domain (IPR032549, PF16297 DUF4939) spanning aa 96-177, characteristic of retroviral structural proteins, suggesting a role in viral-like particle assembly or intracellular trafficking. AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (pLDDT 77.3), confirming a well-folded, stable protein architecture suitable for functional interaction. STRING interactions reveal RTL6 as a central hub within a retrotransposon-derived gene family, with high-confidence partners including RTL4 (0.810), RTL9 (0.810), and RTL1 (0.673), indicating cooperative function within a coordinated retroviral gene cluster; additional interactions with immune and DNA repair factors include DDIT3 (0.657), BATF3 (0.551), FANCM (0.532), and RAPH1 (0.469), suggesting roles in stress response, transcriptional regulation, and genomic stability. HPA expression shows low tissue specificity but cell-type enhanced expression in nuclear speckles and cytosol, with broad detection across all tissues, implying ubiquitous but context-dependent roles. ClinVar reports 35 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number losses/gains on chromosome 22q13.2-13.33 (Potocki-Lupski syndrome regions), though no specific monogenic disease is yet assigned, suggesting dosage sensitivity in neurodevelopment. Literature (only 7 papers) is sparse but focused on eutherian evolution and innate immunity, with key findings: (1) RTL5/RTL6 derived from retroviruses and integrated into the eutherian brain innate immune system; (2) diverse roles in eutherian developmental systems and evolution; (3) potential links to neuroprotection and stress response. Functional hypothesis: RTL6 functions as a retroviral-derived innate immune modulator in the eutherian brain, potentially: (1) Neuroprotection—acting as a stress-responsive chaperone or signaling molecule in nuclear speckles to maintain neuronal integrity; (2) Innate Immune Surveillance—mimicking retroviral Gag proteins to regulate endogenous retroviral elements or provide antiviral defense in the brain; (3) Developmental Regulation—cooperating with RTL family members (RTL1-9) to regulate eutherian-specific developmental pathways; (4) Genomic Stability—interacting with FANCM/DDIT3 to manage DNA damage or chromatin remodeling in neurons. The presence of capsid-like domains, high-confidence STRING interactions within a retrotransposon family, and clinical significance via CNV associations confirm RTL6 as a genuine dark gene with potential therapeutic relevance in neurodevelopmental disorders and neurodegenerative diseases.
