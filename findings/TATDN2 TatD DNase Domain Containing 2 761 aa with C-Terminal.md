## Description
TATDN2 (TatD DNase Domain Containing 2) is a 761-amino acid intracellular nuclease that functions as a 3'-5' RNA/DNA exonuclease involved in genome stability maintenance, R-loop resolution, and endoplasmic reticulum stress responses. InterPro analysis reveals a specialized domain architecture: an N-terminal region of unknown function (residues 1-494) that may contain regulatory or protein-interaction motifs, and a C-terminal TatD-type metallo-dependent hydrolase domain (cd01310, residues 495-760; IPR001130, residues 495-760; PF01026, residues 496-760; PTHR46363, residues 48-761) containing three conserved catalytic signatures (PS01137 residues 495-503, PS01090 residues 626-636, PS01091 residues 693-709) essential for metal ion coordination and nuclease activity. The protein belongs to the metal-dependent hydrolase superfamily (G3DSA:3.20.20.140, residues 495-761; SSF51556, residues 495-760; IPR032466, residues 495-760). It is cell type enhanced in single cell analysis, indicating specialized functions in specific cell populations.\n\nClinVar reports 44 pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number variations spanning 3p26.3-14.3 (coordinates include chr3:2263690-55016039x3 gain, chr3:61892-10562002x1 loss, chr3:10239102-11732086x1 loss, chr3:9974258-11078781x1 loss, chr3:60000-34461438x3 gain, chr3:61891-33946644x3 gain, chr3:6306331-11468530x1 loss, chr3:310747-28297447x3 gain). Key findings include its 2025 characterization as a DNA repair factor in primordial germ cells, its 2023 discovery as an R-loop resolution enzyme synthetic lethal with BRCA1 deficiency, its 2023 association with type 1 diabetes susceptibility, and its 2023 role in ER stress prognostic signatures.\n\nWe hypothesize that TATDN2 functions through a multi-modal nuclease mechanism: (1) R-loop resolution—TATDN2 cleaves RNA-DNA hybrids that form during transcription, preventing replication-transcription conflicts and genome instability, explaining its essential role in PGCs where high transcriptional activity occurs; (2) ER stress integration—XBP1 interaction suggests TATDN2 is transcriptionally induced during unfolded protein response, possibly clearing aberrant RNA species accumulated during stress; (3) BRCA1 synthetic lethality—BRCA1-deficient cells rely on TATDN2 for R-loop clearance; miR-4638-5p downregulation in BRCA1 mutants upregulates TATDN2, creating dependency that can be exploited therapeutically; (4) Nuclear speckle function—localization to splicing factor compartments suggests TATDN2 couples RNA processing with DNA repair, possibly resolving R-loops at active genes; (5) Immune modulation—IRAK2 interaction links TATDN2 to innate immune signaling, potentially via nucleic acid sensing pathways; (6) Chromatin regulation—BAHD1 interaction suggests roles in heterochromatin formation or silencing, possibly via RNA-mediated mechanisms; (7) Type 1 diabetes—germline variants may affect pancreatic beta cell genome stability or ER stress responses, contributing to autoimmune destruction.
