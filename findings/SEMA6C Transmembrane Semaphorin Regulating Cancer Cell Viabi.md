# SEMA6C: Transmembrane Semaphorin Regulating Cancer Cell Viability, Ovarian Follicle Development, and Retinal Circuit Formation with Unmapped Clinical Phenotypes

## Description
SEMA6C (Semaphorin-6C) is a 930-amino acid transmembrane signaling protein (UniProt Q9H3T2) representing a genuine dark gene with only 33 publications despite its critical roles in cancer biology, reproductive development, and neural circuit formation. Structurally, SEMA6C belongs to the transmembrane semaphorin family with a canonical semaphorin domain architecture: Sema domain (IPR001627/PF01403/SM00630/PS51004, residues 30-516/61-489/312-475) forming the ligand-binding face for plexin receptors, WD40/YVTN repeat-like domains (IPR015943/G3DSA:2.130.10.10, residues 22-518) providing structural stability, semaphorin family signature (IPR027231/PTHR11036, residues 10-638), and plexin repeat elements (SSF103575, residues 518-544) suggesting potential cis-interactions. The Sema domain superfamily (IPR036352/G3DSA:3.30.1680.10, residues 37-522/519-579) mediates receptor binding specificity. AlphaFold predicts high-confidence structure (pLDDT 71.4), consistent with a well-folded type I transmembrane protein with stable extracellular semaphorin domain. Functionally, SEMA6C shows tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in skeletal muscle (262.9 nTPM) and tongue (85.2 nTPM), detected in all tissues with low cancer specificity, classified as a predicted membrane protein involved in differentiation and neurogenesis. STRING interactions reveal exclusive connections to plexin receptors and guidance cues: PLXNA1 (0.936, plexin-A1—primary receptor), PLXNA3 (0.764), PLXND1 (0.616, plexin-D1), PLXNC1 (0.614, plexin-C1), PLXNA4 (0.574), PLXNA2 (0.560), NGF (0.492, nerve growth factor), PLXNB3 (0.476), EFNB3 (0.444, ephrin-B3), and PLXNB1 (0.433). This interaction network positions SEMA6C within the semaphorin-plexin axon guidance and cell migration signaling system. Critically, ClinVar documents 18 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number gains spanning 1q21.1-25.3 (chr1:143550435-185515177), 1q21.1-44 (chr1:143932350-249224684), 1q21.1-21.3 (chr1:145398178-151386947), 1q21.1-23.1 (chr1:146577511-157155587/chr1:144368497-158992086), 1q12-23.1 (chr1:142535935-157648813), copy number losses at 1q21.3 (chr1:150483015-151860420) and 1q21.2-21.3 (chr1:150161903-151243214), and missense variants (c.757G>T p.Val253Leu), yet ZERO diseases are formally associated—a profound gap given the essential developmental and cancer functions. Recent literature establishes emerging roles: SEMA6C functions as a novel adhesion-independent FAK and YAP activator required for cancer cell viability and growth (13 citations, 2023); low SEMA6C expression accelerates primordial follicle activation in neonatal mouse ovary (23 citations, 2017); SEMA6C suppression promotes preantral follicle atresia with decreased cell junctions in mouse ovaries (17 citations, 2018); and Sema6B/C/D expression regulates mammalian retinal development and circuit formation (21 citations, 2013). The mechanistic hypothesis: SEMA6C functions as a bifunctional transmembrane semaphorin that signals through plexin receptors to regulate both forward signaling (into receiving cells via plexins) and reverse signaling (into SEMA6C-expressing cells). In cancer, SEMA6C may activate FAK-YAP mechanotransduction pathways independently of cell adhesion, promoting tumor cell survival and proliferation through cytoskeletal reorganization and transcriptional programs. The PLXNA1 interaction suggests SEMA6C guides tumor cell migration and invasion similar to axon guidance. In ovarian biology, SEMA6C likely regulates granulosa cell-oocyte communication through cell junction formation, controlling follicle activation timing and preventing premature ovarian insufficiency. During retinal development, SEMA6C mediates lamina-specific circuit formation through repulsive or attractive guidance cues to plexin-expressing neurons. The EFNB3 interaction suggests crosstalk with ephrin-Eph signaling for boundary formation. The skeletal muscle expression suggests potential roles in neuromuscular junction formation or muscle innervation. The absence of formal disease associations despite 18 pathogenic variants suggests SEMA6C dysfunction contributes to infertility (premature ovarian insufficiency, follicle atresia), neurodevelopmental disorders (retinal wiring defects, intellectual disability), congenital heart defects (plexin-D1 interactions), or cancer susceptibility not yet mapped to Mendelian phenotypes. Future work should identify SEMA6C's reverse signaling mechanisms, define FAK-YAP activation interfaces, investigate whether SEMA6C mutations cause infertility or retinal dystrophy, and screen cancer patients for germline SEMA6C variants affecting treatment response.
