# MTG2: Mitochondrial Obg-Type GTPase Driving Mitoribosome Large Subunit Maturation and 16S rRNA Methylation in Oxidative Phosphorylation Assembly and Uveal Melanoma Progression

## Description
MTG2 (Mitochondrial Ribosome-associated GTPase 2, also known as GTPBP5, UniProt Q9H4K7) is a 406 aa mitochondrial GTPase belonging to the Obg (HflX-like) family of P-loop GTPases that functions in mitoribosome large subunit (mtLSU) biogenesis and 16S rRNA methylation. The molecular architecture consists of two functional domains: an N-terminal GTP1/OBG domain (IPR006169/G3DSA:2.70.210.12, positions 70-224) involved in ribosome binding and assembly factor recruitment, and a C-terminal Obg GTPase domain (IPR005225/cd01898, positions 225-385) containing the conserved P-loop GTP-binding motif (G3DSA:3.40.50.300, positions 224-396) for nucleotide hydrolysis-driven conformational changes. Literature (29 papers) establishes MTG2 roles in: (1) MTG2 as causal and druggable target in uveal melanoma via mitochondrial DNA dynamics (2026, functional validation), (2) human GTPBP5/MTG2 fueling mitoribosome large subunit maturation by facilitating 16S rRNA methylation at specific nucleotides (2020, 33 citations), (3) late stages of yeast mitoribosome large subunit biogenesis involving conserved Obg GTPases (2025), (4) MTG2 depletion impairing mitochondrial translation and oxidative phosphorylation complex assembly. HPA confirms ubiquitous expression across all tissues with low tissue specificity and mitochondrial localization, consistent with housekeeping role in mitochondrial protein synthesis. STRING interactions reveal mitoribosome assembly complex: MALSU1 (0.987) - mitochondrial assembly of ribosomal large subunit factor 1; MTERF4 (0.946) - mitochondrial transcription termination factor 4 regulating ribosome biogenesis; NSUN4 (0.943) - rRNA methyltransferase forming complex with MTERF4 for mtLSU maturation; MTG1 (0.882) - mitochondrial GTPase 1, paralog involved in mtLSU assembly; MRPL20/32/21/27/17 (0.803-0.831) - mitochondrial ribosomal protein L subunits; MRM2 (0.808) - mitochondrial rRNA methyltransferase 2. ClinVar contains 19 pathogenic variants including large copy number gains at 20q13.13-13.33, deletions (c.827-154G>T, c.353-23_22del), and chromosomal rearrangements, though specific monogenic disease phenotypes remain under investigation. AlphaFold shows confident structure (pLDDT 75.6) indicating well-folded two-domain architecture with stable GTPase core, typical of active Obg family GTPases. Conservation: highly conserved across eukaryotes with yeast ortholog Mtg2 (518 aa) and orthologs in mammals. Functional hypothesis: MTG2 functions as a late-stage mitoribosome large subunit assembly factor that couples GTP hydrolysis to 16S rRNA methylation and ribosomal protein incorporation. The mechanistic model proposes: (1) MTG2 binds to pre-55S mitoribosome particles via its N-terminal GTP1/OBG domain, positioning the C-terminal GTPase domain near the peptidyl transferase center, (2) GTP binding induces conformational change that recruits NSUN4-MTERF4 methyltransferase complex to catalyze 16S rRNA methylation at specific nucleotides (likely m5C or m2G modifications), (3) GTP hydrolysis triggers MTG2 dissociation, allowing final ribosomal protein (MRPL subunits) incorporation and subunit joining, (4) mature 55S mitoribosomes translate 13 mtDNA-encoded OXPHOS subunits. In uveal melanoma, MTG2 upregulation enhances mitochondrial translation capacity, supporting increased OXPHOS dependency and tumor progression. Pathogenic CNVs at 20q13 disrupt MTG2 dosage, potentially causing mitochondrial translation defects and OXPHOS deficiency. MTG2 represents an understudied mitochondrial ribosome assembly factor linking GTPase-driven conformational switching to rRNA modification and cancer metabolism.
