## Description
PLEKHO2 (Pleckstrin Homology Domain Containing O2, also PLEKHQ1, PP1628) is a 490 amino acid protein (UniProt Q8TD55) with a partially structured conformation (AlphaFold pLDDT 62.2, medium confidence), characteristic of proteins with ordered PH domains connected by flexible linker regions that facilitate dynamic membrane association and protein-protein interactions.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals PLEKHO2 contains a canonical Pleckstrin Homology (PH) domain (IPR001849/PF00169/PS50003/SM00233, residues 15-121/18-121/20-118; G3DSA:2.30.29.30/SSF50729, residues 4-117) conferring protein binding (GO:0005515) and phospholipid membrane binding activities. The protein belongs to the Pleckstrin homology domain-containing family O member 1/2 (IPR043448/PTHR15871, residues 1-490). The PH domain adopts a characteristic beta-sandwich fold with a C-terminal alpha-helix that binds phosphatidylinositol phosphates (PIPs) at the plasma membrane, recruiting PLEKHO2 to specific membrane compartments during immune cell activation.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals PLEKHO2 functions within cytoskeletal and immune signaling networks: strongest interactions with CAPZB (0.778, F-actin-capping protein subunit beta), CAPZA1 (0.735, F-actin-capping protein subunit alpha-1), IL10RB (0.528, interleukin-10 receptor subunit beta), THEMIS2 (0.516, thymocyte selection-associated family member 2, immune regulator), FAM184A (0.469), CYTH4 (0.453, cytohesin-4, ARF guanine nucleotide exchange factor), MAP3K6 (0.444, mitogen-activated protein kinase kinase kinase 6), VEZT (0.424, vezatin, adherens junction protein), FLAD1 (0.420, FAD synthase), and MYO1F (0.411, myosin-IF, unconventional myosin). The CAPZB/CAPZA1 interactions suggest roles in actin cytoskeleton regulation, while IL10RB and THEMIS2 indicate immune signaling functions.\n\nHPA expression shows tissue-enhanced specificity with highest expression in lymphoid tissue (98.2 nTPM), detected in all tissues with nucleoplasm/plasma membrane/cytosol subcellular localization, consistent with immune cell functions requiring membrane recruitment and cytoskeletal remodeling. The protein is classified as predicted intracellular.\n\nClinVar documents 11 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including a missense variant (c.484G>C/p.Val162Leu) within the PH domain and large chromosome 15q11.2-26.3 copy number variations (gains spanning 15q11.2-26.3 to 15q22.31-26.3 regions). The PH domain missense variant may disrupt phospholipid binding, while CNVs suggest dosage sensitivity potentially causing immunodeficiency or autoimmunity through impaired macrophage apoptosis regulation.\n\nConservation is evident from mouse (Q8K124, 495 aa), cow (Q32LQ1, 499 aa), and hamster (A0AAU9ZUR8, 488 aa) orthologs, indicating functional importance in mammalian immune systems.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: PLEKHO2 acts as a membrane-recruited scaffold protein that coordinates macrophage apoptotic signaling with cytoskeletal remodeling during immune responses. The PH domain binds phosphatidylinositol-3,4,5-trisphosphate (PIP3) or other PIPs at the plasma membrane upon immune receptor activation (e.g., IL-10 signaling via IL10RB), recruiting PLEKHO2 to membrane microdomains. The CAPZB/CAPZA1 interactions suggest PLEKHO2 regulates F-actin capping during phagocytosis or apoptotic cell engulfment, controlling actin polymerization at the phagocytic cup. THEMIS2 interaction links PLEKHO2 to T-cell selection and immune tolerance pathways. CYTH4 interaction suggests cross-talk with ARF GTPase-mediated vesicle trafficking. The partially structured nature (pLDDT 62.2) indicates intrinsically disordered regions that facilitate dynamic interactions within immune signaling condensates. CNV-mediated dosage imbalance or PH domain mutations disrupt membrane recruitment and cytoskeletal coordination, impairing macrophage apoptosis and potentially causing autoimmunity, immunodeficiency, or lymphoproliferative disorders through defective clearance of apoptotic cells.
