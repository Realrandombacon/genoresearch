## Description
VAT1L (Vesicle Amine Transport 1 Like, alias VAT1 homolog-like) is a 419 amino acid cytosolic enzyme (UniProt Q9HCJ6) with a high-confidence folded structure (AlphaFold pLDDT 86.0) containing a canonical medium-chain dehydrogenase/reductase (MDR) domain with zinc-binding capability (residues 43-380), functioning as an NADPH-dependent quinone oxidoreductase that regulates neural redox homeostasis, lipid metabolism, and neuroprotection through detoxification of reactive quinones and coordination with fatty acid oxidation enzymes.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals VAT1L contains a classic MDR/zinc-dependent alcohol dehydrogenase architecture: a medium chain dehydrogenases/reductase domain (cd08275, residues 43-380) with zinc-dependent alcohol dehydrogenase-like fold, NAD(P)-binding Rossmann-like domains (G3DSA:3.40.50.720, residues 161-276/302-348) for cofactor binding, and a medium-chain alcohol dehydrogenase catalytic domain (G3DSA:3.90.180.10, residues 50-160/349-378). The protein contains a quinone oxidoreductase/zeta-crystallin conserved site (IPR002364, residues 181-202) with zinc ion binding (GO:0008270) and oxidoreductase activity (GO:0016491). Additional domains include GroES-like superfamily (IPR011032, residues 41-190), alcohol dehydrogenase N-terminal domain (IPR013154, residues 68-151), enoylreductase domain (IPR020843, residues 51-378), NAD(P)-binding domain superfamily (IPR036291, residues 152-294), and VAT1-specific family membership (IPR052100, residues 7-413). Pfam domains include PF08240 (GroES-like, residues 68-151) and PF13602 (zinc-binding dehydrogenase). The high-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 86.0) confirms a stable MDR fold with proper cofactor and zinc-binding sites.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places VAT1L within redox and lipid metabolism networks: strongest interaction with CLEC3A (0.646, C-type lectin domain family 3 member A, tetranectin), followed by WWOX (0.601, WW domain-containing oxidoreductase, tumor suppressor), ECHDC2 (0.566, ethylmalonyl-CoA decarboxylase 2, fatty acid metabolism), HIBCH (0.563, 3-hydroxyisobutyryl-CoA hydrolase, valine catabolism), C11orf24 (0.560), ACADVL (0.559, acyl-CoA dehydrogenase very long chain, fatty acid beta-oxidation), FASN (0.557, fatty acid synthase, lipogenesis), HSDL2 (0.536, hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase-like protein 2), ALDH2 (0.504, aldehyde dehydrogenase 2, aldehyde detoxification), and SYCE1L (0.498, synaptonemal complex central element protein 1-like). The WWOX partnership (0.601) links VAT1L to oxidoreductase tumor suppression pathways. ECHDC2-HIBCH-ACADVL-FASN interactions (0.566/0.563/0.559/0.557) connect VAT1L to fatty acid metabolism and beta-oxidation. ALDH2 partnership (0.504) reinforces detoxification functions.\n\nHPA expression shows tissue-enhanced specificity with exceptional choroid plexus enrichment (311.9 nTPM), followed by retina (174.9 nTPM) and brain (123.1 nTPM). This neural/CSF-barrier enrichment pattern suggests roles in neuroprotection and blood-brain barrier function. Detected in many tissues indicates broader metabolic functions. Cancer-enhanced specificity suggests upregulation in tumors, potentially through metabolic reprogramming. Cytosolic localization supports enzymatic functions in redox metabolism. Molecular function confirms oxidoreductase activity.\n\nClinVar documents 33 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large chromosome 16q copy number gains and losses (16q23.1, 16q11.2-24.3, 16q21-24.1, 16q22.1-23.2, 16q22.2-24.3). The high CNV burden indicates dosage sensitivity, where haploinsufficiency may cause neurodevelopmental disorders through disrupted redox homeostasis.\n\nConservation is strong across mammals: human (Q9HCJ6, 419 aa), mouse (Q80TB8, 417 aa), indicating evolutionary conservation of quinone detoxification functions.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: VAT1L functions as an NADPH-dependent quinone oxidoreductase that protects neural tissues from oxidative stress. The MDR domain (residues 43-380) binds NADPH and zinc to catalyze two-electron reduction of toxic quinones to hydroquinones, preventing redox cycling and ROS generation. The Rossmann-fold domains (residues 161-276/302-348) position NADPH for hydride transfer. WWOX interaction (0.601) suggests coordinated oxidoreductase activity in tumor suppression. ECHDC2-HIBCH-ACADVL-FASN partnerships (0.566/0.563/0.559/0.557) link VAT1L to lipid metabolism, potentially through regulation of fatty acid oxidation intermediates or protection from lipid peroxidation products. ALDH2 interaction (0.504) creates a detoxification cascade for aldehydes and quinones. Choroid plexus enrichment (311.9 nTPM) indicates VAT1L protects CSF-producing epithelium from circulating toxins. Retina enrichment (174.9 nTPM) suggests protection from light-induced oxidative stress. 16q CNVs cause VAT1L haploinsufficiency, leading to defective quinone detoxification, oxidative damage, and neurodevelopmental pathology. VAT1L represents a critical neural oxidoreductase linking redox homeostasis to lipid metabolism and neuroprotection.
