## Description
SMARCE1 encodes a 411 aa nuclear protein (UniProt Q969G3), also known as BAF57, that serves as an essential subunit of the SWI/SNF (BAF) ATP-dependent chromatin remodeling complex. The protein contains a conserved high mobility group (HMG) box domain (IPR009071, PF00505, PS50118, SM00398, residues 66-134) that recognizes and binds distorted DNA structures including 4-way junctions, which mimic the topology of DNA entering/exiting nucleosomes. This HMG box belongs to the HMG-box superfamily (IPR036910, G3DSA:1.10.30.10, SSF47095, residues 57-168) and enables sequence-independent DNA binding through minor groove recognition and DNA bending. STRING interactions place SMARCE1 at the absolute core of the BAF complex with ultra-high confidence partnerships to all major subunits: SMARCA4/BRG1 and SMARCA2/BRM (0.999), the catalytic ATPase engines; SMARCB1/SNF5 (0.999), a tumor suppressor; ARID1A and ARID2 (0.972-0.999), DNA-binding specificity factors; SMARCC1/BAF155 (0.994), scaffold subunits; BRD7 (0.999), bromodomain-containing regulator; PHF10 (0.999), PBAF-specific subunit; and SMARCD3/BAF60C (0.961), tissue-specific targeting factor. HPA confirms ubiquitous expression across all tissues with exclusive nucleoplasmic localization, annotating roles in chromatin regulation, DNA-binding, and neurogenesis, with disease associations including intellectual disability and cancer. ClinVar lists 161 pathogenic variants including numerous frameshift mutations (p.Lys112fs, p.Leu41fs, p.Lys258fs, p.Ile57fs, p.Ser157fs, p.Leu234fs), deletions, duplications, and missense variants (p.Glu120Lys within the HMG box), demonstrating extreme dosage sensitivity and linking SMARCE1 haploinsufficiency to meningioma predisposition and neurodevelopmental disorders. AlphaFold structure prediction yields medium confidence (global pLDDT 69.6), consistent with a structured HMG box domain embedded in flexible disordered regions typical of chromatin regulators requiring conformational adaptability for multi-protein complex assembly and nucleosome engagement. Mechanistically, SMARCE1 functions through multiple modes: (1) The HMG box binds distorted DNA at nucleosome entry/exit sites, stabilizing the DNA wrapping state and facilitating SWI/SNF complex recruitment; (2) SMARCE1 acts as a molecular bridge between the catalytic ATPase subunits (SMARCA4/2) and DNA, transmitting conformational changes during ATP-dependent nucleosome sliding; (3) The protein may recognize specific chromatin states or DNA damage structures through its 4-way junction binding capability, targeting BAF complexes to sites requiring repair or transcriptional activation; (4) Within the BAF complex, SMARCE1 likely helps position the complex on nucleosomes for optimal remodeling activity. Disruption of SMARCE1 would impair chromatin accessibility, leading to transcriptional dysregulation of developmentally critical genes, defective DNA repair, and tumorigenesis—phenotypes consistent with the meningioma predisposition and intellectual disability observed in patients with SMARCE1 mutations.
