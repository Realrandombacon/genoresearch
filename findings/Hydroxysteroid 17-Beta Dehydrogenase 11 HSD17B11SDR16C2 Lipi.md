## Description
HSD17B11 encodes a 300 aa intracellular enzyme (UniProt Q8NBQ5), also known as SDR16C2 or DHRS8, that belongs to the classical short-chain dehydrogenase/reductase (SDR) superfamily. The protein contains a conserved NAD(P)-binding Rossmann-fold domain (IPR036291, G3DSA:3.40.50.720, SSF51735, residues 33-289) characteristic of oxidoreductases, and a specific SDR catalytic domain (IPR002347, PF00106, cd05339, residues 38-227) with signature motifs for cofactor binding and substrate recognition. HSD17B11 is a member of the human 17-beta-hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase-like family (PTHR24322) and shares structural homology with other steroid-metabolizing enzymes. STRING interactions place HSD17B11 in the context of lipid and steroid metabolism with moderate-confidence partnerships to: HSD17B2 (0.608), another 17-beta-HSD isoform involved in steroid inactivation; NR5A1/SF1 (0.608), a nuclear receptor regulating steroidogenic gene expression; HSD17B4 (0.579), a peroxisomal enzyme involved in fatty acid beta-oxidation and estradiol metabolism; ADAMTSL3 (0.546), a secreted glycoprotein; PEX19 (0.523), a peroxisomal biogenesis factor; and PECR (0.512), a peroxisomal trans-2-enoyl-CoA reductase. HPA shows tissue-enhanced expression peaking in the intestine (271.8 nTPM) and liver (225.9 nTPM)—key sites for lipid absorption and steroid metabolism—with exclusive localization to lipid droplets, confirming its role in lipid storage and mobilization. ClinVar lists 30 pathogenic variants including large copy number gains/losses spanning chromosome 4q21-22, deletions, duplications, and missense variants (p.His248Tyr within the catalytic domain), demonstrating dosage sensitivity and linking HSD17B11 disruption to developmental disorders though no specific disease phenotype has been definitively assigned. AlphaFold structure prediction yields very high confidence (global pLDDT 93.6), revealing a canonical SDR fold with a well-defined Rossmann fold for NAD(P)H binding and a substrate-binding pocket, consistent with an active oxidoreductase. Mechanistically, HSD17B11 likely functions as a lipid droplet-associated oxidoreductase: (1) Its localization to lipid droplets suggests it metabolizes lipophilic substrates stored within these organelles, potentially converting active steroids (e.g., estradiol, testosterone) to inactive forms or vice versa depending on cellular needs; (2) The interaction with HSD17B2 and HSD17B4 suggests functional redundancy or cooperation in the systemic regulation of steroid hormone levels; (3) Association with peroxisomal proteins (PEX19, PECR, HSD17B4) hints at a role in coordinating lipid droplet dynamics with peroxisomal fatty acid oxidation; (4) High expression in intestine and liver aligns with roles in processing dietary lipids and first-pass metabolism of steroids. Disruption of HSD17B11 would cause dysregulated local steroid concentrations within lipid droplets, impaired lipid mobilization, and potentially metabolic syndrome or reproductive disorders—phenotypes consistent with the pathogenic CNVs observed in patients with 4q deletion/duplication syndromes.
