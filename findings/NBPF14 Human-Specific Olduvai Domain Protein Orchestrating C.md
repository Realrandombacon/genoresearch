## Description
NBPF14 (NBPF Member 14, UniProt Q5TI25) is a 2988 aa human-specific intrinsically disordered protein that functions as a regulator of cortical progenitor abundance during neocortex development. Structurally, AlphaFold predicts a highly disordered fold (pLDDT=36.4), consistent with intrinsically disordered proteins that function through dynamic protein-protein interactions rather than enzymatic catalysis. The protein contains three tandem Olduvai domains (IPR010630, PF06758, also known as DUF1220, SM01148) at positions 165-259, 436-528, and 529-600, within the NBPF family region (IPR055306, aa 222-876). Olduvai/DUF1220 domains are primate-specific protein domains that have undergone dramatic copy number expansion in humans (human: ~300 copies, chimpanzee: ~125, mouse: 1), correlating with brain size evolution. Functionally, STRING interactions reveal connections to other primate-specific proteins including PPIAL4F (0.492) and PPIAL4D (0.490) (peptidyl-prolyl isomerase A-like family), C2orf78 (0.447), and NBPF19-2 (0.441), suggesting coordination within the NBPF network. HPA shows ubiquitous expression with subcellular localization to Golgi apparatus, vesicles, microtubules, cytokinetic bridge, and primary cilium—structures critical for cell division and ciliary signaling in neural progenitors. ClinVar identifies 59 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly copy number gains and losses at 1q21.1-21.2, a region associated with neurodevelopmental disorders including microcephaly, macrocephaly, autism, and schizophrenia. Literature (9 papers) reveals NBPF14 as a recently identified (2025) human-specific gene that coevolved with NOTCH2NLB to orchestrate cortical progenitor abundance crucial for human neocortex expansion, and is differentially expressed in brain metastatic breast cancer. The gene represents a 'genuine dark gene' where human-specific evolutionary innovation in brain development remains mechanistically unexplored—Olduvai domain copy number variation may directly modulate neural progenitor symmetric vs. asymmetric division through interactions with ciliary and mitotic machinery.
