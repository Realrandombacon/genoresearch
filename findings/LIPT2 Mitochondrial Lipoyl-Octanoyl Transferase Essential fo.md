## Description
LIPT2 (Lipoyl(octanoyl) transferase 2) is a 231-amino acid mitochondrial enzyme that catalyzes the transfer of octanoyl groups from octanoyl-ACP to specific lysine residues on target apoproteins, a critical step in the de novo biosynthesis of lipoic acid. Lipoic acid is an essential cofactor for five major mitochondrial enzyme complexes: pyruvate dehydrogenase (PDH), alpha-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase (KGDH), branched-chain alpha-ketoacid dehydrogenase (BCKDH), the glycine cleavage system (GCS), and 2-oxoadipate dehydrogenase. InterPro analysis confirms LIPT2 belongs to the LipB family of octanoyltransferases (IPR000544, IPR004143, PF21948), containing a conserved catalytic domain (cd16444) spanning residues 7-219. AlphaFold predicts a highly stable, well-folded structure with a global pLDDT score of 93.1, indicating a rigid enzymatic core consistent with its housekeeping metabolic role.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals a tightly coordinated functional network with perfect confidence scores for key partners: LIPT1 (0.998), the downstream lipoyltransferase; LIAS (0.995), lipoic acid synthetase; and GCSH (0.923), a direct substrate. Additional partners include NDUFAB1 (0.843) and FASN (0.805), linking it to fatty acid synthesis and mitochondrial complex I assembly. Human Protein Atlas data shows LIPT2 is expressed in many tissues with peak expression in testis (10.9 nTPM), classified as 'tissue enhanced', reflecting the high metabolic demand of germ cells. ClinVar reports 17 pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants, including missense mutations (e.g., p.Gly109Asp, p.Ile36Thr) and large copy number gains. Recent literature (2024-2025) explicitly links biallelic LIPT2 variants to infantile-onset dystonia and a distinctive 'Ears of the Lynx' MRI sign, expanding the clinical spectrum of lipoic acid deficiency disorders beyond classic Leigh-like syndrome. Furthermore, LIPT2 is implicated in cuproptosis (copper-induced cell death) and is being evaluated as a pan-cancer prognostic marker, while NEDD4L-mediated degradation of LIPT2 drives mitochondrial dysfunction in Huntington's disease.\n\nWe hypothesize that LIPT2 functions as the gatekeeper of mitochondrial lipoylation status, where its activity directly limits the assembly of active dehydrogenase complexes. The specific vulnerability of the developing brain to LIPT2 loss (causing dystonia) suggests that neuronal energy metabolism, particularly at synaptic terminals with high PDH/KGDH demand, is exquisitely sensitive to partial lipoylation defects. The 'Ears of the Lynx' sign may reflect specific demyelination or metabolic stress in the splenium of the corpus callosum due to localized energy failure. Mechanistically, LIPT2 likely operates in a metabolon with LIPT1 and LIAS to channel octanoyl intermediates efficiently, preventing toxic accumulation of free octanoate or incomplete lipoylation intermediates.
