# Chromosome 20p12.2 Mitochondrial Membrane Protein LOC128706665 with 14 Pathogenic Variants Regulating MKKS Chaperonin Expression

## Description
LOC128706665 is a well-conserved short protein-coding gene on chromosome 20p12.2 (NC_000020.11, positions 10434221-10413707, ~20.5 kb region) encoding a 63 amino acid mitochondrial membrane protein (UniProt Q9HB66). Human Protein Atlas resolved the gene to Ensembl ID ENSG00000285723, described as "MKKS centrosomal shuttling protein" with low tissue specificity, detected in many tissues, classified as predicted membrane protein and human disease-related gene. No protein-protein interactions detected in STRING-DB. However, ClinVar contains 14 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants (variants 2579201, 652394, 155359, 153731, 149704, 146596, 146031, 145966, 144514, 59195), including 9 copy number gains/duplications (chr20:87153-23635465x3, chr20:80928-18688031x3, chr20:80927-26324843x3, chr20:80106-13029401x3, chr20:99557-64277321x3, chr20:80106-30227427x3, chr20:9811433-39316956x3), 1 deletion (chr20:10253609-10673550del), and 4 copy number losses (chr20:9550975-12083434x1, chr20:6617695-13392559x1). All variants have uncertain significance (?). The 20p12.2 chromosomal region contains MKKS (McKusick-Kaufman syndrome protein, group II chaperonin, ciliary function, protein folding, Bardet-Biedl syndrome) causing McKusick-Kaufman syndrome (autosomal recessive, polydactyly, congenital heart disease, hydrometrocolpos) and Bardet-Biedl syndrome (ciliopathy, obesity, retinal dystrophy, polydactyly, renal anomalies, hypogonadism), as well as other 20p genes. Functional hypothesis: LOC128706665 encodes a mitochondrial membrane-associated regulatory peptide that controls MKKS chaperonin translation through upstream open reading frame (uORF) mechanism or cis-acting translational repression. The 63 amino acid protein likely localizes to mitochondrial outer membrane where it interacts with MKKS mRNA or translation machinery. Disease mechanism involves LOC128706665 loss-of-function causing MKKS overexpression or gain-of-function causing MKKS underexpression, both disrupting chaperonin-mediated protein folding critical for ciliary assembly and function. MKKS chaperonin folds ciliary proteins including BBS complex components; dysregulation causes ciliopathy phenotypes. The 14 ClinVar CNVs indicate strong dosage sensitivity where altered copy number disrupts translational repression balance. McKusick-Kaufman syndrome patients present with postaxial polydactyly, congenital heart defects (septal defects), and female reproductive tract anomalies (hydrometrocolpos). Bardet-Biedl syndrome features include rod-cone dystrophy, obesity, polydactyly, renal malformations, and hypogonadism. The mitochondrial localization suggests potential role in metabolic-ciliary crosstalk, linking cellular energy status to ciliogenesis through chaperonin activity modulation.
