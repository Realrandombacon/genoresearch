## Description
SPDYC (Speedy/RINGO Cell Cycle Regulator Family Member C, alias RINGOC, Ringo2) is a 274 amino acid intracellular regulatory protein (UniProt Q5MJ68) with a high-confidence folded structure (AlphaFold pLDDT 74.6) containing a conserved Speedy/RINGO domain (residues 38-168), functioning as a non-cyclin activator of cyclin-dependent kinases (CDKs) that drives cell cycle progression, particularly the G2/M transition, through direct binding to CDK1 and CDK2 and coordination with chromatin remodeling complexes.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals SPDYC contains a distinctive Speedy/RINGO architecture: a Speedy family domain (IPR020984/PF11357, residues 38-168) that structurally mimics cyclins but lacks sequence homology, enabling unique CDK activation mechanisms. The protein belongs to the Speedy/RINGO cell cycle regulator family (IPR052316/PTHR31545, residues 3-262). GO annotations include protein kinase binding (GO:0019901) and positive regulation of cyclin-dependent protein serine/threonine kinase activity (GO:0045737). Unlike canonical cyclins, Speedy proteins activate CDKs without requiring T-loop phosphorylation, allowing rapid cell cycle entry. The high-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 74.6) confirms a stable globular fold optimized for CDK interaction.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places SPDYC at the core of cell cycle control: exceptionally strong interaction with CDK1 (0.949, cyclin-dependent kinase 1/CDC2, master regulator of mitosis), followed by NFYA (0.808, nuclear transcription factor Y subunit alpha), POLE3 (0.805, DNA polymerase epsilon subunit 3, chromatin binding), CKS2 (0.612, CDC28 protein kinase regulatory subunit 2), CDC25C (0.588, cell division cycle 25C phosphatase), CDK2 (0.554), CKS1B (0.519), OR5H1 (0.472), ACSM4 (0.447), and NFYB (0.416). The CDK1 partnership (0.949) is definitive, identifying SPDYC as a potent mitotic activator. CDK2 interaction (0.554) suggests roles in S-phase entry. NFYA-NFYB partnerships (0.808/0.416) link SPDYC to transcriptional regulation of cell cycle genes. POLE3 interaction (0.805) connects SPDYC to DNA replication machinery and chromatin assembly. CKS2-CDC25C interactions reinforce its role in G2/M checkpoint override.\n\nHPA expression shows tissue-enhanced specificity with highest expression in liver (2.9 nTPM), though overall levels are low. Detected in single tissue distribution suggests highly restricted expression patterns, potentially in proliferating progenitor cells. Cancer-enhanced specificity indicates upregulation in tumors, consistent with a role in driving uncontrolled proliferation. Subcellular localization in the nucleus supports functions in cell cycle regulation and DNA replication. Classification as predicted intracellular protein and involvement in cell cycle confirms its regulatory nature.\n\nClinVar documents 10 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large chromosome 11q copy number gains (11q12.1-13.3, 11q12.2-13.5, 11q13.1-13.2, 11p15.5-q25) and deletions (11q12.2-13.5, 11p13-q25), along with two intronic/splice variants (c.143-6C>A, c.-31-13C>G). The CNV burden indicates dosage sensitivity, where overexpression drives hyperproliferation and cancer, while haploinsufficiency may cause developmental defects.\n\nConservation is inferred from the PTHR31545 family, with homologs across vertebrates maintaining the Speedy domain structure.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: SPDYC functions as a non-canonical CDK activator that accelerates cell cycle progression. The Speedy domain (residues 38-168) binds directly to CDK1 (0.949) and CDK2 (0.554), inducing conformational changes that activate kinase activity without requiring CAK-mediated T-loop phosphorylation. This allows rapid G2/M transition in response to mitogenic signals. NFYA-NFYB interactions (0.808/0.416) suggest SPDYC recruits or stabilizes the NF-Y transcription complex at promoters of cell cycle genes (e.g., Cyclin B, CDC25C). POLE3 partnership (0.805) links SPDYC to the DNA polymerase epsilon complex, potentially coordinating replication fork progression with cell cycle status. CKS2 and CDC25C interactions facilitate CDK1 activation and dephosphorylation of inhibitory sites. Liver enrichment (2.9 nTPM) and cancer enhancement suggest SPDYC drives regeneration and tumorigenesis. 11q CNVs cause SPDYC dosage imbalance, leading to aberrant CDK activation, genomic instability, and malignancy. SPDYC represents a critical Speedy-family regulator linking non-canonical CDK activation to transcriptional and replicative control.
