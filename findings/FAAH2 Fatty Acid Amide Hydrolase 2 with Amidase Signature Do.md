## Description
FAAH2 encodes Fatty Acid Amide Hydrolase 2 (UniProt Q6GMR7), a 532-amino acid membrane-associated serine hydrolase that degrades endocannabinoids and fatty acid amides through amidase signature domain catalysis. The protein contains conserved amidase signature (AS) domain architecture including AS superfamily fold (IPR036928/G3DSA:3.90.1300.10/SSF75304, positions 38-525/51-525/38-523) forming alpha/beta hydrolase structure with catalytic serine nucleophile, amidase domain (IPR023631/PF01425, positions 69-513) containing the catalytic machinery, conserved amidase catalytic site (IPR020556/PS00571, positions 204-235) with signature GXSXG motif for serine hydrolase activity, and FAAH2-specific family membership (IPR052739/PTHR43372, positions 9-531) distinguishing it from FAAH1 paralog, classified within fungal-type amidase family (PIRSF001221, positions 52-522) with GO annotations for lipid degradation (GO:0044257), lipid metabolism (GO:0006629), and hydrolase activity (GO:0016787), indicating essential role in endocannabinoid tone regulation through hydrolysis of anandamide (AEA), 2-arachidonoylglycerol (2-AG), and N-acylethanolamines. AlphaFold predicts very high-confidence structure (pLDDT 93.9, residues 1-532) indicating exceptionally well-folded protein with stable alpha/beta hydrolase architecture suitable for membrane association and catalytic activity. STRING interaction analysis reveals FAAH2 forms a high-confidence interaction network with endocannabinoid metabolism and aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase complex partners: NAPEPLD (0.807; N-acylphosphatidylethanolamine phospholipase D, anandamide biosynthesis enzyme), NAAA (0.751; N-acylethanolamine acid amidase, parallel fatty acid amide hydrolase), DARS2 (0.900; aspartyl-tRNA synthetase 2, mitochondrial), GATB (0.837; glutamyl-tRNA amidotransferase subunit B), GMPS (0.774; GMP synthase), EPRS1 (0.723; glutamyl-prolyl-tRNA synthetase), PC (0.716; pyruvate carboxylase), ALDH18A1 (0.579; aldehyde dehydrogenase 18 family member A1), ATIC (0.485; bifunctional purine biosynthesis protein), and TMEM109 (0.478; transmembrane protein 109), confirming FAAH2's integration into endocannabinoid metabolism (NAPEPLD/NAAA, scores 0.751-0.807) for coordinated N-acylethanolamine biosynthesis and degradation, plus unexpected aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase complex associations (DARS2/GATB/EPRS1, 0.723-0.900) suggesting potential moonlighting functions or complex co-localization. HPA data shows low tissue specificity with expression detected in many tissues, immune cell enhanced blood cell specificity consistent with endocannabinoid immunomodulation function, classified as enzymes/metabolic proteins/predicted intracellular proteins, cell type enhanced single cell specificity, low cancer specificity. With 33 publications, FAAH2 is a PARTIALLY CHARACTERIZED gene with emerging mechanistic and clinical understanding: hemizygous FAAH2 variant in neurological/metabolic disorders (2025, 6 cit), FAAH2 as drug-metabolizing enzyme for DS-8500a hydrolysis (2026, 0 cit), FAAH2 in preeclampsia biomarker signatures (2024-2025, 1-14 cit). The functional hypothesis: FAAH2 serves as an X-linked endocannabinoid-degrading enzyme where amidase signature domain (positions 51-525, pLDDT 93.9) provides alpha/beta hydrolase fold with catalytic serine nucleophile (GXSXG motif, positions 204-235) for hydrolyzing fatty acid amides including anandamide and N-acylethanolamines; very high structural confidence indicates stable folded catalytic domain suitable for membrane association and substrate access; NAPEPLD interaction (0.807) confirms integration into endocannabinoid metabolic pathway where NAPEPLD synthesizes anandamide from NAPE and FAAH2 degrades it, creating metabolic cycle; NAAA interaction (0.751) suggests parallel/compensatory fatty acid amide hydrolysis; X-chromosome location explains hemizygous variant effects in males; immune cell enhanced expression validates endocannabinoid immunomodulation role in inflammation and immune response regulation; 134 ClinVar variants including CNVs suggest FAAH2 dosage sensitivity affects neurological function, metabolism, and pregnancy outcomes (preeclampsia) through altered endocannabinoid tone; hemizygous variants cause neurological/metabolic disorders (2025, 6 cit) through impaired endocannabinoid degradation leading to elevated AEA/NAE levels affecting cannabinoid receptor signaling (CB1/CB2); drug metabolism function (2026, 0 cit) reveals pharmacological relevance for therapeutic compound hydrolysis; 33 papers indicates transitional status from dark gene toward characterization with defined enzymatic function but complete disease spectrum and substrate specificity still being explored in neurology and metabolism.
