## Description
ZBTB17/MIZ1 (Q13105, 803 aa) is a partially structured (AlphaFold pLDDT: 64.1) BTB/POZ domain-containing zinc finger transcription factor functioning as a critical regulator of peroxisome biogenesis, MYC transcriptional antagonism, and cellular senescence programs. InterPro analysis reveals a characteristic architecture with an N-terminal BTB/POZ protein interaction domain (IPR000210/PF00651, residues 14-116) enabling homodimerization and corepressor recruitment, followed by multiple C2H2-type zinc finger DNA-binding domains (IPR013087/PF00096, residues 306-328/390-412/446-468) recognizing specific promoter elements, classifying it within the SKP1/BTB/POZ superfamily (IPR011333). HPA data confirms ubiquitous expression across all tissues with low tissue specificity but cell type-enhanced patterns, consistent with fundamental roles in transcriptional regulation across diverse cellular contexts. STRING interactions define a high-confidence network linking ZBTB17 to MYC (0.999), MAX (0.947), SP1 (0.903), paralog BCL6 (0.888), ZBTB4 (0.841), E3 ligase HUWE1 (0.826), XBP1 (0.594), MYCN (0.585), TOPBP1 (0.520), and EP300 (0.512), positioning it at the intersection of MYC-driven proliferation, stress responses, and epigenetic regulation. Literature (56 papers) establishes emerging mechanistic insights: 2025 studies demonstrate ZBTB17 promotes peroxisome biogenesis through direct transcriptional activation of PEX13, linking nuclear signaling to organelle homeostasis; CRISPR screens confirm ZBTB17 as a peroxisome regulator; meta-analyses implicate ZBTB17 alongside NKRF in NF-κB signaling contributing to shared molecular mechanisms of Alzheimer's disease and atherosclerosis; 2023 work reveals ZBTB17 controls cellular senescence via direct interaction with nuclear receptor RXRA and downstream calcium signaling modulation; and 2026 findings show BMI1 activated by ZBTB17 stabilizes SMAD2 to promote chondrocyte anabolism and alleviate osteoarthritis. ClinVar documents 26 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number losses and gains on chromosome 1p36 regions, implicating ZBTB17 dosage sensitivity in developmental disorders though specific monogenic phenotypes remain undefined. The convergence of BTB-mediated protein interactions, MYC antagonism, peroxisome regulation, RXRA-dependent senescence control, and 1p36 syndrome associations suggests ZBTB17 functions as a multifunctional transcriptional hub coordinating proliferation-quiescence decisions, organelle biogenesis, and age-related disease pathways, with therapeutic potential in neurodegeneration, cardiovascular disease, and osteoarthritis.
