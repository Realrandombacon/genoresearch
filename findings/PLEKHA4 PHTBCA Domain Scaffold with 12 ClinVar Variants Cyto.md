## Description
PLEKHA4 (Pleckstrin Homology Domain-Containing Family A Member 4; UniProt Q9H4M7) is a 779 amino acid intracellular scaffold protein with only 22 publications, classifying it as a genuine dark gene despite emerging roles in cancer metabolism and development. \n\nSTRUCTURAL ARCHITECTURE: InterPro analysis reveals a bipartite domain structure: an N-terminal Pleckstrin Homology (PH) domain (IPR001849/PF00169, residues 55-152) for phosphoinositide binding and membrane recruitment, and a central TBCA domain (IPR057971/PF25541, residues 363-494) of unknown specific function but conserved across the PLEKHA4-7 subfamily. The protein belongs to the phosphoinositol 3-phosphate-binding protein family (PTHR12752). AlphaFold predicts a medium-confidence structure (global pLDDT 57.1), consistent with a structured core containing flexible linker regions typical of signaling adaptors.\n\nINTERACTION NETWORK: STRING-DB identifies high-confidence interactions with KLHL12 (0.815, a BTB-Cullin ubiquitin ligase substrate adaptor), suggesting a role in targeted protein degradation. Other partners include PLEK2 (0.651), PLEK (0.648), EFTUD2 (0.593), HSPA1B (0.573), and metabolic enzymes like PKM (0.541), linking PLEKHA4 to cytoskeletal dynamics, stress response, and glycolysis.\n\nEXPRESSION & LOCALIZATION: HPA data shows ubiquitous expression with enhancement in specific cell types. Subcellular localization is dynamic, targeting the plasma membrane (via PH domain), cytosol, centriolar satellites, and notably the cytokinetic bridge during cell division. This specific localization implies a critical function in abscission or midbody regulation.\n\nDISEASE LANDSCAPE: ClinVar lists 12 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains at 19q13.33-q13.43 encompassing PLEKHA4. While no monogenic disease is yet defined, these CNVs are associated with developmental anomalies. Recent 2025 literature establishes PLEKHA4 as a driver of glycolytic reprogramming in glioblastoma via the HOXD9-STAT3/SOCS-1 axis, a regulator of apoptosis in melanoma through MAPK/β-catenin pathways, and a biomarker for acute kidney injury progression.\n\nFUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: PLEKHA4 likely functions as a membrane-recruited scaffold that coordinates signal transduction at the midbody and plasma membrane. By binding phosphoinositides via its PH domain, it may recruit KLHL12-mediated ubiquitination machinery to regulate the stability of cytokinesis factors or metabolic enzymes (like PKM). Its upregulation in diverse cancers suggests it acts as an oncogenic hub coupling cell division machinery with metabolic reprogramming (Warburg effect) and Wnt/β-catenin signaling. The 19q13 CNVs warrant investigation for dosage-sensitive effects on neurodevelopment.
