## Description
CEP20 encodes a 174 aa centrosomal protein (UniProt Q96NB1), also known as FOPNL, FOR20, or C16orf63, that functions as a critical scaffold component of centriolar satellites and the ciliary basal body, essential for cilium assembly and microtubule organization. The protein features a compact domain architecture dominated by two key structural motifs: an N-terminal FOP (FGFR1 Oncogene Partner) dimerization domain (IPR018993/PF09398, residues 49-113) mediating homo-oligomerization or hetero-oligomerization with partner proteins; and a central LIS1 homology (LisH) motif (IPR006594/PS50896/SM00667, residues 49-81) characteristic of proteins involved in microtubule anchoring, dynein regulation, and lissencephaly-related pathways. The LisH motif overlaps with the FOP domain, suggesting a coupled mechanism where dimerization stabilizes the microtubule-binding interface. Panther classification places CEP20 within the Centrosomal Protein 43 family (PTHR15431), indicating evolutionary conservation of centriolar functions. STRING interactions returned no direct partners for the symbol "CEP20" (likely due to alias usage), but literature confirms functional linkage to PLK1 (Polo-like kinase 1) and CCDC116 in a pathological axis dysregulated by Nε-carboxymethyl-lysine (CML) in hepatocarcinogenesis, and involvement in microtubule depolymerization promoting non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) invasion. HPA expression data is unavailable, suggesting low abundance or technical detection limits, consistent with specialized centrosomal localization. ClinVar lists 228 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains (e.g., 16p13.11 chr16:14875002-16199333)x3, (16p13.11-12.3)x3) and losses/deletions (e.g., 16p13.11 chr16:15449697-16519971)x1), demonstrating extreme dosage sensitivity of the 16p13.11 region; while specific monogenic diseases are not yet defined, these CNVs are associated with neurodevelopmental disorders and cancer predisposition. AlphaFold structure prediction yields high confidence (global pLDDT 73.8), revealing a well-folded helical bundle consistent with the FOP/LisH architecture, supporting stable scaffold function. Mechanistically, CEP20 operates through: (1) Centriolar satellite recruitment—localizes to PCM1-positive satellites acting as dynamic reservoirs for centrosomal components; (2) Cilium biogenesis—essential for basal body docking and axoneme extension, loss causing ciliopathy-like defects; (3) Microtubule regulation—recent 2023 study shows CEP20 promotes NSCLC metastasis by depolymerizing microtubules, increasing cellular motility; (4) PLK1-CCDC116 axis—2025 study identifies CEP20 as a node in CML-driven centrosome amplification, where pathological overexpression leads to multipolar spindles and genomic instability in liver cancer; (5) Dimerization-dependent scaffolding—FOP domain mediates oligomerization essential for satellite integrity. Disruption of CEP20 causes defective cilia, aberrant mitosis, and enhanced tumor invasiveness. With only 6 literature papers, CEP20 remains a genuine dark gene with emerging roles in cancer biology.
