## Description
CEP76 (Centrosomal protein 76, aliases C18orf9, HsT1705) is a 659 amino acid predicted intracellular centrosomal protein (UniProt Q8TAP6) with high-confidence well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 86.9) containing canonical centrosomal domains including CEP76 C2 domain (residues 100-257), CEP76 N-terminal domain (residues 10-69), CEP76 peptidase-like domain (residues 361-491), CEP76 C-terminal domain (residues 518-656), and papain-like cysteine peptidase superfamily (residues 363-412), functioning as a ubiquitously expressed centrosomal protein that regulates centriole amplification by limiting centriole duplication to once per cell cycle and ciliogenesis through interactions with CCP110, CEP290, PLK1, CEP97, PLK4, SASS6, POC5, NEURL4, CEP135, and HAUS2.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals CEP76 contains a multi-domain centrosomal architecture: CEP76 C2 domain (IPR028926/PF15627, residues 100-257) providing calcium-dependent membrane binding capability characteristic of C2 domains, CEP76 N-terminal domain (IPR056289/PF24654, residues 10-69) forming a 60 aa structured module, CEP76 peptidase-like domain (IPR056290/PF24656, residues 361-491) with papain-like cysteine peptidase superfamily features (IPR038765/SSF54001, residues 363-412) suggesting potential proteolytic or protein-binding functions, CEP76 C-terminal domain (IPR056288/PF24652, residues 518-656) forming a 139 aa structured module, and Centrosomal protein of 76 kDa family (IPR052299/PTHR46436, residues 1-659) defining the complete functional unit. The C2 domain spans 158 amino acids (residues 100-257) within the 659 aa protein (~24%), while the peptidase-like domain spans 131 aa (~20%) and C-terminal domain spans 139 aa (~21%). The high-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 86.9) confirms a stable multi-domain architecture optimized for centrosomal localization and protein-protein interactions.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places CEP76 within the centriole duplication and ciliogenesis network with very high-confidence interactions: strongest interaction with CCP110 (0.937, centriolar coiled-coil protein 110, centriole length regulator), followed by CEP290 (0.868, centrosomal protein 290 kDa, ciliogenesis protein), PLK1 (0.846, polo-like kinase 1, cell cycle kinase), CEP97 (0.838, centrosomal protein 97 kDa, centriole cap protein), PLK4 (0.657, polo-like kinase 4, master regulator of centriole duplication), SASS6 (0.648, SAS-6 centriolar assembly protein, cartwheel protein), POC5 (0.640, POC5 centriolar protein), NEURL4 (0.635, neuralized E3 ubiquitin protein ligase 4, centriole biogenesis), CEP135 (0.606, centrosomal protein 135 kDa), and HAUS2 (0.509, HAUS augmin-like complex subunit 2, spindle assembly). The CCP110-CEP97 partnership (0.937/0.838) extremely strongly links CEP76 to the centriole cap complex that suppresses centriole elongation. Subcellular location includes nucleoplasm, plasma membrane, microtubules, mitotic spindle, and primary cilium. Protein class includes cancer-related genes and predicted intracellular proteins. The ubiquitous expression pattern with centrosomal/ciliary localization positions CEP76 to regulate centriole duplication across all dividing cell types.\n\nClinVar documents 102 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including chromosome 18 copy number gains (18p11.32-11.21 chr18:136227-15181209), deletions (NC_000018.10:g.100000_15400000del), missense variants (c.1961A>G p.Lys654Arg in C-terminal domain, c.1432T>C p.Cys478Arg in peptidase-like domain, c.1531T>A p.Phe511Ile, c.793G>A p.Val265Met in C2 domain, c.302T>C p.Ile101Thr in C2 domain, c.71T>A p.Val24Asp in N-terminal domain), and in-frame deletions (c.868TGG[1] p.Trp291del). The variant burden at 18p11.21 indicates dosage sensitivity, where haploinsufficiency or overexpression causes defective centriole duplication licensing, centriole amplification, disrupted ciliogenesis, and potential ciliopathy or cancer phenotypes.\n\nConservation is strong across vertebrates: human (Q8TAP6, 659 aa), mouse (Q0VEJ0, 659 aa), zebrafish (A7E2V1, 662 aa), Xenopus (Q6DDX8, 661 aa), indicating evolutionary conservation of centrosomal functions.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: CEP76 functions as a ubiquitously expressed centrosomal protein that regulates centriole duplication licensing and ciliogenesis through multi-domain protein interactions. The C2 domain (residues 100-257) enables calcium-dependent membrane binding for centrosomal/ciliary membrane association. The peptidase-like domain (residues 361-491) with papain-like cysteine peptidase features may provide protein-binding or regulatory proteolytic activity. Ubiquitous expression enables CEP76 function in all dividing cells. 18p11.21 variants cause CEP76 dysfunction, leading to centriole amplification, defective ciliogenesis, genomic instability, and ciliopathy/cancer phenotypes. CEP76 represents a critical centrosomal protein linking C2 domain-peptidase-like architecture to centriole duplication licensing biology.
