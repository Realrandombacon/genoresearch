## Description
HARS1 (Histidyl-tRNA Synthetase 1) is a 509 aa cytosolic enzyme essential for protein translation, catalyzing the ATP-dependent ligation of histidine to its cognate tRNA(His). Despite 14 literature references, HARS1 represents a 'partially characterized' dark gene where the molecular mechanisms linking enzymatic dysfunction to specific neuropathies (Charcot-Marie-Tooth type 2W, Usher syndrome, multisystem ataxia) remain incompletely understood. InterPro analysis reveals a canonical class II aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase architecture: an N-terminal WHEP-TRS domain (IPR000738, aa 3-60) unique to metazoan ARSs and implicated in protein-protein interactions and inflammatory signaling, a central catalytic HisRS-like core (cd00773, aa 69-394) containing the class II signature motifs for ATP binding and histidine activation, and a C-terminal anticodon-binding domain (IPR004154, aa 410-500) that recognizes tRNA(His) specificity determinants. AlphaFold predicts a very high-confidence structure (pLDDT 91.8), reflecting a tightly folded enzymatic core consistent with strong evolutionary constraint. HPA confirms ubiquitous expression across all tissues with cytosolic localization, classifying it as an essential housekeeping enzyme. STRING interactions place HARS1 in a tight network with other cytoplasmic aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases: GARS1 (0.969), HARS2 (0.956, mitochondrial paralog), YARS1 (0.898), AARS1 (0.881), and SARS1 (0.840), suggesting potential multi-synthetase complex (MSC) association. ClinVar lists 103 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including missense mutations (p.Thr164Ala, p.Leu56Phe, p.Asp364His), frameshifts (p.Ser245fs), and splice variants, associated with Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease type 2W (dominant peripheral neuropathy), Usher syndrome (recessive deafness-blindness), and a novel multisystem ataxic syndrome (recessive). Recent literature (2020-2022) reveals that pathogenic HARS1 mutations trigger the integrated stress response (ISR) via eIF2α phosphorylation, and that valproic acid can ameliorate neuronal differentiation defects through JNK pathway modulation, indicating non-canonical signaling roles beyond translation.
