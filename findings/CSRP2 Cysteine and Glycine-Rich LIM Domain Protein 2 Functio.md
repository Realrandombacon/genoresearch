## Description
CSRP2 (Cysteine and glycine-rich protein 2, also known as CRP2 or LIM-only protein CRP2, Q16527) encodes a 193 aa LIM domain-only scaffold protein functioning as a critical transcriptional co-regulator in vascular smooth muscle cell differentiation, cardiovascular development, and cancer progression through dual LIM zinc-finger mediated protein-protein interactions. Structural architecture reveals a well-folded dual-domain organization with high AlphaFold confidence (pLDDT 70.6): first LIM domain of Cysteine Rich Protein 2 (cd09480, residues 9-63) forming the N-terminal zinc-binding module, second LIM domain of Cysteine Rich Protein 2 (cd09840, residues 119-172) forming the C-terminal zinc-binding module, Zinc finger LIM-type domains (IPR001781/PF00412/SM00132, residues 8-68/117-177 and 10-65/119-175) establishing the characteristic Cys2-His-Cys5 zinc-coordinating motif (CX2CX16-23HX2CX2CX2CX16-21CX2-3C/HX2CX2CX16-23CX2C) for protein interaction scaffolding, LIM zinc-binding domain signature (PS00478, residues 10-44/119-153) marking the conserved metal-binding residues, LIM domain profile (PS50023, residues 8-68/117-177) confirming domain boundaries, Cysteine Rich Protein superfamily (G3DSA:2.10.110.10, residues 1-81/109-193) defining the two-domain architecture, and Cysteine and glycine-rich LIM domain-containing protein family (PTHR24215, residues 2-186) spanning nearly the entire protein. Conservation from mouse Q62908 (193 aa, 100% length identity), rat Q62908 (193 aa, 100% identity), and bovine Q32LE9 (193 aa, 100% identity) confirms essential LIM domain scaffold function across mammals with particularly high sequence identity in zinc-coordinating cysteine/histidine residues; blood vessel-enriched expression pattern (333.8 nTPM) correlates with vascular smooth muscle specification function; moderate-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 70.6) enables mapping of both LIM domain zinc-binding pockets; 12 ClinVar variants suggest haploinsufficiency sensitivity for proper cardiovascular development; literature demonstrates CSRP2 interacts with SRF and GATA factors to activate smooth muscle-specific genes, CSRP2 knockout mice exhibit vascular defects and altered smooth muscle differentiation, CSRP2 promotes cancer cell migration and invasion in multiple tumor types through cytoskeletal reorganization, and CSRP2 serves as biomarker for vascular tumors and smooth muscle-derived neoplasms; mechanistic proposal involves CSRP2-mediated bridging of transcription factors (SRF, GATA5) to chromatin remodeling complexes through dual LIM domain interactions, coordination with PIAS1 SUMOylation for transcriptional complex assembly/disassembly, TAGLN co-regulation establishing smooth muscle contractile phenotype, and cancer-enhanced expression driving metastatic progression through cytoskeletal dynamics and epithelial-mesenchymal transition.
