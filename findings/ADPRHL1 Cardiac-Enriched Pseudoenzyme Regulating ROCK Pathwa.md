## Description
ADPRHL1 encodes a 354 aa inactive ADP-ribosyltransferase (UniProt Q8NDY3), a pseudoenzyme that has lost catalytic activity but retains regulatory function through protein-protein interactions and structural scaffolding. InterPro analysis reveals: (1) ADP-ribosylation/Crystallin J1 family (IPR005502, aa 6-327); (2) ADP-ribosylarginine hydrolase family (IPR012108, aa 1-354) with GO terms for magnesium ion binding and protein de-ADP-ribosylation; (3) ADP-ribosylglycohydrolase domain (PF03747, aa 6-327); (4) Panther family PTHR16222 (aa 1-353). Despite domain architecture suggesting enzymatic function, ADPRHL1 is catalytically inactive—the ancestral catalytic site is disrupted. AlphaFold predicts an exceptionally high-confidence structure (pLDDT 93.8), indicating a stable, well-folded protein optimized for scaffolding rather than catalysis. STRING interactions reveal connections to: GRTP1 (0.737, growth factor receptor-bound protein), DCUN1D2 (0.633, cullin-associated NEDD8-transfer protein), TMCO3 (0.546), LSMEM2 (0.502), and CUL3/CUL1 (0.402, cullin-RING E3 ubiquitin ligase components), suggesting roles in protein ubiquitination and degradation pathways. HPA shows group-enriched expression with exceptional levels in heart muscle (274.1 nTPM), skeletal muscle (246.1 nTPM), and tongue (213.8 nTPM), with subcellular localization to nucleoli and plasma membrane. ClinVar reports 74 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly 13q31.3-34 copy number losses/gains, though specific disease associations remain under investigation. Literature (21 papers) reveals: (1) ADPRHL1 affects cardiac function by regulating ROCK pathway (2023, 2 citations)—knockout causes defective heart chamber growth and myofibrillogenesis; (2) Recurrent germline mutation activates PARP1 and confers prostate cancer risk in African American families (2022, 5 citations); (3) 104-bp structural variation associated with growth traits in chickens (2021, 5 citations); (4) Xenopus laevis studies show ancestral catalytic site disruption causes cardiac developmental defects (2020, 13 citations). Mechanistically, ADPRHL1 functions through: (1) Structural scaffolding—maintaining protein fold despite catalytic inactivation to serve as interaction hub; (2) ROCK pathway regulation—modulating Rho-associated kinase signaling critical for cardiac myofibril assembly; (3) PARP1 activation—germline mutations increase PARP1 activity, linking ADP-ribosylation signaling to prostate cancer susceptibility; (4) Potential ubiquitination coordination via CUL1/CUL3 interactions. In disease, ADPRHL1 dysfunction contributes to: congenital heart defects via disrupted myofibrillogenesis; prostate cancer predisposition (especially in African American populations) through PARP1 hyperactivation; potential growth disorders. The pseudoenzyme status makes ADPRHL1 a unique therapeutic target—unlike enzymes, it cannot be inhibited catalytically but may be targeted through protein-protein interaction disruption or expression modulation.
