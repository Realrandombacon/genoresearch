## Description
UPK2 (Uroplakin-2) is a 184-amino acid single-pass transmembrane protein with 62 publications, representing a moderately characterized urothelial differentiation marker. The protein contains a conserved Uroplakin II domain (cd09967, positions 23-176) and belongs to the uroplakin-2 family (IPR009952, PF07353, PIRSF016439, PTHR17573) that forms heterotetrameric complexes with other uroplakins. AlphaFold predicts a well-folded structure (pLDDT 76.8), consistent with stable transmembrane protein architecture. HPA reveals extreme tissue-specific expression with urinary bladder enrichment (61.6 nTPM), confirming urothelial specificity, plus cancer-enriched expression pattern useful for tumor identification. ClinVar documents 29 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants (primarily chromosome 11q23.3-24.2/25 copy number gains, deletions, and duplications), though specific disease phenotypes beyond urothelial carcinoma remain uncharacterized. STRING interactions show UPK2 functions within the uroplakin complex with very high confidence connections to UPK1B (0.999), UPK1A (0.999), UPK3B (0.943), and UPK3A (0.447), plus interactions with urothelial differentiation markers GATA3 (0.572), KRT20 (0.537), KRT7 (0.521), KRT5 (0.423), and uncharacterized protein C17orf97 (0.585). Recent studies (2020-2025) established UPK2 as a diagnostic biomarker for urothelial carcinoma via RNAscope and immunohistochemistry, identified UPK2 expression in aggressive colorectal cancers with distinct molecular features, and demonstrated potential for cell-free UPK2 RNA as a blood biomarker for lung adenocarcinoma recurrence monitoring. We hypothesize UPK2 functions as a structural component of the urothelial plaque that heterodimerizes with UPK1A/UPK1B and tetramerizes with UPK3A/UPK3B to form the asymmetric unit membrane (AUM), creating an impermeable barrier protecting underlying tissues from urine toxicity. The GATA3 interaction suggests transcriptional co-regulation during urothelial differentiation, while aberrant UPK2 expression in colorectal and lung cancers indicates potential oncogenic reprogramming of urothelial differentiation programs in non-urothelial tumors—a mechanism that may serve as both diagnostic marker and therapeutic target.
