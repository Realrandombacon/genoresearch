# LMAN2: Golgi-Resident L-Type Lectin VIP36 Mediating ER-Golgi Glycoprotein Cargo Sorting, Quality Control, and Kv1.2 Channel Trafficking in HER2-Positive Breast Cancer and Septic Shock

## Description
LMAN2 (Lectin Mannose-Binding 2, also known as VIP36 - Vesicular Integral-membrane Protein 36, UniProt Q12907) is a 356 aa type I transmembrane L-type lectin that functions in ER-Golgi glycoprotein cargo sorting, quality control, and ion channel trafficking. The molecular architecture consists of an N-terminal cytoplasmic tail, single transmembrane domain, and large C-terminal carbohydrate recognition domain (CRD) belonging to the legume-like lectin family (IPR005052/PF03388/PS51328, positions 52-277) within the concanavalin A-like lectin superfamily (IPR013320/SSF49899/G3DSA:2.60.120.200, positions 53-276). The VIP36-specific lectin domain (IPR035664/cd06901, positions 53-301) mediates high-affinity mannose binding for glycoprotein cargo recognition. Literature (50 papers) establishes LMAN2 roles in: (1) serum LMAN2 as prognostic biomarker in septic shock patients (2025, 2 citations), (2) LMAN2-HEATR3 interaction expediting HER2-positive breast cancer advancement through Akt/ERK/NF-κB signaling (2025, 1 citation), (3) LMAN2 regulating Kv1.2 potassium channel redox-sensitive gating in neuronal excitability (2024, 3 citations), (4) LMAN2 expression profiles in breast cancer under hypoxia/normoxia conditions (2025). HPA confirms ubiquitous expression across all tissues with low tissue specificity, Golgi apparatus localization, and classification as transporter/protein transport protein, consistent with ER-Golgi trafficking function. STRING interactions reveal glycoprotein trafficking network: MCFD2 (0.879) - multiple coagulation factor deficiency protein 2, cargo receptor partner forming LMAN2-MCFD2 complex; SEC13 (0.686) - COPII vesicle coat component; SEC22A (0.678) - SNARE protein for ER-Golgi fusion; MOGS (0.663) - mannosyl-oligosaccharide glucosidase for N-glycan processing; PREB (0.573) - prolactin regulatory element-binding protein in ER-Golgi transport; TMED10 (0.552) - transmembrane emp24 domain-containing protein 10; CTSZ (0.528) - cathepsin Z lysosomal protease; TMED2 (0.517) - transmembrane emp24 domain-containing protein 2; MLEC (0.449) - malectin ER lectin; LMAN1 (0.449) - lectin mannose-binding 1 (ERGIC-53), paralog cargo receptor. ClinVar contains 62 pathogenic variants including copy number losses at 5q35.2-35.3 (chr5:175416095-177250474), gains, deletions, duplications, and SNVs (c.197-6C>G, c.788C>T p.Ser263Phe), though specific monogenic disease phenotypes remain under investigation. AlphaFold shows high-confidence structure (pLDDT 84.1) indicating well-folded lectin domain with stable β-sandwich CRD typical of functional L-type lectins. Conservation: mouse Lman2 (Q9DBH5) is 358 aa, canine (P49256) is 356 aa - highly conserved across mammals. Functional hypothesis: LMAN2 functions as a pH-dependent mannose-binding cargo receptor that cycles between ER and Golgi to sort newly synthesized glycoproteins for forward transport or retrieval. The mechanistic model proposes: (1) LMAN2 binds high-mannose N-glycans on properly folded glycoprotein cargo in ER (pH 7.2) via CRD carbohydrate-binding pocket, (2) COPII vesicle incorporation via cytoplasmic tail interactions with SEC13/SEC22A mediates ER exit, (3) acidic Golgi environment (pH 6.0-6.5) triggers cargo release through protonation of CRD histidine residues, (4) LMAN2-MCFD2 complex specifically recognizes coagulation factors V and VIII, explaining bleeding diathesis in LMAN2 deficiency, (5) LMAN2 regulates Kv1.2 channel surface expression by controlling glycosylation-dependent trafficking, modulating neuronal excitability. In HER2+ breast cancer, LMAN2-HEATR3 interaction activates Akt/ERK/NF-κB signaling promoting tumor progression. In septic shock, elevated serum LMAN2 reflects ER stress and glycoprotein secretion, serving as prognostic biomarker. Pathogenic CNVs at 5q35 disrupt LMAN2 dosage, potentially causing coagulation factor deficiency and glycoprotein trafficking defects. LMAN2 represents an understudied lectin cargo receptor linking ER quality control to cancer signaling and neurological function.
