## Description
TMEM39B (Transmembrane Protein 39B) is a multi-pass membrane protein of 463 amino acids (UniProt Q9GZU3) localized primarily to the Endoplasmic Reticulum. InterPro analysis reveals a conserved DUF4570 domain (IPR025522) characteristic of this family, suggesting a role in ion transport or membrane scaffolding. AlphaFold prediction (pLDDT ~85) indicates a stable helical bundle structure consistent with channel-like topology. Tissue expression (HPA) is ubiquitous but elevated in kidney (nTPM ~45) and testis (nTPM ~38), implying a housekeeping role with specific reproductive/renal requirements. STRING interactions link TMEM39B to ER calcium handling machinery, including strong associations with SERCA2 (ATP2A2, score 0.89) and STIM1 (score 0.75), the master sensor of ER calcium stores. ClinVar data reports 10 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains/losses on chromosome 1p, hinting at dosage sensitivity. Conservation is moderate (~70% identity with mouse). \n\nHYPOTHESIS: TMEM39B functions as a regulatory subunit or auxiliary pore for ER calcium leak channels, fine-tuning basal ER calcium levels to prevent premature activation of Store-Operated Calcium Entry (SOCE). By modulating the threshold for STIM1 oligomerization, TMEM39B ensures cellular calcium homeostasis. Dysregulation via CNVs leads to aberrant calcium signaling, potentially contributing to developmental defects or renal dysfunction observed in 1p deletion/duplication syndromes.
