# FERMT3: Kindlin-3 Integrin Co-Activator with FERM and PH Domains Essential for Hematopoietic Cell Adhesion, Immune Cell Trafficking, and Platelet Aggregation Through Talin-Independent Integrin β Cytoplasmic Tail Binding

## Description
FERMT3 (Fermitin Family Homolog 3, also known as Kindlin-3 or UNC112, Q86UX7) encodes a 667 aa intracellular focal adhesion protein functioning as a critical co-activator of integrin signaling that binds directly to integrin β cytoplasmic tails to induce conformational changes enabling high-affinity ligand binding essential for leukocyte adhesion, platelet aggregation, and immune cell trafficking. Structural architecture reveals modular domain organization characteristic of the kindlin family: N-terminal FERM F0 subdomain (cd17182, residues 15-97) mediating protein-protein interactions potentially with phosphoinositides or signaling partners, FERM F1 subdomain (cd17185, residues 98-254) providing structural scaffold, central FERM domain (IPR019748/IPR019749, residues 263-557/94-558) containing the primary integrin-binding interface, pleckstrin homology PH domain (IPR001849/G3DSA:2.30.29.30/cd01237, residues 350-459/350-487) binding phosphatidylinositol lipids for membrane recruitment, and C-terminal FERM F3 lobe (cd13205, residues 552-643) completing the integrin recognition surface; AlphaFold prediction (pLDDT 82.9, high confidence) confirms well-folded multi-domain architecture consistent with stable scaffold protein requiring precise domain positioning for simultaneous membrane anchoring and integrin engagement. STRING interactions define the complete integrin activation machinery: integrin β3 ITGB3 (0.992, highest confidence) and integrin β2 ITGB2 (0.960) as primary binding partners mediating platelet and leukocyte adhesion respectively, talin TLN1 (0.940)/TLN2 (0.817) as parallel integrin activators working synergistically with FERMT3, hematopoietic adaptor APBB1IP (0.955, also known as ICAP1β) linking to Rap1 signaling pathway controlling integrin affinity, actin regulator FBLIM1 (0.928) connecting to cytoskeletal remodeling during adhesion, guanine nucleotide exchange factor RASGRP2 (0.907, also known as CalDAG-GEFI) activating Rap1 upstream of FERMT3 recruitment, pleckstrin PLEK (0.602) as PKC substrate in platelet activation, integrin α2b ITGA2B (0.517) forming αIIbβ3 heterodimer with ITGB3, and paralog FERMT1 (0.413) sharing structural homology but distinct tissue distribution. HPA localizes FERMT3 to vesicles as predicted intracellular protein involved in cell adhesion with tissue-enhanced expression showing strong hematopoietic restriction: lymphoid tissue (125.3 nTPM, highest), bone marrow (95.6 nTPM), detected in many tissues but absent from non-hematopoietic lineages, group-enriched single cell type specificity reflecting exclusive role in blood cells, low cancer specificity indicating housekeeping adhesion function, classified as disease-related and essential protein with plasma protein detection. ClinVar documents 45 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including truncating mutations causing complete loss-of-function: nonsense variant c.1426C>T/p.Gln476Ter eliminating C-terminal integrin-binding region, insertion c.1295_1296insACTGCGGTGCCAGGATGTGAGTGAGATCTA/p.Tyr432Ter creating premature stop codon within PH domain disrupting membrane targeting, frameshift deletions c.224del/p.Gln75fs and c.1462del/p.His488fs abolishing protein stability, splice variants c.1204+1G>T and c.787-14_788del disrupting exon-intron boundaries, missense variant c.334G>A/p.Arg134Gln in F1 subdomain potentially impairing structural integrity, and promoter variant c.-14-9T>A reducing expression; these mutations cause leukocyte adhesion deficiency type III (LAD-III), a severe autosomal recessive immunodeficiency characterized by recurrent bacterial infections, bleeding diathesis due to platelet dysfunction, and osteopetrosis from impaired osteoclast adhesion. Literature reveals disease mechanisms: FERMT3 operates downstream of Rap1-RASGRP2 signaling where activated Rap1 recruits FERMT3 to plasma membrane via PH domain-phosphoinositide interaction, FERMT3 then binds integrin β cytoplasmic tails at NPxY motifs distinct from talin-binding sites enabling cooperative integrin activation, in platelets FERMT3-ITGB3 interaction is essential for fibrinogen binding and thrombus formation explaining bleeding phenotype in LAD-III patients, in leukocytes FERMT3-ITGB2 interaction enables firm adhesion to endothelial ICAM-1/VCAM-1 permitting extravasation to infection sites, high lymphoid tissue expression (125.3 nTPM) reflects constitutive requirement for immune surveillance, vesicular localization suggests regulated trafficking between intracellular stores and plasma membrane during activation, high AlphaFold confidence (82.9 pLDDT) indicates rigid domain arrangement optimized for mechanical force transmission during adhesion, conservation from mouse (Q8K1B8, 665 aa) to bovine (Q32LP0, 665 aa) underscores essential vertebrate-specific adaptation for adaptive immunity.
