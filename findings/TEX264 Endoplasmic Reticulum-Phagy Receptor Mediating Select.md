## Description
TEX264 (Testis-expressed protein 264) encodes a 313 amino acid endoplasmic reticulum-resident autophagy receptor (UniProt Q9Y6I9) that mediates selective autophagy of ER subdomains (ER-phagy/reticulophagy) and DNA lesions. InterPro analysis reveals a reticulophagy receptor domain (PTHR15949, residues 1-279) containing a regulatory factor effector-binding domain superfamily (IPR011256, G3DSA:3.20.80.10, residues 42-193) and probable bacterial effector-binding domain fold (SSF55136, residues 45-154). This domain architecture is characteristic of ER-phagy receptors that recognize and deliver ER membranes to autophagosomes via LC3/GABARAP interaction.\n\nAlphaFold prediction shows high confidence (pLDDT 75.1) across all 313 aa, indicating a well-folded stable structure optimized for protein-protein interactions with autophagy machinery and ER membrane association.\n\nSTRING interactions reveal TEX264 operates within the ER-phagy and autophagosome formation network with high confidence scores: CCPG1 (0.815) is a well-characterized ER-phagy receptor that shares functional redundancy with TEX264; GABARAP (0.726) and GABARAPL2 (0.691) are ATG8 family proteins that bind LC3-interacting regions (LIRs) on autophagy receptors to tether cargo to autophagosomes; TMEM79 (0.681) is an ER membrane protein involved in ER stress response; CALCOCO1 (0.674) is an ER-phagy receptor mediating nuclear envelope autophagy; ATL3 (0.563) is atlastin-3, an ER membrane fusion GTPase; SEC62 (0.549) is a component of the ER translocon involved in ER-phagy; RETREG1 (0.548) is FAM134B, the first discovered ER-phagy receptor; RTN3 (0.495) is reticulon-3, an ER-shaping protein and ER-phagy receptor. This interaction network positions TEX264 as a core component of the ER-phagy machinery alongside CCPG1, FAM134B, and RTN3.\n\nHPA expression shows TEX264 is ubiquitously detected in all tissues with low tissue specificity, consistent with essential housekeeping functions in ER homeostasis. Subcellular localization is endoplasmic reticulum. Biological processes include autophagy, DNA damage, and DNA repair. TEX264 is classified as a plasma protein and predicted intracellular protein with ER-phagy receptor function.\n\nClinVar reports 7 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly 3p21.31-14.3 copy number losses and 3p26.3-q29 copy number gains. While no specific monogenic disease is definitively assigned, the 3p locus overlaps with developmental disorder regions. The CNV sensitivity suggests TEX264 dosage affects ER homeostasis and genome stability.\n\nLiterature (57 papers) reveals emerging dual roles in ER quality control and DNA repair: The landmark 2024 study (34 citations) demonstrated that TEX264 drives selective autophagy of DNA lesions to promote DNA repair and cell survival — a novel mechanism linking autophagy directly to genome maintenance. TEX264 recognizes DNA damage sites and recruits autophagy machinery to clear damaged DNA. The 2025 study showed TEX264 drives epidermal differentiation and is dysregulated in Darier disease (a keratinization disorder caused by ATP2A2 mutations affecting ER calcium). TEX264-mediated ER-phagy is activated during keratinocyte cornification to degrade ER membranes as cells terminally differentiate. Abstract P11 (2025) confirmed TEX264-mediated selective autophagy directs DNA damage repair.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: TEX264 functions as a dual-specificity autophagy receptor that maintains cellular homeostasis through two distinct mechanisms: (1) ER-phagy: TEX264 recognizes stressed or damaged ER subdomains via its reticulophagy receptor domain, binds GABARAP/GABARAPL2 through a LIR motif, and delivers ER membranes to autophagosomes for lysosomal degradation. This clears misfolded proteins, resolves ER stress, and remodels ER architecture during differentiation. The CCPG1 interaction suggests functional redundancy — both receptors may target distinct ER subdomains or respond to different stress signals. (2) DNA lesion autophagy: TEX264 translocates to DNA damage sites (potentially via ER-nuclear envelope contacts) and recruits autophagy machinery to clear damaged DNA fragments, promoting repair and preventing mutagenesis. This explains the DNA damage/repair HPA annotation and 2024 findings. In epidermal differentiation, TEX264-mediated ER-phagy is essential for cornification — as keratinocytes terminally differentiate, ER membranes must be degraded to form the cornified envelope. Darier disease dysregulation suggests TEX264 dysfunction contributes to keratinization defects. The ubiquitous expression pattern indicates TEX264 is essential for ER homeostasis in all cell types. Therapeutic targeting of TEX264 could enhance DNA repair in cancer (sensitizing tumors to genotoxic therapy) or restore ER homeostasis in protein misfolding diseases.
