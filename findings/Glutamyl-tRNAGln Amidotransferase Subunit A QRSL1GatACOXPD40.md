## Description
QRSL1 (UniProt Q9H0R6), also known as GatA or COXPD40, encodes a 528 aa mitochondrial enzyme functioning as the catalytic A subunit of the heterotrimeric glutamyl-tRNA(Gln) amidotransferase (GatCAB) complex that performs indirect charging of mitochondrial tRNA(Gln) through a two-step transamidation pathway essential for mitochondrial protein synthesis. InterPro analysis reveals conserved amidase signature architecture: Amidase signature domain (IPR000120/IPR023631/IPR036928/PF01425/G3DSA:3.90.1300.10/SSF75304, residues 5-502/25-488/3-502/4-500) forming the catalytic core with serine-cis-serine-lysine catalytic triad characteristic of AS enzymes; Glutamyl-tRNA(Gln) amidotransferase A subunit family (IPR004412/MF_00120/TIGR00132/PTHR11895, residues 9-499/11-499/5-502) conferring glutaminyl-tRNA synthase glutamine-hydrolyzing activity (GO:0050567), translation (GO:0006412), and glutamyl-tRNA(Gln) amidotransferase complex membership (GO:0030956); and catalytic activity (GO:0003824) for amide bond hydrolysis. AlphaFold predicts very high confidence structure (pLDDT 93.5) indicating an exceptionally well-folded enzyme with stable catalytic domain suitable for precise tRNA recognition and amidotransfer chemistry. STRING interactions define ultra-high-confidence mitochondrial translation network: GATC (0.999, amidotransferase subunit C, obligate heterotrimer partner), GATB (0.958, amidotransferase subunit B, second obligate partner forming complete GatCAB complex), DARS2 (0.935, mitochondrial aspartyl-tRNA synthetase), EARS2 (0.908, mitochondrial glutamyl-tRNA synthetase), LDB1 (0.800, LIM domain binding protein 1), NARS2 (0.749, mitochondrial asparaginyl-tRNA synthetase), QARS1 (0.700, cytoplasmic glutaminyl-tRNA synthetase), DARS1 (0.525, cytoplasmic aspartyl-tRNA synthetase), EPRS1 (0.523, bifunctional glutamyl-prolyl-tRNA synthetase), and NARS1 (0.447, cytoplasmic asparaginyl-tRNA synthetase). The perfect GATC score and strong GATB linkage confirm QRSL1 functions exclusively as part of heterotrimeric GatCAB complex (QRSL1-GATB-GATC) in mitochondria. HPA classifies QRSL1 as disease-related gene/enzyme/potential drug target/predicted intracellular protein with ubiquitous expression (detected in all tissues, low tissue specificity), localized to vesicles/centrosome (note: mitochondrial targeting may be context-dependent or HPA annotation incomplete), functioning in protein biosynthesis as ligase, with documented involvement in primary mitochondrial disease and disease variants. ClinVar lists 35 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including frameshift deletions causing premature termination (c.742del p.Ala248fs, c.45del p.Gly16fs, c.498del p.Leu166_Ile167insTer, c.24del p.Val9fs, c.600del p.Ala201fs), nonsense mutations truncating catalytic domain (c.893G>A p.Trp298Ter, c.1495C>T p.Gln499Ter), splice site mutations disrupting mRNA processing (c.1366+2T>G, c.557+1G>A), and large copy number loss (6q15-21 chr6:92468126-109410569x1); these cause autosomal recessive combined oxidative phosphorylation deficiency 40 (COXPD40) featuring lactic acidosis, hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, encephalopathy, developmental delay, and early mortality due to defective mitochondrial translation and respiratory chain assembly. Functional hypothesis: QRSL1 operates through integrated transamidation mechanisms: (1) Indirect charging pathway - Unlike most organisms where GlnRS directly charges tRNA(Gln) with glutamine, mitochondria lack canonical GlnRS; instead QRSL1-containing GatCAB complex performs two-step reaction: first EARS2 charges tRNA(Gln) with glutamate forming misacylated Glu-tRNA(Gln), then QRSL1-GATB-GATC transamidates Glu-tRNA(Gln) to correct Gln-tRNA(Gln) using glutamine as amide donor; (2) Catalytic mechanism - Amidase signature domain contains nucleophilic elbow with Ser-Ser-Lys catalytic triad hydrolyzing glutamine to release ammonia for transamidation; (3) Complex assembly - Perfect GATC interaction indicates 1:1:1 stoichiometry (QRSL1:GATB:GATC) required for activity; monomeric QRSL1 is non-functional; (4) Mitochondrial translation fidelity - Ensures accurate decoding of CAA/CAG glutamine codons in 13 mitochondrially-encoded OXPHOS subunits (MT-CO1/2/3, MT-ND1-6, MT-ATP6/8, MT-CYB); (5) Disease mechanism - Loss-of-function mutations abolish transamidation causing: accumulation of misacylated Glu-tRNA(Gln), mistranslation of mitochondrial proteins, defective OXPHOS complex assembly (I, III, IV, V), reduced ATP production, lactic acidosis from anaerobic metabolism, cardiomyopathy from energy deficit in heart muscle, encephalopathy from neuronal energy failure; frameshift/nonsense mutations cause complete loss-of-function (severe neonatal phenotype); missense mutations in catalytic domain may cause partial activity (milder later-onset); (6) Conservation - Mouse Q9CZN8 (525 aa), Rat Q5FWT5 (525 aa), Bovine Q29RP9 (526 aa), Xenopus Q0VFI5 (524 aa) indicate essential conserved function across vertebrates. Dark aspects requiring investigation include: precise structural mechanism of tRNA(Gln) recognition and discrimination from other tRNAs, contribution of vesicular/centrosomal localization (HPA) vs. canonical mitochondrial localization, tissue-specific thresholds for QRSL1 activity (why heart and brain most vulnerable), therapeutic potential of glutamine supplementation or transamidation pathway enhancement, whether heterozygous carriers have subtle phenotypes (exercise intolerance, late-onset cardiomyopathy), crosstalk between mitochondrial and cytoplasmic tRNA charging pathways, regulation by post-translational modifications (acetylation, phosphorylation), interaction with mitochondrial ribosome during translation, potential synthetic lethality with EARS2 or other mitochondrial aaRSs in cancer, contribution to neurodegeneration beyond COXPD40, and whether QRSL1 has non-canonical functions independent of GatCAB complex.
