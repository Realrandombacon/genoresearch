# PRELID3B: Mitochondrial Phospholipid Transfer Protein Complexed with TRIAP1 Regulating Cardiolipin Homeostasis and Cellular Energetics

## Description
PRELID3B (PRELI Domain Containing Protein 3B, alias SLMO2) is a 194-amino acid mitochondrial intermembrane space protein that functions as a phospholipid transfer protein essential for cardiolipin and phosphatidylethanolamine homeostasis. With only 6 publications, it represents a genuine 'dark gene' despite having a solved crystal structure and clear clinical variant data. The protein contains a canonical PRELI/MSF1 domain (IPR006797, residues 1-172) within the Slowmo/Ups family (IPR037365, PF04707), which forms a hydrophobic pocket for lipid binding and transfer across the mitochondrial intermembrane space. AlphaFold predicts a highly stable, well-folded structure (global pLDDT 82.1), consistent with the 2019 X-ray crystal structure of the human PRELID3B-TRIAP1 complex that revealed the molecular basis for lipid specificity.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis confirms an obligate heterocomplex with TRIAP1 (TP53-regulated inhibitor of apoptosis 1, score 0.985), which stabilizes PRELID3B and prevents its degradation. Additional partners include ATP5F1E (ATP synthase subunit epsilon, 0.647), linking lipid transfer to oxidative phosphorylation efficiency, and PRELID1 (0.583), a paralog with overlapping function. STARD7 (0.489), another lipid transfer protein, suggests functional redundancy or coordination in mitochondrial lipid trafficking. Human Protein Atlas data shows ubiquitous expression ('detected in all') with low tissue specificity and nucleoplasm localization, though InterPro GO annotations place it in the mitochondrial intermembrane space (GO:0005758)—this discrepancy may reflect dual localization or antibody cross-reactivity.\n\nClinVar reports 19 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, exclusively large copy number gains and losses spanning chromosome 20q13 (the gene's locus), though no specific Mendelian disease is yet assigned. The dosage sensitivity implied by these CNVs, combined with the essential protein classification in HPA, suggests PRELID3B deficiency may cause embryonic lethality or severe metabolic disease. Recent literature links PRELID3B to mitochondrial energy metabolism, with phosphatidylethanolamine synthesis affecting ATP production efficiency. We hypothesize that PRELID3B-TRIAP1 complexes shuttle phosphatidic acid or phosphatidylethanolamine from the outer to inner mitochondrial membrane for cardiolipin synthesis, and that dosage imbalance disrupts cristae architecture, impairing respiratory chain supercomplex assembly and predisposing to metabolic or neurodegenerative disease.
