## Description
AP1M1 encodes the medium mu-1 chain (423 amino acids, UniProt Q9BXS5) of the heterotetrameric AP-1 clathrin adaptor complex, a critical regulator of vesicle-mediated protein trafficking between the trans-Golgi network and endosomes. The protein contains two major structural domains: an N-terminal Longin-like domain (aa 4-142, IPR011012) that mediates complex assembly, and a C-terminal mu homology domain (aa 157-422, IPR028565) responsible for cargo recognition via tyrosine-based sorting signals. AlphaFold prediction shows exceptional structural confidence (pLDDT 94.2), indicating a well-folded, stable protein architecture. STRING interaction network reveals AP1M1 functions within a tight complex with AP1S2 (score 0.999), AP1G1 (0.928), AP1B1 (0.819), and AP1S1 (0.650), while also connecting to ARF1 (0.976) for membrane recruitment and AP2B1 (0.968) for coordination with plasma membrane endocytosis. HPA data shows ubiquitous expression across all tissues with vesicular and cytosolic localization, consistent with housekeeping trafficking functions. ClinVar contains 12 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly copy number losses and gains on chromosome 19p13.11, suggesting dosage sensitivity. The gene's essential role in Golgi-endosome trafficking makes it critical for receptor sorting, lysosome biogenesis, and polarized protein delivery. Disruption likely causes multi-system trafficking defects affecting neuronal development, immune function, and epithelial polarity.
