## Description
LOC129390562 is a 200bp cis-regulatory element (CRE) located on chromosome 12q24.13 (GRCh38: 113601770-113601970), functionally validated as a transcriptional silencer through STARR-seq massively parallel reporter assays (MPRAs) in K562 erythroleukemia cells. This element lacks canonical promoter, enhancer, or CTCF-bound insulator chromatin signatures but demonstrates robust repressive activity against the super core promoter SCP1. As a non-coding regulatory locus, it has no UniProt accession, protein domains, or STRING interaction partners. Human Protein Atlas could not resolve this locus to an Ensembl ID, consistent with its non-coding regulatory classification.\n\nClinVar analysis reveals 2 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variants disrupting this region—a balanced burden with both deletion and duplication events (1:1 ratio). The deletion spans 12q24.13-24.21 (~1.3Mb) while the duplication spans 12p13.33-q24.33 (entire chromosome 12, ~133Mb). These recurrent CNVs are associated with 12q deletion/duplication syndromes featuring developmental delay, intellectual disability, craniofacial dysmorphism, cardiac defects, and skeletal anomalies. The 12q24 region is gene-dense, containing critical dosage-sensitive genes such as TBX3 (T-Box Transcription Factor 3, mutations cause ulnar-mammary syndrome), ACTN3 (alpha-actinin-3), and multiple metabolic enzymes.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: LOC129390562 functions as a critical distal silencer within the 12q24 topologically associating domain (TAD), regulating expression of dosage-sensitive developmental genes. Candidate targets include TBX3 (limb and mammary gland development) or neighboring cardiac transcription factors. The silencer likely recruits co-repressor complexes (PRC2, NCoR/SMRT, HDACs) to deposit H3K27me3 marks at target promoters. The balanced CNV burden (1 deletion:1 duplication) suggests both haploinsufficiency and triplosensitivity intolerance where both silencer loss and gain cause derepression or hyper-repression of developmental genes. The K562 validation suggests potential roles in hematopoietic lineage specification, consistent with 12q\'s enrichment for metabolic and developmental genes.
