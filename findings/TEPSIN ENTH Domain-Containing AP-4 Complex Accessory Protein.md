# TEPSIN: ENTH Domain-Containing AP-4 Complex Accessory Protein Regulating Trans-Golgi Network Vesicle Trafficking

**Date:** 2026-04-06T12:58:07.782340

**Quality Score:** 3.05/10  (E=5.1, D=1.0) [WEAK]

## Description
TEPSIN (AP-4 Complex Accessory Subunit Tepsin, also C17orf56, ENTHD2) is a 525 amino acid intracellular protein (UniProt Q96N21) with a partially structured conformation (AlphaFold pLDDT 67.8, medium confidence), characteristic of vesicle trafficking adaptors containing both ordered membrane-binding domains and flexible interaction regions.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals TEPSIN contains an N-terminal ENTH (Epsin N-Terminal Homology) domain (IPR013809/PF01417, residues 24-131; IPR008942/SSF48464, residues 8-137) for membrane binding and phosphoinositide recognition, plus a Tepsin-specific domain (IPR039273/PTHR21514, residues 178-523) and a VHS/ENTH-like domain (IPR058028/PF25827, residues 244-348; IPR035802, residues 16-134). The ENTH domain superfamily (G3DSA:1.25.40.90) confers vesicle trafficking and membrane curvature sensing functions.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals TEPSIN functions as an integral AP-4 complex component: strongest interactions with AP4M1 (0.886, AP-4 complex subunit mu-1), ANKRD13D (0.644, ankyrin repeat domain-containing protein 13D), LRRC73 (0.625, leucine-rich repeat protein 73), AP4B1 (0.585, AP-4 complex subunit beta-1), AP4S1 (0.577, AP-4 complex subunit sigma-1), AP4E1 (0.562, AP-4 complex subunit epsilon-1), VSIG10 (0.531, V-set and immunoglobulin domain-containing protein 10), RUSC2 (0.509, RUN and SH3 domain-containing protein 2), TMEM87A (0.508), and GGA3 (0.507, Golgi-associated gamma adaptin ear-containing ARF-binding protein 3). The AP-4 complex interactions (AP4M1, AP4B1, AP4S1, AP4E1) confirm its role as an AP-4 accessory subunit.\n\nHPA expression shows ubiquitous detection across all tissues with low tissue specificity and vesicular subcellular localization, consistent with housekeeping vesicle trafficking functions. The protein is classified as predicted intracellular protein and transporter with cell type enhanced single cell specificity.\n\nClinVar documents 18 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all large chromosome 17q25.3 copy number variations (gains and losses spanning 17q24.1-q25.3 and 17p13.3-q25.3 regions). While specific TEPSIN point mutations are not yet catalogued, these CNVs suggest dosage sensitivity potentially causing developmental disorders through impaired vesicle trafficking and protein sorting.\n\nConservation is evident from mouse ortholog (Q3U3N6, 573 aa) and rat ortholog (G3V8Y7, 569 aa), indicating functional importance across mammals.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: TEPSIN acts as an AP-4 complex accessory protein that links the AP-4 adaptor complex to specific cargo and membranes during trans-Golgi network vesicle formation. The ENTH domain binds phosphatidylinositol-4,5-bisphosphate (PI(4,5)P2) on Golgi membranes to recruit the AP-4 complex to budding sites. The VHS/ENTH-like domain may recognize cargo proteins bearing specific sorting signals. Interaction with GGA3 suggests coordination between AP-4 and GGA trafficking pathways. TEPSIN likely stabilizes AP-4 complex assembly or enhances cargo selection specificity. Vesicular localization and ubiquitous expression indicate essential roles in constitutive protein trafficking from the trans-Golgi network to endosomes or lysosomes. CNV-mediated dosage imbalance disrupts AP-4 complex stoichiometry, causing impaired cargo sorting that manifests as developmental disorders through defective protein localization, potentially affecting neuronal development given AP-4 complex roles in axonal trafficking.
