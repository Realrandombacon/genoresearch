# CDIP1: p53-Regulated Apoptotic Effector with LITAF Domain and Centrosomal Localization Linking ER Stress to Mitochondrial Apoptosis via BCAP31 Interaction

## Description
CDIP1 (UniProt Q9H305) encodes Cell Death-Inducing p53-Target Protein 1, a 208 aa regulatory protein that functions as a proapoptotic effector downstream of p53 and ER stress, integrating cellular stress signals into mitochondrial apoptosis. InterPro analysis reveals a single, well-defined C-terminal LITAF (LPS-Induced TNF-Alpha Factor) domain (IPR006629, PF10601, aa 122-206), a zinc-binding motif (PS51837) involved in membrane association and protein-protein interactions, classifying CDIP1 within the LITAF domain-containing family (IPR037519). AlphaFold predicts a medium-confidence structure (pLDDT 55.3), indicating a partially disordered protein with a structured LITAF domain and flexible N-terminus—consistent with a signaling scaffold that undergoes conformational changes upon activation. STRING interactions define a functional network centered on apoptosis regulation: BCAP31 (0.943), an ER membrane protein that bridges CDIP1 to the mitochondrial apoptosis pathway; ITCH and NEDD4 (0.486, 0.485), E3 ubiquitin ligases suggesting CDIP1 turnover regulation; FANCM (0.532), linking CDIP1 to DNA damage response; and LITAF (0.474), its structural homolog, implying potential heterodimerization. HPA confirms CDIP1 as a tissue-enhanced, ubiquitously expressed protein with highest levels in brain (159.5 nTPM), localized to nucleoplasm and centrosomes—positioning it at key sites for cell cycle control and stress signaling. ClinVar reports 22 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all large copy number gains/losses at 16p13.3, a region associated with developmental disorders and cancer, though no monogenic disease is yet assigned solely to CDIP1. Literature (31 papers) establishes roles in: (1) p53-mediated apoptosis—upregulated by p53 in response to genotoxic stress; (2) ER stress-induced cell death—activated by unfolded protein response (UPR) pathways; (3) miRNA-regulated survival—targeted by miR-133b-3p in cardiac hypertrophy and miR-21-5p in post-MI angiogenesis; (4) Cancer progression—modulated by the miR-31-5p/CDIP1 axis in colorectal cancer. Functional hypothesis: CDIP1 functions through: (1) ER-Mitochondria Apoptotic Coupling—the LITAF domain mediates membrane association, allowing CDIP1 to interact with BCAP31 at ER-mitochondria contact sites (MAMs), triggering BAX/BAK activation and cytochrome c release; (2) Centrosomal Surveillance—localization to centrosomes suggests a role in monitoring mitotic fidelity, potentially eliminating cells with spindle defects; (3) Stress Signal Integration—acts as a node integrating p53, ER stress, and DNA damage signals to commit cells to apoptosis; (4) Ubiquitin-Mediated Regulation—interactions with ITCH/NEDD4 imply tight control of CDIP1 stability, preventing inappropriate cell death; (5) Disease Mechanism—16p13.3 CNVs may dysregulate CDIP1 dosage, contributing to neurodevelopmental phenotypes or cancer susceptibility by altering apoptotic thresholds. Despite moderate literature, CDIP1 remains under-characterized mechanistically, particularly its structural dynamics and precise activation mechanism, qualifying it as a partially dark gene with high therapeutic relevance.
