## Description
ZNF430 (zinc finger protein 430) encodes a 570 amino acid C2H2-type zinc finger transcription factor (UniProt Q9H8G1) localized to chromosome 19p12. The protein contains a canonical KRAB (Krüppel-associated box) repression domain (IPR001909/PF01352, residues 34-74) at the N-terminus, followed by multiple C2H2 zinc finger DNA-binding domains (IPR013087/PF00096, 3+ fingers spanning residues 195-311). This domain architecture is characteristic of KRAB-ZFP family proteins that recruit transcriptional corepressors to silence target genes.\n\nSTRING interaction network identifies 9 protein partners with medium confidence scores. The strongest interaction is with TRIM28 (KAP1, score 0.605), the canonical corepressor that binds KRAB domains and recruits heterochromatin-forming complexes including SETDB1 histone methyltransferase and NuRD. Additional partners include EIF2S3B (0.572, translation initiation), ZNF223 (0.443, related zinc finger), and C19orf44 (0.436, chromosome 19 neighbor). The TRIM28 interaction strongly supports a transcriptional repression mechanism via heterochromatin deposition.\n\nClinVar documents 7 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all large-scale chromosomal rearrangements (copy number gains, multi-megabase duplications spanning 19p13.11-q13.43). No single-gene disorder is established, but the recurrent CNVs suggest ZNF430 locus susceptibility to genomic instability or dosage sensitivity. Gene Alliance annotations predict involvement in substantia nigra development, suggesting potential neurodevelopmental roles.\n\nEvolutionary conservation is confirmed: orthologs exist in Pan troglodytes (Q9H8G1, 570 aa, 100% identity) and Cercocebus atys (A0A2K5MRZ4, 537 aa), indicating functional constraint across primates. The KRAB domain and zinc finger array are highly conserved, reflecting purifying selection on DNA-binding and corepressor recruitment functions.\n\nFunctional hypothesis: ZNF430 functions as a sequence-specific transcriptional repressor that binds GC-rich DNA motifs via its C2H2 zinc fingers and recruits TRIM28/KAP1 to establish repressive H3K9me3 chromatin marks. Target genes may include neurodevelopmental pathways (substantia nigra development annotation) or cell cycle regulators. Chromosomal duplications disrupting ZNF430 dosage could alter repression of oncogenic or neurodevelopmental targets, contributing to disease phenotypes.
