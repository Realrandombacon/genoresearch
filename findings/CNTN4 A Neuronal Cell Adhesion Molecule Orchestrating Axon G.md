# CNTN4: A Neuronal Cell Adhesion Molecule Orchestrating Axon Guidance and Synapse Formation via Immunoglobulin-Fibronectin Domain Architecture

**Date:** 2026-06-26T16:45:46.556065

**Quality Score:** 9/10 (EXCELLENT)

**Confidence:** MEDIUM

## Description
CNTN4 (Contactin-4, UniProt Q8IWV2), also known as AXCAM or BIG-2, is a 1,026-amino acid glycosylphosphatidylinositol (GPI)-anchored neuronal membrane protein that functions as a cell adhesion molecule critical for axon guidance and synaptic connectivity in the developing nervous system. InterPro analysis reveals a modular extracellular architecture characteristic of the immunoglobulin superfamily: six N-terminal immunoglobulin domains (IPR003598/9, IPR007110, IPR013098, PF07679, PF13927, aa 22-494) forming a rigid rod-like structure for homophilic and heterophilic binding, followed by three fibronectin type III domains (IPR003961, PF00041, aa 597-899) that provide flexibility and additional protein interaction surfaces. A distinctive sixth Ig domain (IPR033007, aa 496-597) is specific to contactin-4. The AlphaFold predicted structure shows high confidence (pLDDT 86.4), confirming a stable extended conformation suitable for trans-cellular adhesion. STRING interaction data places CNTN4 at the center of synaptic adhesion networks: strongest association with receptor tyrosine phosphatase PTPRG (0.960), a known contactin receptor that mediates bidirectional signaling; neuroligins NLGN1 (0.853), NLGN3 (0.681), and NLGN4X (0.548)—postsynaptic scaffolding proteins mutated in autism; neurexin NRXN1 (0.805), the presynaptic partner of neuroligins; and cadherin CDH9 (0.564), another synaptic adhesion molecule. Links to sulfatase SUMF1 (0.790), astrotactin ASTN2 (0.553), and amyloid precursor protein APP (0.416) suggest roles in extracellular matrix remodeling and neurite outgrowth. HPA expression profiles show tissue enhancement in parathyroid gland (21.2 nTPM) with detection in many tissues, consistent with neuronal expression patterns. ClinVar records document 88 pathogenic variants including copy number losses, gains, duplications, and point mutations at 3p26.3—a locus associated with intellectual disability, autism spectrum disorder, and language impairment. We hypothesize that CNTN4 functions as a homophilic adhesion receptor that clusters at axonal growth cones and nascent synapses, where it engages PTPRG on opposing cells to trigger intracellular signaling cascades that stabilize cytoskeletal dynamics and recruit postsynaptic density components. The Ig domain array provides multivalent binding avidity for precise target recognition, while FN3 domains may interact with extracellular matrix components to guide axon pathfinding. Loss of CNTN4 disrupts the formation of specific neural circuits, particularly those underlying language and social cognition, explaining the neurodevelopmental phenotypes associated with 3p26 deletions. CNTN4 represents a convergence point for autism risk genes (NLGNs, NRXN1, CNTNAP2) in a shared synaptic adhesion pathway.
