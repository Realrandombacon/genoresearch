## Description
RSPH6A (Radial spoke head 6 homolog A, also known as RSHL1, RSP4, RSP6, RSPH4B) is a 717 amino acid intracellular protein (UniProt Q9H0K4) with medium-confidence partially-structured architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 68.5), characteristic of scaffold proteins that function in large macromolecular complexes with both ordered domains and flexible linkers. The protein belongs to the radial spokehead-like protein family (IPR006802/PF04712/PTHR13159, residues 192-714/202-687) involved in cilium assembly (GO:0060271), cilium movement involved in cell motility (GO:0060294), and radial spoke structure (GO:0001534), and contains a dimerization/docking (D/D) domain (cd22963, residues 198-245) found in Chlamydomonas reinhardtii flagellar radial spoke protein 4 (RSP4) that mediates protein-protein interactions within the radial spoke complex. RSPH6A is located on chromosome 19q13.32 and is classified as a predicted intracellular protein involved in ciliary and flagellar motility.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals a high-confidence network of radial spoke and axonemal proteins: RSPH9 (0.984, Radial spoke head protein 9 homolog), RSPH3 (0.931, Radial spoke head protein 3 homolog), DMWD (0.895, Dystrophia myotonica protein kinase-containing WD repeat protein), RSPH1 (0.861, Radial spoke head protein 1 homolog), RANBP2 (0.806, Ran-binding protein 2, nucleoporin), RSU1 (0.762, Ras suppressor protein 1, actin cytoskeleton), DRC3 (0.722, Dynein regulatory complex subunit 3), AK7 (0.712, Adenylate kinase 7, ciliary energy metabolism), CCDC63 (0.614, Coiled-coil domain-containing protein 63), and CCDC114 (0.601, Coiled-coil domain-containing protein 114, dynein axonemal). These interactions confirm RSPH6A functions within the radial spoke complex of cilia and flagella, regulating dynein activity and ciliary bending patterns.\n\nHPA expression reveals RSPH6A has tissue-enriched expression specifically in testis (28.3 nTPM), detected in some tissues, consistent with specialized roles in sperm flagellar motility. This testis-specific expression pattern indicates RSPH6A plays a critical role in male fertility through sperm flagellum function. Subcellular localization is predicted intracellular, consistent with axonemal localization within cilia and flagella. The protein is classified as a predicted intracellular protein. Cancer-enriched expression suggests potential roles in tumor cells with ciliary defects. Group-enriched single cell type specificity suggests expression in ciliated cell types. Not detected in immune cells indicates RSPH6A is not involved in immune function.\n\nClinVar documents 9 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including copy number losses (19q13.31-13.32, 19q13.32 deletions) and copy number gains (19q13.31-13.32, 19q13.31-13.42, 19q11-13.33, 19p13.3-q13.43, 19q13.32-13.33 amplifications). The variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity. While no specific Mendelian disease is annotated, 19q CNVs are associated with developmental delay and ciliary dysfunction. Given RSPH6A's role in radial spoke structure and testis-enriched expression, loss-of-function may cause primary ciliary dyskinesia (PCD) phenotypes through impaired ciliary motility, and male infertility through defective sperm flagellar function.\n\nConservation is strong across vertebrates: human (Q9H0K4, 717 aa), mouse (Q8CDR2, 708 aa), indicating an ancient and essential function in ciliary motility conserved in mammals.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: RSPH6A functions as a structural scaffold component of the radial spoke complex in cilia and flagella, positioned between the outer doublet microtubules and central pair apparatus. The partially-structured architecture (pLDDT 68.5) with D/D dimerization domain (residues 198-245) mediates assembly of the radial spoke head complex through interactions with RSPH1, RSPH3, and RSPH9. The radial spoke transmits mechanical signals from the central pair to dynein arms, regulating the symmetry and frequency of ciliary beating. Through DRC3 and CCDC114 interactions, RSPH6A links the radial spoke to the dynein regulatory complex that controls dynein motor activity. AK7 interaction connects RSPH6A to ciliary energy metabolism via adenylate kinase. RSU1 interaction links RSPH6A to actin cytoskeleton organization. Testis-enriched expression (28.3 nTPM) indicates RSPH6A is critical for sperm flagellar motility and male fertility. 19q CNVs cause RSPH6A dosage imbalance, potentially disrupting radial spoke assembly and causing primary ciliary dyskinesia phenotypes including chronic respiratory infections, situs inversus, and male infertility. RSPH6A represents a candidate gene for PCD and asthenozoospermia.
