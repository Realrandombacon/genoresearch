## Description
IL1RAPL2 (Interleukin-1 receptor accessory protein-like 2, 686 aa, UniProt Q9NP60) is an X-linked type I transmembrane receptor of the IL-1 receptor family with emerging roles in synaptic organization, social behavior circuitry, and neurodevelopmental disorders, representing a partially characterized neuronal protein with significant clinical implications in autism spectrum disorders (ASD) and intellectual disability. Structural analysis reveals multi-domain receptor architecture: (1) Three immunoglobulin-like domains (IPR003599/G3DSA:2.60.40.10, aa 26-137, 138-235, 236-351) forming extracellular ligand-binding region characteristic of IL-1 receptor superfamily; (2) Ig I-set domain (IPR013098, aa 246-346) providing structural stability for extracellular interactions; (3) Toll/interleukin-1 receptor homology (TIR) domain (IPR000157/G3DSA:3.40.50.10140, aa 400-566) mediating intracellular signal transduction (GO:0007165) through homotypic TIR-TIR interactions with adaptor proteins; (4) IL-1 receptor type II family membership (IPR004077/IPR015621) with blocking receptor activity (GO:0004910) suggesting potential decoy or modulatory function; (5) Second Ig-like domain of IL-1R (cd05757, aa 146-237) defining receptor subclass. AlphaFold structure shows high confidence (pLDDT 75.1), indicating well-folded stable architecture with ordered extracellular Ig domains and intracellular TIR signaling domain—suggests functional receptor requiring precise structural organization for ligand binding and downstream signaling. Literature (33 papers) reveals emerging roles: (1) Autism spectrum disorders—fine mapping of Xq11.1-q21.33 identifies IL1RAPL2 as ASD susceptibility gene, mutation screening in ASD cohorts reveals rare variants; (2) Intellectual disability—association studies between IL1RAPL2 and non-specific mental retardation in Chinese children, genetic variants correlate with cognitive impairment; (3) Social behavior—molecularly defined mPFC-BLA (medial prefrontal cortex-basolateral amygdala) circuit regulates social novelty preference, IL1RAPL2 expressed in these regions modulating social interaction; (4) CNS specificity—IL1RAPL2 maps to Xq22 and is specifically expressed in central nervous system, excluding peripheral tissues; (5) Synaptic function—IL1RAPL2 homolog IL1RAPL1 (paralog) regulates excitatory synapse differentiation through trans-synaptic interaction with PTPδ (PTPRD), suggesting conserved mechanism for IL1RAPL2. Functional hypothesis: IL1RAPL2 functions as (a) trans-synaptic adhesion receptor organizing excitatory synapses through extracellular Ig domain-mediated interaction with presynaptic PTPRD/PTPRF, positioning postsynaptic density components (NCS1, DACH2) for synaptic transmission; (b) IL-1 cytokine signaling modulator in CNS through TIR domain intracellular signaling, potentially acting as decoy receptor (IL-1R type II-like) sequestering IL-1A/IL-18 or transducing anti-inflammatory signals (IL-37, IL-38) regulating neuroinflammation; (c) X-linked neurodevelopmental regulator controlling synaptic maturation and circuit formation in mPFC-BLA pathway essential for social behavior, with haploinsufficiency causing ASD and intellectual disability. Mechanistic proposal: IL1RAPL2 localizes to postsynaptic membrane (HPA plasma membrane) where three extracellular Ig domains (aa 26-351) bind presynaptic PTPRD/PTPRF receptor tyrosine phosphatases in trans-synaptic complex—this adhesion organizes excitatory synapse structure recruiting PSD-95, NMDA receptors, and calcium signaling components (NCS1); intracellular TIR domain (aa 400-566) recruits adaptor proteins (MyD88-like) activating downstream signaling (NF-κB, MAPK pathways) modulating synaptic plasticity and gene expression; in social behavior circuits, IL1RAPL2-PTPRD interaction in mPFC-BLA pathway regulates synaptic strength controlling social novelty preference (2025 circuit mapping); IL1RAPL2 may bind IL-1 family cytokines (IL1A, IL18, IL37, IL38) through extracellular domains, acting as decoy receptor preventing proinflammatory signaling or transducing anti-inflammatory neuroprotective signals—dual adhesion/signaling function distinguishes IL1RAPL2 from classical IL-1 receptors; X-linked inheritance explains male predominance in ASD/ID—hemizygous males with IL1RAPL2 deletions/mutations show severe neurodevelopmental phenotypes; CNVs disrupt IL1RAPL2 dosage—deletions cause haploinsufficiency impairing synapse organization and social circuit development (ASD, ID); duplications cause triplosensitivity disrupting trans-synaptic stoichiometry (PTPRD-IL1RAPL2 balance) causing synaptic dysfunction—positioning IL1RAPL2 as diagnostic marker for X-linked ASD/ID (genetic testing), therapeutic target for social behavior disorders (synaptic adhesion modulation), and model for IL-1 receptor evolution in CNS (decoy vs. signaling receptor functions).
