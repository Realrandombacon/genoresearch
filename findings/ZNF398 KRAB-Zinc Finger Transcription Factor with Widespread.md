## Description
ZNF398 (Zinc Finger Protein 398) is a well-characterized Kruppel-associated box (KRAB) C2H2-type zinc finger transcription factor encoded on chromosome 7q36.1. The 642-amino acid protein (UniProt Q8TD17) contains multiple functional domains: a canonical KRAB repressor domain (residues 142-183, PF01352; IPR001909) that recruits heterochromatin protein 1 and histone methyltransferases for transcriptional silencing, and at least 10 C2H2-type zinc finger motifs (IPR013087, PF00096) spanning residues 339-585 that mediate sequence-specific DNA binding. Human Protein Atlas data confirms ubiquitous expression across all tissues with low tissue specificity, nuclear/nucleoplasmic localization, and classification as a transcriptional activator. ClinVar documents 64 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variants encompassing the ZNF398 locus, including both deletions (CNV losses) and duplications (CNV gains) on 7q34-q36.3, implicating dosage sensitivity in developmental disorders. The KRAB-ZNF architecture suggests ZNF398 functions as a sequence-specific repressor that recruits the KAP1/TRIM28 corepressor complex to silence target genes through H3K9me3 deposition. Given its ubiquitous expression and extensive clinical variant burden, ZNF398 likely regulates fundamental cellular processes such as cell cycle progression or differentiation, with haploinsufficiency or overexpression contributing to developmental phenotypes. While STRING and AlphaFold queries failed technically, the domain architecture strongly predicts chromatin-binding and transcriptional regulatory function.
