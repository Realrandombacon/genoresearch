## Description
CCDC136 (UniProt Q96JN2) is a 1154 amino acid coiled-coil domain-containing protein representing a moderately characterized but functionally important gene in the human genome with clear roles in spermatogenesis and potential centrosomal functions. The protein architecture features an extensive CCDC136/SLMAP coiled-coil domain (IPR060659/PF28341, positions 1-1123) spanning nearly the entire protein length, placing it within the Centrosomal and Immune Signaling Modulators family (IPR051176/PTHR15715, positions 1-615). This domain architecture strongly suggests CCDC136 functions as a large structural scaffold protein, potentially mediating protein-protein interactions and macromolecular complex assembly through its extensive coiled-coil regions. AlphaFold predicts a medium-confidence structure (global pLDDT 68.5) spanning residues 1-1154, indicating a partially structured protein consistent with coiled-coil architecture that may contain both ordered helical domains and flexible linker regions typical of scaffold proteins. HPA classifies CCDC136 as a predicted intracellular and membrane protein with group-enriched RNA expression detected in many tissues, showing exceptionally high expression in testis (175.1 nTPM), substantial expression in brain (82.4 nTPM) and retina (48.7 nTPM). Subcellular localization includes Golgi apparatus, vesicles, plasma membrane, and sperm mid piece. Biological processes include differentiation, fertilization, and spermatogenesis. This expression profile strongly suggests critical roles in sperm development and function (midpiece localization, fertilization), neuronal development or function (brain expression), and photoreceptor biology (retina expression). The interaction profile suggests potential roles in membrane trafficking (GCC2, AP2M1), cytoskeletal organization (CTTNBP2), ciliary/centrosomal function (ALMS1), and cell-cell recognition (MEGF family). All variants classified with uncertain significance but marked pathogenic/likely pathogenic. The chromosomal region 7q is associated with multiple developmental disorders. Evolutionary conservation across mammals (mouse Q3TVA9, 1136 aa) indicates strong functional constraint. The mechanistic model positions CCDC136 as a centrosomal/Golgi-associated scaffold protein potentially involved in vesicular trafficking during spermatogenesis, linking Golgi-derived vesicles to the developing sperm midpiece where mitochondria assemble for motility. The coiled-coil architecture enables oligomerization and binding to multiple partners simultaneously, coordinating membrane trafficking (GCC2, AP2M1), cytoskeletal remodeling (CTTNBP2), and ciliary/centrosomal assembly (ALMS1 interaction). Loss-of-function would disrupt sperm midpiece formation explaining fertilization defects, while brain and retina expression suggests potential roles in neuronal development or photoreceptor maintenance.
