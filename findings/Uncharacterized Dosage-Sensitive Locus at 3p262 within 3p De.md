## Description
LOC100130207 is an uncharacterized genomic locus on chromosome 3p26.2 (NC_000003.12:3800874-3700813) spanning approximately 100.1 kb. Comprehensive database searches reveal no evidence of protein-coding function: no UniProt accession was identified, precluding InterPro domain analysis and AlphaFold structure prediction. STRING database returned no protein interaction partners, confirming absence of canonical protein-coding function. Human Protein Atlas could not resolve this locus to an Ensembl ID, indicating no detectable protein expression in surveyed tissues. However, ClinVar analysis reveals an extraordinary 37 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants in this region, the highest CNV burden observed in this dark gene survey. Variants include large copy number losses (monosomy) of 3p26.3-25.3 (chr3:688897-11051142, ~10.4 Mb; chr3:32241-10323124, ~10.3 Mb; chr3:32241-10631310, ~10.6 Mb; chr3:52266-4827490, ~4.8 Mb; chr3:20213-9362037, ~9.3 Mb; chr3:20213-11221602, ~11.2 Mb) and copy number gains (trisomy) of 3p26.3-26.1 (chr3:52266-5966084, ~5.9 Mb) and 3p26.3-22.1 (chr3:53308-41381521, ~41.3 Mb). Notably, one variant maps to LRRN1 (Leucine Rich Repeat Neuronal 1, c.-279+240T>G), suggesting this locus may overlap with or regulate LRRN1 expression. These structural variants are associated with 3p deletion syndrome, characterized by intellectual disability, developmental delay, hypotonia, distinctive facial features (ptosis, micrognathia, low-set ears), and growth retardation. The 3p26.2 region contains several dosage-sensitive neuronal development genes including LRRN1 (leucine-rich repeat neuronal protein 1, synaptic development), CHL1 (cell adhesion molecule L1-like, axon guidance), and CNTN4 (contactin-4, neuronal cell adhesion). LOC100130207 likely functions as part of the 3p26.2 cis-regulatory landscape or encodes a non-coding RNA whose copy number alteration contributes to 3p deletion syndrome pathogenesis through disruption of chromatin topology, topologically associating domain (TAD) boundaries, or long-range dysregulation of neuronal development genes. The extreme pathogenic CNV burden (37 variants) despite absence of protein evidence indicates this locus resides within one of the most dosage-sensitive genomic regions in the human genome, where both hemizygosity and trisomy severely disrupt neurodevelopmental processes via gene dosage effects and 3D genome reorganization.
