## Description
LOC105374312 is an uncharacterized genomic locus on chromosome 3q21.2 (NC_000003.12:125774713-125797952) spanning approximately 23.2 kb. Comprehensive database searches reveal no evidence of canonical protein-coding function: no UniProt accession was identified, precluding InterPro domain analysis and AlphaFold structure prediction. STRING database returned no protein interaction partners, confirming absence of characterized interactome. Human Protein Atlas resolved this locus to Ensembl ID ENSG00000284624 but reported no detectable protein expression data in surveyed tissues. However, ClinVar analysis reveals 6 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including both copy number losses and gains affecting 3q: deletions spanning 3q13.32-21.3 (chr3:118673898-126540730, ~7.9 Mb), 3q13.33-21.3 (chr3:121925147-126782249, ~4.9 Mb), 3q13.2-21.3 (chr3:112620977-128734134, ~16.1 Mb), 3q13.32-21.2 (chr3:119117166-125920734, ~6.8 Mb) and duplications spanning 3q11.1-24 (chr3:93800620-145695381, ~51.9 Mb), 3q13.11-29 (chr3:103426882-198110178, ~94.7 Mb). The 3q21 region contains several dosage-sensitive genes including FOXP1 (forkhead box P1, intellectual disability, autism spectrum disorder), ZATB2 (zinc finger homeodomain transcription factor, craniofacial development), MITF (microphthalmia-associated transcription factor, Waardenburg syndrome), SLC2A2 (GLUT2 glucose transporter, Fanconi-Bickel syndrome), and THRB (thyroid hormone receptor beta, thyroid hormone resistance). LOC105374312 likely functions as: (1) a cis-regulatory enhancer or silencer controlling 3q dosage-sensitive gene expression, (2) a long non-coding RNA (lncRNA) modulating 3q transcription factor gene transcription, (3) a chromatin boundary element within the 3q21 topologically associating domain (TAD), or (4) an alternative transcript isoform of neighboring genes. The moderate pathogenic CNV burden (6 variants) despite absence of protein evidence indicates this locus resides within a critical 3q dosage-sensitive genomic region where copy number alterations disrupt: (1) FOXP1 haploinsufficiency leading to neurodevelopmental phenotypes, (2) 3q chromatin architecture affecting transcription factor expression, (3) craniofacial development programs mediated by ZATB2, or (4) thyroid hormone signaling via THRB, leading to 3q deletion syndrome (developmental delay, intellectual disability, autism spectrum disorder, craniofacial dysmorphism, cardiac defects) or 3q duplication syndrome (developmental delay, intellectual disability, dysmorphic features, neurological abnormalities).
