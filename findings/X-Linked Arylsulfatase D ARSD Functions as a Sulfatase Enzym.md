## Description
ARSD (Arylsulfatase D) is a 593 aa sulfatase enzyme functioning in glycosaminoglycan (GAG) catabolism and emerging as a tumor suppressor in breast cancer and glioblastoma. With 238 literature references, ARSD represents a 'partially characterized' dark gene where its enzymatic function is established but cancer-related mechanisms are only recently emerging (2021-2025). InterPro analysis reveals a canonical sulfatase architecture: an N-terminal sulfatase domain (IPR000917/PF00884, aa 41-426) containing the catalytic formylglycine residue within conserved signatures (PS00149/PS00523, aa 87-99/140-150), an alkaline phosphatase-like core domain superfamily (IPR017850/G3DSA:3.40.720.10, aa 40-588) that forms the catalytic fold, and a C-terminal aryl-sulfatase region (PF14707, aa 451-585) unique to this enzyme family. AlphaFold predicts a very high-confidence structure (pLDDT 92.4), indicating a well-folded catalytic enzyme under strong evolutionary constraint. HPA confirms ubiquitous expression with low tissue specificity, detected in all tissues, localizing to vesicles and lipid droplets—consistent with a lysosomal enzyme involved in substrate degradation. STRING interactions reveal connections to other sulfatases (ARSI: 0.650, ARSK: 0.646, ARSJ: 0.457), sulfatase-modifying factors (SUMF1: 0.701, SUMF2: 0.670) required for catalytic activation, the XG blood group antigen (0.776) which shares sulfatase homology, and the cAMP-dependent protein kinase PRKX (0.664) suggesting regulatory phosphorylation. ClinVar lists 217 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number losses and gains on Xp22.33-p11.4, implicating ARSD dosage sensitivity in X-linked disorders, though specific monogenic diseases remain undefined. Recent literature (2021-2025) establishes ARSD as: (1) Novel ERα downstream target that inhibits breast cancer proliferation/migration via Hippo/YAP pathway activation; (2) TRIM67 ubiquitination target in glioblastoma where ARSD degradation promotes TMZ resistance through β-catenin pathway activation; (3) Potential link between breast cancer and Alzheimer's disease inverse incidence patterns.
