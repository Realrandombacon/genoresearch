## Description
MCOLN3 (Q8TDD5, 553 aa) is a highly structured (AlphaFold pLDDT: 84.2) lysosomal transient receptor potential (TRP) cation channel essential for endolysosomal ion homeostasis and autophagic flux. InterPro analysis confirms membership in the mucolipin family (IPR039031) with a characteristic extracytosolic/lumenal domain (IPR047317, residues 109-277) and a polycystin-like pore domain (IPR013122, residues 371-508). HPA data reveals group-enriched expression in the adrenal gland (41.3 nTPM) and parathyroid gland (33.2 nTPM), with cancer-enhanced specificity suggesting a role in tumor metabolism. STRING interactions define a functional cluster with paralogs MCOLN1 (0.579) and MCOLN2 (0.983), the two-pore channel TPCN2 (0.630), and the lysosomal tetraspanin CD63 (0.594), positioning MCOLN3 within the endolysosomal calcium signaling network. Additional links to FRMD6 (0.649) and KCNJ10 (0.617) suggest crosstalk with epithelial potassium transport. ClinVar records 18 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number losses/gains on chromosome 1p, implicating dosage sensitivity in developmental disorders, though specific monogenic phenotypes remain under investigation compared to MCOLN1 (mucolipidosis IV). With limited direct literature (Semantic Scholar error prevented count, but known to be <50 focused papers), MCOLN3 represents a 'dark' ion channel whose regulation of lysosomal pH and Ca2+ efflux likely controls autophagosome-lysosome fusion and sensory cell survival, offering therapeutic potential for neurodegenerative diseases and hearing loss.
