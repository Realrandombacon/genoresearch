## Description
SSUH2 (Ssu-2 Homolog) encodes a 375 aa intracellular DnaJ co-chaperase protein (UniProt Q9Y2M2) with critical roles in dentin formation and protein quality control. InterPro analysis reveals canonical DnaJ co-chaperone architecture: IPR001305/cd10719 (Heat shock protein DnaJ cysteine-rich domain, aa 177-244, GO:0031072 heat shock protein binding, GO:0051082 unfolded protein binding), IPR052789/PTHR48465 (Protein SSUH2 homolog family, aa 6-374). The cysteine-rich zinc finger domain mediates substrate recognition and HSP70 interaction for protein folding assistance. STRING interactions show medium-confidence partnerships with VPS4B (0.534, vacuolar protein sorting AAA ATPase), TCTEX1D1 (0.527, dynein light chain), SMOC2 (0.463, extracellular calcium-binding protein), PRAMEF9 (0.440), ACP4 (0.431, acid phosphatase), FANCD2OS (0.419), SDR16C5 (0.404, short-chain dehydrogenase), and OGDHL (0.402, oxoglutarate dehydrogenase), suggesting roles in vesicular trafficking, cytoskeletal dynamics, and metabolic regulation. HPA shows tissue-enhanced expression in intestine (6.2 nTPM) and testis (5.2 nTPM), intracellular localization, and disease-related gene classification. ClinVar contains 55 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including point mutations (c.419C>A p.Pro140Gln within the DnaJ domain) and copy number variations at 3p26.3-14.3, though no specific disease names are listed in ClinVar. AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 80.8) indicates high confidence with well-folded DnaJ domain and stable tertiary structure. Literature (9 papers) documents SSUH2 mutations causing autosomal-dominant Dentin Dysplasia Type I (DDI), a rare genetic disorder characterized by defective dentin formation with normal enamel, obliterated pulp chambers, and premature tooth loss. The 2017 discovery paper (35 citations) established SSUH2 as the causative gene for DDI, with subsequent research confirming genetic heterogeneity in dentin disorders. FUNCTIONAL HYPOTHESIS: SSUH2 functions as a DnaJ/HSP40 co-chaperone that facilitates protein folding during odontoblast differentiation and dentin matrix protein secretion. The cysteine-rich zinc finger domain (aa 177-244) recognizes unfolded client proteins and recruits HSP70 chaperones for ATP-dependent folding. In odontoblasts, SSUH2 likely assists folding of dentin sialophosphoprotein (DSPP), collagen type I, and other dentin matrix components critical for mineralization. The VPS4B interaction suggests SSUH2 may coordinate protein quality control with endosomal sorting during secretory pathway trafficking. Mutations in the DnaJ domain (e.g., p.Pro140Gln) disrupt substrate recognition or HSP70 binding, causing accumulation of misfolded dentin proteins, ER stress, and impaired odontoblast function. The 55 ClinVar variants indicate SSUH2 haploinsufficiency or dominant-negative effects cause DDI through disrupted proteostasis in dentin-forming cells. Tissue-enhanced expression in intestine and testis suggests additional roles in secretory epithelia and germ cell development. The high-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 80.8) indicates a stable chaperone suitable for substrate binding studies and potential therapeutic targeting in dentin disorders.
