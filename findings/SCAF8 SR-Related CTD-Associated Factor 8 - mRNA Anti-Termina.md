## Description
SCAF8 (SR-related and CTD-associated factor 8, UniProt Q9UPN6) is a 1271 amino acid nuclear protein functioning as an mRNA anti-terminator that suppresses premature transcription termination and alternative polyadenylation. Despite only 24 literature references (partially characterized status), SCAF8 has emerging roles in transcription regulation, diabetic complications, and cancer. The protein contains a CTD-interacting domain (CID, IPR006569, PF04818, aa 1-139) that binds phosphorylated carboxyl-terminal domain (CTD) repeats of RNA polymerase II, and an RNA recognition motif (RRM, IPR000504, PF00076, aa 477-551) for RNA binding. AlphaFold predicts a partially structured protein (pLDDT 51.4) with ordered CID and RRM domains embedded in intrinsically disordered regions, characteristic of transcriptional regulators that require conformational flexibility for multi-protein complex assembly. STRING interactions reveal high-confidence partnerships with transcription/RNA processing machinery: POLR2A (0.897 - RNA Pol II largest subunit), LEMD3 (0.771), RPRD2 (0.757 - RNA Pol II regulator), PCF11 (0.754 - cleavage/polyadenylation factor), CPSF3 (0.746), CPSF7 (0.732), CPSF2 (0.568), SSU72 (0.555 - CTD phosphatase), and SETX (0.625 - senataxin helicase), confirming integration into the transcription elongation and 3' end processing complexes. HPA shows ubiquitous expression across all tissues with nucleoplasmic localization, consistent with housekeeping transcriptional functions. ClinVar documents 29 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants in the 6q25.2-27 region, including copy number gains/losses and splice site mutations (c.2072-1G>T, c.2071+1del), though specific SCAF8-associated diseases require further characterization. A 2017 study linked SCAF8-CNKSR3 region variants (rs955333) to diabetic kidney disease and retinopathy in Chinese populations. Recent 2024 research demonstrates circSCAF8 (circular RNA derived from SCAF8) promotes gastric cancer tumor growth and metastasis via miR-1293/TIMP1 signaling axis. The 2019 review (115 citations) established SCAF8 and SCAF4 as anti-terminators preventing early polyadenylation site usage, while 2008 structural work (65 citations) showed SCAF8 CID domain binds different CTD phosphorylation states (Ser2-P, Ser5-P, Ser7-P) during transcription cycle. Functional hypothesis: SCAF8 acts as a molecular bridge between elongating RNA Pol II and 3' end processing factors, using its CID domain to track CTD phosphorylation status and its RRM to engage nascent RNA, thereby preventing premature termination at cryptic polyadenylation sites and ensuring proper transcript elongation. The intrinsically disordered regions may facilitate dynamic interactions with multiple partners during the transcription cycle.
