## Description
CASD1 (CAS1 domain containing 1) is a 797 amino acid N-acetylneuraminate (7)9-O-acetyltransferase that modifies sialic acid residues on glycoproteins and glycolipids. InterPro analysis reveals a large catalytic Cas1p 10-transmembrane acyl transferase domain (IPR012419, PF07779) spanning residues 296-775, an N-terminal cyclin-like superfamily domain (IPR036915, SSF47954) at positions 35-114, and an NXPE family C-terminal domain (IPR057106, PF24536) at residues 64-135. The protein is classified as a polysaccharide O-acetyltransferase (PTHR13533, positions 240-783). AlphaFold structure (AF-Q96PB1-F1) shows high confidence (pLDDT 87.8) across all 797 residues, indicating a stable multi-domain architecture. HPA data confirms ubiquitous expression across all tissues with nucleoplasmic localization. STRING interactions reveal functional coupling to sialic acid metabolism and glycosylation pathways including ST8SIA1 (0.597), ST3GAL5 (0.518), ST6GAL1 (0.472), NEU4 (0.568), SIAE (0.438), and ST6GALNAC1 (0.406), plus BTNL3 (0.599) and PPP1R9A (0.551). ClinVar contains 223 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including deletions and splice site mutations, suggesting disease relevance though specific associations remain uncharacterized. The cyclin-like N-terminal domain may regulate catalytic activity or substrate recognition, while the large transmembrane acyltransferase core transfers acetyl groups to the 7 or 9 position of sialic acid, modifying cell surface glycans that influence immune recognition, pathogen binding, and cell-cell interactions. CASD1 represents an understudied enzyme linking glycan modification to human disease.
