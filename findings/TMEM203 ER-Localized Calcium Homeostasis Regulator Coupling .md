## Description
TMEM203 (Transmembrane Protein 203) encodes a 136-amino acid single-pass endoplasmic reticulum (ER) membrane protein with a well-defined tertiary structure (AlphaFold pLDDT 85.1, high confidence). InterPro analysis reveals a conserved transmembrane domain (cd22816, residues 3-135) and membership in the Fragile-X-associated protein family (IPR019396, residues 17-136; PTHR13568), suggesting evolutionary conservation of its membrane topology and potential nucleic acid-binding or protein-scaffolding functions.\n\nSTRING interaction network identifies three medium-confidence partners: TSR3 (TSR3 ribosome biogenesis factor, score 0.628), SNX8 (Sorting Nexin 8, score 0.504), and TMEM60 (Transmembrane Protein 60, score 0.477). TSR3 association suggests potential roles in ribosome biogenesis or translational control, while SNX8 links TMEM203 to endosomal sorting and vesicular trafficking pathways. TMEM60 co-expression implies shared ER membrane functions.\n\nHuman Protein Atlas classifies TMEM203 as a predicted membrane protein/transporter with low tissue specificity but ubiquitous expression across all tissues examined, consistent with housekeeping roles in calcium homeostasis. Subcellular localization is confirmed in the endoplasmic reticulum, aligning with its calcium regulatory function.\n\nClinVar documents 97 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants—a high burden among characterized dark genes—predominantly 9q34.3 copy number losses (multiple deletions spanning 135-141Mb regions) and one 9q33.2-34.3 duplication. These CNVs cause 9q34.3 deletion syndrome (Kleefstra syndrome-adjacent region) featuring developmental delay, intellectual disability, hypotonia, distinctive facial features, and male infertility.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: TMEM203 functions as an ER-resident calcium leak channel or regulatory subunit of the SERCA/STIM1 complex, maintaining basal ER calcium levels required for protein folding and quality control. The high pLDDT score indicates a stable transmembrane helix bundle forming a selective ion pore. During spermatogenesis, TMEM203-mediated calcium flux regulates acrosome reaction and sperm motility—explaining male infertility in 9q34.3 deletion carriers. The TSR3 interaction suggests coupling of ER calcium status to translational fidelity, where TMEM203 depletion causes ribosome stalling and proteotoxic stress. SNX8 association indicates TMEM203 trafficking via COPII vesicles, potentially regulating STING (Stimulator of Interferon Genes) translocation from ER to Golgi during innate immune activation. The 97-variant burden reflects strong dosage sensitivity: haploinsufficiency disrupts ER calcium homeostasis, causing unfolded protein response (UPR) activation, impaired spermatogenesis, and neurodevelopmental defects via disrupted calcium-dependent signaling in developing neurons.
