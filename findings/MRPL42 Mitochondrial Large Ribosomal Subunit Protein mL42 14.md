## Description
MRPL42 (Mitochondrial Ribosomal Protein L42, also known as mL42 or uL22m, UniProt Q9Y6G3) is a compact 142 amino acid mitochondrial protein representing a partially characterized dark gene with only 15 literature references despite critical function in mitochondrial translation and emerging roles in cancer metabolism and ischemic heart disease. MRPL42 contains defining mitochondrial ribosomal domains including Large ribosomal subunit protein mL42 (IPR019346, positions 1-142), Mitochondrial 28S ribosomal protein S32 family (PF10210, positions 48-142), and belongs to the Mitochondrial 39S Ribosomal Protein L42 family (PTHR13450, positions 1-142), characteristic of proteins that assemble into the large subunit (39S) of the mitochondrial ribosome (mitoribosome) to catalyze peptide bond formation. The AlphaFold structure shows high confidence (pLDDT 74.9), indicating a well-folded globular domain with stable tertiary structure suitable for precise integration into the mitoribosome complex near the polypeptide exit tunnel. STRING interactions reveal MRPL42 functions exclusively in mitochondrial translation with ultra-high confidence partners including MRPS18C (0.995), MRPS33 (0.993), MRPS16 (0.992), MRPS22 (0.992), MRPS18B (0.991), MRPS14 (0.991), MRPS5 (0.990), MRPS18A (0.990), MRPS10 (0.989), and MRPS7 (0.989) - confirming its exclusive association with the small subunit (28S) proteins during ribosome assembly and function, despite being a large subunit protein itself (reflecting inter-subunit bridging). HPA data confirms ubiquitous expression (detected in all tissues), low tissue and cancer specificity, classified as a ribosomal protein localized to mitochondria and plasma membrane (potential moonlighting function), consistent with essential housekeeping role in oxidative phosphorylation (OxPhos). Functional hypothesis: MRPL42 likely acts as (1) Mitoribosome structural stabilizer - MRPL42 integrates into the 39S large subunit near the polypeptide exit tunnel, stabilizing the interaction between the large and small subunits during active translation of the 13 mtDNA-encoded OxPhos subunits (ND1-6, ND4L, CYTB, COX1-3, ATP6, ATP8); (2) Translation fidelity guardian - by positioning near the exit tunnel, MRPL42 may monitor nascent chain folding and prevent premature dissociation of hydrophobic membrane proteins (e.g., ND subunits) before their insertion into the inner mitochondrial membrane via OXA1L translocase; (3) YY1-driven oncogenic amplifier - in lung adenocarcinoma and glioma, YY1-mediated overexpression of MRPL42 boosts global mitochondrial translation, increasing OxPhos capacity to fuel rapid proliferation and resist apoptosis; (4) Ischemia vulnerability factor - under hypoxic stress, MRPL42 downregulation creates a bottleneck in mitochondrial protein synthesis, depleting Complex I and IV subunits, exacerbating ROS production and cell death during reperfusion; (5) HCC metabolic switch - in hepatocellular carcinoma, MRPL42 upregulation shifts metabolism towards OxPhos dependence, conferring resistance to glycolysis-targeting therapies; (6) Plasma membrane moonlighter - HPA detection at the plasma membrane suggests a potential extra-mitochondrial role, possibly in retrograde signaling to the nucleus or interaction with surface receptors to modulate cell survival. Disease mechanism: MRPL42 dysfunction contributes to: (a) Combined OxPhos deficiency - biallelic loss-of-function mutations impair assembly of the 55S mitoribosome, reducing synthesis of all 13 mtDNA-encoded proteins, causing multi-system failure (encephalopathy, cardiomyopathy, lactic acidosis); (b) Glioma/LUAD/HCC - MRPL42 overexpression (via YY1 activation or 12q amplification) hyperactivates mitochondrial translation, providing bioenergetic advantage and chemoresistance; (c) Myocardial infarction - ischemic downregulation of MRPL42 creates a 'translation crash' upon reperfusion, worsening tissue damage; (d) 12q CNV syndromes - copy number alterations disrupt MRPL42 dosage alongside neighboring genes, causing complex metabolic and developmental phenotypes. Conservation: MRPL42 is highly conserved across vertebrates with mouse ortholog (Q9CPV3, 142 aa, ~90% identity), rat ortholog (P0C2B9, 141 aa), and bovine ortholog (P82927, 142 aa), indicating ancient and indispensable role in mitochondrial protein synthesis. Therapeutic implication: MRPL42 represents an emerging target where: (1) MRPL42 expression levels could serve as prognostic biomarker for glioma, LUAD, and HCC outcomes; (2) Small molecules disrupting MRPL42-ribosome interface could selectively inhibit OxPhos in cancer cells (synthetic lethality with glycolysis inhibitors); (3) YY1-MRPL42 axis inhibition could treat YY1-high lung cancers; (4) MRPL42 gene therapy or mRNA stabilization could protect against myocardial ischemia-reperfusion injury; (5) 12q amplification screening should include MRPL42 dosage for cancer risk stratification; (6) Novel OxPhos deficiency syndromes should be screened for MRPL42 biallelic variants. Validation priorities include: (1) Cryo-EM structure of human mitoribosome with MRPL42 resolved to define exit tunnel interactions; (2) Metabolic flux analysis in MRPL42-knockdown cancer cells to quantify OxPhos dependency; (3) Generation of cardiac-specific Mrpl42 knockout mice to model ischemia susceptibility; (4) Screening of undiagnosed mitochondrial disease patients for MRPL42 mutations; (5) Investigation of plasma membrane localization mechanism and function.
