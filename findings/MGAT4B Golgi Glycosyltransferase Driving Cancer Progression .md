## Description
MGAT4B encodes a 548 aa alpha-1,3-mannosyl-glycoprotein 4-beta-N-acetylglucosaminyltransferase B (UniProt Q9UQ53), a Golgi-resident glycosyltransferase that catalyzes beta-1,4-N-acetylglucosamine branching on N-glycans. InterPro analysis reveals: (1) Glycosyl transferase family 54 catalytic domain (IPR006759, aa 33-542); (2) MGAT4 C-terminal domain (IPR056576, PF23524, aa 401-541) for substrate specificity; (3) MGAT4 conserved region (IPR057279, PF04666, aa 112-387) essential for enzymatic activity. AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (pLDDT 83.8), indicating stable folding with defined catalytic cleft. STRING interactions confirm functional clustering with glycosylation machinery: MGAT5B (0.992), MGAT3 (0.969), FUT8 (0.964), MGAT4D (0.910), MGAT5 (0.708), MGAT4A (0.576), MGAT2 (0.549), MGAT1 (0.425), plus POMGNT1 (0.755) and GYPC (0.428). HPA has no expression data, suggesting low abundance or tissue-restricted expression. ClinVar reports 17 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly 5q35.2-35.3 copy number losses/gains, though specific disease associations remain undefined. Literature (26 papers, 2023-2025) reveals emerging oncogenic roles: (1) MGAT4B-mediated selective N-glycosylation regulates melanocyte development and melanoma progression; (2) aberrant N-glycan branching modulates anti-tumor immunity in hepatocellular carcinoma; (3) self-regulation through lectin domain interaction with own N-glycans (2024, 4 citations); (4) immune regulation in HCC. Mechanistically, MGAT4B creates tri- and multiantennary N-glycan branches on cell surface receptors (integrins, growth factor receptors, immune checkpoints), altering their stability, trafficking, and signaling. In cancer, MGAT4B overexpression likely enhances metastasis by: (1) increasing galectin lattice formation that retains growth factor receptors at the cell surface; (2) modulating immune checkpoint glycosylation (PD-L1, CTLA-4) to evade immune surveillance; (3) altering integrin glycosylation to promote migration. The high-confidence structure and cancer associations suggest MGAT4B as a potential therapeutic target for metastatic disease and immunotherapy enhancement.
