## Description
ENO4 (Enolase 4, also known as sperm-specific enolase) is a 625-amino acid cytosolic enzyme characterized by a canonical enolase domain architecture (IPR000941/IPR020810/IPR020811/IPR029017/IPR036849/IPR047500/PF00113/PTHR11902/SM01192/G3DSA:3.20.20.120/G3DSA:3.30.390.10/cd22974, positions 14-78 for dimerization/docking domain, 61-267 for N-terminal domain, 256-584 for C-terminal TIM barrel domain, 66-571 for enolase core) containing the conserved magnesium-binding active site and TIM barrel fold mediating dehydration of 2-phosphoglycerate to phosphoenolpyruvate, classified under glycolytic process (GO:0006096), phosphopyruvate hydratase activity (GO:0004634), and magnesium ion binding (GO:0000287). STRING interactions reveal a focused functional network exclusively with glycolytic and gluconeogenic enzymes: PGAM1 (0.973, phosphoglycerate mutase 1 - highest confidence partner indicating sequential glycolytic pathway position), GPI (0.973, glucose-6-phosphate isomerase), BPGM (0.960, bisphosphoglycerate mutase), PGAM4 (0.958, phosphoglycerate mutase 4), GLYCTK (0.938, glycerate kinase), PGAM2 (0.861, muscle phosphoglycerate mutase), PKLR (0.733, pyruvate kinase L/R), PKM (0.700, pyruvate kinase M), PCK2 (0.538, phosphoenolpyruvate carboxykinase 2), and PCK1 (0.490), indicating ENO4 functions as an integral component of central carbon metabolism. HPA data shows tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in choroid plexus (5.2 nTPM) and fallopian tube (4.7 nTPM), detected in some tissues, cancer-enhanced specificity, not detected in immune cells, cell type-enhanced single cell specificity, classified as an enzyme and lyase localized to vesicles and cytosol. ClinVar contains 29 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number gains and losses spanning 10q24.1-26.3, 10q23.33-26.3, 10q25.1-26.13, and 10p14-q26.3 regions (deletions and duplications affecting chromosome 10 long arm including the ENO4 locus); specific disease associations include human male infertility from a 2023 study identifying ENO4 variants causing sperm structural defects, though most variant significance remains marked '?' pending phenotypic correlation. AlphaFold structure (AF-A6NNW6-F1) predicts high confidence folding (pLDDT 84.4) consistent with a well-structured metabolic enzyme containing ordered TIM barrel catalytic domain typical of enolase family members. With only 20 papers, ENO4 represents a genuine 'dark' gene with emerging mechanistic understanding (2013-2023): disruption of mouse Eno4 causes sperm structural defects and male infertility establishing reproductive function; human ENO4 variants cause non-syndromic male infertility through defective lipidated protein transport in retinal stem cell models revealing conserved fertility mechanism; ENO4 expression correlates with ATP synthesis in bubaline spermatozoa during cryopreservation and capacitation; and ENO4 shows cancer-enhanced expression patterns suggesting metabolic reprogramming in tumors. The mechanistic hypothesis posits that ENO4 functions as a sperm-specific glycolytic enzyme that: (1) catalyzes the penultimate step of glycolysis converting 2-phosphoglycerate to phosphoenolpyruvate through Mg2+-dependent dehydration mechanism using the TIM barrel active site; (2) forms homodimers via the N-terminal dimerization domain (positions 14-78) enabling cooperative substrate binding; (3) provides ATP through glycolysis specifically in sperm midpiece mitochondria explaining infertility phenotypes when disrupted; (4) cooperates with PGAM1 (0.973 score) and PKM (0.700 score) creating a metabolic channel for efficient energy production in motile sperm; (5) exhibits cancer-enhanced expression potentially supporting Warburg effect metabolism in rapidly dividing tumors; (6) shows unexpected expression in choroid plexus (5.2 nTPM) and fallopian tube (4.7 nTPM) suggesting roles in cerebrospinal fluid production and oviductal metabolism; chromosome 10q CNVs may contribute to metabolic disease phenotypes through dosage effects on glycolytic flux.
