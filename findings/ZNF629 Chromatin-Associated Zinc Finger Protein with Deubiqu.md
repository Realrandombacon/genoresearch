## Description
ZNF629 (zinc finger protein 629) encodes an 869 amino acid nuclear protein (UniProt Q9UEG4) containing three canonical C2H2-type zinc finger domains (IPR013087, PF00096, residues 150-233) that confer DNA-binding capability. The protein shows medium structural confidence in AlphaFold prediction (pLDDT 63.4), suggesting a combination of structured zinc finger regions and intrinsically disordered segments typical of transcriptional regulators.

STRING interaction analysis reveals ZNF629 functions at the intersection of ubiquitin signaling and chromatin regulation. High-confidence interactions include deubiquitinases YOD1 (0.788), OTUD7A (0.740), USP44 (0.642), and USP5 (0.564), plus ubiquitin conjugate UBC (0.821). Chromatin modifier interactions include histone methyltransferase SETDB1 (0.707), histone deacetylase HDAC6 (0.671), and transcriptional corepressor TRIM28 (0.400). The top partner ZUP1 (0.856) is a zinc finger protein with similar domain architecture.

HPA expression shows ubiquitous detection across all tissues with low tissue specificity, localized to nucleoplasm and Golgi apparatus. This pattern suggests constitutive nuclear function with potential secretory pathway involvement. ClinVar contains 10 pathogenic copy number variants (gains and losses) on chromosome 16p11.2-q24.3, indicating genomic dosage sensitivity and clinical relevance in chromosomal rearrangement syndromes.

The mechanistic hypothesis: ZNF629 functions as a chromatin-associated scaffold that recruits deubiquitinase complexes to specific genomic loci via its C2H2 zinc fingers. By interacting with SETDB1 and HDAC6, it may coordinate histone methylation and deacetylation with transcription factor deubiquitination, stabilizing chromatin-bound regulators. The disordered regions may enable conformational flexibility for multi-protein complex assembly. Dosage sensitivity suggests ZNF629 levels must be tightly regulated for proper chromatin homeostasis.
