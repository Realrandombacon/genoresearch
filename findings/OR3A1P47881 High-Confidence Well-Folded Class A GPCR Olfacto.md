## Description
OR3A1 (Olfactory receptor family 3 subfamily A member 1, also known as OR17-40 or OR40) is a 315 amino acid integral membrane protein localized to the plasma membrane of olfactory sensory neurons (UniProt P47881) with high-confidence well-folded architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 86.9). The receptor features the canonical class A G-protein coupled receptor (GPCR) architecture comprising seven transmembrane alpha-helices connected by extracellular and intracellular loops. Key domains include the Olfactory receptor family domain (IPR000725/PF13853, residues 35-309) forming a ~275 aa core module (~87%) specific to chemosensation, and the broader Rhodopsin-like GPCR family domain (IPR000276/IPR017452/G3DSA:1.20.1070.10, residues 26-314/44-293) defining its structural fold. Conserved signatures include the G-protein coupled receptors family 1 profile (PS50262) and signature (PS00237). Molecular functions include olfactory receptor activity (GO:0004984), G protein-coupled receptor signaling pathway (GO:0007186), and sensory perception of smell, placing OR3A1 as a primary detector of volatile organic compounds in the nasal epithelium.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places OR3A1 within the canonical olfactory signal transduction machinery: strongest interactions with ARRB2 (0.688, Beta-arrestin-2) and ARRB1 (0.688, Beta-arrestin-1), which mediate receptor desensitization and internalization upon odorant binding. High confidence links include GNAL (0.686, G-protein subunit alphaolf, the specific G-alpha subunit in olfactory neurons that activates adenylate cyclase), GNB1 (0.682, G-protein subunit beta-1), and GNG7 (0.678, G-protein subunit gamma-7), forming the heterotrimeric G-protein complex. Connections to GRK3 (0.675, G-protein coupled receptor kinase 3) and GRK2 (0.569) indicate the phosphorylation mechanism for arrestin recruitment. Downstream effectors include PRKACA (0.650) and PRKACB (0.650), catalytic subunits of Protein Kinase A, which are activated by cAMP produced after GNAL stimulation. GNG13 (0.566) is another gamma subunit potentially involved in specific neuronal contexts.\n\nHPA expression data reports 'Not detected' in standard tissues, which is a known artifact for olfactory receptors due to their highly restricted expression to the olfactory neuroepithelium (a tissue not typically sampled in bulk RNA-seq datasets like GTEx used by HPA). However, biological context confirms exclusive expression in olfactory sensory neurons of the posterior nasal cavity. The 'G-protein coupled receptor' and 'Predicted membrane protein' classifications align with its function.\n\nClinVar documents 41 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains and losses affecting the 17p13.3 region: CNV losses (17p13.3-13.2 deletions) and CNV gains (17p13.3-11.2 duplications). The moderate variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity within the olfactory receptor cluster. Haploinsufficiency may cause specific anosmia (loss of smell for certain odorants) or hyposmia, while duplications could lead to altered odorant sensitivity or ectopic expression. Large 17p deletions are associated with Miller-Dieker syndrome and other neurodevelopmental disorders; loss of OR3A1 may contribute to sensory deficits in these syndromes.\n\nConservation is strong across primates: human (P47881, 315 aa), gorilla (Q9TU89, 315 aa), and other mammals possess orthologs, reflecting evolutionary pressure to maintain the detection of specific ecological volatiles.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: OR3A1 functions as a ligand-gated GPCR on the cilia of olfactory sensory neurons. Upon binding specific volatile odorant molecules in the mucus layer, OR3A1 undergoes a conformational change that activates the associated G-protein (GNAL-GNB1-GNG7). This triggers the exchange of GDP for GTP on GNAL, causing dissociation and activation of Adenylate Cyclase III (ADCY3), leading to a surge in cAMP. Elevated cAMP opens cyclic nucleotide-gated (CNG) channels, allowing Ca2+ influx and subsequent Cl- efflux via ANO2, depolarizing the neuron and generating an action potential. Beta-arrestins (ARRB1/2) and GRKs rapidly phosphorylate and internalize the receptor to reset sensitivity. Dysregulation via 17p CNVs disrupts this precise detection system: loss causes specific anosmia or contributes to the sensory phenotype of 17p deletion syndromes, while gain may alter odorant discrimination thresholds.
