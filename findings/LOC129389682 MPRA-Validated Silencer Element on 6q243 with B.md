## Description
LOC129389682 is a 200bp cis-regulatory element (CRE) located on chromosome 6q24.3 (GRCh38: 148364658-148364858), functionally validated as a transcriptional silencer through STARR-seq massively parallel reporter assays (MPRAs) in K562 erythroleukemia cells. This element lacks canonical promoter, enhancer, or CTCF-bound insulator chromatin signatures but demonstrates robust repressive activity against the super core promoter SCP1. As a non-coding regulatory locus, it has no UniProt accession, protein domains, or STRING interaction partners. Human Protein Atlas could not resolve this locus to an Ensembl ID, consistent with its non-coding regulatory classification.\n\nClinVar analysis reveals 8 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variants disrupting this region—a balanced burden with equal deletion and duplication events (4 deletions:4 duplications ratio). Deletions span 6q24.3-25.1 regions (~1.5-6Mb) while duplications span 6q23.2-27 and 6q24.1-27 regions (~20-32Mb). This 1:1 ratio suggests both haploinsufficiency and triplosensitivity intolerance where both silencer loss and gain cause pathogenic phenotypes through derepression or hyper-repression mechanisms. These recurrent CNVs are associated with 6q deletion/duplication syndromes featuring developmental delay, intellectual disability, craniofacial dysmorphism, cardiac defects, and early-onset Parkinsonism. The 6q24-27 region is gene-dense, containing critical dosage-sensitive genes such as PARK2 (Parkinson Protein 2/parkin, E3 ubiquitin ligase, mitochondrial quality control), SIM1 (Single-Minded Homolog 1, hypothalamic development and obesity regulation), MYCNBP (MYCN proto-oncogene binding protein), and IMMP2L (inner mitochondrial membrane peptidase).\n\nHYPOTHESIS: LOC129389682 functions as a critical distal silencer within the 6q24-27 topologically associating domain (TAD), regulating expression of dosage-sensitive neuronal and metabolic genes. Candidate targets include PARK2 (mitochondrial quality control, dopaminergic neuron survival) or SIM1 (hypothalamic development, appetite regulation). The silencer likely recruits co-repressor complexes (PRC2, NCoR/SMRT, HDACs) to deposit H3K27me3 marks at target promoters. The balanced CNV burden (4:4 ratio) indicates dual intolerance where both silencer loss causes target gene derepression (PARK2/SIM1 overexpression causing mitochondrial stress or hypothalamic dysfunction) and silencer gain causes hyper-repression (PARK2/SIM1 underexpression causing dopaminergic toxicity or obesity). The K562 validation suggests potential roles in hematopoietic lineage specification. This balanced pattern contrasts with other 6q silencers (LOC129389608 showed 9:1 deletion bias), suggesting distinct target gene regulation within the 6q TAD architecture.
