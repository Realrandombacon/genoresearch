## Description
TBX22 (T-Box Transcription Factor 22) is a 520-amino acid X-linked transcription factor with 116 publications, representing a moderately characterized developmental regulator. The protein contains a conserved T-box DNA-binding domain (IPR001699, positions 56-465; IPR046360, positions 80-283) with p53-like transcription factor fold (IPR008967) and conserved T-box sites (IPR018186, positions 101-120 and 178-196) that mediate sequence-specific DNA binding to T-box recognition elements. AlphaFold predicts a partially structured protein (pLDDT 58.4), consistent with intrinsically disordered transactivation domains common in developmental transcription factors. HPA has no expression data, suggesting restricted spatiotemporal expression during embryonic palate development. ClinVar documents 165 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants (including copy number gains/losses at Xq13.1-22.3 and missense variants p.Glu111Lys, p.Phe256Ser, p.Arg191Trp, and splice variant c.356+5G>C), causing X-linked cleft palate and Abruzzo-Erickson syndrome. STRING interactions reveal TBX22 functions within a chromatin remodeling and transcriptional regulation network with high-confidence connections to CLPB (0.868), CLPX (0.725), SP100 (0.668), CLPP (0.651), plus developmental transcription factors MSX1 (0.641), IRF6 (0.620), BARX1 (0.619), and SUMO1 (0.600). The CLP family interactions (CLPB/CLPX/CLPP/CLPS) suggest involvement in AAA+ ATPase-mediated protein remodeling, while SUMO1 interaction indicates post-translational modification regulating transcriptional activity. Recent studies (2022-2026) identified TBX22 variants in Indonesian, Pakistani, and Slovenian families with non-syndromic cleft palate, and unique variants causing Abruzzo-Erickson syndrome with broader phenotypic spectrum. We hypothesize TBX22 functions as a palate-specific transcriptional regulator that binds T-box elements in target gene enhancers (including IRF6, MSX1, BARX1) during embryonic palatogenesis, with SUMO1-mediated modification fine-tuning transcriptional output, and CLP AAA+ ATPase complex interactions enabling chromatin remodeling for proper palatal shelf fusion—a mechanism disrupted in X-linked cleft palate syndromes.
