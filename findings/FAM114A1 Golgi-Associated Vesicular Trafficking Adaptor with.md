## Description
FAM114A1 encodes a 563-amino acid nucleocytoplasmic protein containing a conserved domain of unknown function DUF719 (IPR007998/PF05334, residues 126-299, full protein 1-560) belonging to the PTHR12842 family. AlphaFold structure prediction (pLDDT=63.6) indicates medium-confidence folding with potentially flexible regions, consistent with adaptor proteins that undergo conformational changes during binding partner recruitment. Protein interaction analysis reveals functionally coherent partnerships with vesicular trafficking machinery: RAB2A (0.640, Ras-related GTPase regulating ER-to-Golgi transport), RAB2B (0.530, paralogous GTPase with overlapping function), UNC119B (0.436, lipid-binding adaptor for ciliary vesicle transport), and POSTN (0.425, periostin extracellular matrix protein). The RAB2A/RAB2B interaction specificity suggests FAM114A1 functions as a RAB effector or guanine nucleotide exchange factor (GEF) regulator at ER-Golgi intermediate compartments. Tissue expression is ubiquitous (detected in all tissues, low tissue specificity) with group enrichment in blood cells and cell type enhancement in specific lineages, consistent with housekeeping vesicular trafficking function. Subcellular localization in nucleoplasm, Golgi apparatus, and cytosol supports dual roles in nuclear processes and secretory pathway organization. Clinical significance includes 16 pathogenic/likely pathogenic ClinVar variants, exclusively copy number gains affecting 4p16.3-11 region, indicating dosage sensitivity where gene duplication disrupts vesicular trafficking homeostasis. Evolutionary conservation is evident: mouse ortholog (569 aa) maintains similar length and domain architecture. The combination of DUF719 domain architecture, RAB GTPase interactions, Golgi localization, and CNV dosage sensitivity strongly suggests FAM114A1 functions as a vesicular trafficking adaptor protein that recruits cargo or regulatory factors to RAB2-positive ER-Golgi transport vesicles, potentially analogous to RABIP4/RUFY1 family effectors but with distinct DUF719-mediated binding specificity.
