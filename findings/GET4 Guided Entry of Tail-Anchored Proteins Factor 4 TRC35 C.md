# GET4 (Guided Entry of Tail-Anchored Proteins Factor 4, TRC35, CEE): Cytosolic and Nuclear TPR Repeat Scaffold Protein Mediating Tail-Anchored Protein Targeting to ER via BAT3 Complex Assembly with BAG6-UBL4A-GET3, Congenital Disorder of Glycosylation Type IIy, Radioresistance in Hepatocellular Carcinoma, and Neuroprotection Through Mitochondria-ER Contact Site Regulation

**Quality Score:** 7/10 (GOOD)

**Confidence:** MEDIUM-HIGH

## Description
GET4 encodes a 327 aa intracellular scaffold protein (UniProt Q7L5D6), also known as TRC35 or CEE, that functions as an essential component of the GET (Guided Entry of Tail-anchored proteins) pathway and BAT3 complex required for post-translational targeting of tail-anchored (TA) membrane proteins to the endoplasmic reticulum. This well-studied gene (98 publications) remains clinically significant as causative of congenital disorder of glycosylation type IIy. The protein features extensive tetratricopeptide repeat architecture: a GET4 family domain (IPR007317/PF04190/PTHR12875, residues 7-311/56-302) mediating TA protein recognition; multiple TPR-like helical domain superfamily repeats (IPR011990/G3DSA:1.25.40.10/SSF48371, residues 15-307) providing protein-protein interaction scaffolding with GO term for protein insertion into ER membrane and protein binding. STRING interactions reveal GET4 operates within extremely high-confidence TA protein targeting and quality control networks: BAG6 (0.999), UBL4A (0.999), GET3 (0.999) forming the core BAT3-GET4-GET3 targeting complex; GET1 (0.958), GET pathway receptor at ER membrane; RNF126 (0.956), ring finger protein 126 E3 ubiquitin ligase in TA protein quality control; SGTB (0.899), small glutamine-rich tetratricopeptide repeat containing beta co-chaperone; CAMLG (0.888), calcium modulating ligand in ER calcium homeostasis; AMFR (0.796), autocrine motility factor receptor E3 ligase; SEC61B (0.612), SEC61 translocon beta subunit; and SGTA (0.568), small glutamine-rich tetratricopeptide repeat containing alpha. HPA confirms ubiquitous expression with low tissue specificity (detected in all tissues), low cancer and immune cell specificity, nucleoplasm/nucleoli/mitotic chromosome/cytosol subcellular localization, classified as predicted intracellular protein involved in transport with congenital disorder of glycosylation disease involvement. ClinVar lists 46 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including extensive copy number gains (7p22.3-21.3 41kb-12Mb x3, 7p22.3-14.3 158kb-29Mb x3, 7p22.3-22.1 43kb-5.9Mb x3, 7p22.3-21.3 43kb-8.8Mb x3) and losses (7p22.3-22.2 41kb-3.8Mb x1, 7p22.3 43kb-2Mb x1, large deletion ?_193kb to 1.4Mb_?), plus intronic splice region variants (c.746+221A>G, c.746+218T>G, c.746+5G>A), demonstrating strong dosage sensitivity though specific monogenic diseases beyond CDG-IIy remain undefined. AlphaFold structure prediction yields high confidence (global pLDDT 87.6), revealing a well-folded TPR repeat solenoid structure characteristic of scaffold proteins mediating multiprotein complex assembly. Mechanistically, GET4 operates through integrated modes: (1) TA protein targeting—forms heterotrimeric complex with BAG6 and UBL4A (BAT3 complex) that captures newly synthesized TA proteins from ribosomes and transfers them to GET3 ATPase for delivery to GET1 receptor at ER membrane; (2) Ribosome-associated capture—2021 study (25 citations) demonstrates ribosome-bound GET4/5 facilitates TA protein capture by Sgt2 chaperone in yeast, conserved mechanism in humans; (3) GET3 remodeling—2025 cryo-EM studies show GET4/5 drives conformational remodeling of GET3 substrate-binding chamber enabling efficient TA protein loading; (4) Congenital disorder of glycosylation—gene summary confirms GET4 mutations cause CDG type IIy through defective ER protein targeting affecting glycosylation enzyme localization; (5) Hepatocellular carcinoma radioresistance—2025 study (3 citations) shows circular SNX25 promotes radioresistance by targeting BAG6-GET4 interaction, enhancing DNA damage repair; (6) Neuroprotection—2024 study (11 citations) demonstrates GET4 loss increases mitochondria-ER contact sites (MERCS) providing neuroprotection, suggesting therapeutic target for neurodegenerative diseases; (7) Protein quality control—RNF126/AMFR interactions link to ubiquitin-mediated degradation of mislocalized TA proteins. Loss-of-function causes defective TA protein targeting to ER, impaired glycosylation enzyme localization, congenital disorder of glycosylation type IIy, altered mitochondria-ER contact sites, increased neuronal survival under stress, and modified cancer radioresistance.
