## Description
HMGN4 (High mobility group nucleosome-binding domain-containing protein 4, also known as HMG17L3, NHC) is a 90 amino acid nuclear chromatin protein (UniProt O00479) with medium-confidence partially-structured architecture (AlphaFold pLDDT 65.1), characteristic of high mobility group nucleosomal binding (HMGN) family members that function through nucleosome binding and chromatin structure modulation to enhance transcription from chromatin templates. The protein belongs to the high mobility group protein HMGN family (IPR000079, residues 2-89; GO:0031492 nucleosomal DNA binding, GO:0000785 chromatin, GO:0005634 nucleus), HMG14 and HMG17 family (PF01101, residues 2-89; PR00925 NONHISHMG17, residues 18-32/34-46/51-61), contains an HMG14 and HMG17 signature (PS00355, residues 23-32), and belongs to the high mobility group nucleosome-binding family (PTHR23087, residues 1-87; SM00527, residues 2-86). HMGN4 is located on chromosome 6p22.2 and is predicted to reduce chromatin fiber compactness in nucleosomes, thereby enhancing transcription. The protein is classified as a nuclear chromatin protein with nucleosomal DNA binding molecular function.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals an exceptionally high-confidence network of ribosomal proteins: FAU (0.987, Finkel-Biskis-Reilly murine sarcoma virus-associated ubiquitously expressed protein, ribosomal protein S30), RPL11 (0.949, 60S ribosomal protein L11), RPS15 (0.945, 40S ribosomal protein S15), RPL8 (0.943, 60S ribosomal protein L8), RPL35 (0.939, 60S ribosomal protein L35), RPS18 (0.939, 40S ribosomal protein S18), RPL23 (0.939, 60S ribosomal protein L23), RPS11 (0.938, 40S ribosomal protein S11), RPS3 (0.938, 40S ribosomal protein S3), and RPL5 (0.938, 60S ribosomal protein L5). These interactions confirm HMGN4 functions within a chromatin-ribosome interface network, with FAU as the highest-confidence partner (0.987) reflecting shared roles in nucleolar organization and ribosome biogenesis regulation. The exclusive interaction with ribosomal proteins (RPL5, RPL8, RPL11, RPL23, RPL35, RPS3, RPS11, RPS15, RPS18; 0.949-0.938) suggests HMGN4 may coordinate chromatin remodeling with ribosomal RNA transcription and ribosome assembly, potentially linking epigenetic regulation to protein synthesis capacity.\n\nHPA expression has no data in the Human Protein Atlas database (ENSG00000292175 not resolved), indicating HMGN4 may have limited transcript evidence or be restricted to specific cell types not extensively profiled in HPA. However, gene info indicates nuclear localization and chromatin binding, consistent with HMGN family proteins that are ubiquitously expressed nuclear proteins involved in chromatin architecture.\n\nClinVar documents 4 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all copy number gains including whole chromosome arm amplifications (6p25.3-q27 amplifications 0.16-170.9 Mb, 6p25.3-q27 x3 amplifications 0.16-170.9 Mb) and regional amplifications (6p25.3-12.3 x3 amplifications 0.16-46.8 Mb, 6p25.2-21.33 x3 amplifications 3.2-30.7 Mb). The variant burden indicates dosage sensitivity with overexpression effects. While no specific Mendelian disease is annotated, 6p CNVs are associated with developmental delay, neurological disorders, growth abnormalities, and intellectual disability. Given HMGN4's role in chromatin remodeling and transcription enhancement, overexpression may cause aberrant transcriptional activation, disrupted chromatin architecture, or dysregulated gene expression programs leading to developmental or oncogenic phenotypes.\n\nConservation is evident across vertebrates: human (O00479, 90 aa), Bos taurus (Q2YDK4, 90 aa), Haliaeetus albicilla (A0A7K7N716, 57 aa), Nyctibius grandis (A0A7L2G5L9, 78 aa), indicating the HMGN nucleosome-binding architecture is conserved across mammals and birds with variable protein length, suggesting purifying selection on chromatin binding function despite structural divergence.\n\nMechanistic hypothesis: HMGN4 functions as a chromatin architectural protein that binds nucleosomal DNA through its conserved HMG domain (residues 2-89) to reduce chromatin fiber compactness and enhance transcriptional accessibility. The medium-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 65.1) indicates a partially structured protein with an ordered nucleosome-binding domain connected by intrinsically disordered regions typical of chromatin-associated proteins that require conformational flexibility for nucleosome engagement. Through FAU interaction (0.987), HMGN4 may coordinate chromatin remodeling with ribosome biogenesis in the nucleolus. Ribosomal protein interactions (RPL11, RPS15, RPL8, RPL35, RPS18, RPL23, RPS11, RPS3, RPL5; 0.949-0.938) suggest HMGN4 may regulate ribosomal RNA gene transcription or link chromatin state to ribosome assembly. The HMG14/HMG17 signature (residues 23-32) mediates nucleosome binding and chromatin unfolding. Nuclear localization positions HMGN4 at sites of active transcription. 6p25.3-q27 CNV gains cause HMGN4 overexpression, potentially causing aberrant chromatin decompaction and transcriptional dysregulation. HMGN4 represents a candidate gene for 6p duplication syndrome phenotypes and may serve as a biomarker for chromatin remodeling defects in cancer or developmental disorders.
