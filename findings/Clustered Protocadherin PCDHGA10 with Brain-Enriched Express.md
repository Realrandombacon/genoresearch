## Description
PCDHGA10 (Protocadherin Gamma Subfamily A, 10) is a 936-amino acid single-pass transmembrane protein belonging to the clustered protocadherin family, functioning in calcium-dependent homophilic cell-cell adhesion critical for neuronal circuit assembly and synaptic specificity. InterPro analysis reveals a canonical multi-domain cadherin architecture: six extracellular cadherin repeats (IPR002126/Cadherin-like at positions 49-137, 138-246, 246-351; PF00028 at 144-236, 251-341, 360-446), N-terminal cadherin domain (IPR013164, 34-116), C-terminal catenin-binding domain (IPR031904, 815-936), and cytoplasmic C-terminal domain (IPR032455, 692-776), consistent with classical cadherin-mediated adhesion requiring calcium ion binding (GO:0005509). STRING interactions identify clustering with other protocadherin family members: PCDHA11 (0.592), PCDHGA4 (0.572), PCDHA4 (0.524), PCDHA9 (0.523), PCDHGA8 (0.439), PCDHGB5 (0.428), and PCDHA12 (0.418), suggesting coordinated expression in neuronal self-avoidance and dendritic arborization; unexpected partners include olfactory receptor OR6P1 (0.476) and peptidase PGPEP1L (0.448), hinting at potential neuroendocrine or proteolytic regulatory roles. HPA data shows tissue-enriched RNA expression with highest levels in brain (14.9 nTPM), group-enriched single-cell specificity, low cancer specificity, and multi-compartment localization (plasma membrane, nucleoplasm, vesicles, cytosol), classified as a predicted membrane protein involved in cell adhesion. ClinVar contains 23 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including nonsense mutations (p.Gln243Ter, p.Gln457Ter, p.Arg415Ter, p.Lys714Ter, p.Gln40Ter), frameshift deletions (p.Gln65fs, p.Lys184fs, p.Leu228fs, p.Phe108fs), and missense variants (p.Ala488Val), though specific monogenic disease phenotypes remain under characterization (significance marked '?' for most); the variant burden suggests potential neurodevelopmental relevance given the protocadherin family's role in brain wiring. AlphaFold structure (AF-Q9Y5H3-F1) predicts confident folding (pLDDT 74.4) across the full-length protein. With only 10 papers, PCDHGA10 represents a genuine 'dark' gene transitioning to gray: recent studies (2024) identify it as a potential prognostic biomarker in lung squamous cell carcinoma and gastric cancer, correlating with immune infiltration patterns; its brain-enriched expression and conserved adhesion domains suggest a primary role in establishing neuronal connectivity through stochastic homophilic recognition, while dysregulation may contribute to tumorigenesis via loss of contact inhibition or altered cell migration.
