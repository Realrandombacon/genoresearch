# GFM2: Mitochondrial Ribosome-Releasing Factor 2 (mtEFG2) Essential for Mitochondrial Translation Termination and Ribosome Recycling, Mutated in Early-Onset Neurological Mitochondrial Diseases with Impaired OXPHOS Subunit Expression

**Date:** 2026-06-14T19:04:00.363873

**Quality Score:** 10/10 (EXCELLENT)

**Confidence:** HIGH

## Description
GFM2 (Q969S9) encodes a 779 amino acid mitochondrial ribosome-releasing factor 2 (mtEFG2) belonging to the translational GTPase superfamily that functions in the termination and ribosome recycling phases of mitochondrial protein synthesis. InterPro analysis reveals a multi-domain architecture characteristic of elongation factor G proteins: a translational GTP-binding domain (IPR000795/PF00009, positions 68-353) containing P-loop nucleotide triphosphate hydrolase fold (G3DSA:3.40.50.300, positions 64-355) with GTPase activity (GO:0003924) and GTP binding capability (GO:0005525); domain II (cd04092, positions 367-448; G3DSA:2.40.30.10, positions 356-491) mediating ribosome interaction; domain III (cd16262, positions 486-561; G3DSA:3.30.70.870, positions 492-565) contributing to factor stability; domain IV (IPR005517/PF02473, positions 561-682; cd01693, positions 562-682; G3DSA:3.30.230.10, positions 566-687) involved in tRNA mimicry for ribosome A-site recognition; domain V (IPR000640, positions 684-771; cd03713, positions 687-763) unique to mtEFG2 proteins; and a C-terminal extension (G3DSA:3.30.70.240, positions 688-758) potentially mediating mitochondrial-specific interactions. AlphaFold predicts a well-folded structure (pLDDT 78.6, high confidence) consistent with stable multi-domain GTPase architecture required for precise ribosome engagement during translation termination. STRING interactions demonstrate exclusive partnerships with mitochondrial ribosomal machinery: MRRF (0.992), mitochondrial ribosome-recycling factor acting sequentially with GFM2 to dissociate post-termination ribosomal complexes; MRPL12 (0.958), MRPL27 (0.951), MRPL20 (0.947), MRPL19 (0.933), MRPL13 (0.928), MRPL36 (0.920), MRPL58 (0.919), MRPL4 (0.919), MRPL9 (0.919), all large subunit ribosomal proteins forming the platform for GFM2-mediated peptide release and ribosome disassembly. HPA shows ubiquitous expression across all tissues with low specificity, triple compartment localization (mitochondria primary site, plus nucleoplasm and midbody ring suggesting potential cell cycle-regulated functions), classified under metabolic proteins/disease-related genes with protein biosynthesis biological process and hydrolase molecular function, directly implicated in primary mitochondrial disease. ClinVar contains 79 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including nonsense mutations (c.349C>T p.Arg117Ter in GTP-binding domain), frameshift deletions (c.1400del p.Lys467fs, c.1113del p.Phe372fs disrupting domains III-IV), splice variants (c.2028+1G>A affecting domain V splicing), and missense mutations (c.2195C>T p.Pro732Leu, c.314A>G p.Asp105Gly, c.1626T>G p.Ile542Met, c.346G>C p.Glu116Gln) distributed across functional domains, though specific monogenic disease names remain undefined in ClinVar. Literature (22 papers) demonstrates GFM2 variants cause early-onset neurological presentations of mitochondrial disease with impaired expression of oxidative phosphorylation (OXPHOS) subunits encoded by mitochondrial DNA; recent work shows co-regulation and synteny of GFM2 with NSA2 (cytosolic ribosomal biogenesis factor) linking mitochondrial and cytosolic ribosomal function in chronic kidney disease; splice-switching oligonucleotide therapy can rescue aberrant GFM2 pseudoexon inclusion restoring mitochondrial activity in vitro. The mechanistic hypothesis: GFM2 functions as the mitochondrial-specific translation termination factor that recognizes stop codons in the mitochondrial ribosome A-site through domain IV tRNA mimicry, hydrolyzes GTP via its P-loop GTPase domain (positions 68-353) to trigger peptidyl-tRNA hydrolysis releasing the nascent polypeptide chain, then collaborates with MRRF (score 0.992) to dissociate the post-termination ribosomal complex into subunits ready for new translation initiation cycles; the multi-domain architecture (domains II-V) enables sequential conformational changes coupling GTP hydrolysis to mechanical movements that drive tRNA/mRNA translocation and ribosome splitting; high-confidence AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 78.6) indicates stable inter-domain interfaces essential for coordinated GTPase cycle; ubiquitous expression reflects fundamental requirement for mitochondrial protein synthesis in all tissues, particularly energy-demanding tissues like brain explaining early-onset neurological phenotypes when mutated; 79 pathogenic variants distributed across all functional domains disrupt distinct steps of the termination/recycling cycle causing global impairment of mitochondrial DNA-encoded OXPHOS subunit synthesis (complexes I, III, IV, V) leading to respiratory chain deficiency and energy failure; nucleoplasm/midbody ring localization suggests potential non-canonical roles in cell division or nuclear-mitochondrial communication; GFM2-NSA2 synteny indicates evolutionary coordination between mitochondrial and cytosolic translation machineries potentially explaining multi-system disease manifestations including kidney pathology.
