## Description
RENBP (Renin Binding Protein, alias N-acylglucosamine 2-epimerase) is a 427-amino acid intracellular enzyme that catalyzes the epimerization of N-acylglucosamine to N-acylmannosamine, a critical step in sialic acid biosynthesis. InterPro analysis confirms membership in the N-acylglucosamine 2-epimerase/Cellobiose 2-epimerase family (IPR010819, residues 47-386) with the canonical AGE domain (IPR034116/cd00249, residues 14-407) that forms the catalytic core. The protein belongs to the six-hairpin glycosidase superfamily (IPR008928, residues 20-415; IPR012341, residues 11-412), indicating a conserved (β/α)8 barrel fold typical for carbohydrate-processing enzymes. The PF07221 Pfam domain (residues 47-386) encompasses the epimerase catalytic region. AlphaFold predicts a very high-confidence structure (pLDDT 94.4), indicating a well-folded enzyme with stable secondary structure throughout, consistent with a housekeeping metabolic enzyme requiring precise active site geometry.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis places RENBP within the sialic acid biosynthesis pathway with exceptionally high confidence for NAGK (0.950, N-acetylglucosamine kinase), GNE (0.944, UDP-N-acetylglucosamine 2-epimerase/N-acetylmannosamine kinase, the rate-limiting enzyme in sialic acid synthesis), NANS (0.941, N-acetylneuraminate synthase), and NPL (0.941, N-acylneuraminate pyruvate lyase). Additional partners include HEXA (0.907) and HEXB (0.903, beta-hexosaminidase subunits), CHIT1 (0.901) and CHIA (0.900, chitinases), NAA10 (0.900, N-alpha-acetyltransferase), and critically REN (0.871, renin). This interaction profile strongly supports dual functionality: (1) canonical role in sialic acid biosynthesis as part of the GNE-NANS-NPL metabolic cascade, and (2) non-canonical regulation of the renin-angiotensin system via direct renin binding.\n\nHuman Protein Atlas classifies RENBP as 'tissue enhanced' with highest expression in lymphoid tissue (68.0 nTPM) and kidney (42.7 nTPM), consistent with roles in immune cell glycosylation and renal renin regulation. It is categorized as cancer enhanced and immune cell enhanced, with cell type-enhanced expression in specific populations. The lymphoid-kidney expression pattern aligns with literature linking RENBP to cardiomyopathy and immune glycoengineering.\n\nClinVar reports 270 pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains and losses spanning Xq26.3-28 (coordinates: chrX:137491159-155700385, chrX:153828334-154347735, chrX:113417246-155233731). The X-chromosomal location (Xq28) places RENBP in a region associated with multiple X-linked disorders. While no specific monogenic syndrome is named in ClinVar, the extraordinary burden of CNVs (270 variants) indicates strong dosage sensitivity, suggesting that both haploinsufficiency and triplosensitivity may contribute to disease phenotypes. The variant spectrum includes whole-arm X chromosome CNVs, focal duplications, and deletions.\n\nLiterature analysis (30 papers) confirms RENBP as a partially characterized gene with emerging functional insights. A landmark 2025 study demonstrated that RENBP inhibition amplifies metabolic glycan labeling efficiency of antigen-presenting cells, revealing its role as a bottleneck in sialic acid-mediated immune cell engineering. Recent 2026 work identified sex-specific RENBP protein expression in cardiomyopathy patients, suggesting X-linked dosage effects contribute to cardiac disease susceptibility. Earlier studies (1994) mapped RENBP to Xq28, and functional work established its dual identity as both a metabolic enzyme and renin-binding protein.\n\nWe hypothesize that RENBP functions as a metabolic-immune hub that coordinates sialic acid biosynthesis with renin-angiotensin system regulation. In the canonical pathway, RENBP converts N-acetylglucosamine to N-acetylmannosamine, feeding into the GNE-NANS-NPL cascade that produces CMP-sialic acid for cell surface glycosylation. In immune cells, RENBP activity limits the efficiency of metabolic glycoengineering, suggesting it operates near saturation under physiological conditions. The REN interaction suggests a non-canonical role: RENBP may bind and regulate renin activity or trafficking in kidney juxtaglomerular cells, linking cellular metabolic state to blood pressure control. The six-hairpin glycosidase fold (pLDDT 94.4) provides a stable scaffold for catalysis, with the AGE domain forming the active site pocket. In lymphoid tissue, RENBP may regulate immune cell surface sialylation patterns that control cell-cell interactions, checkpoint receptor function, or antigen presentation. The X-linked location and massive CNV burden suggest RENBP dosage is critical for normal development, potentially explaining X-linked cardiomyopathy or immune dysregulation phenotypes. Sex-specific expression in cardiomyopathy patients supports a role in cardiac metabolism or renin signaling. RENBP represents a candidate gene for X-linked metabolic disorders, a target for enhancing cancer immunotherapy via glycoengineering, and a potential modifier of hypertension and heart failure.
