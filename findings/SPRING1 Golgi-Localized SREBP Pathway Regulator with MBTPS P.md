## Description
SPRING1 (SREBP Regulating Gene Protein 1, alias C12orf49) is a 205-amino acid Golgi-resident protein that functions as a critical regulator of sterol regulatory element-binding protein (SREBP) signaling and lipid homeostasis. InterPro analysis confirms membership in the SREBP regulating gene protein family (IPR019352, residues 11-204; PF10218, residues 72-195; PTHR13481), with GO-annotated positive regulation of SREBP signaling pathway (GO:2000640). AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (pLDDT 81.3), indicating a well-folded globular protein despite lacking homology to characterized enzymatic domains.\n\nSTRING interaction analysis reveals SPRING1 operates within the SREBP processing machinery, with highest confidence for MBTPS1 (0.799), the site-1 protease (S1P) that cleaves SREBP precursors in the Golgi. Additional partners include TMEM41A (0.550), a transmembrane protein implicated in lipid droplet formation and autophagy; MBTPS2 (0.415), the site-2 protease (S2P) that performs the second cleavage of SREBP; RGL4 (0.414), a Ral guanine nucleotide exchange factor; RAB3GAP2 (0.409), a Rab3 GTPase-activating protein; and SOWAHC (0.405), a Golgi-associated protein. This interaction network positions SPRING1 as a potential scaffold or cofactor that facilitates SREBP cleavage by the MBTPS1/2 protease complex.\n\nHuman Protein Atlas classifies SPRING1 as 'tissue enhanced' with expression detected in all tissues, highest in thyroid gland (35.0 nTPM). The protein is predicted intracellular with Golgi localization consistent with its role in SREBP processing. ClinVar reports 12 pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains and losses spanning 12q24.21-24.33, plus a missense variant p.Arg150Gln within the conserved SREBP-regulating domain, indicating dosage sensitivity and potential loss-of-function mechanisms.\n\nLiterature analysis (33 papers) reveals emerging roles beyond lipid metabolism: a 2022 study demonstrates SPRING1 is required for proper SREBP-mediated lipid synthesis, while recent work implicates aberrant SPRING1 expression in B-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia (B-ALL) progression. We hypothesize that SPRING1 functions as a Golgi-localized cofactor that stabilizes the MBTPS1-SREBP substrate complex, enhancing proteolytic efficiency and ensuring proper lipid homeostasis. The MBTPS1 interaction (0.799) suggests direct physical association during SREBP cleavage. In B-ALL, dysregulated SPRING1 may drive oncogenic lipid metabolism programs that support rapid proliferation of malignant B-cells. The thyroid gland expression peak suggests specialized roles in thyroid hormone-regulated lipid metabolism. The 12q24 CNVs implicate SPRING1 dosage in developmental disorders, where haploinsufficiency may disrupt lipid-dependent processes including myelination and membrane biogenesis. SPRING1 represents an understudied node connecting lipid metabolism, cancer, and developmental disease.
