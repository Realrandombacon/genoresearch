## Description
CNTROB (Centrobin) is a 903 amino acid centrosomal protein that functions as a critical regulator of centriole duplication, primary ciliogenesis, and spermatid head shaping through coiled-coil domain-mediated interactions with the BRCA2-NIN-CNTLN centrosomal network for spindle assembly and flagellar centriole formation in proliferating and differentiating tissues. InterPro analysis identifies characteristic centrobin architecture including Centrobin family (IPR038923, PTHR34439, residues 3-903) defining evolutionary relationship to centriole replication proteins with centriole replication function (GO:0007099) and cilium assembly regulation (GO:1902017), and Centrobin coiled-coil region (PF30878, residues 187-569) forming the extended structural scaffold for protein-protein interactions with centrosomal components. AlphaFold structure (AF-Q8N137-F1) predicts medium confidence (pLDDT 66.0) across the 903 residue sequence, indicating a partially structured protein with ordered coiled-coil domains interspersed with flexible regions suitable for dynamic centrosomal scaffolding and centriole engagement. HPA data classifies CNTROB as a predicted intracellular protein involved in cell cycle and cell division with centrosome, basal body, cytosol, and flagellar centriole subcellular localization reflecting centriolar and ciliary functions, ubiquitous expression (detected in all tissues), low tissue specificity, low cancer specificity, low immune cell specificity, and cell type-enhanced distribution consistent with regulated centriole duplication function. STRING interactions reveal tight coupling to centrosomal machinery: NIN (Ninein, 0.894) as primary scaffold partner for microtubule anchoring; CNTLN (Centriolin, 0.845) for cytokinesis completion; ODF2 (Outer Dense Fiber Protein 2, 0.815) for flagellar assembly; BRCA2 (Breast Cancer Type 2 Susceptibility Protein, 0.771) linking to DNA repair and breast cancer; CCP110 (Centrosomal Protein 110, 0.761) for centriole length control; PCNT (Pericentrin, 0.673) for pericentriolar matrix organization; CEP135 (Centrosomal Protein 135, 0.582) and CEP120 (0.517) for centriole biogenesis; SASS6 (SAS-6 Centriolar Protein, 0.479) for cartwheel assembly; and CETN2 (Centrin-2, 0.439) for centriole duplication fidelity. ClinVar lists 23 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number gains on 17p13.2-12 (chr17:4678235-14745263), 17p13.3-11.2 (chr17:525-21510992), 17p13.3-12 (chr17:165730-11404096), copy number losses on 17p13.1 (chr17:7241916-8692213, chr17:7020054-8086290), and deletions spanning 17p13 region (multiple independent CNV events), though no specific monogenic syndrome is defined, suggesting roles in developmental disorders and cancer susceptibility. Literature (13 papers) establishes CNTROB as an emerging centrosomal regulator: evaluation of association between centrosome amplification in breast cancer tumor tissue and CNTROB expression changes revealing cancer biomarker potential; centrobin controls primary ciliogenesis in vertebrates establishing ciliary function; rat hd mutation reveals essential role of centrobin in spermatid head shaping and head-tail coupling apparatus assembly confirming male fertility role; dynamics of gene expression during and post meiosis sets sperm agenda indicating spermatogenesis involvement. As an emerging centrosomal protein with ciliary and reproductive functions, CNTROB represents a critical node connecting centriole duplication to ciliogenesis, male fertility, and cancer through coiled-coil domain-mediated BRCA2-NIN-CNTLN network integration for centrosome scaffolding, primary ciliogenesis control for Hedgehog signaling competence, spermatid head shaping and head-tail coupling apparatus assembly for sperm motility, centrosome amplification in breast cancer progression, and 17p13 copy number variation disorder pathogenesis disrupted in male infertility, ciliopathy-like phenotypes, breast cancer centrosome abnormalities, and developmental disorders.
