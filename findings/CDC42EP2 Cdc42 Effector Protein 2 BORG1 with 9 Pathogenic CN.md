## Description
CDC42EP2 (Cdc42 Effector Protein 2, also known as BORG1) encodes a 210-amino acid intracellular protein that functions as a CDC42 GTPase effector regulating cytoskeletal organization. This LIGHT GRAY gene has only 15 literature references, with recent 2026 work revealing its critical role in CNS myelin integrity through septin filament regulation. InterPro analysis reveals multiple functional domains: CRIB/Cdc42-Rac-binding domain (IPR000095, PF00786, residues 29-86) for CDC42 interaction, Cdc42 effector protein 2 family signature (IPR017363, PIRSF038036, residues 1-210), Cdc42 effector-like domains (IPR029273, PF14957, residues 103-143 and 165-201), and BORG/CEP family membership (IPR051296, PTHR15344, residues 6-202). AlphaFold predicts a partially structured protein (pLDDT 61.7), consistent with intrinsically disordered regions enabling dynamic protein-protein interactions typical of cytoskeletal regulators. Human Protein Atlas shows ubiquitous expression with low tissue specificity, localized to vesicles, microtubules, and cytosol, with group enrichment in blood cells, supporting a role in cell shape regulation. STRING interactions reveal the complete CDC42 effector network: CDC42EP2 binds CDC42EP5 (0.976, very high confidence), CDC42 (0.966, very high confidence), ARHGAP26 (0.734), SEPTIN7 (0.724), SEPTIN6 (0.598), SLC25A45 (0.541), and RHOQ (0.413), placing it at the intersection of Rho GTPase signaling and septin cytoskeleton organization. ClinVar documents 9 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains and deletions spanning 11q12.1-13.5 and 11p13-q25, indicating dosage sensitivity though specific monogenic phenotypes remain undefined. Functional hypothesis: CDC42EP2 acts as a molecular scaffold linking active CDC42 to septin filament assembly, stabilizing higher-order cytoskeletal structures required for membrane compartmentalization in oligodendrocytes and blood cells. Mechanistic proposal: Through its CRIB domain, CDC42EP2 binds GTP-loaded CDC42, recruiting and organizing SEPTIN6/SEPTIN7 hetero-oligomers into filaments that scaffold the inner tongue of CNS myelin; loss-of-function or copy number alterations disrupt septin organization, compromising myelin integrity and potentially contributing to neurodevelopmental or demyelinating phenotypes. The 2026 literature directly implicates CDC42 effector proteins in CNS myelin integrity, suggesting CDC42EP2 dysfunction may underlie undiagnosed leukodystrophies or white matter disorders.
