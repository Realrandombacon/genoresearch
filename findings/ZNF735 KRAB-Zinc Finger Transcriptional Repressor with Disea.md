## Description
ZNF735 encodes a 412 amino acid Krüppel-associated box-containing zinc finger protein (KRAB-ZFP) localized to chromosome 7q11.21. The protein contains an N-terminal KRAB domain (residues 16-87, PF01352, IPR001909) and nine C-terminal C2H2-type zinc finger domains (PF00096, IPR013087) spanning residues 185-410. KRAB-ZFPs represent the largest family of mammalian transcriptional repressors, functioning through recruitment of the nuclear co-factor KAP1 (TRIM28) to establish heterochromatin via histone methylation.

STRING interaction analysis reveals ZNF735 associates with TRIM28 (score 0.522), confirming the canonical KRAB-KAP1 repression mechanism. Additional partners include SEC14L6 (0.494), OR52E4 (0.468), and BPY2 (0.447), suggesting potential roles in lipid metabolism and olfactory receptor regulation. HPA expression profiling shows ZNF735 is not detected in most tissues but displays cell type-enhanced expression, consistent with tissue-specific transcriptional regulation.

ClinVar documents 15 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number gains/losses at 7q11.21, inversions, and the TMEM106B-BRAF fusion. These structural variants disrupt the ZNF735 locus and are associated with developmental phenotypes, supporting its role in chromatin regulation during development. The zinc finger array (9 fingers) suggests sequence-specific DNA binding to ~27bp target sites, with the KRAB domain recruiting KAP1/SETDB1 complexes for H3K9me3 deposition and transcriptional silencing.

AlphaFold structure prediction failed (500 error), but domain architecture predicts an extended conformation with flexible zinc finger linkers enabling DNA major groove recognition. Conservation across primates (Pan paniscus, Pongo abelii, Macaca fascicularis) indicates functional constraint on both KRAB and zinc finger regions.
