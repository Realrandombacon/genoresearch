## Description
WFDC9 (WAP four-disulfide core domain 9, UniProt Q8NEX5) is an 89-amino acid secreted protein located on chromosome 20q11.22 within the WFDC gene cluster. As a member of the WFDC family, this protein contains the characteristic WAP (Whey Acidic Protein) four-disulfide core domain that defines a class of small secreted protease inhibitors. AlphaFold structure prediction achieves high confidence (pLDDT 79.8), indicating a well-folded stable structure with conserved disulfide-bonded architecture typical of WAP domain proteins that maintain structural integrity in extracellular environments. Human Protein Atlas shows extraordinary epididymis-enriched expression (933.0 nTPM — among the highest tissue-specific expression values observed), with tissue-enriched specificity and cell type-enriched pattern, classified as a predicted secreted protein. This extreme epididymal specificity strongly suggests WFDC9 functions in male reproductive biology, particularly sperm maturation and storage within the epididymal lumen. STRING interaction network reveals 10 partners comprising the epididymal secretory proteome: WFDC8 (0.683), WFDC10A (0.667), WFDC13 (0.575) — other WFDC family members co-expressed in epididymis; SPINT4 (0.582) and SPINT3 (0.434) — serine protease inhibitors of the Kunitz type; DEFB119/DEFB118/DEFB129 (0.528/0.513/0.424) — beta-defensin antimicrobial peptides abundant in epididymis; and EDDM3A/EDDM3B (0.437/0.425) — epididymal secretory proteins. This interactome confirms WFDC9 functions within the epididymal secretory milieu alongside protease inhibitors and antimicrobial peptides. ClinVar contains 10 pathogenic variants including copy-number gains and losses across 20p13-q13.33, demonstrating dosage sensitivity where genomic rearrangements disrupting chromosome 20 cause disease phenotypes potentially affecting male fertility. Conservation across mammals (mouse Q3UW41, 83 aa; rat Q6IE41, 74 aa; capuchin A0A6J3GNY4, 96 aa) indicates ancient evolutionary origin tied to mammalian reproductive function. WFDC9 likely functions as a protease inhibitor in the epididymal lumen, using its four-disulfide core WAP domain to inhibit specific serine or cysteine proteases that would otherwise damage spermatozoa during maturation and storage, working coordinately with other WFDC proteins, SPINT inhibitors, and defensins to create a protective microenvironment essential for sperm viability and male fertility.
