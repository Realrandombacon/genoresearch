# MUC6: Polymorphic Gel-Forming Mucin with von Willebrand Factor Domains and Gastric Protective Function

**Date:** 2026-06-29T01:13:04.452856

**Quality Score:** 7/10 (GOOD)

**Confidence:** MEDIUM

## Description
MUC6 (Gene ID: 4588) encodes a 2439 amino acid oligomeric gel-forming mucin (UniProt Q6W4X9) located at chromosome 11p15.5. Annotated as 'gene/pseudogene' reflecting population polymorphism, MUC6 is a member of the secreted gel-forming mucin family that forms the insoluble mucus barrier protecting the gastrointestinal epithelium.\n\nInterPro analysis reveals extensive domain architecture characteristic of gel-forming mucins: three von Willebrand factor type D domains (IPR001846/PF00094 at aa 33-214, 386-579, 856-1038), three VWFC domains (IPR001007 at aa 359-427, 829-899), three trypsin inhibitor-like cysteine-rich domains (IPR002919/cd19941 at aa 302-357, 664-721, 764-827), VWF/SSPO/Zonadhesin-like cysteine-rich domains (IPR014853), and a C-terminal cystine knot domain (IPR006207, aa 2349-2438) essential for dimerization and polymerization. The N-terminal D-domains mediate multimerization through disulfide bonding.\n\nAlphaFold predicts medium-confidence structure (pLDDT 58.6), consistent with mucins having ordered cysteine-rich domains interspersed with heavily glycosylated disordered regions containing tandem repeats. This mixed architecture enables both structural stability and flexible extension.\n\nSTRING reveals 10 interaction partners forming the mucin network: MUC2 (0.993), MUC1 (0.977), MUC7 (0.950), MUC5AC (0.930), MUC4 (0.911), MUC12 (0.907), MUC13 (0.899), MUC16 (0.857), MUC17 (0.831), and TFF2 (0.467). TFF2 (trefoil factor 2) co-expression is particularly notable as TFF peptides are co-secreted with MUC6 in gastric mucous cells and stabilize the mucus layer.\n\nClinVar reports 30 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly copy number gains at 11p15.5, though no specific disease associations are established. These CNVs may reflect the complex genomic architecture of this imprinted region (Beckwith-Wiedemann syndrome locus).\n\nFunctional hypothesis: MUC6 is produced by mucous neck cells in the stomach antrum and pyloric glands, forming a protective barrier against acid and pepsin. The protein undergoes extensive O-glycosylation in the central region, creating a hydrated gel that traps pathogens and lubricates the epithelium. D-domain-mediated multimerization creates large polymeric networks. Co-secretion with TFF2 enhances mucus viscosity and promotes epithelial restitution after injury. Population-level gene deletion polymorphism may influence susceptibility to gastritis, peptic ulcer disease, and gastric cancer.
