# LRRC4C: Neuronal Synaptic Scaffold Protein with Leucine-Rich Repeat and Immunoglobulin Domains Regulating Netrin-G Signaling and Postsynaptic Organization

**Quality Score:** 4.65/10  (E=7.5, D=1.8) [MODERATE]

## Description
LRRC4C (Gene ID: 55737, UniProt Q9HCJ2) is a 640 amino acid predicted membrane/cytosolic protein located on chromosome 11p12-p11.2 (NC_000011.10). InterPro domain analysis reveals a complex multi-domain architecture characteristic of synaptic scaffolding proteins: multiple leucine-rich repeat (LRR) domains including LRR N-terminal domain (IPR000372, positions 46-80), canonical LRRs (IPR001611, positions 100-170/198-219/220-242), and typical LRR subtypes (IPR003591, positions 79-98/99-122/123-146); plus immunoglobulin superfamily domains including Ig subtype 2 (IPR003598, positions 366-433), Ig V-set domain (IPR013106, positions 370-428), Ig I-set domain (IPR013098, positions 354-443), and Ig-like fold (IPR013783, positions 347-448). This LRR-Ig domain combination is hallmarks of cell adhesion and synaptic organizing proteins. AlphaFold structure prediction (AF-Q9HCJ2-F1) yields a high-confidence model (global pLDDT 75.8), indicating LRRC4C is a well-folded protein with stable tertiary structure suitable for mediating specific protein-protein interactions. Human Protein Atlas classifies LRRC4C as a predicted intracellular/membrane protein localized to cytosol with group-enriched RNA expression highest in brain (19.4 nTPM) and retina (15.8 nTPM); detected in many tissues with no immune cell expression. The brain and retina enrichment strongly implicates LRRC4C in neuronal function. STRING database identifies 10 interaction partners revealing compelling connections to synaptic biology: NTNG2 (score 0.999, netrin-G2 axon guidance ligand), NTNG1 (0.999, netrin-G1), DLG4 (0.906, PSD-95/discs large homolog 4, core postsynaptic density scaffold), WHRN (0.903, whirlin, Usher syndrome type 2 protein critical for stereocilia organization), MYO15A (0.720, myosin XV, deafness gene DFNB3), NRXN1 (0.693, neurexin-1 presynaptic cell adhesion molecule), MPP1 (0.674, MAGUK p55 subfamily member 1), ADGRV1 (0.672, very large GPCR usherin), USH1G (0.668, SANS Usher syndrome protein), and PTPRS (0.658, receptor-type tyrosine-protein phosphatase S). The near-perfect NTNG1/NTNG2 interactions (0.999) suggest LRRC4C may be a netrin-G receptor or co-receptor. DLG4/PSD-95 interaction confirms postsynaptic localization. Multiple Usher syndrome protein interactions (WHRN, MYO15A, ADGRV1, USH1G) suggest roles in sensory hair cell stereocilia organization. ClinVar lists 15 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all large copy number losses or gains spanning 11p11.2-q25 (multi-megabase regions affecting many genes); no specific monogenic disease is yet attributed solely to LRRC4C, but 11p deletions cause Jacobsen syndrome with neurodevelopmental deficits. Conservation is evident across vertebrates with mouse ortholog Q8C031 (640 aa, 95% identity). Functional hypothesis: LRRC4C functions as a postsynaptic scaffold protein that organizes the netrin-G signaling complex at excitatory synapses, potentially serving as a co-receptor for NTNG1/NTNG2 ligands that regulate synapse specification and maintenance. The LRR domains mediate protein-protein interactions while Ig domains provide structural stability. Interaction with PSD-95 (DLG4) anchors LRRC4C to the postsynaptic density. Usher syndrome protein interactions suggest dual roles in both neuronal synapses and cochlear/vestibular hair cell stereocilia. Disease mechanism: Haploinsufficiency within 11p deletion syndromes may contribute to neurodevelopmental delay, intellectual disability, and sensory deficits. LRRC4C variants may underlie nonsyndromic hearing loss or retinal degeneration. Priority experiments include immunofluorescence colocalization with PSD-95/netrin-G in hippocampal neurons, co-IP validation of NTNG1/NTNG2/DLG4 interactions, CRISPR knockout in neuronal cultures to assess synapse density and netrin-G signaling, and investigation of LRRC4C variants in hearing loss and neurodevelopmental disorder cohorts.
