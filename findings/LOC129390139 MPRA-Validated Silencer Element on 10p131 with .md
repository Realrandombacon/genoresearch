## Description
LOC129390139 is a 200bp cis-regulatory element (CRE) located on chromosome 10p13.1 (GRCh38: 16465896-16466096), functionally validated as a transcriptional silencer through STARR-seq massively parallel reporter assays (MPRAs) in K562 erythroleukemia cells. This element lacks canonical promoter, enhancer, or CTCF-bound insulator chromatin signatures but demonstrates robust repressive activity against the super core promoter SCP1. As a non-coding regulatory locus, it has no UniProt accession, protein domains, or STRING interaction partners. Human Protein Atlas could not resolve this locus to an Ensembl ID, consistent with its non-coding regulatory classification.\n\nClinVar analysis reveals 5 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variants disrupting this region—a balanced burden with both deletion and duplication events (3 deletions:2 duplications ratio). Deletions span 10p15.3-12.31 regions (~19Mb) and 10p14-12.31 (~14Mb); duplications span 10p15.3-12.1 (~26Mb) and 10p15.3-12.31 (~19Mb). These recurrent CNVs are associated with 10p deletion/duplication syndromes featuring developmental delay, intellectual disability, craniofacial dysmorphism, cardiac defects, and hypoparathyroidism. The 10p13-15 region is gene-dense, containing critical dosage-sensitive genes such as KLF6 (Krüppel-Like Factor 6, tumor suppressor, cell cycle regulation), MGMT (O-6-Methylguanine-DNA Methyltransferase, DNA repair), GATA3 (GATA Binding Protein 3, T-cell development, HDR syndrome), and neuregulin signaling components.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: LOC129390139 functions as a critical distal silencer within the 10p13-15 topologically associating domain (TAD), regulating expression of dosage-sensitive developmental and tumor suppressor genes. Candidate targets include KLF6 (cell cycle checkpoint, p53 pathway) or GATA3 (T-cell lineage specification, parathyroid development). The silencer likely recruits co-repressor complexes (PRC2, NCoR/SMRT, HDACs) to deposit H3K27me3 marks at target promoters. The balanced CNV burden (3:2 ratio) suggests both haploinsufficiency and triplosensitivity intolerance where both silencer loss and gain cause derepression or hyper-repression of developmental genes. The K562 validation suggests potential roles in hematopoietic/immune lineage specification, consistent with GATA3 T-cell functions. Deletions may cause KLF6 or GATA3 derepression leading to cell cycle dysregulation or T-cell defects while duplications may cause excessive silencing, contributing to 10p deletion/duplication syndrome with hypoparathyroidism and developmental delay.
