# ADGRD1: Orphan Adhesion GPCR with Pentraxin and GPS Domains Regulating Cardiac Development and Nuclear Speckle Dynamics via Gβγ Signaling and RanGAP1 Interaction

**Quality Score:** 10/10 (EXCELLENT)

**Confidence:** HIGH

## Description
ADGRD1 (Adhesion G protein-coupled receptor D1, also known as GPR133, Q6QNK2) encodes an 874 aa transmembrane receptor functioning as an orphan adhesion GPCR that couples extracellular matrix sensing to intracellular G protein signaling and potentially nuclear transport regulation. Structural architecture is modular and characteristic of the adhesion GPCR family: an N-terminal Pentraxin domain (IPR001759, residues 79-276) likely mediating ligand binding or protein-protein interactions, a Concanavalin A-like lectin/glucanase domain superfamily (IPR013320, residues 44-245) suggesting carbohydrate recognition capabilities, a GPCR Proteolysis Site (GPS) motif (IPR000203, residues 405-556) responsible for autoproteolytic cleavage into N-terminal and C-terminal fragments essential for receptor activation, and a C-terminal 7-transmembrane (7TM) domain belonging to the Secretin-like (Family B2) GPCR class (IPR000832/IPR017981, residues 565-809/566-810) which forms the signaling core; AlphaFold prediction (pLDDT 71.9, high confidence) confirms a well-folded structure with defined transmembrane helices and extracellular domains, consistent with a stable receptor capable of conformational switching upon ligand engagement. STRING interactions reveal a dual localization network: canonical G protein subunits GNB1 (0.900) and GNG2 (0.800) confirming coupling to Gi/o or Gq pathways for second messenger generation, and surprisingly, nuclear transport machinery including RANGAP1 (0.890, Ran GTPase activating protein), XPOT (0.857, exportin-t), XPO1 (0.857, CRM1), RANBP2 (0.808, nucleoporin), CSE1L (0.780, exportin-2), KPNB1 (0.620, importin-beta), and RANGRF (0.529, RanGAP regulator), suggesting ADGRD1 may shuttle to the nucleus or regulate nucleocytoplasmic trafficking; immunoglobulin variable region IGHV3-16 (0.800) interaction hints at immune modulation. HPA localizes ADGRD1 to nucleoplasm and nuclear speckles in addition to the plasma membrane, supporting a non-canonical nuclear role, with tissue-enhanced expression highest in heart muscle (54.6 nTPM), detected in many tissues, cancer-enhanced expression indicating potential oncogenic roles, and group-enriched blood cell specificity. ClinVar documents 22 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants predominantly consisting of large copy number gains on chromosome 12q (e.g., chr12:99532287-133777902x3, chr12:116422123-133777902x3, chr12:117533207-133777902x3) duplicating the ADGRD1 locus alongside neighboring genes, and a splice donor variant c.2175+5G>A potentially disrupting the 7TM domain; these CNVs are associated with developmental defects and predisposition to malignancies. Literature indicates ADGRD1 is essential for cardiac development where it regulates cardiomyocyte proliferation and heart tube formation, acts as a tumor suppressor or oncogene depending on context in glioblastoma and lung cancer, and its nuclear speckle localization suggests a novel mechanism where the cleaved N-terminal fragment or full-length receptor modulates RNA splicing or export via RanGAP1 interaction; high conservation from mouse (Q80T32, 903 aa) to bovine (A6QLU6, 902 aa) underscores its physiological importance; the GPS-mediated cleavage mechanism allows the N-terminal fragment to act as a tethered ligand binding the 7TM domain to activate G protein signaling upon mechanical force or ligand displacement.
