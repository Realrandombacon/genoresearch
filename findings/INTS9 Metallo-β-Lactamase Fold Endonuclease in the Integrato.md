# INTS9: Metallo-β-Lactamase Fold Endonuclease in the Integrator Complex Catalyzing snRNA 3'-End Processing via INTS11 Heterodimerization and BRAT1-Mediated Neurodevelopmental Gene Regulation

## Description
INTS9 (Q9NV88) encodes a 658 amino acid essential nuclear protein that functions as a critical catalytic component of the Integrator complex, a multi-subunit RNA polymerase II-associated machinery responsible for 3'-end processing of small nuclear RNAs (snRNAs) and enhancer RNAs. InterPro analysis reveals a sophisticated metalloenzyme architecture: an N-terminal metallo-β-lactamase fold (IPR001279/PF16661, positions 85-237; cd16294 MBL-fold metallo-hydrolase, 1-265) providing the catalytic core, a central Beta-Casp domain (IPR022712/PF10996, positions 305-433/308-424) characteristic of metallo-β-lactamase superfamily nucleases, and a C-terminal domain (IPR048660/PF21382, positions 587-657) mediating complex assembly. AlphaFold predicts an exceptionally well-folded structure (pLDDT 90.9, very high confidence) consistent with stable catalytic activity and precise protein-protein interactions. STRING interactions demonstrate exclusive high-confidence partnerships with other Integrator complex subunits: INTS6, INTS3, INTS5, INTS1, INTS4, INTS11 (all 0.999), INTS8 (0.995), INTS7 (0.988), INTS2 (0.988), and INTS10 (0.962), reflecting the stable holocomplex architecture. HPA shows ubiquitous expression across all tissues with nucleoplasmic localization, classified as an essential protein with low tissue specificity consistent with housekeeping snRNA processing functions. ClinVar contains 76 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including copy number gains/losses spanning chromosome 8p regions and splice site mutations (c.401+2T>G, c.402-217G>T), though specific monogenic disease associations remain undefined. Literature (23 papers) demonstrates INTS9 forms a heterodimeric cleavage module with INTS11 that executes endonucleolytic cleavage of snRNA precursors, associates with BRAT1 to regulate neurodevelopmental genes, and shows prognostic significance in glioma linked to TP53 mutations and E2F signaling. The mechanistic hypothesis: INTS9 serves as the catalytic engine of the Integrator complex, using its metallo-β-lactamase fold to coordinate divalent metal ions (likely zinc) for site-specific endonucleolytic cleavage at snRNA 3'-ends; the Beta-Casp domain positions substrate RNA while the C-terminal domain anchors INTS9 within the holocomplex via interactions with INTS4 scaffold; INTS9-INTS11 heterodimerization creates the active site cleft that recognizes specific RNA sequence/structure motifs; BRAT1 association extends INTS9 function beyond snRNA processing to regulate neurodevelopmental gene expression programs, explaining why copy number alterations cause developmental phenotypes; in glioma, INTS9 dysregulation disrupts global transcriptional termination and RNA processing, cooperating with TP53 mutations to drive oncogenic E2F signaling and inflammatory microenvironment remodeling.
