## Description
BTBD2 (BTB/POZ domain-containing protein 2) is a 525 aa nuclear/cytoplasmic protein representing a genuine dark gene with only 19 literature references despite critical roles in DNA repair, antiviral immunity, and transcriptional regulation. Multi-omics data reveals BTBD2 as a multi-domain scaffold protein integrating topoisomerase activity with ubiquitin-proteasome signaling. InterPro analysis confirms a tripartite domain architecture: an N-terminal BTB/POZ domain (IPR000210/PF00651, aa 110-216) mediating protein-protein interactions and CUL3 E3 ligase binding, a central BACK domain (IPR011705/PF07707, aa 222-330) characteristic of BTB-Kelch family proteins, and a C-terminal PHR domain (IPR012983/PF08005, aa 375-524) of unknown function but structurally related to photolyase DNA repair enzymes (G3DSA:2.60.120.820). AlphaFold predicts high-confidence structure (pLDDT 82.6), indicating a well-folded multi-domain protein with rigid interdomain connections. HPA shows brain-enriched expression (159.5 nTPM, highest of all tissues), consistent with roles in neuronal DNA repair and HIV-1 neuropathogenesis. STRING interactions reveal two functional modules: (1) DNA topology complex with TOP1 (0.903, topoisomerase I) and BTBD1 (0.743, paralog); (2) Ubiquitin signaling complex with CUL3 (0.886, E3 ligase scaffold) and COP9 signalosome subunits COPS5/COPS3/COPS6/COPS2/COPS8 (0.837-0.761, deneddylase complex). ClinVar lists 22 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly 19p13.3 copy number gains (e.g., chr19:260912-2934171x3, chr19:260912-7246777x3) and one splice variant (c.1182-12C>G), suggesting dosage sensitivity and potential neurodevelopmental phenotypes. Literature (2002-2013) identifies BTBD2 as: (1) A TOP1-interacting protein affecting HIV-1 permissiveness in Old World monkey and human cells; (2) A colocalizer with TRIM5δ in cytoplasmic bodies; (3) A negative regulator of IRF-associated signaling via a shorter splice variant; (4) A glioblastoma survival predictor alongside LIG4, HMGA2, and RTEL1. The scarcity of literature (19 papers, none since 2013 focused on BTBD2 specifically) despite clear mechanistic roles makes BTBD2 a paradigmatic dark gene awaiting modern characterization.
