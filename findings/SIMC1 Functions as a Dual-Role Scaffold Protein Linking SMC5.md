## Description
SIMC1 (SUMO-interacting motif-containing protein 1) is an 872 aa nuclear/cytoplasmic protein representing a genuine dark gene with only 6 literature references, functioning as a critical molecular switch that bridges genome stability maintenance, antiviral immunity, and skeletal muscle proteostasis. Multi-omics data reveals SIMC1 as a bifunctional scaffold with distinct roles in the nucleus (SMC5/6 complex regulation) and cytoplasm (calpain-3 modulation). InterPro analysis confirms a unique domain architecture centered on the 'Elongin BC-PRC2 Interaction & Viral Restriction' family (IPR052119/PTHR23187, aa 23-858), indicating membership in a recently defined class of proteins that recruit ubiquitin ligases and chromatin modifiers to restrict viral DNA and regulate transcription. Unlike typical structured enzymes, AlphaFold predicts a medium-confidence structure (pLDDT 58.3), suggesting a largely intrinsically disordered protein (IDP) with flexible linkers connecting functional motifs—a hallmark of scaffolds that must bind multiple diverse partners. HPA shows tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in the ovary (38.7 nTPM), ubiquitous detection across tissues, and specific localization to the nucleoplasm and nucleoli fibrillar centers, consistent with roles in ribosomal biogenesis and nuclear organization. STRING interactions reveal two distinct functional clusters: (1) The SMC5/6 antiviral axis: SLF2 (0.542), SMC6 (0.494), NSMCE1 (0.613), UBE2I (0.484, SUMO E2), linking SIMC1 to the suppression of extrachromosomal DNA (ecDNA) and viral genomes; (2) The muscle protease axis: CAPN3 (0.654, calpain-3), linking SIMC1 to skeletal muscle integrity. Additional partners include RNF111 (0.549, E3 ligase) and FAM153B (0.479). ClinVar lists 47 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, exclusively large copy number variations at 5q35 (gains: chr5:175416095-177250474x3, chr5:170350336-180719789x3; losses: chr5:176144576-178014188x1, chr5:175470000-177450000x1), implicating SIMC1 dosage sensitivity in 5q35 deletion/duplication syndromes characterized by developmental delay and intellectual disability. The 6 literature references document its dual nature: (1) A seminal 2022 study (19 citations) identifying the SIMC1-SLF2 complex as the recruiter of SMC5/6 to viral replication centers (e.g., SV40, HPV) to silence ecDNA transcription; (2) A 2025 study showing SIMC1-SLF2 directs plasmid silencing antagonized by SV40 Large T antigen; (3) An earlier 2013 study (20 citations) identifying SIMC1 (then called PLEIAD/C5orf25) as a regulator of CAPN3 autolysis in skeletal muscle, scaffolding the substrate CTBP1. This represents a rare case of a single dark gene having two completely distinct, well-supported biological functions in different tissues.
