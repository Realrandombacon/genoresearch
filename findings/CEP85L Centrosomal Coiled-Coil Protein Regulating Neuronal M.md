## Description
CEP85L (Centrosomal Protein of 85 kDa-Like) is an 805 amino acid centrosomal protein (UniProt Q5SZL2) with partial characterization (27 papers) that functions in neuronal migration, centrosome organization, and ciliogenesis. The protein contains a Cep85/Cep85L family domain (IPR040210, residues 13-805) and a CC4 coiled-coil domain (IPR058190/PF24555, residues 606-681), indicating capacity for protein-protein interactions and centrosomal scaffold assembly. STRING interactions reveal partnerships with GOPC (0.631), a Golgi-associated PDZ protein involved in protein trafficking, SLC35F1 (0.605), a solute carrier, ROS1 (0.527), a receptor tyrosine kinase that forms oncogenic fusions with CEP85L in cancer, and CDK5 (0.454), a kinase critical for neuronal migration. HPA data shows tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in testis (28.0 nTPM), consistent with centrosome/cilia functions in spermatogenesis. ClinVar contains 105 pathogenic variants including missense mutations (p.Trp360Leu, p.Glu423Lys, p.Pro430Thr, p.Ser202Arg, p.Val776Met) causing lissencephaly type 10 with posterior predominant gyration abnormalities, seizures, and developmental delays. AlphaFold structure (pLDDT 64.0) indicates a partially structured protein with coiled-coil regions typical of centrosomal scaffolds. Functional hypothesis: CEP85L acts as a centrosomal scaffold protein that organizes protein complexes at the centrosome through coiled-coil mediated oligomerization, regulating microtubule nucleation and neuronal migration during cortical development, with loss-of-function causing lissencephaly through disrupted centrosome positioning and nucleokinesis, while oncogenic CEP85L-ROS1 fusions drive cancer through constitutive kinase activation.
