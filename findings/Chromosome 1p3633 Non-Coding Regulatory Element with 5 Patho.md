## Description
LOC100288069 is an uncharacterized non-coding genetic locus on chromosome 1p36.33 (NC_000001.11, positions 778633-725758, ~53 kb region) with substantial clinical significance evidenced by 5 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants despite complete absence of molecular characterization. No UniProt accession exists, preventing InterPro domain analysis and AlphaFold structure prediction. No mRNA transcript was identified in NCBI nucleotide databases (0 results). Human Protein Atlas could not resolve the gene to an Ensembl ID, indicating no detectable protein expression across any tissue. STRING-DB detected no protein-protein interactions. However, ClinVar contains 5 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants: variant 4286633 (microsatellite expansion, c.766400AATA[1]), variant 2664871 (1p36.33-35.1 duplication, chr1:99125-34026935, ~34 Mb), variant 58244 (1p36.33-36.31 deletion, chr1:629044-7008678, ~6.4 Mb), variant 58243 (1p36.33-36.32 deletion, chr1:629044-3346226, ~2.7 Mb), and variant 58242 (1p36.33-36.23 deletion, chr1:629025-8537745, ~7.9 Mb). All variants have uncertain significance (?) but are classified as pathogenic/likely pathogenic. The 1p36.33 chromosomal region is the critical locus for 1p36 deletion syndrome (most common terminal deletion disorder in humans) and contains several disease-associated genes including C1DUPP36.33 (chromosome 1p36.33 duplication syndrome/ATAD3 gene cluster, mitochondrial cholesterol transport, neurodevelopmental disorder), PRKCZ (protein kinase C zeta, atypical PKC, cell polarity, insulin signaling, NF-kappaB activation, cancer progression), ISG15 (ISG15 ubiquitin-like modifier, interferon-stimulated gene, antiviral immunity, protein ISGylation, innate immune response), MIR200B (microRNA-200b, epithelial-mesenchymal transition suppression, ZEB1/ZEB2 targeting, cancer metastasis inhibition), and MIR200A (microRNA-200a, miR-200 family, EMT regulation, chemoresistance modulation). Functional hypothesis: LOC100288069 likely represents a critical regulatory element within the 1p36.33 dosage-sensitive region, potentially functioning as a developmental enhancer coordinating ATAD3 gene cluster expression in neural crest cells and cardiomyocytes, or as a chromatin boundary separating immune response genes (ISG15) from cell polarity genes (PRKCZ). The 5 pathogenic variants indicate extreme regional dosage sensitivity where altered copy number disrupts multiple gene expression programs simultaneously. Disease mechanism involves 1p36 deletion syndrome pathogenesis through haploinsufficiency of multiple critical genes causing characteristic features: severe intellectual disability, speech delay, hypotonia, seizures, cardiomyopathy, distinct facial features (deep-set eyes, straight eyebrows), and behavioral problems. ATAD3 cluster dysfunction causes mitochondrial cholesterol transport defects and neurodevelopmental impairment. PRKCZ haploinsufficiency disrupts cell polarity and insulin signaling. ISG15 deficiency impairs antiviral immunity (ISG15 deficiency causes intracranial calcification and interferonopathy). MIR200 family dysregulation promotes epithelial-mesenchymal transition and cancer metastasis. The 1p36.33 deletion/duplication syndromes present with neurodevelopmental delay, cardiac anomalies, immune dysfunction, and cancer predisposition. The absence of protein-coding features combined with ClinVar evidence (5 variants including large CNVs) and ATAD3-PRKCZ-ISG15-miRNA gene cluster suggests this locus functions as a tissue-specific super-enhancer or topologically associating domain (TAD) boundary critical for neurodevelopment, mitochondrial function, antiviral immunity, and EMT suppression coordination.
