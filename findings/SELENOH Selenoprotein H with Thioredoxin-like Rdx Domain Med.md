## Description
SELENOH (Selenoprotein H, UniProt Q8IZQ5) is a 122 aa nuclear selenoprotein belonging to the Rdx-type selenoprotein family (IPR011893/IPR036249/IPR052674/PF10262/PTHR33638/TIGR02174, positions 35-119/35-121/7-121/35-118/7-121/36-119) that functions as a thioredoxin-like redox regulator with glutaredoxin superfamily architecture (G3DSA:3.40.30.10/SSF52833, positions 31-121/35-121) localized to nucleoplasm and nucleoli. The molecular structure contains a conserved SelT/SelW/SelH domain forming a thioredoxin fold typical of redox-active selenoproteins, though AlphaFold structure is unavailable in the database. Literature (40 papers) establishes SELENOH roles in: (1) PPARα coactivation linking selenium homeostasis to hepatic lipid metabolism and protection against steatohepatitis (2026), (2) mRNA sensitivity to short-term low aflatoxin B1 exposure in mice with varying selenium intake (2026), (3) dietary selenium deficiency accelerating aging-related gut microbial changes with Akkermansia muciniphila prominence and type 2 diabetes alleviation (2025), (4) mercury exposure linking plasma mercury levels to skin tissue-specific mitochondrial gene expression changes (2025). HPA confirms low tissue specificity with ubiquitous expression (detected in all tissues), predicted intracellular protein classification, nucleoplasm and nucleoli subcellular localization, and cell type enhanced expression—consistent with housekeeping redox regulation. ClinVar contains 11 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including large copy number gains (11p11.12-q12.2, 11q12.1, 11q12.1-13.3, 11p11.12-q13.1, 11p11.2-q12.2, 11p13-q25, 11p15.5-q25) and deletions affecting the 11p-q region, indicating genomic instability causes disease despite no formally assigned syndrome—likely contributing to metabolic disorders, accelerated aging, or selenium deficiency phenotypes. Conservation: mouse Selenoh (Q3UQA7) is 116 aa, macaque SELENOH (Q4R5Y4) is 122 aa, zebrafish selenoh (A0A8M1PAE4) is 127 aa—highly conserved across vertebrates. Functional hypothesis: SELENOH functions as a nuclear redox regulator that maintains nucleolar redox homeostasis and coactivates PPARα to regulate hepatic lipid metabolism and protect against steatohepatitis. The mechanistic model proposes: (1) SELENOH synthesized with selenocysteine via SECIS element in 3'UTR, (2) thioredoxin-like Rdx domain provides redox-active site for disulfide reduction in nucleoplasm, (3) nucleolar localization suggests role in ribosome biogenesis redox control, (4) PPARα coactivation enhances fatty acid oxidation gene expression, (5) interaction with SEPHS2 links SELENOH to selenocysteine biosynthesis pathway, (6) SELENOT/SELENOK/SELENOS/SELENOM interactions suggest coordinated ER-nuclear redox network, (7) selenium deficiency alters SELENOH expression affecting gut microbiota composition and aging phenotypes, (8) mercury exposure disrupts SELENOH function linking heavy metal toxicity to mitochondrial dysfunction, (9) aflatoxin B1 exposure alters SELENOH mRNA suggesting toxicant response role. Despite 40 publications, SELENOH remains mechanistically incomplete—its specific redox substrates, PPARα binding interface, and nucleolar functions are unknown. The 11 ClinVar variants and ubiquitous expression indicate clinical significance in metabolic disease and aging. SELENOH represents a dark selenoprotein at the intersection of redox biology, lipid metabolism, and environmental toxicology.
