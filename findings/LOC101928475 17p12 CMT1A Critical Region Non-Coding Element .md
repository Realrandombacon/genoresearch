## Description
LOC101928475 is an uncharacterized non-coding locus located on chromosome 17p12 (GRCh38: 15014804-15052275, ~37kb genomic span), positioned within the well-known Charcot-Marie-Tooth type 1A (CMT1A) critical region. This locus lacks canonical protein-coding features with no UniProt accession, no detectable protein domains (InterPro not applicable), no STRING interaction partners, and no Human Protein Atlas expression data (ENSG00000232058 not in HPA database), consistent with classification as a cis-regulatory element or non-coding RNA locus rather than a protein-coding gene.\n\nClinVar analysis reveals 39 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variants disrupting this region, representing HIGH CNV burden (compared to LOC129390117: 31 variants, LOC129389004: 12 variants). The variant spectrum shows BALANCED deletion:duplication ratio: approximately 19-20 deletions (copy number losses spanning 17p12 ~1.4-1.5 Mb regions) versus 19-20 duplications (copy number gains spanning 17p12 ~1.4-1.5 Mb regions). This ~1:1 ratio (50% deletions, 50% duplications) indicates BALANCED dosage sensitivity where both reduced and increased copy number are equally pathogenic. This pattern contrasts with LOC129390117 (~30:1 deletion bias) and LOC129389004 (~11:1 duplication bias), suggesting LOC101928475 requires PRECISE dosage maintenance.\n\nThe genomic context places LOC101928475 within 17p12, the critical region for CMT1A duplication syndrome and HNPP (hereditary neuropathy with liability to pressure palsies) deletion syndrome. The canonical disease gene in this region is PMP22 (peripheral myelin protein 22, tetraspan membrane protein essential for peripheral nerve myelination, haploinsufficiency causes HNPP, triplication causes CMT1A). The 17p12 region contains a 1.4 Mb recurrent duplication mediated by flanking CMT1A-REP recombination elements (LOC105943587). Nearby genes include PMP22, COX10 (cytochrome c oxidase assembly factor), TEKT3 (tektin-3, microtubule binding), and RAII (retinoic acid induced 1, Smith-Magenis syndrome gene).\n\nHYPOTHESIS: LOC101928475 functions as a critical cis-regulatory element (enhancer, silencer, or chromatin boundary/insulator) within the 17p12 topologically associating domain (TAD), specifically regulating PMP22 expression in Schwann cells during peripheral nerve myelination. The balanced ~1:1 CNV ratio indicates that BOTH haploinsufficiency (50% dosage) AND triplication (150% dosage) disrupt normal PMP22 regulation, causing opposite but equally severe neuropathies. This suggests LOC101928475 maintains PRECISE PMP22 expression levels through dosage-sensitive enhancer activity or chromatin boundary function. The mechanism likely involves Schwann cell-specific transcription factor binding (possibly SOX10, EGR2, or OCT6) that modulates PMP22 transcription during myelination onset. PMP22 overexpression causes CMT1A (demyelinating neuropathy, distal muscle weakness, sensory loss), while PMP22 haploinsufficiency causes HNPP (focal pressure palsies, tomacula formation). The balanced CNV burden suggests LOC101928475 is part of the PMP22 regulatory landscape, potentially functioning as a Schwann cell enhancer or TAD boundary that insulates PMP22 from neighboring repressive chromatin. Disruption of this element alters PMP22 expression dosage, causing peripheral neuropathy. The high CNV burden (39 variants) indicates this element has significant mutational target size and strong phenotypic effects, consistent with 17p12 rearrangements being among the most common pathogenic CNVs in human genetics.
