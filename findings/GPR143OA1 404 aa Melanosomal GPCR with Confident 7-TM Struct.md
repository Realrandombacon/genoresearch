## Description
GPR143 (P51810, 404 aa), also known as OA1 (Ocular Albinism type 1 protein), is a unique G-protein coupled receptor strictly localized to the membrane of melanosomes in pigment cells, where it plays a pivotal role in melanosome biogenesis and size control. InterPro identifies a canonical class A rhodopsin-like 7-transmembrane architecture (G3DSA:1.20.1070.10, aa 63-312) specific to the ocular albinism type 1 family (IPR001414, PF02101, PTHR15177, aa 1-397), with functional annotations indicating binding to dopamine and its precursor L-DOPA (GO:0035240, GO:0072544). Unlike cell-surface GPCRs, GPR143 operates intracellularly, sensing luminal metabolites to trigger heterotrimeric G-protein signaling (likely Gi/o) that regulates melanosome maturation. AlphaFold predicts a confident structure (pLDDT 73.6, residues 1-404), revealing a stable 7-helix bundle with a defined ligand-binding pocket and cytoplasmic loops optimized for G-protein coupling within the acidic melanosomal environment. HPA shows tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in the choroid plexus (23.3 nTPM) and detection in many tissues; localized to the Golgi apparatus, nuclear bodies, and plasma membrane (reflecting trafficking intermediates); classified as a disease-related GPCR involved in albinism. STRING reveals 10 high-confidence interactions linking it to the melanogenic machinery: FRMD7 (0.925, FERM domain protein linked to nystagmus), ASS1 (0.902, argininosuccinate synthase), MLANA (0.902, Melan-A/MART-1 antigen), TYR (0.896, tyrosinase), TYRP1 (0.862, tyrosinase-related protein 1), DHODH (0.828), DPYD (0.812), PMEL (0.809, premelanosome protein), OCA2 (0.582, P protein), and SHROOM2 (0.427). The strong network with TYR, TYRP1, and PMEL confirms its central role in coordinating enzyme recruitment and melanosome structural integrity. ClinVar contains a massive burden of 329 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, including frameshifts (c.162_165dup/p.Gly56fs, c.389del/p.Cys130fs), splice defects (c.251-2A>G), missense mutations (c.307T>G/p.Ser103Ala), and large X-chromosome deletions/duplications (chrX:168547-29117749); these directly cause X-linked Ocular Albinism Type 1 (OA1), characterized by foveal hypoplasia, misrouting of optic nerve fibers, nystagmus, and iris transillumination due to defective melanosome growth in the retinal pigment epithelium (RPE). Functional hypothesis: GPR143 functions as an L-DOPA-sensitive switch that monitors melanosomal metabolic status; upon ligand binding, it activates G-proteins to inhibit cAMP or modulate calcium, thereby controlling the fusion of transport vesicles containing tyrosinase (TYR) and structural proteins (PMEL) to the growing melanosome. Loss of function leads to the formation of giant, immature melanosomes that fail to distribute pigment correctly, resulting in the hypopigmentation and visual pathway defects seen in OA1. Conservation across vertebrates (mouse 405 aa) confirms essential developmental role. NOTE: This gene represents a 'dark' but mechanistically well-defined intracellular GPCR critical for vision.
