## Description
RUSC1-AS1 (RUSC1 antisense RNA 1, UniProt Q66K80, alias C1orf104) encodes a putative 236-amino acid peptide transcribed from the antisense strand of chromosome 1q22, overlapping the RUSC1 (RUN and SH3 domain containing 1/NESCA) locus. The protein sequence exhibits amino acid composition characteristic of intrinsically disordered proteins (IDPs): high Proline (35 residues, 14.9%), Glycine (30, 12.7%), Serine (27, 11.4%), and Alanine (27, 11.4%) content totaling 50.4% disorder-promoting residues, with low hydrophobicity (31.8%) and moderate charged residue content (18.2%). AlphaFold structure prediction yields low confidence (global pLDDT 48.7), confirming the predominantly disordered nature, suggesting the peptide may function through dynamic protein-protein interactions rather than structured enzymatic activity. RUSC1-AS1 is genomically positioned antisense to RUSC1, which encodes an actin-binding protein involved in protein polyubiquitination and cytoskeletal organization. The antisense orientation suggests potential regulatory mechanisms including transcriptional interference (RNA polymerase collision), RNA duplex formation triggering RNAi pathways, or recruitment of epigenetic silencing complexes (e.g., PRC2) to the RUSC1 promoter. ClinVar contains 22 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants encompassing copy number gains and losses spanning 1q21-23, implicating RUSC1-AS1 dosage in disease pathogenesis, though specific phenotypes remain undefined. No InterPro domains were detected, STRING interactions were not found (consistent with poor characterization), and HPA expression data is unavailable. BLAST analysis revealed no detectable orthologs in non-human species, suggesting RUSC1-AS1 may be primate-specific or rapidly evolving. The mechanistic hypothesis proposes that RUSC1-AS1 functions as a cis-acting regulatory element controlling RUSC1 expression levels through antisense-mediated transcriptional silencing, with the encoded 236 aa disordered peptide potentially serving as a trans-acting modulator of RUSC1 protein stability or actin-binding function through transient protein-protein interactions.
