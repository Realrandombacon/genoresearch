## Description
DMGDH (Dimethylglycine Dehydrogenase, Mitochondrial; UniProt Q9UI17) is an 866 amino acid mitochondrial enzyme with 59 publications, representing a partially characterized FAD-dependent oxidoreductase that catalyzes the second step in glycine degradation pathway, converting dimethylglycine to sarcosine while transferring one-carbon units to tetrahydrofolate, recently implicated as a metastasis suppressor in hepatocellular carcinoma.\n\nSTRUCTURAL ARCHITECTURE: InterPro analysis reveals a complex multi-domain architecture optimized for FAD-dependent oxidation and aminomethyl transfer reactions. The N-terminal region contains a FAD-dependent oxidoreductase domain (IPR006076, residues 52-414) providing the catalytic core for substrate oxidation. The central region contains GCVT N-terminal domains (IPR006222, residues 480-750) homologous to glycine cleavage system T-protein, enabling tetrahydrofolate binding. The C-terminal region contains an aminomethyltransferase C-terminal domain (IPR013977, residues 770-848) and beta-barrel domains (G3DSA:2.40.30.110/3.30.9.10, residues 767-844/735-760) for substrate recognition.
