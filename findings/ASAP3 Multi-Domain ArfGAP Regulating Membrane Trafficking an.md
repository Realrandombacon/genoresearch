## Description
ASAP3 encodes a 903 aa Arf-GAP with SH3 domain, ANK repeat and PH domain-containing protein 3 (UniProt Q8TDY4), functioning as a multi-domain Arf GTPase-activating protein that regulates membrane trafficking, cytoskeletal dynamics, and organelle crosstalk. The protein contains a BAR domain (cd07640, aa35-243) for membrane curvature sensing and dimerization, a PH domain (IPR001849, cd13251, aa294-400/302-396) for phosphoinositide binding and membrane recruitment, an ArfGAP domain (IPR001164, cd17900, aa424-546/426-551) catalyzing GTP hydrolysis on Arf GTPases, ankyrin repeats (IPR002110, aa621-679) for protein-protein interactions, and belongs to ArfGAP superfamily (G3DSA:1.10.220.150, aa421-542). AlphaFold prediction shows high confidence structure (pLDDT 73.8), indicating a well-folded multi-domain scaffold with stable architecture suitable for membrane association and protein interactions. HPA has no expression data available. STRING interactions reveal ASAP3 forms ArfGAP regulatory network: ASAP1 (0.964, paralogous ArfGAP—highest confidence), ASAP2 (0.907, paralogous ArfGAP), CRK (0.645, adaptor protein linking signaling to cytoskeleton), TULP3 (0.561, tubby-like protein 3), PDZD3 (0.523, PDZ domain protein), ARF6 (0.492, Arf GTPase substrate), ARAP2 (0.463, ArfGAP with RhoGAP domain), ARF5 (0.445, Arf GTPase), CYHR1 (0.435, cysteine/histidine-rich protein), GIT1 (0.425, G protein-coupled receptor kinase interactor). ClinVar contains 7 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including missense SNVs (p.Arg477Leu, p.Lys595Thr, p.Gly767Arg in ArfGAP/ankyrin regions) and large CNVs (1p36.33-35.1 deletion, 1p36.33-q44 copy number gains), though no specific disease is yet assigned. Literature (64 papers) documents ASAP3 roles in: (1) adult glioma prognosis correlating with NOTCH3 expression; (2) colorectal cancer metastasis suppression as miR-143-3p target (miR-143-3p inhibits ASAP3/ITGA6 to reduce metastasis); (3) lung adenocarcinoma progression as HIF-1α downstream target critical for tumor growth; (4) myocardial ischemia-reperfusion injury protection via RAC1 activation inhibition and mitochondria-ER crosstalk attenuation; (5) cell proliferation promotion in various cancers. ASAP3 functions as an ArfGAP—the ArfGAP domain (aa424-546) catalyzes GTP hydrolysis on Arf6/Arf5 (0.445-0.492), converting active Arf-GTP to inactive Arf-GDP to terminate Arf signaling. The BAR domain (aa35-243) senses/bends membranes, positioning ASAP3 at curved membrane sites like endocytic pits or trafficking vesicles. The PH domain (aa294-400) binds phosphoinositides (PIP2/PIP3), recruiting ASAP3 to specific membrane compartments. Ankyrin repeats (aa621-679) mediate protein-protein interactions with signaling partners. High-confidence ASAP1/ASAP2 interactions (0.907-0.964) indicate functional redundancy among ArfGAP family members. CRK (0.645) and GIT1 (0.425) connections place ASAP3 in integrin signaling and focal adhesion dynamics. The glioma-NOTCH3 correlation suggests ASAP3 may regulate NOTCH trafficking or signaling. miR-143-3p targeting in colorectal cancer indicates post-transcriptional regulation of ArfGAP activity. HIF-1α regulation in lung cancer links ASAP3 to hypoxia response—tumors may upregulate ASAP3 to enhance membrane trafficking under low oxygen. Myocardial protection via RAC1 inhibition reveals unexpected crosstalk: ASAP3 may inhibit RAC1 indirectly through Arf6 regulation (Arf6 activates RAC1), reducing oxidative stress and mitochondrial-ER contact sites during ischemia-reperfusion. The 7 ClinVar variants in catalytic/structural domains may cause ArfGAP dysfunction contributing to developmental disorders, cancer susceptibility, or cardiovascular disease. The BAR-PH-ArfGAP-Ankyrin architecture enables ASAP3 to integrate membrane curvature sensing (BAR), lipid recognition (PH), GTPase regulation (ArfGAP), and scaffolding (Ankyrin) for coordinated control of vesicle trafficking, cell migration, and signal transduction.
