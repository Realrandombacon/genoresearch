# DEFB119: Epididymis-Enriched Beta-Defensin Chemoattractant Mediating Sperm Chemotaxis and Seminal Microbiome Homeostasis in Male Fertility

## Description
DEFB119 (UniProt Q8N690), also known as Beta-Defensin 119, encodes an 84 aa secreted antimicrobial peptide with specialized roles in male reproductive tract immunity and sperm guidance, functioning as a physiological chemoattractant for sperm chemotaxis toward the egg. InterPro analysis reveals a canonical big defensin architecture: an N-terminal big defensin domain (aa 2-50, IPR028060, PF14862) forming the characteristic defensin fold with antimicrobial activity against both Gram-negative and Gram-positive bacteria (GO:0050829, GO:0050830), classified within the Beta-defensin antimicrobial peptide family (PTHR47902, aa 1-84). AlphaFold predicts a high-confidence structure (global pLDDT 74.4), indicating a well-folded, stable defensin fold resistant to proteolytic degradation—a hallmark of secreted antimicrobial peptides that must survive harsh extracellular environments. STRING interactions define DEFB119 as a node within the beta-defensin cluster network on chromosome 20q11, interacting exclusively with other defensin family members: DEFB121 (0.855), DEFB127 (0.832), DEFB125 (0.827), DEFB115 (0.798), DEFB116 (0.787), DEFB129 (0.699), DEFB128 (0.605), DEFB118 (0.590), DEFB123 (0.588), and DEFB124 (0.511), indicating co-expression and potential cooperative antimicrobial activity within the reproductive tract microenvironment. HPA expression shows extraordinarily restricted tissue distribution with epididymis enrichment (1809.3 nTPM—one of the highest expression levels observed in dark gene research), classified as tissue-enriched and cancer-enriched, with predicted secreted localization and mitochondrial annotation suggesting potential roles in sperm energy metabolism; notably absent from immune cells, confirming epithelial-specific production in the male reproductive tract. ClinVar reports 10 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly large copy number gains on chromosome 20p11.23-q13.33 (spanning the defensin cluster region at 20q11.21-11.23), indicating genomic instability at this locus potentially linked to male infertility susceptibility, though no specific monogenic disease has been definitively assigned. Literature (11 papers) highlights: (1) Sperm Chemotaxis—DEFB119 (also called β-defensin 19/119) was identified as a physiological chemoattractant guiding sperm toward the egg, with distorted networks in seminal microbiome associated with idiopathic male infertility; (2) Seminal Microbiome Dysbiosis—elevated DEFB119 levels correlate with altered seminal microbiome composition in infertile men, suggesting a role in maintaining reproductive tract microbial homeostasis; (3) Male Infertility Marker—DEFB119 expression stratifies dysbiosis patterns in seminal fluid, potentially serving as a diagnostic biomarker for male-factor infertility. Functional hypothesis: DEFB119 functions as a: (1) Sperm Guidance Molecule—secreted by epididymal epithelium and/or oviductal cells to create a chemotactic gradient that guides capacitated sperm toward the oocyte during fertilization; (2) Reproductive Tract Guardian—maintains sterile conditions in the epididymis and seminal fluid via direct antimicrobial activity against ascending pathogens; (3) Microbiome Regulator—shapes the seminal microbiome composition through selective antimicrobial pressure, with dysregulation contributing to infertility-associated dysbiosis; (4) Mitochondrial Modulator—potential localization to sperm mitochondria suggests roles in regulating sperm motility and energy metabolism. Mechanistic proposal: DEFB119 is secreted as a cationic amphipathic peptide from epididymal epithelial cells into the luminal fluid. The big defensin domain binds to specific G-protein coupled receptors (GPCRs) on the sperm surface (potentially formyl peptide receptors or defensin-specific receptors), triggering intracellular calcium signaling that directs flagellar beating toward the chemoattractant source. Simultaneously, DEFB119 binds negatively charged bacterial membranes in the seminal fluid, inserting via the disulfide-stabilized beta-sheet core to form pores that disrupt membrane potential and cause bacterial lysis, maintaining a healthy microbiome. The extraordinarily high epididymal expression (1809.3 nTPM) ensures sufficient peptide concentration for both chemotactic and antimicrobial functions. Chromosome 20q11 amplifications may alter DEFB119 dosage, disrupting the chemotactic gradient or antimicrobial balance and contributing to idiopathic male infertility. The combination of a well-folded high-confidence defensin structure, exclusive beta-defensin interaction network, extreme epididymal enrichment, cancer-associated copy number variations, and demonstrated roles in sperm chemotaxis and seminal microbiome homeostasis positions DEFB119 as a genuine dark gene with significant clinical relevance for male infertility diagnosis and treatment.
