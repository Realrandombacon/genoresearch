## Description
FAM20C (UniProt Q8IXL6, Extracellular serine/threonine protein kinase FAM20C/Golgi casein kinase/Dentin matrix protein 4/DMP4) is a 584 amino acid (~66 kDa) medium-sized secreted protein representing a conserved member of the FAM20 protein family with distinctive C-terminal kinase domain architecture characteristic of proteins involved in secretory pathway phosphorylation, bone/tooth biomineralization, extracellular matrix regulation, and Raine syndrome pathogenesis. The protein exhibits focused domain organization centered on the Golgi casein kinase catalytic module: (1) C-terminal putative kinase domain (cd10471 C-terminal putative kinase domain of FAM20C/Dentin Matrix Protein 4 DMP4, positions 354-565) providing a 212-amino acid catalytic core though functioning as an atypical protein kinase within the secretory pathway rather than cytoplasm, (2) FAM20 C-terminal domain (IPR009581 FAM20 C-terminal, positions 353-569; PF06702 Golgi casein kinase C-terminal Fam20, positions 353-569) overlapping with kinase domain establishing identity as the bona fide Golgi casein kinase responsible for phosphorylating secreted proteins, (3) FAM20 protein family membership (IPR024869 FAM20, positions 3-578; PTHR12450 DENTIN MATRIX PROTEIN 4 PROTEIN FAM20, positions 3-578) spanning nearly the entire protein length confirming conservation across vertebrates as essential biomineralization regulator, (4) N-terminal lipoprotein lipid attachment site (PS51257 Prokaryotic membrane lipoprotein lipid attachment site profile, positions 1-21) though in eukaryotes this likely functions as signal peptide for secretory pathway targeting, (5) Molecular function involves ATP-dependent serine/threonine protein kinase activity (EC 2.7.11.1) within the Golgi apparatus phosphorylating S-x-E/pS motifs on secreted substrates. This domain architecture positions FAM20C as the master regulator of secretory pathway phosphorylation—the only known kinase operating within the Golgi lumen to modify hundreds of secreted proteins critical for biomineralization, coagulation, and extracellular matrix assembly.
