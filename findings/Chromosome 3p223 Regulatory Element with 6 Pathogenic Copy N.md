## Description
LOC101928135 is an uncharacterized non-coding genetic locus on chromosome 3p22.3 (NC_000003.12, positions 35394022-34875794, ~518 kb region) with moderate clinical significance despite lacking protein-coding features. No UniProt accession exists, preventing InterPro domain analysis and AlphaFold structure prediction. No mRNA transcript was identified in NCBI nucleotide databases, and Human Protein Atlas could not resolve the gene to an Ensembl ID, indicating no detectable protein expression. STRING-DB detected no protein-protein interactions. However, ClinVar contains 6 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, all representing large-scale copy number variations spanning the 3p22.1-26.3 region (variants include 153415, 145586, 57978, 57770, 57769, 57397). These CNVs include both deletions (copy number loss, hemizygous) and duplications (copy number gain, trisomy) ranging from focal alterations (~3-7 Mb) to whole-arm changes (41+ Mb). The 3p22.3 chromosomal location is clinically notable as this region is associated with 3p deletion syndrome (characterized by developmental delay, intellectual disability, growth retardation, and dysmorphic features) and various lysosomal storage disorders. The region contains multiple dosage-sensitive genes including GLB1 (beta-galactosidase, lysosomal enzyme, GM1 gangliosidosis and Morquio B disease), CCR4 (C-C motif chemokine receptor 4, T-cell trafficking), DYNC1LI1 (dynein cytoplasmic light intermediate chain 1, neuronal migration), GPD1L (glycerol-3-phosphate dehydrogenase 1-like, Brugada syndrome susceptibility), and CMTM8 (chemokine-like factor, tumor suppressor). Functional hypothesis: LOC101928135 likely represents a cis-regulatory element or chromatin boundary controlling expression of neighboring dosage-sensitive genes in the 3p22.3 critical region, particularly GLB1 which is essential for lysosomal glycosphingolipid degradation. The 6 pathogenic CNVs indicate dosage sensitivity where copy number alterations disrupt normal transcriptional regulation of lysosomal and developmental genes. Disease mechanism likely involves haploinsufficiency of GLB1 (leading to GM1 gangliosidosis-like phenotypes), dysregulation of DYNC1LI1 (affecting neuronal migration and cortical development), or altered CCR4/CMTM8 expression affecting immune function and tumor suppression. The absence of protein-coding features combined with ClinVar evidence and lysosomal/neurodevelopmental disease association suggests this locus functions as a regulatory hub controlling lysosomal enzyme expression and neuronal development through long-range gene regulation.
