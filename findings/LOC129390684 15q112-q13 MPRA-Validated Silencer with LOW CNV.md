# LOC129390684: 15q11.2-q13 MPRA-Validated Silencer with LOW CNV Burden (6 Variants) and Moderate Deletion Bias (~67% Losses) in Imprinted Prader-Willi/Angelman Syndrome Region

## Description
LOC129390684 is a 200bp cis-regulatory element (CRE) located on chromosome 15q11.2-q13 (GRCh38: 34586953-34587153), functionally validated as a transcriptional silencer through STARR-seq massively parallel reporter assays (MPRAs) in K562 erythroleukemia cells. This locus lacks canonical promoter, enhancer, or CTCF-bound insulator chromatin signatures but demonstrates repressive activity against the super core promoter SCP1. As a non-coding regulatory sequence, it has no UniProt accession, protein domains, STRING interaction partners, or Human Protein Atlas expression data (HPA could not resolve to Ensembl ID - expected for non-coding locus).\n\nClinVar analysis reveals 6 pathogenic/likely pathogenic copy number variants disrupting this region, representing LOW CNV burden (compared to FAM53A: 146 variants, RNF115: 134 variants, LOC101928389: 82 variants, ZNF500: 30 variants, LOC129389193: 19 variants, LOC129389757: 16 variants). The variant spectrum shows MODERATE DELETION BIAS: 4 copy number losses (deletions spanning 15q11.2-14 through 15q13.3-14, ~5-15 Mb genomic spans) versus 2 copy number gains (duplications spanning 15q11.2-14 ~15 Mb and 15q13.3-14 ~3 Mb). This ~2:1 ratio (67% deletions, 33% duplications) indicates moderate loss-of-function dosage sensitivity where reduced silencing activity is more pathogenic than increased silencing, contrasting with duplication-biased elements like LOC129389193 (100% duplications) and LOC132205950 (~86% duplications).\n\nThe genomic context places LOC129390684 within the 15q11.2-q13 imprinted region, one of the most clinically significant genomic domains associated with Prader-Willi syndrome (PWS, paternal deletion/maternal UPD), Angelman syndrome (AS, maternal deletion/paternal UPD), and 15q duplication syndrome (characterized by intellectual disability, autism spectrum disorder, seizures, and hypotonia). This region contains multiple imprinted genes with parent-of-origin-specific expression including SNRPN (small nuclear ribonucleoprotein polypeptide N, PWS critical region), UBE3A (ubiquitin protein ligase E3A, maternal expression in brain, Angelman syndrome gene), GABRB3/GABRA5 (GABA-A receptor subunits, seizure susceptibility), CHRNA7 (cholinergic receptor alpha-7, neuronal signaling), and multiple snoRNA clusters (SNORD116, SNORD115 - PWS pathogenesis). The 15q11.2-q13 region is critical for neurodevelopment, genomic imprinting, and epigenetic regulation.\n\nHYPOTHESIS: LOC129390684 functions as a distal silencer within the 15q11.2-q13 imprinted domain, potentially regulating imprinted gene expression (SNRPN, UBE3A, snoRNA clusters) through allele-specific repression or chromatin boundary formation. The moderate ~2:1 deletion bias suggests that LOSS-of-silencing (haploinsufficiency, 50% activity) is moderately more pathogenic than GAIN-of-silencing (triplication, 150% activity). This indicates the target imprinted gene(s) require MAXIMAL repression on one allele - reduced silencing causes ectopic biallelic expression disrupting genomic imprinting. The mechanism likely involves: (1) allele-specific silencer activity maintaining maternal or paternal imprinting marks; (2) recruitment of DNA methyltransferases (DNMTs) or histone deacetylases (HDACs) to maintain differentially methylated regions (DMRs); (3) chromatin boundary/insulator function preventing ectopic enhancer-promoter interactions across imprinted domains; or (4) long-range chromatin looping regulating imprinting control region (ICR) activity. In K562 cells (erythroleukemia), this silencer may regulate hematopoietic expression of 15q11.2-q13 genes. The 15q11.2-q13 location within the PWS/AS critical region raises the possibility that LOC129390684 maintains imprinting at the PWS-SR (PWS imprinting center) or AS-SR (Angelman imprinting center). UBE3A maternal loss causes Angelman syndrome (severe intellectual disability, ataxia, seizures, happy demeanor). SNRPN paternal loss causes Prader-Willi syndrome (hypotonia, hyperphagia, obesity, intellectual disability). The LOW CNV burden (6 variants) indicates this element has modest mutational target size, with moderate loss-of-function dosage sensitivity suggesting 15q11.2-q13 deletion phenotypes may involve silencer-mediated imprinting dysregulation. Deletions encompassing 15q11.2-q13 cause PWS/AS through loss of imprinted gene expression. Duplications cause 15q duplication syndrome with autism, intellectual disability, and seizures. LOC129390684 dosage alterations may contribute to 15q11.2-q13 phenotypes through disruption of allele-specific silencing mechanisms.
