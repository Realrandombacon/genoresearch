## Description
TMEM74 encodes a 305 amino acid transmembrane protein (UniProt Q96NL1) that functions as an essential autophagy regulator through PI3K signal transduction. InterPro analysis reveals three conserved domains: IPR029695 (Transmembrane protein 74-like, residues 9-304), PF14927 (Neurensin/TMEM74 family, residues 169-258), and PTHR16125 (panther family, residues 9-304), indicating evolutionary conservation across the full protein length. STRING interaction network identifies high-confidence binding partners including ATG16L1 (score 0.808) and ATG9A (score 0.755), both core autophagy machinery components, plus ATG9B (0.597), BIRC5 (0.533), ATIC (0.507), DAPK2 (0.495), TM9SF1 (0.482), ATG16L2 (0.463), HSPB8 (0.434), and NRG3 (0.433). This interaction profile strongly positions TMEM74 within the autophagosome formation pathway. Human Protein Atlas shows tissue-enhanced expression with highest levels in brain (2.7 nTPM) and retina (2.3 nTPM), subcellular localization to Golgi apparatus and cytosol, consistent with its role in vesicular trafficking during autophagy. ClinVar documents 37 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants, predominantly copy number gains and losses spanning 8q23.1, suggesting dosage sensitivity and potential disease mechanisms through gene copy alteration. Mouse ortholog Q8BQU7 shares identical 305 aa length, indicating strong structural conservation. The convergence of autophagy protein interactions, Golgi localization, neural tissue expression, and clinical variant burden supports TMEM74 as a scaffold protein that recruits ATG16L1-ATG9A complexes to Golgi membranes for autophagosome initiation via PI3K-dependent signaling.
