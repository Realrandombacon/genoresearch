# ZNF681: KRAB-Zinc Finger Transcriptional Repressor with Cancer-Enhanced Expression and Dosage Sensitivity

## Description
ZNF681 (Zinc Finger Protein 681) is a 645-amino acid KRAB domain-containing C2H2 zinc finger transcription factor encoded on chromosome 19p12 (UniProt Q96N22). InterPro analysis reveals a canonical KRAB repressor domain (residues 3-75, PF01352; IPR001909; IPR036051; cd07765) that mediates transcriptional silencing through recruitment of the KAP1/TRIM28 corepressor complex and associated histone methyltransferases for H3K9me3 deposition. The protein contains multiple C2H2-type zinc finger motifs (IPR013087, PF00096, PF13465, PS00028) spanning residues 142-601 with at least 6-8 canonical fingers adopting the classic beta-beta-alpha fold (G3DSA:3.30.160.60, G3DSA:6.10.140.140) for sequence-specific DNA binding in the major groove. ZNF681 belongs to the PRDM4/PRDM1/PRDM14-like zinc finger family (IPR050331, residues 360-507), suggesting functional homology with PRDM transcriptional regulators. Human Protein Atlas classifies ZNF681 as a transcription factor with DNA-binding molecular function, low tissue specificity (detected in many tissues), cancer-enhanced expression pattern, and involvement in transcription and transcription regulation biological processes. STRING database identifies 8 protein interaction partners with low-to-medium confidence scores (0.400-0.540), including OR52N4 (0.540), TRIM28 (0.522; KAP1 corepressor), TIGD7 (0.480), ERVV-1 (0.476; endogenous retroviral element), SYNGR4 (0.471), TIGD6 (0.446), RGPD3 (0.435), and CRYBG2 (0.400). The interaction with TRIM28 strongly supports the canonical KRAB-ZNF repression mechanism. ClinVar documents 9 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants encompassing ZNF681, including large-scale copy number gains on chromosome 19 (19p13.11-q13.2, 19p13.3-q13.43, 19p13.12-12, 19p12, 19p13.2-q13.31, 19p13.12-q11, 19p13.11-q13.11 regions) and a 3-prime UTR single nucleotide variant (c.*1121G>T), indicating strong dosage sensitivity. The KRAB-ZNF architecture and PRDM family membership strongly suggest ZNF681 functions as a sequence-specific transcriptional repressor that recruits the TRIM28-SETDB1 complex to silence target genes through heterochromatin formation. Given cancer-enhanced expression, TRIM28 interaction, and extensive clinical CNV burden, ZNF681 likely regulates fundamental cellular processes such as cell cycle control, differentiation, or retroelement silencing, with haploinsufficiency or overexpression contributing to oncogenic phenotypes through dysregulation of downstream target gene networks including endogenous retroviral elements.
