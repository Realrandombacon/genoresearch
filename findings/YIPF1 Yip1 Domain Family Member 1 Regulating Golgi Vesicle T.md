## Description
YIPF1 (Yip1 Domain Family Member 1) is a 306-amino acid integral membrane protein (UniProt Q9Y548) representing a genuine dark gene with only 9 publications despite its critical role in Golgi apparatus vesicle trafficking and glycan synthesis. Structurally, YIPF1 belongs to the Yip1 protein family characterized by a conserved Yip1 domain (IPR006977/PF04893, residues 101-273) embedded within the broader Yip5/YIPF1/YIPF2 family (IPR039765/PTHR12822, residues 26-282). The Yip1 domain is predicted to form multiple transmembrane helices creating a channel or scaffold for small GTPase binding (GO:0031267), consistent with its annotated role in vesicle-mediated transport (GO:0016192) at the Golgi apparatus (GO:0005794). AlphaFold predicts high-confidence structure (pLDDT 76.8), consistent with a well-folded multi-pass transmembrane protein capable of stable membrane integration and partner interactions. Functionally, YIPF1 localizes to nucleoplasm, Golgi apparatus, and plasma membrane with ubiquitous expression across all tissues (HPA: detected in all, low tissue specificity), classified as a predicted membrane protein and transporter with cell type-enhanced specificity. STRING interactions reveal connections to trafficking machinery, ubiquitination, and muscle proteins: FBXO32 (0.946, F-box protein/atrogin-1 involved in muscle atrophy via ubiquitin-proteasome pathway), YIPF6 (0.900, paralog—Yip1 domain family member 6), TRIM63 (0.875, E3 ubiquitin ligase/MuRF1 regulating muscle protein degradation), YIPF3 (0.835, paralog), FBXO25 (0.776, F-box protein), YIPF4 (0.711, paralog), MSTN (0.666, myostatin—negative regulator of muscle growth), MYH6 (0.639, alpha-myosin heavy chain in cardiac muscle), YIPF2 (0.632, paralog—medial/trans-Golgi localized), and YIPF5 (0.593, paralog). This interaction network suggests YIPF1 functions in Golgi trafficking with potential links to muscle protein homeostasis through ubiquitin ligase interactions. Critically, ClinVar documents 15 pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants including splice site mutations (c.32-3C>A, c.481+2T>C, c.649-7C>G), large copy number gains spanning 1p36.33-q44 (chr1:849467-249224684), copy number losses (chr1:51941877-56688514, chr1:53675707-66644963), copy number gains (chr1:47493178-57042671, chr1:44475302-89585894), and inversions, yet ZERO diseases are formally associated—a profound gap given the essential trafficking function. The landmark 2017 study (15 citations) established that YIPF1, YIPF2, and YIPF6 localize to medial-/trans-Golgi and trans-Golgi network, playing roles in Golgi reassembly after mitotic fragmentation and glycan synthesis. Additional functional characterization of the YIPF protein family in mammalian cells (24 citations, 2016) provides foundational knowledge. Recent literature links YIPF1 to hepatocellular carcinoma prognosis via machine learning models (4 citations, 2025). The mechanistic hypothesis: YIPF1 functions as a multi-pass transmembrane scaffold at the Golgi that facilitates vesicle budding and cargo selection by recruiting small GTPases (likely Rab or Arf family members) through its Yip1 domain. As part of the YIPF family complex (YIPF1/2/3/4/5/6), it maintains Golgi structure during cell division and ensures proper glycosylation enzyme trafficking for glycan synthesis. The strong interactions with FBXO32/TRIM63 suggest YIPF1 may undergo ubiquitin-dependent regulation linking Golgi trafficking to protein quality control and degradation pathways, potentially explaining muscle-related interaction partners (MSTN, MYH6)—YIPF1 dysfunction could impair trafficking of muscle-specific glycoproteins or receptors. In cancer, altered YIPF1 expression may disrupt glycosylation patterns affecting cell surface receptor signaling and metastasis. The absence of formal disease associations despite 15 pathogenic variants suggests either embryonic lethality of homozygous loss, functional redundancy among YIPF paralogs, or that YIPF1 variants contribute to congenital disorders of glycosylation (CDG), myopathies, or cardiomyopathies not yet mapped. Future work should define YIPF1's specific small GTPase partners, investigate whether YIPF1 mutations cause CDG or muscle wasting phenotypes, and screen patients with 1p32-36 deletion/duplication syndromes for Golgi trafficking defects and glycosylation abnormalities.
